# Human

**Humans** (***Homo sapiens***, meaning 'thinking man' or 'wise man') are the most abundant and widespread [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species "Species") of [primates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primates "Primates"), characterized by [bipedalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipedalism "Bipedalism"), minimal body hair, and large, complex brains enabling the development of advanced [technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology "Technology"), [culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture "Culture"), and [language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language "Language"). Humans are highly social beings and tend to live in complex [social structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_structure "Social structure") composed of many cooperating and competing groups, from [families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family "Family") and [kinship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinship "Kinship") networks to political [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_(polity) "State (polity)"). [Social interactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_interaction "Social interaction") between humans have established a wide variety of values, [social norms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norm_(sociology) "Norm (sociology)"), and [rituals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritual "Ritual"), which bolster human [society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society "Society"). [Curiosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curiosity "Curiosity") and the human desire to understand and influence the environment have motivated humanity's development of [art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art "Art"), [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science "Science"), [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy "Philosophy"), [religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion "Religion"), [mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myth "Myth"), and other fields of knowledge.

Humans have a large and highly developed [prefrontal cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefrontal_cortex "Prefrontal cortex"), the region of the brain associated with higher [cognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognition "Cognition"). They are intelligent beings, capable of [episodic memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episodic_memory#In_animals "Episodic memory"), flexible [facial expressions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facial_expression "Facial expression"), [self-awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-awareness "Self-awareness") and a [theory of mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_mind "Theory of mind"). The human mind is capable of [introspection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introspection "Introspection") ([metacognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacognition "Metacognition")), private [thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thought "Thought"), [imagination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imagination "Imagination"), [volition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volition_(psychology) "Volition (psychology)"), and forming views on [existence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existence "Existence"). Humans can also mentally travel through time ([chronesthesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronesthesia "Chronesthesia")), which signifies [episodic foresight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foresight_(psychology) "Foresight (psychology)"). These attributes have allowed them to achieve large [technological advancements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_advancement "Technological advancement") through [reason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reason "Reason") and the transmission of [knowledge to future generations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_of_human_intelligence "Evolution of human intelligence"). In [anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology "Anthropology"), the cumulative preservation and advancement of knowledge across generations is known as the [ratchet effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratchet_effect "Ratchet effect").

Humans are [omnivorous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnivorous "Omnivorous"), capable of consuming a wide variety of plant and animal material, and have [used fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_of_fire_by_early_humans "Control of fire by early humans") to prepare and [cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooking "Cooking") food. They can survive for up to eight weeks without food, and three or four days without water. Humans are generally [diurnal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diurnality "Diurnality"), sleeping on average seven to nine hours per day. Human [childbirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Childbirth "Childbirth") is dangerous, with a high risk of complications and death. Both the mother and the father typically provide care for human offspring, who are [helpless at birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precociality_and_altriciality "Precociality and altriciality").

[Genes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene "Gene") and the [environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environment_(biophysical) "Environment (biophysical)") influence [human biological variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_variability "Human variability") in [appearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_physical_appearance "Human physical appearance"), [physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology "Physiology"), [immune system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immune_system "Immune system"), [mental abilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_intelligence "Human intelligence"), [body size](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_height "Human height"), and [lifespan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy "Life expectancy"). Though humans vary in many traits, any two humans are on average over 99% genetically similar, with the most genetically diverse populations from [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa "Africa"). The greatest degree of genetic variation exists between males and females. The genetic difference between [males](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man "Man") and [females](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woman "Woman") is around 1% to 2%. On average, males have greater [body strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_strength "Physical strength") and females generally have a higher [body fat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_fat "Body fat") percentage. Females undergo [menopause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menopause "Menopause") and become [infertile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infertility "Infertility") potentially decades before the end of their lives. They also have a [longer life span](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_differences_in_longevity "Sex differences in longevity") in almost every population around the world. The division into male and female [gender roles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_role "Gender role") has varied historically, and challenges to predominant gender norms have recurred in many societies.

[The evolutionary branch humans descended from](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_evolution "Human evolution") split from other [hominins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominini "Hominini") in Africa several million years ago. Although some scientists equate humans with all members of the genus *[Homo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo "Homo")*, in common usage it generally refers to *Homo sapiens*, the only [extant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extant_taxon "Extant taxon") member. *Homo sapiens* [emerged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_human "Early modern human") around 300,000 years ago and migrated [out of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recent_African_origin_of_modern_humans "Recent African origin of modern humans"), gradually replacing local populations of [archaic humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaic_humans "Archaic humans"). Early humans were [hunter-gatherers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer "Hunter-gatherer"), before the [invention of agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_Revolution "Neolithic Revolution") and [domestication of animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestication_of_vertebrates "Domestication of vertebrates") led to permanent settlement in the [Fertile Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertile_Crescent "Fertile Crescent") and other [cradles of civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cradle_of_civilization "Cradle of civilization"). As food surpluses enabled populations to become [larger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth "Population growth") and [denser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization "Urbanization"), forms of [governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governance "Governance") developed, the use of [writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writing "Writing") became necessary, and a number of [civilizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization "Civilization") rose and fell. Humans have continued to expand, with over 8.3 billion humans occupying almost all regions of Earth in 2026, and have even [visited the Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_landing "Moon landing"). This expansion, combined with [industrialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialisation "Industrialisation"), has led to [environmental destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_destruction "Environmental destruction") and [pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution "Pollution") that significantly contributes to the ongoing [mass extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene_extinction "Holocene extinction") of many other forms of life.

## Etymology and definition

All modern humans are classified into the [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species "Species") *Homo sapiens*, coined by [Carl Linnaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Linnaeus "Carl Linnaeus") in his 1735 work *[Systema Naturae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systema_Naturae "Systema Naturae")*. The [generic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Name_of_a_biological_genus "Name of a biological genus") name *[Homo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo "Homo")* is a learned 18th-century derivation from Latin *homō*, which refers to humans of either sex. The word *human* can refer to all members of the *Homo* genus. The name *Homo sapiens* means 'wise man' or 'knowledgeable man'. There is [disagreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species#The_species_problem "Species") about whether certain extinct members of the genus, namely [Neanderthals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neanderthal "Neanderthal"), should be included as a separate species of humans or as a [subspecies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subspecies "Subspecies") of *H. sapiens*.

*Human* is a [loanword](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loanword "Loanword") of [Middle English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_English "Middle English") from [Old French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_French "Old French") *humain*, ultimately from [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin") *hūmānus*, the adjectival form of *homō* ('man' – in the sense of humanity). The native English term *[man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_(word) "Man (word)")* can refer to the species generally (a synonym for *humanity*) as well as to human males. It may also refer to individuals of either sex.

Although the word *animal* is colloquially used as an antonym for *human*, and contrary to a [common biological misconception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_common_misconceptions_about_science,_technology,_and_mathematics#Evolution_and_paleontology "List of common misconceptions about science, technology, and mathematics"), humans are [animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal "Animal"). The word *[person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Person "Person")* is often used interchangeably with *human*, but philosophical debate exists as to whether [personhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personhood "Personhood") applies to all humans or all [sentient beings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentient_being "Sentient being"), and further if a human can lose personhood (such as by going into a [persistent vegetative state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persistent_vegetative_state "Persistent vegetative state")) and what the [beginning of human personhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beginning_of_human_personhood "Beginning of human personhood") is.

## Evolution

Humans belong to the biological [family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_(taxonomy) "Family (taxonomy)") of great apes (family [Hominidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominidae "Hominidae"), [superfamily Hominoidea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominoidea "Hominoidea")). The [lineage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lineage_(evolution) "Lineage (evolution)") of apes that eventually gave rise to humans first split from [gibbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbon "Gibbon") (family Hylobatidae), next [orangutans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orangutan "Orangutan") (genus *Pongo*), then [gorillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorilla "Gorilla") (genus *Gorilla*), and finally, [chimpanzees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimpanzee "Chimpanzee") and [bonobos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonobo "Bonobo") (genus *[Pan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_(genus) "Pan (genus)")*). The last split, between the human and chimpanzee–bonobo lineages, took place around 4-8 million years ago, in the late [Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miocene "Miocene") epoch. During this split, [chromosome 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromosome_2 "Chromosome 2") was formed in humans from the joining of two other chromosomes, leaving humans with only 23 pairs of chromosomes, compared to 24 for the other apes. Following their split with chimpanzees and bonobos, the [hominins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominini "Hominini") diversified into many species and at least two distinct genera. All but one of these lineages – representing the genus *[Homo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo "Homo")* and its sole extant species *Homo sapiens* – are now extinct.

The genus *Homo* evolved from *[Australopithecus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australopithecus "Australopithecus")*. Though [fossils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_fossils "Human fossils") from the transition are scarce, the earliest members of *Homo* share several key traits with *Australopithecus*. Due to the scant available evidence, the time of the divergence to the genus *Homo* does not have a consensus. Some studies using [molecular clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_clock "Molecular clock") techniques estimate the *Homo* genus appeared 4.30–2.56 million years ago, while others contest that some early *Homo* species are incorrectly included in the genus and therefore put this estimate at about 1.87 million years ago.

The earliest record of *Homo* is the 2.8 million-year-old specimen [LD 350-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LD_350-1 "LD 350-1") from [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia "Ethiopia"), and the earliest named species are *[Homo habilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_habilis "Homo habilis")* and *[Homo rudolfensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_rudolfensis "Homo rudolfensis"),* which evolved by 2.3 million years ago. *[H. erectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_erectus "Homo erectus")* (the African variant is sometimes called *[H. ergaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_ergaster "Homo ergaster")*) evolved 2 million years ago and was the first [archaic human](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaic_human "Archaic human") species to leave Africa and disperse across Eurasia. *H. erectus* also was the first to evolve a characteristically human [body plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_plan "Body plan"). *Homo sapiens* emerged in Africa at least 300,000 years ago from a species commonly designated as either *[H. heidelbergensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_heidelbergensis "Homo heidelbergensis")* or *[H. rhodesiensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_rhodesiensis "Homo rhodesiensis")*, the descendants of *H. erectus* that remained in Africa. *H. sapiens* migrated out of the continent, gradually replacing or interbreeding with local populations of archaic humans. Humans began exhibiting [behavioral modernity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavioral_modernity "Behavioral modernity") about 160,000–70,000 years ago, and possibly earlier. This development was likely selected amidst [natural climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_variability_and_change "Climate variability and change") in [Middle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Pleistocene "Middle Pleistocene") to [Late Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Pleistocene "Late Pleistocene") Africa.

The ["out of Africa" migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recent_African_origin_of_modern_humans "Recent African origin of modern humans") took place in at least two waves, the first around 130,000 to 100,000 years ago, the second ([Southern Dispersal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Dispersal "Southern Dispersal")) around 70,000 to 50,000 years ago. *H. sapiens* proceeded to colonize all the continents and larger islands [except Antarctica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonization_of_Antarctica "Colonization of Antarctica"), arriving in [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia "Eurasia") 125,000 years ago, Australia around 65,000 years ago, the Americas around 15,000 years ago, and remote islands such as [Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiian_Islands "Hawaiian Islands"), [Easter Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Island "Easter Island"), [Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar "Madagascar"), and [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand "New Zealand") in the years 300 to 1280 CE.

Human evolution was not a simple linear or branched progression but involved [interbreeding between related species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interbreeding_between_archaic_and_modern_humans "Interbreeding between archaic and modern humans"). Genomic research has shown that hybridization between substantially diverged lineages was common in human evolution. [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA") evidence suggests that several genes of Neanderthal origin are present among all non-sub-Saharan African populations, and Neanderthals and other hominins, such as [Denisovans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denisovan "Denisovan"), may have contributed up to 6% of their [genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genome "Genome") to present-day non-sub-Saharan African humans.

Human evolution is characterized by a number of [morphological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphology_(biology) "Morphology (biology)"), [developmental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_development_(biology) "Human development (biology)"), [physiological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_physiology "Human physiology"), and [behavioral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_behavior "Human behavior") changes that have taken place since the split between the [last common ancestor of humans and chimpanzees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimpanzee%E2%80%93human_last_common_ancestor "Chimpanzee–human last common ancestor"). The most significant of these adaptations are [hairlessness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory_of_nakedness_and_clothing#Evolution_of_hairlessness "Prehistory of nakedness and clothing"), obligate bipedalism, increased brain size, and decreased [sexual dimorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_dimorphism "Sexual dimorphism") ([neoteny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoteny "Neoteny")). The relationship between all these changes is the subject of ongoing debate.

## History

### Prehistory

Until about 12,000 years ago, all humans lived as [hunter-gatherers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer "Hunter-gatherer"). The [Neolithic Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_Revolution "Neolithic Revolution") (the invention of [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture "Agriculture")) first [took place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origins_of_agriculture_in_West_Asia "Origins of agriculture in West Asia") in [Southwest Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Asia "Southwest Asia") and spread through large parts of the [Old World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World "Old World") over the following millennia. It also occurred independently in [Mesoamerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica "Mesoamerica") (about 6,000 years ago), China, [Papua New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papua_New_Guinea "Papua New Guinea"), and the [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel "Sahel") and [West Savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanian_savanna "Sudanian savanna") regions of Africa.

The formation of permanent [human settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_settlement "Human settlement"), the [domestication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestication "Domestication") of animals, and the [use of metal tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcolithic "Chalcolithic") were followed by a permanent food surplus, for the first time in history. Agriculture and a sedentary lifestyle led to the emergence of early civilizations.

### Ancient

An [urban revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_revolution "Urban revolution") took place in the 4th millennium BCE with the development of [city-states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City-state "City-state"), particularly [Sumerian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumer "Sumer") cities located in [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia "Mesopotamia"). It was in these cities that the earliest known form of writing, [cuneiform script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuneiform_script "Cuneiform script"), appeared around 3000 BCE. Other major civilizations to develop around this time were [Ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt "Ancient Egypt") and the [Indus Valley Civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilization "Indus Valley Civilization"). They eventually traded with each other and invented technology such as wheels, plows and sails. Emerging by 3000 BCE, the [Caral–Supe civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caral%E2%80%93Supe_civilization "Caral–Supe civilization") is the oldest complex civilization in the Americas. Astronomy and mathematics were also developed, and the [Great Pyramid of Giza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza "Great Pyramid of Giza") was built. There is evidence of a [severe drought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4.2-kiloyear_event "4.2-kiloyear event") lasting about a hundred years that may have caused the decline of these civilizations, with new ones appearing in the aftermath. [Babylonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonia "Babylonia") came to dominate Mesopotamia while others—such as the [Poverty Point culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_Point_culture "Poverty Point culture"), [Minoans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoan_civilization "Minoan civilization"), and the [Shang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang_dynasty "Shang dynasty")—rose to prominence in new areas. The [Late Bronze Age collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Bronze_Age_collapse "Late Bronze Age collapse") around 1200 BCE resulted in the disappearance of a number of civilizations and the beginning of the [Greek Dark Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Dark_Ages "Greek Dark Ages"). During this period iron started replacing bronze, leading to the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age "Iron Age").

In the 5th century BCE, history started being [recorded as a discipline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historiography "Historiography"), which provided a much clearer picture of life at the time. Between the 8th and 6th centuries BCE, Europe entered the [classical antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_antiquity "Classical antiquity") age, a period when [ancient Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece "Ancient Greece") and [ancient Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome "Ancient Rome") flourished. Around this time, other civilizations also came to prominence. The [Maya civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_civilization "Maya civilization") started to build cities and create [complex calendars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_calendar "Maya calendar"). In Africa, the [Kingdom of Aksum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aksum "Kingdom of Aksum") overtook the declining [Kingdom of Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kush "Kingdom of Kush") and facilitated trade between India and the Mediterranean. In West Asia, the [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire "Achaemenid Empire")'s system of centralized governance became the precursor to many later empires, while the [Gupta Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire "Gupta Empire") in India and the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty "Han dynasty") in China have been described as [golden ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_ages "Golden ages") in their respective regions.

### Post-classical

Following the [fall of the Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Western_Roman_Empire "Fall of the Western Roman Empire") in 476, Europe entered the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages "Middle Ages"). During this period, [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity") and the [Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church "Catholic Church") would act as a source of authority and education. In the Middle East, [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam") became the prominent religion and expanded into North Africa. It led to an [Islamic Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Golden_Age "Islamic Golden Age"), inspiring achievements in [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture "Architecture"), the revival of old advances in science and technology, and the formation of a distinct way of life. The [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christendom "Christendom") and [Islamic worlds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_world "Islamic world") would eventually clash, with the [Kingdom of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_England "Kingdom of England"), the [Kingdom of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_France "Kingdom of France"), and the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire "Holy Roman Empire") declaring a series of [holy wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades "Crusades") to regain control of the [Holy Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Land "Holy Land") from [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim "Muslim").

In the Americas, between 200 and 900 CE, [Mesoamerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica "Mesoamerica") was in its [Classic Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerican_Classic_period "Mesoamerican Classic period"), while further north, complex [Mississippian societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippian_culture "Mississippian culture") would arise starting around 800 CE. The [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire "Mongol Empire") would conquer much of [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia "Eurasia") in the 13th and 14th centuries. Over this same time period, the [Mali Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_Empire "Mali Empire") in Africa grew to be the largest empire on the continent, stretching from [Senegambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegambia "Senegambia") to [Ivory Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory_Coast "Ivory Coast"). Oceania would see the rise of the [Tuʻi Tonga Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tu%CA%BBi_Tonga_Empire "Tuʻi Tonga Empire"), which expanded across many islands in the South Pacific. By the late 15th century, the [Aztecs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztecs "Aztecs") and [Inca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca "Inca") had become the dominant power in Mesoamerica and the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andean_civilizations "Andean civilizations"), respectively.

### Modern

The [early modern period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_period "Early modern period") in Europe and the Near East (c. 1450–1800) began with the [final defeat of the Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Constantinople "Fall of Constantinople") and the [rise of the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire "Ottoman Empire"). Meanwhile, Japan entered the [Edo period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period "Edo period"), the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty "Qing dynasty") rose in China, and the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire "Mughal Empire") ruled much of India. Europe underwent the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance "Renaissance"), starting in the 15th century, and the [Age of Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery "Age of Discovery") began with the exploration and [colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonialism "Colonialism") of new regions. This included the [colonization of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas "European colonization of the Americas") and the [Columbian Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbian_Exchange "Columbian Exchange"). This expansion led to the [Atlantic slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_slave_trade "Atlantic slave trade") and the [genocide of the Americas' indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide_of_indigenous_people_in_the_Americas "Genocide of indigenous people in the Americas"). This period also marked the [Scientific Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_Revolution "Scientific Revolution"), with great advances in [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics"), [mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics"), [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy "Astronomy"), and [physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology "Physiology").

The [late modern period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_modern_period "Late modern period") (1800–present) saw the [Industrial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution "Industrial Revolution") and [Technological Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_Revolution "Technological Revolution") bring such discoveries as [imaging technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaging_technology "Imaging technology"), major innovations in transport, and [energy development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_development "Energy development"). Influenced by [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment "Age of Enlightenment") ideals, the Americas and Europe experienced a period of political revolutions known as the [Age of Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Revolution "Age of Revolution"). The [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars "Napoleonic Wars") raged through Europe in the early 1800s, Spain lost most of its colonies in the [New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World "New World"), while Europeans continued [expansion into Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scramble_for_Africa "Scramble for Africa") – where European control went from 10% to almost 90% in less than 50 years – and Oceania. In the 19th century, the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire") expanded to become the [world's largest empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_empires "List of largest empires").

A tenuous [balance of power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balance_of_power_(international_relations) "Balance of power (international relations)") among European nations collapsed in 1914 with the outbreak of the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), one of the deadliest conflicts in history. In the 1930s, [a worldwide economic crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression "Great Depression") led to the rise of [authoritarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarian "Authoritarian") regimes and a [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), involving [almost all of the world's countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_by_country "World War II by country"). The war's destruction led to the collapse of most global empires, leading to widespread decolonization.

Following the conclusion of the Second World War in 1945, the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") emerged as the remaining [global superpowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_superpower "Global superpower"). This led to a [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War") that saw a struggle for global influence, including a [nuclear arms race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_arms_race "Nuclear arms race") and a [space race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Race "Space Race"), ending in the collapse of the Soviet Union. The current [Information Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_Age "Information Age"), spurred by the development of the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet "Internet"), sees the world becoming increasingly [globalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization "Globalization") and interconnected.

## Habitat and population

Early human settlements were dependent on proximity to [water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_resources "Water resources") and – depending on the lifestyle – other [natural resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_resource "Natural resource") used for [subsistence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsistence "Subsistence"), such as populations of animal prey for [hunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunting "Hunting") and [arable land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_land "Arable land") for growing crops and grazing livestock. Modern humans, however, have a great capacity for altering their [habitats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat_(ecology) "Habitat (ecology)") by means of technology, [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation "Irrigation"), [urban planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning "Urban planning"), construction, [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation "Deforestation") and [desertification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desertification "Desertification"). [Human settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_settlements "Human settlements") continue to be [vulnerable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulnerability "Vulnerability") to [natural disasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_disasters "Natural disasters"), especially those placed in hazardous locations and with low quality of construction. Grouping and deliberate habitat alteration are often done with the goals of providing protection, accumulating comforts or material wealth, expanding the available food, improving [aesthetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aesthetics "Aesthetics"), increasing knowledge, or enhancing the exchange of resources.

Humans are one of the most [adaptable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptation "Adaptation") species, despite having a low or narrow tolerance for many of the Earth's extreme environments. Currently the species is present in all eight [biogeographical realms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biogeographical_realms "Biogeographical realms"), although their presence in the [Antarctic realm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic_realm "Antarctic realm") is limited to [research stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_stations_in_Antarctica "Research stations in Antarctica"), and annually there is a population decline in the winter months of this realm. Humans have had a dramatic [effect on the environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_impact_on_the_environment "Human impact on the environment"). Human population growth, [industrialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialization "Industrialization"), [land development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_development "Land development"), [overconsumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overconsumption "Overconsumption"), and combustion of [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuels "Fossil fuels") have led to [environmental destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_destruction "Environmental destruction") and [pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution "Pollution") that significantly contributes to the ongoing [mass extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene_extinction "Holocene extinction") of other forms of life.

Within the last century, humans have also explored the [deep sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_sea "Deep sea") and [outer space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_space "Outer space"). Human habitation within these hostile environments is restrictive and expensive, typically limited in duration, and restricted to [scientific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific "Scientific"), [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military "Military"), or [industrial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industry_(economics) "Industry (economics)") expeditions. Humans [have visited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploration_of_the_Moon "Exploration of the Moon") the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon "Moon") and made their presence known on other [celestial bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_bodies "Celestial bodies") through human-made [robotic spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotic_spacecraft "Robotic spacecraft"). Since 2000, there has been continuous human presence [in space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_presence_in_space "Human presence in space") through habitation on the [International Space Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station "International Space Station").

By using advanced tools and [clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothing "Clothing"), humans have been able to extend their tolerance to a wide variety of temperatures, [humidities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humidity "Humidity"), and altitudes. As a result, humans are a [cosmopolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmopolitan_distribution "Cosmopolitan distribution") species found in almost all regions of the world, including [tropical rainforests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest "Tropical rainforest"), [arid deserts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert "Desert"), extremely cold [Arctic regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_region "Arctic region"), and heavily polluted cities; in comparison, most other species are confined to a few geographical areas by their limited adaptability. The [human population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_population "Human population") is not, however, uniformly distributed on the [Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth "Earth")'s surface, because the population density varies from one region to another, and large stretches of surface are almost completely uninhabited, like [Antarctica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctica "Antarctica") and vast swathes of the ocean. Most humans (61%) live in Asia; the remainder live in the Americas (14%), Africa (14%), Europe (11%), and Oceania (0.5%).

Estimates of the population at the time agriculture emerged in around 10,000 BC have ranged between 1 million and 15 million. Around 50–60 million people lived in the combined eastern and western [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire "Roman Empire") in the 4th century AD. [Bubonic plagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubonic_plague "Bubonic plague"), first recorded in the 6th century AD, reduced the population by 50%, with the [Black Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Death "Black Death") killing 75–200 million people in [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia "Eurasia") and [North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Africa "North Africa") alone. The human population is believed to have reached one billion in 1800. It has since then increased exponentially, reaching two billion in 1930 and three billion in 1960, four in 1975, five in 1987, and six billion in 1999. It passed seven billion in 2011 and passed eight billion in 2022. It took over two million years of [human prehistory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory "Prehistory") and [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_history "Human history") for the human population to reach one [billion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billion "Billion"), and only 207 years more to grow to 7 billion. The combined [biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass_(ecology) "Biomass (ecology)") of the carbon of all the humans on Earth in 2018 was estimated at 60 million tons, about 10 times larger than that of all non-domesticated mammals.

In 2018, 4.2 billion humans (55%) lived in urban areas, up from 751 million in 1950. The most urbanized regions are Northern America (82%), Latin America (81%), Europe (74%), and Oceania (68%), with Africa and Asia having nearly 90% of the world's 3.4 billion rural population. Problems for humans living in cities include various forms of pollution and [crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime "Crime"), especially in inner-city and suburban [slums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slum "Slum").

## Biology

### Anatomy and physiology

Most aspects of human physiology are closely [homologous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_(biology) "Homology (biology)") to corresponding aspects of animal physiology. The [dental formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dental_formula "Dental formula") of humans is: 2.1.2.32.1.2.3, like other [catarrhines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catarrhini "Catarrhini"). Humans have proportionately shorter [palates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palate "Palate") and much smaller [teeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_tooth "Human tooth") than other primates. They are the only primates to have short, relatively flush [canine teeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canine_teeth "Canine teeth"). Humans have characteristically crowded teeth, with gaps from lost teeth usually closing up quickly in young individuals. Humans are gradually losing their [third molars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisdom_teeth "Wisdom teeth"), with some individuals having them congenitally absent.

Humans share with chimpanzees a [vestigial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vestigiality "Vestigiality") tail, [appendix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appendix_(anatomy) "Appendix (anatomy)"), flexible shoulder joints, grasping fingers and [opposable thumbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposable_thumb "Opposable thumb"). Humans also have a more barrel-shaped chest in contrast to the funnel shape of other apes, an adaptation for bipedal respiration. Apart from bipedalism and brain size, humans differ from chimpanzees mostly in [smelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smelling "Smelling"), [hearing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearing "Hearing"), and [digesting proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digestion#Protein_digestion "Digestion"). While humans have a density of [hair follicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hair_follicle "Hair follicle") comparable to other apes, it is predominantly [vellus hair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vellus_hair "Vellus hair"), most of which is so short and wispy as to be practically invisible. Humans have about 2 million [sweat glands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweat_gland "Sweat gland") spread over their entire bodies, many more than chimpanzees, whose sweat glands are scarce and are mainly located on the palm of the hand and on the soles of the feet.

It is estimated that the worldwide average [height for an adult human](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_height "Human height") male is about 171 cm (5 ft 7 in), while the worldwide average height for adult human females is about 159 cm (5 ft 3 in). After birth, human growth proceeds slowly compared to other primates until [puberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puberty "Puberty"), where growth accelerates for a few years. Shrinkage of stature may begin in middle age in some individuals but tends to be typical in the extremely [aged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_age "Old age"). Throughout history, human populations have universally become taller, probably as a consequence of better nutrition, healthcare, and living conditions. The average [mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_weight "Body weight") of an adult human is 59 kg (130 lb) for females and 77 kg (170 lb) for males. Like many other conditions, body weight and body type are influenced by both [genetic susceptibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_predisposition "Genetic predisposition") and environment and vary greatly among individuals.

Humans have a far faster and more accurate [throw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Throw "Throw") than other animals. Humans are also among the best long-distance runners in the animal kingdom, but slower over short distances. Humans' thinner body hair and more productive sweat glands help avoid [heat exhaustion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_exhaustion "Heat exhaustion") while running for long distances. Compared to other apes, the human [heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart "Heart") produces greater [stroke volume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroke_volume "Stroke volume") and [cardiac output](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiac_output "Cardiac output"), and the [aorta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aorta "Aorta") is proportionately larger.

### Genetics

Humans are, like all animals, [plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant "Plant"), and [fungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fungus "Fungus"), a [eukaryotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eukaryote "Eukaryote") species, and like most animals, a [diploid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ploidy "Ploidy") species. Each [somatic cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somatic_cell "Somatic cell") has two sets of 23 [chromosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromosome "Chromosome"), each set received from one parent; [gametes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamete "Gamete") have only one set of chromosomes, which is a mixture of the two parental sets. Among the 23 pairs of chromosomes, there are 22 pairs of [autosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autosome "Autosome") and one pair of [sex chromosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex-determination_system "Sex-determination system"). In humans, sex determination is primarily mediated by the [SRY gene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex-determining_region_Y_protein "Sex-determining region Y protein") located on the Y chromosome. While the typical configuration follows an [XY sex-determination system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XY_sex-determination_system "XY sex-determination system") (XX for females, XY for males), the genetic expression of the Sex-determining Region Y protein is the critical factor in initiating male gonadal differentiation. Consequently, individuals may be born with a chromosomal [genotype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genotype "Genotype") that does not align with their [phenotypic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenotype "Phenotype") sex, a condition often resulting in infertility due to the absence of specific genes required for [gametogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gametogenesis "Gametogenesis"). [Genes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene "Gene") and [environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environment_(biophysical) "Environment (biophysical)") influence human biological variation in visible characteristics, physiology, disease susceptibility and mental abilities. The exact influence of genes and environment on certain traits is not well understood.

While no humans – not even [monozygotic twins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monozygotic_twins "Monozygotic twins") – are genetically identical, two humans on average will have a genetic similarity of 99.5%-99.9%. This makes them more [homogeneous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_genetic_variation "Human genetic variation") than other great apes, including chimpanzees. This small variation in human DNA compared to many other species suggests a [population bottleneck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_bottleneck "Population bottleneck") during the [Late Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Pleistocene "Late Pleistocene") (around 100,000 years ago), in which the human population was reduced to a small number of breeding pairs. The forces of [natural selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_selection "Natural selection") have continued to operate on human populations, with evidence that certain regions of the [genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genome "Genome") display [directional selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directional_selection "Directional selection") in the past 15,000 years.

The majority of the [human genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_genome "Human genome") was first sequenced in 2001, with complete sequencing accomplished in 2022. As of 2023[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Human&action=edit), it was estimated that humans have around 19,500 [protein-coding genes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein-coding_genes "Protein-coding genes"), with many different [variants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isoform "Isoform"), and an unknown number of [non-coding RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-coding_RNA "Non-coding RNA") genes. African populations harbor the highest number of private genetic variants. While many of the common variants found in populations outside of Africa are also found on the African continent, there are still large numbers that are private to these regions, especially [Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania "Oceania") and [the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Americas "The Americas"). By comparing [mitochondrial DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MtDNA "MtDNA"), which is inherited only from the mother, geneticists have concluded that the last female common ancestor whose [genetic marker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_marker "Genetic marker") is found in all modern humans, the so-called [mitochondrial Eve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondrial_Eve "Mitochondrial Eve"), must have lived around 90,000 to 200,000 years ago.

### Life cycle

Most [human reproduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_reproduction "Human reproduction") takes place by [internal fertilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_fertilization "Internal fertilization") via [sexual intercourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_sexual_intercourse "Human sexual intercourse"), but can also occur through [assisted reproductive technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assisted_reproductive_technology "Assisted reproductive technology") procedures. The average [gestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestation "Gestation") period is 38 weeks, but a normal pregnancy can vary by up to 37 days. Embryonic development in humans covers the first eight weeks of development; at the beginning of the ninth week the embryo is termed a [fetus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetus "Fetus"). Humans are able to [induce early labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_induction "Labor induction") or perform a [caesarean section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesarean_section "Caesarean section") if the child needs to be born earlier for medical reasons. In developed countries, [infants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant "Infant") are typically 3–4 kg (7–9 lb) in weight and 47–53 cm (19–21 in) in height at birth. However, [low birth weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_birth_weight "Low birth weight") is common in developing countries, and it contributes to the high levels of [infant mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality "Infant mortality") in these regions.

Compared with other species, human childbirth is dangerous, with a much higher risk of complications and death. The size of the fetus's head is more closely matched to the [pelvis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelvis "Pelvis") than in other primates. The reason for this is not completely understood, but it contributes to a painful labor that can last 24 hours or more. The chances of a successful labor increased significantly during the 20th century in wealthier countries with the advent of new medical technologies. In contrast, pregnancy and [natural childbirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_childbirth "Natural childbirth") remain hazardous ordeals in developing regions of the world, with [maternal death rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternal_death_rates "Maternal death rates") approximately 100 times greater than in developed countries.

Both the mother and the father provide care for human offspring, in contrast to other primates, where parental care is mostly done by the mother. [Helpless at birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altricial "Altricial"), humans continue to grow for some years, typically reaching [sexual maturity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_maturity "Sexual maturity") at 15 to 17 years of age. The human life span has been split into various stages ranging from three to twelve. Common stages include [infancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant "Infant"), [childhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Childhood "Childhood"), [adolescence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolescence "Adolescence"), [adulthood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult "Adult") and [old age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_age "Old age"). The lengths of these stages have varied across cultures and time periods but are typified by an unusually rapid growth spurt during adolescence. Human females undergo [menopause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menopause "Menopause") and become [infertile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infertility "Infertility") at around the age of 50. It has been proposed that menopause increases a woman's overall reproductive success by allowing her to invest more time and resources in her existing offspring, and in turn their children (the [grandmother hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grandmother_hypothesis "Grandmother hypothesis")), rather than by continuing to bear children into old age.

The life span of an individual depends on two major factors, genetics and lifestyle choices. For various reasons, including biological/genetic causes, women live on average about four years longer than men. As of 2018[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Human&action=edit), the global average [life expectancy at birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy_at_birth "Life expectancy at birth") of a girl is estimated to be 74.9 years compared to 70.4 for a boy. There are significant geographical variations in human life expectancy, mostly correlated with economic development – for example, life expectancy at birth in [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong "Hong Kong") is 87.6 years for girls and 81.8 for boys, while in the [Central African Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_African_Republic "Central African Republic"), it is 55.0 years for girls and 50.6 for boys. The developed world is generally aging, with the median age around 40 years. In the [developing world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_world "Third world"), the median age is between 15 and 20 years. While one in five Europeans is 60 years of age or older, only one in twenty Africans is 60 years of age or older. In 2012, the United Nations estimated that there were 316,600 living [centenarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centenarians "Centenarians") (humans of age 100 or older) worldwide.

### Diet

Humans are [omnivorous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnivorous "Omnivorous"), opportunistic feeders capable of consuming a wide variety of plant and animal material. Human groups have adopted a range of diets from purely [vegan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegan "Vegan") to primarily [carnivorous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnivorous "Carnivorous"). In some cases, dietary restrictions in humans can lead to [deficiency diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deficiency_diseases "Deficiency diseases"); however, stable human groups have adapted to many dietary patterns through both genetic specialization and cultural conventions to use nutritionally balanced food sources. Diet plays a large role in shaping the [gut microbiome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gut_microbiota "Gut microbiota"), which in turn affects human health. The human diet is prominently reflected in human culture and has led to the development of [food science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_science "Food science").

Until the development of agriculture, *Homo sapiens* employed a hunter-gatherer method as their sole means of food collection. This involved combining stationary food sources (such as fruits, grains, tubers, and mushrooms, insect larvae, and aquatic mollusks) with [wild game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_(food) "Game (food)"), which must be hunted and captured in order to be consumed. It has been proposed that humans have used fire to prepare and [cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooking "Cooking") food since the time of *[Homo erectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_erectus "Homo erectus")*. Human [domestication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestication "Domestication") of wild plants began about 11,700 years ago, leading to the [development of agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_agriculture "History of agriculture"), a gradual process called the [Neolithic Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_Revolution "Neolithic Revolution"). These dietary changes may also have altered human biology; the spread of [dairy farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_farming "Dairy farming") provided a new and rich source of food, leading to the evolution of the ability to digest [lactose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactose "Lactose") in some adults. The types of food consumed, and how they are prepared, have varied widely by time, location, and culture.

In general, humans can survive for up to eight weeks without food, depending on stored body fat. Survival without water is usually limited to three or four days, with a maximum of one week. In 2020, it was estimated that 9 million humans die every year from causes directly or indirectly related to [starvation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starvation "Starvation"). Childhood malnutrition is also common and contributes to the [global burden of disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disease_burden "Disease burden"). However, global food distribution is not even, and [obesity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obesity "Obesity") among some human populations has increased rapidly, leading to health complications and increased mortality in some [developed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_country "Developed country") and a few [developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_countries "Developing countries"). Worldwide, over one billion people are obese, with 16.2% of the population classified as overweight in 2024. Obesity is caused by consuming more [calories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calorie "Calorie") than are expended, so excessive weight gain is usually caused by an energy-dense diet.

Food consumption is the first step of the [digestive process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_digestive_system "Human digestive system"), in which humans ultimately expel [feces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_feces "Human feces") between three times per day and three times per week, with higher frequency in early childhood. Diet strongly affects the composition of [human waste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_waste "Human waste") and the rate of its evacuation.

### Biological variation

There is biological variation in the human species – with traits such as [blood type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_type "Blood type"), [genetic diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_diseases "Genetic diseases"), [cranial features](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_skull "Human skull"), [facial features](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_face "Human face"), [organ systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_systems "Organ systems"), [eye color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_color "Eye color"), [hair color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hair_color "Hair color") and [texture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hair_texture "Hair texture"), [height](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_height "Human height") and [build](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_shape "Body shape"), and [skin color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_skin_color "Human skin color") varying across the globe. The typical height of an adult human is between 1.4 and 1.9 m (4 ft 7 in and 6 ft 3 in), although this varies significantly depending on sex, [ethnic origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_origin "Ethnic origin"), and family bloodlines. Body size is partly determined by genes and is also significantly influenced by environmental factors such as [diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diet_(nutrition) "Diet (nutrition)"), exercise, and [sleep patterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleep_pattern "Sleep pattern").

There is evidence that populations have adapted genetically to various external factors. The genes that allow adult humans to [digest lactose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactose_tolerance "Lactose tolerance") are present in high frequencies in populations that have long histories of cattle domestication and are more dependent on [cow milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cow_milk "Cow milk"). [Sickle cell anemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sickle_cell_anemia "Sickle cell anemia"), which may provide increased resistance to [malaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaria "Malaria"), is frequent in populations where malaria is endemic. Populations that have for a very long time inhabited specific climates tend to have developed specific [phenotypes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenotype "Phenotype") that are beneficial for those environments – [short stature and stocky build in cold regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen%27s_rule "Allen's rule"), tall and lanky in hot regions, and with high lung capacities or other [adaptations at high altitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-altitude_adaptation_in_humans "High-altitude adaptation in humans"). Some populations have evolved highly unique adaptations to very specific environmental conditions, such as those advantageous to ocean-dwelling lifestyles and [freediving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freediving "Freediving") in the [Bajau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bajau "Bajau").

Human hair ranges in color from [red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_hair "Red hair") to [blond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blond "Blond") to [brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_hair "Brown hair") to [black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_hair "Black hair"), which is the most frequent. Hair color depends on the amount of [melanin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanin "Melanin"), with concentrations fading with increased age, leading to [grey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_hair "Grey hair") or even white hair. Skin color can range from [darkest brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_skin "Dark skin") to [lightest peach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_skin "Light skin"), or even nearly white or colorless in cases of [albinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albinism "Albinism"). It tends to vary [clinally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinal_variation "Clinal variation") and generally correlates with the level of [ultraviolet radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraviolet "Ultraviolet") in a particular geographic area, with darker skin mostly around the equator. Skin darkening may have evolved as protection against ultraviolet solar radiation. Light skin pigmentation protects against depletion of [vitamin D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_D "Vitamin D"), which requires [sunlight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunlight "Sunlight") to make. Human skin also has a capacity to darken (tan) in response to exposure to ultraviolet radiation.

There is relatively little variation between human geographical populations, and most of the variation that occurs is at the individual level. Much of human variation is continuous, often with no clear points of demarcation. Genetic data shows that no matter how population groups are defined, two people from the same population group are almost as different from each other as two people from any two different population groups. Dark-skinned populations that are found in Africa, Australia, and South Asia are not closely related to each other.

Genetic research has demonstrated that human populations native to the [African continent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_continent "African continent") are the most genetically diverse and genetic diversity decreases with migratory distance from Africa, possibly the result of [bottlenecks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_bottleneck "Evolutionary bottleneck") during human migration. These non-African populations acquired new genetic inputs from local [admixture with archaic populations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interbreeding_between_archaic_and_modern_humans "Interbreeding between archaic and modern humans") and have much greater variation from Neanderthals and [Denisovans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denisovans "Denisovans") than is found in Africa, though Neanderthal admixture into African populations may be underestimated. Furthermore, recent studies have found that populations in [sub-Saharan Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-Saharan_Africa "Sub-Saharan Africa"), and particularly [West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Africa "West Africa"), have ancestral genetic variation which predates modern humans and has been lost in most non-African populations. Some of this ancestry is thought to originate from admixture with an [unknown archaic hominin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interbreeding_between_archaic_and_modern_humans#Archaic_African_hominins "Interbreeding between archaic and modern humans") that diverged before the split of Neanderthals and modern humans.

Humans are a [gonochoric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonochorism "Gonochorism") species, meaning they are divided into male and female [sexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_sex "Biological sex"). The greatest degree of genetic [variation exists between males and females](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_differences_in_humans "Sex differences in humans"). While the [nucleotide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleotide_diversity "Nucleotide diversity") genetic variation of individuals of the same sex across global populations is no greater than 0.1%–0.5%, the genetic difference between [males](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man "Man") and [females](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woman "Woman") is between 1% and 2%. Males on average are 15% heavier and 15 cm (6 in) taller than females. On average, males have about 40–50% more upper-body strength and 20–30% more lower-body strength than females at the same weight, due to higher amounts of muscle and larger muscle fibers. Females generally have a higher [body fat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_fat "Body fat") percentage than males. Females have [lighter skin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_skin_color#Sexual_dimorphism "Human skin color") than males of the same population; this has been explained by a higher need for vitamin D in females during pregnancy and [lactation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactation "Lactation"). As there are chromosomal differences between females and males, some X- and Y-chromosome-related conditions and [disorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disease "Disease") only affect either males or females. After allowing for body weight and volume, the male voice is on average an [octave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octave "Octave") deeper than the female voice. Females have a [longer life span](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_differences_in_longevity "Sex differences in longevity") in almost every population around the world. There are [intersex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersex "Intersex") variations in the human population; estimates range from 0.018% to 1.7%.

## Psychology

The [human brain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_brain "Human brain") is the locus of "higher" order functioning such as [thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thought "Thought"), [reasoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reason "Reason"), and [abstraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstraction "Abstraction"). These [cognitive processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_function "Mental function") constitute the [mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind "Mind"), and, along with their [behavioral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavior "Behavior") consequences, are studied in the field of [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology "Psychology").
Humans have a larger and more developed [prefrontal cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefrontal_cortex "Prefrontal cortex") than other primates, the region of the brain associated with higher [cognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognition "Cognition"). This has led humans to proclaim themselves to be more intelligent than any other known species. Objectively defining intelligence is difficult, with other animals adapting senses and excelling in areas that humans are unable to.

There are some traits that, although not strictly unique, do set humans apart from other animals. Humans may be the only animals who have [episodic memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episodic_memory#In_animals "Episodic memory") and who can engage in "[mental time travel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_time_travel#Evolution_and_human_uniqueness "Mental time travel")". Even compared with other social animals, humans have an unusually high degree of flexibility in their facial expressions. Humans are the only animals known to cry emotional tears. Humans are one of the few animals able to self-recognize in [mirror tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_test "Mirror test"), and there is also debate over to what extent humans are the only animals with a [theory of mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_mind "Theory of mind").

### Sleep and dreaming

Humans are generally [diurnal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diurnality "Diurnality"). The average sleep requirement is between seven and nine hours per day for an adult and nine to ten hours per day for a child; elderly people usually sleep for six to seven hours. Having less sleep than this is common among humans, even though [sleep deprivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleep_deprivation "Sleep deprivation") can have negative health effects. A sustained restriction of adult sleep to four hours per day has been shown to correlate with changes in physiology and mental state, including reduced memory, fatigue, aggression, and bodily discomfort.

During sleep humans dream, where they experience sensory images and sounds. Dreaming is stimulated by the [pons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pons "Pons") and mostly occurs during the [rapid eye movement (REM) phase of sleep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/REM_phase_of_sleep "REM phase of sleep"). The length of a dream can vary, from a few seconds up to 30 minutes. Humans have three to five dreams per night, and some may have up to seven. Dreamers are more likely to remember the dream if awakened during the REM phase. The events in dreams are generally outside the control of the dreamer, with the exception of [lucid dreaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucid_dream "Lucid dream"), where the dreamer is [self-aware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-aware "Self-aware"). Dreams can at times make a [creative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creativity "Creativity") thought occur or give a sense of [inspiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artistic_inspiration "Artistic inspiration"), [as is the case with](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_works_based_on_dreams "List of works based on dreams") some notable works of fiction, scientific concepts, and art.

### Consciousness and thought

Human consciousness, at its simplest, is [sentience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentience "Sentience") or [awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awareness "Awareness") of internal or external existence. Despite centuries of analyses, definitions, explanations and debates by philosophers and scientists, the underlying nature of consciousness remains enigmatic and poorly understood, being "at once the most familiar and most mysterious aspect of our lives". The only widely agreed notion about the topic is the intuition that it exists. Opinions differ about what exactly needs to be studied and explained as consciousness. Some philosophers divide consciousness into phenomenal consciousness, which is sensory experience itself, and access consciousness, which can be used for reasoning or directly controlling actions. It is sometimes synonymous with 'the mind', and at other times, an aspect of it. Historically it is associated with [introspection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introspection "Introspection"), private [thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thought "Thought"), [imagination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imagination "Imagination"), and [volition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volition_(psychology) "Volition (psychology)"). It now often includes some kind of [experience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experience "Experience"), [cognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognition "Cognition"), [feeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feeling "Feeling") or [perception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perception "Perception"). It may be 'awareness', or '[awareness of awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta-cognition "Meta-cognition")', or [self-awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-awareness "Self-awareness"). There might be different levels or [orders of consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher-order_theories_of_consciousness "Higher-order theories of consciousness"), or different kinds of consciousness, or just one kind with different features.

The process of acquiring knowledge and understanding through thought, experience, and the senses is known as cognition. The human brain [perceives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perception "Perception") the external world through the [senses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sense "Sense"), and each individual human is influenced greatly by their experiences, leading to [subjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subjectivity "Subjectivity") views of [existence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existence "Existence") and the passage of time. The nature of thought is central to psychology and related fields. [Cognitive psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_psychology "Cognitive psychology") studies [cognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognition "Cognition"), the [mental processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_function "Mental function") underlying behavior. Largely focusing on the development of the human mind through the life span, [developmental psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developmental_psychology "Developmental psychology") seeks to understand how people come to perceive, understand, and act within the world and how these processes change as they age. This may focus on intellectual, cognitive, neural, social, or [moral development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_development "Moral development"). [Psychologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychologists "Psychologists") have developed intelligence tests and the concept of [intelligence quotient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_quotient "Intelligence quotient") in order to assess the relative intelligence of human beings and study its [distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_(mathematics) "Distribution (mathematics)") among the population.

### Motivation and emotion

Human motivation is not yet wholly understood. From a psychological perspective, [Maslow's hierarchy of needs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maslow%27s_hierarchy_of_needs "Maslow's hierarchy of needs") is a well-established theory that can be defined as the process of satisfying certain needs in ascending order of complexity. From a more general, philosophical perspective, human motivation can be defined as a commitment to, or withdrawal from, various goals requiring the application of human ability. Furthermore, [incentive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incentive "Incentive") and [preference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preference "Preference") are both factors, as are any perceived links between incentives and preferences. [Volition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volition_(psychology) "Volition (psychology)") may also be involved, in which case willpower is also a factor. Ideally, both motivation and volition ensure the selection, striving for, and [realization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realisation_(metrology) "Realisation (metrology)") of goals in an optimal manner, a [function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_(biology) "Function (biology)") beginning in childhood and continuing throughout a lifetime in a process known as [socialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialization "Socialization").

Emotions are [biological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological "Biological") states associated with the nervous system brought on by [neurophysiological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurophysiology "Neurophysiology") changes variously associated with thoughts, feelings, behavioral responses, and a degree of [pleasure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleasure "Pleasure") or [displeasure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffering "Suffering"). They are often [intertwined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reciprocal_influence "Reciprocal influence") with [mood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mood_(psychology) "Mood (psychology)"), [temperament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperament "Temperament"), [personality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personality_psychology "Personality psychology"), [disposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disposition "Disposition"), [creativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creativity "Creativity"), and motivation. Emotion has a significant influence on human behavior and their ability to learn. Acting on extreme or uncontrolled emotions can lead to social disorder and crime, with studies showing criminals may have lower [emotional intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emotional_intelligence "Emotional intelligence") than normal.

Emotional experiences perceived as [pleasant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleasure "Pleasure"), such as [joy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joy "Joy"), [interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_(emotion) "Interest (emotion)"), or [contentment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contentment "Contentment"), contrast with those perceived as [unpleasant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffering "Suffering"), like [anxiety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anxiety "Anxiety"), [sadness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadness "Sadness"), [anger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anger "Anger"), and [despair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_(mood) "Depression (mood)"). [Happiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happiness "Happiness"), or the state of being happy, is a human emotional condition. The definition of happiness is a common philosophical topic. Some define it as experiencing the [feeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feeling "Feeling") of positive [emotional affects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affect_(psychology) "Affect (psychology)"), while avoiding the negative ones. Others see it as an appraisal of [life satisfaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_satisfaction "Life satisfaction") or [quality of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_of_life "Quality of life"). Recent research suggests that being happy might involve experiencing some negative emotions when humans feel they are warranted.

### Sexuality and love

For humans, sexuality involves [biological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological "Biological"), [erotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erotic "Erotic"), [physical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_intimacy "Physical intimacy"), [emotional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emotional_intimacy "Emotional intimacy"), [social](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social "Social"), or [spiritual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirituality "Spirituality") feelings and behaviors. Because it is a broad term that has varied with historical contexts over time, it lacks a precise definition. The biological and physical aspects of sexuality largely concern the [human reproductive functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_reproduction "Human reproduction"), including the [human sexual response cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_sexual_response_cycle "Human sexual response cycle"). Sexuality also affects and is affected by cultural, political, legal, philosophical, [moral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morality "Morality"), [ethical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethical "Ethical"), and religious aspects of life. Sexual desire ([libido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libido "Libido")) is a basic mental state present at the beginning of sexual behavior. Studies have found that men report stronger sexual desire than women and [masturbate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masturbation "Masturbation") more frequently.

Humans can fall anywhere along a continuous scale of [sexual orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_orientation "Sexual orientation"), although most humans are [heterosexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterosexual "Heterosexual"). While [homosexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosexuality "Homosexuality") behavior [occurs in some other animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosexual_behavior_in_animals "Homosexual behavior in animals"), only humans and [domestic sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep "Sheep") have so far been found to exhibit exclusive preference for same-sex relationships. Most evidence supports nonsocial, [biological causes of sexual orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology_and_sexual_orientation "Biology and sexual orientation"), as cultures that are very tolerant of homosexuality do not have significantly higher rates of it. Research in [neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroscience "Neuroscience") and [genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics "Genetics") suggests that other aspects of human sexuality are biologically influenced as well.

Love most commonly refers to a feeling of strong attraction or emotional [attachment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attachment_(psychology) "Attachment (psychology)"). It can be impersonal (the love of an object, ideal, or strong political or spiritual connection) or interpersonal (love between humans). In the emotional state of [romantic love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romantic_love "Romantic love"), neurochemicals such as [dopamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopamine "Dopamine"), [norepinephrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norepinephrine "Norepinephrine"), [serotonin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serotonin "Serotonin"), and [oxytocin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxytocin "Oxytocin") stimulate the brain's [reward system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reward_system "Reward system") through the [mesocorticolimbic pathway (MCL)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopaminergic_pathways "Dopaminergic pathways"). This [dopaminergic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopaminergic "Dopaminergic") pathway may result in side effects such as [tachycardia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tachycardia "Tachycardia") (increased [heart rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_rate "Heart rate")), loss of appetite, [insomnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insomnia "Insomnia") ([sleeplessness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleep_deprivation "Sleep deprivation")), and [intense euphoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphoria "Euphoria").

## Culture

Humanity's large set of intellectual skills was a key factor in the species' eventual technological advancement and concomitant domination of the biosphere. Disregarding extinct hominids, humans are the only animals known to teach generalizable information, innately deploy recursive [embedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dependent_clause "Dependent clause") to generate and communicate complex concepts, engage in the "[folk physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_physics "Folk physics")" required for competent tool design, or cook food in the wild. The ability to preserve and teach information to future generations in one or more societies is known as the [ratchet effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratchet_effect "Ratchet effect") in [anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology "Anthropology"). According to the metaphor, once a group of people understands a technology, it is hard for them to unlearn it, and it may be used to learn technologies of further complexity. Teaching and learning preserves the cultural and ethnographic identity of human societies. Other traits and behaviors that are mostly unique to humans include starting fires, [phoneme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoneme "Phoneme") structuring and [vocal learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocal_learning "Vocal learning").

### Language

While many species [communicate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_communication "Animal communication"), [language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language "Language") is unique to humans,
a defining feature of humanity,
and a [cultural universal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_universal "Cultural universal"). Unlike the limited communication systems of other animals, human language is open – an infinite number of meanings can be produced by combining a limited number of symbols. Human language also has the capacity of [displacement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displacement_(linguistics) "Displacement (linguistics)"), using words to represent things and happenings that are not presently or locally occurring but reside in the shared imagination of interlocutors.

Language differs from other forms of communication in that it is [modality independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_of_speech#Modality-independence "Origin of speech"); people can convey the same meanings through different media: audibly in [speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech "Speech"), visually by [sign language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sign_language "Sign language") or writing, and through tactile media such as [braille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braille "Braille"). Language is central to communication between humans, and to the sense of [identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_(social_science) "Identity (social science)") that can unite nations, cultures, and ethnic groups.

As of 1996[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Human&action=edit), humans used approximately six thousand different languages, including sign languages, and many thousands more are [extinct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinct_language "Extinct language").

### The arts

Human arts can take many forms, including [visual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_arts "Visual arts"), [literary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_arts "Literary arts"), and [performing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performing_arts "Performing arts"). Visual art can range from [paintings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painting "Painting") and [sculptures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sculpture "Sculpture") to [film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film "Film"), [fashion design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_design "Fashion design"), and [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture "Architecture"). Literary arts can include [prose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prose "Prose"), [poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poetry "Poetry"), and [dramas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama "Drama"). The performing arts generally involve [theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater "Theater"), [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music "Music"), and [dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance "Dance"). Humans often combine the different forms (for example, music videos). Other entities that have been described as having artistic qualities include [food preparation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culinary_arts "Culinary arts"), [video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game "Video game"), and [medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine "Medicine"). As well as providing entertainment and transferring knowledge, the arts are also used for [political purposes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_arts_and_politics "The arts and politics").

[Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art "Art") is a defining characteristic of humans, and there is evidence for a relationship between creativity and language. The earliest evidence of art was shell engravings made by *Homo erectus* 300,000 years before modern humans evolved. Art attributed to *H. sapiens* existed at least 75,000 years ago, with jewelry and drawings found in caves in South Africa. There are various hypotheses as to why humans have [adapted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptation "Adaptation") to the arts. These include allowing them to better problem-solve issues, providing a means to control or influence other humans, encouraging cooperation and contribution within a society, or increasing the chance of attracting a potential mate. The use of imagination developed through art, combined with logic, may have given early humans an evolutionary advantage.

Evidence of humans engaging in musical activities predates cave art, and so far music has been [practiced by virtually all known human cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_universal "Cultural universal"). There exists a wide variety of [music genres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_genre "Music genre") and [ethnic musics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_music "Ethnic music"), with humans' musical abilities being related to other abilities, including complex social human behaviors. It has been shown that human brains respond to music by becoming synchronized with the rhythm and beat, a process called [entrainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entrainment_(biomusicology) "Entrainment (biomusicology)"). Dance is also a form of human expression found in all cultures and may have evolved as a way to help early humans communicate. Listening to music and observing dance stimulates the [orbitofrontal cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbitofrontal_cortex "Orbitofrontal cortex") and other pleasure-sensing areas of the brain.

Unlike speaking, reading and writing do not come naturally to humans and must be taught. Still, [literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature "Literature") has been present before the invention of words and language, with 30,000-year-old paintings on walls inside some caves portraying a series of dramatic scenes. One of the oldest surviving works of literature is the *[Epic of Gilgamesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_of_Gilgamesh "Epic of Gilgamesh")*, first engraved on ancient [Babylonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonia "Babylonia") tablets about 4,000 years ago. Beyond simply passing down knowledge, the use and sharing of imaginative [fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiction "Fiction") through stories might have helped develop humans' capabilities for communication and increased the likelihood of securing a mate. Storytelling may also be used as a way to provide the audience with moral lessons and encourage cooperation.

### Tools and technologies

Stone tools were used by [proto-humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-humans "Proto-humans") at least 2.5 million years ago. The use and manufacture of tools has been put forward as the ability that defines humans more than anything else and has historically been seen as an important evolutionary step. The technology became much more sophisticated about 1.8 million years ago, with the [controlled use of fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_of_fire_by_early_humans "Control of fire by early humans") beginning around 1 million years ago. The wheel and wheeled vehicles appeared simultaneously in several regions sometime in the fourth millennium BC. The development of more complex tools and technologies allowed land to be [cultivated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_land "Arable land") and animals to be [domesticated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestication "Domestication"), thus proving essential in the development of [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture "Agriculture") – what is known as the [Neolithic Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_Revolution "Neolithic Revolution").

[China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") developed [paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper "Paper"), the [printing press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing_press "Printing press"), [gunpowder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunpowder "Gunpowder"), the [compass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass "Compass") and [other important inventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_inventions "List of Chinese inventions"). The continued improvements in [smelting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smelting "Smelting") allowed the [forging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forging "Forging") of copper, bronze, iron, and eventually [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel "Steel")—which is used in [railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railways "Railways"), [skyscrapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyscraper "Skyscraper"), and many other products. This coincided with the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution "Industrial Revolution"), where the invention of automated machines brought major changes to humans' lifestyles. Modern technology is observed as [progressing exponentially](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accelerating_change "Accelerating change"), with major innovations in the 20th century including: [electricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_generation "Electricity generation"), [penicillin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penicillin "Penicillin"), [semiconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor "Semiconductor"), [internal combustion engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_combustion_engine "Internal combustion engine"), the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet "Internet"), [nitrogen-fixing fertilizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertilizer "Fertilizer"), [airplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airplane "Airplane"), [computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer "Computer"), [automobiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car "Car"), [contraceptive pills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_oral_contraceptive_pill "Combined oral contraceptive pill"), [nuclear fission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fission "Nuclear fission"), the [green revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Revolution "Green Revolution"), [radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio "Radio"), scientific [plant breeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_breeding "Plant breeding"), [rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket "Rocket"), [air conditioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_conditioning "Air conditioning"), [television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television "Television") and the [assembly line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_line "Assembly line").

### Religion and spirituality

[Definitions of religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Definition_of_religion "Definition of religion") vary; according to one definition, a religion is a [belief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belief "Belief") system concerning the [supernatural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supernatural "Supernatural"), [sacred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred "Sacred") or [divine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divinity "Divinity"), and practices, [values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Values "Values"), institutions and [rituals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritual "Ritual") associated with such belief. Some religions also have a [moral code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_code "Moral code"). The [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_psychology_of_religion "Evolutionary psychology of religion") and the history of the [first religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_origin_of_religions "Evolutionary origin of religions") have become areas of active scientific investigation. Credible evidence of religious behavior dates to the [Middle Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Paleolithic "Middle Paleolithic") era (45–200 [thousand years ago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tya_(unit) "Tya (unit)")). It may have evolved to play a role in helping enforce and encourage cooperation between humans.

Religion manifests in diverse forms. Religion can include a belief in [life after death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_after_death "Life after death"), the [origin of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_of_life "Origin of life"), the nature of the [universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe "Universe") ([religious cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_cosmology "Religious cosmology")) and its [ultimate fate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_fate "Ultimate fate") ([eschatology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eschatology "Eschatology")), and [moral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morality "Morality") or [ethical teachings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_ethics "Religious ethics"). Views on [transcendence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendence_(religion) "Transcendence (religion)") and [immanence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanence "Immanence") vary substantially; traditions variously espouse [monism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monism "Monism"), [deism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deism "Deism"), [pantheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantheism "Pantheism"), and [theism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theism "Theism") (including [polytheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytheism "Polytheism") and [monotheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotheism "Monotheism")).

Although measuring religiosity is difficult, a majority of humans profess some variety of religious or spiritual belief. In 2020, the plurality was [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christians "Christians"), followed by [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims "Muslims"), [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus "Hindus"), and [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"). As of 2020, about 24%, or 1.9 billion humans, were [irreligious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligious "Irreligious"), including those with no religious beliefs or no identity with any religion.

### Science and philosophy

A defining attribute of humanity is the capacity for [cumulative cultural evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_evolution "Cultural evolution") by [transmitting knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_transfer "Knowledge transfer") across generations by continuously building on past knowledge to develop tools, [scientific laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_law "Scientific law"), and other advancements to pass on to future generations. This accumulated knowledge can be tested to answer various questions or make predictions about how the universe functions, driving ongoing human advancement. This phenomenon is known as the ratchet effect in anthropology.

[Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle "Aristotle") has been described as the first scientist, and preceded the rise of scientific thought through the [Hellenistic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period "Hellenistic period"). Other early advances in science came from the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_technology_of_the_Han_dynasty "Science and technology of the Han dynasty") in China and during the [Islamic Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Golden_Age "Islamic Golden Age"). The [Scientific Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_Revolution "Scientific Revolution"), near the end of the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance "Renaissance"), led to the emergence of [modern science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_science "Modern science").

A chain of events and influences led to the development of the [scientific method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_method "Scientific method"), a process of observation and experimentation that is used to differentiate science from [pseudoscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoscience "Pseudoscience"). An understanding of [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics") is unique to humans, although other species of animals have some [numerical cognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_cognition "Numerical cognition"). All of science can be divided into three major branches, the [formal sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_sciences "Formal sciences") (e.g., [logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic "Logic") and mathematics), which are concerned with [formal systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_systems "Formal systems"), the [applied sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_sciences "Applied sciences") (e.g., engineering, medicine), which are focused on practical applications, and the empirical sciences, which are based on [empirical observation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empirical_observation "Empirical observation") and are in turn divided into [natural sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_sciences "Natural sciences") (e.g., [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics"), [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry"), [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology "Biology")) and [social sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_sciences "Social sciences") (e.g., [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology "Psychology"), economics, sociology).

Philosophy is a field of study where humans seek to understand fundamental truths about themselves and the world in which they live. Philosophical inquiry has been a major feature in the development of humans' intellectual history. It has been described as the "no man's land" between definitive scientific knowledge and dogmatic religious teachings. Major fields of philosophy include [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics "Metaphysics"), [epistemology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology "Epistemology"), [logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic_(philosophy) "Logic (philosophy)"), and [axiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiology "Axiology") (which includes [ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics "Ethics") and [aesthetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aesthetics "Aesthetics")).

## Society

Society is the system of [organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization "Organization") and [institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institution "Institution") arising from interaction between humans. Humans are highly social and tend to live in large complex social groups. They can be divided into different groups according to their [income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income "Income"), [wealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth "Wealth"), [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_(social_and_political) "Power (social and political)"), [reputation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reputation "Reputation"), and other factors. The structure of [social stratification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_stratification "Social stratification") and the degree of [social mobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_mobility "Social mobility") differ, especially between modern and traditional societies. Human groups range from the size of [families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family "Family") to [nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation "Nation"). The first form of human social organization is thought to have resembled [hunter-gatherer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer "Hunter-gatherer") [band societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_society "Band society").

### Gender

Human societies typically exhibit [gender identities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_identity "Gender identity") and [gender roles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_role "Gender role") that distinguish between [masculine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masculinity "Masculinity") and [feminine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Femininity "Femininity") characteristics and prescribe the range of acceptable behaviors and attitudes for their members based on their sex. The most common categorization is a [gender binary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_binary "Gender binary") of [men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men "Men") and [women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women "Women"). Some societies recognize a [third gender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_gender "Third gender"), or less commonly a fourth or fifth. In some other societies, [non-binary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-binary_gender "Non-binary gender") is used as an umbrella term for a range of gender identities that are not solely male or female.

Gender roles are often associated with a division of [norms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_norm "Social norm"), [practices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Practice_(social_theory) "Practice (social theory)"), [dress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothing "Clothing"), [behavior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_behavior "Social behavior"), [rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rights "Rights"), [duties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duty "Duty"), [privileges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privilege_(social_inequality) "Privilege (social inequality)"), [status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_status "Social status"), and [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_(social_and_political) "Power (social and political)"), with men enjoying more rights and privileges than women in most societies, both today and in the past. As a [social construct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_constructionism "Social constructionism"), gender roles are not fixed and vary historically within a society. Challenges to predominant gender norms have recurred in many societies. Little is known about gender roles in the earliest human societies. [Early modern humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_human "Early modern human") probably had a range of gender roles similar to that of modern cultures from at least the [Upper Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Paleolithic "Upper Paleolithic"), while the Neanderthals were less sexually dimorphic and there is evidence that the behavioral difference between males and females was minimal.

### Kinship

All human societies organize, recognize, and classify types of social relationships based on relations between parents, children, and other descendants ([consanguinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consanguinity "Consanguinity")), and relations through [marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage "Marriage") ([affinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affinity_(law) "Affinity (law)")). There is also a third type applied to [godparents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godparent "Godparent") or [adoptive children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adoption "Adoption") ([fictive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fictive_kinship "Fictive kinship")). These culturally defined relationships are referred to as kinship. In many societies, it is one of the most important social organizing principles and plays a role in transmitting status and [inheritance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inheritance "Inheritance"). All societies have rules of [incest taboo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incest_taboo "Incest taboo"), according to which marriage between certain kinds of kin relations is prohibited, and some also have rules of preferential marriage with certain kin relations.

Pair bonding is a ubiquitous feature of human sexual relationships, whether it is manifested as serial monogamy, [polygyny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygyny "Polygyny"), or [polyandry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyandry "Polyandry"). Genetic evidence indicates that humans were predominantly polygynous for most of their existence as a species, but that this began to shift during the Neolithic, when [monogamy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monogamy "Monogamy") started becoming widespread concomitantly with the transition from nomadic to sedentary societies. Anatomical evidence in the form of second-to-fourth digit ratios, a biomarker for prenatal androgen effects, likewise indicates modern humans were polygynous during the Pleistocene.

### Ethnicity

Human ethnic groups are a social category that [identifies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_(social_science) "Identity (social science)") people together based on shared attributes that distinguish them from other groups. These can be a common set of traditions, [ancestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestry "Ancestry"), [language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language "Language"), [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History "History"), [society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society "Society"), [culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture "Culture"), [nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation "Nation"), [religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion "Religion"), or social treatment within their residing area. Ethnicity is separate from the concept of [race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_(human_categorization) "Race (human categorization)"), which is based on physical characteristics, although both are [socially constructed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_constructionism "Social constructionism"). Assigning ethnicity to a certain population is complicated, as even within common ethnic designations there can be a diverse range of subgroups, and the makeup of these ethnic groups can change over time at both the collective and individual level. Also, there is no generally accepted definition of what constitutes an ethnic group. Ethnic groupings can play a powerful role in the [social identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_identity "Social identity") and solidarity of ethnopolitical units. This has been closely tied to the rise of the [nation state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation_state "Nation state") as the predominant form of political organization in the 19th and 20th centuries.

### Government and politics

As farming populations gathered in larger and denser communities, interactions between these different groups increased. This led to the development of governance within and between the communities. Humans have evolved the ability to change affiliation with various social groups relatively easily, including previously strong political alliances, if doing so is seen as providing personal advantages. This [cognitive flexibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_flexibility "Cognitive flexibility") allows individual humans to change their political ideologies, with those with higher flexibility less likely to support authoritarian and nationalistic stances.

Governments create [laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law "Law") and [policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Policies "Policies") that affect the citizens they govern. There have been [many forms of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_forms_of_government "List of forms of government") throughout human history, each having various means of obtaining power and the ability to exert diverse controls on the population. As of 2021[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Human&action=edit), approximately 47% of humans lived in some form of a [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy "Democracy"), 17% in a [hybrid regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_regime "Hybrid regime"), and 37% in an [authoritarian regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarian_regime "Authoritarian regime"). Many countries belong to [international organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_organization "International organization") and [alliances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliances "Alliances"); the largest of these is the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations"), with [193 member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_states_of_the_United_Nations "Member states of the United Nations").

### Trade and economics

Trade, the voluntary exchange of goods and services, is seen as a characteristic that differentiates humans from other animals and has been cited as a practice that gave *Homo sapiens* a major advantage over other hominids. Evidence suggests early *H. sapiens* made use of long-distance trade routes to exchange goods and ideas, leading to [cultural explosions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_explosion "Cultural explosion") and providing additional food sources when hunting was sparse, while such trade networks did not exist for the now extinct Neanderthals. Early trade likely involved materials for creating tools like [obsidian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obsidian "Obsidian"). The first truly international trade routes were around the [spice trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice_trade "Spice trade") through the Roman and medieval periods.

Early human [economies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy "Economy") were more likely to be based around [gift-giving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gift_economy "Gift economy") instead of a [bartering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barter "Barter") system. Early [money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money "Money") consisted of [commodities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodity_money "Commodity money"); the oldest being in the form of cattle and the most widely used being [cowrie shells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowrie_shells "Cowrie shells"). Money has since evolved into government-issued [coins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coins "Coins"), [paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper_money "Paper money"), and [electronic money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_money "Electronic money"). Economics is a [social science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_science "Social science") that studies how societies distribute scarce resources among different people. There are massive [inequalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_inequality "Economic inequality") in the division of [wealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth "Wealth") among humans; the eight richest humans are worth the same monetary value as the poorest half of all the human population.

### Conflict

Humans commit violence on other humans at a rate comparable to other primates, but have an increased preference for killing adults, [infanticide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infanticide_(zoology) "Infanticide (zoology)") being more common among other primates. Phylogenetic analysis predicts that 2% of early *H. sapiens* were [murdered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder "Murder"), rising to 12% during the medieval period, before dropping to below 2% in modern times. There is great variation in violence between human populations, with rates of homicide about 0.01% in societies that have [legal systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_legal_systems "List of national legal systems") and strong cultural attitudes against violence. Other factors influencing homicide rates are relative resource discrepancies and the cost-benefit analysis of murder versus other problem-solving strategies.

The willingness of humans to kill other members of their species en masse through organized conflict (i.e., [war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War "War")) has long been the subject of debate. One school of thought holds that war evolved as a means to eliminate competitors, and has always been an innate human characteristic. Another suggests that war is a relatively recent phenomenon and has appeared due to changing social conditions. While not settled, current evidence indicates that interpersonal violence has been a ubiquitous part of human history since the beginnings of the species' existence, with the [bioarchaeological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioarchaeology "Bioarchaeology") record showing significant incidence of violent trauma believed to be a result of war in hunter-gatherer populations long before the emergence of agriculture and settled societies.

War has had a high cost on human life; it is estimated that during the 20th century, between 167 million and 188 million people died as a result of war. War casualty data is less reliable for pre-medieval times, especially global figures. But compared with any period over the past 600 years, the past 80 years (post-1945) have seen a very significant drop in global military and civilian death rates due to armed conflict.
