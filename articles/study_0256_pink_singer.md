# Pink (singer)

**Alecia Beth Moore-Hart** (born September 8, 1979), known professionally as **Pink** (stylized as **P!nk** or **P!NK**, in [all caps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_caps "All caps")), is an American singer and songwriter. She is known for her acrobatic stage presence.

At age 15, Pink formed the short-lived girl group Choice, which signed with [LaFace Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaFace_Records "LaFace Records") in 1995, although they disbanded without major releases. Her debut studio album, *[Can't Take Me Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Can%27t_Take_Me_Home "Can't Take Me Home")* (2000), received double platinum certification by the [Recording Industry Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America "Recording Industry Association of America") (RIAA). Produced by label boss [Babyface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babyface_(musician) "Babyface (musician)") and influenced by [contemporary R&B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_R%26B "Contemporary R&B"), the album spawned US [*Billboard* Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100 "Billboard Hot 100") Top 10 singles: "[There You Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/There_You_Go "There You Go")" and "[Most Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most_Girls_(Pink_song) "Most Girls (Pink song)")". Pink gained further recognition for her 2001 collaborative single "[Lady Marmalade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Marmalade#Moulin_Rouge!_version "Lady Marmalade")" from the [soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moulin_Rouge!_Music_from_Baz_Luhrmann%27s_Film "Moulin Rouge! Music from Baz Luhrmann's Film") of *[Moulin Rouge!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moulin_Rouge! "Moulin Rouge!")*, which peaked atop 13 international charts, including the US, and earned her first [Grammy Award for Best Pop Collaboration with Vocals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Pop_Collaboration_with_Vocals "Grammy Award for Best Pop Collaboration with Vocals"). Pink shifted to [pop rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_rock "Pop rock") with her second album, *[Missundaztood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missundaztood "Missundaztood")* (2001), which sold over 13 million copies worldwide and yielded three Top 10 singles: "[Get the Party Started](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Get_the_Party_Started "Get the Party Started")", "[Don't Let Me Get Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don%27t_Let_Me_Get_Me "Don't Let Me Get Me")", and "[Just Like a Pill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_Like_a_Pill "Just Like a Pill")".

While Pink's third album *[Try This](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Try_This "Try This")* (2003) sold significantly less than her second album, it earned a [Grammy Award for Best Female Rock Vocal Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Female_Rock_Vocal_Performance "Grammy Award for Best Female Rock Vocal Performance"). Her fourth and fifth studio albums, *[I'm Not Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%27m_Not_Dead "I'm Not Dead")* (2006) and *[Funhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funhouse_(Pink_album) "Funhouse (Pink album)")* (2008), saw a commercial rebound and spawned the Top 10 singles "[Who Knew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who_Knew "Who Knew")" and "[U + Ur Hand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_%2B_Ur_Hand "U + Ur Hand")", as well as the US No. 1 single "[So What](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/So_What_(Pink_song) "So What (Pink song)")". She scored her third and fourth US No. 1 hits with "[Raise Your Glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raise_Your_Glass "Raise Your Glass")" as well as "[Just Give Me a Reason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_Give_Me_a_Reason "Just Give Me a Reason")" from her sixth album *[The Truth About Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Truth_About_Love "The Truth About Love")* (2012), which became her first to top the US [*Billboard* 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200"). In 2014, Pink formed the collaborative [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") duo [You+Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You%2BMe "You+Me") with [Dallas Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_and_Colour "City and Colour") and released the album *[Rose Ave.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Ave. "Rose Ave.")*. Her following albums, *[Beautiful Trauma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beautiful_Trauma "Beautiful Trauma")* (2017) and *[Hurts 2B Human](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurts_2B_Human "Hurts 2B Human")* (2019), saw success and debuted at No. 1 on the *Billboard* 200 chart, with the former becoming the [third best-selling album of that year worldwide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_best-selling_albums#Best-selling_album_by_year_worldwide "List of best-selling albums"). Her ninth and latest studio album, *[Trustfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trustfall "Trustfall")* (2023), peaked at No. 2 on the chart.

Pink has sold more than 135 million records worldwide, making her one of the [world's best-selling music artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_best-selling_music_artists "List of best-selling music artists"). Pink is the most-played female solo artist in the UK during the 21st century, while [*Billboard*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_(magazine) "Billboard (magazine)") named Pink the [Pop Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_Airplay "Pop Airplay") [Artist of the 2000s Decade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Decade-End#2000s "Billboard Decade-End"). [Her accolades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Pink "List of awards and nominations received by Pink") include three [Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Awards "Grammy Awards"), two [Brit Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brit_Awards "Brit Awards"), a [Daytime Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daytime_Emmy_Award "Daytime Emmy Award"), seven [MTV Video Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Awards "MTV Video Music Awards"), and two [MTV Europe Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Europe_Music_Awards "MTV Europe Music Awards"). At the 63rd [BMI Pop Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMI_Award "BMI Award"), she received the BMI President's Award for "her outstanding achievement in songwriting and global impact on pop culture and the entertainment industry", and was honored with the [People's Champion Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Choice_Awards "People's Choice Awards"), the [iHeartRadio Music Award Icon Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHeartRadio_Music_Awards "IHeartRadio Music Awards"). *Billboard* named Pink the 2013 Woman of the Year at the [*Billboard* Women in Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Women_in_Music "Billboard Women in Music") and honored her with the [*Billboard* Icon Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Icon_Award "Billboard Icon Award") and the [*Billboard* Legend of Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Live_Music_Awards "Billboard Live Music Awards"). [VH1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VH1 "VH1") ranked her 10th on its list of the [100 Greatest Women in Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Greatest_(VH1_TV_series) "The Greatest (VH1 TV series)"). In 2026, Pink was nominated for the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame "Rock and Roll Hall of Fame").

## Early life and family

Alecia Beth Moore was born on September 8, 1979, in [Doylestown, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doylestown,_Pennsylvania "Doylestown, Pennsylvania"), to emergency room nurse Judith Moore (née Kugel) and insurance salesman James Moore. She has described herself as an "[Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Americans "Irish Americans")-[German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans "German Americans")-[Lithuanian Jew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Jew "Lithuanian Jew")", and her mother is [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Jews "American Jews"). Although a healthy baby, she developed asthma that plagued her through her early years. When Pink was a toddler, her parents began having marital problems; they divorced before she was 10.

Pink trained as a competitive gymnast between the ages 4 and 12. She attended [Central Bucks High School West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bucks_High_School_West "Central Bucks High School West"). In high school, Pink joined her first band, Middleground, but it disbanded upon losing a [Battle of the Bands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Bands "Battle of the Bands") competition. As a teenager, she wrote lyrics as an outlet for her feelings, and her mother commented, "Her initial writings were always very introspective. Some of it was very black, and very deep, almost worrisome."

Pink began performing in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia") clubs when she was about 14 years old. She adopted her nickname "Pink" as her stage name around this time. She has given different explanations about how she came to be given that nickname, which she has had since she was a child. At 14, she was convinced to audition to become a member of the all-female group **Basic Instinct**, and earned a spot in the lineup. Ultimately, the group disbanded without releasing any material.

## Career

### 1995–1998: Career beginnings

At 15, Pink and two other teenage girls, Sharon Flanagan[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] and Chrissy Conway, formed the R&B group Choice. A copy of their first song, "Key to My Heart", was sent to [LaFace Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaFace_Records "LaFace Records") in [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta "Atlanta"), Georgia, where [L.A. Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L.A._Reid "L.A. Reid") overheard it and arranged for the group to fly there so he could see them perform. Afterward, he signed them to a recording contract with the label. Since the three girls were under 18 at the time, their parents had to cosign the contract. The group relocated to the label's then-headquarters in [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta "Atlanta") to record an album. Despite it failing to see a commercial release, their song "Key to My Heart" appeared on the [soundtrack to the 1996 film *Kazaam*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazaam_(soundtrack) "Kazaam (soundtrack)"). During a Christmas party, Reid gave Pink an ultimatum: "go solo or go home." Choice subsequently disbanded in 1998.

### 1999–2002: *Can't Take Me Home* and *Missundaztood*

After Choice disbanded, Pink signed a recording contract with LaFace Records and began working on her first solo album with producers such as [Babyface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babyface_(musician) "Babyface (musician)"), [Kandi Burruss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kandi_Burruss "Kandi Burruss") and [Tricky Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricky_Stewart "Tricky Stewart"). Her first solo single, "[There You Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/There_You_Go "There You Go")", was released in February 2000 and became her first Top 10 hit on the [*Billboard* Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100 "Billboard Hot 100") chart, where it peaked at No. 7. Internationally, the song also charted inside the Top 10 in Australia, New Zealand, and the United Kingdom. In April, Pink's album, *[Can't Take Me Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Can%27t_Take_Me_Home "Can't Take Me Home")*, was released to commercial success. It peaked at No. 26 on the [*Billboard* 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200") chart and was certified double platinum by the [Recording Industry Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America "Recording Industry Association of America") (RIAA) for two million units shipped in the U.S. It also went platinum in the United Kingdom and multi-platinum in Australia and Canada, while selling more than four million copies worldwide. Critical reception to the album was mixed. The album's second single, "[Most Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most_Girls_(Pink_song) "Most Girls (Pink song)")", peaked at No. 4 on the *Billboard* Hot 100 and became her first chart-topping single in Australia. "[You Make Me Sick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_Make_Me_Sick "You Make Me Sick")" was released as the final single and reached No. 33 on the Hot 100.

Pink won the trophy for Female New Artist of the Year at the 2000 [*Billboard* Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Music_Award "Billboard Music Award"). She was billed as a supporting act on the North American leg of [NSYNC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSYNC "NSYNC")'s [No Strings Attached Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Strings_Attached_Tour "No Strings Attached Tour") throughout the summer of 2000. In 2001, Pink, alongside singers [Christina Aguilera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christina_Aguilera "Christina Aguilera") and [Mýa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%BDa "Mýa") as well as rapper [Lil' Kim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lil%27_Kim "Lil' Kim"), performed a cover of "[Lady Marmalade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Marmalade "Lady Marmalade")" for the soundtrack of the film *[Moulin Rouge!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moulin_Rouge! "Moulin Rouge!")*. In the US it became the most successful airplay-only single in history, as well as Pink's first No. 1 single. The success of the single was helped by its music video, which was popular on music channels and won the [MTV Video Music Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Award "MTV Video Music Award") for [Video of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Award_for_Best_Video_of_the_Year "MTV Video Music Award for Best Video of the Year"). The song won Pink's first [Grammy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award "Grammy Award") for [Best Pop Collaboration with Vocals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Pop_Collaboration_with_Vocals "Grammy Award for Best Pop Collaboration with Vocals").

Tired of being marketed as another [cookie cutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cookie_cutter#Alternative_definition "Cookie cutter") pop act, as well as eager both to be seen as a more serious songwriter and musician and to perform the type of music she wanted to, Pink took her sound in a new direction and sought more artistic and creative control during the recording of her second album, *[Missundaztood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missundaztood "Missundaztood")*. She recruited [Linda Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Perry "Linda Perry"), former singer of [4 Non Blondes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4_Non_Blondes "4 Non Blondes") (one of Pink's favorite groups in her teenage years). Pink moved into Perry's Los Angeles home where the pair spent several months writing songs for the album. Perry co-wrote and co-produced the album with [Dallas Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Austin "Dallas Austin") and [Scott Storch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Storch "Scott Storch"), and according to [VH1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VH1 "VH1")'s *Driven* program, [Antonio "LA" Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L.A._Reid "L.A. Reid") of LaFace Records was not initially content with the new music Pink was making. The album, named *Missundaztood* because of Pink's belief that people had a wrong image of her, was released in November 2001.

"[Get the Party Started](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Get_the_Party_Started "Get the Party Started")" was released as the lead single and peaked at No. 4 on the *Billboard* Hot 100. It also became a worldwide hit, reaching No. 1 in Australia, Ireland, New Zealand, Romania, and Spain, as well as spending four weeks at the top of the [European Hot 100 Singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Hot_100_Singles "European Hot 100 Singles") chart. At the [2002 MTV Video Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_MTV_Video_Music_Awards "2002 MTV Video Music Awards"), its music video won in the categories of [Best Female Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Female_Video "Best Female Video") and [Best Dance Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Dance_Video "Best Dance Video"). The album's other singles—"[Don't Let Me Get Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don%27t_Let_Me_Get_Me "Don't Let Me Get Me")", "[Just Like a Pill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_Like_a_Pill "Just Like a Pill")", and "[Family Portrait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Portrait_(song) "Family Portrait (song)")"—were also radio and chart successes, with "Just Like a Pill" becoming Pink's second No. 1 hit in the United Kingdom. *Missundaztood* remains Pink's best-selling record with more than 13 million copies sold worldwide. According to the [International Federation of the Phonographic Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Federation_of_the_Phonographic_Industry "International Federation of the Phonographic Industry") (IFPI), *Missundaztood* was the eighth best-selling album of 2002 globally. Pink won a [World Music Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Music_Award "World Music Award") for Best Selling American Pop/Rock Female Artist. She was also nominated for [Best Pop Vocal Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Pop_Vocal_Album "Grammy Award for Best Pop Vocal Album") and [Best Female Pop Vocal Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Female_Pop_Vocal_Performance "Grammy Award for Best Female Pop Vocal Performance") at the [45th Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/45th_Grammy_Awards "45th Grammy Awards"). [Faith Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith_Hill "Faith Hill")'s 2002 album, *[Cry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cry_(Faith_Hill_album) "Cry (Faith Hill album)")*, features a song co-written by Pink and Perry ("If You're Gonna Fly Away"). In 2002, Pink headlined a tour of America, Europe, and Australia named the [Party Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_Tour "Party Tour"), as well as becoming a supporting act for [Lenny Kravitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenny_Kravitz "Lenny Kravitz")'s American tour. Pink was named the Top Female *Billboard* 200 Artist of 2002.

### 2003–2007: *Try This* and *I'm Not Dead*

In mid-2003, Pink contributed the song "[Feel Good Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feel_Good_Time "Feel Good Time")" to the soundtrack of the film *[Charlie's Angels: Full Throttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie%27s_Angels:_Full_Throttle "Charlie's Angels: Full Throttle")*, in which she had a [cameo appearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance "Cameo appearance") as a [motocross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motocross "Motocross") race ramp owner/promoter. Featuring [electronic music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_music "Electronic music") artist [William Orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Orbit "William Orbit"), it became Pink's first single to miss the Top 40 on *Billboard*'s Hot 100 chart, although it was a hit in Europe and in Australia. It was later included on non-US editions of Pink's third album, *[Try This](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Try_This "Try This")*, which was released on November 11, 2003. Eight of the 13 tracks were co-written with [Tim Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Armstrong "Tim Armstrong") of the band [Rancid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancid_(band) "Rancid (band)"). Linda Perry was featured on the album as a writer and musician. Despite the album reaching the Top 10 on album charts in the US, in Canada, in the UK, and in Australia, sales were considerably lower than those of *Missundaztood*. However, it did go platinum in the US. The singles "[Trouble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trouble_(Pink_song) "Trouble (Pink song)")" and "[God Is a DJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_Is_a_DJ_(Pink_song) "God Is a DJ (Pink song)")" did not reach the US top 40 but did reach the top ten in other countries, and "[Last to Know](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_to_Know "Last to Know")" was released as a single outside North America. "Trouble" earned Pink the Grammy award in [Best Female Rock Vocal Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Female_Rock_Vocal_Performance "Grammy Award for Best Female Rock Vocal Performance") category at the [46th Annual Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/46th_Annual_Grammy_Awards "46th Annual Grammy Awards"), and "Feel Good Time" was nominated for [Best Pop Collaboration with Vocals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Pop_Collaboration_with_Vocals "Grammy Award for Best Pop Collaboration with Vocals"). She toured extensively on the [Try This Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Try_This_Tour "Try This Tour") through Europe and Australia, where the album was better received.

During the same period, Pink co-wrote the song "Take a Picture" with [Damon Elliott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damon_Elliott "Damon Elliott") which was released on Mýa's album *[Moodring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moodring "Moodring")*. In 2005, Pink collaborated with [Lisa Marie Presley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Marie_Presley "Lisa Marie Presley") on the track "Shine", released on Presley's second album *[Now What](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Now_What_(Lisa_Marie_Presley_album) "Now What (Lisa Marie Presley album)")*. Pink took a break to write the songs for her fourth album, *[I'm Not Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%27m_Not_Dead "I'm Not Dead")*, which she said she titled as such because "It's about being alive and feisty and not sitting down and shutting up even though people would like you to." Pink worked with producers [Max Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Martin "Max Martin"), [Billy Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Mann "Billy Mann"), [Christopher Rojas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Rojas "Christopher Rojas"), [Butch Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butch_Walker "Butch Walker"), [Lukasz Gottwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lukasz_Gottwald "Lukasz Gottwald"), and [Josh Abraham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Abraham "Josh Abraham") on the album. The album's release through LaFace Records in April 2006 was a substantial success throughout the world, particularly in Australia. The album reached the Top 10 in the US, the Top 5 in the UK, No. 1 in Germany, and was No. 1 in Australia for two non-consecutive weeks.

The album's lead single, "[Stupid Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stupid_Girls "Stupid Girls")", was Pink's biggest US hit since 2002 and earned her a Grammy Award nomination for Best Female Pop Vocal Performance. Its music video, in which she parodies celebrities such as [Lindsay Lohan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindsay_Lohan "Lindsay Lohan"), [Jessica Simpson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Simpson "Jessica Simpson"), [Mary-Kate Olsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary-Kate_Olsen "Mary-Kate Olsen"), and [Paris Hilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Hilton "Paris Hilton"), won the [MTV Video Music Award for Best Pop Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Award_for_Best_Pop_Video "MTV Video Music Award for Best Pop Video"). Subsequent singles "[Who Knew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who_Knew "Who Knew")" and "[U + Ur Hand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_%2B_Ur_Hand "U + Ur Hand")" were substantial hits in Australia and Europe, and they later became Top 10 singles in the US. in 2007. The non-US singles were "[Nobody Knows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobody_Knows_(Pink_song) "Nobody Knows (Pink song)")", a minor hit in the UK, Australia and Germany; "[Dear Mr. President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dear_Mr._President_(Pink_song) "Dear Mr. President (Pink song)")", an open letter to the US President [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush") that featured the [Indigo Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigo_Girls "Indigo Girls") and became a No. 1 hit in Belgium as well as a Top 5 hit in Germany, Australia, and other countries; "[Leave Me Alone (I'm Lonely)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leave_Me_Alone_(I%27m_Lonely) "Leave Me Alone (I'm Lonely)")", a UK Top 40 and Australian Top 5 entry; and "['Cuz I Can](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%27Cuz_I_Can_(Pink_song) "'Cuz I Can (Pink song)")". The album has sold more than 1.3 million copies in the US, as well as over 700,000 copies in Australia. The album proved very popular in Australia, with six Top 5 singles and a record-breaking 62 weeks in the Top 10; so far the album has gone 10 times platinum.

In support of the album, Pink embarked on the world [I'm Not Dead Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%27m_Not_Dead_Tour "I'm Not Dead Tour"), for which ticket sales in Australia were particularly high; she sold approximately 307,000 tickets in Australia, giving her the record for the biggest concert attendance for an arena tour by a female artist. One of the London shows on the tour was taped and released as a DVD, *[Pink: Live from Wembley Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink:_Live_from_Wembley_Arena "Pink: Live from Wembley Arena")*, where she sang Linda Perry's "[Whats Up?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What%27s_Up%3F_(4_Non_Blondes_song) "What's Up? (4 Non Blondes song)")". In 2006, Pink was chosen to sing the theme song for *[NBC Sunday Night Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_Sunday_Night_Football "NBC Sunday Night Football")*, "Waiting All Day for Sunday Night", which is a take on "[I Hate Myself for Loving You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Hate_Myself_for_Loving_You "I Hate Myself for Loving You")" by [Joan Jett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Jett "Joan Jett"). She contributed a cover of [Rufus's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rufus_(band) "Rufus (band)") "Tell Me Something Good" to the soundtrack of the film *[Happy Feet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happy_Feet "Happy Feet")*, and lent her name to [PlayStation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation "PlayStation") to promote the [PSP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Portable "PlayStation Portable"), a special Pink edition of which was released.

Pink collaborated with several other artists in 2006 and 2007 when she opened for [Justin Timberlake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Timberlake "Justin Timberlake") on the American leg of his [FutureSex/LoveShow Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_2007_FutureSex/LoveShow "The 2007 FutureSex/LoveShow"). She sang on the Indigo Girls album *[Despite Our Differences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Despite_Our_Differences "Despite Our Differences")*. She was featured on [India.Arie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India.Arie "India.Arie")'s song "[I Am Not My Hair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Am_Not_My_Hair "I Am Not My Hair")" from the [Lifetime Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifetime_Television "Lifetime Television") film *Why I Wore Lipstick to My Mastectomy*. She wrote a song, "I Will", for [Natalia's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalia_(Belgian_singer) "Natalia (Belgian singer)") third album, *[Everything and More](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everything_and_More_(Natalia_album) "Everything and More (Natalia album)")*. "Outside of You", another song she co-wrote, was recorded by [dance-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance-pop "Dance-pop") singer [Hilary Duff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilary_Duff "Hilary Duff") and released on her 2007 album *[Dignity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dignity_(album) "Dignity (album)")*. Pink recorded a song with [Annie Lennox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_Lennox "Annie Lennox") and twenty-two other female acts for Lennox's fourth solo studio album, *[Songs of Mass Destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songs_of_Mass_Destruction "Songs of Mass Destruction")*; titled "[Sing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sing_(Annie_Lennox_song) "Sing (Annie Lennox song)")"; it was written as an anthem for HIV/AIDS, according to Lennox's website. In December 2007, a special edition *Pink Box*, which comprises her second to fourth albums and the DVD *Live in Europe*, was released in Australia. It reached the Top 20 on the albums chart and was certified Gold, selling more than 35,000 units.

### 2008–2011: *Funhouse* and *Greatest Hits... So Far!!!*

On August 7, 2008, Pink's single "[So What](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/So_What_(Pink_song) "So What (Pink song)")" was leaked online, and radio stations across Australia were quick to give it massive airplay. Less than six hours after the leak, "So What" was voted No. 1 on Nova 100 Melbourne and shot to No. 1 on the [Today Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Today_Network "Today Network")'s national radio Hot30 Countdown. On August 22, Pink announced a new track, titled "Crystal Ball". On September 18, 2008, "So What" became her second number-one hit on the *Billboard* Hot 100. Pink was the guest of honor at the 2008 ARIA Music Awards, which were held in Sydney, Australia, in October 2008. There she sang "So What". On November 3, 2008, *[Funhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funhouse_(Pink_album) "Funhouse (Pink album)")* debuted at No. 1 on the ARIA charts. In Australia it sold over 86,000 units in its first week, and was eventually certified eleven times platinum.

On November 23, 2008, Pink performed "[Sober](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sober_(Pink_song) "Sober (Pink song)")", the second single from *Funhouse*, at the [American Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Music_Awards "American Music Awards"). The third single was "[Please Don't Leave Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Please_Don%27t_Leave_Me "Please Don't Leave Me")", with a video directed by [Dave Meyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Meyers_(director) "Dave Meyers (director)"). In Australia, "[Bad Influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Influence_(song) "Bad Influence (song)")" was released as the album's fourth single as a promotional single for her [Funhouse Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funhouse_Tour "Funhouse Tour"), and "[Funhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funhouse_(song) "Funhouse (song)")" was later released as the fifth single. However, "Bad Influence" was not released as a single in Europe until March 2010, which was after "Funhouse" had been released. In May 2009, Pink released a four-CD box set of her first four albums; this set peaked at No. 7 in the [UK Album Chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Album_Chart "UK Album Chart"). In 2009, Pink performed in *[The People Speak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_People_Speak_(film) "The People Speak (film)")*, a documentary feature film that uses dramatic and musical performances of the letters, diaries, and speeches of everyday Americans, based on historian [Howard Zinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Zinn "Howard Zinn")'s *[A People's History of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_People%27s_History_of_the_United_States "A People's History of the United States")*.

Pink's Funhouse Tour started in France on February 24, 2009, and continued through Europe until mid-May, with supporting act [Raygun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raygun_(band) "Raygun (band)"). Pink then performed a series of shows in Australia, all of which sold out. Between May and August 2009, she performed for a total of more than 600,000 Australian fans at 58 shows around the country.

On September 13, 2009, Pink performed "Sober" while doing a [trapeze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapeze "Trapeze") act at the [2009 MTV Video Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_MTV_Video_Music_Awards "2009 MTV Video Music Awards"), where she was nominated for Best Female Video for "So What". On January 31, 2010, Pink did another circus act in the form of [aerial silks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_silks "Aerial silks") at the [2010 Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/52nd_Annual_Grammy_Awards "52nd Annual Grammy Awards"), this time performing the song "[Glitter in the Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glitter_in_the_Air "Glitter in the Air")". She received a standing ovation. In 2013, [*Billboard*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_(magazine) "Billboard (magazine)") ranked the performance as the best between 2000 and 2012. *Billboard* recognized Pink as the [Pop Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainstream_Top_40 "Mainstream Top 40") [Artist of the Decade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Decade-End#2000s "Billboard Decade-End"). According to the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") countdown compiled by [PPL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonographic_Performance_Limited "Phonographic Performance Limited"), Pink was the second most-played female solo artist in the United Kingdom during the 2000s decade, behind [Madonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna "Madonna").

Pink was a soloist in the [remake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Are_the_World_25_for_Haiti "We Are the World 25 for Haiti") of the 1985 charity single, "[We Are the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Are_the_World "We Are the World")". She collaborated on the 2010 [Herbie Hancock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbie_Hancock "Herbie Hancock") album, *[The Imagine Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Imagine_Project "The Imagine Project")*, in which she sang [Peter Gabriel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Gabriel "Peter Gabriel")'s "[Don't Give Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don%27t_Give_Up_(Peter_Gabriel_and_Kate_Bush_song) "Don't Give Up (Peter Gabriel and Kate Bush song)")" with [John Legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Legend "John Legend") and contributed vocals to [John Lennon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lennon "John Lennon")'s "[Imagine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imagine_(John_Lennon_song) "Imagine (John Lennon song)")" with [Seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seal_(musician) "Seal (musician)"), [India.Arie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India.Arie "India.Arie"), [Jeff Beck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Beck "Jeff Beck"), [Konono Nº1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konono_N%C2%BA1 "Konono Nº1"), [Oumou Sangaré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oumou_Sangar%C3%A9 "Oumou Sangaré"), and others. The last collaboration earned Pink a [Grammy Award for Best Pop Collaboration with Vocals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Pop_Collaboration_with_Vocals "Grammy Award for Best Pop Collaboration with Vocals"). She was featured on a track titled "[Won't Back Down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Won%27t_Back_Down_(Eminem_song) "Won't Back Down (Eminem song)")" for [Eminem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eminem "Eminem")'s 2010 album *[Recovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recovery_(Eminem_album) "Recovery (Eminem album)")*; Eminem explained that he included Pink because he "felt like she would smash this record."

On July 15, 2010, during a concert in Nuremberg, Germany, Pink was preparing to end her concert with an aerial acrobatic routine when she was pulled offstage and onto a barricade below. Her left-side flywire had been activated before the right-side one had been properly attached to her harness. She was taken to a local hospital where it was determined that she had not been seriously injured. Pink sold a total of 3,000,000 concert tickets on her 2009–10 worldwide tour, according to a statement on behalf of UK tour promoter Marshall Arts.

In the first week of October 2010, Pink released "[Raise Your Glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raise_Your_Glass "Raise Your Glass")", the first single from her first compilation album, *[Greatest Hits... So Far!!!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greatest_Hits..._So_Far!!!_(Pink_album) "Greatest Hits... So Far!!! (Pink album)")*. The song celebrates a decade of solo work, and is dedicated to her fans who have been supporting her over the years. The song reached the top of the *Billboard* Hot 100, becoming Pink's tenth Top 10 hit, and her third number-one on the chart.
She released the compilation album on November 12, 2010, and almost a month later she released the album's second single, named "[Fuckin' Perfect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuckin%27_Perfect "Fuckin' Perfect")". The song reached number two on the *Billboard* Hot 100, and peaked at number one on the airplay charts in Germany. On the German singles chart, the song entered at number seven in March 2011.

Pink voiced the character of Gloria in *[Happy Feet Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happy_Feet_Two "Happy Feet Two")*, which premiered on November 18, 2011, in the United States. She also sings the movie's theme song, "[Bridge of Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridge_of_Light_(song) "Bridge of Light (song)")".

On October 7, 2011, [RCA Music Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCA_Music_Group "RCA Music Group") announced that it would be disbanding Jive Records, along with Arista Records and J Records. With the shutdown, Pink and all other artists previously signed to the labels would release any future material through [RCA Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCA_Records "RCA Records").

### 2012–2015: *The Truth About Love* and You+Me

In February 2012, Pink confirmed that she was in the writing process for her next studio album, *The Truth About Love*. She was scheduled to perform at a fundraiser for the presidential campaign of [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") that June, but had to cancel her performance after she was hospitalized and underwent the [removal of her gallbladder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholecystectomy "Cholecystectomy"). *The Truth About Love* was preceded with the release of its lead single, "[Blow Me (One Last Kiss)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blow_Me_(One_Last_Kiss) "Blow Me (One Last Kiss)")", in July. The single peaked at number five on the *Billboard* Hot 100, while reaching number one in Australia and Hungary, and the top five in Canada, Japan, and the United Kingdom. Released in September, *The Truth About Love* made its debut atop the *Billboard* 200 with first-week sales of 281,000, making it her first number-one album in the United States. It also topped the charts in Australia, Austria, Canada, Germany, New Zealand, Sweden, and Switzerland, and became the world's sixth best-selling album of 2012 according to the IFPI. The album was certified double platinum by the RIAA for two million copies shipped and has sold over seven million copies worldwide. *The Truth About Love* received positive response from music critics and was nominated for [Best Pop Vocal Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Pop_Vocal_Album "Best Pop Vocal Album") at the [55th Annual Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/55th_Annual_Grammy_Awards "55th Annual Grammy Awards").

"[Try](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Try_(Pink_song) "Try (Pink song)")" was released as the second single from *The Truth About Love* in October 2012 and became a worldwide top-ten hit, peaking at number nine on the *Billboard* Hot 100. In February 2013, Pink released the fourth single, "[Just Give Me a Reason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_Give_Me_a_Reason "Just Give Me a Reason")", featuring guest vocals by [Nate Ruess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nate_Ruess "Nate Ruess") of [Fun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fun_(band) "Fun (band)"). It became the most successful single from *The Truth About Love*, topping the record charts in more than 20 countries worldwide and becoming Pink's fourth number-one hit on the *Billboard* Hot 100. According to the IFPI, the song was the fourth best-selling digital single of 2013 with 9.9 million copies sold worldwide. The song won the *Billboard* Mid-Year Award for Favorite Hot 100 No. 1 Song, and garnered two nominations for [Best Pop Duo/Group Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Pop_Duo/Group_Performance "Grammy Award for Best Pop Duo/Group Performance") and [Song of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Song_of_the_Year "Grammy Award for Song of the Year") at the [56th Annual Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/56th_Annual_Grammy_Awards "56th Annual Grammy Awards"). Three further singles, "[True Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Love_(Pink_song) "True Love (Pink song)")", "[Walk of Shame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walk_of_Shame_(song) "Walk of Shame (song)")", and "Are We All We Are", was released throughout 2013 to less commercial success. On February 13, 2013, Pink kicked off her sixth tour, known as [The Truth About Love Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Truth_About_Love_Tour "The Truth About Love Tour"), in [Phoenix, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix,_Arizona "Phoenix, Arizona"). *Billboard* released a statement on June 14, announcing that Pink held the No. 1 spot on their Hot Tours chart, as the American leg of her Truth About Love Tour grossed over $23.6 million. She still held the title a week later, as the European leg grossed $30.7 million.

In addition to her work for *The Truth About Love*, Pink appeared on the track "Guns and Roses" on [T.I.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T.I. "T.I.")'s album *[Trouble Man: Heavy Is the Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trouble_Man:_Heavy_Is_the_Head "Trouble Man: Heavy Is the Head")*. The song has been certified [Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARIA_Charts "ARIA Charts") by the [ARIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Recording_Industry_Association "Australian Recording Industry Association") for sales of 35,000 digital downloads shipped in Australia. She also wrote two songs, "[I Walk Alone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Walk_Alone_(Cher_song) "I Walk Alone (Cher song)")" and "Lie to Me", for [Cher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cher "Cher")'s new album, *[Closer to the Truth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closer_to_the_Truth_(Cher_album) "Closer to the Truth (Cher album)")*. Pink starred as a sex addict alongside [Gwyneth Paltrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwyneth_Paltrow "Gwyneth Paltrow") and [Mark Ruffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Ruffalo "Mark Ruffalo") in the 2012 movie *[Thanks for Sharing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanks_for_Sharing "Thanks for Sharing")*. The official trailer was released on June 27 and the movie premiered in the United States until September 20, 2013. Her legal name, Alecia Moore, is used for the movie credits. Her role as Dede was heavily praised by critics. rogerebert.com commented on her performance saying "Of all the cast here, the least experienced is the pop singer Pink, yet she does the best acting in the film: natural, a little harsh, a little unstable. Pink, like [Macy Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macy_Gray "Macy Gray") in her [Lee Daniels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Daniels "Lee Daniels") movie roles, knows instinctively how to behave on camera by just pretending that the camera isn't there."

*Billboard* named Pink Woman of the Year 2013. In December, the magazine also named [The Truth About Love Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Truth_About_Love_Tour "The Truth About Love Tour") the third best selling tour of 2013 with $147.9 million in ticket sales; falling only behind [Bon Jovi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Jovi "Bon Jovi") and [Michael Jackson: The Immortal World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jackson:_The_Immortal_World_Tour "Michael Jackson: The Immortal World Tour"). Also in Billboard's end of year charts, Pink was ranked the sixth top artist of 2013 and she scored her highest charting end-of-year song and album; with [Just Give Me a Reason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_Give_Me_a_Reason "Just Give Me a Reason") sitting at number 7 on the [*Billboard* Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100 "Billboard Hot 100") and [The Truth About Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Truth_About_Love_(Pink_album) "The Truth About Love (Pink album)") placing at number 8 on the [*Billboard* 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200"). In Australia, Pink has had an album placed at number one or two in the ARIA End of Year Albums Chart for six out of the past seven years as *The Truth About Love* topped the chart for two years in a row, making it the first album in Australian chart history to do so. She was the ninth top grossing music artist of 2013, with $20,072,072.32 earned. [RCA Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCA_Records "RCA Records") later announced that they have signed Pink for a multi-album deal that will last for years to come. The singer was quoted about the deal saying "I am super-duper excited to continue onwards and upwards with RCA and my team there".

It was announced in September 2014, that Pink and [Dallas Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Green_(musician) "Dallas Green (musician)"), lead singer of [City and Colour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_and_Colour "City and Colour"), worked together on a collaborative album, under the band name [You+Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You%2BMe "You+Me"). The album, titled *[Rose Ave.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Ave._(album) "Rose Ave. (album)")*, was released on October 14, 2014. The album debuted at number four on the [*Billboard* 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200") and at number one on the US [Folk Albums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_Albums "Folk Albums") chart.

In August 2015, Pink recorded the theme song for the 13th season of *[The Ellen DeGeneres Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ellen_DeGeneres_Show "The Ellen DeGeneres Show")*. The song, "[Today's the Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Today%27s_the_Day_(Pink_song) "Today's the Day (Pink song)")", was performed during the show's premiere week in New York City on September 10, 2015.

### 2016–2020: *Beautiful Trauma*, *Hurts 2B Human* and hiatus

It was announced in February 2016 that Pink would cover [the Beatles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beatles "The Beatles")' song "[Lucy in the Sky with Diamonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucy_in_the_Sky_with_Diamonds "Lucy in the Sky with Diamonds")" for the upcoming [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") original series *[Beat Bugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beat_Bugs "Beat Bugs")*. In the same month, it was announced that she had recorded a cover of "[White Rabbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Rabbit_(Jefferson_Airplane_song) "White Rabbit (Jefferson Airplane song)")" for the movie *[Alice Through the Looking Glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Through_the_Looking_Glass_(2016_film) "Alice Through the Looking Glass (2016 film)")*, while in April it was revealed that she contributed the song "[Just like Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_like_Fire "Just like Fire")" to the soundtrack of the movie. In Australia, it topped the [ARIA Charts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARIA_Charts "ARIA Charts"). The following July, it was announced that Pink had written a song for French-Canadian singer [Celine Dion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celine_Dion "Celine Dion") called "[Recovering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recovering "Recovering")" for inclusion on her upcoming English-language album. Pink provided guest vocals on country singer [Kenny Chesney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenny_Chesney "Kenny Chesney")'s single "[Setting the World on Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Setting_the_World_on_Fire "Setting the World on Fire")" which was released on August 1, 2016. The single topped on the *Billboard* [Hot Country Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Country_Songs "Hot Country Songs") and went platinum in the United States and Canada. On March 10, 2017, Pink teamed up with [Stargate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargate_(production_team) "Stargate (production team)") and Australian star [Sia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sia "Sia") on the former's debut single, "[Waterfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterfall_(Stargate_song) "Waterfall (Stargate song)")".

Pink took a break to write songs for her upcoming seventh album. In June 2017, she confirmed her next studio album was in the works. "[What About Us](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_About_Us_(Pink_song) "What About Us (Pink song)")", the lead single from Pink's seventh studio album, *[Beautiful Trauma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beautiful_Trauma "Beautiful Trauma")*, was released on August 10, 2017 and reached number one in Australia. The album was released on October 13, 2017, and became the third best-selling album of the year worldwide. Pink received the [Michael Jackson Video Vanguard Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jackson_Video_Vanguard_Award "Michael Jackson Video Vanguard Award") at the [2017 MTV Video Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_MTV_Video_Music_Awards "2017 MTV Video Music Awards"). She also performed a medley of some of her songs, including her new single, "What About Us", before accepting the award, which was presented to her by [Ellen DeGeneres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellen_DeGeneres "Ellen DeGeneres"). "What About Us" reached number one on the [Adult Pop Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_Pop_Songs "Adult Pop Songs") chart, earning Pink her ninth leader on the chart, breaking her out of a tie with [Katy Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katy_Perry "Katy Perry") for the solo female artist with the most number-ones in the chart's history and placing her in second place among all acts. The song received one nomination at the [60th Annual Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/60th_Annual_Grammy_Awards "60th Annual Grammy Awards") for [Best Pop Solo Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Pop_Solo_Performance "Grammy Award for Best Pop Solo Performance"). "[Beautiful Trauma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beautiful_Trauma_(song) "Beautiful Trauma (song)")" was released on November 21, 2017, as the second single from *Beautiful Trauma* to less commercial success, reaching top thirty in Australia, the United Kingdom, and France. While it only managed to peak at number seventy-eight on the *Billboard Hot 100*, it topped on the *Billboard* [Dance Club Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance_Club_Songs "Dance Club Songs") chart. On December 5, 2017, rapper [Eminem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eminem "Eminem") revealed that Pink would be collaborating on the song "Need Me" for his ninth studio album *[Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revival_(Eminem_album) "Revival (Eminem album)")*. The album received the nomination for [Best Pop Vocal Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Pop_Vocal_Album "Grammy Award for Best Pop Vocal Album") earned Pink's 20th nomination at the [Annual Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/61st_Annual_Grammy_Awards "61st Annual Grammy Awards").

Although sick with influenza, Pink sang the [US national anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Star-Spangled_Banner "The Star-Spangled Banner") ahead of [Super Bowl LII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LII "Super Bowl LII"), a dream she had since, as a child, seeing [Whitney Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Houston "Whitney Houston") sing at [Super Bowl XXV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXV "Super Bowl XXV") in 1991. On March 1, 2018, Pink started her seventh concert tour, the [Beautiful Trauma World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beautiful_Trauma_World_Tour "Beautiful Trauma World Tour"), which was scheduled to visit North America and Oceania until September 8, 2018. She later decided to extend the tour until May 2019 including Europe. On April 6, 2018, she was featured on [Elton John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elton_John "Elton John")'s *[Revamp & Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revamp_%26_Restoration "Revamp & Restoration")*, singing the song "[Bennie and the Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bennie_and_the_Jets "Bennie and the Jets")", with Elton John himself and [Logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic_(rapper) "Logic (rapper)").

On April 17, 2018, *[People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_(magazine) "People (magazine)")* teased its 2018 "Most Beautiful" cover star by calling her "a performer, mother and role model whose honesty, humour, confidence and sheer star power make her one of the most beloved and fascinating entertainers on the planet." The next day the magazine revealed the cover, which features Pink with her two kids Willow and Jameson. The magazine issue was named the "beautiful issue". Similar covers had featured [Julia Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Roberts "Julia Roberts") and [Jennifer Aniston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Aniston "Jennifer Aniston").
On October 23, 2018, Pink released her version of the song "[A Million Dreams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Million_Dreams "A Million Dreams")" from the upcoming "[*The Greatest Showman – Reimagined*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Greatest_Showman "The Greatest Showman")" album, a reworking of the soundtrack with contributions from various artists including [Kelly Clarkson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_Clarkson "Kelly Clarkson"), [Kesha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kesha "Kesha"), [Jess Glynne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jess_Glynne "Jess Glynne") and [Missy Elliott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missy_Elliott "Missy Elliott"). Pink's daughter, Willow Sage Hart is also featured on the album performing the song's reprise.

On February 5, 2019, Pink received a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame "Hollywood Walk of Fame"); she further announced the release of her eighth album, *[Hurts 2B Human](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurts_2B_Human "Hurts 2B Human")*, which was released on April 26, 2019. The album's lead single, "[Walk Me Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walk_Me_Home_(Pink_song) "Walk Me Home (Pink song)")" was released on February 20, 2019. ## Artistry

### Influences

Recognized for her acrobatic stage presence, Pink started studying [Aerial silks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_silk "Aerial silk") after watching a [Cher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cher "Cher") concert from her [Living Proof: The Farewell Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_Proof:_The_Farewell_Tour "Living Proof: The Farewell Tour") in 2004; Cher would later guest-star as God in Pink's music video for "All I Know So Far". Pink has also identified [Madonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna "Madonna") and [Janis Joplin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janis_Joplin "Janis Joplin") as some of her musical influences. In a 2000 interview with [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV "MTV"), she said that during her childhood she used to think that she was Madonna's daughter, saying: "I've always been the type of person that followed Madonna like a lost puppy. I didn't speak to my mother for a year, because I was sure she adopted me." She added, "Madonna has always been an inspiration for me... I was a fan right from the first time I heard '[Holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holiday_(Madonna_song) "Holiday (Madonna song)")'." Pink also won her first talent show singing Madonna's "[Oh Father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oh_Father "Oh Father")". Of Joplin, Pink said, "She was so inspiring by singing blues music when it wasn't culturally acceptable for white women, and she wore her heart on her sleeve. She was so witty and charming and intelligent, but she also battled an ugly-duckling syndrome. I would love to play her in a movie." In a tribute performance on her [Try This Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Try_This_Tour "Try This Tour"), Pink called Joplin "a woman who inspired me when everyone else ... didn't!" Other artists who have inspired her include [Whitney Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Houston "Whitney Houston"), [Mary J. Blige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_J._Blige "Mary J. Blige"), [Billy Joel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Joel "Billy Joel"), [Donny Hathaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donny_Hathaway "Donny Hathaway"), and [PJ Harvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PJ_Harvey "PJ Harvey").

As a child, Pink admired the lead singer of [4 Non Blondes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4_Non_Blondes "4 Non Blondes"), [Linda Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Perry "Linda Perry"), whom she tracked down and formed a close relationship with on her second album. She said:

> Literally this woman spoke to me. Being in pain and being on drugs and being misunderstood and, yeah she spoke my language. Without having to say anything, she could sing a note and it was what I was feeling. I used to sit at three or four o' clock in the morning, tripping on whatever, screaming 4 Non Blondes out the window until the cops were called.

### Voice and timbre

Throughout her career, Pink has received acclaim from critics for her powerful singing abilities. Pink is a [contralto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contralto "Contralto"), whose voice has been described as "raspy", "husky", and "distinctive". NBC News music critic [Maura Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maura_Johnston "Maura Johnston") said that, at times, "the sheer power of her alto" has been overshadowed by her skills as an [aerialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerialist "Aerialist"). James Montgomery of [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV "MTV") calls her "a deceptively good singer ... who can out-sing almost anyone". *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* called her voice "prodigious". [Ann Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Powers "Ann Powers") of the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")* labeled her "a powerhouse vocalist". Pink has also been noted for her "raw", "soulful" voice and her ability to emote. *[The Inquirer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Inquirer "The Inquirer")* called her voice "husky" and "gutsy", and wrote that she had developed into a "powerfully emotive vocalist", comparing her to [Janis Joplin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janis_Joplin "Janis Joplin"). The *[Star Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Tribune "Star Tribune")* wrote, "Her slightly raspy, slightly soulful voice made you feel the dysfunction in '[Family Portrait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Portrait_(song) "Family Portrait (song)")', the longing of '[Who Knew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who_Knew "Who Knew")' and the empowerment in '[Perfect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuckin%27_Perfect "Fuckin' Perfect")'." [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN "CNN") has said that Pink is known for singing "with the right level of emotion". Kelly Clarkson has called Pink's voice "the best of our generation". Troy L. Smith, writing for [Cleveland.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland.com "Cleveland.com"), called Pink one of her generation's most underrated vocalists, writing that she is capable of "sing[ing] anything, from rock and pop to folk and R&B." Smith named her 2006's second best vocalist, runner-up to [Carrie Underwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrie_Underwood "Carrie Underwood").

### Musical style

Pink stated she "doesn't want to be stuck in a box" and wants to "be bold and go all out". Although Pink's music is primarily rooted in [pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_music "Pop music"), she often incorporates elements of [rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_music "Rock music"), and her early work has been categorized as [R&B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_R%26B "Contemporary R&B"). Her sound has been described as [pop rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_rock "Pop rock"), [pop-punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop-punk "Pop-punk"), and [power pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_pop "Power pop"). She also delved into [punk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock "Punk rock"), [dance-rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance-rock "Dance-rock"), [electronic rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_rock "Electronic rock"), [funk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funk_rock "Funk rock"), [soft rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_rock "Soft rock"), [country pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_pop "Country pop"), [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music"), [dance-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance-pop "Dance-pop"), [synth-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synth-pop "Synth-pop") and [EDM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDM_(genre) "EDM (genre)").

[*Variety*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)") described her as "pop's [bionic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bionics "Bionics") woman", "[gymnast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnastics "Gymnastics")-[therapist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Therapy "Therapy")" and "music's most radio-friendly [rebel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebellion "Rebellion")".

### Feminism and LGBTQ rights

Pink identifies as a [feminist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminism "Feminism") and [pro-choice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion-rights_movement "Abortion-rights movement"). Pink attended the [2017 Women's March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Women%27s_March "2017 Women's March"), a widely attended protest in favor of women's rights and equality alongside her family. In 2018, she released "[Wild Hearts Can't Be Broken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_Hearts_Can%27t_Be_Broken_(Pink_song) "Wild Hearts Can't Be Broken (Pink song)")" in support of the [Suffragette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffragette "Suffragette") movement. On [Women's Equality Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_Equality_Day "Women's Equality Day") in 2019, Gillie and Marc Art unveiled a bronze sculpture of Pink as a part of [Statues for Equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statues_for_Equality "Statues for Equality") mission. In July 2021, she offered to pay the fines handed out to the Norwegian women's beach handball team after they wore shorts like their male counterparts instead of bikini bottoms. In 2023 and 2025, Pink hosted the Desert Smash Celebrity Charity Tennis Event benefitting the [WTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_Tennis_Association "Women's Tennis Association") Foundation Global Women's Health Fund, that supports access to "life-saving women's health and nutrition products and services. In April 2026, Pink received the "Champion of Change Award" from [Planned Parenthood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planned_Parenthood "Planned Parenthood") for "her outspoken support of reproductive rights for all".

Pink is also outspoken about [LGBTQ rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_rights "LGBTQ rights") and supports [same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage "Same-sex marriage"). In her 2006 song "[Dear Mr. President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dear_Mr._President_(Pink_song) "Dear Mr. President (Pink song)")", she criticized then-President [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush")'s opposition to gay marriage, singing: "What kind of father would hate his own daughter if she were gay?". In November 2008, Pink marched against [Proposition 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_California_Proposition_8 "2008 California Proposition 8"), a proposition to ban gay marriage, at a rally in downtown Los Angeles. The singer was awarded with the "Ally for Equality Award" at the [Human Rights Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Campaign "Human Rights Campaign") Dinner in 2010. In an interview with [Gaydar Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaydar_Radio "Gaydar Radio") from 2012, the singer stated: "I think that the best day will be when we no longer talk about being gay or straight – it's not a 'gay wedding', it's just a 'wedding'; it's not a 'gay marriage', it's just 'a marriage.'" In 2012, she told *[The Advocate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Advocate_(magazine) "The Advocate (magazine)")* that she had girlfriends in her twenties but does not define her [sexual orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_orientation "Sexual orientation"), saying, "I never felt the need to." During the same interview, the singer mentioned her appreciation for her lesbian and bisexual female fans. In 2023, *[Billboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_(magazine) "Billboard (magazine)")* named her song "[Raise Your Glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raise_Your_Glass "Raise Your Glass")" as one of the top LGBT Anthems of All Time at number 31. In an interview with *[Gay Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_Times "Gay Times")*, Pink stated that the [LGBTQ community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_community "LGBTQ community") means "everything to her". She added: "I think [my LGBTQ+ following] just speaks to who I am and what I believe in, and what I've always been. I am definitely an ally and to be understood for that is a wonderful feeling. For any of us to be understood, that's what we want in life, right? We want to love, be loved and be seen."

### Animal rights

Pink is an [animal rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_rights "Animal rights") [activist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Activism "Activism"). In 2003, she declined an invitation to perform at [Prince William](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William,_Prince_of_Wales "William, Prince of Wales")'s 21st birthday bash on account of the [Royal Family's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_royal_family "British royal family") controversial stance on [animal hunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunting_and_shooting_in_the_United_Kingdom "Hunting and shooting in the United Kingdom"). Pink is a prominent campaigner for [People for the Ethical Treatment of Animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_for_the_Ethical_Treatment_of_Animals "People for the Ethical Treatment of Animals") (PETA), contributing her voice to causes such as protest against [KFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KFC "KFC"). In conjunction with PETA, she criticized the Australian wool industry for its use of [mulesing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulesing "Mulesing"). In 2007, she said PETA had misled her about mulesing and that she had not done enough research before lending her name to the campaign. Her campaigning led to a headlining concert called PAW (Party for Animals Worldwide) in [Cardiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff "Cardiff"), Wales, on August 21, 2007. As of May 2008,[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pink_(singer)&action=edit) she has been officially recognized as an advocate for [RSPCA Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSPCA_Australia "RSPCA Australia"). The singer joined [Ricky Gervais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricky_Gervais "Ricky Gervais") for PETA's "Stolen for Fashion" advertisement, which opposes wearing fur and animal skin. In 2014, Pink stated she opposes carriage riding in New York City. The singer asked [Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II "Elizabeth II") why the bear fur on the [Guards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King%27s_Guard "King's Guard")' caps hadn't been replaced with a synthetic, cruelty-free material in 2013. She posed nude in 2015 for PETA's "I'd Rather Go Naked Than Wear Fur" campaign. The singer opposed [SeaWorld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SeaWorld "SeaWorld") in 2018 for "locking up" marine animals in sanctuaries where all they are able to do is "swim in endless circles".

### Childhood hunger activism

In December 2015, Pink was appointed as a UNICEF Ambassador and the agency's Kid Power National Spokesperson to help raise awareness about its lifesaving health and nutrition programs around the world. She followed UNICEF to [Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiti "Haiti") and witnessed the impact therapeutic food has on [malnourished](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malnutrition "Malnutrition") children. In 2023, the singer promoted the charity during the North American leg of the [Summer Carnival Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_Carnival_(tour) "Summer Carnival (tour)"). QR codes for UNICEF USA were placed at all merchandise stands, which directed fans to purchase basic necessities and school supplies for children in need.

In 2017, Pink and her husband joined a fundraising bike ride that raised $2,000,000 for the [Share Our Strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Share_Our_Strength "Share Our Strength")'s No Kid Hungry campaign. She partnered with "Save With Stories", a No Kid Hungry collaboration with [Save the Children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Children "Save the Children"), and read stories on [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram "Instagram") for children out of school due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"). In April 2023, Pink received the "National Champion Award" from No Kid Hungry for "her commitment and contributions to help advance No Kid Hungry's national campaign to end childhood hunger in America". Pink and Kelly Clarkson helped raise $60,000 for No Kid Hungry and [Sweet Relief Musicians Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_Relief_Musicians_Fund "Sweet Relief Musicians Fund") by auctioning off "one-of-a-kind" art pieces depicting their songs in sound waves. Later that year, Pink and her daughter collaborated with [Williams Sonoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_Sonoma "Williams Sonoma") and designed spatulas for the annual "Tools for Change" fundraising program by No Kid Hungry. In October 2025, Pink received the "Humanitarian Award" from [Action Against Hunger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_Against_Hunger "Action Against Hunger") for "her extensive advocacy for children and families" and "championing disaster relief and the ongoing fight against hunger".

### Political views

During the [2008 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election "2008 United States presidential election"), Pink said that [Sarah Palin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Palin "Sarah Palin") was "terrifying" and criticized her attitudes towards women and animals, and her inexperience on foreign issues. Pink endorsed [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton "Hillary Clinton") during the [2016 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election "2016 United States presidential election").

Pink supported [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden") during the [2020 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election "2020 United States presidential election"). In June 2020, she mocked [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") over the low attendance of his [rally in Tulsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Donald_Trump_rally_in_Tulsa "2020 Donald Trump rally in Tulsa"). In July 2024, she attended "White Women: Answer the Call", a [Zoom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoom_(software) "Zoom (software)") call by [gun violence prevention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_violence_prevention "Gun violence prevention") activist [Shannon Watts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannon_Watts "Shannon Watts") that [fundraised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundraising "Fundraising") on behalf of the [Kamala Harris presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris_2024_presidential_campaign "Kamala Harris 2024 presidential campaign").

#### Israel

In October 2023, Pink condemned [Hamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamas "Hamas") and violence at [protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_war_protests_in_the_United_States "Gaza war protests in the United States") related to the [Gaza war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_war "Gaza war"), while stating that [Palestinians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinians "Palestinians") deserved a solution that recognized their "aspirations and humanity" and that protecting civilian lives should be paramount. That month, she also denied flying [Israeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel") flags during her concerts, saying the blue-and-white fabric used in the show predated the war and that she did not fly any national flags.

In September 2026, Pink faced backlash after reposting a statement from [pro-Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-Israel "Pro-Israel") advocacy group [StopAntisemitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StopAntisemitism "StopAntisemitism") that called for an apology from [Macklemore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macklemore "Macklemore") for [advocating for Palestinians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macklemore%E2%80%93Ed_Sheeran_tour_controversy "Macklemore–Ed Sheeran tour controversy") during his opening act for [Ed Sheeran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Sheeran "Ed Sheeran") in New Jersey. Following the backlash, Pink said that the post she had shared "said things in words I didn't write, and I wouldn't have chosen all of them... I'm not going to litigate a complex war in an Instagram caption, and I'm not going to recite anybody's slogan to earn back a follower." She said that she supported Palestinian humanity, but that Macklemore's advocacy had "scared [her]". In response to Pink's actions regarding Macklemore's pro-Palestinian stance, a petition calling for her removal as a [UNICEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNICEF "UNICEF") ambassador had amassed more than 162,000 signatures by September 22.[*[needs update](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")*] The petition's organizers said UNICEF ambassadors should meet the "highest standards of neutrality and compassion" when addressing the [impact of conflict on children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children_in_the_Gaza_war "Children in the Gaza war").[*[needs update](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")*] Actor [Javier Bardem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_Bardem "Javier Bardem") shared multiple posts on [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram "Instagram") in support of the petition, including posts encouraging his followers to sign a [Change.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Change.org "Change.org") petition calling for Pink's dismissal as a UNICEF ambassador.

## Other ventures

### Two Wolves Wine

In 2013, Pink purchased an 17-acre [organic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_farming "Organic farming") vineyard in [Santa Barbara County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Barbara_County,_California "Santa Barbara County, California"), which included [cabernet sauvignon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabernet_Sauvignon "Cabernet Sauvignon"), [grenache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenache "Grenache"), [graciano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graciano "Graciano"), [syrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrah "Syrah"), [petit verdot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petit_Verdot "Petit Verdot"), [grenache blanc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenache_blanc "Grenache blanc"), and [cabernet franc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabernet_Franc "Cabernet Franc"). In 2015, she added 8 acres and additionally planted [sémillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9millon "Sémillon") and [merlot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merlot "Merlot"). Though a high-school dropout, the singer signed up for online [Wine and Spirit Educational Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine_%26_Spirit_Education_Trust "Wine & Spirit Education Trust") courses and studied [oenology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oenology "Oenology") and [viticulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viticulture "Viticulture") at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles "University of California, Los Angeles") and [University of California, Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Davis "University of California, Davis"). The first non-commercial [vintage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vintage "Vintage") was released in 2014.

In 2018, she officially launched her own brand of wine called "Two Wolves". The [winery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winery "Winery") is being run by five women. All of the wines are [single varietals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varietal "Varietal"). The wine is also available at select restaurants in [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California "California"), [Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois "Illinois"), [Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montana "Montana"), [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_(state) "New York (state)"), [Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon "Oregon"), [Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee "Tennessee"), [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia"), [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom") and [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium "Belgium").

## Legacy

She has been called "the most trailblazing artist from the famous teen pop class of circa 1999." Robert Hilburn of the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")* says, "Pink stood up for her music, broke the music industry's mold and scored a breakout hit, challenging a school of teen singers to find their own sounds as well." He adds, "[Pink] also started a race among other teen pop stars like [Christina Aguilera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christina_Aguilera "Christina Aguilera") to add substance to their own sound." [Ann Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Powers "Ann Powers") refers to her as a "powerhouse vocalist", stating her mix of rebellion, emotional rawness, humor, and "infectious" dance beats created "a model for the mashup approach of latter-day divas such as Katy Perry, Kesha, and [Rihanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rihanna "Rihanna")." [Rob Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Sheffield "Rob Sheffield") of *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")* commented: "I think people respond to her sense of independence and dedication. It inspires people... This is a prolific pop artist who is sometimes famous and successful, sometimes obscure, who nonetheless keeps making her own kind of music."

James Montgomery of [MTV News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_News "MTV News") describes her as "a fabulously fearless pop artist" who can "out-sing almost anyone out there. She can out-crazy Gaga or [Lily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lily_Allen "Lily Allen"). She's the total pop-star package, everything you'd want in a singer/entertainer/icon. And still, she remains oddly off the radar. Such is the price of busting borders". *[Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")* said: "She essentially invented the whole modern wave of Pop Diva Domination: You can draw a straight line from 'Get This Party Started' to Katy Perry, Kesha, pre-messianic [Lady Gaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Gaga "Lady Gaga"), and post-weird Rihanna." *[Glamour Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glamour_Magazine "Glamour Magazine")* wrote: "When Pennsylvania-born Alecia Moore debuted in 2000, pop was dominated by long-locked blonds like Britney Spears, Christina Aguilera and [Jessica Simpson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Simpson "Jessica Simpson"). Pink changed the game. Without her, the last 13 years of big-voiced, tough chick music is hard to imagine."[*[better source needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")*]

British soul singer [Adele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adele "Adele") considers Pink's performance at [Brixton Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brixton_Academy "Brixton Academy") in London one of "the most defining moments" in her life, saying, "It was the *[Missundaztood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missundaztood "Missundaztood")* record, so I was about 13 or 14. I had never heard, being in the room, someone sing like that live. I remember sort of feeling like I was in a wind tunnel, her voice just hitting me. It was incredible."

Pink's work has inspired many other artists, including [Christina Aguilera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christina_Aguilera "Christina Aguilera"), [Demi Lovato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demi_Lovato "Demi Lovato"), Kelly Clarkson, Katy Perry, [Tegan and Sara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tegan_and_Sara "Tegan and Sara"), [Ashley Tisdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashley_Tisdale "Ashley Tisdale"), [Alessia Cara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessia_Cara "Alessia Cara"), [Victoria Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Justice "Victoria Justice"), [Adele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adele "Adele"), [Julia Michaels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Michaels "Julia Michaels"), Ben Hopkins of [Pwr Bttm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pwr_Bttm "Pwr Bttm"), [Dua Lipa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dua_Lipa "Dua Lipa"), [Bebe Rexha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bebe_Rexha "Bebe Rexha"), [Halsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halsey_(singer) "Halsey (singer)"), [Anne-Marie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne-Marie "Anne-Marie"), [Kehlani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kehlani "Kehlani"), [Chappell Roan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chappell_Roan "Chappell Roan") and [Daya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daya_(singer) "Daya (singer)").

Aside from her music, Pink has been noted for her fashion style, such as her "adventurous" hairstyles, which have ranged from fluorescent spikes to pink-streaked [dreadlocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreadlocks "Dreadlocks") to a pitch-black skater cut. *[Billboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_(magazine) "Billboard (magazine)")* described her style as "androgynous" and "gender-bending". She told *[InStyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InStyle "InStyle")*, "I'm eclectic. I'm a tomboy, but I'm kind of a hippie and kind of a gangster ... I don't know if that's a good thing, but it is my thing."

## Personal life

Pink met professional [motocross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motocross "Motocross") racer [Carey Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carey_Hart "Carey Hart") at the 2001 [X Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_Games "X Games") in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia"). Following a brief separation in 2003, Pink proposed to Hart in June 2005 during a [Mammoth Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammoth_Lakes,_California "Mammoth Lakes, California") motocross race; she was "assisting" in his race and wrote "Will U Marry Me?" on a pit board. He either did not notice or ignored it and continued for another lap. Then Pink added "Serious!" to the board, and he pulled off the track to accept. She then made him finish the race. They married in [Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rica "Costa Rica") on January 7, 2006.

After months of speculation, Pink announced in February 2008 that she and Hart had separated. Hart subsequently appeared in the video for her 2008 song "[So What](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/So_What_(Pink_song) "So What (Pink song)")", which deals with their separation. The couple sought [marriage counseling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage_counseling "Marriage counseling") during their separation in hopes of reconciliation. In May 2009, Pink confirmed that she and Hart were back together. Hart also appears with Pink in the videos for her songs "Just Like a Pill" (from her 2001 album *Missundaztood*), "Just Give Me a Reason", "True Love" (both from her 2012 album *The Truth About Love*), "[Just Like Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_Like_Fire "Just Like Fire")" (from the soundtrack to the 2016 film *[Alice Through the Looking Glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Through_the_Looking_Glass_(2016_film) "Alice Through the Looking Glass (2016 film)")*), "[90 Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/90_Days_(Pink_song) "90 Days (Pink song)")" (from her 2019 album *Hurts 2B Human*) and "[All I Know So Far](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_I_Know_So_Far_(song) "All I Know So Far (song)")" (from her 2021 live album *[All I Know So Far: Setlist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_I_Know_So_Far:_Setlist "All I Know So Far: Setlist")*). *[People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_(magazine) "People (magazine)")* and *[Us Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Us_Weekly "Us Weekly")* incorrectly claimed that Pink and Hart separated again in February 2026; the singer criticized both outlets for spreading [misinformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misinformation "Misinformation").

In November 2010, Pink announced on *[The Ellen DeGeneres Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ellen_DeGeneres_Show "The Ellen DeGeneres Show")* that she and Hart were expecting their first child. In June 2011, she gave birth to their daughter, Willow Sage. In 2018, Willow released a cover of the reprise of "[A Million Dreams (Reprise)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Million_Dreams#Reprise "A Million Dreams")" for *[The Greatest Showman: Reimagined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Greatest_Showman_(soundtrack)#The_Greatest_Showman:_Reimagined "The Greatest Showman (soundtrack)")* soundtrack, and in 2021, she released "[Cover Me in Sunshine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cover_Me_in_Sunshine "Cover Me in Sunshine")" with her mother. In December 2016, Pink gave birth to their second child, a son. Pink is a supporter of [attachment parenting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attachment_parenting "Attachment parenting").

In 2019, Pink revealed to [Carson Daly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carson_Daly "Carson Daly") that she struggles with [anxiety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anxiety "Anxiety") and depression. The singer had a [miscarriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscarriage "Miscarriage") when she was 17. She revealed that her song "[Long Way to Happy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%27m_Not_Dead "I'm Not Dead")" was written about surviving [sexual abuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_abuse "Sexual abuse") by a [police officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_officer "Police officer") when she was a teen.

On April 4, 2020, amid the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"), Pink announced that she and her three-year-old son showed symptoms for [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19 "COVID-19") and she subsequently tested positive, but fully recovered. She has had [asthma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asthma "Asthma") her whole life, and the initial days of fighting the virus exacerbated her condition.

## Achievements

Pink has three [Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Awards "Grammy Awards"), two [Brit Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brit_Awards "Brit Awards") (including [Outstanding Contribution to Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brit_Award_for_Outstanding_Contribution_to_Music "Brit Award for Outstanding Contribution to Music")), a [Daytime Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daytime_Emmy_Award "Daytime Emmy Award") and seven [MTV Video Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Award "MTV Video Music Award") (including the [Michael Jackson Video Vanguard Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jackson_Video_Vanguard_Award "Michael Jackson Video Vanguard Award")). *[Billboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_(magazine) "Billboard (magazine)")* named her Woman of the Year in 2013. Pink received the BMI President's Award for "her outstanding achievement in songwriting and global impact on pop culture and the entertainment industry." In 2019, the singer received a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame "Hollywood Walk of Fame"). She was also named as *[Pollstar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollstar "Pollstar")*'s 2019 Artist of the Year. Following the success of [The Beautiful Trauma Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beautiful_Trauma_World_Tour "Beautiful Trauma World Tour"), Pink accepted the Legend of Live Award at the Billboard Live Music Summit. Three years later, Pink received the [Icon Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Icon_Award "Billboard Icon Award") at the [*Billboard* Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Music_Awards "Billboard Music Awards").

In 2023, [iHeartRadio Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHeartRadio_Music_Awards "IHeartRadio Music Awards") awarded the singer with the Icon Award to recognize her "impact on pop culture, longevity and continued relevance as a touring and radio force with a loyal fan base worldwide." She was additionally honored by the [Songwriters of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songwriters_of_North_America "Songwriters of North America") for her achievement in the industry and her activism efforts. Pink has also been nominated for the [Songwriters Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songwriters_Hall_of_Fame "Songwriters Hall of Fame") and [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame "Rock and Roll Hall of Fame"). In 2026, the singer received the [NMPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Music_Publishers%27_Association "National Music Publishers' Association") Songwriter Icon Award.

According to [Nielsen SoundScan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_SoundScan "Nielsen SoundScan"), Pink's record sales stand at 16 million copies in the United States. Pink has sold over 60 million albums and 75 million singles worldwide, making her one of the [world's best-selling music artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_best-selling_music_artists "List of best-selling music artists"). *Billboard* included the singer in its list of Greatest of All Time Pop Songs Artists (2018) at number two and named her one of the 100 most successful artists of the 2010s by including her in its Top Artists of the 2010s Chart (2019). She was also ranked at number 7 on the magazine's 2025 "Top 100 Women Artists of the 21st Century" list and chosen as an honorable mention for their "The 25 Greatest Pop Stars of the 21st Century" list. *Billboard* additionally included her second album *Missundaztood* among the Greatest of All Time *Billboard* 200 Albums (2015) list at number 157. [Phonographic Performance Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonographic_Performance_Limited "Phonographic Performance Limited") (PPL) announced in 2021, that Pink was the most-played female artist of the 21st century in the United Kingdom. In 2025, *Pollstar* ranked her at number 11 on their "Most Popular Touring Artists Of The Millennium" list and number 2 on their "The Millennium's Most Popular Women In Touring" list.
