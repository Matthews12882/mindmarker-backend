# Human history

**Human history**, or **world history**, is the record of [humankind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human "Human") from [prehistory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory "Prehistory") to the [present](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Present "Present"). [Modern humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_human "Early modern human") evolved in [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa "Africa") around 300,000 years ago and initially lived as [hunter-gatherers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer "Hunter-gatherer"). They [migrated out of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_expansions_of_hominins_out_of_Africa "Early expansions of hominins out of Africa") during the [Last Ice Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Ice_Age "Last Ice Age"), spreading to every continent except [Antarctica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctica "Antarctica") by its end, 12,000 years ago. Soon afterwards, the [Neolithic Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_Revolution "Neolithic Revolution") in [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia "West Asia") included the first systematic [husbandry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture "Agriculture") of plants and animals, and saw many humans transition from [nomadic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomad "Nomad") lives to [sedentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedentism "Sedentism") existences as farmers in [permanent settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization "Civilization"). The growing complexity of human societies necessitated systems of [accounting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accounting "Accounting") and [writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writing "Writing").

[Early civilizations then emerged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cradle_of_civilization "Cradle of civilization") in [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia "Mesopotamia"), [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt "Ancient Egypt"), [Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caral%E2%80%93Supe_civilization "Caral–Supe civilization"), the [Indus Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley "Indus Valley"), and [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_China "History of China"), marking the beginning of the [ancient period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_period "Ancient period") in the 4th millennium BCE. These civilizations enabled the establishment of regional empires and provided fertile ground for transformative philosophical and religious ideas. [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism") originated during the late [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age "Bronze Age") and was followed by the many seminal belief systems of the [Axial Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_Age "Axial Age"): [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"), [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism "Confucianism"), [Greek philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_philosophy "Greek philosophy"), [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism "Jainism"), [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism "Judaism"), [Taoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism "Taoism"), and [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism "Zoroastrianism"). [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity") began later as an offshoot of Judaism. The subsequent [post-classical period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-classical_history "Post-classical history"), from about 500 to 1500 CE, included the rise of [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam") and the flourishing of China under the [Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty "Tang dynasty") and [Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty "Song dynasty") dynasties, while civilization expanded to new parts of the world and trade between societies increased. Major empires rose and fell, such as the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire "Byzantine Empire"), the Islamic [caliphates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate "Caliphate"), and the [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire "Mongol Empire"). This period's invention of [gunpowder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunpowder "Gunpowder") and the [printing press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing_press "Printing press") greatly affected later history.

During the [early modern period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_period "Early modern period"), from approximately 1500 to 1800 CE, [European powers explored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery "Age of Discovery") and [colonized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonized "Colonized") regions worldwide, intensifying cultural and economic exchange. This era's intellectual, cultural, and technological changes in Europe included the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance "Renaissance"), [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation "Reformation"), [Scientific Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_Revolution "Scientific Revolution"), and [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment "Age of Enlightenment"). By the 18th century, the accumulation of knowledge and technology had reached a [critical mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_mass_(sociodynamics) "Critical mass (sociodynamics)") that brought about the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution "Industrial Revolution"), underpinning the [Great Divergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Divergence "Great Divergence") and ushering in the [modern period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_period "Modern period") starting around 1800 CE. The rapid growth in productive power further increased [international trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_trade "International trade") and [colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonization "Colonization"), linking the different civilizations in the process of [globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization "Globalization") and cementing European dominance throughout the 19th century. Over the last 250 years, which included two devastating [world wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_war "World war"), there has been a great acceleration in many spheres, including [human population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth "Population growth"), agriculture, industry, commerce, scientific knowledge, technology, communications, military capabilities, and [environmental degradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_degradation "Environmental degradation").

The study of human history relies on insights from academic disciplines such as [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History "History"), [archeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology "Archaeology"), [anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology "Anthropology"), [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics "Linguistics"), and [genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics "Genetics"). To provide an accessible overview, researchers divide human history by a variety of periodizations.

## Prehistory

### Human origins

Humans evolved in Africa from [great apes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_apes "Great apes") through the lineage of [hominins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominins "Hominins"), which arose 7–5 million years ago. Unlike other primates, hominins developed [bipedalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipedalism "Bipedalism"), the ability to walk on two legs. Hominins began to use rudimentary stone tools c. 3.3 million years ago, marking the advent of the [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic "Paleolithic") era. Early hominin evolution coincided with climatic changes in Africa that made the continent drier, colder, and less forested. These changes, and especially the cycle of alternating [glacial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial_period "Glacial period") and [interglacial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interglacial "Interglacial") periods that began 3.2 million years ago, may have been key drivers of human evolution.

The genus *[Homo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo "Homo")* evolved from *[Australopithecus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australopithecus "Australopithecus")*. The earliest record of *Homo* is a 2.8 million-year-old [jawbone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LD_350-1 "LD 350-1") from Ethiopia, and the earliest named species is *[Homo habilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_habilis "Homo habilis")*, which evolved by 2.3 million years ago. The most important difference between *Homo habilis* and *Australopithecus* was a 50% increase in brain size. *[H. erectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._erectus "H. erectus")* evolved about 2 million years ago and was the first hominin species to [leave Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_expansions_of_hominins_out_of_Africa "Early expansions of hominins out of Africa") and disperse across Eurasia. Perhaps as early as 1.5 million years ago, but certainly by 400,000 years ago, hominins [began to use fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_of_fire_by_early_humans "Control of fire by early humans") for heat and cooking.

Beginning about 600,000 years ago, *Homo* diversified into several new species, first *[H. heidelbergensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_heidelbergensis "Homo heidelbergensis")* in Africa and Europe and then the [Neanderthals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neanderthal "Neanderthal") in Europe and the [Denisovans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denisovan "Denisovan") in [Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberia "Siberia"). Human evolution was not a simple linear or branched progression but involved [interbreeding between related species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interbreeding_between_archaic_and_modern_humans "Interbreeding between archaic and modern humans"). Neanderthals, Denisovans, *Homo sapiens*, and other unidentified hominins all interbred with one another and [hybridized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_(biology) "Hybrid (biology)").

### Early humans

*Homo sapiens* evolved in Africa around 300,000 years ago from *H. heidelbergensis*. Humans continued to develop over the succeeding millennia and became [anatomically modern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_human "Early modern human") by 125,000 years ago. By 100,000 years ago, they buried their dead, wore jewelry, and adorned the body with red [ochre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ochre "Ochre"). One of the most important changes (the date of which is unknown) was the [development of syntactic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_of_language "Origin of language"), which dramatically improved the human ability to communicate.

Paleolithic humans lived as [hunter-gatherers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer "Hunter-gatherer") and were generally [nomadic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomad "Nomad"). Signs of early artistic expression can be found in the form of [cave paintings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_painting "Cave painting") and sculptures made from ivory, stone, and bone. The cave paintings suggest a form of spirituality generally interpreted as [animism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animism "Animism") or [shamanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamanism "Shamanism"). The earliest known musical instruments besides the human voice are [bone flutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone_flutes "Bone flutes") from the [Swabian Jura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caves_and_Ice_Age_Art_in_the_Swabian_Jura "Caves and Ice Age Art in the Swabian Jura") in Germany, dated around 40,000 years old.

Humans [migrated out of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recent_African_origin_of_modern_humans "Recent African origin of modern humans") in multiple waves beginning 194,000–177,000 years ago. The [dominant view among scholars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Dispersal "Southern Dispersal") is that the early waves of migration died out, and all modern non-Africans are descended from a single group that left Africa 70,000–50,000 years ago. *H. sapiens* proceeded to colonize all the continents and larger islands, arriving in [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory_of_Australia "Prehistory of Australia") 65,000 years ago, [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_Europe "Prehistoric Europe") 45,000 years ago, and the [Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peopling_of_the_Americas "Peopling of the Americas") 21,000 years ago. These migrations occurred during the [most recent Ice Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Period "Last Glacial Period"), when various temperate regions of today were inhospitable. Nevertheless, by the end of the Ice Age some 12,000 years ago, humans had colonized nearly all ice-free parts of the globe. Human expansion coincided with both the [Quaternary extinction event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternary_extinction_event "Quaternary extinction event") and the [Neanderthal extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neanderthal_extinction "Neanderthal extinction"). These extinctions were probably caused by climate change, human activity, or a combination of the two.

### Neolithic

Beginning around 10,000 BCE, the [Neolithic Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_Revolution "Neolithic Revolution") marked the development of [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture "Agriculture"), which fundamentally changed the human lifestyle. Agriculture began independently in different parts of the globe, and included a diverse range of [taxa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxa "Taxa"), in at least 11 separate [centers of origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centers_of_origin "Centers of origin"). [Cereal crop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cereal_crop "Cereal crop") cultivation and [animal domestication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_domestication "Animal domestication") had occurred in [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia "Mesopotamia") by at least 8500 BCE in the form of wheat, [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley "Barley"), sheep, and goats. The [Yangtze River Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River_Valley "Yangtze River Valley") in China domesticated rice around 8000–7000 BCE; the [Yellow River Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River_Valley "Yellow River Valley") may have cultivated [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet "Millet") by 7000 BCE. Pigs were the most important domesticated animal in early China. People in Africa's [Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara "Sahara") cultivated [sorghum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorghum "Sorghum") and several other crops between 8000 and 5000 BCE, while other agricultural centers arose in the [Ethiopian Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Highlands "Ethiopian Highlands") and the West African rainforests. In the [Indus River Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River_Valley "Indus River Valley"), crops were cultivated by 7000 BCE, and cattle were domesticated by 6500 BCE. In the Americas, [squash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cucurbita "Cucurbita") was cultivated by at least 8500 BCE in South America, and domesticated [arrowroot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrowroot "Arrowroot") appeared in Central America by 7800 BCE. Potatoes were first cultivated in the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes "Andes") of South America, where the [llama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llama "Llama") was also domesticated. It is likely that women played a central role in plant domestication throughout these developments.

Various explanations of the causes of the Neolithic Revolution have been proposed. Some theories identify population growth as the main factor, leading people to seek out new food sources. Others see population growth not as the cause but as the effect of the associated improvements in food supply. Further suggested factors include climate change, resource scarcity, and ideology. The transition to agriculture created food surpluses that could support people not directly engaged in food production, permitting far denser populations and the creation of the first cities and [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_(polity) "State (polity)").

Cities were centers of [trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade "Trade"), [manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing "Manufacturing"), and [political power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_power "Political power"). They developed mutually beneficial relationships with the inhabitants of the surrounding [countrysides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_area "Rural area"), receiving agricultural products and providing manufactured goods and varying degrees of political control in return. [Pastoral societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastoral_society "Pastoral society") based on nomadic animal herding also developed, mostly in dry areas unsuited to plant cultivation, such as the [Eurasian Steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Steppe "Eurasian Steppe") and the African [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel "Sahel"). Conflict between nomadic herders and [sedentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedentism "Sedentism") agriculturalists was frequent and became a recurring theme in world history.

[Metalworking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metalworking "Metalworking") was first used in the creation of copper tools and ornaments around 6400 BCE. Gold and silver soon followed, primarily for use in ornaments. The first signs of [bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze "Bronze"), an alloy of copper and [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin "Tin"), date to around 4500 BCE, but the alloy was not widely used until the 3rd millennium BCE.

## Ancient

### Cradles of civilization

The Bronze Age saw the development of cities and [civilizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization "Civilization"). Early civilizations arose close to rivers, first in Mesopotamia (3300 BCE) along the [Tigris and Euphrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigris%E2%80%93Euphrates_river_system "Tigris–Euphrates river system"), followed by the [Egyptian civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt "Ancient Egypt") along the [Nile River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile_River "Nile River") (3200 BCE), the [Norte Chico civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norte_Chico_civilization "Norte Chico civilization") in coastal [Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru "Peru") (3100 BCE), the [Indus Valley Civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation "Indus Valley Civilisation") in Pakistan and northwestern India (2500 BCE), and the [Chinese civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_China "Ancient China") along the [Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze "Yangtze") and [Yellow Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River "Yellow River") (2200 BCE).

These societies developed a number of shared characteristics, including a central government, a complex economy and social structure, and systems for keeping records. These cultures variously invented the [wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel#History "Wheel"), [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics"), bronze-working, sailing boats, the [potter's wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potter%27s_wheel "Potter's wheel"), [woven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaving "Weaving") cloth, construction of monumental buildings, and [writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_writing "History of writing"). [Polytheistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytheistic "Polytheistic") religions developed, centered on temples where [priests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priest "Priest") and priestesses performed [sacrificial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacrificial "Sacrificial") rites.

Writing facilitated the administration of cities, the expression of ideas, and the preservation of information. It may have independently developed in at least four ancient civilizations: Mesopotamia (3300 BCE), Egypt (around 3250 BCE), China (1200 BCE), and lowland [Mesoamerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica "Mesoamerica") (by 650 BCE). The earliest system of writing was the Mesopotamian [cuneiform script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuneiform_script "Cuneiform script"), which began as a system of [pictographs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pictographs "Pictographs"), whose pictorial representations eventually became simplified and more abstract. Other influential early writing systems include [Egyptian hieroglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_hieroglyphs "Egyptian hieroglyphs") and the [Indus script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_script "Indus script"). In China, writing was first used during the [Shang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang_dynasty "Shang dynasty") (1766–1045 BCE).

Transport was facilitated by waterways, including rivers and seas, which fostered the projection of military power and the exchange of goods, ideas, and inventions. The Bronze Age also saw new land technologies, such as horse-based [cavalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavalry "Cavalry") and [chariots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chariot "Chariot"), that allowed [armies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armies "Armies") to move faster. Trade became increasingly important as urban societies exchanged manufactured goods for raw materials from distant lands, creating vast commercial networks and the beginnings of [archaic globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaic_globalization "Archaic globalization"). Bronze production in Southwest Asia, for example, required the import of tin from as far away as England.

The growth of cities was often followed by the establishment of states and empires. In Egypt, the initial division into [Upper and Lower Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_and_Lower_Egypt "Upper and Lower Egypt") was followed by the unification of the whole valley around 3100 BCE. Around 2600 BCE, the Indus Valley civilization built major cities at [Harappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harappa "Harappa") and [Mohenjo-daro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohenjo-daro "Mohenjo-daro"). Mesopotamian history was characterized by frequent wars between city-states, leading to shifts in [hegemony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegemony "Hegemony") from one city to another. In the 25th–21st centuries BCE, the empires of [Akkad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_Empire "Akkadian Empire") and the [Neo-Sumerians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Dynasty_of_Ur "Third Dynasty of Ur") arose in this area. In Crete, the [Minoan civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoan_civilization "Minoan civilization") emerged by 2000 BCE and is regarded as the first civilization in Europe.

Over the following millennia, civilizations developed across the world. By 1600 BCE, [Mycenaean Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenaean_Greece "Mycenaean Greece") began to develop. It flourished until the [Late Bronze Age collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Bronze_Age_collapse "Late Bronze Age collapse") that affected many Mediterranean civilizations between 1300 and 1000 BCE. The foundations of many cultural aspects in India were laid in the [Vedic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_period "Vedic period") (1750–600 BCE), including the emergence of [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism"). From around 550 BCE, many independent kingdoms and republics known as the [Mahajanapadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahajanapadas "Mahajanapadas") were established across the subcontinent.

Speakers of the [Bantu languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_languages "Bantu languages") began [expanding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_expansion "Bantu expansion") across Central, Eastern, and Southern Africa as early as 3000 BCE until 1000 CE. Their expansion and encounters with other groups resulted in the displacement of the [Pygmy peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Pygmies "African Pygmies") and the [Khoisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoisan "Khoisan"), and in the spread of [mixed farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_farming "Mixed farming") and [ironworking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironworking "Ironworking") throughout sub-Saharan Africa, laying the foundations for later states.

The [Lapita culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapita_culture "Lapita culture") emerged in the [Bismarck Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bismarck_Archipelago "Bismarck Archipelago") near [New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Guinea "New Guinea") around 1500 BCE and colonized many uninhabited islands of [Remote Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_Oceania "Remote Oceania"), reaching as far as [Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samoa "Samoa") by 700 BCE.

In the Americas, the Norte Chico culture emerged in Peru around 3100 BCE. The Norte Chico built public monumental architecture at the city of [Caral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caral "Caral"), dated 2627–1977 BCE. The later [Chavín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chav%C3%ADn "Chavín") polity is sometimes described as the first [Andean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andean_civilizations "Andean civilizations") state, centered on the religious site at [Chavín de Huántar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chav%C3%ADn_de_Hu%C3%A1ntar "Chavín de Huántar"). Other important Andean cultures include the [Moche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_culture "Moche culture"), whose ceramics depict many aspects of daily life, and the [Nazca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazca_culture "Nazca culture"), who created animal-shaped designs in the desert called [Nazca lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazca_lines "Nazca lines"). The [Olmecs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olmecs "Olmecs") of Mesoamerica developed by about 1200 BCE and are known for the [colossal stone heads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olmec_colossal_heads "Olmec colossal heads") that they carved from [basalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basalt "Basalt"). They also devised the [Mesoamerican calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerican_calendar "Mesoamerican calendar") that was used by later cultures such as the [Maya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_civilization "Maya civilization") and [Teotihuacan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teotihuacan "Teotihuacan"). Societies in North America were primarily egalitarian hunter-gatherers, supplementing their diet with the plants of the [Eastern Agricultural Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Agricultural_Complex "Eastern Agricultural Complex"). They built earthworks such as [Watson Brake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watson_Brake "Watson Brake") (4000 BCE) and [Poverty Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_Point "Poverty Point") (3600 BCE), both in Louisiana.

### Axial Age

From 800 to 200 BCE, the Axial Age saw the emergence of transformative philosophical and religious ideas that developed in many different places mostly independently of each other. Chinese [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism "Confucianism"), Indian [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism") and [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism "Jainism"), and Jewish [monotheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotheism "Monotheism") all arose during this period. Persian [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism "Zoroastrianism") began earlier, perhaps around 1000 BCE, but was institutionalized by the [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire "Achaemenid Empire") during the Axial Age. [New philosophies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_philosophy "Ancient Greek philosophy") took hold in Greece during the 5th century BCE, epitomized by thinkers such as [Plato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plato "Plato") and [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle "Aristotle"). The first [Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Olympic_Games "Ancient Olympic Games") were held in 776 BCE, marking a period known as "[classical antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_antiquity "Classical antiquity")". In 508 BCE, [the world's first democratic system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_democracy "History of democracy") of government was instituted in [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athenian_democracy "Athenian democracy").

Axial Age ideas shaped subsequent intellectual and religious history. Confucianism was one of the three schools of thought that came to dominate Chinese thinking, along with [Taoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism "Taoism") and [Legalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legalism_(Chinese_philosophy) "Legalism (Chinese philosophy)"). The Confucian tradition, which would become particularly influential, looked for [political morality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_ethics "Political ethics") not to the force of law but to the power and example of tradition. Confucianism would later spread to [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea "Korea") and Japan. Buddhism reached China in about the 1st century CE and spread widely, with 30,000 Buddhist temples in northern China alone by the 7th century CE. Buddhism became the main religion in much of South, Southeast, and East Asia. The Greek philosophical tradition diffused throughout the Mediterranean world and as far as India, starting in the 4th century BCE after the conquests of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great "Alexander the Great") of [Macedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedon "Macedon"). Both [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity") and [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam") developed from the beliefs of [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism "Judaism").

### Regional empires

The millennium from 500 BCE to 500 CE saw a series of empires of unprecedented size develop. Well-trained professional armies, unifying ideologies, and advanced bureaucracies created the possibility for emperors to rule over large domains whose populations could attain numbers upwards of tens of millions of subjects. [International trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_trade "International trade") also expanded, most notably the massive trade routes in the Mediterranean Sea, the [maritime trade web in the Indian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean_trade "Indian Ocean trade"), and the [Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road "Silk Road").

The kingdom of the [Medes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medes "Medes") helped to destroy the [Assyrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_Empire "Assyrian Empire") in tandem with the nomadic [Scythians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythians "Scythians") and the [Babylonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonia "Babylonia"). [Nineveh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineveh "Nineveh"), the capital of Assyria, was sacked by the Medes in 612 BCE. The [Median Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_Empire "Median Empire") gave way to successive [Iranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Iran "History of Iran") states, including the [Achaemenid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid "Achaemenid") (550–330 BCE), [Parthian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthian_Empire "Parthian Empire") (247 BCE – 224 CE), and [Sasanian Empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire "Sasanian Empire") (224–651 CE).

Two major empires began in modern-day [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece "Greece"). In the late 5th century BCE, several Greek [city-states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_states "City states") checked the Achaemenid Persian advance in Europe through the [Greco-Persian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Persian_Wars "Greco-Persian Wars"). These wars were followed by the [Golden Age of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Age_of_Athens "Golden Age of Athens"), the seminal period of ancient Greece that laid many of the foundations of [Western civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_civilization "Western civilization"), including the [first theatrical performances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_of_ancient_Greece "Theater of ancient Greece"). The wars led to the creation of the [Delian League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delian_League "Delian League"), founded in 477 BCE, and eventually the [Athenian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athenian_Empire "Athenian Empire") (454–404 BCE), which was defeated by a Spartan-led coalition during the [Peloponnesian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnesian_War "Peloponnesian War"). [Philip of Macedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_of_Macedon "Philip of Macedon") unified the Greek city-states into the [Hellenic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Corinth "League of Corinth"), and his son Alexander the Great (356–323 BCE) founded an empire extending from present-day Greece to India. The empire divided into several [successor states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diadochi "Diadochi") shortly after his death, resulting in the founding of many cities and the spread of Greek culture throughout conquered regions, a process referred to as [Hellenization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenization "Hellenization"). The [Hellenistic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period "Hellenistic period") lasted from the death of Alexander in 323 BCE until 31 BCE, when [Ptolemaic Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_Egypt "Ptolemaic Egypt") fell to Rome.

In Europe, the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic "Roman Republic") was founded in the 6th century BCE and began expanding its territory in the 3rd century BCE. Prior to this, the [Carthaginian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthaginian_Empire "Carthaginian Empire") had dominated the Mediterranean; however, it lost [three successive wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic_Wars "Punic Wars") to the Romans. The Republic became [an empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire "Roman Empire"), and by the time of [Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus "Augustus") (63 BCE – 14 CE), it had established dominion over most of the Mediterranean Sea. The empire continued to grow and reached its peak under [Trajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan "Trajan") (53–117 CE), controlling much of the land from England to Mesopotamia. The two centuries that followed are known as the *[Pax Romana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pax_Romana "Pax Romana")*, a period of unprecedented peace, prosperity, and political stability in most of Europe. Christianity was [legalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_the_Great_and_Christianity "Constantine the Great and Christianity") by [Constantine I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_I "Constantine I") in 313 CE after three centuries of [imperial persecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_early_Christians_in_the_Roman_Empire "Persecution of early Christians in the Roman Empire"). It became the sole official religion of the empire in 380 CE while the emperor [Theodosius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodosius_I "Theodosius I") outlawed [pagan religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paganism "Paganism") in 391–392 CE.

In South Asia, [Chandragupta Maurya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandragupta_Maurya "Chandragupta Maurya") founded the [Maurya Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire "Maurya Empire") (320–185 BCE), which flourished under [Ashoka the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka_the_Great "Ashoka the Great"). From the 4th to 6th centuries CE, the [Gupta Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire "Gupta Empire") oversaw the period referred to as ancient India's golden age. The resulting stability helped usher in a flourishing period for Hindu and Buddhist culture in the 4th and 5th centuries, as well as major advances in science and mathematics. In [South India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_India "South India"), three prominent [Dravidian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravidian_peoples "Dravidian peoples") kingdoms emerged: the [Cheras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheras "Cheras"), [Cholas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola_dynasty "Chola dynasty"), and [Pandyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandyas "Pandyas").

In China, [Qin Shi Huang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_Shi_Huang "Qin Shi Huang") put an end to the chaotic [Warring States period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warring_States_period "Warring States period") by uniting all of China under the [Qin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_dynasty "Qin dynasty") (221–206 BCE). Qin Shi Huang was an adherent of the Legalist school of thought, and he displaced the hereditary aristocracy by creating an efficient system of administration staffed by officials appointed according to merit. The harshness of the Qin dynasty led to rebellions and the dynasty's fall. It was followed by the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty "Han dynasty") (202 BCE – 220 CE), which combined the Legalist bureaucratic system with Confucian ideals. The Han dynasty was comparable in power and influence to the Roman Empire that lay at the other end of the Silk Road. As economic prosperity fueled their military expansion, the Han conquered parts of Mongolia, Central Asia, [Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchuria "Manchuria"), Korea, and northern Vietnam. As with other empires during the classical period, Han China advanced significantly in the areas of government, education, science, and technology. The Han invented the [compass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass "Compass"), one of China's [Four Great Inventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Great_Inventions "Four Great Inventions").

In Africa, the [Kingdom of Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kush "Kingdom of Kush") prospered through its interactions with both Egypt and sub-Saharan Africa. It ruled Egypt as the [Twenty-fifth Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-fifth_Dynasty "Twenty-fifth Dynasty") from 712 to 650 BCE, then continued as an agricultural and trading state based in the city of [Meroë](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mero%C3%AB "Meroë") until the fourth century CE. The [Kingdom of Aksum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aksum "Kingdom of Aksum"), centered in present-day Ethiopia, established itself by the 1st century CE as a major trading empire, dominating its neighbors in [South Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Arabia "South Arabia") and Kush and controlling the [Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea "Red Sea") trade. It minted its own currency and carved enormous monolithic [stelae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stelae "Stelae") to mark its emperors' graves.

Successful regional empires were also established in the Americas, arising from cultures established as early as 2500 BCE. In Mesoamerica, vast [pre-Columbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian "Pre-Columbian") societies were built, the most notable being the [Zapotec civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zapotec_civilization "Zapotec civilization") (700 BCE – 1521 CE), and the [Maya civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_civilization "Maya civilization"), which reached its highest state of development during the Mesoamerican classic period (c. 250–900 CE), but continued throughout the post-classic period. The great Maya [city-states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City-state "City-state") slowly rose in number and prominence, and Maya culture spread throughout the [Yucatán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yucat%C3%A1n_Peninsula "Yucatán Peninsula") and surrounding areas. The Maya developed [a writing system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_script "Maya script") and used the [concept of zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/0#Pre-Columbian_Americas "0") in their mathematics. West of the Maya area, in central Mexico, the city of [Teotihuacan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teotihuacan "Teotihuacan") prospered due to its control of the [obsidian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obsidian "Obsidian") trade. Its power peaked around 450 CE, when its 125,000–150,000 inhabitants made it one of the world's largest cities.

[Technology developed sporadically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_technology "History of technology") in the ancient world. There were periods of rapid technological progress, such as the Greco-Roman era in the Mediterranean region. [Greek science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_science "Greek science"), [technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_technology "Ancient Greek technology"), and [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mathematics "Greek mathematics") are generally considered to have reached their peak during the Hellenistic period, typified by devices such as the [Antikythera mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antikythera_mechanism "Antikythera mechanism"). There were also periods of technological decay, such as the Roman Empire's decline and fall and the ensuing early medieval period. Two of the most important innovations were paper (China, 1st and 2nd centuries CE) and the [stirrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stirrup "Stirrup") (India, 2nd century BCE and Central Asia, 1st century CE), both of which diffused widely throughout the world. The Chinese learned to make silk and built massive engineering projects such as the [Great Wall of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Wall_of_China "Great Wall of China") and the [Grand Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Canal_(China) "Grand Canal (China)"). The Romans were also accomplished builders, inventing [concrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concrete "Concrete"), perfecting the use of [arches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arch "Arch") in construction, and creating [aqueducts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_aqueduct "Roman aqueduct") to transport water over long distances to urban centers.

Most ancient societies practiced [slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery "Slavery"), which was particularly prevalent in [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_ancient_Greece "Slavery in ancient Greece") and [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_ancient_Rome "Slavery in ancient Rome"), where slaves made up a large proportion of the population and were foundational to the economy. [Patriarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchy "Patriarchy") was also common, with men controlling more political and economic power than women.

### Declines, falls, and resurgence

The ancient empires faced common problems associated with maintaining huge armies and supporting a central [bureaucracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureaucracy "Bureaucracy"). In Rome and Han China, the state began to decline, and [barbarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbarian "Barbarian") pressure on the frontiers hastened internal dissolution. The Han dynasty fell into civil war in 220 CE, beginning the [Three Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms "Three Kingdoms") period, while its Roman counterpart became increasingly decentralized and divided about the same time in what is known as the [Crisis of the Third Century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crisis_of_the_Third_Century "Crisis of the Third Century"). From the Eurasian steppe, [horse-based nomads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_nomads "Eurasian nomads") dominated a large part of the continent. The development of the stirrup and the use of [horse archers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_archers "Horse archers") made the nomads a constant threat to sedentary civilizations.

In the 4th century CE, the Roman Empire split into western and eastern regions, with usually separate emperors. The [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire "Western Roman Empire") [fell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Western_Roman_Empire "Fall of the Western Roman Empire") in 476 CE to German influence under [Odoacer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odoacer "Odoacer") in the [Migration Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_Period "Migration Period") of the [Germanic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_peoples "Germanic peoples"). The Eastern Roman Empire, known as the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire "Byzantine Empire"), was more long-lasting. In China, [dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynasties_of_China "Dynasties of China") rose and fell, but, in sharp contrast to the Mediterranean-European world, political unity was always eventually restored. After the fall of the [Eastern Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Han_dynasty "Eastern Han dynasty") and the demise of the Three Kingdoms, nomadic tribes from the north began to invade, causing many Chinese people to flee southward.

## Post-classical

The post-classical period, dated roughly from 500 to 1500 CE, was characterized by the rise and spread of major religions while civilization expanded to new parts of the world and trade between societies intensified. From the 10th to 13th centuries, the [Medieval Warm Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_Warm_Period "Medieval Warm Period") in the Northern Hemisphere aided agriculture and led to population growth in parts of Europe and Asia. It was followed by the [Little Ice Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Ice_Age "Little Ice Age"), which, along with the plagues of the 14th century, put downward pressure on the population of Eurasia. Major inventions of the period were [gunpowder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunpowder "Gunpowder"), guns, and printing, all of which originated in China.

The post-classical period encompasses the [early Muslim conquests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_conquests "Early Muslim conquests"), the [Islamic Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Golden_Age "Islamic Golden Age"), and the commencement and expansion of the [Arab slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_slave_trade "Arab slave trade"), followed by the [Mongol invasions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasions "Mongol invasions") and the founding of the Ottoman Empire. South Asia had a series of [middle kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_kingdoms_of_India "Middle kingdoms of India"), followed by the establishment of [Islamic empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_period_in_the_Indian_subcontinent "Muslim period in the Indian subcontinent") in India.

In West Africa, the [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_Empire "Mali Empire") and [Songhai Empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songhai_Empire "Songhai Empire") rose. On the southeast coast of Africa, Arabic ports were established where gold, [spices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice_trade "Spice trade"), and other commodities were traded. This allowed Africa to join the Southeast Asia trading system, bringing it into contact with Asia; this resulted in the [Swahili culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_culture "Swahili culture").

China experienced the relatively successive Sui, Tang, [Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty "Song dynasty"), [Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty "Yuan dynasty"), and early [Ming dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty "Ming dynasty"). Middle Eastern trade routes along the Indian Ocean, and the Silk Road through the [Gobi Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gobi_Desert "Gobi Desert"), provided limited economic and cultural contact between Asian and European civilizations. During the same period, civilizations in the Americas, such as the [Mississippians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippian_culture "Mississippian culture"), [Aztecs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec_Empire "Aztec Empire"), Maya, and [Inca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca "Inca") reached their zenith.

### West and Central Asia

Before the advent of Islam in the 7th century, the Middle East was dominated by the [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire "Byzantine Empire") and [Sasanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire "Sasanian Empire") Empires, which frequently fought each other for control of several disputed regions. The [birth of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Islam#Origins_of_Islam "History of Islam") created a new contender that quickly surpassed both of these empires. [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad "Muhammad"), the founder of Islam, initiated the [early Muslim conquests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_conquests "Early Muslim conquests") in the 7th century. The subsequent [Abbasid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate "Abbasid Caliphate") oversaw the Islamic Golden Age, an era of learning, science, and invention during which [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_philosophy "Islamic philosophy"), [art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_art "Islamic art"), and [literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_literature "Islamic literature") flourished. Islamic civilization expanded both by conquest and through its merchant economy. Merchants brought goods and their Islamic faith to [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_China "Islam in China"), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_India "Islam in India"), [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Southeast_Asia "Islam in Southeast Asia"), and [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Africa "Islam in Africa").

Arab domination of the Middle East ended in the mid-11th century with the arrival of the [Seljuk Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Turks "Seljuk Turks"), migrating south from the Turkic homelands. The Seljuks were challenged by Europe during the [Crusades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades "Crusades"), a series of religious wars aimed at rolling back Muslim territory and regaining control of the [Holy Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Land "Holy Land"). The Crusades were ultimately unsuccessful and served more to weaken the Byzantine Empire. The Turks founded the Ottoman Empire in modern-day Turkey around 1299. From the 10th century, the Abbasid Caliphate's African territory was consumed by the [Fatimid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimid_Caliphate "Fatimid Caliphate") centered on Egypt, who were supplanted by the [Ayyubids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayyubids "Ayyubids") in the 12th century, and then later by the [Mamluks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Sultanate "Mamluk Sultanate") in the 13th century. In the [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb "Maghreb") and [Western Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Sahara "Western Sahara"), the [Almoravids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almoravids "Almoravids") dominated from the 11th century, until it was subsumed by the [Almohad Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almohad_Caliphate "Almohad Caliphate") in the 12th century. The Almohads' collapse gave rise to the [Marinids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinids "Marinids") in Morocco, the [Zayyanids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Tlemcen "Kingdom of Tlemcen") in Algeria, and the [Hafsids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafsids "Hafsids") in Tunisia.

Steppe nomads from Central Asia, such as the [Karluks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karluks "Karluks") and [Kipchaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kipchaks "Kipchaks"), continued to threaten sedentary societies in the post-classical era, but they also faced incursions from the Arabs and Chinese. In the 8th century, Islam began to penetrate the region and soon became the sole faith of most of the population. From the 9th to 13th centuries, Central Asia was divided among several powerful states, including the [Samanid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samanid "Samanid"), [Seljuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Empire "Seljuk Empire"), and [Khwarazmian Empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khwarazmian_Empire "Khwarazmian Empire"). These states were succeeded by the Mongols in the 13th century. In 1370, [Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur "Timur") conquered most of the region and founded the [Timurid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_Empire "Timurid Empire"), which collapsed soon after his death. His descendants retained control of a core area in Central Asia and Iran, overseeing the [Timurid Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_Renaissance "Timurid Renaissance") of art and architecture.

### Europe

Since at least the 4th century, Christianity has played a [prominent role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Role_of_Christianity_in_civilization "Role of Christianity in civilization") in shaping the culture, values, and institutions of Western civilization, primarily through Catholicism and later also [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism "Protestantism"). Europe during the [Early Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Middle_Ages "Early Middle Ages") was characterized by depopulation, [deurbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deurbanization "Deurbanization"), and barbarian invasions, all of which had begun in [late antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_antiquity "Late antiquity"). The barbarian invaders formed their own new kingdoms in the remains of the Western Roman Empire. Christianity expanded in Western Europe, and monasteries were founded. In the 7th and 8th centuries, the [Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks "Franks") under the [Carolingian dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_dynasty "Carolingian dynasty") established an empire covering much of Western Europe; it lasted until the 9th century, when it succumbed to pressure from new invaders—the [Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikings "Vikings"), [Magyars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarians#Entering_the_Carpathian_Basin_(c._862–895) "Hungarians"), and Arabs. During the Carolingian era, churches developed a form of musical notation called [neume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neume "Neume"), which became the basis for the modern notation system. [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus%27 "Kievan Rus'") expanded from its capital in [Kiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev "Kiev") to become the largest state in Europe by the 10th century. In 988, [Vladimir the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_the_Great "Vladimir the Great") adopted [Orthodox Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church "Russian Orthodox Church") as the state religion.

During the [High Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Middle_Ages "High Middle Ages"), which began after 1000, the population of Europe increased as technological and agricultural innovations allowed trade to flourish and crop yields to increase. The establishment of the [feudal system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudal_system "Feudal system") affected the structure of medieval society. It included [manorialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manorialism "Manorialism"), the organization of peasants into villages that owed rents and labor service to nobles, and [vassalage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassal "Vassal"), a political structure whereby [knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight "Knight") and lower-status nobles owed military service to their overlords in return for the right to rents from lands and manors. Kingdoms became more centralized after the decentralizing effects of the breakup of the [Carolingian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_Empire "Carolingian Empire"). In 1054, the [Great Schism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East%E2%80%93West_Schism "East–West Schism") between the Catholic and Eastern Orthodox Churches led to the prominent cultural differences between Western and Eastern Europe. The [Crusades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades "Crusades") were a series of religious wars waged by Christians to wrest control of the Holy Land from the Muslims and succeeded for long enough to establish some [Crusader states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusader_states "Crusader states") in the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant "Levant"). Intellectual life was marked by [scholasticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholasticism "Scholasticism") and the [founding of universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_university "Medieval university"), while the building of [Gothic cathedrals and churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_cathedrals_and_churches "Gothic cathedrals and churches") was one of the outstanding artistic achievements of the age.

The Caucasus was fought over in a [series of wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman%E2%80%93Persian_Wars#Byzantine–Sasanian_wars "Roman–Persian Wars") between the Byzantine and Sasanian Empires. However, the two opposing powers became exhausted due to continuous conflict. Hence, the Rashidun Caliphate was able to freely expand into the region during the early Muslim conquests. The Seljuk Turks later subjugated [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagratid_Armenia "Bagratid Armenia") and [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Georgia "Kingdom of Georgia") in the 11th century. The Mongols subsequently invaded the Caucasus in the 13th century.

The [Mongols reached Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Europe "Mongol invasion of Europe") in 1236 and [conquered Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Kievan_Rus%27 "Mongol invasion of Kievan Rus'"), along with briefly invading [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Mongol_invasion_of_Poland "First Mongol invasion of Poland") and [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Mongol_invasion_of_Hungary "First Mongol invasion of Hungary"). [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania "Grand Duchy of Lithuania") cooperated with the Mongols but remained independent and, in the late 14th century, formed a [personal union with Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Krewo "Union of Krewo"). The [Late Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Middle_Ages "Late Middle Ages") were marked by difficulties and calamities. Famine, plague, and war devastated the population of Western Europe. The [Black Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Death "Black Death") alone killed approximately 75 to 200 million people between 1347 and 1350. It was one of the deadliest pandemics in human history. Starting in Asia, the disease reached the Mediterranean and Western Europe during the late 1340s, and killed tens of millions of Europeans in six years; between a quarter and a third of the population perished.

### Africa

Sub-Saharan Africa was home to many different civilizations. In [Nubia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubia "Nubia"), the [Kingdom of Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kush "Kingdom of Kush") was succeeded by the Christian kingdoms of [Makuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makuria "Makuria"), [Alodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alodia "Alodia"), and [Nobatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobatia "Nobatia"). In the 7th century, Makuria conquered Nobatia to become the dominant power in the region and [resisted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_battle_of_Dongola "First battle of Dongola") Muslim expansion. They later entered a severe decline following civil war and [Arab migrations to the Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamization_of_the_Sudan_region "Islamization of the Sudan region") and had disintegrated by the 15th century, giving rise to the [Funj Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funj_Sultanate "Funj Sultanate").
In the [Horn of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horn_of_Africa "Horn of Africa"), Islam spread among the [Somalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somalis "Somalis").

In the [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel "Sahel") region of West Africa, the [Ghana Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Empire "Ghana Empire") formed between the 2nd and 8th centuries, while from the 7th century the [Gao Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gao_Empire "Gao Empire") ruled to its east. Almoravid capture of royal [Aoudaghost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aoudaghost "Aoudaghost") led to Ghana's conversion to Islam in the 11th century, and climatic changes led to Ghana's conquest by its vassal [Sosso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sosso "Sosso") in the 13th century. Sosso was quickly overthrown by the [Mali Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_Empire "Mali Empire") who conquered Gao and dominated the [trans-Saharan trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Saharan_trade "Trans-Saharan trade"). The [Mossi Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mossi_Kingdoms "Mossi Kingdoms") were established to its south. To the east, the [Kanem–Bornu Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanem%E2%80%93Bornu_Empire "Kanem–Bornu Empire") ruled from the 6th century, and it projected power over the [Hausa Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausa_Kingdoms "Hausa Kingdoms"). The 15th century saw the crumbling of the Mali Empire, with the dominant power in the region becoming the [Songhai Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songhai_Empire "Songhai Empire") centered on [Gao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gao "Gao").

In the [forest regions of West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinean_forest%E2%80%93savanna_mosaic "Guinean forest–savanna mosaic"), various kingdoms and empires flourished, such as the [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_people "Yoruba people") empires of [Ife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ife_Empire "Ife Empire") and [Oyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyo_Empire "Oyo Empire"), the [Igbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igbo_people "Igbo people") [Kingdom of Nri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Nri "Kingdom of Nri"), the [Edo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_people "Edo people") [Kingdom of Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Benin "Kingdom of Benin") (famous for [its art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_of_the_Kingdom_of_Benin "Art of the Kingdom of Benin")), the [Dagomba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagomba_people "Dagomba people") [Kingdom of Dagbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Dagbon "Kingdom of Dagbon"), and the [Akan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akan_people "Akan people") kingdoms of [Bonoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonoman "Bonoman") and [Adanse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adanse "Adanse"). They came into contact with the Portuguese in the 15th century, which saw the start of the [Atlantic slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_slave_trade "Atlantic slave trade").

In the western [Congo Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Basin "Congo Basin"), by the 13th century, there were three main confederations of states: the [Seven Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Kingdoms_of_Kongo_dia_Nlaza "Seven Kingdoms of Kongo dia Nlaza"), [Mpemba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mpemba "Mpemba"), and one led by [Vungu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vungu "Vungu"). In the 14th century, the [Kingdom of Kongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kongo "Kingdom of Kongo") emerged and dominated the region. Further east, the [Luba Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luba_Empire "Luba Empire") was founded in the [Upemba Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upemba_Depression "Upemba Depression") in the 15th century. In the northern [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Great_Lakes "African Great Lakes"), the [Empire of Kitara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Kitara "Empire of Kitara") rose around the 11th century, famed for its [oral traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Kitara#Legends "Empire of Kitara"). It collapsed in the 15th century following [Luo migrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luo_peoples#Uganda "Luo peoples") to the region.

On the [Swahili coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_coast "Swahili coast"), the [Swahili city-states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_city-states "Swahili city-states") thrived off of the [Indian Ocean trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean_trade "Indian Ocean trade") and gradually Islamized, giving rise to the [Kilwa Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilwa_Sultanate "Kilwa Sultanate") from the 10th century. Madagascar was settled by [Austronesian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples "Austronesian peoples") between the 5th and 7th centuries, as societies organized at the behest of *[hasina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasina_(Madagascar) "Hasina (Madagascar)")*. In Southern Africa, an early kingdom was [Mapungubwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapungubwe "Mapungubwe"), followed by the [Kingdom of Great Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Zimbabwe "Kingdom of Great Zimbabwe") in the 13th century, and the [Mutapa Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutapa_Empire "Mutapa Empire") and [Kingdom of Butua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Butua "Kingdom of Butua") in the 15th century.

### South Asia

After the fall of the Gupta Empire in 550 CE, [North India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_India "North India") was divided into a complex and fluid network of smaller kingdoms. Early [Muslim incursions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquests_in_the_Indian_subcontinent "Muslim conquests in the Indian subcontinent") began in the northwest in 711 CE, when the Arab Umayyad Caliphate [conquered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_campaigns_in_India "Umayyad campaigns in India") much of present-day Pakistan. The Arab military advance was largely halted at that point, but Islam still spread in India, largely due to the influence of Arab merchants along the western coast. The 9th century saw the [Tripartite Struggle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripartite_Struggle "Tripartite Struggle") for control of North India between the [Pratihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratihara "Pratihara"), [Pala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pala_Empire "Pala Empire"), and [Rashtrakuta Empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtrakuta_dynasty "Rashtrakuta dynasty").

Post-classical dynasties in South India included those of the [Chalukyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalukyas "Chalukyas"), [Hoysalas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoysalas "Hoysalas"), and Cholas. Literature, architecture, sculpture, and painting flourished under the patronage of these kings. Some of the other important states that emerged in South India during this time included the [Bahmani Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahmani_Sultanate "Bahmani Sultanate") and the [Vijayanagara Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayanagara_Empire "Vijayanagara Empire").

### Northeast Asia

After a period of relative disunity, [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_China "History of China") was reunified by the Sui dynasty in 589, later succeeded by the Tang dynasty in 618. Both dynasties instituted the long-lasting [imperial examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_examination "Imperial examination") system. China later competed with [Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Empire "Tibetan Empire") (618–842) for control of areas in Inner Asia. However, the Tang dynasty eventually splintered. After [half a century of turmoil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Dynasties_and_Ten_Kingdoms_period "Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms period"), the Song dynasty reunified much of China. Pressure from nomadic empires to the north became increasingly urgent. By 1127, northern China had been lost to the [Jurchens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurchens "Jurchens") in the [Jin–Song Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin%E2%80%93Song_Wars "Jin–Song Wars"), and the Mongols [conquered all of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_conquest_of_China "Mongol conquest of China") in 1279. After about a century of Mongol Yuan dynasty rule, the ethnic Chinese reasserted control with the founding of the Ming dynasty in 1368.

In [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Japan "History of Japan"), the imperial lineage was established during the 3rd century CE, and a centralized state developed during the [Yamato period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamato_period "Yamato period") (c. 300–710). Buddhism was introduced, and there was an emphasis on the adoption of elements of Chinese culture and Confucianism. The [Nara period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nara_period "Nara period") (710–794) was characterized by the appearance of a nascent [literary culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_literature "Japanese literature"), as well as the development of Buddhist-inspired artwork and [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Monuments_of_Ancient_Nara "Historic Monuments of Ancient Nara"). The [Heian period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heian_period "Heian period") (794–1185) saw the peak of imperial power, followed by the rise of militarized clans and the [samurai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samurai "Samurai"). It was during the Heian period that [Murasaki Shikibu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murasaki_Shikibu "Murasaki Shikibu") penned *[The Tale of Genji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tale_of_Genji "The Tale of Genji")*, sometimes considered the world's first novel. From 1185 to 1868, Japan was dominated by powerful regional lords ([daimyos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daimyo "Daimyo")) and the military rule of warlords ([shoguns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shogun "Shogun")) such as the [Ashikaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashikaga_shogunate "Ashikaga shogunate") and [Tokugawa shogunates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokugawa_shogunate "Tokugawa shogunate"). The emperor remained but did not wield significant influence. Meanwhile, the power of merchants grew. An influential art style known as *[ukiyo-e](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukiyo-e "Ukiyo-e")* arose during the Tokugawa years, consisting of [woodblock prints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodblock_printing_in_Japan "Woodblock printing in Japan") which originally depicted famous [courtesans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oiran "Oiran").

Post-classical [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Korea "History of Korea") saw the end of the [Three Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms_of_Korea "Three Kingdoms of Korea") era, in which the kingdoms of [Goguryeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goguryeo "Goguryeo"), [Baekje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baekje "Baekje"), and [Silla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silla "Silla") had competed for hegemony. This period ended when Silla conquered Baekje in 660 and Goguryeo in 668, marking the beginning of the [Northern and Southern States period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_and_Southern_States_period "Northern and Southern States period"), with [Unified Silla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Silla "Unified Silla") in the south and [Balhae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balhae "Balhae"), a successor state to Goguryeo, in the north. In 892 CE, this arrangement reverted to the [Later Three Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_Three_Kingdoms "Later Three Kingdoms"), with Goguryeo emerging as dominant, unifying the entire peninsula by 936. The founding Goryeo dynasty ruled until 1392, succeeded by the [Joseon dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseon_dynasty "Joseon dynasty"), which ruled for approximately 500 years.

In [Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Mongolia "History of Mongolia"), [Genghis Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan "Genghis Khan") united various Mongol and Turkic tribes under one banner in 1206. The [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire "Mongol Empire") expanded from North Asia to comprise all of China and Central Asia, as well as large parts of Russia and the Middle East, to become [the largest contiguous empire in history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_empires "List of largest empires"). After [Möngke Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B6ngke_Khan "Möngke Khan") died in 1259, the Mongol Empire was [divided into four successor states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_the_Mongol_Empire "Division of the Mongol Empire"): the [Yuan Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_Dynasty "Yuan Dynasty") in China, the [Chagatai Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagatai_Khanate "Chagatai Khanate") in Central Asia, the [Golden Horde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horde "Golden Horde") in Eastern Europe and Russia, and the [Ilkhanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilkhanate "Ilkhanate") in Iran.

### Southeast Asia

The Southeast Asian polity of [Funan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funan "Funan"), which had originated in the 2nd century CE, went into decline in the 6th century as Chinese trade routes shifted away from its ports. It was replaced by the [Khmer Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_Empire "Khmer Empire") in 802 CE. The capital city of the [Khmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmers "Khmers") at [Angkor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angkor "Angkor") was the most extensive in the world before the industrial age and contained [Angkor Wat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angkor_Wat "Angkor Wat"), the world's largest religious monument. The [Sukhothai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhothai_Kingdom "Sukhothai Kingdom") (mid-13th century) and [Ayutthaya Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayutthaya_Kingdom "Ayutthaya Kingdom") (1351) were major powers of the [Thais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_people "Thai people"), who were influenced by the Khmers.

Starting in the 9th century, the [Pagan Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagan_Kingdom "Pagan Kingdom") rose to prominence in modern [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar"). Its collapse brought about political fragmentation that ended with the rise of the [Toungoo Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toungoo_Empire "Toungoo Empire") in the 16th century. Other notable kingdoms of the period include [Srivijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivijaya "Srivijaya") and [Lavo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavo_Kingdom "Lavo Kingdom") (both coming into prominence in the 7th century), [Champa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champa "Champa") and [Hariphunchai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hariphunchai "Hariphunchai") (both about 750), [Đại Việt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90%E1%BA%A1i_Vi%E1%BB%87t "Đại Việt") (968), [Lan Na](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lan_Na "Lan Na") (13th century), [Majapahit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majapahit "Majapahit") (1293), [Lan Xang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lan_Xang "Lan Xang") (1353), and [Ava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Ava "Kingdom of Ava") (1365). Hinduism and Buddhism had been spreading in Southeast Asia since the 1st century CE when, beginning in the 13th century, Islam arrived and made its way to regions such as present-day Indonesia. This period also saw the emergence of the [Malay states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Malaysia "Peninsular Malaysia"), including [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruneian_Sultanate_(1368%E2%80%931888) "Bruneian Sultanate (1368–1888)") and [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca_Sultanate "Malacca Sultanate"). In the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines "History of the Philippines"), several polities were formed, such as [Tondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tondo_(historical_polity) "Tondo (historical polity)"), [Cebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebu_(historical_polity) "Cebu (historical polity)"), and [Butuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butuan_(historical_polity) "Butuan (historical polity)").

### Oceania

The [Polynesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesians "Polynesians"), descendants of the [Lapita peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapita_culture "Lapita culture"), colonized vast reaches of [Remote Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_Oceania "Remote Oceania") beginning around 1000 CE. Their voyages resulted in the colonization of hundreds of islands, including the [Marquesas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquesas "Marquesas"), Hawaii, [Rapa Nui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapa_Nui "Rapa Nui") (Easter Island), and New Zealand.

The [Tuʻi Tonga Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tu%CA%BBi_Tonga_Empire "Tuʻi Tonga Empire") was founded in the 10th century CE and expanded between 1250 and 1500. Tongan culture, language, and hegemony spread widely throughout eastern [Melanesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanesia "Melanesia"), [Micronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronesia "Micronesia"), and central [Polynesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesia "Polynesia") during this period. They influenced eastern ['Uvea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%27Uvea "'Uvea"), [Rotuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotuma "Rotuma"), [Futuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futuna_(Wallis_and_Futuna) "Futuna (Wallis and Futuna)"), [Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samoan_Islands "Samoan Islands"), and [Niue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niue "Niue"), as well as specific islands and parts of Micronesia, [Vanuatu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanuatu "Vanuatu"), and [New Caledonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Caledonia "New Caledonia"). In Northern Australia, there is evidence that [Aboriginal Australians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aboriginal_Australians "Aboriginal Australians") regularly [traded with Makassan trepangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makassan_contact_with_Australia "Makassan contact with Australia") from Indonesia before the arrival of Europeans. In Aboriginal societies, leadership was [based on achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achieved_status "Achieved status") while the social structure of Polynesian societies was characterized by hereditary [chiefdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiefdom "Chiefdom").

### Americas

In North America, this period saw the rise of the [Mississippian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippian_culture "Mississippian culture") in the modern-day United States c. 950 CE, marked by the extensive 11th-century urban complex at [Cahokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cahokia "Cahokia"). The [Ancestral Puebloans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestral_Puebloans "Ancestral Puebloans") and their predecessors (9th–13th centuries) built extensive permanent settlements, including stone structures that remained the largest buildings in North America until the 19th century.

In Mesoamerica, the [Teotihuacan civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teotihuacan#History "Teotihuacan") fell, and the [classic Maya collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_Maya_collapse "Classic Maya collapse") occurred. The [Aztec Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec_Empire "Aztec Empire") came to dominate much of Mesoamerica in the 14th and 15th centuries.

In South America, the 15th century saw the rise of the Inca. The [Inca Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_Empire "Inca Empire"), with its capital at [Cusco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cusco "Cusco"), spanned the entire [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes "Andes"), making it the most extensive [pre-Columbian civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_civilization "Pre-Columbian civilization"). The Inca were prosperous and advanced, known for an excellent [road system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_road_system "Inca road system") and elegant stonework.

## Early modern

The early modern period is the era following the European Middle Ages until 1789 or 1800. A common break with the medieval period is placed between 1450 and 1500, which includes a number of significant events: the fall of [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople "Constantinople") to the [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire "Ottoman Empire"), the spread of [printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing "Printing"), and European voyages of discovery to America and along the African coast. The nature of warfare evolved as the size and organization of military forces on land and sea increased, alongside the wider propagation of gunpowder. The early modern period is significant for the start of [proto-globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-globalization "Proto-globalization"), increasingly centralized bureaucratic states and early forms of [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism "Capitalism"). European powers also began colonizing large parts of the world through maritime empires: first the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire "Portuguese Empire") and [Spanish Empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire "Spanish Empire"), then the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire "French colonial empire"), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_overseas_possessions "English overseas possessions"), and [Dutch Empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_colonial_empire "Dutch colonial empire"). Historians still debate the causes of Europe's rise, which is known as the [Great Divergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Divergence "Great Divergence").

Capitalist economies emerged, initially in the [northern Italian republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_republics "Maritime republics") and some Asian port cities. European states practiced [mercantilism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercantilism "Mercantilism") by implementing one-sided trade policies designed to benefit the mother country at the expense of its colonies. Starting at the end of the 15th century, the Portuguese established [trading posts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factory_(trading_post) "Factory (trading post)") across Africa, Asia, and Brazil for commodities such as gold and spices while also practicing [slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery "Slavery"). In the 17th century, private [chartered companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chartered_companies "Chartered companies") were established, such as the [English East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_East_India_Company "English East India Company") in 1600 – often described as the first [multinational corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_corporation "Multinational corporation") – and the [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company "Dutch East India Company") in 1602. Meanwhile, in much of the European sphere, [serfdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serfdom "Serfdom") declined and eventually disappeared while the power of the Catholic Church waned.

The [Age of Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery "Age of Discovery") was the first period in which the [Old World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World "Old World") engaged in substantial cultural, material, and biological exchange with the [New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World "New World"). It began in the late 15th century, when [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Portugal_(1415%E2%80%931578) "History of Portugal (1415–1578)") and [Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castile "Crown of Castile") sent the first exploratory voyages to the Americas, where [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus "Christopher Columbus") first arrived in 1492. Global integration continued as [European colonization of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas "European colonization of the Americas") initiated the [Columbian exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbian_exchange "Columbian exchange"): the exchange of plants, animals, foods, human populations (including slaves), [communicable diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communicable_diseases "Communicable diseases"), and culture between the [Eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Hemisphere "Eastern Hemisphere") and [Western Hemispheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Hemisphere "Western Hemisphere"). It was one of history's most important global events, involving ecology and agriculture. New crops brought from the Americas by 16th-century European seafarers substantially contributed to world population growth.

### West and Central Asia

The Ottoman Empire quickly came to dominate the Middle East after conquering Constantinople in 1453, which marked the end of the Byzantine Empire. Persia came under the rule of the [Safavids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty "Safavid dynasty") in 1501, succeeded by the [Afshars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afsharid_dynasty "Afsharid dynasty") in 1736, the [Zands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zands "Zands") in 1751, and the [Qajars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajars "Qajars") in 1794. The Safavids [established](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_conversion_of_Iran_to_Shia_Islam "Safavid conversion of Iran to Shia Islam") [Shia Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Islam "Shia Islam") as Persia's official religion, thus giving Persia a separate identity from its [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni "Sunni") neighbors. Along with the [Mughals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughals "Mughals") in India, the Ottomans and Safavids are known as the [gunpowder empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunpowder_empires "Gunpowder empires") because of their early adoption of firearms. Throughout the 16th century, the Ottomans conquered all of North Africa save for Morocco, which came under the rule of the [Saadi dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saadi_dynasty "Saadi dynasty") at the same time, and then the [Alawi dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alawi_dynasty "Alawi dynasty") in the 17th century.

The [Uzbeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbek_Khanate "Uzbek Khanate") replaced the [Timurids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_Empire "Timurid Empire") as the preeminent power in Central Asia by the 16th century. In the early 18th century, Russia began [its expansion into Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_conquest_of_Central_Asia "Russian conquest of Central Asia") that lasted until the late 19th century.

### Europe

The early modern period in Europe was an era of intense intellectual ferment. The [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance "Renaissance") – the "rebirth" of classical culture, beginning [in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Renaissance "Italian Renaissance") in the 14th century and extending into the 16th – comprised the rediscovery of the [classical world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_world "Classical world")'s cultural, scientific, and technological achievements, and the economic and social rise of Europe. This period is also celebrated for its artistic and literary attainments. [Petrarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrarch "Petrarch")'s poetry, [Giovanni Boccaccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Boccaccio "Giovanni Boccaccio")'s *[Decameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decameron "Decameron")*, and the paintings and sculptures of [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci "Leonardo da Vinci") and [Albrecht Dürer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albrecht_D%C3%BCrer "Albrecht Dürer"), as part of the [Northern Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Renaissance "Northern Renaissance"), are some of the great works of the age. After the Renaissance came the [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation "Reformation"), an anti-clerical theological and social movement started in Germany by [Martin Luther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther "Martin Luther") that resulted in the creation of [Protestant Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Christianity "Protestant Christianity").

The Renaissance also engendered a culture of inquisitiveness which ultimately led to [humanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism "Humanism") and the [Scientific Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_Revolution "Scientific Revolution"), an effort to understand the natural world through direct observation and experiment. The success of the new scientific techniques inspired attempts to apply them to political and social affairs, known as the [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment "Age of Enlightenment"), by thinkers such as [John Locke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Locke "John Locke") and [Immanuel Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant "Immanuel Kant"). This development was accompanied by [secularization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularization "Secularization") as a continued decline of the influence of religious beliefs and authorities in the public and private spheres. [Johannes Gutenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Gutenberg "Johannes Gutenberg")'s invention of [movable type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movable_type "Movable type") printing in 1440 helped spread the ideas of the new intellectual movements.

In addition to changes wrought by incipient capitalism and colonialism, early modern Europeans experienced an increase in the power of the state. [Absolute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutism_(European_history) "Absolutism (European history)") monarchs in [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_France "Kingdom of France"), [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsardom_of_Russia "Tsardom of Russia"), the [Habsburg lands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_lands "Habsburg lands"), and [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia "Kingdom of Prussia") produced powerful centralized states, with strong armies and efficient bureaucracies, all under the control of the king. In Russia, [Ivan the Terrible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_the_Terrible "Ivan the Terrible") was crowned in 1547 as the first [tsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsar "Tsar") of Russia, and by annexing the Turkic khanates in the east, transformed Russia into a regional power, eventually [replacing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitions_of_Poland "Partitions of Poland") the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth "Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth") as a major power in Eastern Europe. At the end of the 18th century, the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire") began its [conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_conquest_of_the_Caucasus "Russian conquest of the Caucasus") of the Caucasus. The countries of Western Europe, while expanding prodigiously through technological advances and colonial conquest, competed with each other economically and militarily in a state of almost constant war. Wars of particular note included the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years%27_War "Thirty Years' War"), the [War of the Spanish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession "War of the Spanish Succession"), the [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years%27_War "Seven Years' War"), and the [French Revolutionary Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolutionary_Wars "French Revolutionary Wars"). The [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution "French Revolution"), starting in 1789, laid the groundwork for liberal democracy by overthrowing the monarchy. It led to the rise of [Napoleon Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_Bonaparte "Napoleon Bonaparte") and the subsequent [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars "Napoleonic Wars") of the early 19th century.

### Africa

In the [Horn of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horn_of_Africa "Horn of Africa"), there was the [Oromo expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oromo_expansion "Oromo expansion") in the 16th century, which weakened [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Empire "Ethiopian Empire") and caused [Adal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adal_Sultanate "Adal Sultanate")'s collapse. [Ajuran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajuran_Sultanate "Ajuran Sultanate") was succeeded by the [Geledi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geledi_Sultanate "Geledi Sultanate"). In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, Ethiopia rapidly expanded.

In West Africa, the [Songhai Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songhai_Empire "Songhai Empire") fell to [Moroccan invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan_invasion_of_the_Songhai_Empire "Moroccan invasion of the Songhai Empire") in the late 16th century. They were succeeded by the [Bamana Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamana_Empire "Bamana Empire"). The [Fula jihads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fula_jihads "Fula jihads") beginning in the 18th century led to the establishment of the [Sokoto Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokoto_Caliphate "Sokoto Caliphate"), the [Massina Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massina_Empire "Massina Empire"), and the [Tukulor Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tukulor_Empire "Tukulor Empire"). In the forest regions, the [Asante Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asante_Empire "Asante Empire") was established in present-day Ghana. Between 1515 and 1800, 8 million Africans were exported in the [Atlantic slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_slave_trade "Atlantic slave trade").

In the western Congo Basin, [Kongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kongo "Kingdom of Kongo") fought three wars against the Portuguese who had begun [colonizing Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonization_of_Angola "Colonization of Angola"), ending in the conquest of [Ndongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ndongo "Ndongo") in the 17th century. Further east, the [Lunda Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunda_Empire "Lunda Empire") rose to dominate the region. It fell to the [Chokwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chokwe_people#History "Chokwe people") in the 19th century. In the northern [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Great_Lakes "African Great Lakes"), the kingdoms of [Bunyoro-Kitara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunyoro-Kitara "Bunyoro-Kitara"), [Buganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buganda "Buganda"), and [Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Rwanda "Kingdom of Rwanda") struggled for hegemony among others.

[Kilwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilwa_Sultanate "Kilwa Sultanate") was conquered by the Portuguese in the 16th century as they began [colonizing Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Mozambique "Portuguese Mozambique"). They were defeated by the [Omani Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omani_Empire "Omani Empire") who took control of the [Swahili coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_coast "Swahili coast"). In Madagascar, the 16th century onward saw the emergence of [Imerina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imerina "Imerina"), the [Betsileo kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betsileo_people#History "Betsileo people"), and the [Sakalava empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakalava_empire "Sakalava empire"); Imerina conquered most of the island in the 19th century. In the Zambezi Basin, [Mutapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutapa "Mutapa") was followed by the [Rozvi Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rozvi_Empire "Rozvi Empire"), with [Maravi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maravi "Maravi") around [Lake Malawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Malawi "Lake Malawi") to its north. [Mthwakazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mthwakazi "Mthwakazi") succeeded Rozvi. Further south, the Dutch began [colonizing South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_South_Africa "History of South Africa") in the 16th century, but lost it to the British. In the 19th century, Dutch settlers formed various [Boer Republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boer_Republics "Boer Republics") while the [Mfecane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mfecane "Mfecane") ravaged the region and led to the establishment of various [African kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_kingdoms_and_empires_in_African_history#Southern_Africa "List of kingdoms and empires in African history").

### South Asia

In the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent"), the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire "Mughal Empire") was established under [Babur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur "Babur") in 1526 and lasted for two centuries. Starting in the northwest, it brought the entire subcontinent under Muslim rule by the late 17th century, except for the southernmost Indian provinces, which remained independent. To resist the Muslim rulers, the Hindu [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire "Maratha Empire") was founded by [Shivaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivaji "Shivaji") on the western coast in 1674. The Marathas gradually gained territory from the Mughals over several decades, particularly in the [Mughal–Maratha Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal%E2%80%93Maratha_Wars "Mughal–Maratha Wars") (1680–1707).

[Sikhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism "Sikhism") developed at the end of the 15th century from the spiritual teachings of ten [gurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_gurus "Sikh gurus"). In 1799, [Ranjit Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranjit_Singh "Ranjit Singh") established the [Sikh Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_Empire "Sikh Empire") in the [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab "Punjab").

### Northeast Asia

In 1644, the Ming [were supplanted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_from_Ming_to_Qing "Transition from Ming to Qing") by the [Manchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_people "Manchu people") [Qing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty "Qing dynasty"), the last [conquest dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_dynasty "Conquest dynasty"), which ruled China until 1912. By the late 18th century, Russia completed [its subjugation of Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_conquest_of_Siberia "Russian conquest of Siberia"), with the territory between the Ural Mountains and the Pacific Ocean falling under its control.

Japan experienced its [Azuchi–Momoyama period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azuchi%E2%80%93Momoyama_period "Azuchi–Momoyama period") (1568–1600), followed by the [Edo period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period "Edo period") (1600–1868). The Korean Joseon dynasty (1392–1910) ruled throughout this period, repelling invasions from Japan and China in the 16th and 17th centuries. Expanded maritime trade with Europe significantly affected China and Japan during this period, particularly through the Portuguese in [Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Macau "Portuguese Macau") and the Dutch in [Nagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dejima "Dejima"). However, China and Japan later pursued [isolationist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isolationist "Isolationist") policies designed to eliminate foreign influences.

### Southeast Asia

In 1511, the Portuguese overthrew the [Malacca Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca_Sultanate "Malacca Sultanate") in present-day Malaysia and Indonesian [Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatra "Sumatra"). The Portuguese held this important trading territory (and the valuable associated navigational strait) until overthrown by the Dutch in 1641. The [Johor Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor_Sultanate "Johor Sultanate"), centered on the southern tip of the Malay Peninsula, became the dominant trading power in the region. [European colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonisation_of_Southeast_Asia "European colonisation of Southeast Asia") expanded with the Dutch in [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_Indies "Dutch East Indies"), the Portuguese in [Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Timor "Portuguese Timor"), and the Spaniards in the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_East_Indies "Spanish East Indies").

### Oceania

The Pacific Islands of Oceania were also affected by European contact, starting with the [circumnavigational voyage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magellan_expedition "Magellan expedition") of [Ferdinand Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Magellan "Ferdinand Magellan") (1519–1522), who landed in the [Marianas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marianas "Marianas") and other islands. [Abel Tasman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_Tasman "Abel Tasman") (1642–1644) sailed to present-day [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Australia "History of Australia"), [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_New_Zealand "History of New Zealand"), and nearby islands. [James Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cook "James Cook") (1768–1779) made the first recorded European contact with [Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Hawaii "History of Hawaii"). In 1788, Britain founded its [first Australian colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_New_South_Wales "Colony of New South Wales").

### Americas

Several European powers colonized the Americas, largely displacing the native populations and conquering the advanced civilizations of the Aztecs and Inca. [Diseases introduced by Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_disease_and_epidemics#European_contact "Native American disease and epidemics") devastated American societies, killing 60–90 million people by 1600 and reducing the population by 90–95%. In some cases, colonial policies included the deliberate [genocide of indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide_of_Indigenous_peoples#Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas_(pre-1948) "Genocide of Indigenous peoples"). Spain, Portugal, Britain, and France all made extensive territorial claims, and they undertook [large-scale settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settler_colonialism "Settler colonialism"), including the importation of large numbers of African slaves. One side-effect of the slave trade was cultural exchange through which various African traditions found their way to the Americas, including cuisine, music, and dance. Portugal claimed [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Brazil "Colonial Brazil"), while Spain seized the rest of South America, Mesoamerica, and southern North America. The Spaniards mined and exported prodigious amounts of gold and silver, leading to a surge in inflation known as the [Price Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_Revolution "Price Revolution") in the 16th and 17th centuries in Western Europe.

In North America, Britain colonized the east coast while France settled the central region. Russia made incursions into the northwest coast of North America. France lost its North American territory to England and Spain after the Seven Years' War (1756–1763). Britain's [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies "Thirteen Colonies") [declared independence as the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution "American Revolution") in 1776, ratified by the [Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1783) "Treaty of Paris (1783)") in 1783, ending the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War "American Revolutionary War"). In 1791, African slaves [launched a successful rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_Revolution "Haitian Revolution") in the French colony of [Saint-Domingue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Domingue "Saint-Domingue"). France won back its continental claims from Spain in 1800, but sold them to the United States in the [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase "Louisiana Purchase") of 1803.

## Modern

### Long nineteenth century

The [long nineteenth century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_nineteenth_century "Long nineteenth century") traditionally starts with the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution "French Revolution") in 1789, and lasts until the outbreak of World War I in 1914. It saw the global spread of the Industrial Revolution, the greatest transformation of the world economy since the Neolithic Revolution. The Industrial Revolution began in Great Britain around 1770 and used new modes of production—the factory, [mass production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_production "Mass production"), and [mechanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanization "Mechanization")—to manufacture a wide array of goods faster while using less labor than previously required. As a consequence of the tensions between the ruling elite and the working class that had been raised by the Industrial Revolution, this led to massive political upheavals, with individuals such as [Karl Marx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Marx "Karl Marx") being major figureheads within the movement.

Industrialization raised the global [standard of living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_of_living "Standard of living") but caused upheaval as factory owners and workers clashed over wages and working conditions. Along with industrialization came modern [globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization "Globalization"), the increasing interconnection of world regions in the economic, political, and cultural spheres. Globalization began in the early 19th century and was enabled by improved transportation technologies such as railroads and [steamships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steamship "Steamship").

European empires [lost territories in Latin America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonization_of_the_Americas "Decolonization of the Americas"), which [won independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_American_wars_of_independence "Spanish American wars of independence") by the 1820s through military campaigns, but expanded elsewhere as their industrial economies gave them an advantage over the rest of the world. Britain gained control of the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj"), [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_rule_in_Burma "British rule in Burma"), [Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Malaya "British Malaya"), [North Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Borneo "North Borneo"), [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Hong_Kong "British Hong Kong"), and [Aden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aden_Province "Aden Province"); the French took [Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina "French Indochina"); and the Dutch cemented their rule over [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_Indies "Dutch East Indies"). The British also colonized Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and South Africa, with large numbers of British colonists emigrating to these colonies, displacing indigenous peoples in several areas.

Russia colonized large pre-agricultural areas of Siberia. The United States completed its [westward expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_frontier "American frontier"), establishing control over the territory from the Atlantic to the Pacific coast.

In the late 19th century to the early 20th century, the European powers, driven by the [Second Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Industrial_Revolution "Second Industrial Revolution"), rapidly [conquered and colonized almost the entirety of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scramble_for_Africa "Scramble for Africa"). Only Ethiopia and [Liberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberia "Liberia") remained independent. Imperial rule in Africa involved many atrocities such as [those in the Congo Free State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atrocities_in_the_Congo_Free_State "Atrocities in the Congo Free State") and the [Herero and Nama genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herero_and_Nama_genocide "Herero and Nama genocide").

Within Europe, economic and military competition fostered the creation and consolidation of nation-states, and other ethno-cultural communities began to identify themselves as distinctive nations with aspirations for their own cultural and political autonomy. This [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism "Nationalism") became important to peoples across the world in the 19th and 20th centuries. In the [first wave of democratization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waves_of_democracy "Waves of democracy"), between 1828 and 1926, democratic institutions were established in 33 countries worldwide.

Most of the world [abolished slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism "Abolitionism") and serfdom in the 19th century. Over several decades, beginning in the late 19th century and continuing throughout the 20th, in many countries the [women's suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_suffrage "Women's suffrage") movement won women the right to vote, and women began to enjoy greater access to education and to professions beyond domestic employment.

In response to encroachment by European powers, several countries undertook programs of industrialization and political reform along Western lines. The [Meiji Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Restoration "Meiji Restoration") in [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan "Empire of Japan") led to the establishment of a [colonial empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_colonial_empire "Japanese colonial empire"), while the *[Tanzimat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzimat "Tanzimat")* reforms in the Ottoman Empire did little to slow the Ottoman decline. China achieved some success with its [Self-Strengthening Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-Strengthening_Movement "Self-Strengthening Movement") but was devastated by the [Taiping Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiping_Rebellion "Taiping Rebellion"), history's bloodiest civil war, which between 1850 and 1864 killed 20–30 million people.

By the end of the century, the United States became the world's largest economy. During the [Second Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Industrial_Revolution "Second Industrial Revolution"), new technological advances, involving [electric power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_power "Electric power"), the [internal combustion engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_combustion_engine "Internal combustion engine"), and [assembly-line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly-line "Assembly-line") manufacturing, further increased productivity. Technological innovations also provided new avenues for artistic expression through the media of [photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photography "Photography"), [sound recording](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_recording "Sound recording"), and [film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film "Film").

Meanwhile, [industrial pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_pollution "Industrial pollution") and [environmental degradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_degradation "Environmental degradation") accelerated drastically. [Balloon flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balloon_flight "Balloon flight") had been invented in the late 18th century, but it was only at the beginning of the 20th century that [powered aircraft were developed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_Flyer "Wright Flyer").

The 20th century opened with Europe at an apex of wealth and power. Much of the world was under its direct colonial control or its indirect influence through heavily Europeanized nations like the United States and Japan. As the century unfolded, however, the global system dominated by rival powers experienced severe strains and ultimately yielded to a more fluid structure of independent [nation-states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation_state "Nation state").

### World wars

This transformation was catalyzed by wars of unparalleled scope and devastation. [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I") was a global conflict from 1914 to 1918 between the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I "Allies of World War I"), led by France, Russia, and the United Kingdom, and the [Central Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Powers "Central Powers"), led by [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire "German Empire"), [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary "Austria-Hungary"), the Ottoman Empire, and Bulgaria. It had an estimated death toll ranging from 10 to 22.5 million and resulted in the collapse of four empires – the Austro-Hungarian, German, Ottoman, and Russian Empires. Its new emphasis on industrial technology had made traditional military tactics obsolete.

The [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_genocide "Armenian genocide"), [Assyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayfo "Sayfo"), and [Greek genocides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_genocide "Greek genocide") saw the systematic destruction, mass murder, and expulsion of those populations in the Ottoman Empire. From 1918 to 1920, the [Spanish flu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_flu "Spanish flu") caused the deaths of at least 25 million people.

In the war's aftermath, a [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations") was formed in the hope of averting future international conflicts, and powerful ideologies rose to prominence. The [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution "Russian Revolution") of 1917 created the Soviet Union, which would be known as the first [communist state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_state "Communist state"), while the 1920s and 1930s saw [fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist "Fascist") political parties gain control in [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_Italy "Fascist Italy") and [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany"). The Soviet Union, during [Stalinist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalinism "Stalinism") rule from 1924 to 1953, committed [countless atrocities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excess_mortality_in_the_Soviet_Union_under_Joseph_Stalin "Excess mortality in the Soviet Union under Joseph Stalin") against its own people, including [mass purges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purges_of_the_Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union "Purges of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union"), [forced labor camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulag "Gulag"), and [widespread famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_famine_of_1930%E2%80%931933 "Soviet famine of 1930–1933") caused by state policies.

Ongoing national rivalries, exacerbated by the economic turmoil of the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression "Great Depression"), helped precipitate [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). In that war, the vast majority of the world's countries, including all the [great powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_power "Great power"), fought as part of two opposing [military alliances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_alliance "Military alliance"): the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II "Allies of World War II") and the [Axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers "Axis powers"). The leading Axis powers were [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany"), [Imperial Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan "Empire of Japan"), and the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy "Kingdom of Italy"); while the United Kingdom, the United States, the Soviet Union, and the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_(1912%E2%80%931949) "Republic of China (1912–1949)") were the "[Big Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Policemen "Four Policemen")" Allied powers.

The [militaristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militaristic "Militaristic") governments of Germany and Japan pursued [imperialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperialist "Imperialist") [expansionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expansionism "Expansionism"). In the course of doing so, Germany killed [6 million Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust "The Holocaust") and millions of non-Jews under the rule of [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler") and the [Nazi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Party "Nazi Party"), while Japan [murdered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_war_crimes "Japanese war crimes") millions of Chinese. The war also saw the introduction and use of [nuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapons "Nuclear weapons"), which brought unprecedented destruction and ultimately led to Japan's surrender. Estimates of the war's total casualties range from 55 to 80 million.

### Contemporary history

When World War II ended in 1945, the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations") was founded in the hope of preventing future wars, as the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations") had been formed following World War I. The United Nations championed the [human rights movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_movement "Human rights movement"), in 1948 adopting the [Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Declaration_of_Human_Rights "Universal Declaration of Human Rights"). Several European countries formed what would later become the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union").

World War II had opened the way for the advance of communism into Eastern and Central Europe, China, [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea "North Korea"), [North Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vietnam "North Vietnam"), and [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba "Cuba"). To [contain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Containment "Containment") this advance, the United States established a global network of alliances. The largest, [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO"), was established in 1949. In response, in 1955 the Soviet Union and its Eastern European allies formed the [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact "Warsaw Pact").

The United States and the Soviet Union emerged as the primary global powers in the aftermath of World War II. Both nations harbored deep suspicions and fears about the global spread of the other's political-economic system—capitalism for the United States and communism for the Soviet Union. This mutual distrust sparked the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War"), a 45-year stand-off and [arms race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arms_race "Arms race") between the two nations and their allies.

With the development of nuclear weapons during World War II and their subsequent [proliferation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_proliferation "Nuclear proliferation"), all of humanity was put at risk of [nuclear war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_war "Nuclear war") between the two superpowers, as demonstrated by [many incidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_close_calls "Nuclear close calls"), most prominently the October 1962 [Cuban Missile Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_Missile_Crisis "Cuban Missile Crisis"). Such war [being viewed as impractical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_holocaust "Nuclear holocaust"), the superpowers instead waged [proxy wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proxy_war "Proxy war") in non-nuclear-armed [Third World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_World "Third World") countries. The Cold War ended peacefully in 1991 after the [Soviet Union collapsed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union "Dissolution of the Soviet Union"), partly due to its inability to compete economically with the United States and Western Europe.

Cold War preparations to deter or fight a [third world war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_world_war "Third world war") accelerated advances in technologies that, though conceptualized before World War II, had been implemented for that war's exigencies, such as [jet aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_aircraft "Jet aircraft"), [rocketry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket "Rocket"), and computers. In the decades after World War II, these advances led to jet travel; [artificial satellites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_satellites "Artificial satellites") with innumerable applications, including [GPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPS "GPS"); and the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet "Internet"), which in the 1990s began to gain traction as a form of communication. These inventions revolutionized the movement of people, ideas, and information.

The second half of the 20th century also saw groundbreaking scientific and technological developments such as the worldwide [eradication of smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eradication_of_smallpox "Eradication of smallpox"), the [Green Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Revolution "Green Revolution") in agriculture, the discovery of [plate tectonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plate_tectonics "Plate tectonics"), the [moon landings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_program "Apollo program"), crewed and uncrewed [exploration of space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploration_of_space "Exploration of space"), advances in [energy technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_technologies "Energy technologies"), and foundational discoveries in [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics").
These technical innovations had far-reaching effects. During the 20th century, the world's population quadrupled to six billion, while world economic output increased by a factor of 20. Toward the end of the 20th century, the rate of [population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth "Population growth") started to decline.

[Public health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health "Public health") measures and advances in [medical science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_science "Medical science") contributed to a sharp increase in global [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy "Life expectancy") at birth from about 31 years in 1900 to over 66 years in 2000. In 1820, 75% of humanity lived on less than one dollar a day, while in 2001 only about 20% did. At the same time, economic inequality increased both within individual countries and between rich and poor countries. The importance of public education had already begun to increase in the 18th and 19th centuries, but it was not until the end of the 20th and the beginning of the 21st century that compulsory free education was provided to [most children worldwide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_access_to_education "Universal access to education").

In China, the [Maoist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maoist "Maoist") government implemented industrialization and [collectivization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectivization "Collectivization") policies as part of the [Great Leap Forward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Leap_Forward "Great Leap Forward"), leading to the [starvation deaths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Chinese_Famine "Great Chinese Famine") of 30–40 million people. China later entered a period of [economic liberalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalization "Economic liberalization") and rapid growth.

In the postwar decades, in a process of [decolonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonization "Decolonization"), the [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation_of_Africa "Decolonisation of Africa"), [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation_of_Asia "Decolonisation of Asia"), and [Oceanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation_of_Oceania "Decolonisation of Oceania") colonies of European empires won their formal independence. Postcolonial states in Africa struggled to grow their economies.
The [Near East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_East "Near East") experienced numerous conflicts, including the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War "Iran–Iraq War"), the [first](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War "Gulf War") and [second Gulf wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_War "Iraq War"), and the [Syrian civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_civil_war "Syrian civil war"), as well as [tensions and conflicts between Israel and Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_conflict "Israeli–Palestinian conflict").

The early 21st century was marked by growing [economic globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_globalization "Economic globalization"), as well as the [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession "Great Recession"). Communications expanded, with [smartphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone "Smartphone") and [social media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_media "Social media") becoming ubiquitous worldwide by the mid-2010s. By the early 2020s, [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence") systems improved to the point of outperforming humans at many circumscribed tasks, initiating the [AI boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_boom "AI boom") in the early 2020s.

The influence of religion continued to decline in many Western countries, while some parts of the Muslim world saw the rise of [fundamentalist movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_fundamentalism "Islamic fundamentalism"). In 2020, the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic") substantially disrupted global trade, caused recessions in the global economy, and spurred cultural [paradigm shifts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradigm_shift "Paradigm shift"). [Environmental degradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_degradation "Environmental degradation"), including multifarious forms of [pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution "Pollution"), and [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming "Global warming") became increasingly evident, while [mitigation efforts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_mitigation "Climate change mitigation") made gradual progress.

## Academic research

The study of human history has a long tradition, and early precursors were already practiced in the ancient period as attempts to provide comprehensive accounts of the history of the world. Most research before the 20th century focused on histories of individual communities and societies after the prehistoric period. This changed in the late 20th century, when attempts to integrate the diverse narratives into a common context reaching back to the emergence of the first humans became a central research topic. This transition to a widened perspective was accompanied by questioning [Eurocentrism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocentrism "Eurocentrism") and the Western-focused perspective that had previously dominated academic history.

Like in other historical disciplines, the [methodology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_method "Historical method") of analyzing textual sources to construct narratives and interpretations of past events plays a central role in the study of human history. The scope of its topic poses the unique challenge of synthesizing a coherent and comprehensive narrative spanning different cultures, regions, and time periods while taking diverse individual perspectives into account. This is also reflected in its [interdisciplinary approach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interdisciplinarity "Interdisciplinarity") by integrating insights from fields belonging to the [humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanities "Humanities") and the [social](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_sciences "Social sciences"), biological, and [physical sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_sciences "Physical sciences"), such as other [historical disciplines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History "History"), [archeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology "Archaeology"), [anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology "Anthropology"), [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics "Linguistics"), [genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics "Genetics"), [paleontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleontology "Paleontology"), and [geology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology "Geology"). The interdisciplinary approach is of particular importance to the study of human history before the invention of writing.

### Periodization

To provide an accessible overview, historians divide human history into different periods organized around key themes, events, or developments that have shaped human societies over time. The number of periods and their time frames depend on the chosen topics, and the transitions between periods are often more fluid than static periodization schemes suggest.

A traditionally influential periodization in European scholarship distinguishes between the ancient, medieval, and modern periods, organized around historical events responsible for major shifts in political, economic, and cultural structures to mark the transitions between the periods: first the fall of the Western Roman Empire and later the emergence of the Renaissance. Another periodization divides human history into three periods based on the way humans engage with nature to produce goods. The first transition happened with the emergence of agriculture and husbandry to replace hunting and gathering as the main means of food production. The Industrial Revolution constitutes the second transition.

[A further approach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rise_of_the_West "The Rise of the West") uses the relations between societies to divide the history of the world into the periods of Middle Eastern dominance before 500 BCE, Eurasian cultural balance until 1500 CE, and Western dominance afterwards. The invention of writing is often used to demarcate prehistory from the ancient period, while another approach divides early history based on the type of tools used in the Stone, Bronze, and Iron Ages. Historians focusing on religion and culture identify the Axial Age as a key turning point that laid the spiritual and philosophical foundations of many of the world's major civilizations. Some historians draw on elements from different approaches to arrive at a more nuanced periodization.
