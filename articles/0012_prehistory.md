# Prehistory

**Prehistory**, sometimes referred to as **pre-literary history**, is the period of [human history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_history "Human history") between the first known use of [stone tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_tool "Stone tool") by [hominins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominin "Hominin") c. 3.3 [million years ago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Million_years_ago "Million years ago") and the beginning of [recorded history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recorded_history "Recorded history") with the invention of [writing systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writing_system "Writing system"). The use of symbols, marks, and images appears very early among humans, but the earliest known writing systems appeared c. 5,200 years ago. The adoption of writing across the globe was a gradual process, so that the end of prehistory occurred at different times in different places, and the term is less often used in discussing societies where prehistory ended relatively recently. The period when a culture is written about by others but has not developed its own writing system is often known as the [protohistory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protohistory "Protohistory") of the culture.

Human prehistory differs from history not only in terms of its [chronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronology "Chronology") but also in the way it deals with the activities of [archaeological cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_culture "Archaeological culture") rather than named [nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation "Nation") or [individuals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual "Individual"). Restricted to material processes, remains, and artefacts rather than written records, prehistory is anonymous. Because of this, reference terms that prehistorians use, such as "[Neanderthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neanderthal "Neanderthal")" or "[Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age "Iron Age")", are modern labels with definitions sometimes subject to debate. In most cases however, and in particular as one goes back further in time, these assigned labels are the only way of distinguishing groups of prehistoric peoples and conveying information about them in the present.

By definition, there are no written records from human prehistory, which can only be known from material [archaeological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology "Archaeology") and [anthropological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology "Anthropology") evidence: prehistoric materials and human remains. These were at first understood by the collection of [folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore "Folklore") and by analogy with pre-literate societies observed in modern times.

## History of the term

The notion of "prehistory" emerged during the Enlightenment in the work of antiquarians who used the word "primitive" to describe societies that existed before written records. The word "prehistory" first appeared in English in 1836 in the *Foreign [Quarterly Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarterly_Review "Quarterly Review")*.

The geologic time scale for pre-human time periods, and the [three-age system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-age_system "Three-age system") for human prehistory, were systematised during the nineteenth century in the work of British, French, German, and Scandinavian [anthropologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology "Anthropology"), [archaeologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology "Archaeology"), and [antiquarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiquarian "Antiquarian"). An early conception of "prehistory" arose suggesting that without written records there could be no history and no way to speak meaningfully about the people who left behind no writing. The most common conception today is that history is based on a wide range of evidence, and while some historians have been uncomfortable with using prehistory as a term, the concept of prehistory has been questioned but not discarded. Archaeologists in particular view the term as a meaningful and necessary distinction of the type of work they do and the stories they can convey about the past.

## Means of research

The main source of information for prehistory is the archaeological record, frequently studied in context with research from other disciplines such as the natural sciences.

The primary researchers into human prehistory are anthropologists (archaeologists and [physical anthropologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_anthropology "Physical anthropology")) who use excavation, geologic and geographic surveys, and other scientific analysis to reveal and interpret the nature and behavior of pre-literate and non-literate peoples. Human population [geneticists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneticists "Geneticists") and [historical linguists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_linguistics "Historical linguistics") are also providing valuable insight. Cultural anthropologists help provide context for societal interactions, by which objects of human origin pass among people, allowing an analysis of any article that arises in a human prehistoric context. Therefore, data about prehistory is provided by a wide variety of natural and social sciences, such as [anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology "Anthropology"), [archaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology "Archaeology"), [archaeoastronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeoastronomy "Archaeoastronomy"), [comparative linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_linguistics "Comparative linguistics"), [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology "Biology"), [geology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology "Geology"), [molecular genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_genetics "Molecular genetics"), [paleontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleontology "Paleontology"), [palynology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palynology "Palynology"), [physical anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_anthropology "Physical anthropology"), and many others.

The key step to understanding prehistoric evidence is dating, and reliable dating techniques have developed steadily since the nineteenth century. The most common of these dating techniques is [radiocarbon dating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiocarbon_dating "Radiocarbon dating"). Further evidence has come from the reconstruction of [ancient spoken languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-language "Proto-language"). More recent techniques include forensic chemical analysis to reveal the use and provenance of materials, and genetic analysis of bones to determine kinship and physical characteristics of prehistoric peoples.

## Definition

### Beginning and end

The beginning of prehistory is normally taken to be marked by human-like beings appearing on Earth. The date marking its end is typically defined as the advent of the contemporary [written historical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_languages_by_first_written_accounts "List of languages by first written accounts") record.

Both dates consequently vary widely from region to region. For example, [African prehistory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_Africa "Prehistoric Africa") begins c. 2.5 million years ago, while [prehistoric Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_Asia "Prehistoric Asia") date back to 1.8 million years ago; and [European prehistory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_Europe "Prehistoric Europe") begins c. 1.3 million years ago upon the first arrival of humans to the region. Depending on the date when relevant records become a useful academic resource, its end date also varies. For example, in [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt") it is generally accepted that prehistory ended around 3100 BCE, whereas in [New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Guinea "New Guinea") the end of the prehistoric era is set much more recently, in the 1870s, when the Russian anthropologist [Nicholai Miklukho-Maklai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholai_Miklukho-Maklai "Nicholai Miklukho-Maklai") spent several years living among native peoples, and described their way of life in a comprehensive treatise. In Europe the relatively well-documented classical cultures of [Ancient Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece "Ancient Greece") and [Ancient Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome "Ancient Rome") had neighbouring cultures, including the [Celts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts "Celts") and the [Etruscans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruscan_civilization "Etruscan civilization"), with little writing. Historians debate how much weight to give to the sometimes biased accounts of these protohistoric cultures found in Greek and Roman literature.

### Time periods

In dividing up human prehistory in Eurasia, historians typically use the three-age system, whereas scholars of pre-human time periods typically use the [well-defined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronostratigraphy "Chronostratigraphy") [geologic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geologic_record "Geologic record") and its internationally defined [stratum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratum "Stratum") base within the [geologic time scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Era_(geology) "Era (geology)"). The three-age system is the [periodization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodization "Periodization") of human prehistory into three consecutive [time periods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_period "Time period"), named for their predominant tool-making technologies: [Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age "Stone Age"), [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age "Bronze Age") and [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age "Iron Age"). In some areas, there is also a transition period between Stone Age and Bronze Age, the [Chalcolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcolithic "Chalcolithic") or Copper Age.

In the early [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age "Bronze Age"), [Sumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumer "Sumer") in [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia "Mesopotamia"), the [Indus Valley Civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation "Indus Valley Civilisation"), and [ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt "Ancient Egypt") were the first civilizations to develop their own scripts and keep historical records, with their neighbors following. Most other civilizations reached their end of prehistory during the following [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age "Iron Age"). The [three-age division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-age_system "Three-age system") of prehistory into [Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age "Stone Age"), [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age "Bronze Age"), and [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age "Iron Age") remains in use for much of [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia "Eurasia") and [North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Africa "North Africa"), but is not generally used in those parts of the world where [the working of hard metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metalworking "Metalworking") arrived abruptly from contact with Eurasian cultures, such as [Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania "Oceania"), [Australasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australasia "Australasia"), much of [Sub-Saharan Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-Saharan_Africa "Sub-Saharan Africa"), and parts of the [Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americas "Americas"). With some exceptions in [pre-Columbian civilizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_era "Pre-Columbian era") in the Americas, these areas did not develop writing systems before the arrival of Eurasians, so their prehistory reaches into relatively recent periods; for example, 1788 is usually taken as the end of the [prehistory of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory_of_Australia "Prehistory of Australia").
For the prehistory of the Americas see [Pre-Columbian era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_era "Pre-Columbian era").

## Stone Age

The concept of a "Stone Age" is found useful in the archaeology of most of the world, although in the [archaeology of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology_of_the_Americas "Archaeology of the Americas") it is called by different names and begins with a [Lithic stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithic_stage "Lithic stage"), or sometimes [Paleo-Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleo-Indians "Paleo-Indians"). The sub-divisions described below are used for Eurasia, and not consistently across the whole area.

The period lasted for roughly 3.4 million years and ended between 4000 BC and 2000 BC, with the advent of metalworking. It was followed by the Copper Age, then the Bronze Age. Because of its enormous timescale, the Stone Age encompasses 99% of human history.

### Palaeolithic

"Palaeolithic" means "Old Stone Age", and begins with the first use of [stone tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_tool "Stone tool"). The Paleolithic is the earliest period of the [Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age "Stone Age"). It extends from the earliest known use of stone tools by [hominins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominini "Hominini") c. 3.3 million years ago, to the end of the [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene "Pleistocene") c. 11,650 [BP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Before_Present "Before Present") (before the present period).

The early part of the Palaeolithic is called the [Lower Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Paleolithic "Lower Paleolithic") (as in excavations it appears underneath the Upper Paleolithic), beginning with the earliest stone tools dated to around 3.3 million years ago at the [Lomekwi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lomekwi "Lomekwi") site in Kenya. These tools predate the genus *Homo* and were probably used by *[Kenyanthropus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenyanthropus "Kenyanthropus")*. Evidence of [control of fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_of_fire_by_early_humans "Control of fire by early humans") by early hominins during the Lower Palaeolithic Era is uncertain and has at best limited scholarly support. The most widely accepted claim is that *H. erectus* or *[H. ergaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_ergaster "Homo ergaster")* made fires between 790,000 and 690,000 BP in a site at [Bnot Ya'akov Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bnot_Ya%27akov_Bridge "Bnot Ya'akov Bridge"), [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel"). The use of fire enabled early humans to cook food, provide warmth, have a light source, deter animals at night and meditate.

[Early *Homo sapiens*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Homo_sapiens "Early Homo sapiens") originated some 300,000 years ago, ushering in the [Middle Palaeolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Palaeolithic "Middle Palaeolithic"). Anatomic changes indicating modern language capacity also arise during the Middle Palaeolithic. During the Middle Palaeolithic Era, there is the first definitive evidence of human use of fire. Sites in Zambia have charred logs, charcoal and carbonized plants, that have been dated to 180,000 BP. The systematic [burial of the dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic_religion "Paleolithic religion"), [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_of_music "Origin of music"), [prehistoric art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_art "Prehistoric art"), and the use of increasingly sophisticated multi-part tools are highlights of the Middle Paleolithic.

The [Upper Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Paleolithic "Upper Paleolithic") extends from 50,000 and 12,000 years ago, with the first organized settlements and blossoming of artistic work.

Throughout the Palaeolithic, humans generally lived as [nomadic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomad "Nomad") [hunter-gatherers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer "Hunter-gatherer"). [Hunter-gatherer societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer#Social_and_economic_structure "Hunter-gatherer") tended to be very small and egalitarian, although hunter-gatherer societies with abundant resources or advanced food-storage techniques sometimes developed sedentary lifestyles with complex social structures such as chiefdoms, and [social stratification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_stratification "Social stratification"). Long-distance contacts may have been established, as in the case of [Indigenous Australian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_Australian "Indigenous Australian") "highways" known as [songlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songlines "Songlines").

### Mesolithic

The Mesolithic, or Middle Stone Age (from the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language "Greek language") *mesos*, 'middle', and *lithos*, 'stone'), was a period in the development of human [technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology "Technology") between the Palaeolithic and [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic "Neolithic").

The [Mesolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesolithic "Mesolithic") period began with the retreat of glaciers at the end of the [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene "Pleistocene") epoch, some 10,000 BP, and ended with [the introduction of agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture#History "Agriculture"), the date of which varied by geographic region. In some areas, such as the [Near East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_East "Near East"), agriculture was already underway by the end of the [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene "Pleistocene"), and there the Mesolithic is short and poorly defined. In areas with limited [glacial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial "Glacial") impact, the term "[Epipalaeolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epipalaeolithic "Epipalaeolithic")" is preferred.

Regions that experienced greater environmental effects as the [last ice age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_glacial_period "Last glacial period") ended have a much more evident Mesolithic era, lasting millennia. In [Northern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Europe "Northern Europe"), societies were able to live well on rich food supplies from the [marshlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsh "Marsh") fostered by the warmer climate. Such conditions produced distinctive human behaviours that are preserved in the material record, such as the [Maglemosian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maglemosian "Maglemosian") and [Azilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azilian "Azilian") cultures. These conditions also delayed the coming of the Neolithic until as late as 4000 BCE (6,000 [BP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Before_Present "Before Present")) in northern Europe.

Remains from this period are few and far between, often limited to [middens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midden "Midden"). In forested areas, the first signs of [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation "Deforestation") have been found, although this would only begin in earnest during the Neolithic, when more space was needed for [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture "Agriculture").

The Mesolithic is characterized in most areas by small composite [flint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flint "Flint") tools: [microliths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microlith "Microlith") and [microburins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microburin "Microburin"). [Fishing tackle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_tackle "Fishing tackle"), stone [adzes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adze "Adze"), and wooden objects such as [canoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canoe "Canoe") and [bows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bow_(weapon) "Bow (weapon)") have been found at some sites. These technologies first occur in Africa, associated with the Azilian cultures, before spreading to Europe through the [Iberomaurusian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberomaurusian "Iberomaurusian") culture of Northern Africa and the [Kebaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kebaran "Kebaran") culture of the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant "Levant"). However, independent discovery is not ruled out.

### Neolithic

The Neolithic period, meaning "New Stone Age", began around 10,200 BCE in some regions of the Middle East, or later in other parts of the world, and ended between 4,500 and 2,000 BCE. Although there were several species of humans during the [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic "Paleolithic"), by the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic "Neolithic") only *[Homo sapiens sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_sapiens_sapiens "Homo sapiens sapiens")* remained. This was a period of [technological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological "Technological") and [social](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social "Social") developments which established most of the basic elements of historical cultures, such as the domestication of crops and [animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestication "Domestication"), and the establishment of permanent settlements and early chiefdoms. The era commenced with the beginning of [farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_agriculture "History of agriculture"), which produced the "[Neolithic Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_Revolution "Neolithic Revolution")". It ended when metal tools became widespread (in the [Copper Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcolithic "Chalcolithic") or [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age "Bronze Age"); or, in some geographical regions, in the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age "Iron Age")). The term *Neolithic* is commonly used in the [Old World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World "Old World"); its application to cultures in the [Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americas "Americas") and [Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania "Oceania") is complicated by the fact that standard progression from stone to metal tools, as seen in the Old World, does not neatly apply.

Early Neolithic farming was limited to a narrow range of plants, both wild and domesticated, which included [einkorn wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einkorn_wheat "Einkorn wheat"), [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet "Millet") and [spelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spelt "Spelt"), and the keeping of [dogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_of_the_domestic_dog "Origin of the domestic dog"), [sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep "Sheep"), and [goats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goat#History "Goat"). By about 6,900–6,400 BCE, it included domesticated [cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle "Cattle") and pigs, the establishment of permanently or seasonally inhabited settlements, and the use of [pottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pottery "Pottery"). The Neolithic period saw the development of early [villages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village "Village"), [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture "Agriculture"), animal [domestication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestication "Domestication"), [tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tool "Tool"), and the onset of the earliest recorded incidents of warfare.

Settlements became more permanent, some with circular houses made of [mudbrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudbrick "Mudbrick") with a single room. These settlements may have a surrounding stone wall to keep domesticated animals in and hostile tribes out. Later settlements have rectangular mud-brick houses in which families lived in single or multiple rooms. Burial findings suggest an [ancestor cult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestor_cult "Ancestor cult") with [preserved skulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastered_human_skulls "Plastered human skulls") of the dead. The [Vinča culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vin%C4%8Da_culture "Vinča culture") may have created the earliest system of writing. The [megalithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalith "Megalith") temple complexes of [Ġgantija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%A0gantija "Ġgantija") are notable for their gigantic structures. Although some late Eurasian Neolithic societies formed complex stratified chiefdoms or even states, states evolved in Eurasia only with the rise of metallurgy, and most Neolithic societies on the whole were relatively simple and egalitarian. Most clothing appears to have been made of animal skins, as indicated by finds of large numbers of bone and antler pins which are ideal for fastening leather. [Wool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woolen "Woolen") cloth and [linen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linen "Linen") might have become available during the later Neolithic, as suggested by finds of perforated stones that (depending on size) may have served as [spindle whorls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spindle_(textiles) "Spindle (textiles)") or [loom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loom "Loom") weights.

## Chalcolithic

In Old World archaeology, the "Chalcolithic", "Eneolithic", or "Copper Age" refers to a transitional period where early [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper "Copper") metallurgy appeared alongside the widespread use of stone tools. During this period, some weapons and tools were made of copper. This period was still largely Neolithic in character. It is a phase of the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age "Bronze Age") before it was discovered that adding [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin "Tin") to [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper "Copper") formed the harder [bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze "Bronze"). The Copper Age is seen as a transition period between the Stone Age and Bronze Age.

An archaeological site in [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia "Serbia") contains the oldest securely dated evidence of copper making at high temperature, from 7,500 years ago. The find in 2010 extends the known record of copper [smelting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smelting "Smelting") by about 800 years, and suggests that copper smelting may have been invented independently in separate parts of Asia and Europe at that time, rather than spreading from a single source. The emergence of [metallurgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgy "Metallurgy") may have occurred first in the [Fertile Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertile_Crescent "Fertile Crescent"), where it gave rise to the Bronze Age in the [4th millennium BCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_millennium_BCE "4th millennium BCE") (the traditional view), although finds from the [Vinča culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vin%C4%8Da_culture "Vinča culture") in Europe have now been securely dated to slightly earlier than those of the Fertile Crescent. [Timna Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timna_Valley "Timna Valley") contains evidence of copper mining 7,000 years ago. The process of transition from [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic "Neolithic") to Chalcolithic in the Middle East is characterized in archaeological stone tool assemblages by a decline in high quality raw material procurement and use. North Africa and the Nile Valley imported its iron technology from the [Near East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_East "Near East") and followed the Near Eastern course of Bronze Age and [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age "Iron Age") development.

## Bronze Age

The Bronze Age is the earliest period in which some civilizations reached the end of prehistory, by introducing written records. The Bronze Age is only considered to be part of prehistory for the regions and civilizations who didn't develop a system of keeping written records until later periods. The [invention of writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_writing "History of writing") coincides in some areas with the beginnings of the Bronze Age. After the appearance of writing, people started creating texts including written records of administrative matters.

The Bronze Age refers to a period in human cultural development when the most advanced [metalworking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metalworking "Metalworking") (at least in systematic and widespread use) included techniques for smelting copper and [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin "Tin") from naturally occurring outcroppings of ores, and then combining them to cast [bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze "Bronze"). These naturally occurring ores typically included [arsenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenic "Arsenic") as a common impurity. Tin ores are rare, as reflected in the fact there were no tin bronzes in [Western Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Asia "Western Asia") before 3000 BCE. The Bronze Age forms part of the three-age system for prehistoric societies. In this system, it follows the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic "Neolithic") in some areas of the world.

While copper is a common ore, deposits of tin are rare in the [Old World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World "Old World"), and often had to be traded or carried considerable distances from the few mines, stimulating the creation of extensive trading routes. In many areas as far apart as China and England, the valuable new material was used for weapons, but for a long time apparently not available for agricultural tools. Much of it seems to have been hoarded by social elites, and sometimes deposited in extravagant quantities, from [Chinese ritual bronzes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_ritual_bronze "Chinese ritual bronze") and [Indian copper hoards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_Hoard_Culture "Copper Hoard Culture"), to European [hoards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoard "Hoard") of unused axe-heads.

By the end of the Bronze Age large states, whose armies imposed themselves on people with a different culture, and are often called empires, had arisen in Egypt, China, [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia "Anatolia") (the [Hittites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittites "Hittites")), and [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Mesopotamia "History of Mesopotamia"), all of them literate.

## Iron Age

The Iron Age is not part of prehistory for all civilizations who had introduced written records during the Bronze Age. Most remaining civilizations did so during the Iron Age, often through conquest by empires, which continued to expand during this period. For example, in most of Europe conquest by the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire "Roman Empire") means the term Iron Age is replaced by "Roman", "[Gallo-Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallo-Roman "Gallo-Roman")", and similar terms after the conquest. Even before conquest, many areas began to have a protohistory, as they were written about by literate cultures; the [protohistory of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protohistory_of_Ireland "Protohistory of Ireland") is an example.

In archaeology, the Iron Age refers to the advent of [ferrous metallurgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrous_metallurgy "Ferrous metallurgy"). The adoption of [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron "Iron") coincided with other changes, often including more sophisticated agricultural practices, religious beliefs and artistic styles, which makes the archaeological Iron Age coincide with the "[Axial Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_Age "Axial Age")" in the history of philosophy. Although iron ore is common, the metalworking techniques necessary to use iron are different from those needed for the metal used earlier, more heat is required. Once the technical challenge had been solved, iron replaced bronze as its higher abundance meant armies could be armed much more easily with iron weapons.

### Paleolithic

[Lower Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Paleolithic "Lower Paleolithic")

* c. 3.3 million BP – Earliest stone tools
* c. 2.8 million BP – Genus *[Homo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo "Homo")* appears
* c. 600,000 BP – [Hunting-gathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunting-gathering "Hunting-gathering")
* c. 400,000 BP – [Control of fire by early humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_of_fire_by_early_humans "Control of fire by early humans")

[Middle Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Paleolithic "Middle Paleolithic")

* c. 300,000 BP – [Anatomically modern humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomically_modern_humans "Anatomically modern humans") *([Homo sapiens sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_sapiens_sapiens "Homo sapiens sapiens"))* appear in Africa, one of whose characteristics is a lack of significant body hair compared to other primates. See [Jebel Irhoud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jebel_Irhoud "Jebel Irhoud").
* c. 300,000–30,000 BP – [Mousterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mousterian "Mousterian") ([Neanderthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neanderthal "Neanderthal")) culture in Europe.
* c. 170,000–83,000 BP – Invention of [clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothing "Clothing")
* c. 75,000 BP – [Toba Volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toba_catastrophe_theory "Toba catastrophe theory") supereruption.
* c. 80,000–50,000 BP – *[Homo sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_sapiens "Homo sapiens")* exit Africa as a single population. In the next millennia, descendants from this population migrate to southern [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India"), [the Malay islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Malaysia "List of islands of Malaysia"), [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia"), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan"), China, [Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberia "Siberia"), [Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska "Alaska"), and the northwestern coast of North America.
* c. 80,000–50,000? BP – [Behavioral modernity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavioral_modernity "Behavioral modernity"), by this point including [language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language "Language") and sophisticated cognition

[Upper Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Paleolithic "Upper Paleolithic")

* c. 45,000 BP / 43,000 BCE – Beginnings of [Châtelperronian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C3%A2telperronian "Châtelperronian") culture in France.
* c. 43,000 BP - 37,000 BP The [Aurignacian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurignacian "Aurignacian") culture began, for example in the German [Swabian Jura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caves_and_Ice_Age_Art_in_the_Swabian_Jura "Caves and Ice Age Art in the Swabian Jura")
* c. 40,000 BP / 38,000 BCE – First human settlement in the [southern half of the Australian mainland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Australia "Southern Australia"), by [indigenous Australians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_Australian "Indigenous Australian") (including the future sites of [Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney "Sydney"), [Perth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth "Perth"), and [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne "Melbourne").)
* c. 32,000 BP / 30,000 BCE – Beginnings of [Aurignacian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurignacian "Aurignacian") culture, exemplified by the [cave paintings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_paintings "Cave paintings") ("[parietal art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parietal_art "Parietal art")") of [Chauvet Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvet_Cave "Chauvet Cave") in France.
* c. 30,500 BP / 28,500 BCE – New Guinea is populated by colonists from Asia or Australia.
* c. 30,000 BP / 28,000 BCE – A herd of [reindeer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reindeer "Reindeer") is slaughtered and butchered by humans in the Vezere Valley in what is today France.
* c. 28,000–20,000 BP – [Gravettian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravettian "Gravettian") period in Europe. Harpoons, needles, and saws invented.
* c. 26,500 BP – [Last Glacial Maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Maximum "Last Glacial Maximum") (LGM). Subsequently, the ice melts and the glaciers retreat again ([Late Glacial Maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Glacial_Maximum "Late Glacial Maximum")). During this latter period human beings return to Western Europe (see [Magdalenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalenian "Magdalenian") culture) and enter North America from Eastern Siberia for the first time (see [Paleo-Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleo-Indians "Paleo-Indians"), [pre-Clovis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Clovis "Pre-Clovis") culture and [Settlement of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_migration_and_settlement_of_the_Americas_from_Asia "Prehistoric migration and settlement of the Americas from Asia")).
* c. 26,000 BP / 24,000 BCE – People around the world use fibres to make baby-carriers, clothes, bags, baskets, and nets.
* c. 25,000 BP / 23,000 BCE – [A settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doln%C3%AD_V%C4%9Bstonice_(archaeology) "Dolní Věstonice (archaeology)") consisting of huts built of rocks and [mammoth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammoth "Mammoth") bones is founded near what is now [Dolní Věstonice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doln%C3%AD_V%C4%9Bstonice "Dolní Věstonice") in [Moravia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravia "Moravia") in the [Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Republic "Czech Republic"). This is the oldest human permanent settlement that has been found by archaeologists.
* c. 23,000 BP / 21,000 BCE – Small-scale trial cultivation of plants in [Ohalo II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohalo_II "Ohalo II"), a hunter-gatherers' sedentary camp on the shore of the Sea of Galilee, Israel.
* c. 16,000 BP / 14,000 BCE – [Wisent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisent "Wisent") (bison) sculpted in clay deep inside the cave now known as [Le Tuc d'Audoubert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_of_the_Trois-Fr%C3%A8res "Cave of the Trois-Frères") in the French [Pyrenees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrenees "Pyrenees") near what is now the border of Spain.
* c. 14,800 BP / 12,800 BCE – The [Humid Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_humid_period "African humid period") begins in North Africa. The region that would later become the [Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara "Sahara") is wet and fertile, and the [aquifers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquifer "Aquifer") are full.

### Mesolithic/Epipaleolithic

* c. 12,500 to 9,500 BCE – [Natufian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natufian_culture "Natufian culture"): a culture of sedentary hunter-gatherers who may have cultivated [rye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rye "Rye") in the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant "Levant") ([Eastern Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Mediterranean "Eastern Mediterranean"))

### Neolithic

* c. 9,400–9,200 BCE – [Figs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figs "Figs") of a [parthenocarpic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthenocarpic "Parthenocarpic") (and therefore sterile) type are cultivated in the early [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic "Neolithic") village [Gilgal I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilgal_I "Gilgal I") (in the [Jordan Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Valley_(Middle_East) "Jordan Valley (Middle East)"), 13 km north of [Jericho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jericho "Jericho")). The find predates the domestication of [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat "Wheat"), [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley "Barley"), and [legumes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legume "Legume"), and may thus be the first known instance of agriculture.
* c. 9,000 BCE – Circles of T-shaped stone pillars erected at [Göbekli Tepe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6bekli_Tepe "Göbekli Tepe") in the [Southeastern Anatolia Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeastern_Anatolia_Region "Southeastern Anatolia Region") of Turkey during [pre-pottery Neolithic A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-pottery_Neolithic_A "Pre-pottery Neolithic A") (PPNA) period. As yet unexcavated structures at the site are thought to date back to the epipaleolithic.
* c. 8,000–7,000 BCE – In northern [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia "Mesopotamia"), now northern [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq "Iraq"), cultivation of barley and wheat begins. At first they are used for [beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer "Beer"), [gruel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruel "Gruel"), and [soup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soup "Soup"), eventually for [bread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bread "Bread"). In early agriculture at this time the planting stick is used, but it is replaced by a primitive [plough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plough "Plough") in subsequent centuries. Around this time, a round stone tower, now preserved at about 8.5 meters high and 8.5 meters in diameter is built in [Jericho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jericho "Jericho").
* c. 4,700–4,500 BCE In Central Europe [Neolithic circulars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_circular_enclosures_in_Central_Europe "Neolithic circular enclosures in Central Europe") emerge.

### Chalcolithic

* c. 3,700 BCE – [Pictographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pictogram "Pictogram") [proto-writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-writing "Proto-writing"), known as [proto-cuneiform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuneiform#History "Cuneiform"), appears in [Sumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumer "Sumer"), and records begin to be kept. According to the majority of specialists, the first Mesopotamian writing (actually still pictographic proto-writing at this stage) was a tool for record-keeping that had little connection to the spoken language.
* c. 3,300 BCE – Approximate date of death of "[Ötzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96tzi "Ötzi") the Iceman", found preserved in ice in the [Ötztal Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96tztal_Alps "Ötztal Alps") in 1991. A copper-bladed axe, which is a characteristic technology of this era, was found with the corpse.
* c. 3,100 BCE – [Skara Brae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skara_Brae "Skara Brae") is constructed. This stone-built village consisted of ten clustered houses with stone hearths, beds, cupboards, and an ancient sewer system. This village was occupied for 600 years before being abandoned in c. 2,500 BCE.
* c. 3,000 BCE – [Stonehenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonehenge "Stonehenge") construction begins. In its first version, it consisted of a circular ditch and bank, with 56 wooden posts.
* c. 3,000 BCE – The [Yamnaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamnaya_culture "Yamnaya culture") expansions from the [Pontic–Caspian steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontic%E2%80%93Caspian_steppe "Pontic–Caspian steppe") into Europe and Asia. These migrations are thought to have spread Yamnaya [Steppe pastoralist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Steppe_Herders "Western Steppe Herders") ancestry and [Indo-European languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_languages "Indo-European languages") across large parts of Eurasia.
