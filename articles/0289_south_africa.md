# South Africa

**South Africa**, officially the **Republic of South Africa** (**RSA**), is the [southernmost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Africa "Southern Africa") country in [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa "Africa"). The country consists of [nine provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_South_Africa "Provinces of South Africa"), and is bounded to the south by 2,798 kilometres (1,739 miles) of [coastline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastline "Coastline") that stretches along the [South Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Ocean "Atlantic Ocean") and [Indian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean "Indian Ocean"); to the north by the neighbouring countries of [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia "Namibia"), [Botswana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botswana "Botswana"), and [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe "Zimbabwe"); and to the east and northeast by [Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique "Mozambique") and [Eswatini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eswatini "Eswatini"). South Africa also encloses [Lesotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesotho "Lesotho"). Covering an area of 1,221,037 square kilometres (471,445 square miles), the country has [a population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_South_Africa "Demographics of South Africa") of over 63 million people, making it the [sixth-most populated country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_African_countries_by_population "List of African countries by population") in Africa, and [24th-most](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population "List of countries and dependencies by population") in the world. [Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria "Pretoria") is the [administrative capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_city "Capital city"), while [Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town "Cape Town"), as the seat of [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_South_Africa "Parliament of South Africa"), is the legislative capital, and [Bloemfontein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloemfontein "Bloemfontein") is regarded as the judicial capital. The largest and most populous city is [Johannesburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannesburg "Johannesburg"), followed by Cape Town and [Durban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durban "Durban").

[Archaeological findings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cradle_of_Humankind "Cradle of Humankind") suggest that various [hominid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominid "Hominid") species existed in South Africa about 2.5 million years ago, and [modern humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_humans "Modern humans") inhabited the region over 100,000 years ago. The first known people were the indigenous [Khoisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoisan "Khoisan"), and [Bantu-speaking peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_peoples_of_South_Africa "Bantu peoples of South Africa"), who migrated in waves, from west and central Africa to the region 2,000 to 1,000 years ago. In the north, the [Kingdom of Mapungubwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Mapungubwe "Kingdom of Mapungubwe") formed in the 13th century, and the [Venda Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venda_Kingdom "Venda Kingdom") in the 17th century. In 1652, the [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_colonial_empire "Dutch colonial empire") established the first European settlement at [Table Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_Bay "Table Bay"), [Dutch Cape Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Cape_Colony "Dutch Cape Colony"). Its [invasion in 1795](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_the_Cape_Colony "Invasion of the Cape Colony"), and the [Battle of Blaauwberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blaauwberg "Battle of Blaauwberg") in 1806 led to British occupation. The [Mfecane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mfecane "Mfecane"), a period of significant upheaval, led to the formation of various African kingdoms, including the [Zulu Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulu_Kingdom "Zulu Kingdom"). The region was further colonised, and the [Mineral Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_Revolution "Mineral Revolution") saw a shift towards industrialisation and urbanisation. Following the [Second Boer War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Boer_War "Second Boer War"), the [Union of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_South_Africa "Union of South Africa") was [created in 1910](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_Act_1909 "South Africa Act 1909") after the amalgamation of the [Cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Colony "Cape Colony"), [Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Natal "Colony of Natal"), [Transvaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transvaal_Colony "Transvaal Colony"), and [Orange River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_River_Colony "Orange River Colony") colonies, becoming a [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic "Republic") after the [1961 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_South_African_republic_referendum "1960 South African republic referendum"). The multi-racial [Cape Qualified Franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Qualified_Franchise "Cape Qualified Franchise") in the Cape was gradually eroded, and the vast majority of [black South Africans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_South_Africans "Black South Africans") were not [enfranchised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffrage "Suffrage") until 1994.

The [National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Party_(South_Africa) "National Party (South Africa)") imposed [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid "Apartheid") in 1948, institutionalising previous [racial segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation "Racial segregation"). After a [struggle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_resistance_to_apartheid "Internal resistance to apartheid") by the [African National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_National_Congress "African National Congress") and other [anti-apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Apartheid_Movement "Anti-Apartheid Movement") activists, both inside and outside the country, the repeal of discriminatory laws began in the mid-1980s. Universal elections [took place in 1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_South_African_general_election "1994 South African general election"), following which all racial groups have held political representation in the country's [liberal democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_democracy "Liberal democracy"), which comprises a [parliamentary republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_republic "Parliamentary republic") and nine provinces. Recognised as a [middle power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_power "Middle power") in international affairs, South Africa maintains significant [regional influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_power "Regional power") and is a member of [BRICS+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRICS%2B "BRICS+"), the [African Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union "African Union") (hosting the seat of the [Pan-African Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-African_Parliament "Pan-African Parliament")), [SADC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_African_Development_Community "Southern African Development Community"), [SACU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_African_Customs_Union "Southern African Customs Union"), the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations "Commonwealth of Nations"), and the [G20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G20 "G20").

A [developing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_country "Developing country") and [newly industrialised country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newly_industrialised_country "Newly industrialised country"), it has the [largest economy in Africa by nominal GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_African_countries_by_GDP_(nominal) "List of African countries by GDP (nominal)"), is tied with [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia "Ethiopia") for the most [UNESCO World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_Africa "List of World Heritage Sites in Africa") in [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa "Africa"), and is a [biodiversity hotspot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_hotspot "Biodiversity hotspot") with unique [biomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomes "Biomes"), plant, and animal life. South Africa encompasses [a variety of cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_South_Africa "Culture of South Africa"), [languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_South_Africa "Languages of South Africa"), and [religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_South_Africa "Religion in South Africa"), and has been called the "[rainbow nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_nation "Rainbow nation")", especially in the wake of apartheid, to describe its diversity. Since the end of apartheid, government accountability and [quality of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_of_life "Quality of life") have substantially improved for non-white citizens. However, [crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_in_South_Africa "Crime in South Africa"), violence, [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_South_Africa "Poverty in South Africa"), and [inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inequality_in_post-apartheid_South_Africa "Inequality in post-apartheid South Africa") remain widespread, with about 32% of the population unemployed as of 2024[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=South_Africa&action=edit), while some 56% lived below the [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty "Poverty") line in 2014. Having the highest [Gini coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gini_coefficient "Gini coefficient") of 0.67, South Africa is considered one of the most economically unequal countries in the world.

## Etymology

The name "South Africa" is derived from the country's geographic location at the southern tip of Africa. Upon formation, the country was named the [Union of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_South_Africa "Union of South Africa") in English and *Unie van Zuid-Afrika* in [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language "Dutch language"), reflecting its origin from the unification of four British colonies. Since 1961, the long formal name in English has been the "Republic of South Africa" and *Republiek van Suid-Afrika* in [Afrikaans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrikaans "Afrikaans"). The country has an official name in [12 official languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_South_Africa "Languages of South Africa").

**Mzansi**, derived from the [Xhosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xhosa_language "Xhosa language") noun *uMzantsi* meaning "south", is a [colloquial name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colloquialism "Colloquialism") for South Africa, while some [Pan-Africanist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Africanism "Pan-Africanism") political parties prefer the term "[Azania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azania "Azania")", which is an archaic word for the coast of southeastern Africa.

## History

### Prehistory

South Africa contains some of the oldest archaeological and [human-fossil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_human_evolution_fossils "List of human evolution fossils") sites in the world. Archaeologists have recovered extensive fossil remains from a series of caves in [Gauteng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauteng "Gauteng") known as the [Cradle of Humankind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cradle_of_Humankind "Cradle of Humankind"), which has been declared a [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO") [World Heritage Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Site "World Heritage Site"). The sites include [Sterkfontein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterkfontein "Sterkfontein"), one of the richest sites for [hominin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominin "Hominin") fossils in the world, as well as [Swartkrans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swartkrans "Swartkrans"), [Gondolin Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondolin_Cave "Gondolin Cave"), [Kromdraai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kromdraai_fossil_site "Kromdraai fossil site"), [Cooper's Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper%27s_Cave "Cooper's Cave") and [Malapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malapa_Fossil_Site,_Cradle_of_Humankind "Malapa Fossil Site, Cradle of Humankind"). The first hominin fossil discovered in Africa, the [Taung Child](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taung_Child "Taung Child"), was found near [Taung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taung "Taung") in 1924. Other hominin remains have come from the sites of [Makapansgat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makapansgat "Makapansgat") in [Limpopo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limpopo "Limpopo"); [Cornelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelia,_Free_State "Cornelia, Free State") and [Florisbad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florisbad "Florisbad") in the [Free State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_State_(province) "Free State (province)"); [Border Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_Cave "Border Cave") in [KwaZulu-Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KwaZulu-Natal "KwaZulu-Natal"); [Klasies River Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klasies_River_Caves "Klasies River Caves") in the [Eastern Cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Cape "Eastern Cape"); and [Pinnacle Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinnacle_Point "Pinnacle Point"), [Elandsfontein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saldanha_man "Saldanha man") and Die Kelders Cave in the [Western Cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Cape "Western Cape").

These finds suggest that various hominid species existed in South Africa from about three million years ago, starting with *[Australopithecus africanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australopithecus_africanus "Australopithecus africanus"),* followed by *[Australopithecus sediba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australopithecus_sediba "Australopithecus sediba")*, *[Homo ergaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_ergaster "Homo ergaster")*, *[Homo erectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_erectus "Homo erectus")*, *[Homo rhodesiensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_rhodesiensis "Homo rhodesiensis")*, *[Homo helmei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_helmei "Homo helmei")*, *[Homo naledi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_naledi "Homo naledi")* and modern [humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human "Human") (*Homo sapiens*). Modern humans have inhabited Southern Africa for at least 170,000 years. Various researchers have located [pebble tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldowan "Oldowan") within the [Vaal River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaal_River "Vaal River") valley.

### Pre-colonial period

The indigenous [San](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_people "San people") and [Khoikhoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoikhoi "Khoikhoi") peoples of Southern Africa were predominantly [hunter-gatherers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherers "Hunter-gatherers") and, in the case of the Khoikhoi, also practised [pastoralism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastoralism "Pastoralism"). [Khoisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoisan "Khoisan") peoples may be the descendants of an early dispersal of [anatomically modern humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomically_modern_humans "Anatomically modern humans") to Southern Africa before 150,000 years ago. They were largely displaced or absorbed by the [Bantu expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_expansion "Bantu expansion") which took place between 1,500 and 2,000 years ago.

Bantu [settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settlers "Settlers") expanded from West Africa from approximately 3,000 BCE. Settlements of [Bantu-speaking peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_peoples_of_South_Africa "Bantu peoples of South Africa"), who were iron-using agriculturists and [herdsmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herder "Herder"), were present south of the [Limpopo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limpopo_River "Limpopo River") (now the northern border with [Botswana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botswana "Botswana") and [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe "Zimbabwe")) by the 4th or 5th century. The earliest [ironworks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironworks "Ironworks") in modern-day KwaZulu-Natal are believed to date from around 1050. The southernmost group was the [Xhosa people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xhosa_people "Xhosa people"), whose language incorporates certain linguistic traits from the Khoisan languages, and who eventually reached the [Great Fish River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Fish_River "Great Fish River") in the present-day Eastern Cape. As they migrated, these larger [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age "Iron Age") populations displaced or assimilated earlier peoples. In [Mpumalanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mpumalanga "Mpumalanga"), several stone circles have been found along with a stone arrangement that has been named [Adam's Calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaauboschkraal_stone_ruins "Blaauboschkraal stone ruins"), and the ruins are thought to be created by the [Bakone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bokoni "Bokoni"), a [Northern Sotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Sotho_language "Northern Sotho language") group.

Around 1220, in the [Limpopo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limpopo_River "Limpopo River")-[Shashe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashe_River "Shashe River") Basin, the elite of [K2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambandyanalo "Bambandyanalo") moved to settle the flat-topped summit of Mapungubwe Hill, with the population settling below. [Rainmaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainmaking_(ritual) "Rainmaking (ritual)") was crucial to the development of [sacral kingship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacral_kingship "Sacral kingship"), and several rainmaking sites have been found nearby including [Ratho Kroonkop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratho_Kroonkop "Ratho Kroonkop"). By 1250, the capital of the [Mapungubwe Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapungubwe_Kingdom "Mapungubwe Kingdom") had a population of 5,000 and the state covered 30,000 km2 (11,500 square miles), growing wealthy through the [Indian Ocean trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean_trade "Indian Ocean trade"). The events around Mapungubwe's collapse circa 1300 are unknown; however, trade routes shifted north from the Limpopo to the [Zambezi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambezi "Zambezi"), precipitating the rise of [Great Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Zimbabwe "Great Zimbabwe"). The hill was abandoned and Mapungubwe's population scattered.

In the [Soutpansberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soutpansberg "Soutpansberg"), interactions between early [Shona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shona_people "Shona people") inhabitants (possible from Mapungubwe), late-comer Shona and [Sotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sotho_people "Sotho people")-speakers between the 15th and 17th centuries culminated in the formation of the [Venda language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venda_language "Venda language") and identity. In the late-17th century, a splinter group from the dynasty of the [Rozvi Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rozvi_Empire "Rozvi Empire") migrated south; they were the Singo. The Singo settled at [Dzata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzata "Dzata"), which became the capital of the [Venda Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venda_Kingdom "Venda Kingdom"), and came to subdue all of the Soutpansberg. In the late-18th century, following trade routes moving south, the state collapsed. The most powerful dynasties that remained were the Ramabulana Singo in the western Soutpansberg, and the Tshivhase Singo and Mphaphuli Singo in the eastern Soutpansberg.

### Portuguese exploration

In 1487, the Portuguese explorer [Bartolomeu Dias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartolomeu_Dias "Bartolomeu Dias") led the first European voyage to land in southern Africa. On 4 December, he landed at Walfisch Bay (now known as [Walvis Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walvis_Bay "Walvis Bay") in present-day Namibia). This was south of the furthest point reached in 1485 by his predecessor, the Portuguese navigator [Diogo Cão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diogo_C%C3%A3o "Diogo Cão"), being [Cape Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Cross "Cape Cross"), north of the bay. Dias continued down the western coast of southern Africa. After 8 January 1488, prevented by storms from proceeding along the coast, he sailed out of sight of land and passed the southernmost point of Africa without seeing it. He reached as far up the eastern coast of Africa as, what he called, *Rio do Infante*, probably the present-day [Groot River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groot_River_(Eastern_Cape) "Groot River (Eastern Cape)"), in May 1488. On his return, he saw the cape, which he named *Cabo das Tormentas* ('Cape of Storms'). King [John II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_II_of_Portugal "John II of Portugal") renamed the point *Cabo da Boa Esperança*, or [Cape of Good Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_of_Good_Hope "Cape of Good Hope"), as it led to the riches of the [East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Indies "East Indies"). Dias' feat of navigation was immortalised in [Luís de Camões](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu%C3%ADs_de_Cam%C3%B5es "Luís de Camões")' 1572 epic poem, *[Os Lusíadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Os_Lus%C3%ADadas "Os Lusíadas")*.

In 1497 [Vasco Da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_Da_Gama "Vasco Da Gama") set sail from Lisbon and became the first European to reach India in 1498 via the Cape of Good Hope, opening up oceanic trade between Europe and Asia. He bypassed the coast of present-day South Africa and made landfall in present-day Mozambique.

### Dutch and British colonisation of the Cape

The Dutch first made contact with the coast of Southern Africa in 1595. With Portuguese maritime power declining in the early 17th century, English and Dutch merchants competed to dislodge Portugal's lucrative monopoly on the [spice trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice_trade "Spice trade"). British [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company "East India Company") representatives sporadically called at the Cape in search of provisions from as early as 1601 but later came to favour [Ascension Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascension_Island "Ascension Island") and [Saint Helena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Helena "Saint Helena") as ports of refuge. Dutch interest was aroused after 1647, when two employees of the [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company "Dutch East India Company") were shipwrecked at the Cape for several months, and managed to survive by obtaining fresh water and meat from the indigenous peoples, and sowing crops in the fertile soil. Upon their return to Holland, they reported favourably on the Cape's potential as a "warehouse and garden" for provisions to stock passing ships for long voyages.

In 1652, a century and a half after the discovery of the [Cape Route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Route "Cape Route"), [Jan van Riebeeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_van_Riebeeck "Jan van Riebeeck") established a *[victualling](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/victual#English "wikt:victual")* station at the Cape of Good Hope, at what would become [Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town "Cape Town"), on behalf of the Dutch East India Company. In time, the Cape became home to a large population of [Free Burghers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Burghers_in_the_Dutch_Cape_Colony "Free Burghers in the Dutch Cape Colony"). Dutch traders also brought thousands of [enslaved people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery "Slavery") to the fledgling colony from present-day Indonesia, Madagascar, and eastern Africa. Some of the earliest mixed race communities in the country were formed between Free Burghers, enslaved people, and indigenous peoples. This led to the development of a new ethnic group, the [Cape Coloureds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Coloureds "Cape Coloureds"), most of whom adopted the Dutch language and Christian faith. Conflicts over resources between the indigenous Khoisan people and Dutch settlers began in the 17th century and continued for centuries.

Dutch colonists' eastward expansion caused [wars with the southwesterly migrating Xhosa nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xhosa_Wars "Xhosa Wars"), as both sides competed for the pastureland near the Great Fish River, which the colonists desired for grazing cattle. Free Burghers who became independent farmers on the frontier were known as *[Boers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boers "Boers")*, with some (the *[trekboers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trekboers "Trekboers")*) adopting semi-nomadic lifestyles. The Boers formed loose [militias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militia "Militia"), which they termed *commandos*, and forged alliances with Khoisan peoples to repel Xhosa raids. Both sides launched bloody but inconclusive offensives, and sporadic violence, often accompanied by livestock theft, remained common for several decades.

Great Britain occupied Cape Town between 1795 and 1803 to prevent it from falling under the control of the [French First Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_First_Republic "French First Republic"), which had invaded the [Low Countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Countries "Low Countries"). After briefly returning to Dutch rule under the [Batavian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batavian_Republic "Batavian Republic") in 1803, the Cape was occupied again by the British in 1806. Following the end of the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars "Napoleonic Wars"), it was formally ceded to Great Britain and became an integral part of the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire"). British emigration to South Africa began around 1818, culminating in the arrival of the [1820 Settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1820_Settlers "1820 Settlers"), the purpose of which was primarily to increase the size of the European workforce and bolster frontier regions against Xhosa incursions.

### Mfecane and colonial expansion

In the early 1800s, the [Mfecane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mfecane "Mfecane") (lit. 'crushing') saw a heightened period of conflict, migration, and state formation among native groups, caused by the complex interplay of international trade, environmental instability, and European colonisation. Chiefdoms grew wealthier and competed over trade routes and grazing land, leading to the formation of the [Ndwandwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ndwandwe "Ndwandwe") and [Mthethwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mthethwa_Kingdom "Mthethwa Kingdom") Paramountcies in the east of the country. Ndwandwe defeated Mthethwa, which in turn split into several groups, one of which was led by [Shaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaka "Shaka") of the [amaZulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AmaZulu "AmaZulu"). The 1810s saw the fourth and fifth [Xhosa Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xhosa_Wars "Xhosa Wars") as British colonisation expanded. The Ndwandwe Paramountcy splintered amid costly raids, and Shaka's [Zulu Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulu_Kingdom "Zulu Kingdom") rose to fill the power vacuum. The [Gaza Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Empire "Gaza Empire") concurrently formed, and while the Zulu managed to totally defeat the Ndwandwe, they were repelled by Gaza.

During the early 19th century, many Dutch settlers departed from the [Cape Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Colony "Cape Colony"), where they had been subjected to British control, in a series of migrant groups who came to be known as *[Voortrekkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voortrekkers "Voortrekkers")* (lit. 'pathfinders' or 'pioneers'). They migrated to the future [Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natal_(province) "Natal (province)"), Free State, and [Transvaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transvaal_(province) "Transvaal (province)") regions, and following their victory over the Zulu Kingdom at the [Battle of Blood River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blood_River "Battle of Blood River") on 16 December 1838, founded the [Boer republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boer_republics "Boer republics"): the [Natalia Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalia_Republic "Natalia Republic"), the [South African Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Republic "South African Republic") and the [Orange Free State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Free_State "Orange Free State"). In the interior, the Cape Colony expanded at the expense of the [Batswana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tswana_people "Tswana people") and [Griqua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griqua_people "Griqua people"), and Boer expansion destabilised the middle Orange River region. The [Matabele kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matabele_kingdom "Matabele kingdom") came to dominate the eastern interior, and raided the [Venda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venda_people "Venda people") kingdom.

The discovery of diamonds in 1867 and gold in 1884 in the interior initiated the [Mineral Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_Revolution "Mineral Revolution"), which increased economic growth and immigration and intensified British subjugation of the indigenous peoples. The struggle to control these important economic resources shaped relations between Europeans and the indigenous population, as well as between the Boers and the British.

On 16 May 1876, President [Thomas François Burgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Fran%C3%A7ois_Burgers "Thomas François Burgers") of the South African Republic declared war against the [Pedi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedi_people "Pedi people"). [King Sekhukhune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sekhukhune "Sekhukhune") defeated the army on 1 August 1876, with another attack by the Lydenburg Volunteer Corps being similarly repulsed. On 16 February 1877, the two parties signed a peace treaty at [Botshabelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botshabelo,_Mpumalanga "Botshabelo, Mpumalanga"). The Boers' inability to subdue the Pedi led to the departure of Burgers in favour of [Paul Kruger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Kruger "Paul Kruger") and the British annexation of the South African Republic. In 1878 and 1879, three British attacks were successfully repelled until [Garnet Wolseley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garnet_Wolseley,_1st_Viscount_Wolseley "Garnet Wolseley, 1st Viscount Wolseley") defeated Sekhukhune in November 1879 with an army of 2,000 British soldiers, Boers and 10,000 Swazis.

The [Anglo-Zulu War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Zulu_War "Anglo-Zulu War") was fought in 1879 between the British and the [Zulu Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulu_Kingdom "Zulu Kingdom"). Following [Lord Carnarvon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Herbert,_4th_Earl_of_Carnarvon "Henry Herbert, 4th Earl of Carnarvon")'s successful introduction of [federation in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation "Canadian Confederation"), it was thought that similar political effort, coupled with military campaigns, might succeed with the African kingdoms, tribal areas and Boer republics in South Africa. In 1874, [Henry Bartle Frere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Bartle_Frere "Henry Bartle Frere") was sent to South Africa as the British [High Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Commissioner_for_Southern_Africa "High Commissioner for Southern Africa") to bring such plans into being. Among the obstacles were the presence of the independent states of the Boers, and the Zulu army. The Zulu nation defeated the British at the [Battle of Isandlwana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Isandlwana "Battle of Isandlwana") but ultimately lost the war, resulting in the termination of its independence.

### Boer Wars and independence

The Boer republics successfully resisted British encroachments during the [First Boer War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Boer_War "First Boer War") (1880–1881) using [guerrilla warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerrilla_warfare "Guerrilla warfare") tactics, which were well-suited to local conditions. The British returned with greater numbers, more experience, and new strategy in the [Second Boer War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Boer_War "Second Boer War") (1899–1902) and, although suffering heavy casualties due to Boer [attrition warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attrition_warfare "Attrition warfare"), were ultimately successful due in part to [scorched earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scorched_earth "Scorched earth") tactics and [concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Boer_War_concentration_camps "Second Boer War concentration camps"), in which 27,000 Boer civilians died due to a combination of disease and neglect. South Africa's urban population grew rapidly from the end of the 19th century onward. After the devastation of the wars, Boer farmers fled into [Transvaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transvaal_Colony "Transvaal Colony") and Orange Free State cities, many of whom would come to constitute an urban class of "[poor whites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poor_whites "Poor whites")".

Anti-British policies among white South Africans focused on independence. During the Dutch and British colonial years, [racial segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation "Racial segregation") was mostly informal, though some legislation was enacted to control the settlement and movement of indigenous people, including the [Native Location Act of 1879](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Location_Act_of_1879 "Native Location Act of 1879") and the system of [pass laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pass_laws "Pass laws").

Eight years after the end of the Second Boer War, and after four years of negotiation, the [South Africa Act 1909](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_Act_1909 "South Africa Act 1909") created the [Union of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_South_Africa "Union of South Africa") on 31 May 1910, granting the country nominal independence. The union was a [dominion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion "Dominion") that included the former territories of the Cape, Transvaal and Natal colonies, as well as the Orange Free State republic. The [Natives Land Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natives_Land_Act,_1913 "Natives Land Act, 1913") of 1913, which was passed by the British ruling the Parliament of the newly formed Union of South Africa, severely restricted the ownership of land by [black South Africans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_South_Africans "Black South Africans"), who at that stage controlled only 7% of the country. The amount of land reserved for indigenous peoples was later marginally increased.

In 1931, the union became fully sovereign from the United Kingdom with the passage of the [Statute of Westminster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_Westminster_1931 "Statute of Westminster 1931"), which abolished the last powers of the [Parliament of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_the_United_Kingdom "Parliament of the United Kingdom") to legislate in the country. In 1934, the [South African Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Party "South African Party") and [National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Party_(South_Africa) "National Party (South Africa)") merged to form the [United Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Party_(South_Africa) "United Party (South Africa)"), seeking reconciliation between [Afrikaners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrikaner "Afrikaner") and English-speaking whites. In 1939, the party split over the entry of the Union into [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") as an ally of the United Kingdom, a move which National Party followers opposed.

### Apartheid era

In 1948, the National Party was elected to power. It strengthened the racial segregation begun under Dutch and British colonial rule. Taking Canada's [Indian Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Act "Indian Act") as a framework, the [nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism "Nationalism") government classified all peoples into three races (being "Whites", "Blacks", and "Indians and Coloured people") and developed rights and limitations for each. The white-led [minoritarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoritarian "Minoritarian") government instituted a policy of legally institutionalised segregation which came to be known as *[apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid "Apartheid")*. While whites enjoyed a higher [standard of living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_of_living "Standard of living") comparable to [First World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World "First World") Western nations, the black majority remained disadvantaged by almost every standard, including income, education, housing, and life expectancy. The [Freedom Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Charter "Freedom Charter"), adopted in 1955 by the [Congress Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_Alliance "Congress Alliance"), which was multi-racial in makeup, demanded a non-racial society and an end to discrimination.

On 31 May 1961, the country became a republic following [a referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_South_African_republic_referendum "1960 South African republic referendum") (only open to white voters) which narrowly passed; the British-dominated Natal province largely voted against the proposal. [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II "Elizabeth II") lost the title [Queen of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_South_Africa "Monarchy of South Africa"), and the last [Governor-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_South_Africa "Governor-General of South Africa"), [Charles Robberts Swart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._R._Swart "C. R. Swart"), became [state president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_President_of_South_Africa "State President of South Africa"). As a concession to the [Westminster system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_system "Westminster system"), the appointment of the president remained by parliament, and the position was virtually powerless until [P. W. Botha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._W._Botha "P. W. Botha")'s [Constitution Act of 1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Constitution_of_1983 "South African Constitution of 1983"), which eliminated the office of [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_South_Africa "Prime Minister of South Africa") and instated a unique "strong presidency" [responsible to parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system "Parliamentary system"). Pressured by other [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations "Commonwealth of Nations") countries, South Africa withdrew from the organisation in 1961.

Despite [opposition to apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_resistance_to_apartheid "Internal resistance to apartheid") both within and outside the country from all racial backgrounds, the government legislated for a continuation of apartheid. The security forces cracked down on internal dissent, and violence became widespread, with anti-apartheid organisations such as the [African National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_National_Congress "African National Congress") (ANC), the [Azanian People's Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azanian_People%27s_Organisation "Azanian People's Organisation"), and the [Pan-Africanist Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Africanist_Congress_of_Azania "Pan Africanist Congress of Azania") carrying out guerrilla warfare, urban sabotage and, according to [Oxford Academic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_Academic "Oxford Academic"), [acts of terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism "Terrorism"). The three rival resistance movements also engaged in occasional inter-factional clashes as they jockeyed for domestic influence. Apartheid became increasingly controversial, and several countries began to boycott business with the South African government because of its racial policies. The boycotts and restrictions were later extended to international sanctions and the [divestment of holdings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disinvestment_from_South_Africa "Disinvestment from South Africa") by foreign investors.

### Post-apartheid

The [Mahlabatini Declaration of Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahlabatini_Declaration_of_Faith "Mahlabatini Declaration of Faith"), signed by [Mangosuthu Buthelezi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangosuthu_Buthelezi "Mangosuthu Buthelezi") and [Harry Schwarz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Schwarz "Harry Schwarz") in 1974, enshrined the principles of [peaceful transition of power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peaceful_transition_of_power "Peaceful transition of power") and equality for all, the first of such agreements by black and white political leaders in South Africa. Ultimately, [F.W. de Klerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._W._de_Klerk "F. W. de Klerk") opened bilateral discussions with [Nelson Mandela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Mandela "Nelson Mandela") in 1993 for a transition of policies and government.

In 1990, the National Party government took the first step towards dismantling apartheid when it lifted the ban on the ANC and other political organisations. It released Nelson Mandela from prison after 27 years of serving a sentence for sabotage and treason. A [negotiation process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negotiations_to_end_apartheid_in_South_Africa "Negotiations to end apartheid in South Africa") followed. With approval from the white electorate in a [1992 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_South_African_apartheid_referendum "1992 South African apartheid referendum"), the government continued negotiations to end apartheid. South Africa held its first universal [elections in 1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_South_African_general_election "1994 South African general election"), which the ANC won by an overwhelming majority. The country rejoined the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations "Commonwealth of Nations") and became a member of the [Southern African Development Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_African_Development_Community "Southern African Development Community").

The post-apartheid period, whilst plagued by high unemployment and increasing income inequality, has been characterised by relative political stability. The country has, however, seen periodic outbreaks of violence, most notably the [2008 xenophobic riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_2008_South_Africa_riots "May 2008 South Africa riots")—during which over 60 people were killed, and an estimated 100,000 people were driven from their homes—and the [Marikana massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marikana_massacre "Marikana massacre"), which constituted the most lethal use of force by South African security forces against civilians since the Soweto uprising. Widespread [political corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_South_Africa "Corruption in South Africa") and [state capture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_capture "State capture") took place under the [presidency of Jacob Zuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Jacob_Zuma "Presidency of Jacob Zuma"), whose brief imprisonment for [contempt of court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contempt_of_court "Contempt of court") during the course of his [trial for corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Zuma_corruption_charges "Jacob Zuma corruption charges") in 2021 led to [widespread unrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest "2021 South African unrest") which left 354 people dead.

The [electoral dominance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominant_party_system "Dominant party system") of the ANC has gradually declined since the early 2000s, with the party failing to secure a parliamentary majority for the first time in the [2024 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_South_African_general_election "2024 South African general election"). President [Cyril Ramaphosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyril_Ramaphosa "Cyril Ramaphosa") subsequently formed the [Government of National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_cabinet_of_Cyril_Ramaphosa "Third cabinet of Cyril Ramaphosa") with, amongst others, the [Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alliance_(South_Africa) "Democratic Alliance (South Africa)").

## Geography

South Africa is in [southern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Africa "Southern Africa"), with a coastline that stretches more than 2,500 km (1,553 mi) and along two oceans—the [South Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic "South Atlantic") and the [Indian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean "Indian Ocean"). At 1,219,912 km2 (471,011 sq mi) in area, South Africa is the 24th-largest country in the world. Excluding the [Prince Edward Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Edward_Islands "Prince Edward Islands"), the country lies between latitudes [22°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22nd_parallel_south "22nd parallel south") and [35°S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/35th_parallel_south "35th parallel south"), and longitudes [16°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16th_meridian_east "16th meridian east") and [33°E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/33rd_meridian_east "33rd meridian east"). The interior of South Africa consists of a large, and in most places almost flat, [plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plateau "Plateau") with an altitude of between 1,000 m (3,300 ft) and 2,100 m (6,900 ft). It is highest in the east and slopes gently downwards towards the west and north, and slightly to the south and south-west. This plateau is surrounded by the [Great Escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Escarpment,_Southern_Africa "Great Escarpment, Southern Africa") whose eastern, and highest, stretch is known as the [Drakensberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drakensberg "Drakensberg"). [Mafadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mafadi "Mafadi") in the Drakensberg is the highest peak in the country at 3,450 m (11,320 ft) above sea level. The KwaZulu-Natal–Lesotho international border is formed by the highest portion of the Great Escarpment which reaches an altitude of over 3,000 m (9,800 ft).

The south and south-western parts of the plateau (at approximately 1,100–1,800 m above sea level) and the adjoining plain below (at approximately 700–800 m above sea level – see map on the right) are together known as the [Great Karoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karoo "Karoo"), which consists of sparsely populated [shrubland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrubland "Shrubland"). To the north, the Great Karoo fades into the more arid [Bushmanland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bushmanland,_Northern_Cape "Bushmanland, Northern Cape"), which eventually becomes the [Kalahari Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalahari_Desert "Kalahari Desert") in the north-west of the country. The mid-eastern and highest part of the plateau is known as the [Highveld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highveld "Highveld"). This relatively well-watered area is home to a great proportion of the country's commercial farmlands and contains its largest conurbation, [Gauteng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauteng#Demographics#Urban_conurbation "Gauteng"). To the north of the Highveld, from about the 25° 30' S line of latitude, the plateau slopes downwards into the [Bushveld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bushveld "Bushveld"), which ultimately gives way to the [Limpopo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limpopo_River "Limpopo River") lowlands, the [Lowveld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veld#Highveld_and_Lowveld "Veld").

The coastal belt, below the Great Escarpment, moving clockwise from the northeast, consists of the Limpopo Lowveld, which merges into the Mpumalanga Lowveld, below the Mpumalanga Drakensberg (the eastern portion of the Great Escarpment). This is hotter, drier and less intensely cultivated than the Highveld above the escarpment. The [Kruger National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kruger_National_Park "Kruger National Park"), located in the provinces of Limpopo and Mpumalanga in north-eastern South Africa, occupies a large portion of the Lowveld, covering 19,633 square kilometres (7,580 sq mi)

The coastal belt below the south and south-western stretches of the Great Escarpment contains several ranges of [Cape Fold Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Fold_Belt "Cape Fold Belt") which run parallel to the coast, separating the Great Escarpment from the ocean. The land between the [Outeniqua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outeniqua_Mountains "Outeniqua Mountains") and [Langeberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langeberg "Langeberg") ranges to the south and the [Swartberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swartberg "Swartberg") range to the north is known as the [Little Karoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karoo "Karoo"), which consists of semi-desert shrubland similar to that of the Great Karoo, except that its northern strip along the foothills of the Swartberg Mountains has a somewhat higher rainfall and is, therefore, more cultivated than the Great Karoo.

The Little Karoo is famous for its [ostrich farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_ostrich#Humans#Economic_use "Common ostrich") around [Oudtshoorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oudtshoorn "Oudtshoorn"). The lowland area to the north of the Swartberg range up to the Great Escarpment is the lowland part of the Great Karoo, which is climatically and botanically almost indistinguishable from the Karoo above the Great Escarpment. The narrow coastal strip between the Outeniqua and Langeberg ranges and the ocean, known as the [Garden Route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_Route "Garden Route"), has a moderately high year-round rainfall, and hosts the [most extensive area of forest in the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knysna%E2%80%93Amatole_montane_forests "Knysna–Amatole montane forests").

In the south-west corner of the country, the [Cape Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Peninsula "Cape Peninsula") forms the southernmost tip of the coastal strip which borders the Atlantic Ocean and ultimately terminates at the country's border with Namibia at the [Orange River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_River "Orange River"). The Cape Peninsula has a [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate "Mediterranean climate"), making it and its immediate surrounds the only portion of [Sub-Saharan Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-Saharan_Africa "Sub-Saharan Africa") which receives most of its rainfall in winter.

The coastal belt to the north of the Cape Peninsula is bounded on the west by the Atlantic Ocean and the first row of north–south running Cape Fold Mountains to the east. The Cape Fold Mountains peter out at about the 32° S line of latitude, after which the Great Escarpment bounds the coastal plain. The most southerly portion of this coastal belt is known as the [Swartland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swartland "Swartland") and Malmesbury Plain, which is an important wheat-growing region, relying on winter rains. The region further north is known as [Namaqualand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namaqualand "Namaqualand"), which becomes more arid near the Orange River. The little rain that falls tends to fall in winter, which results in one of the world's most spectacular displays of flowers carpeting huge stretches of [veld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veld "Veld") in spring (August–September).

South Africa also has one offshore possession, the small [sub-Antarctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subantarctic "Subantarctic") archipelago of the Prince Edward Islands, consisting of [Marion Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marion_Island "Marion Island") (290 km2 or 110 sq mi) and [Prince Edward Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Edward_Islands "Prince Edward Islands") (45 km2 or 17 sq mi).

### Climate

South Africa has a generally [temperate climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_climate "Temperate climate") because it is surrounded by the Atlantic and Indian Oceans on three sides, because it is located in the climatically milder [Southern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Hemisphere "Southern Hemisphere"), and because its average elevation rises steadily toward the north (toward the equator) and further inland. This varied topography and oceanic influence result in a great variety of climatic zones. The climatic zones range from the extreme desert of the southern [Namib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namib "Namib") in the farthest northwest to the lush subtropical climate in the east along the border with Mozambique and the Indian Ocean. Winters in South Africa occur between June and August. The extreme southwest has a climate similar to that of the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate "Mediterranean climate") with wet winters and hot, dry summers, hosting the famous [fynbos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fynbos "Fynbos") [biome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biome "Biome") of shrubland and [thicket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albany_thickets "Albany thickets"). This area produces much of the wine in South Africa and is known for its wind, which blows intermittently almost all year. The severity of this wind made passing around the Cape of Good Hope particularly treacherous for sailors, causing many shipwrecks. Further east on the south coast, rainfall is distributed more evenly throughout the year, producing a green landscape. The annual rainfall increases south of the Lowveld, especially near the coast, which is [subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropics "Subtropics"). The Free State is particularly flat because it lies centrally on the high plateau. North of the [Vaal River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaal_River "Vaal River"), the Highveld becomes better watered and does not experience subtropical extremes of heat. Johannesburg, in the centre of the Highveld, is at 1,740 m (5,709 ft) above sea level and receives an annual rainfall of 760 mm (29.9 in). Winters in this region are cold, although snow is rare.

The coldest place on mainland South Africa is Buffelsfontein in the [Eastern Cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Cape "Eastern Cape"), where a temperature of −20.1 °C (−4.2 °F) was recorded in 2013. The Prince Edward Islands have colder average annual temperatures, but Buffelsfontein has colder extremes. The deep interior of mainland South Africa has the hottest temperatures: a temperature of 51.7 °C (125.06 °F) was recorded in 1948 in the Northern Cape Kalahari near [Upington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upington "Upington"), but this temperature is unofficial and was not recorded with standard equipment; the official highest temperature is 48.8 °C (119.84 °F) at [Vioolsdrif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vioolsdrif "Vioolsdrif") in January 1993.

[Climate change in South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_South_Africa "Climate change in South Africa") is leading to increased temperatures and rainfall variability. [Extreme weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_weather "Extreme weather") events are becoming more prominent. This is a critical concern for South Africans as climate change will affect the overall status and wellbeing of the country, for example with regards to [water resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_resources "Water resources"). Speedy environmental changes are resulting in clear effects on the community and environmental level in different ways and aspects, starting with air quality, to temperature and weather patterns, reaching out to food security and disease burden. According to computer-generated climate modelling produced by the South African National Biodiversity Institute, parts of southern Africa will see an increase in temperature by about 1 °C (1.8 °F) along the coast to more than 4 °C (7.2 °F) in the already hot [hinterland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinterland "Hinterland") such as the Northern Cape in late spring and summertime by 2050. The [Cape Floral Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Floristic_Region "Cape Floristic Region") is predicted to be hit very hard by climate change. Drought, increased intensity and frequency of fire, and climbing temperatures are expected to push many rare species towards extinction. South Africa has published two national climate change reports in 2011 and 2016. South Africa contributes considerable [carbon dioxide emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions "Greenhouse gas emissions"), being the 14th largest emitter of carbon dioxide, primarily from its heavy reliance on coal and oil for [energy production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_development "Energy development"). As part of its international commitments, South Africa has pledged to peak emissions between 2020 and 2025.

### Biodiversity

South Africa signed the Rio [Convention on Biological Diversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_on_Biological_Diversity "Convention on Biological Diversity") on 4 June 1994 and became a party to the convention on 2 November 1995. It has subsequently produced a [National Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_action_plan "Biodiversity action plan"), which was received by the convention on 7 June 2006. The country is ranked sixth out of the world's seventeen [megadiverse countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megadiverse_countries "Megadiverse countries"). [Ecotourism in South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism_in_South_Africa "Ecotourism in South Africa") has become more prevalent in recent years, as a possible method of maintaining and improving biodiversity.

Numerous mammals are found in the Bushveld including lions, [African leopards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_leopard "African leopard"), [South African cheetahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_African_cheetah "Southeast African cheetah"), [southern white rhinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_white_rhinoceros "Southern white rhinoceros"), [blue wildebeest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_wildebeest "Blue wildebeest"), [kudus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kudu "Kudu"), [impalas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impala "Impala"), [hyenas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyena "Hyena"), [hippopotamuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippopotamus "Hippopotamus") and [South African giraffes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_giraffe "South African giraffe"). A significant extent of the Bushveld exists in the north-east including Kruger National Park and the [Sabi Sand Game Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabi_Sand_Game_Reserve "Sabi Sand Game Reserve"), as well as in the far north in the [Waterberg Biosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterberg_Biosphere "Waterberg Biosphere"). South Africa houses many [endemic species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism "Endemism"), among them the critically endangered [riverine rabbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverine_rabbit "Riverine rabbit") (*Bunolagus monticullaris*) in the Karoo.

Up to 1945, more than 4,900 species of [fungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fungus "Fungus") (including [lichen-forming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichen#Fungi "Lichen") species) had been recorded. In 2006, the number of fungi in South Africa was estimated at 200,000 species but did not take into account fungi associated with insects. If correct, then the number of South African fungi dwarfs that of its plants. In at least some major South African ecosystems, an exceptionally high percentage of fungi are highly specific in terms of the plants with which they occur. The country's Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan does not mention fungi (including lichen-forming fungi).

With more than 22,000 different [vascular plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vascular_plant "Vascular plant"), or about 9% of all the known species of plants on Earth, South Africa is particularly rich in plant diversity. The most prevalent biome is the [grassland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grassland "Grassland"), particularly on the Highveld, where the plant cover is dominated by different [grasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poaceae "Poaceae"), low shrubs, and [acacia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acacia "Acacia"), mainly camel-thorn (*[Vachellia erioloba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vachellia_erioloba "Vachellia erioloba")*). Vegetation is sparse towards the north-west because of low rainfall. There are numerous species of water-storing succulents, like [aloes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aloe "Aloe") and [euphorbias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphorbia "Euphorbia"), in the very hot and dry Namaqualand area. And according to the [World Wildlife Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Fund_for_Nature "World Wide Fund for Nature"), South Africa is home to around a third of all succulent species. The grass and thorn [savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savanna "Savanna") turns slowly into a bush savanna towards the north-east of the country, with denser growth. There are significant numbers of [baobab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adansonia "Adansonia") trees in this area, near the northern end of Kruger National Park.

The [fynbos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fynbos "Fynbos") biome, which makes up the majority of the area and plant life in the [Cape Floristic Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Floristic_Region "Cape Floristic Region"), is located in a small region of the Western Cape and contains more than 9,000 of those species, or three times more plant species than found in the [Amazon rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_rainforest "Amazon rainforest"), making it among the richest regions on Earth in terms of plant diversity. Most of the plants are [evergreen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evergreen "Evergreen") hard-leaf plants with fine, needle-like leaves, such as the [sclerophyllous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sclerophyll "Sclerophyll") plants. Another uniquely South African flowering plant group is the genus *[Protea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protea "Protea")*, with around 130 different species. While South Africa has a great wealth of flowering plants, only 1% of the land is forest, almost exclusively in the humid [coastal plain of KwaZulu-Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KwaZulu%E2%80%93Cape_coastal_forest_mosaic "KwaZulu–Cape coastal forest mosaic"), where there are also areas of [Southern Africa mangroves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Africa_mangroves "Southern Africa mangroves") in river mouths. Even smaller reserves of forests are out of the reach of fire, known as [montane forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knysna%E2%80%93Amatole_montane_forests "Knysna–Amatole montane forests"). Plantations of imported tree species are predominant, particularly the non-native [eucalyptus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucalyptus "Eucalyptus") and pine.

South Africa has lost a large area of natural habitat in the last four decades, primarily because of overpopulation, sprawling development patterns, and deforestation during the 19th century. The country had a 2019 [Forest Landscape Integrity Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Landscape_Integrity_Index "Forest Landscape Integrity Index") mean score of 4.94/10, ranking it 112th globally out of 172 countries. South Africa is one of the worst affected countries in the world when it comes to invasion by [alien species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introduced_species "Introduced species") with many (e.g., [black wattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acacia_mearnsii "Acacia mearnsii"), [Port Jackson willow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acacia_saligna "Acacia saligna"), *[Hakea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakea "Hakea")*, *[Lantana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lantana "Lantana")* and *[Jacaranda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacaranda "Jacaranda")*) posing a significant threat to the native biodiversity and the already scarce water resources. Also, [woody plant encroachment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_plant_encroachment "Woody plant encroachment") of native plants in grasslands poses a threat to biodiversity and related ecosystem services, affecting over 7 million hectares. The original [temperate forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_broadleaf_and_mixed_forests "Temperate broadleaf and mixed forests") found by the first European settlers was exploited until only small patches remained. Currently, South African hardwood trees like [real yellowwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_yellowwood "Real yellowwood") (*[Podocarpus latifolius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podocarpus_latifolius "Podocarpus latifolius")*), stinkwood (*[Ocotea bullata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocotea_bullata "Ocotea bullata")*), and South African black ironwood (*[Olea capensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olea_capensis "Olea capensis")*) are under strict government protection. Statistics from the [Department of Environmental Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Environment,_Forestry_and_Fisheries "Department of Environment, Forestry and Fisheries") show a record 1,215 rhinos were killed in 2014. Since South Africa is home to a third of all succulent species (many endemic to the Karoo), it makes it a hotspot for plant poaching, leading to many species to be threatened with extinction.

## Government and politics

South Africa is a [parliamentary republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_republic "Parliamentary republic"), but unlike most such republics, the [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_South_Africa "President of South Africa") is both [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state "Head of state") and [head of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_government "Head of government") and depends for their tenure on the [confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence_and_supply "Confidence and supply") of [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_South_Africa "Parliament of South Africa"). The executive, legislature, and judiciary are all subject to the supremacy of the [Constitution of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_South_Africa "Constitution of South Africa"), and the [superior courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courts_of_South_Africa "Courts of South Africa") have the power to strike down executive actions and acts of Parliament if they are unconstitutional. The [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_South_Africa "National Assembly of South Africa"), the lower house of Parliament, consists of 400 members and is elected every five years by a system of [party-list proportional representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party-list_proportional_representation "Party-list proportional representation"). The [National Council of Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_of_Provinces "National Council of Provinces"), the upper house, consists of ninety members, with each of the nine [provincial legislatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_legislature_(South_Africa) "Provincial legislature (South Africa)") electing ten members.

After each parliamentary election, the National Assembly elects one of its members as president; hence the president serves a term of office the same as that of the Assembly, normally five years. No president may serve more than two terms in office. The president appoints a [deputy president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_President_of_South_Africa "Deputy President of South Africa") and [ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_(government) "Minister (government)") (each representing a [department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_(government_department) "Ministry (government department)")) who form the [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_South_Africa "Cabinet of South Africa"). The National Assembly may remove the president and the cabinet by a [motion of no confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_of_no_confidence "Motion of no confidence"). In the [most recent election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_South_African_general_election "2024 South African general election"), held on 29 May 2024, the ANC lost its majority for the first time since the end of Apartheid, winning only 40% of the vote and 159 seats, while the main opposition, the [Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alliance_(South_Africa) "Democratic Alliance (South Africa)") (DA), won 22% of the vote and 87 seats. [uMkhonto weSizwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UMkhonto_weSizwe_(political_party) "UMkhonto weSizwe (political party)"), a new party founded by former [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_South_Africa "President of South Africa") and ANC leader [Jacob Zuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Zuma "Jacob Zuma"), won 14.6% of the vote and 58 seats, while the [Economic Freedom Fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Freedom_Fighters "Economic Freedom Fighters"), founded by [Julius Malema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Malema "Julius Malema"), former president of the [ANC Youth League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_National_Congress_Youth_League "African National Congress Youth League") who was later expelled from the ANC, won 9.5% of the vote and 39 seats. After the election, the ANC formed a [Government of National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_National_Unity_(South_Africa) "Government of National Unity (South Africa)") with the DA and several smaller parties.

South Africa has no legally defined capital city. The fourth chapter of the constitution states "The seat of Parliament is Cape Town, but an Act of Parliament enacted in accordance with section 76(1) and (5) may determine that the seat of Parliament is elsewhere." The country's three branches of government are split over different cities. Cape Town, as the seat of Parliament, is the legislative capital; Pretoria, as the seat of the president and cabinet, is the administrative capital; and Bloemfontein is the seat of the [Supreme Court of Appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Appeal_(South_Africa) "Supreme Court of Appeal (South Africa)"), and is regarded as the judicial capital; although the highest court, the [Constitutional Court of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_South_Africa "Constitutional Court of South Africa") has been based in Johannesburg since 1994. Most foreign embassies are located in Pretoria.

Since 2004, South Africa has had many thousands of popular protests, some violent, making it, according to one academic, the "most protest-rich country in the world". There have been numerous incidents of [political repression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_repression_in_post-apartheid_South_Africa "Political repression in post-apartheid South Africa") as well as threats of future repression in violation of the constitution, leading some analysts and [civil society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_society "Civil society") organisations to conclude that there is or could be a new climate of political repression.

In 2022, South Africa was placed sixth out of 48 sub-Saharan African countries on the [Ibrahim Index of African Governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Index_of_African_Governance "Ibrahim Index of African Governance"). South Africa scored well in the categories of [Rule of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_law "Rule of law"), [Transparency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transparency_(behavior) "Transparency (behavior)"), Corruption, [Participation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_participation_(decision_making) "Public participation (decision making)") and Human Rights, but scored low in Safety and Security. In 2006, South Africa became one of the first jurisdictions in the world to legalise [same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_South_Africa "Same-sex marriage in South Africa").

The Constitution of South Africa is the supreme rule of law in the country. The primary sources of [South African law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_South_Africa "Law of South Africa") are [Roman-Dutch mercantile law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman-Dutch_law "Roman-Dutch law") and personal law and [English Common law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_law "English law"), as imports of Dutch settlements and British colonialism. The first European-based law in South Africa was brought by the Dutch East India Company and is called Roman-Dutch law. It was imported before the [codification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codification_(law) "Codification (law)") of European law into the [Napoleonic Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Code "Napoleonic Code") and is comparable in many ways to [Scots law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scots_law "Scots law"). This was followed in the 19th century by English law, both [common](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_law "Common law") and [statutory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statutory_law "Statutory law"). After unification in 1910, South Africa had its own parliament which passed laws specific for South Africa, building on those previously passed for the individual member colonies. The judicial system consists of the [magistrates' courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magistrate%27s_court_(South_Africa) "Magistrate's court (South Africa)"), which hear lesser criminal cases and smaller civil cases; the [High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_South_Africa "High Court of South Africa"), which has divisions that serve as the courts of [general jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_jurisdiction "General jurisdiction") for specific areas; the Supreme Court of Appeal; and the Constitutional Court, which is the highest court.

### Administrative divisions

Each of the nine provinces is governed by a [unicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicameralism "Unicameralism") [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_legislature_(South_Africa) "Provincial legislature (South Africa)"), which is elected every five years by [party-list proportional representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party-list_proportional_representation "Party-list proportional representation"). The legislature elects a [premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_(South_Africa) "Premier (South Africa)") as head of government, and the premier appoints an [Executive Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Council_(South_Africa) "Executive Council (South Africa)") as a provincial cabinet. The powers of provincial governments are limited to topics listed in the constitution; these topics include such fields as health, education, public housing and transport.

The provinces are in turn divided into 52 [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_South_Africa "Districts of South Africa"): eight [metropolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_municipality_(South_Africa) "Metropolitan municipality (South Africa)") and 44 [district municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_municipality_(South_Africa) "District municipality (South Africa)"). The district municipalities are further subdivided into 205 [local municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_municipality_(South_Africa) "Local municipality (South Africa)"). The metropolitan municipalities, which govern the largest urban agglomerations, perform the functions of both district and local municipalities.

### Foreign relations

As the Union of South Africa, the country is a founding member of the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations") (UN), with Prime Minister [Jan Smuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Smuts "Jan Smuts") writing the [preamble to the UN Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preamble_to_the_United_Nations_Charter "Preamble to the United Nations Charter"). South Africa is one of the founding members of the [African Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union "African Union") (AU) and has the [largest economy of all the members](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_African_countries_by_GDP_(nominal) "List of African countries by GDP (nominal)"). It is a founding member of the AU's [New Partnership for Africa's Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Partnership_for_Africa%27s_Development "New Partnership for Africa's Development"). After apartheid ended, South Africa was readmitted to the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations "Commonwealth of Nations"). The country is a member of the [Group of 77](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_77 "Group of 77") and chaired the organisation in 2006. South Africa is also a member of the [Southern African Development Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_African_Development_Community "Southern African Development Community"), [South Atlantic Peace and Cooperation Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic_Peace_and_Cooperation_Zone "South Atlantic Peace and Cooperation Zone"), [Southern African Customs Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_African_Customs_Union "Southern African Customs Union"), [Antarctic Treaty System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic_Treaty_System "Antarctic Treaty System"), [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization "World Trade Organization"), International Monetary Fund, [G20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G20 "G20"), [G8+5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G8%2B5 "G8+5"), and the [Port Management Association of Eastern and Southern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Management_Association_of_Eastern_and_Southern_Africa "Port Management Association of Eastern and Southern Africa").

South Africa has played a key role as a mediator in African conflicts over the last decade, such as in Burundi, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Comoros, Mozambique and Zimbabwe.

President [Jacob Zuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Zuma "Jacob Zuma") and Chinese President [Hu Jintao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hu_Jintao "Hu Jintao") upgraded bilateral ties between the two countries in 2010 when they signed the Beijing Agreement which elevated South Africa's earlier "strategic partnership" with China to the higher level of "comprehensive strategic partnership" in both economic and political affairs, including the strengthening of exchanges between their respective ruling parties and legislatures. In 2011, South Africa joined the Brazil-Russia-India-China ([BRICS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRICS "BRICS")) grouping of countries, identified by Zuma as the country's largest trading partners and also the largest trading partners with Africa as a whole. Zuma asserted that BRICS member countries would also work with each other through the UN, G20, and the India, Brazil South Africa ([IBSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBSA_Dialogue_Forum "IBSA Dialogue Forum")) forum.

### Military

The [South African National Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_National_Defence_Force "South African National Defence Force") (SANDF) serves as the unified [armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military "Military") of South Africa. Established in 1994, it was formed as a [voluntary military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volunteer_military "Volunteer military") through the integration of the former [South African Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Defence_Force "South African Defence Force") (SADF) and several [liberation movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_movement "Liberation movement") forces. The SANDF is organised into four branches: the [South African Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Army "South African Army"), the [South African Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Air_Force "South African Air Force"), the [South African Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Navy "South African Navy"), and the [South African Military Health Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Military_Health_Service "South African Military Health Service"). As of 2025, the force comprises nearly 100,000 personnel, of whom around 75,000 are active [professional soldiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_army "Standing army"), and operates under the authority of the president of South Africa, who serves as [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief "Commander-in-chief"). The SANDF emerged as a prominent African [peacekeeping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacekeeping "Peacekeeping") force in the early 2000s, deploying as a leading contributor to United Nations and African Union missions in [Burundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burundian_Civil_War "Burundian Civil War") (2001), [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Darfur "War in Darfur") (2004) and the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MONUSCO "MONUSCO") (2003). It has since participated in regional security operations in countries including [Lesotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_intervention_in_Lesotho "South African intervention in Lesotho") and [Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurgency_in_Cabo_Delgado "Insurgency in Cabo Delgado"), while continuing to contribute to multinational [United Nations peacekeeping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_peacekeeping "United Nations peacekeeping") missions, including the [Force Intervention Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Force_Intervention_Brigade "United Nations Force Intervention Brigade").

Supporting these armed forces is a [domestic defence industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_industry_of_South_Africa "Defence industry of South Africa") that is the most advanced in Africa and one of the world's leading [defence industrial bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_industrial_base "Defense industrial base") outside the major military powers. Coordinated by the state-owned [Armaments Corporation of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armscor_(South_Africa) "Armscor (South Africa)") (Armscor), it includes major companies such as [Denel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denel "Denel"), [Paramount Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Group "Paramount Group"), and [Milkor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milkor "Milkor"). The industry produces a wide range of advanced systems, including armoured vehicles, military aircraft, naval vessels, and missiles, with about 80% of SANDF equipment made in South Africa. The country also remains a significant arms exporter and maintains self-sufficiency in most conventional military technologies.

South Africa is the only African nation to have successfully developed [nuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon "Nuclear weapon"). Between 1980 and 1990, six operational nuclear devices were covertly assembled before the arsenal was voluntarily dismantled in 1991, making South Africa the first country to relinquish its nuclear capability. The country is also alleged to have conducted a [nuclear test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapons_testing "Nuclear weapons testing") over the Atlantic in 1979, known as the "[Vela incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vela_incident "Vela incident")", although this is officially denied; then-President F.W. de Klerk later asserted that South Africa had "never conducted a clandestine nuclear test". Despite dismantling its arsenal, South Africa still has the technical capability to restart its [weapons of mass destruction programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_and_weapons_of_mass_destruction "South Africa and weapons of mass destruction") if it ever chose to do so, as its [Pelindaba Nuclear Research Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelindaba "Pelindaba") still stores [enriched uranium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enriched_uranium "Enriched uranium") from the former programme. However, this material is now used primarily for civilian nuclear research and [medical isotope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_isotope "Medical isotope") production, reflecting South Africa's commitment to the peaceful use of nuclear energy. South Africa remains a strong advocate of [nuclear disarmament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_disarmament "Nuclear disarmament"), having ratified the UN [Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Prohibition_of_Nuclear_Weapons "Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons") in 2019.

### Law enforcement and crime

Law enforcement in South Africa is primarily the responsibility of the [South African Police Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Police_Service "South African Police Service") (SAPS), the national police force operating more than 1,150 police stations and employing around 150,950 officers. The SAPS is tasked with crime prevention, investigation, and national security. It also maintains an elite tactical unit, the [Special Task Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Task_Force_(SAPS) "Special Task Force (SAPS)") (STF), which specialises in [counter-terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterterrorism "Counterterrorism"), [counter-insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterinsurgency "Counterinsurgency"), and [hostage rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hostage_rescue "Hostage rescue") operations. In the 2023 International SWAT Competition, the STF ranked 9th out of 55 international law enforcement teams, making it the highest-ranked African police unit and one of the world's best. Alongside formal policing, [South Africa has the world's largest private security industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_security_industry_in_South_Africa "Private security industry in South Africa"), comprising over 10,000 companies and more than 2.5 million registered personnel, exceeding the combined size of both the police and the military.

South Africa continues to experience high levels of [violent crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violent_crime_in_South_Africa "Violent crime in South Africa"), among the highest in the world and it leads Africa in this regard. From April 2017 to March 2018, an average of 57 murders per day were recorded, with a [homicide rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_intentional_homicide_rate "List of countries by intentional homicide rate") more than five times the global average. Serious crimes such as armed robbery, hijackings, [cash-in-transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash-in-transit "Cash-in-transit") heists, gang-related killings, and [sexual violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_violence_in_South_Africa "Sexual violence in South Africa") are common. South Africa also records one of the world's highest [reported rates of rape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rape_statistics "Rape statistics"), with tens of thousands of cases each year, though many go unreported. Between 1994 and 2019, more than 526,000 murders were reported nationwide. [Gang violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangs_in_South_Africa "Gangs in South Africa") remains a major driver of the homicide rate, particularly in the [Cape Flats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Flats "Cape Flats") region of Cape Town, where rival groups compete over territory, [drug trafficking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_trafficking "Drug trafficking") routes, and extortion. These conflicts often result in civilian casualties, with bystanders and children frequently caught in the crossfire.

Despite ongoing reforms, South Africa's criminal justice system faces persistent challenges, including corruption, inefficiency, incompetency, and underreporting, which have undermined public confidence in law enforcement. The [Department of Justice and Constitutional Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Justice_and_Constitutional_Development "Department of Justice and Constitutional Development") oversees the court system, while the [Department of Correctional Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Correctional_Services_(South_Africa) "Department of Correctional Services (South Africa)") manages the country's prisons. South Africa has the highest prison population in Africa, with chronic overcrowding and human rights concerns frequently reported. Critics argue that systemic weaknesses and uneven access to justice have fostered a culture of impunity, sustaining high crime rates and public mistrust. These issues continue to shape national debates on security, governance, and human rights.

## Economy

South Africa has a [mixed economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_economy "Mixed economy") characterised by a large [services sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_sector "Service sector"), diversified industrial base and abundant [natural resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_resource "Natural resource"). It is the [largest economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_African_countries_by_GDP_(nominal) "List of African countries by GDP (nominal)") in Africa and is widely recognised as the continent's most industrialised, technologically advanced and diversified nation. As of 2026, South Africa has a nominal [gross domestic product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_domestic_product "Gross domestic product") (GDP) exceeding US$450 billion, making it the world's [39th-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(nominal) "List of countries by GDP (nominal)") economy and one of the highest [GDPs per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(nominal)_per_capita "List of countries by GDP (nominal) per capita") in sub-Saharan Africa. The [South African rand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_rand "South African rand") (ZAR) is Africa's most traded currency, while the [Johannesburg Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JSE_Limited "JSE Limited") (JSE) is the continent's largest [stock exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_exchange "Stock exchange") and ranks among the world's 20 largest by [market capitalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_capitalization "Market capitalization"). South Africa is the only African member of the [G20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G20 "G20") and is also a member of [BRICS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRICS "BRICS").

In 2023, South Africa ranked 106th on the [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index "Human Development Index") (HDI), placing it in the high human development category. Its [labour force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_force "Labour force") numbered approximately 27.8 million in 2024, the [26th-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_labour_force "List of countries by labour force") in the world. Despite its economic development, South Africa continues to face high [unemployment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment "Unemployment"), widespread [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty "Poverty") and severe [income inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inequality_in_post-apartheid_South_Africa "Inequality in post-apartheid South Africa"), with an official unemployment rate of 33.6% as of the second quarter of 2026. Approximately 23 million people (37.9% of the population) live below the [lower-bound poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_threshold "Poverty threshold"), while 10.8 million live below the [food poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_South_Africa "Poverty in South Africa"), and the country consistently records one of the world's highest [Gini coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gini_coefficient "Gini coefficient").

South Africa is a major [trading nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trading_nation "Trading nation"), ranking among the world's 50 largest exporters and importers. Its principal exports include minerals, precious metals, vehicles, machinery, agricultural products and manufactured goods. As of 2026, its largest export markets are [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") (12.5%), the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") (7.7%), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany") (7.4%), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan") (5.6%) and the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom") (4.0%), while its principal import sources are China (22.7%), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") (12.2%), [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria "Nigeria") (6.8%), the United States (6.3%) and Germany (5.7%). In 2025, exports accounted for approximately 31.4% of GDP.

### Industries and services

South Africa is a highly industrialised country with Africa's most diversified industrial sector. Industry accounted for approximately 24% of GDP in 2024, with [manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing_in_South_Africa "Manufacturing in South Africa") contributing around 13% and ranking [40th globally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing#List_of_countries_by_manufacturing_output "Manufacturing") by output. Major industries include [construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction "Construction"), motor vehicles, [chemicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substance_(chemistry) "Substance (chemistry)"), [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel "Steel"), machinery, [processed foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_processing "Food processing"), textiles and military hardware. The country also has [shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipbuilding "Shipbuilding") and [aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace "Aerospace") industries, with a significant focus on defence-related products. [State-owned enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State-owned_enterprise "State-owned enterprise"), including [Eskom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eskom "Eskom"), [Transnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transnet "Transnet") and [Denel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denel "Denel"), also play a role in the industrial economy, particularly in the energy, transport and defence sectors.

Within manufacturing, the [automotive industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_industry_in_South_Africa "Automotive industry in South Africa") is a major component. [Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Motor_Company "Ford Motor Company") opened the country's first assembly plant in 1924, followed by [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors "General Motors") in 1926. Other international manufacturers subsequently established assembly operations in the country, including [Toyota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota "Toyota"), [Volkswagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen "Volkswagen"), [BMW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW "BMW"), [Mercedes-Benz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz "Mercedes-Benz") and [Nissan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan "Nissan"). South Africa is Africa's largest [motor vehicle producer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_motor_vehicle_production "List of countries by motor vehicle production"), producing around 600,000 vehicles a year, with exports accounting for approximately two-thirds of local production.

[Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector "Tertiary sector") accounted for 63.1% of South Africa's GDP in 2025 and include a well-developed [financial services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_services_in_South_Africa "Financial services in South Africa") industry. Johannesburg is Africa's leading financial centre and home to the JSE, while South Africa's [stock market capitalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_stock_market_capitalization "List of countries by stock market capitalization") reached approximately US$1.53 trillion in 2026, ranking 16th globally. Retail is also significant, with the country having the world's sixth-largest number of [shopping centres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shopping_center "Shopping center"). Other major services include [real estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate "Real estate"), [telecommunications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications "Telecommunications") and [business services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_services "Corporate services"), with Cape Town serving as a leading [technology hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tech_hub "Tech hub") on the African continent.

South African companies feature prominently among Africa's most valuable businesses, including [AngloGold Ashanti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AngloGold_Ashanti "AngloGold Ashanti"), [Gold Fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Fields "Gold Fields"), [Impala Platinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impala_Platinum "Impala Platinum"), [Naspers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naspers "Naspers"), [MTN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTN_Group "MTN Group"), [Vodacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vodacom "Vodacom"), [Sanlam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanlam "Sanlam"), [Sasol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasol "Sasol"), [FirstRand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FirstRand "FirstRand") and [Standard Bank Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Bank_Group "Standard Bank Group"). Their activities span mining, finance, telecommunications, insurance, chemicals, media and technology, with many operating across Africa and internationally. In a 2026 *[African Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Business "African Business")* ranking, all 20 South African companies listed were among the 30 leading African companies by market value.

### Mining

The [mining industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining_industry_of_South_Africa "Mining industry of South Africa") remains an important part of South Africa's economy, contributing around 6.0% of GDP in 2024, making South Africa the world's fifth-largest mining economy by contribution to GDP. Mineral products accounted for around 52% of the value of the country's total exports in 2025. The industry has historically played a major role in the country's industrial development, following the discovery of [diamonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond "Diamond") near the [Orange River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_River "Orange River") in 1867 and [gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold "Gold") on the [Witwatersrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witwatersrand "Witwatersrand") in 1886. The sector is regulated by the [Department of Mineral and Petroleum Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Mineral_and_Petroleum_Resources "Department of Mineral and Petroleum Resources"), which is responsible for the mining industry, the exploitation of mineral resources and the petroleum industry.

South Africa is one of the world's leading producers of mineral resources. In 2024, South Africa accounted for substantial shares of global mined mineral production, including [platinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum "Platinum") (70%), [chromium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromium "Chromium") (46%), [manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese "Manganese") (40%), [palladium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palladium "Palladium") (38%), [vermiculite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vermiculite "Vermiculite") (35%), [rutile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutile "Rutile") and [zircon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zircon "Zircon") (22% each), [ilmenite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilmenite "Ilmenite") (14%), industrial diamond (8%), [vanadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanadium "Vanadium") (7%), industrial garnet and [talc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talc "Talc") (about 5% each), [fluorspar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorite "Fluorite") (4%), and gemstone diamond, gold and [iron ore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_ore "Iron ore") (3% each). South Africa also possesses mineral reserves valued at more than US$2.5 trillion, including substantial shares of global reserves of [platinum-group metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum_group_metals "Platinum group metals") (88%), manganese (80%), [chromite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromite "Chromite") (72%), vanadium (32%), [zirconium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zirconium "Zirconium") (25%), fluorspar (17%), gold (13%), [titanium minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanium "Titanium") (10%), [uranium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium "Uranium") (5%), and [antimony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony "Antimony") (2%).

### Agriculture

The [agricultural sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_South_Africa "Agriculture in South Africa") contributed approximately 2.7% to South Africa's GDP in 2025 while supporting [food security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_security "Food security"), rural livelihoods and exports. It is considered the most modern, productive and diverse agricultural sector in Africa. According to the 2017 Census of Commercial Agriculture, South Africa had 40,122 commercial farming units, of which 33.9% were engaged in [livestock farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livestock "Livestock"), 31.1% in [mixed farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_farming "Mixed farming") and 21.3% in [field crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crop "Crop"). In September 2018, commercial agriculture covered 46.4 million hectares (114.7 million acres), or 37.9% of the country's land area. Of this land, 36.5 million hectares (78.7%) were used for [grazing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grazing "Grazing") and 7.6 million hectares (16.4%) for [arable farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_land "Arable land"), with the remainder devoted to other uses.

South Africa is a leading producer and exporter of [citrus fruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrus "Citrus"), [grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grape "Grape"), [wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine "Wine"), [apples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple "Apple"), [pears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pear "Pear"), [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize "Maize"), [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane "Sugarcane"), [nuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nut_(fruit) "Nut (fruit)") and [wool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wool "Wool"). South Africa became the world's largest citrus exporter by volume in 2025, overtaking Spain after exporting approximately 2.9 million tonnes. Its [wine industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_wine "South African wine") is the seventh-largest in the world. South Africa is a net exporter of agricultural products and exports approximately half of its agricultural production. It is also the world's largest producer of [mohair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohair "Mohair") and [ostrich meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrich_meat "Ostrich meat").

### Science and technology

Several important scientific and technological developments have originated in South Africa. The first human-to-human [heart transplant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_transplantation "Heart transplantation") was performed by cardiac surgeon [Christiaan Barnard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiaan_Barnard "Christiaan Barnard") at [Groote Schuur Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groote_Schuur_Hospital "Groote Schuur Hospital") in December 1967; [Max Theiler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Theiler "Max Theiler") developed a [vaccine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_fever_vaccine "Yellow fever vaccine") against [yellow fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_fever "Yellow fever"), [Allan MacLeod Cormack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allan_MacLeod_Cormack "Allan MacLeod Cormack") pioneered X-ray computed tomography ([CT scan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CT_scan "CT scan")); and [Aaron Klug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Klug "Aaron Klug") developed [crystallographic electron microscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_crystallography "Electron crystallography") techniques. Cormack and Klug received [Nobel Prizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") for their work. [Sydney Brenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Brenner "Sydney Brenner") won in 2002, for his pioneering work in [molecular biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_biology "Molecular biology"). [Mark Shuttleworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Shuttleworth "Mark Shuttleworth") founded [Thawte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thawte "Thawte"), an early Internet security company pioneering digital certificates and online security. South Africa was ranked 61st in the [Global Innovation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Innovation_Index "Global Innovation Index") in 2025, reflecting its continued scientific and technological capabilities.

South Africa has a burgeoning [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy "Astronomy") community and hosts the [Southern African Large Telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_African_Large_Telescope "Southern African Large Telescope"), the largest [optical telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_telescope "Optical telescope") in the [Southern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Hemisphere "Southern Hemisphere"), and the [MeerKAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MeerKAT "MeerKAT") radio telescope, a precursor to the €1.5 billion [Square Kilometre Array](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_Kilometre_Array "Square Kilometre Array") project. The [South African National Space Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_National_Space_Agency "South African National Space Agency") (SANSA), established in 2010, oversees the country's space activities. South Africa launched its first locally developed satellite, [SUNSAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SUNSAT "SUNSAT"), in 1999 and had Africa's second-highest number of satellites in space, with 13 as of 2025, behind [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt") with 14. South Africa is also the continent's leading satellite developer, with a high proportion of satellite components and systems developed locally.

South Africa has also made significant contributions to [military technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_technology "Military technology"). The country pioneered [mine-resistant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mine-resistant_ambush_protected_vehicle "Mine-resistant ambush protected vehicle") vehicle designs, including the [Buffel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffel "Buffel") and [Casspir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casspir "Casspir"), which established the use of V-shaped hulls to protect occupants from [landmines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_mine "Land mine") and later influenced modern MRAP vehicles. South Africa also pioneered the operational use of [helmet-mounted sight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmet-mounted_display "Helmet-mounted display") technology on combat aircraft, successfully integrating a locally developed system with [air-to-air missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-to-air_missile "Air-to-air missile") on [Mirage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_III "Dassault Mirage III") fighters in 1975.

### Tourism

South Africa is a major international [tourist destination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_South_Africa "Tourism in South Africa"), known for its diverse landscapes, wildlife and cultural heritage. In 2024, it welcomed approximately 8.9 million international visitors through its various ports of entry. In 2025, *[The Daily Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph "The Daily Telegraph")* ranked it the fourth-best country to visit worldwide and the highest-rated destination in Africa and the Indian Ocean region. Tourism accounted for around 5.8% of South Africa's GDP in 2024.

Tourist attractions include [Kruger National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kruger_National_Park "Kruger National Park"), [Table Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_Mountain "Table Mountain"), the [Garden Route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_Route_National_Park "Garden Route National Park"), the [Drakensberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drakensberg "Drakensberg") and the beaches of the [Western Cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Cape "Western Cape") and [KwaZulu-Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KwaZulu-Natal "KwaZulu-Natal"). [Safari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safari "Safari") tourism is a major attraction, supported by national parks and private game reserves.

Major cities also attract tourists. [Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town "Cape Town") is known for Table Mountain, the [V&A Waterfront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%26A_Waterfront "V&A Waterfront") and the nearby [Cape Winelands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boland,_Western_Cape "Boland, Western Cape"); [Johannesburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannesburg "Johannesburg") for attractions such as the [Apartheid Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid_Museum "Apartheid Museum"), [Constitution Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_Hill,_Johannesburg "Constitution Hill, Johannesburg") and [Gold Reef City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Reef_City "Gold Reef City"); and [Durban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durban "Durban") for its beaches and [Golden Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Mile,_Durban "Golden Mile, Durban"). Wine tourism is also popular in the vineyards of [Stellenbosch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellenbosch "Stellenbosch"), [Franschhoek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franschhoek "Franschhoek") and [Paarl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paarl "Paarl").

South Africa also has twelve [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO") [World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Site "World Heritage Site"), including [Robben Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robben_Island "Robben Island"), the [Cradle of Humankind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cradle_of_Humankind "Cradle of Humankind"), [iSimangaliso Wetland Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISimangaliso_Wetland_Park "ISimangaliso Wetland Park") and the [Cape Floral Region Protected Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Floristic_Region "Cape Floristic Region").

## Infrastructure

### Transport

South Africa has the largest and most extensive [transport infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport "Transport") in Africa, comprising an integrated network of [roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road "Road"), [railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport "Rail transport"), [airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport "Airport"), [seaports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port "Port") and [pipelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipeline "Pipeline"). The country has approximately 750,000 kilometres (470,000 mi) of roads, making it the largest [road network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roads_in_South_Africa "Roads in South Africa") in Africa and the [12th-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_road_network_size "List of countries by road network size") in the world. The [South African National Roads Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SANRAL "SANRAL") (SANRAL) manages the national road system, including major highways such as the [N1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N1_(South_Africa) "N1 (South Africa)"), [N2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N2_(South_Africa) "N2 (South Africa)"), [N3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N3_(South_Africa) "N3 (South Africa)") and [N4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N4_(South_Africa) "N4 (South Africa)"), which connect the country's principal cities and form part of transcontinental routes such as the [Cape to Cairo Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo%E2%80%93Cape_Town_Highway "Cairo–Cape Town Highway").

The [railway network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_South_Africa "Rail transport in South Africa") extends approximately 36,000 kilometres (23,700 mi), making it the largest in Africa and the [seventh-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_rail_transport_network_size "List of countries by rail transport network size") in the world. Freight services are operated primarily by [Transnet Freight Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transnet_Freight_Rail "Transnet Freight Rail"), while commuter rail services are provided by the [Passenger Rail Agency of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passenger_Rail_Agency_of_South_Africa "Passenger Rail Agency of South Africa") (PRASA). The [Gautrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautrain "Gautrain") rapid rail system links Johannesburg and Pretoria, providing the country's only [higher-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher-speed_rail "Higher-speed rail") service.

South Africa has more than 1,500 [airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_South_Africa "List of airports in South Africa"), among the highest numbers in the world and the greatest of any African country. [O.R. Tambo International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O._R._Tambo_International_Airport "O. R. Tambo International Airport") in Johannesburg is the largest and busiest airport in Africa, serving as the country's principal international gateway, while [Cape Town International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town_International_Airport "Cape Town International Airport"), [King Shaka International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Shaka_International_Airport "King Shaka International Airport") and [Chief Dawid Stuurman International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Dawid_Stuurman_International_Airport "Chief Dawid Stuurman International Airport") airports are other major hubs. South Africa also has the continent's largest and most developed aviation market, served by airlines including [South African Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Airways "South African Airways"), [Airlink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airlink "Airlink"), [FlySafair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FlySafair "FlySafair"), [CemAir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CemAir "CemAir") and [LIFT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LIFT_(airline) "LIFT (airline)").

South Africa's maritime sector is centred on the [commercial ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ports_and_harbours_in_South_Africa#List_of_ports_and_harbours_in_South_Africa "Ports and harbours in South Africa") of Durban, Cape Town, Gqeberha, Richards Bay, Saldanha Bay and East London. The [Port of Durban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Durban "Port of Durban") is the busiest container port in sub-Saharan Africa and the fourth-largest in the Southern Hemisphere, while the [Port of Richards Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Richards_Bay "Port of Richards Bay") is one of the world's largest bulk export facilities. Port infrastructure is managed by the [Transnet National Ports Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transnet_National_Ports_Authority "Transnet National Ports Authority"), which plays a central role in supporting domestic logistics and international trade.

### Energy

South Africa has the largest and most advanced [energy sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_South_Africa "Energy in South Africa") in Africa and is the world's [22nd-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_electricity_production "List of countries by electricity production") producer of [electricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity "Electricity"). The sector is dominated by the state-owned utility [Eskom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eskom "Eskom"), Africa's largest [power company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_utility "Electric utility"), which generates approximately 90% of the country's electricity and is a major participant in the [Southern African Power Pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_African_Power_Pool "Southern African Power Pool"), the regional electricity market. South Africa remains heavily reliant on [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_in_South_Africa "Coal in South Africa"), producing more than 200 million tonnes annually, making it the world's [seventh-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_coal_production "List of countries by coal production") coal producer. Around 90% of the coal consumed on the African continent is mined in South Africa, which is also one of the world's 15 largest [greenhouse gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions "Greenhouse gas emissions") emitters.

The South African electricity grid has an installed capacity of around 60 GW. In 2024, electricity generation was sourced from [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal "Coal") (approximately 80–83%), [renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_in_South_Africa "Renewable energy in South Africa") from [wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power "Wind power"), [solar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_power "Solar power") and [hydroelectricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectricity "Hydroelectricity") (9–13%), [nuclear power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power "Nuclear power") (around 4%) and other sources, including gas, diesel and pumped storage (4–6%). Major generating facilities include the [Kusile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kusile_Power_Station "Kusile Power Station"), [Medupi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medupi_Power_Station "Medupi Power Station"), [Majuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majuba_Power_Station "Majuba Power Station") and [Kendal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendal_Power_Station "Kendal Power Station") coal-fired power stations, while the [Koeberg Nuclear Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koeberg_Nuclear_Power_Station "Koeberg Nuclear Power Station") is Africa's first and only commercial nuclear power plant. Despite its substantial installed capacity, South Africa was embroiled in a prolonged [energy crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_energy_crisis "South African energy crisis") during the 2010s and early 2020s, resulting in recurring [load-shedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_blackout "Rolling blackout").

Following the stabilisation of the electricity system in 2024, government policy now focuses on diversifying the electricity mix through the [Integrated Resource Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_Resource_Plan "Integrated Resource Plan") 2025, which targets 105 GW of new generation capacity by 2039 and represents an investment of approximately R2.2 trillion (US$120 billion), making it the single largest investment programme in South Africa's post-apartheid history. The plan invites [independent power producers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_power_producer "Independent power producer") to contribute to the expansion, with the projected 2039 generation mix consisting of coal (27%), wind power (24%), grid-connected solar power (18%), rooftop solar (10%), nuclear power (5%), hydroelectricity (4%), diesel (4.5%) and other sources (7.5%), representing a substantial shift away from coal.

### Water supply and sanitation

Two distinctive features of the South African water sector are the policy of free basic water and the existence of [water boards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_Board_(South_Africa) "Water Board (South Africa)"), which are bulk water supply agencies that operate pipelines and sell water from reservoirs to municipalities. These features have led to significant problems concerning the financial sustainability of service providers, leading to a lack of attention to maintenance. Following the end of apartheid, the country had made improvements in the levels of access to water as those with access increased from 66% to 79% from 1990 to 2010. Sanitation access increased from 71% to 79% during the same period. However, water supply and sanitation has come under increasing pressure in recent years despite a commitment made by the government to improve service standards and provide investment subsidies to the water industry.

The eastern parts of South Africa suffer from periodic droughts linked to the [El Niño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Ni%C3%B1o "El Niño") weather phenomenon. In early 2018, Cape Town, which has different weather patterns to the rest of the country, faced a water crisis as the city's water supply was predicted to run dry before the end of June. Water-saving measures were in effect that required each citizen to use less than 50 litres (13 US gal) per day. Cape Town rejected an offer from Israel to help it build [desalination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desalination "Desalination") plants.

### Telecommunications

South Africa has the most advanced [telecommunications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications "Telecommunications") sector in Africa, regulated by the [Independent Communications Authority of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Communications_Authority_of_South_Africa "Independent Communications Authority of South Africa") (ICASA). The country serves as a key regional hub for digital connectivity, supported by several major [undersea cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_cable "Submarine cable") systems such as [WACS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WACS_(cable_system) "WACS (cable system)"), [SAT-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAT-3/WASC "SAT-3/WASC"), [Seacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEACOM_(African_cable_system) "SEACOM (African cable system)"), and [2Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2Africa "2Africa"), which link South Africa to global networks. Mobile penetration is among the highest on the continent, and [internet access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_access "Internet access") continues to expand rapidly across urban and rural areas.

The [mobile communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_network "Cellular network") market is led by major South African operators including [Vodacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vodacom "Vodacom"), [MTN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTN_Group "MTN Group"), [Telkom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telkom_(South_Africa) "Telkom (South Africa)"), [Cell C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_C "Cell C"), and [Rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_(telecommunications) "Rain (telecommunications)"). MTN, in particular, stands out as Africa's largest mobile network operator and one of the top ten globally, with nearly 290 million users as of 2022. South Africa offers a full range of network technologies from [2G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2G "2G") to [5G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5G "5G"), with MTN, Vodacom, and Rain being the first to launch 5G services on the entire African continent. While [fixed-line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed-line "Fixed-line") connections have declined, [fibre-optic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber-optic_communication "Fiber-optic communication") [broadband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadband "Broadband") is growing quickly through providers such as [Openserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Openserve "Openserve"), [Vumatel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vumatel "Vumatel"), [Frogfoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frogfoot_(South_Africa) "Frogfoot (South Africa)"), [Octotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octotel "Octotel"), and [MetroFibre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_broadband_providers_in_South_Africa "List of broadband providers in South Africa"). [Satellite internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_Internet_access "Satellite Internet access") remains available for remote regions, though [Starlink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starlink "Starlink") has yet to launch locally due to regulatory restrictions.

## Demographics

South Africa is a nation of about 62 million (as of 2022) people of diverse origins, cultures, languages, and religions. The last [census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_National_Census_of_2022 "South African National Census of 2022") was held in 2022, with estimates produced on an annual basis. According to the *[United Nations World Population Prospects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_World_Population_Prospects "United Nations World Population Prospects")*, South Africa's total population was 55.3 million in 2015, compared to only 13.6 million in 1950. South Africa is home to an estimated five million [illegal immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_immigration "Illegal immigration"), including some three million Zimbabweans. A series of [anti-immigrant riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenophobia_in_South_Africa "Xenophobia in South Africa") occurred beginning in May 2008.

[Statistics South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_South_Africa "Statistics South Africa") asks people to describe themselves in the census in terms of five racial population groups. The 2022 census figures for these groups were: [Black African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_peoples_of_South_Africa "Bantu peoples of South Africa") at 81%, [Coloured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Coloureds "Cape Coloureds") at 8.2%, [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_South_Africans "White South Africans") at 7.3%, [Indian or Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_South_Africans "Indian South Africans") at 2.7%, and Other/Unspecified at 0.5%. The first census in 1911 showed that whites made up 22% of the population; this had declined to 16% by 1980.

South Africa hosts a sizeable refugee and asylum seeker population. According to the *World Refugee Survey 2008*, published by the [U.S. Committee for Refugees and Immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Committee_for_Refugees_and_Immigrants "U.S. Committee for Refugees and Immigrants"), this population numbered approximately 144,700 in 2007. Groups of refugees and asylum seekers numbering over 10,000 included people from Zimbabwe (48,400), the DRC (24,800), and Somalia (12,900). These populations mainly lived in Johannesburg, Pretoria, [Durban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durban "Durban"), Cape Town, and [Port Elizabeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Elizabeth "Port Elizabeth").

One online database lists South Africa having more than 12,600 cities and towns.

### Languages

South Africa has 12 official languages: [Afrikaans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrikaans "Afrikaans"), [Zulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulu_language "Zulu language"), [Xhosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xhosa_language "Xhosa language"), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_English "South African English"), [Pedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Sotho "Northern Sotho"), [Tswana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tswana_language "Tswana language"), [Southern Sotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sotho_language "Sotho language"), [Tsonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsonga_language "Tsonga language"), [Swazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swazi_language "Swazi language"), [Venda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venda_language "Venda language"), and [Southern Ndebele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Ndebele_language "Southern Ndebele language") (in order of first language speakers), as well as South African Sign Language, which was recognised as an official language in 2023. In this regard, it is fourth only to [Bolivia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivia "Bolivia"), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_languages_of_India "Official languages of India"), and [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe "Zimbabwe") in number. While all the languages are formally equal, some languages are spoken more than others. According to the 2022 census, the three most spoken first languages are Zulu (24.4%), Xhosa (16.6%), and Afrikaans (10.6%). Although English is recognised as the language of commerce and science, it is only the fifth most common home language, that of only 8.7% of South Africans in 2022; nevertheless, it has become the de facto [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca "Lingua franca") of the nation. Estimates based on the 1991 census suggest just under half of South Africans could speak English. It is the second most commonly spoken language outside of the household, after Zulu.

Other languages are spoken, or were widely used previously, including [Fanagalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanagalo_language "Fanagalo language"), [Khoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoe_language "Khoe language"), [Lobedu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobedu_language "Lobedu language"), [Nama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nama_language "Nama language"), [Northern Ndebele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ndebele_language "Northern Ndebele language"), and [Phuthi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phuthi_language "Phuthi language"). Many of the unofficial [languages of the San and Khoekhoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoisan_languages "Khoisan languages") peoples contain regional dialects stretching northwards into Namibia and Botswana, and elsewhere. These people, who are a physically distinct population from the Bantu people who make up most of the black Africans in South Africa, have their own cultural identity based on their [hunter-gatherer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer "Hunter-gatherer") societies. They have been marginalised, and the remainder of their languages are in danger of becoming [extinct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinct_language "Extinct language").

White South Africans may also speak European languages, including Italian, Portuguese (also spoken by black Angolans and Mozambicans), Dutch, German, and Greek, while some Indian South Africans and more recent migrants from South Asia speak [Indian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_India "Languages of India"), such as Gujarati, Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, and Urdu. French is spoken by migrants from [Francophone Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_French "African French").

### Religion

According to the 2022 census, Christians accounted for 85.3% of the population, with a majority of them being members of various [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism "Protestantism") [denominations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_denomination "Christian denomination") (broadly defined to include [syncretic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncretism "Syncretism") [African-initiated churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African-initiated_church "African-initiated church")) and a minority of [Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church "Catholic Church") and other Christians. Per the 2001 census, the Christian category included [Zion Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zion_Christian_Church "Zion Christian Church") (11.1%), [Pentecostal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostalism "Pentecostalism") ([Charismatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charismatic_movement "Charismatic movement")) (8.2%), [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_South_Africa "Catholic Church in South Africa") (7.1%), [Methodist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist_Church_of_Southern_Africa "Methodist Church of Southern Africa") (6.8%), [Dutch Reformed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Reformed_Church_in_South_Africa_(NGK) "Dutch Reformed Church in South Africa (NGK)") (6.7%), and [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Church_of_Southern_Africa "Anglican Church of Southern Africa") (3.8%). Members of the remaining Christian churches accounted for the rest of the Christian population. Per the 2022 census, [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_South_Africa "Islam in South Africa") accounted for 1.6% of the population, [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_South_Africa "Hinduism in South Africa") 1.1%, [traditional African religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_African_religions "Traditional African religions") 7.8%, 3.1% had no religious affiliation, and 1.1% were "other".

African-initiated churches formed the largest of the Christian groups. It was believed that many of the persons who claimed no affiliation with any organised religion adhered to a traditional African religion. There are an estimated 200,000 [traditional healers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_healers_of_Southern_Africa "Traditional healers of Southern Africa"), and up to 60% of South Africans consult these healers, generally called *sangoma* ('diviner') or *inyanga* ('herbalist'). These healers use a combination of [ancestral spiritual beliefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veneration_of_the_dead "Veneration of the dead") and a belief in the spiritual and medicinal properties of local fauna, flora, and funga commonly known as [*muti*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_African_medicine "Traditional African medicine") ('medicine'), to facilitate healing in clients. Many peoples have syncretic religious practices combining Christian and indigenous influences.

South African Muslims comprise mainly Coloureds and Indians. They have been joined by black or white South African converts as well as those from other parts of Africa. South African Muslims describe their faith as the fastest-growing religion of conversion in the country, with the number of black Muslims growing sixfold, from 12,000 in 1991 to 74,700 in 2004.

There is a substantial [Jewish population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_South_Africa "History of the Jews in South Africa"), descended from [European Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Europe "History of the Jews in Europe") who arrived as a minority amongst other European settlers. This population peaked in the 1970s at 118,000, though only around 75,000 remain today, the rest having emigrated, mostly to Israel. The present-day Jewish community in South Africa is the largest on the African continent, and the twelfth-largest in the world.

### Education

The adult [literacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy "Literacy") rate in 2025 was 95%. This was the second-highest in [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa "Africa"), behind only Seychelles. South Africa has a [three-tier system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-tier_education "Three-tier education") of education starting with primary school, followed by high school, and tertiary education in the form of (academic) universities and universities of technology. Learners have twelve years of formal schooling, from grade 1 to 12. Grade R, or grade 0, is a pre-primary foundation year. Primary schools span the first seven years of schooling. High school education spans a further five years. The [National Senior Certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matriculation_in_South_Africa "Matriculation in South Africa") examination takes place at the end of grade 12, and is necessary for tertiary studies at a [South African university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_South_Africa "List of universities in South Africa"). Public universities are divided into three types: traditional universities, which offer theoretically oriented university degrees; [universities of technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_technology "Institute of technology") (formerly called *[technikons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technikon "Technikon")*), which offer vocationally-oriented diplomas and degrees; and comprehensive universities, which offer both types of qualification. There are 23 public universities in South Africa: 11 traditional universities, 6 universities of technology, and 6 comprehensive universities. There are also a large amount of FET (Further Education and Training) and [TVET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocational_education "Vocational education") (Technical and Vocational Education and Training) [colleges in South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_public_colleges_in_South_Africa "List of public colleges in South Africa").

Under apartheid, schools for black people were subject to discrimination through inadequate funding and a separate syllabus called *[Bantu Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_Education_Act,_1953 "Bantu Education Act, 1953")* which only taught skills sufficient to work as labourers.

In 2004, South Africa started reforming its tertiary education system, merging and incorporating small universities into larger institutions, and renaming all tertiary education institutions "university". By 2015, 1.4 million students in higher education have been aided by a financial aid scheme which was promulgated in 1999.

### Health

According to the [South African Institute of Race Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Institute_of_Race_Relations "South African Institute of Race Relations"), the life expectancy in 2009 was 71 years for a white South African and 48 years for a black South African. The healthcare spending in the country is about 9% of GDP. About 84% of the population depends on the public healthcare system, which is beset with chronic human resource shortages and limited resources. About 20% of the population use private healthcare. Only 16% of the population are covered by [medical aid schemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_insurance "Health insurance"); the rest pay for private care [out-of-pocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out-of-pocket_expense "Out-of-pocket expense") or through in-hospital-only plans. The three dominant hospital groups, [Mediclinic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediclinic_International "Mediclinic International"), [Life Healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LIFE_Healthcare_Group "LIFE Healthcare Group") and [Netcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netcare "Netcare"), together control 75% of the private hospital market. These disparities have driven ongoing policy efforts to reform the healthcare system, including the introduction of [National Health Insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Health_Insurance_Act,_2023 "National Health Insurance Act, 2023") (NHI) aimed at expanding equitable access to healthcare.

#### HIV/AIDS

According to the 2015 [UNAIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_United_Nations_Programme_on_HIV/AIDS "Joint United Nations Programme on HIV/AIDS") medical report, South Africa has an estimated seven million people who are living with HIV – more than any other country in the world. In 2018, HIV prevalence—the percentage of people living with HIV—among adults (15–49 years) was 20.4%, and in the same year 71,000 people died from an AIDS-related illness.

A 2008 study revealed that HIV/AIDS infection is distinctly divided along racial lines: 13.6% of blacks are HIV-positive, whereas only 0.3% of whites have the virus. Most deaths are experienced by economically active individuals, resulting in many [AIDS orphans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDS_orphan "AIDS orphan") who, in many cases, depend on the state for care and financial support. It is estimated that there are 1,200,000 orphans in South Africa.

The link between HIV, a virus spread primarily by sexual contact, and AIDS was long [denied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS_denialism "HIV/AIDS denialism") by President [Thabo Mbeki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thabo_Mbeki "Thabo Mbeki") and his health minister, [Manto Tshabalala-Msimang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manto_Tshabalala-Msimang "Manto Tshabalala-Msimang"), who insisted that the many deaths in the country are caused by [malnutrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malnutrition_in_South_Africa "Malnutrition in South Africa"), and hence poverty, and not HIV. In 2007, in response to international pressure, the government made efforts to fight AIDS. After the [2009 general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_South_African_general_election "2009 South African general election"), President Jacob Zuma appointed [Aaron Motsoaledi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Motsoaledi "Aaron Motsoaledi") as the health minister and committed his government to increasing funding for and widening the scope of HIV treatment, and by 2015, South Africa had made significant progress, with the widespread availability of [antiretroviral drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management_of_HIV/AIDS "Management of HIV/AIDS") resulted in an increase in life expectancy from 52.1 years to 62.5 years.

## Culture

The South African black majority still has a substantial number of rural inhabitants who lead largely impoverished lives. It is among these people that cultural traditions survive most strongly; as black people have become increasingly urbanised and [Westernised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_world "Western world"), aspects of traditional culture have declined. Members of the middle class, who have historically been predominantly white but whose ranks include growing numbers of black, Coloured and Indian people, have lifestyles similar in many respects to that of people found in [Western Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Europe "Western Europe"), [North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_America "North America") and [Australasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australasia "Australasia").

### Arts

[South African art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_art "South African art") includes the oldest art objects in the world, which were discovered in a South African cave and dated from roughly 75,000 years ago. The scattered tribes of the Khoisan peoples moving into South Africa from around 10,000 BC had their own fluent art styles seen today in a multitude of cave paintings. They were superseded by the Bantu/Nguni peoples with their own vocabularies of art forms. Forms of art evolved in the mines and townships: a dynamic art using everything from plastic strips to bicycle spokes. The Dutch-influenced folk art of the Afrikaner [*trekboers*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trekboer "Trekboer") and the urban white artists, earnestly following changing European traditions from the 1850s onwards, also contributed to this eclectic mix which continues to evolve to this day.

### Media

The South African media sector is large, and South Africa is one of Africa's major media centres. While the many broadcasters and publications reflect the diversity of the population as a whole, the most commonly used language is English. However, all ten other official languages are represented to some extent. South Africa's media landscape is diverse and well-developed, comprising the public broadcaster [SABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SABC "SABC"), the private [free-to-air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-to-air "Free-to-air") channel [E.tv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.tv "E.tv"), and [satellite television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_television "Satellite television") provider [MultiChoice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MultiChoice "MultiChoice"), which operates [DStv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DStv "DStv") across sub-Saharan Africa. The country has partially rolled out [digital terrestrial television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_terrestrial_television "Digital terrestrial television") (DTT), though full migration from analogue broadcasting has experienced delays.

### Popular culture

There is great diversity in [South African music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_South_Africa "Music of South Africa"). Black musicians have developed styles called [Kwaito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwaito "Kwaito") and [Amapiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amapiano "Amapiano"). Of note is [Brenda Fassie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenda_Fassie "Brenda Fassie"), who launched to fame with her song "[Weekend Special](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weekend_Special "Weekend Special")", which was sung in English. More famous traditional musicians include [Ladysmith Black Mambazo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladysmith_Black_Mambazo "Ladysmith Black Mambazo"), while the [Soweto String Quartet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soweto_String_Quartet "Soweto String Quartet") performs classical music with an African flavour. South Africa has produced world-famous jazz musicians, notably [Hugh Masekela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Masekela "Hugh Masekela"), [Jonas Gwangwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonas_Gwangwa "Jonas Gwangwa"), [Abdullah Ibrahim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Ibrahim "Abdullah Ibrahim"), [Miriam Makeba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miriam_Makeba "Miriam Makeba"), [Jonathan Butler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Butler "Jonathan Butler"), [Chris McGregor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_McGregor "Chris McGregor"), and [Sathima Bea Benjamin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sathima_Bea_Benjamin "Sathima Bea Benjamin").

Afrikaans music covers multiple genres, such as the contemporary [Steve Hofmeyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Hofmeyr "Steve Hofmeyr"), the [punk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock "Punk rock") band [Fokofpolisiekar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fokofpolisiekar "Fokofpolisiekar"), and the singer-songwriter [Jeremy Loops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Loops "Jeremy Loops"). South African popular musicians that have found international success include [Manfred Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manfred_Mann_(musician) "Manfred Mann (musician)"), [Johnny Clegg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Clegg "Johnny Clegg"), rap-rave duo [Die Antwoord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Antwoord "Die Antwoord"), [Tyla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyla_(South_African_singer) "Tyla (South African singer)"), and rock band [Seether](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seether "Seether"). Rappers such as [AKA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AKA_(rapper) "AKA (rapper)"), [Nasty C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasty_C "Nasty C") and [Cassper Nyovest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassper_Nyovest "Cassper Nyovest") gained notoriety in other avenues like the BET Awards for best African acts.

Although few [South African film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_South_Africa "Cinema of South Africa") productions are known outside South Africa, many foreign films have been produced about South Africa. Arguably, the most high-profile film portraying South Africa in recent years was *[District 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_9 "District 9")*, as well as *[Chappie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chappie_(film) "Chappie (film)")*. Other notable exceptions are the film *[Tsotsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsotsi "Tsotsi")*, which won the [Academy Award for Foreign Language Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_International_Feature_Film "Academy Award for Best International Feature Film") at the [78th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/78th_Academy_Awards "78th Academy Awards") in 2006, as well as *[U-Carmen e-Khayelitsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-Carmen_e-Khayelitsha "U-Carmen e-Khayelitsha")*, which won the [Golden Bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Bear "Golden Bear") at the 2005 [Berlin International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_International_Film_Festival "Berlin International Film Festival"). In 2015, the [Oliver Hermanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Hermanus "Oliver Hermanus") film [*The Endless River*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Endless_River_(film) "The Endless River (film)") became the first South African film selected for the [Venice Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice_Film_Festival "Venice Film Festival").

### Literature

[South African literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_literature "South African literature") emerged from a unique social and political history. It "embraces all the languages and cultures of the people of South Africa" and began as an oral tradition. South Africa has 11 official spoken languages: Sepedi, Sesotho, Setswana, siSwati, Tshivenda, Xitsonga, Afrikaans, English, isiNdebele, isiXhosa and isiZulu.

#### Literature in English

Notable white South African authors include anti-apartheid activist [Alan Paton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Paton "Alan Paton"), who published the novel *[Cry, the Beloved Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cry,_the_Beloved_Country "Cry, the Beloved Country")* in 1948. [Nadine Gordimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadine_Gordimer "Nadine Gordimer") became the first South African to be awarded the [Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "Nobel Prize in Literature"), in 1991. [J.M. Coetzee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._M._Coetzee "J. M. Coetzee") won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 2003. When awarding the prize, the [Swedish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Academy "Swedish Academy") stated that Coetzee "in innumerable guises portrays the surprising involvement of the outsider."

[Antjie Krog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antjie_Krog "Antjie Krog") is a poet, journalist, academic and writer noted for confronting apartheid and its aftermath in her work. She reported on the Truth and Reconciliation Commission for the South African Broadcasting Corporation, and her best-known book is *[Country of My Skull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_of_My_Skull "Country of My Skull")* (1998). Elsa Joubert was an Afrikaans novelist, travel-writer and journalist whose work draws on African landscapes and human experiences. She is best known for the 1979 novel *[Die swerfjare van Poppie Nongena (The Long Journey of Poppie Nongena)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Long_Journey_of_Poppie_Nongena "The Long Journey of Poppie Nongena")*, a story of a black South African woman's endurance under apartheid, translated into many languages and widely celebrated.

One of the first well-known novels written by a black author in an African language was [Solomon Thekiso Plaatje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sol_Plaatje "Sol Plaatje")'s *[Mhudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mhudi "Mhudi")*, written in 1930. During the 1950s, *[Drum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drum_(South_African_magazine) "Drum (South African magazine)")* magazine became a hotbed of political satire, fiction, and essays, giving a voice to the urban black culture.

The plays of [Athol Fugard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athol_Fugard "Athol Fugard") have been regularly premiered in [fringe theatres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fringe_theatre "Fringe theatre") in South Africa, London ([Royal Court Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Court_Theatre "Royal Court Theatre")) and New York. [Olive Schreiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_Schreiner "Olive Schreiner")'s *[The Story of an African Farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Story_of_an_African_Farm "The Story of an African Farm")* (1883) was a revelation in [Victorian literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_literature "Victorian literature"); it has become recognised as one of the first feminist novels.

[Breyten Breytenbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breyten_Breytenbach "Breyten Breytenbach") was jailed for his involvement with the guerrilla movement against apartheid. [André Brink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Brink "André Brink") was the first Afrikaner writer to be [banned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banned_book "Banned book") by the government after he released the novel *[A Dry White Season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Dry_White_Season_(novel) "A Dry White Season (novel)")*.

### Cuisine

[South African cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_cuisine "South African cuisine") is diverse and reflects the country's multicultural heritage, incorporating influences from indigenous African, [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Dutch "Cape Dutch"), [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_cuisine "British cuisine"), [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_South_Africans "Indian South Africans") and [Cape Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Malays "Cape Malays") culinary traditions. Meat plays a central role in many regional food cultures, most notably through the *[braai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braai "Braai")*, an originally [Afrikaner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrikaners "Afrikaners") variation of the [barbecue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbecue "Barbecue") that functions as both a cooking method and a widely shared social custom across communities. Common braai staples include *[boerewors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boerewors "Boerewors")* (spiced sausage), [lamb chops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamb_and_mutton "Lamb and mutton"), [steak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steak "Steak"), [*pap*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mielie_meal "Mielie meal") (maize porridge) and *[chakalaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakalaka "Chakalaka")* (spicy relish). Traditional dishes also vary by region and heritage, including *[bobotie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobotie "Bobotie")*, a curried minced meat dish with an egg-based topping; *[bunny chow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunny_chow "Bunny chow")*, a hollowed-out loaf of bread filled with [curry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curry "Curry") originating in Durban; and *[potjiekos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potjiekos "Potjiekos")*, a slow-cooked stew prepared in a cast-iron pot over an open flame. Street foods such as *[vetkoek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vetkoek "Vetkoek")*, [*gatsby*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatsby_(sandwich) "Gatsby (sandwich)") sandwiches, [*samoosas*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samosa "Samosa") and *[biltong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biltong "Biltong")* (air-dried cured meat) are widely consumed, while popular desserts such as [*milk tart*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melktert "Melktert") and *[koeksisters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koeksister "Koeksister")* reflect the country's layered culinary traditions.

South Africa also has a highly developed [fast-food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_food "Fast food") culture, with several locally founded chains achieving national and international prominence. The most notable is [Nando's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nando%27s "Nando's"), founded in Johannesburg in 1987, which specialises in flame-grilled [peri-peri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piri_piri "Piri piri") chicken and operates more than 1,250 outlets in over 23 countries. Other major South African fast-food franchises include [Wimpy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wimpy_(restaurant) "Wimpy (restaurant)"), [Steers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steers_(restaurant) "Steers (restaurant)"), [Debonairs Pizza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debonairs_Pizza "Debonairs Pizza") and [Chicken Licken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_Licken_(restaurant) "Chicken Licken (restaurant)"), many of which have expanded throughout Africa and other regions. International fast-food brands are also well established in South Africa; the country is among the world's leading markets for [KFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KFC "KFC") outlets, reflecting the widespread popularity of fast food across income groups and urban centres.

In the beverage sector, South Africa has played an influential role in both global [energy drinks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_drink "Energy drink") and [wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine "Wine"). [Monster Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monster_Energy "Monster Energy"), although marketed as an American brand, was founded by South African-born entrepreneurs [Rodney Sacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodney_Sacks "Rodney Sacks") and [Hilton Schlosberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilton_Schlosberg "Hilton Schlosberg"), who relocated to the United States and played a central role in the company's international expansion. [South African wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_wine "South African wine") is also renowned internationally, with [vineyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vineyard "Vineyard") in regions such as [Stellenbosch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellenbosch "Stellenbosch"), [Franschhoek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franschhoek "Franschhoek"), [Paarl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paarl "Paarl") and [Barrydale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrydale "Barrydale"). These wine-producing areas form a significant part of the country's culinary tourism industry, contributing to South Africa's international reputation for food and drink.

### Sports

Sport plays a significant role in South African culture, and the country's most popular sports are [soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football "Association football"), [rugby union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_union "Rugby union") and [cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket "Cricket"). Other sports with notable support are [swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_(sport) "Swimming (sport)"), [athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_of_athletics "Sport of athletics"), [golf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golf "Golf"), [boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxing "Boxing"), mixed [martial arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_arts "Martial arts"), [tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis "Tennis"), [ringball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringball "Ringball"), [field hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_hockey "Field hockey"), [surfing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfing "Surfing") and [netball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netball "Netball").

Soccer is the most popular sport in South Africa. South Africa hosted the [2010 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup "2010 FIFA World Cup"). It hosted the [1996 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_African_Cup_of_Nations "1996 African Cup of Nations"), with the national team [Bafana Bafana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_national_soccer_team "South Africa national soccer team") going on to win the tournament. South Africa's men's U-20 team also won the [2025 U-20 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_U-20_Africa_Cup_of_Nations "2025 U-20 Africa Cup of Nations"). In 2022, the [women's team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_women%27s_national_soccer_team "South Africa women's national soccer team") also won the [Women's Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Women%27s_Africa_Cup_of_Nations "2022 Women's Africa Cup of Nations"), beating [Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco_women%27s_national_football_team "Morocco women's national football team") 2–1 in [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Women%27s_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_Final "2022 Women's Africa Cup of Nations Final"). The women's team went on to reach the last 16 at the [2023 FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2023 FIFA Women's World Cup"), beating [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_women%27s_national_football_team "Italy women's national football team") and tying with [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_women%27s_national_football_team "Argentina women's national football team") in the group stage.

Durban surfer [Jordy Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordy_Smith "Jordy Smith") won the 2010 Billabong J-Bay Open making him the highest ranked surfer in the world. South Africa produced [Formula One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_One "Formula One") motor racing's 1979 world champion [Jody Scheckter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jody_Scheckter "Jody Scheckter"). Famous active [Grand Prix motorcycle racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_motorcycle_racing "Grand Prix motorcycle racing") personalities include [Brad Binder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Binder "Brad Binder") and his younger brother [Darryn Binder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darryn_Binder "Darryn Binder").

In 2004, the swimming team of [Roland Schoeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_Schoeman "Roland Schoeman"), [Lyndon Ferns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_Ferns "Lyndon Ferns"), [Darian Townsend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darian_Townsend "Darian Townsend") and [Ryk Neethling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryk_Neethling "Ryk Neethling") won the gold medal at the [Olympic Games in Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Summer_Olympics "2004 Summer Olympics"), simultaneously breaking the world record in the 4×100 [Freestyle Relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freestyle_Relay "Freestyle Relay"). [Penny Heyns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penny_Heyns "Penny Heyns") won Olympic Gold in the 1996 [Atlanta Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta_Olympic_Games "Atlanta Olympic Games"), and more recently, swimmers [Tatjana Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatjana_Smith "Tatjana Smith") (née Schoenmaker), [Lara van Niekerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lara_van_Niekerk "Lara van Niekerk"), [Akani Simbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akani_Simbine "Akani Simbine") and [Wayde van Niekerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayde_van_Niekerk "Wayde van Niekerk") have all broken records and won medals at both the Olympic and [Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Games "Commonwealth Games"), with Wayde van Niekerk being the world record holder in [400 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/400_metres "400 metres") since 2016. In 2012, [Oscar Pistorius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Pistorius "Oscar Pistorius") became the first double amputee sprinter to compete at the [Olympic Games in London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics "2012 Summer Olympics"). [Gary Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Player "Gary Player") is regarded as one of the greatest golfers of all time, having won the [Career Grand Slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_(golf) "Grand Slam (golf)"), one of five to have done so.
