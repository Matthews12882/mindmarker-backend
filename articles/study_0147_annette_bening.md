# Annette Bening

**Annette Carol Bening** (born May 29, 1958) is an American actress. With a career spanning over four decades, she is known for [her versatile work across screen and stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Annette_Bening_performances "List of Annette Bening performances"). Bening has received [numerous accolades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Annette_Bening "List of awards and nominations received by Annette Bening"), including a [BAFTA Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Awards "British Academy Film Awards"), two [Actor Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actor_Awards "Actor Awards"), and two [Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards "Golden Globe Awards"), in addition to nominations for five [Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards"), two [Tony Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Awards "Tony Awards"), and a [Primetime Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award "Primetime Emmy Award"), making her one of few artists nominated for the [Triple Crown of Acting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Crown_of_Acting "Triple Crown of Acting") without winning.

A graduate of [San Francisco State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_State_University "San Francisco State University") and the [American Conservatory Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Conservatory_Theater "American Conservatory Theater"), Bening started her career on stage with the [Colorado Shakespeare Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Shakespeare_Festival "Colorado Shakespeare Festival") company in 1980, and played [Lady Macbeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Macbeth "Lady Macbeth") in 1984 at the [American Conservatory Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Conservatory_Theater "American Conservatory Theater"). She made her [Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_(theatre) "Broadway (theatre)") debut in the [Tina Howe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tina_Howe "Tina Howe") play *[Coastal Disturbances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Disturbances "Coastal Disturbances")* (1987) for which she received a nomination for the [Tony Award for Best Featured Actress in a Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Featured_Actress_in_a_Play "Tony Award for Best Featured Actress in a Play"). Making her film debut in 1988, she gained further recognition for her role in *[The Grifters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Grifters_(film) "The Grifters (film)")* (1990), for which she received her first Academy Award nomination. This acclaim continued throughout the 1990s and 2000s with further Oscar-nominated performances in the comedy-dramas *[American Beauty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Beauty_(1999_film) "American Beauty (1999 film)")* (1999) and *[Being Julia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Being_Julia "Being Julia")* (2004), which respectively won her the [BAFTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Leading_Role "BAFTA Award for Best Actress in a Leading Role") and [Golden Globe for Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Musical_or_Comedy "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy"). Bening's performance as the [title character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_character "Title character") in the British television film *[Mrs. Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mrs._Harris "Mrs. Harris")* (2005) earned her a nomination for the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Miniseries or Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actress_in_a_Limited_or_Anthology_Series_or_Movie "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Limited or Anthology Series or Movie").

In following decades, Bening received two additional Oscar nominations for her leading roles as a [lesbian mother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_parenting "Same-sex parenting") in *[The Kids Are All Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kids_Are_All_Right_(film) "The Kids Are All Right (film)")* (2010) and swimmer [Diana Nyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diana_Nyad "Diana Nyad") in the [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") biographical film *[Nyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyad_(film) "Nyad (film)")* (2023), the former of which also won her a Golden Globe. She returned to Broadway in the revival of [Arthur Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Miller "Arthur Miller")'s *[All My Sons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_My_Sons "All My Sons")* (2019) earning another Tony nomination for [Best Actress in a Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Play "Tony Award for Best Actress in a Play"). Her other roles during this period include in the films *[20th Century Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Century_Women "20th Century Women")* (2016), *[Film Stars Don't Die in Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_Stars_Don%27t_Die_in_Liverpool "Film Stars Don't Die in Liverpool")* (2017), *[Captain Marvel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Marvel_(film) "Captain Marvel (film)")* (2019) and *[Death on the Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_on_the_Nile_(2022_film) "Death on the Nile (2022 film)")* (2022), the miniseries *[Apples Never Fall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apples_Never_Fall "Apples Never Fall")* (2024) and the series *[Dutton Ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutton_Ranch "Dutton Ranch")* (2026).

## Early life and education

Annette Carol Bening was born on May 29, 1958 in [Topeka, Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topeka,_Kansas "Topeka, Kansas"), to Shirley Katherine (née Ashley) and Arnett Grant Bening. Her mother was a church singer and soloist, and her father was a sales training consultant and insurance salesman. Her parents, originally from [Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa "Iowa"), were practicing [Episcopalians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Church_in_the_United_States_of_America "Episcopal Church in the United States of America") and [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States "Conservatism in the United States") [Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States) "Republican Party (United States)"). She is of mostly German and English descent.

The youngest of four children, she has a sister Jane, and two brothers Bradley and Byron. The family moved to [Wichita, Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wichita,_Kansas "Wichita, Kansas"), in 1959, where she spent her early childhood. When Bening was in elementary school, her father relocated the family to [San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego "San Diego"), California, where she spent the remainder of her youth.

She began acting in junior high school, playing the lead in *[The Sound of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sound_of_Music "The Sound of Music")*. She graduated in 1975 from San Diego's [Patrick Henry High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Henry_High_School_(San_Diego,_California) "Patrick Henry High School (San Diego, California)"), where she studied drama. She then spent a year working as a cook on a charter boat taking fishing parties out on the Pacific Ocean, and scuba diving for recreation. Bening attended [San Diego Mesa College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Mesa_College "San Diego Mesa College") and graduated with a degree in [Theatre Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_Arts "Theatre Arts") at [San Francisco State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_State_University "San Francisco State University").

## Career

### 1986–1999: Initial work, breakthrough and rise to prominence

Bening began her career on stage with the [Colorado Shakespeare Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Shakespeare_Festival "Colorado Shakespeare Festival") company in 1980, and appeared in plays at the [San Diego Repertory Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Repertory_Theatre "San Diego Repertory Theatre"). She was a member of the acting company at the [American Conservatory Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Conservatory_Theater "American Conservatory Theater") in San Francisco while studying acting as part of the Advanced Theatre Training Program. There, she starred in such productions as Shakespeare's *[Macbeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macbeth "Macbeth")* as [Lady Macbeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Macbeth "Lady Macbeth"). Bening also starred in productions of *[Pygmalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pygmalion_(play) "Pygmalion (play)")* and *[The Cherry Orchard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cherry_Orchard "The Cherry Orchard")* at the [Denver Center Theatre Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver_Center_for_the_Performing_Arts "Denver Center for the Performing Arts") during the 1985–86 season. She made her [Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_theatre "Broadway theatre") debut in 1987, garnering a [Tony Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award "Tony Award") nomination for [Best Featured Actress in a Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Featured_Actress_in_a_Play "Tony Award for Best Featured Actress in a Play") and receiving a [Theatre World Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_World_Award "Theatre World Award") for her performance as young photographer Holly Dancer in *[Coastal Disturbances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Disturbances "Coastal Disturbances")*. Bening made her film debut in *[The Great Outdoors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Outdoors_(film) "The Great Outdoors (film)")* (1988), starring [Dan Aykroyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Aykroyd "Dan Aykroyd") and [John Candy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Candy "John Candy"). Her next role was as the Marquise de Merteuil in *[Valmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valmont_(film) "Valmont (film)")* (1989) opposite [Colin Firth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Firth "Colin Firth").

Bening achieved her breakthrough with her performance as [con artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Con_artist "Con artist") Myra Langtry in the neo-noir crime thriller *[The Grifters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Grifters_(film) "The Grifters (film)")* (1990), for which she earned her first nominations for the [Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress "Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress") and the [BAFTA Award for Best Actress in a Supporting Role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Supporting_Role "BAFTA Award for Best Actress in a Supporting Role"). In 1991, she portrayed [Virginia Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Hill "Virginia Hill") in [Barry Levinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Levinson "Barry Levinson")'s biopic *[Bugsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugsy "Bugsy")*, alongside [Warren Beatty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Beatty "Warren Beatty"). For *Bugsy*, she received her first nomination for the [Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Drama "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Drama"). Bening co-starred with [Harrison Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrison_Ford "Harrison Ford") in *[Regarding Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regarding_Henry "Regarding Henry")*. In 1994, Bening and Beatty starred together again, in *[Love Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Affair_(1994_film) "Love Affair (1994 film)")*. In 1995, Bening played a leading role as an environmental [lobbyist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobbyist "Lobbyist") in *[The American President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_American_President "The American President")* with [Michael Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Douglas "Michael Douglas"), a role she followed with [Tim Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Burton "Tim Burton")'s sci-fi spoof *[Mars Attacks!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_Attacks! "Mars Attacks!")* (1996), and *[The Siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Siege_(1998_film) "The Siege (1998 film)")* (1998), a thriller with [Denzel Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denzel_Washington "Denzel Washington") and [Bruce Willis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Willis "Bruce Willis").

Bening garnered acclaim for her starring role in [Sam Mendes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Mendes "Sam Mendes")' directorial debut film, *[American Beauty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Beauty_(1999_film) "American Beauty (1999 film)")* (1999). She starred opposite [Kevin Spacey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Spacey "Kevin Spacey") in the dark comedy-drama about a man suffering a mid-life crisis in 90s American suburbia. The film won five [Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards"), including [Best Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture"). For her performance as Carolyn Burnham, a materialistic wife engaging in an affair, Bening won the [BAFTA Award for Best Actress in a Leading Role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Leading_Role "BAFTA Award for Best Actress in a Leading Role") and the [Actor Award for Outstanding Performance by a Female Actor in a Leading Role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actor_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Female_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role "Actor Award for Outstanding Performance by a Female Actor in a Leading Role"), in addition to her first nomination for the [Academy Award for Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress "Academy Award for Best Actress") and her second nomination for the Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Drama.

In 1999, Bening returned to the stage for the first time in 10 years playing the title role in *[Hedda Gabler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedda_Gabler "Hedda Gabler")* at the [Geffen Playhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geffen_Playhouse "Geffen Playhouse") in Los Angeles. The *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")* praised her performance saying "Bening uses her vocal instrument to fine effect, without throwing it around... In the movies you don't always hear what Bening can do with that voice, especially when she's playing virtuous, "sensible" types... But Ibsen's antiheroine—thwarted sensualist, a woman wrestling with her inner troll, belle of a ball that never comes—is neither virtuous nor sensible. She's no easy-to-read villain, either, nor a mere vindictive brat, though plenty of actresses have reduced her thus. Bening lays into the venomous sarcasm mighty heavily, but she's cagey enough to avoid reductive extremes."

### 2000–2014: Established actress

Bening starred in other films, including *[In Dreams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Dreams_(film) "In Dreams (film)")* (1999) and *[What Planet Are You From?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_Planet_Are_You_From%3F "What Planet Are You From?")* (2000). Bening played Sue Barlow in *[Open Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Range_(2003_film) "Open Range (2003 film)")* (2003). She earned critical acclaim for playing the eponymous lead in the comedy-drama *[Being Julia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Being_Julia "Being Julia")* (2004). For her performance in the film, she won her first [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Comedy_or_Musical "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress – Motion Picture Comedy or Musical") in addition to her second [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress "Academy Award for Best Actress") and [Actor Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actor_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Female_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role "Actor Award for Outstanding Performance by a Female Actor in a Leading Role") nominations. She received [Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actress_in_a_Limited_or_Anthology_Series_or_Movie "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Limited or Anthology Series or Movie") and [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_%E2%80%93_Miniseries_or_Television_Film "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress – Miniseries or Television Film") nominations for her role of convicted celebrity murderer [Jean Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Harris "Jean Harris") in the [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO "HBO") film *[Mrs. Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mrs._Harris "Mrs. Harris")* (2005). She replaced [Julianne Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julianne_Moore "Julianne Moore") and starred as Deirdre Burroughs, an aspiring poet with [bipolar disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipolar_disorder "Bipolar disorder"), in the film adaptation of *[Running with Scissors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_with_Scissors_(film) "Running with Scissors (film)")* (2006).

. She earned her fifth [Golden Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Musical_or_Comedy "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy") nomination. Bening starred in *[The Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Women_(2008_film) "The Women (2008 film)")* (2008) remake. In 2009, Bening starred in a new interpretation of the [Euripides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euripides "Euripides") classic *[Medea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medea_(play) "Medea (play)")* at [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA "UCLA")'s Freud Playhouse. She received positive reviews for her performance in the independent film *[Mother and Child](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_and_Child_(2009_film) "Mother and Child (2009 film)")* (2009).

In 2010, she starred in [*The Female of the Species*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Female_of_the_Species_(play) "The Female of the Species (play)"), [Joanna Murray-Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joanna_Murray-Smith "Joanna Murray-Smith")'s comedy, at the [Geffen Playhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geffen_Playhouse "Geffen Playhouse") in Los Angeles. Later that year, Bening received critical acclaim for her performance in *[The Kids Are All Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kids_Are_All_Right_(film) "The Kids Are All Right (film)")*; a reviewer said that she "deserves an Oscar" and another praised her "sublime" performance. For her role, Bening won her second [Golden Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Musical_or_Comedy "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy") and was nominated for her third Academy Award, third BAFTA, and six and seventh [Actor Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actor_Awards "Actor Awards") (she received nominations for both [Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actor_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Female_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role "Actor Award for Outstanding Performance by a Female Actor in a Leading Role") and [Best Cast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actor_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Cast_in_a_Motion_Picture "Actor Award for Outstanding Performance by a Cast in a Motion Picture")).

In 2012, Bening's audiobook recording of [Virginia Woolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Woolf "Virginia Woolf")'s *[Mrs. Dalloway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mrs._Dalloway "Mrs. Dalloway")* was released at [Audible.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audible.com "Audible.com"). In 2014, she starred in Shakespeare's *[King Lear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Lear "King Lear")* at the [Delacorte Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delacorte_Theater "Delacorte Theater") in [Central Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Park "Central Park"), as part of the Public Theatre's Free [Shakespeare in the Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Shakespeare_Festival "New York Shakespeare Festival"). It marked her first New York stage appearance in twenty years. Bening starred in [Dan Fogelman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Fogelman "Dan Fogelman")'s 2015 American [comedy drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comedy_drama "Comedy drama") [*Danny Collins*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Collins_(film) "Danny Collins (film)") with [Al Pacino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Pacino "Al Pacino").

### 2016–present: Later career

In 2016, Bening starred in [Mike Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mills_(director) "Mike Mills (director)")'s comedy drama *[20th Century Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Century_Women "20th Century Women")* alongside [Elle Fanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elle_Fanning "Elle Fanning"), [Greta Gerwig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greta_Gerwig "Greta Gerwig"), and [Billy Crudup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Crudup "Billy Crudup"). Bening played a chain-smoking first-wave feminist struggling to raise her teenage son. Sheila O'Malley of *[Roger Ebert.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ebert "Roger Ebert")* declared, "Bening has one of the best performances of the year (and one of Bening's personal best as well)". For her performance, she was nominated for the [Independent Spirit Award for Best Female Lead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Spirit_Award_for_Best_Female_Lead "Independent Spirit Award for Best Female Lead") and the [Critics' Choice Movie Award for Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Movie_Award_for_Best_Actress "Critics' Choice Movie Award for Best Actress").

The following year, she portrayed [Gloria Grahame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Grahame "Gloria Grahame") in *[Film Stars Don't Die in Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_Stars_Don%27t_Die_in_Liverpool "Film Stars Don't Die in Liverpool")* (2017) alongside [Jamie Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Bell "Jamie Bell"), [Vanessa Redgrave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanessa_Redgrave "Vanessa Redgrave"), and [Julie Walters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julie_Walters "Julie Walters"). [Peter Bradshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bradshaw "Peter Bradshaw") critic from *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* praised her performance declaring, "Bening is excellent as Grahame: imperious, vulnerable, romantic, sexually excited about her younger man, wanly aware of secrets she cannot share with him". She received her third nomination for the BAFTA Award for Best Actress in a Leading Role for her performance in the film. The following year, she starred as Irina Arkadina in *[The Seagull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Seagull_(2018_film) "The Seagull (2018 film)")* and as Dr. Cait Morris in *[Life Itself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_Itself_(2018_film) "Life Itself (2018 film)")* (2018).

In 2019, Bening returned to the [Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_theatre "Broadway theatre") stage after a 32-year absence, starring in the revival of [Arthur Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Miller "Arthur Miller")'s *[All My Sons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_My_Sons "All My Sons")* alongside [Tracy Letts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracy_Letts "Tracy Letts") at the [Roundabout Theatre Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roundabout_Theatre_Company "Roundabout Theatre Company")'s [American Airlines Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines_Theatre "American Airlines Theatre"). The play opened on April 4, 2019, and closed on June 23, 2019. She was nominated for the [Tony Award for Best Actress in a Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Play "Tony Award for Best Actress in a Play") for her performance. The following year, she portrayed Senator [Dianne Feinstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dianne_Feinstein "Dianne Feinstein") in the political drama film *[The Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Report_(2019_film) "The Report (2019 film)")* (2019) for which she earned her first nomination for the [Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actress – Motion Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture "Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actress – Motion Picture").

Bening joined the [Marvel Cinematic Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe "Marvel Cinematic Universe") playing Dr. Wendy Lawson in *[Captain Marvel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Marvel_(film) "Captain Marvel (film)")* (2019) alongside [Brie Larson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brie_Larson "Brie Larson"). The film was a massive financial box-office hit. In 2022, she acted in [Kenneth Branagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Branagh "Kenneth Branagh")'s *[Death on the Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_on_the_Nile_(2022_film) "Death on the Nile (2022 film)")* and the comedy *[Jerry & Marge Go Large](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_%26_Marge_Go_Large "Jerry & Marge Go Large")* opposite [Bryan Cranston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Cranston "Bryan Cranston"). That same year, she filmed *[Nyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyad_(film) "Nyad (film)")* alongside [Jodie Foster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jodie_Foster "Jodie Foster"), where Bening portrayed [long-distance swimmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-distance_swimming "Long-distance swimming") [Diana Nyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diana_Nyad "Diana Nyad"). The film was distributed by [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") and premiered at the [2023 Toronto International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Toronto_International_Film_Festival "2023 Toronto International Film Festival"). Her performance earned high critical acclaim, and earned her a fifth [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress "Academy Award for Best Actress") nomination.

In 2023, Bening was elected Chair of the Board of Trustees for the [Entertainment Community Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Community_Fund "Entertainment Community Fund"). She next starred in [Liane Moriarty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liane_Moriarty "Liane Moriarty")'s *[Apples Never Fall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apples_Never_Fall "Apples Never Fall")*, based on the bestselling thriller which was adapted by [Peacock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacock_(streaming_service) "Peacock (streaming service)") and also features [Sam Neill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Neill "Sam Neill"). The limited series premiered with all episodes immediately available to stream on March 15, 2024.

## Personal life

While training at the [American Conservatory Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Conservatory_Theater "American Conservatory Theater"), Bening met actor J. Steven White, marrying him in 1984. They moved to Colorado where White managed the [Denver Center for the Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver_Center_for_the_Performing_Arts "Denver Center for the Performing Arts"). They divorced in 1991.

She has been married to actor [Warren Beatty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Beatty "Warren Beatty") since March 3, 1992. They have four children, including actress [Ella Beatty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ella_Beatty "Ella Beatty").

## Acting credits and accolades

Bening has received numerous accolades, including a [BAFTA Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Awards "British Academy Film Awards"), two [Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards "Golden Globe Awards"), and two [Actor Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actor_Awards "Actor Awards"). She has also received nominations for a [Primetime Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award "Primetime Emmy Award") and two [Tony Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award "Tony Award").

She has also received five [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards") nominations, for the following films:

* [63rd Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/63rd_Academy_Awards "63rd Academy Awards"): [Best Supporting Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress "Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress") nomination for *[The Grifters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Grifters_(film) "The Grifters (film)")* (1990)
* [72nd Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/72nd_Academy_Awards "72nd Academy Awards"): [Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress "Academy Award for Best Actress") nomination for *[American Beauty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Beauty_(1999_film) "American Beauty (1999 film)")* (1999)
* [77th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/77th_Academy_Awards "77th Academy Awards"): Best Actress nomination for *[Being Julia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Being_Julia "Being Julia")* (2004)
* [83rd Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/83rd_Academy_Awards "83rd Academy Awards"): Best Actress nomination for *[The Kids Are All Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kids_Are_All_Right_(film) "The Kids Are All Right (film)")* (2010)
* [96th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/96th_Academy_Awards "96th Academy Awards"), Best Actress nomination for *[Nyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyad_(film) "Nyad (film)")* (2023)
