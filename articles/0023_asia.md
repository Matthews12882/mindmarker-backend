# Asia

**Asia** ([/ˈeɪʒə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:En-us-Asia.ogg "File:En-us-Asia.ogg") [*AY-zhə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"), [UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English "British English") also [/ˈeɪʃə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*AY-shə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key")) is the largest [continent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continent "Continent") in the world by both land area and population. It covers an area of more than 44 million square kilometres, about 30% of Earth's total land area and 8% of Earth's total surface area. The continent, which has long been home to the majority of the [human population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_population "Human population"), was the site of many of the [first civilisations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_civilisations "First civilisations"). Its estimated 4.8 billion people in 2025 constituted approximately 59% of the world's population.

Asia shares the [landmass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landmass "Landmass") of [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia "Eurasia") with [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe "Europe"), and of [Afro-Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Eurasia "Afro-Eurasia") with both Europe and [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa "Africa"). In general terms, it is bounded on the east by the [Pacific Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean "Pacific Ocean"), on the south by the [Indian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean "Indian Ocean"), and on the north by the [Arctic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Ocean "Arctic Ocean"). The border of Asia with Europe is a [historical and cultural construct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_constructionism "Social constructionism"), as there is no clear physical or geographical separation between them. A commonly accepted division places Asia east of the [Suez Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Canal "Suez Canal"), which separates it from Africa; and east of the [Turkish Straits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_straits "Turkish straits"), the [Ural Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Mountains "Ural Mountains"), and the [Ural River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_River "Ural River"), and south of the [Caucasus Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_Mountains "Caucasus Mountains") and the [Caspian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_Sea "Caspian Sea") and [Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea "Black Sea") Seas, which separate it from Europe.

Since the concept of Asia derives from the term for the eastern region from a European perspective, Asia is the remaining vast area of Eurasia minus Europe. Therefore, Asia is a region where various independent cultures coexist rather than sharing a single culture, and its boundary with Europe is somewhat arbitrary and has moved since its first conception in [classical antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_antiquity "Classical antiquity"). The division of Eurasia into two continents reflects [East–West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East%E2%80%93West_dichotomy "East–West dichotomy") cultural differences, some of which vary on a spectrum.

[China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") and [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") traded places as the [largest economies in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_regions_by_past_GDP_(PPP) "List of regions by past GDP (PPP)") from 1 to 1800 CE. The [Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road "Silk Road") became the main east–west trading route in the Asian hinterlands, while the [Straits of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straits_of_Malacca "Straits of Malacca") stood as a major sea route. Asia has exhibited [economic dynamism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_dynamism "Economic dynamism") as well as robust population growth during the 20th century, but overall population growth has since fallen. Asia was the birthplace of most of the world's mainstream religions, including [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam"), [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism "Judaism"), [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity"), [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism"), [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"), and many other religions.

Asia varies greatly across and within [its regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Asia "Regions of Asia") with regard to [ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups "Ethnic groups"), cultures, environments, economics, historical ties, and government systems. It also has a mix of many different climates ranging from the equatorial south via the hot deserts in parts of [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia "West Asia"), [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia"), [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia"), and [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia") and tropical jungles in [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia"), temperate areas in the east and the continental centre to vast subarctic and polar areas in [North Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Asia "North Asia").

## Etymology

The term "Asia" is believed to originate in the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age "Bronze Age") toponym *[Assuwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assuwa "Assuwa")* ([Hittite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittite_language "Hittite language"): 𒀸𒋗𒉿, romanized: *aš-šu-wa*) which originally referred only to a portion of northwestern [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia "Anatolia"). The term appears in [Hittite records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittite_texts "Hittite texts") recounting how a confederation of Assuwan states including [Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy "Troy") unsuccessfully rebelled against the Hittite king [Tudhaliya I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudhaliya_I "Tudhaliya I") around 1400 BCE. Roughly contemporary [Linear B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_B "Linear B") documents contain the term *aswia* ([Mycenaean Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenaean_Greek_language "Mycenaean Greek language"): 𐀀𐀯𐀹𐀊, romanized: *a-si-wi-ja*), seemingly in reference to captives from the same area.

Herodotus used the term in reference to [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia "Anatolia") and the territory of the [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire "Achaemenid Empire"), in contrast to Greece and Egypt. He reports that Greeks assumed that Asia was named after the wife of [Prometheus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prometheus "Prometheus"), but that [Lydians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia "Lydia") say it was named after *Asies*, son of Cotys, who passed the name on to a tribe at [Sardis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardis "Sardis"). In [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology "Greek mythology"), "Asia" (Ἀσία or Ἀσίη) was the name of a "[Nymph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nymph "Nymph") or [Titan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titan_(mythology) "Titan (mythology)") goddess of Lydia". The [Iliad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iliad "Iliad") (attributed by the ancient Greeks to [Homer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer "Homer")) mentions two Phrygians in the [Trojan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_War "Trojan War") named [Asios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asius_(mythology) "Asius (mythology)") (literally 'Asian'), and also a marsh or lowland containing a marsh in Lydia as ασιος.

The term was later adopted by the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_people "Roman people"), who used it in reference to the [province of Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Asia "Province of Asia"), located in western Anatolia. One of the first writers to use Asia as a name for the whole continent was [Pliny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder "Pliny the Elder").

## Definition

### Asia–Europe boundary

The threefold division of the [Old World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World "Old World") into Africa, Asia, and Europe has been in use since the 6th century BCE, due to [Greek geographers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_geographers "Greek geographers") such as [Anaximander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaximander "Anaximander") and [Hecataeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hecataeus_of_Miletus "Hecataeus of Miletus"). Anaximander placed the boundary between Asia and Europe along the [Phasis River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phasis_River "Phasis River") (now the Rioni) in [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_(country) "Georgia (country)") of the Caucasus – from its mouth by [Poti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poti "Poti") on the [Black Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea "Black Sea") coast, through the [Surami Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surami_Pass "Surami Pass") and along the [Kura River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kura_(Caspian_Sea) "Kura (Caspian Sea)") to the Caspian Sea. [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus "Herodotus") still followed this convention in the 5th century BCE. During the [Hellenistic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period "Hellenistic period"), this convention was revised, and the boundary between Europe and Asia was now considered to be the [Tanais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_River_(Russia) "Don River (Russia)") (the modern Don River). This is the convention used by Roman-era authors such as [Posidonius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posidonius "Posidonius"), [Strabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabo "Strabo") and [Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy "Ptolemy").

The border between Asia and Europe was later redefined by European academics. In 1730, five years after the death of [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great "Peter the Great"), [Philip Johan von Strahlenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Johan_von_Strahlenberg "Philip Johan von Strahlenberg") published in Sweden a new atlas proposing the Ural Mountains as the border of Asia. [Vasily Tatishchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasily_Tatishchev "Vasily Tatishchev") announced that he had proposed the idea to von Strahlenberg. The latter had suggested the [Emba River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emba_River "Emba River") as the southern part of this boundary. Over the next century various proposals were made until the [Ural River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_River "Ural River") prevailed in the mid-19th century. The border had been moved perforce from the Black Sea (the Romans' definition) to the Caspian Sea into which the Emba and Ural rivers project. Between the Black Sea and the Caspian, the border is often placed along the crest of the [Caucasus Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_Mountains "Caucasus Mountains"), although it is sometimes placed further north, on the [Kuma–Manych Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuma%E2%80%93Manych_Depression "Kuma–Manych Depression").

### Asia–Africa boundary

The boundary between Asia and Africa is the [Suez Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Canal "Suez Canal"), the [Gulf of Suez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Suez "Gulf of Suez"), the [Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea "Red Sea"), and the [Bab-el-Mandeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bab-el-Mandeb "Bab-el-Mandeb"). This makes [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt") a [transcontinental country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcontinental_country "Transcontinental country"), with the [Sinai Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_Peninsula "Sinai Peninsula") in Asia and the remainder of the country in Africa.

### Asia–Oceania boundary

The border between Asia and [Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania "Oceania") is usually placed somewhere in the [Indonesian Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Archipelago "Indonesian Archipelago"), specifically in [Eastern Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Indonesia "Eastern Indonesia"). The [Wallace Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_Line "Wallace Line") separates the Asian and [Wallacea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallacea "Wallacea") biogeographical realms, a transition zone of deep water straits between the Asian and Australian continental shelves. [Weber's Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weber%27s_Line "Weber's Line") splits the region in two with regard to the balance of fauna between Asian origin and Australo-Papuan origin. Wallacea's eastern boundary with [Sahul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_(continent) "Australia (continent)") is represented by the [Lydekker's Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Lydekker "Richard Lydekker"). The [Maluku Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maluku_Islands "Maluku Islands") (except the [Aru Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aru_Islands_Regency "Aru Islands Regency")) are often considered to lie on the border of southeast Asia, with the Aru Islands and [Western New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_New_Guinea "Western New Guinea"), to the east of the Lydekker's Line, being wholly part of Oceania, as both lie on the Australian continental plate. Culturally, the Wallacea region denoted the transition between [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_people "Austronesian people") and [Melanesian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanesian_people "Melanesian people"), with varying degrees of intermixing between the two. In general, the further west and coastal a region is, the stronger the Austronesian influences, and the further east and inland a region is, the stronger the Melanesian influences. The terms Southeast Asia and Oceania, devised in the 19th century, have had several vastly different geographic meanings since their inception. The chief factor in determining which islands of the Indonesian Archipelago are Asian has been the location of the colonial possessions of the various empires there (not all European). Lewis and Wigen assert, "The narrowing of 'Southeast Asia' to its present boundaries was thus a gradual process."

### Asia–North America boundary

The [Bering Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bering_Strait "Bering Strait") and [Bering Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bering_Sea "Bering Sea") separate the landmasses of Asia and [North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_America "North America"), as well as forming the international boundary between Russia and the United States. This [national](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_boundary "National boundary") and continental boundary separates the [Diomede Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diomede_Islands "Diomede Islands") in the Bering Strait, with Big Diomede in [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia "Russia") and Little Diomede in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States"). The [Aleutian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleutian_Islands "Aleutian Islands") are an island chain extending westward from the [Alaskan Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaskan_Peninsula "Alaskan Peninsula") toward Russia's [Komandorski Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komandorski_Islands "Komandorski Islands") and [Kamchatka Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamchatka_Peninsula "Kamchatka Peninsula"). Most of them are always associated with North America, except for the westernmost [Near Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_Islands "Near Islands") group, which is on Asia's continental shelf beyond the [North Aleutians Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Aleutians_Basin "North Aleutians Basin") and on rare occasions could be associated with Asia, which could then allow the United States to be considered a transcontinental state. The Aleutian Islands are sometimes associated with Oceania, owing to their status as remote Pacific islands, and their proximity to the Pacific Plate. This is extremely rare, however, due to their non-tropical biogeography, as well as their [inhabitants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleut "Aleut"), who have historically been related to the [Indigenous peoples of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas "Indigenous peoples of the Americas").

[St. Lawrence Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Lawrence_Island "St. Lawrence Island") in the northern Bering Sea belongs to the US state of [Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska "Alaska") and may be associated with either continent but is almost always considered part of North America, as with the [Rat Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rat_Islands "Rat Islands") in the Aleutian chain. At their nearest points, Alaska and Russia are separated by only 4 kilometres (2.5 miles).

### Ongoing definition

Geographical Asia is a cultural artifact of European conceptions of the world, beginning with the [Ancient Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greeks "Ancient Greeks"), being imposed onto other cultures, an imprecise concept causing endemic contention about what it means. Asia does not exactly correspond to the cultural borders of its various types of constituents.

From the time of Herodotus, a minority of geographers have rejected the three-continent system (Europe, Africa, Asia) on the grounds that there is no substantial physical separation between them. For example, Sir [Barry Cunliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Cunliffe "Barry Cunliffe"), the emeritus professor of European archeology at Oxford, argues that Europe has been geographically and culturally merely "the western excrescence of the continent of Asia".

Geographically, Asia is the major eastern constituent of the continent of [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia "Eurasia"), with Europe being a northwestern [peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsula "Peninsula") of the landmass. Asia, Europe and Africa make up a single continuous landmass – [Afro-Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Eurasia "Afro-Eurasia") – and share a common [continental shelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_shelf "Continental shelf"). Almost all of Europe and a major part of Asia sit atop the [Eurasian Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Plate "Eurasian Plate"), adjoined on the south by the [Arabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Plate "Arabian Plate") and [Indian Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Plate "Indian Plate") and with the easternmost part of Siberia (east of the [Chersky Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chersky_Range "Chersky Range")) on the [North American Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Plate "North American Plate").

## History

### Prehistory

About 1.8 million years ago, *[Homo erectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_erectus "Homo erectus")* left the African continent. This species is believed to have lived in East and Southeast Asia from 1.8 million to 110,000 years ago.["Homo erectus"](https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/human-fossils/species/homo-erectus). *The Smithsonian Institution's Human Origins Program*. Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History. 3 January 2024. Retrieved 14 September 2026.

Researchers believe that the modern human, or *[Homo sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_sapiens "Homo sapiens")*, migrated about 60,000 years ago to [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia") along the Indian Ocean. Modern humans [interbred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interbreeding_between_archaic_and_modern_humans "Interbreeding between archaic and modern humans") with an [archaic human](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaic_human "Archaic human") species called [Denisovans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denisovans "Denisovans") in Southeast Asia. Before the arrival of modern humans, [Flores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flores "Flores") was occupied by *[Homo floresiensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_floresiensis "Homo floresiensis")*, a small archaic human.

Ancestors of [East Eurasians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_East_Eurasians "Ancient East Eurasians") split from Ancient West Eurasians about 46,000 years ago, migrating out of the hub in the [Iranian Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Plateau "Iranian Plateau"). Evidence from full genomic studies suggests that the first people in the Americas diverged from [Ancient East Asians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_history_of_East_Asians "Genetic history of East Asians") about 36,000 years ago and expanded northwards into Siberia, where they encountered and interacted with a different Paleolithic Siberian population (known as [Ancient North Eurasians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_North_Eurasian "Ancient North Eurasian")), giving rise to both [Paleosiberian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleosiberian_peoples "Paleosiberian peoples") and [Ancient Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Beringian "Ancient Beringian").

Modern [South Asians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics_and_archaeogenetics_of_South_Asia "Genetics and archaeogenetics of South Asia") are descendants of a combination of Western Eurasian ancestries (notably [Neolithic Iranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_hunter-gatherers "Iranian hunter-gatherers") and [Western Steppe Herder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Steppe_Herders "Western Steppe Herders") components) with a proposed indigenous [East Eurasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_East_Eurasians "Ancient East Eurasians") component (termed *Ancient Ancestral South Indians*) closest to the non–West Eurasian part extracted from South Asian samples; distantly related to the [Andamanese peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andamanese_people "Andamanese people"), East Asians and [Aboriginal Australians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aboriginal_Australians "Aboriginal Australians").

### Ancient era

The [history of Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Asia "History of Asia") can be seen as the distinct histories of several peripheral coastal regions: [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia"), [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia"), [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia"), [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia"), and [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia "West Asia"). The coastal periphery was home to some of the world's earliest known civilisations, each of them developing around fertile river valleys. The civilisations in [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia "Mesopotamia"), the [Indus Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley "Indus Valley") and the [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River "Yellow River") shared many similarities. These civilisations may well have exchanged technologies and ideas such as [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics") and the [wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel "Wheel"). Other innovations, such as writing, seem to have been developed individually in each area. Cities, states and empires developed in these lowlands.

The central steppe region had long been inhabited by horse-mounted nomads who could reach all areas of Asia from the [steppes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe "Steppe"). The earliest postulated [expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_migrations "Indo-European migrations") out of the steppe is that of the [Indo-Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Europeans "Indo-Europeans"), who spread their languages into West Asia, South Asia, and the borders of China, where the [Tocharians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tocharians "Tocharians") resided. The northernmost part of Asia, including much of [Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberia "Siberia"), was largely inaccessible to the steppe nomads, owing to the dense forests, climate and [tundra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra "Tundra"). These areas remained very sparsely populated.

The center and the peripheries were mostly kept separated by mountains and deserts. The [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus "Caucasus") and [Himalaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalaya "Himalaya") mountains and the [Karakum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakum_Desert "Karakum Desert") and [Gobi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gobi "Gobi") deserts formed barriers that the [steppe horsemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_nomads "Eurasian nomads") could cross only with difficulty. While the urban city dwellers were more advanced technologically and socially, in many cases they could do little in a military aspect to defend against the [mounted hordes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomadic_empire "Nomadic empire") of the steppe. However, the lowlands did not have enough open grasslands to support a large equestrian force; for this and other reasons, the nomads who conquered states in China, India, and the Middle East often found themselves adapting to the local, more affluent societies.

### Medieval era

The Islamic [Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate "Caliphate")'s defeats of the [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine "Byzantine") and Persian empires led to West Asia and southern parts of [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia") and western parts of South Asia under its control during [its conquests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_conquests "Early Muslim conquests") of the 7th century; Islam also [spread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spread_of_Islam "Spread of Islam") over centuries to the southern regions of India and Southeast Asia through trade along the [Maritime Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Silk_Road "Maritime Silk Road"). The [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire "Mongol Empire") conquered a large part of Asia in the 13th century, an area extending from China to Europe. Before the Mongol invasion, the [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty "Song dynasty") reportedly had approximately 120 million citizens; the 1300 census that followed the invasion reported roughly 60 million people.

The [Black Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Death "Black Death"), one of the most devastating [pandemics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandemic "Pandemic") in human history, is thought to have originated in the arid plains of central Asia, where it then travelled along the [Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road "Silk Road").

### Modern era

European involvement in Asia became more significant from the [Age of Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery "Age of Discovery") onward, with Iberian-sponsored sailors such as [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus "Christopher Columbus") and [Vasco da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_da_Gama "Vasco da Gama") paving the way for new routes from [Atlantic Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Europe "Atlantic Europe") to [Pacific Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Asia "Pacific Asia") and the Indian Ocean, respectively, in the late 15th century. The [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire") also began to expand into northwestern Asia from the 17th century, and would eventually take control of all of Siberia and most of Central Asia by the end of the 19th century.

Among non-European empires, the [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire "Ottoman Empire") controlled Anatolia, most of the Middle East, North Africa and the Balkans from the mid-16th century onward, while in the 17th century, the [Manchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu "Manchu") conquered China and established the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty "Qing dynasty"). The Islamic [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire "Mughal Empire") (preceded by the [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate "Delhi Sultanate") of the 13th to early 16th century) and the Hindu [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire "Maratha Empire") controlled much of India in the 16th and 18th centuries, respectively.

[Western imperialism in Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_imperialism_in_Asia "Western imperialism in Asia") from the 18th to 20th centuries coincided with the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution "Industrial Revolution") in the West and the dethroning of India and China as the world's foremost economies. The [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire") first became dominant in South Asia, with most of the region being [conquered by British traders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_rule_in_India "Company rule in India") in the late 18th and early 19th centuries before falling under [direct British rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj") after a failed [1857 revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1857_revolt "1857 revolt"); the 1869 completion of the [Suez Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Canal "Suez Canal"), which increased British access to India, went on to [further European influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Imperialism "New Imperialism") over Africa and Asia. Around this time, Western powers started to dominate China in what later became known as the [century of humiliation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Century_of_humiliation "Century of humiliation"), with the British-supported [opium trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_opium_in_China#Growth_of_the_opium_trade "History of opium in China") and later [Opium Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium_Wars "Opium Wars") resulting in China being forced into an unprecedented situation of importing more than it exported.

Foreign domination of China was furthered by the [Japanese colonial empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_colonial_empire "Japanese colonial empire"), which controlled some of East Asia and briefly much of Southeast Asia (which had earlier been [taken over](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonisation_of_Southeast_Asia "European colonisation of Southeast Asia") by the British, Dutch and French in the late 19th century), [New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Guinea "New Guinea") and the [Pacific islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_islands "Pacific islands"); Japan's domination was enabled by its rapid rise that had taken place during the [Meiji era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_era "Meiji era") of the late 19th century, in which it applied industrial knowledge learned from the West and thus overtook the rest of Asia. One significant influence on Japan had been the United States, which had begun projecting influence across the Pacific after its early-to-mid-19th century [westward expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westward_expansion "Westward expansion"). The [breakup of the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decline_of_the_Ottoman_Empire "Decline of the Ottoman Empire") in the early 20th century led to the Middle East also being [contested and partitioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Middle_East#Modern_Middle_East "History of the Middle East") by the British and French.

Asia was a major theatre of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). The [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War "Second Sino-Japanese War"), which began in 1937, expanded into the wider [Pacific War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War "Pacific War") after Japan attacked American and British territories in December 1941 and occupied large parts of [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia"). The conflict caused widespread destruction and civilian casualties, including mass killings during the [Japanese occupation of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_China "Japanese occupation of China") and other occupied territories. Japan's surrender in 1945 ended its overseas empire and helped accelerate movements against European colonial rule across Asia.

### Contemporary era

With the end of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") in 1945 and the wartime ruination of Europe and imperial Japan, many countries in Asia were able to rapidly [free themselves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation_of_Asia "Decolonisation of Asia") of colonial rule. The [independence of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_India "Independence of India") came along with the [carving out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India "Partition of India") of a separate nation for the majority of [South Asian Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Muslims "South Asian Muslims"), which in 1971 [further split](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War "Bangladesh Liberation War") into Pakistan and [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh"). The [Cold War in Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War_in_Asia "Cold War in Asia") strained relations between India and Pakistan and affected Asia more generally. The [end of the Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_of_the_Cold_War "End of the Cold War") and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union "Dissolution of the Soviet Union") by 1991 saw the independence of the five modern Central Asian countries.

Postwar Asia was also reshaped by political revolutions and [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War") conflicts. Following the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War "Chinese Civil War"), the [Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Communist_Party "Chinese Communist Party") established the [People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Republic_of_China "People's Republic of China") in 1949, while the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_(1912%E2%80%931949) "Republic of China (1912–1949)") government retreated to [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan"). The [Korean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_War "Korean War") (1950–1953) began when [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea "North Korea") invaded [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea "South Korea"), drawing in a United Nations-led force supporting the South and Chinese forces supporting the North. The war ended with an [armistice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Armistice_Agreement "Korean Armistice Agreement") that left the [Korean Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Peninsula "Korean Peninsula") divided. In [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia"), struggles against colonial rule and Cold War rivalries contributed to prolonged conflicts in [Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indochina "Indochina"). The [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War") ended in 1975 with the [Fall of Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Saigon "Fall of Saigon"), followed by the formal [reunification of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reunification_of_Vietnam "Reunification of Vietnam") in 1976.

Some Arab countries took economic advantage of massive oil deposits that were discovered in their territory, becoming globally influential, though stability in the Middle East has been affected since 1948 by the [Arab–Israeli conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_conflict "Arab–Israeli conflict") and American-led [interventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_foreign_policy_in_the_Middle_East "United States foreign policy in the Middle East"). East Asian nations (along with Singapore in Southeast Asia) became economically prosperous with high-growth "[tiger economies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_economies "Tiger economies")"; The [1997 Asian financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Asian_financial_crisis "1997 Asian financial crisis") began in July 1997 when Thailand abandoned the [Thai baht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_baht "Thai baht")'s peg to the United States dollar. The crisis spread to other economies, including [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia") and [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea "South Korea"), causing severe economic contractions and prompting reforms to financial regulation and exchange-rate policies in affected countries. China, having undergone the [reform and opening up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_and_opening_up "Reform and opening up") under [Deng Xiaoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Xiaoping "Deng Xiaoping"), regained its place among the top two economies of the world by the 21st century. India has also grown significantly because of [economic liberalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalisation_in_India "Economic liberalisation in India") that started in the 1990s, with extreme poverty now below 20%; India and China's rise has coincided with growing tensions between the two, with the [Indo-Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pacific "Indo-Pacific") now an actively contested area between China and counterbalancing forces.

## Geography

Asia is the largest continent on Earth. It covers 9% of the Earth's total surface area (or 30% of its land area), and has the longest coastline, at 62,800 kilometres (39,022 mi). Asia is generally defined as comprising the eastern four-fifths of Eurasia. It is located to the east of the [Suez Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Canal "Suez Canal") and the [Ural Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Mountains "Ural Mountains"), and south of the [Caucasus Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_Mountains "Caucasus Mountains") (or the [Kuma–Manych Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuma%E2%80%93Manych_Depression "Kuma–Manych Depression")) and the [Caspian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_Sea "Caspian Sea") and [Black Seas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea "Black Sea"). It is bounded on the east by the [Pacific Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean "Pacific Ocean"), on the south by the Indian Ocean and on the north by the Arctic Ocean. Asia is subdivided into 49 countries; five of them ([Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_(country) "Georgia (country)"), [Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan "Azerbaijan"), [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia "Russia"), [Kazakhstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakhstan "Kazakhstan") and [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey "Turkey")) are [transcontinental countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcontinental_countries "Transcontinental countries") lying partly in Europe.

Asia's physical landscape includes extensive plains, plateaus, deserts and mountain ranges. The [West Siberian Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Siberian_Plain "West Siberian Plain") occupies a large area of northern Asia, while the [Tibetan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Plateau "Tibetan Plateau") is one of the world's highest and largest plateaus. The [Himalayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayas "Himalayas") extend across parts of [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan"), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India"), [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal "Nepal"), [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan "Bhutan") and [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") and contain [Mount Everest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Everest "Mount Everest"), the world's highest mountain above sea level. The [Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze "Yangtze") in China is Asia's longest river; other major rivers include the [Indus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River "Indus River"), [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges "Ganges"), [Brahmaputra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra_River "Brahmaputra River"), [Mekong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong "Mekong"), [Tigris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigris "Tigris") and [Euphrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates "Euphrates"). [Tropical rainforests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest "Tropical rainforest") stretch across much of southern Asia and coniferous and [deciduous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deciduous "Deciduous") forests lie farther north. Between 1990 and 2000, the area of [primary forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_forest "Primary forest") in Asia decreased by 1.06 million hectares per year; between 2000 and 2015, it decreased by 280,000 ha per year.

### Main regions

There are various approaches to the regional division of Asia. One geographical scheme divides the continent into six regions, including [North Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Asia "North Asia"), which encompasses [Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberia "Siberia"). The [United Nations Statistics Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Statistics_Division "United Nations Statistics Division") (UNSD), by contrast, uses five Asian subregions for statistical purposes: Central Asia, Eastern Asia, South-eastern Asia, Southern Asia and Western Asia. The UNSD does not recognize North Asia as a separate statistical subregion; instead, it classifies Russia, including Siberia, under Eastern Europe. The United Nations states that its regional groupings are for statistical convenience and do not imply any political or other affiliation.

The six geographical regions are:

* [North Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Asia "North Asia") ([Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberia "Siberia"))
* [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia")
* [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia "West Asia") (the [Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East "Middle East") or [Near East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_East "Near East") and part of the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus "Caucasus"))
* [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia") ([Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent"))
* [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia") ([Far East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_East "Far East"))
* [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia") ([East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Indies "East Indies") and [Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indochina "Indochina"))

### Climate

Asia has extremely diverse climate features. Climates range from [Arctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic "Arctic") and [subarctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarctic "Subarctic") in Siberia to tropical in southern India and Southeast Asia. It is moist across southeast sections, and dry across much of the interior. Some of the largest daily temperature ranges on Earth occur in western sections of Asia. The [monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon "Monsoon") circulation dominates across southern and eastern sections, due to the presence of the Himalayas forcing the formation of a thermal low which draws in moisture during the summer. Southwestern sections of the continent are hot. Siberia is one of the coldest places in the [Northern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere "Northern Hemisphere"), and it can act as a source of Arctic air masses for North America. The most active place on Earth for [tropical cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclone "Tropical cyclone") activity lies northeast of the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines") and south of Japan.

#### Climate change

[Climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change "Climate change") is particularly important in Asia, as the continent accounts for the majority of the human population. Warming since the 20th century is increasing the threat of [heatwaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heatwave "Heatwave") across the entire continent. Heatwaves lead to increased mortality, and the demand for [air conditioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_conditioning "Air conditioning") is rapidly accelerating as a result. By 2080, around 1 billion people in the cities of [South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia") and [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia") are expected to experience around a month of extreme heat every year. The [impacts on the water cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change_on_the_water_cycle "Effects of climate change on the water cycle") are more complicated: already arid regions, primarily located in [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia "West Asia") and [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia"), will see more [droughts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drought "Drought"), while areas of [East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia"), Southeast and South Asia which are already wet due to the [monsoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon "Monsoon") will experience more flooding.

The waters around Asia are subjected to the same [impacts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change_on_oceans "Effects of climate change on oceans") as elsewhere, such as increased warming and [ocean acidification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_acidification "Ocean acidification"). There are many [coral reefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_reef "Coral reef") in the region, and they are highly vulnerable to climate change, to the point that practically all of them will be lost if the warming exceeds 1.5 °C (2.7 °F). Asia's distinctive [mangrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove "Mangrove") ecosystems are also highly vulnerable to [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise "Sea level rise"). Asia also has more countries with large coastal populations than any other continent, which would cause large economic impacts from sea level rise. Water supplies in the [Hindu Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush "Hindu Kush") region will become more unstable as its enormous [glaciers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacier "Glacier"), known as the "Asian water towers", gradually melt. These changes to the water cycle also affect vector-borne disease distribution, with malaria and dengue fever expected to become more prominent in the tropical and subtropical regions. [Food security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_security "Food security") will become more uneven, and South Asian countries could experience significant impacts from global food price volatility.

Historical emissions from Asia are lower than those from Europe and North America. However, China has been the single largest emitter of greenhouse gases in the 21st century, while India is the third-largest. As a whole, Asia currently accounts for 36% of the world's primary [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy "Energy") consumption, which is expected to increase to 48% by 2050. By 2040, it is also expected to account for 80% of the world's [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal "Coal") and 26% of the world's [natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas "Natural gas") consumption. While the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") remains the world's largest [oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil "Oil") consumer, by 2050 it is projected to move to third place, behind China and India. While nearly half of the world's new [renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy "Renewable energy") capacity is built in Asia, this is not yet sufficient in order to meet the goals of the [Paris Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Agreement "Paris Agreement"). They imply that the renewables would account for 35% of total energy consumption in Asia by 2030.

[Climate change adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_adaptation "Climate change adaptation") is already a reality for many Asian countries, with a wide range of strategies attempted across the continent. Important examples include the growing implementation of [climate-smart agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate-smart_agriculture "Climate-smart agriculture") in certain countries or the "[sponge city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sponge_city "Sponge city")" planning principles in China. While some countries have drawn up extensive frameworks such as the Bangladesh Delta Plan or Japan's Climate Adaptation Act, others still rely on localized actions that are not effectively scaled up.

## Politics

The most [democratic countries in Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_in_Asia "Democracy in Asia") are [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan"), [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan") and [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea "South Korea") according to the [V-Dem Democracy indices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-Dem_Democracy_indices "V-Dem Democracy indices").

Political systems across Asia include [parliamentary republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_republic "Parliamentary republic"), [presidential republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_republic "Presidential republic"), [constitutional monarchies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy "Constitutional monarchy"), [absolute monarchies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_monarchy "Absolute monarchy"), [one-party states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-party_state "One-party state") and military-led governments. Countries differ substantially in their systems of government, levels of electoral competition and protection of political rights and civil liberties.

Regional organisations provide forums for political, economic and security cooperation among Asian countries. The [Association of Southeast Asian Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Southeast_Asian_Nations "Association of Southeast Asian Nations") (ASEAN) promotes political and security cooperation in Southeast Asia, while the [South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Association_for_Regional_Cooperation "South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation") (SAARC) supports economic, social and cultural cooperation in South Asia. The [Shanghai Cooperation Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_Cooperation_Organisation "Shanghai Cooperation Organisation") (SCO) facilitates cooperation on regional security, political and economic matters across Eurasia, and the [Gulf Cooperation Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Cooperation_Council "Gulf Cooperation Council") (GCC) coordinates policies and promotes integration among six Arab states of the Persian Gulf.

## Economy

Asia is both the [largest continental economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_continents_by_GDP "List of continents by GDP") in the world by both [GDP nominal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(nominal) "List of countries by GDP (nominal)") and [PPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity "Purchasing power parity") values and the world's fastest-growing economic region. As of 2026[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Asia&action=edit), [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") is by far the [largest economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Asian_countries_by_GDP "List of Asian countries by GDP") on the continent. Other major Asian economies include [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India"), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan"), [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia"), [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea "South Korea"), [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey "Turkey"), [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia "Saudi Arabia") and [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan").

In the late 1990s and early 2000s, the [economy of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_China "Economy of China") had an average annual growth rate of more than 8%. According to economic historian [Angus Maddison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angus_Maddison "Angus Maddison"), India had the world's largest economy for much of the past three millennia prior to the 19th century, accounting for 25% of the world's industrial output. China was the [largest and most advanced economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_history_of_China "Economic history of China") on Earth for much of recorded history and shared the mantle with India. For several decades in the late twentieth century, Japan was the largest economy in Asia and second-largest in the world, after surpassing the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") (measured in net material product) in 1990 and Germany in 1968. This ended in 2010, when China overtook Japan to become the world's second-largest economy. In 2025, India overtook Japan in terms of nominal GDP to become the [world's 4th largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(nominal) "List of countries by GDP (nominal)") and [Asia's 2nd largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Asian_countries_by_GDP "List of Asian countries by GDP") economy.

In the late 1980s and early 1990s, Japan's GDP by currency exchange rates was almost as large as that of the rest of Asia combined. In 1995, Japan's economy nearly equaled that of the US as the largest economy in the world for a day, after the Japanese currency reached a record high of 79 [yen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yen "Yen")/US$. Economic growth in Asia from [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") to the 1990s had been concentrated in Japan as well as South Korea, Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Singapore, known as the [Four Asian Tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Asian_Tigers "Four Asian Tigers"), which are now all considered developed economies, having among the highest GDP per capita in Asia.[*[failed verification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability "Wikipedia:Verifiability")*]

Asia is the largest continent in the world by a considerable margin, and it is rich in natural resources, such as [petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum "Petroleum"), [forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest "Forest"), [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish "Fish"), [water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water "Water"), [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice "Rice"), [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper "Copper") and [silver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver "Silver"). Manufacturing in Asia has traditionally been strongest in East and Southeast Asia, particularly in China, [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan"), South Korea, Japan, India, the Philippines, and Singapore. Japan and South Korea continue to dominate in the area of [multinational corporations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_corporation "Multinational corporation"), though increasingly China and India are making significant inroads. Many companies from Europe, North America, South Korea, and Japan have operations in Asia's developing countries to take advantage of their abundant supply of cheap labour and relatively developed infrastructure.

[Call centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_center "Call center") and [business process outsourcing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_process_outsourcing "Business process outsourcing") firms (BPOs) are becoming major employers in India and the Philippines due to the availability of a large pool of highly skilled, English-speaking workers. Due to its large and extremely competitive information technology industry, India has become a major hub for outsourcing.

Trade between Asian countries and countries on other continents is largely carried out via sea routes. The main route leads from the Chinese coast south via Hanoi to Jakarta, Singapore, and Kuala Lumpur, through the [Strait of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca "Strait of Malacca") via [Colombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo "Colombo") to the southern tip of India, then via Malé to [Mombasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa "Mombasa") in East Africa (see also [Indo-Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pacific "Indo-Pacific")), from there to [Djibouti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djibouti "Djibouti"), then through the Red Sea and the [Suez Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Canal "Suez Canal") into the Mediterranean (see also: [Indo-Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Mediterranean "Indo-Mediterranean")), then via Haifa, Istanbul, and [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens "Athens") to the northern Italian hub of [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste "Trieste"), with its rail connections to Central and Eastern Europe, or further to [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona "Barcelona") and around Spain and France to northern European ports. A far smaller part of the goods traffic runs via [South Africa to Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Route "Cape Route"). A particularly significant part of Asian goods traffic is carried out on the [Pacific Rim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Rim "Pacific Rim"), toward [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles") and [Long Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Beach "Long Beach"). The melting of the Arctic is also paving the way for [new shipping routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_shipping_routes "Arctic shipping routes") from Northeast Asia to Europe and North America. The land route to Europe is also the subject of construction projects, comparatively smaller in scope. Intra-Asian trade, including sea trade, is growing rapidly.

### Energy

Asia's electricity demand has quadrupled since 2000 (rising from 4,199 TWh to over 16,000 TWh in 2024). This growth, driven largely by China and increasingly by India, Indonesia, and Viet Nam, makes Asia the fastest-growing region for electricity demand globally, at about 5% per year. The region now accounts for about 36% of global [primary energy consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_energy_supply_and_consumption "World energy supply and consumption"), a share projected to approach 50% by 2050.: 1468 [Fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel "Fossil fuel") remain central in Asia: 66% of its energy is generated from fossil fuels, and Asia makes up 83% of the world's coal generation. However, coal is slowly being phased out, and [natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas "Natural gas") is displacing oil and oil products in various sectors, supported by policies and regulations. Natural gas demand in emerging market and developing economies in Asia expanded by around 6% in 2024, accounting for nearly 40% of incremental global gas demand. By 2040, Asia is expected to account for the majority of global coal use and over a quarter of natural gas consumption.: 1468

At the same time, Asia is the global leader in [renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy "Renewable energy") expansion. It is currently the only region on track to meet the goal of tripling renewable capacity by 2030, largely due to China, the leading nation in renewable energy generation. China's wind and solar capacity has surged in recent years, together overtaking that of coal, while in 2024 India became the world's third-largest generator of electricity from wind and solar. Nearly half of the world's new renewable energy capacity is built in Asia.: 1470

### Tourism

Tourism in Asia encompasses travel to cultural and religious sites, major cities, coastal destinations and natural landscapes. Domestic tourism also contributes to the economies of many Asian countries, alongside international travel.
According to [UN Tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Tourism "UN Tourism"), its *Asia and the Pacific* region recorded approximately 331 million international tourist arrivals in 2025, an increase of 6% from 2024, although arrivals remained 9% below their 2019 level. Northeast Asia recorded particularly strong growth, with arrivals increasing by 13% from 2024. UN Tourism's *Asia and the Pacific* region includes Oceania and does not cover all of geographical Asia; its statistics therefore should not be interpreted as totals for the Asian continent.

Demographic trends vary considerably across Asia. Population ageing and declining [fertility rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertility_rate "Fertility rate") are particularly prominent in parts of [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia") and [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia"), although demographic conditions differ substantially between countries. Levels of [human development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_development_(economics) "Human development (economics)") also vary across the continent. The [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index "Human Development Index") (HDI), published by the [United Nations Development Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Development_Programme "United Nations Development Programme"), measures countries' achievements in life expectancy, education and gross national income per capita.

### Languages

Asia has considerable [linguistic diversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_diversity "Linguistic diversity"), with languages belonging to numerous distinct [language families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_family "Language family"). Major families include [Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_languages "Indo-European languages"), represented by languages such as [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi "Hindi"), [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language "Bengali language"), [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language "Persian language") and [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"), and [Sino-Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Tibetan_languages "Sino-Tibetan languages"), which includes [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language "Chinese language"), [Tibetic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetic_languages "Tibetic languages") and [Burmese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_language "Burmese language"). The [Dravidian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravidian_languages "Dravidian languages"), including [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language "Tamil language"), [Telugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_language "Telugu language"), [Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada "Kannada") and [Malayalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam "Malayalam"), are concentrated in southern India and neighbouring regions. [Turkic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_languages "Turkic languages"), such as [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language "Turkish language"), [Uzbek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbek_language "Uzbek language") and [Kazakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakh_language "Kazakh language"), are spoken across much of Central Asia and parts of Western and Northern Asia. [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic "Arabic") and [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language "Hebrew language") belong to the [Semitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitic_languages "Semitic languages") branch of the [Afroasiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afroasiatic_languages "Afroasiatic languages") family.

[Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia") contains several major language families, including [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_languages "Austronesian languages"), represented by [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language "Indonesian language"), [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language "Malay language"), [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_language "Javanese language") and [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language "Tagalog language"); [Austroasiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austroasiatic_languages "Austroasiatic languages"), which includes [Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_language "Vietnamese language") and [Khmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_language "Khmer language"); and [Kra–Dai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kra%E2%80%93Dai_languages "Kra–Dai languages"), which includes [Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language "Thai language") and [Lao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lao_language "Lao language"). [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language "Japanese language") and [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language "Korean language") belong to the separate [Japonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japonic_languages "Japonic languages") and [Koreanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koreanic_languages "Koreanic languages") families, respectively.

Linguistic diversity also exists within individual countries. Indonesia has more than 700 living languages, while India has more than 400, according to figures compiled by *Ethnologue* and reported in a UNESCO regional review.

### Religions

Many of the world's [major religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religions "Major religions") have their origins in Asia, including the five most practiced in the world (excluding [irreligion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion "Irreligion")), which are Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, Chinese folk religion (classified as Confucianism and Taoism), and Buddhism. Asian mythology is complex and diverse. Flood narratives appear in several Asian religious traditions. In ancient [Mesopotamian mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamian_mythology "Mesopotamian mythology"), they are found in the [Atrahasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atra-Hasis "Atra-Hasis") epic and the [Epic of Gilgamesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_of_Gilgamesh "Epic of Gilgamesh"), which share significant similarities with the biblical account of [Noah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah "Noah") in the [Book of Genesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Genesis "Book of Genesis"). The [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran "Quran") also recounts the story of Noah and the flood. [Hindu mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_mythology "Hindu mythology") similarly tells about an [avatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avatar "Avatar") of [Vishnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnu "Vishnu") in the form of a [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsya "Matsya") who warned [Manu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sraddhadeva_Manu "Sraddhadeva Manu") of a terrible flood. Ancient [Chinese mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_mythology "Chinese mythology") also tells of a [Great Flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Flood_(China) "Great Flood (China)") spanning generations, one that required the combined efforts of emperors and divinities to control.

#### Abrahamic

The [Abrahamic religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrahamic_religions "Abrahamic religions") – including [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism "Judaism"), [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity"), [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam"), [Druze faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze_faith "Druze faith"), and [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith "Baháʼí Faith") – originated in West Asia.

[Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism "Judaism"), the oldest of the Abrahamic faiths, is practiced primarily in [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel"), the indigenous homeland and historical birthplace of the [Hebrew nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews"); this today consists both of those Jews who remained in [the Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizrahi_Jews "Mizrahi Jews") and those who returned from the [diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_diaspora "Jewish diaspora") in Europe, North America, [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia "Ethiopia"), India, and other regions, though various Israelite diaspora communities still exist worldwide. Jews are the predominant ethnic group in Israel (75.6%), numbering about 6.1 million. Levels of Jewish religious observance vary within the Jewish State. Outside of Israel, there are small ancient Jewish communities in [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey "Turkey") (17,400), [Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan "Azerbaijan") (9,100), Iran (8,756), India (5,000) and [Uzbekistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbekistan "Uzbekistan") (4,000), among many other places.

[Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity") is practiced throughout Asia, with substantial Christian communities in the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines"), [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China"), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") and [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea "South Korea"). In the Philippines and [Timor-Leste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timor-Leste "Timor-Leste"), [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism "Catholicism") is the predominant religion; it was introduced by the Spaniards and the Portuguese, respectively. In [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia "Armenia") and Georgia, [Eastern Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy "Eastern Orthodoxy") is the predominant religion. In the Middle East, such as in the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant "Levant"), [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia "Anatolia") and [Fars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fars_(territory) "Fars (territory)"), [Syriac Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Christianity "Syriac Christianity") ([Church of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_East "Church of the East")) and [Oriental Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Orthodoxy "Oriental Orthodoxy") are prevalent minority denominations, which are both [Eastern Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Christian "Eastern Christian") sects mainly adhered to by [Assyrian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people "Assyrian people") or Syriac Christians. Vibrant indigenous minorities in [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia "West Asia") adhere to the [Eastern Catholic Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_Churches "Eastern Catholic Churches") and Eastern Orthodoxy. [Saint Thomas Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Thomas_Christians "Saint Thomas Christians") in India trace their origins to the evangelistic activity of [Thomas the Apostle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_the_Apostle "Thomas the Apostle") in the 1st century. Significant Christian communities are also found in [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia"), [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia"), [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia") and [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia").

[Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam") originated in the [Hejaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz "Hejaz") region of the [Arabian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Peninsula "Arabian Peninsula") in the 7th century and is widely practiced across [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia "West Asia"), [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia"), [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia") and [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia"). According to estimates published by the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center "Pew Research Center") in 2025, [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia") had approximately 240 million Muslims in 2020, the largest Muslim population of any country. Indonesia, [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan") and [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") together accounted for approximately one-third of the world's Muslim population. [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca "Mecca"), [Medina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medina "Medina") and [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holiest_sites_in_Islam#Jerusalem "Holiest sites in Islam") are the three holiest cities for Islam in the world. The [Hajj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajj "Hajj") and [Umrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umrah "Umrah") attract large numbers of Muslim devotees from all over the world to Mecca and Medina. Iran is the largest [Shia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia "Shia") country.

The [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze "Druze") originated in West Asia, is a monotheistic religion based on the teachings of figures like [Hamza ibn Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamza_ibn_Ali "Hamza ibn Ali") and [al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Hakim_bi-Amr_Allah "Al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah"), and Greek [philosophers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophers "Philosophers") such as [Plato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plato "Plato") and [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle "Aristotle"). The number of Druze people worldwide is around one million. Around 45–50% live in [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria "Syria"), 35% to 40% live in [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon "Lebanon"), and less than 10% live in [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel"). Recently, there has been a growing Druze diaspora.

The [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith "Baháʼí Faith") originated in Asia, in Iran (Persia), and spread from there to the Ottoman Empire, Central Asia, India, and Burma during the lifetime of [Bahá'u'lláh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%27u%27ll%C3%A1h "Bahá'u'lláh"). Since the middle of the 20th century, growth has particularly occurred in other Asian countries, because Baháʼí activities in many Muslim countries have been [severely suppressed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%ADs "Persecution of Baháʼís") by authorities. [Lotus Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotus_Temple "Lotus Temple") is a big [Baháʼí temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_temple "Baháʼí temple") in India.

#### Indian and East Asian religions

Almost all Asian religions have a philosophical character, and Asian philosophical traditions cover a large spectrum of philosophical thoughts and writings. [Indian philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_philosophy "Indian philosophy") includes [Hindu philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_philosophy "Hindu philosophy") and [Buddhist philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_philosophy "Buddhist philosophy"). They include elements of nonmaterial pursuits, whereas another school of thought from India, [Cārvāka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C4%81rv%C4%81ka "Cārvāka"), preached the enjoyment of the material world. The religions of [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism"), [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"), [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism "Jainism") and [Sikhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism "Sikhism") originated in India, South Asia. In East Asia, particularly in China and Japan, [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism "Confucianism"), [Taoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism "Taoism") and [Zen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen "Zen") Buddhism took shape.

According to estimates published by the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center "Pew Research Center") in 2025, approximately 1.2 billion people worldwide identified as [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism") in 2020, with about 95% living in [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India"). Hinduism is concentrated primarily in [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia"), where it is the majority religion in India and [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal "Nepal"). Hindus accounted for approximately 79% of India's population and 81% of Nepal's population in 2020. Hindu communities are also present in [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh"), [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan"), [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka"), [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan "Bhutan"), and parts of [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia"), including [Bali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bali "Bali"), Indonesia.

[Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism "Jainism") is found mainly in India and in overseas Indian communities such as the United States and Malaysia. [Sikhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism "Sikhism") is found in Northern India and amongst overseas Indian communities in other parts of Asia, especially Southeast Asia. [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism "Confucianism") is found predominantly in mainland China, South Korea, Taiwan and in overseas Chinese populations. [Taoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism "Taoism") is found mainly in mainland China, Taiwan, Malaysia and Singapore. In many Chinese communities, Taoism is easily syncretised with [Mahayana Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahayana_Buddhism "Mahayana Buddhism"), thus exact religious statistics are difficult to obtain and may be understated or overstated.

## Culture

The [culture of Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Asia "Culture of Asia") is a diverse blend of customs and traditions that have been practiced by the various ethnic groups of the continent for centuries. The continent is divided into six geographic sub-regions: [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia"), [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_cultural_sphere "East Asian cultural sphere"), [North Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Asia "North Asia"), [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_ethnic_groups "South Asian ethnic groups"), [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia"), and [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia "West Asia"). These regions are defined by their cultural similarities, including common religions, languages, and ethnicities. West Asia, also known as Southwest Asia or the [Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East "Middle East"), has cultural roots in the ancient civilisations of the [Fertile Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertile_Crescent "Fertile Crescent") and [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia "Mesopotamia"), which gave rise to the [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran"), [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world "Arab world"), and [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire "Ottoman Empire") empires, as well as the Abrahamic religions of Judaism, Christianity and Islam. These civilisations, which are located in the [Hilly flanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilly_flanks "Hilly flanks"), are among the oldest in the world, with evidence of farming dating back to around 9000 BCE. Despite the challenges posed by the vast size of the continent and the presence of natural barriers such as deserts and mountain ranges, trade and commerce have helped to create a [Pan-Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Asian "Pan-Asian") culture that is shared across the region.

Asian societies have developed diverse traditions of [literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature "Literature"), [visual arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_arts "Visual arts"), [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music "Music"), [theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre "Theatre") and [storytelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storytelling "Storytelling"). Examples of practices recognised by [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO") as [intangible cultural heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intangible_cultural_heritage "Intangible cultural heritage") include [Chinese calligraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_calligraphy "Chinese calligraphy"), [Arabic calligraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_calligraphy "Arabic calligraphy"), [Kutiyattam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutiyattam "Kutiyattam"), a Sanskrit theatre tradition practiced in [Kerala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala "Kerala"), India, and [Wayang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayang "Wayang"), a form of puppet theatre originating in [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java "Java"), Indonesia.

Cultural traditions have also been shaped by exchanges between different parts of Asia and neighbouring regions. The overland and maritime [Silk Roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Roads "Silk Roads") facilitated the transmission of artistic practices, literature, religious beliefs and technologies between societies. Asian literary traditions have also gained international recognition in the modern era; the Bengali poet and writer [Rabindranath Tagore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabindranath_Tagore "Rabindranath Tagore") received the [1913 Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1913_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "1913 Nobel Prize in Literature").
