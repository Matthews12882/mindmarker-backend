# Anne Hathaway

**Anne Jacqueline Hathaway** (born November 12, 1982) is an American actress. [Her work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Anne_Hathaway_performances "List of Anne Hathaway performances") encompasses both screen and stage, and [her accolades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Anne_Hathaway "List of awards and nominations received by Anne Hathaway") include an [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award "Academy Award"), a [British Academy Film Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Award "British Academy Film Award"), a [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award "Golden Globe Award"), and a [Primetime Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award "Primetime Emmy Award"). Her films have grossed more than $6.7 billion worldwide.

As a teenager, Hathaway was cast in the television series *[Get Real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Get_Real_(U.S._TV_series) "Get Real (U.S. TV series)")* (1999–2000). Her breakthrough came when she starred as [Mia Thermopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mia_Thermopolis "Mia Thermopolis") in her debut film, the [Disney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney "Disney") comedy *[The Princess Diaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Princess_Diaries_(film) "The Princess Diaries (film)")* (2001). In 2005, she played more mature roles in the dramas *[Havoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havoc_(2005_film) "Havoc (2005 film)")* and *[Brokeback Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brokeback_Mountain "Brokeback Mountain")*. The comedy *[The Devil Wears Prada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Devil_Wears_Prada_(film) "The Devil Wears Prada (film)")* (2006), in which Hathaway played an assistant to a fashion magazine editor, was her biggest commercial success to that point. She played a recovering alcoholic in the drama *[Rachel Getting Married](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Getting_Married "Rachel Getting Married")* (2008), which earned her a nomination for the [Academy Award for Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress "Academy Award for Best Actress"). She subsequently appeared in the commercially successful romantic films *[Bride Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bride_Wars "Bride Wars")* (2009), *[Valentine's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentine%27s_Day_(2010_film) "Valentine's Day (2010 film)")* (2010) and *[Love & Other Drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_%26_Other_Drugs "Love & Other Drugs")* (2010).

In 2012, Hathaway starred as [Selina Kyle / Catwoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catwoman "Catwoman") in *[The Dark Knight Rises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dark_Knight_Rises "The Dark Knight Rises")*, her first of three collaborations with director [Christopher Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan "Christopher Nolan"). That year, she also played [Fantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantine "Fantine"), a [prostitute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostitution "Prostitution") dying of [tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculosis "Tuberculosis"), in the musical romantic drama *[Les Misérables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Mis%C3%A9rables_(2012_film) "Les Misérables (2012 film)")*, for which she earned multiple accolades, including an [Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress "Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress"). Hathaway went on to play a scientist in *[Interstellar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstellar_(film) "Interstellar (film)")* (2014), the owner of an online fashion site in *[The Intern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Intern_(2015_film) "The Intern (2015 film)")* (2015), a haughty actress in *[Ocean's 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean%27s_8 "Ocean's 8")* (2018), a single mother in *[The Idea of You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Idea_of_You "The Idea of You")* (2024), a journalist in *[The Devil Wears Prada 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Devil_Wears_Prada_2 "The Devil Wears Prada 2")* (2026) and [Penelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penelope "Penelope") in *[The Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Odyssey_(2026_film) "The Odyssey (2026 film)")* (2026).

In television, Hathaway won a [Primetime Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Voice-Over_Performance "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Voice-Over Performance") for her guest voice role on *[The Simpsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simpsons "The Simpsons")* in 2010, and starred in the miniseries *[WeCrashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WeCrashed "WeCrashed")* (2022). She has sung for soundtracks and performed on the New York stage, and she advocates for [gender equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_equality "Gender equality") as a [UN Women Goodwill Ambassador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Women_Goodwill_Ambassador "UN Women Goodwill Ambassador"). Hathaway is married to Adam Shulman, with whom she has two children.

## Early life and background

Anne Jacqueline Hathaway was born on November 12, 1982, in the [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn "Brooklyn") borough of New York City. She is of Irish, English, German and French descent. Her father, Gerald, was a labor attorney, and her mother, Kate (née McCauley), is a former actress. Hathaway's maternal grandfather was the [WIP (AM)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTEL_(AM) "WTEL (AM)") Philadelphia radio personality Joe McCauley. According to *[The Daily Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph "The Daily Telegraph")*, Hathaway was named after [William Shakespeare's wife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hathaway_(wife_of_Shakespeare) "Anne Hathaway (wife of Shakespeare)"). She has an older brother, Michael, and a younger brother, Thomas. When Hathaway was six years old, the family moved to [Millburn, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millburn,_New_Jersey "Millburn, New Jersey"), where she was raised.

Hathaway became fascinated with the stage at age eight, when she watched her mother portray the prostitute [Fantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantine "Fantine") in the first national tour of *[Les Misérables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Mis%C3%A9rables_(musical) "Les Misérables (musical)")*. Hathaway's parents were not keen on allowing her to pursue an acting career, and her mother eventually quit acting to raise Hathaway and her brothers. Hathaway was raised as [Roman Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church "Catholic Church") with what she considers to be "really strong values" and wished to be a nun during her childhood, but acting was always a high priority for her. Her relationship with the Catholic Church changed at age 15 after she learned her older brother was gay. Her family left the church, joining the [Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Church_(United_States) "Episcopal Church (United States)") because of its acceptance of homosexuality, but they eventually left that church too. In 2009, Hathaway described her religious beliefs as "a work in progress".

Hathaway attended Brooklyn Heights [Montessori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montessori "Montessori") School and Wyoming Elementary School in Millburn. She studied at the [American Academy of Dramatic Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Dramatic_Arts "American Academy of Dramatic Arts") in 1993 and became the first teenager admitted into [the Barrow Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Barrow_Group "The Barrow Group") Theater Company's acting program. She attended [Millburn High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millburn_High_School "Millburn High School"), where she played soccer and took part in many plays, including *[Once Upon a Mattress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Once_Upon_a_Mattress "Once Upon a Mattress")*, in which she portrayed Winnifred. She also appeared in the plays *[Jane Eyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Eyre "Jane Eyre")* and *[Gigi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigi_(play) "Gigi (play)")* at New Jersey's [Paper Mill Playhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper_Mill_Playhouse "Paper Mill Playhouse"). Between 1998 and 1999, Hathaway sang [soprano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soprano "Soprano") with the All-Eastern U.S. High School Honors Chorus at [Carnegie Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Hall "Carnegie Hall") and acted in plays at [Seton Hall Preparatory School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seton_Hall_Preparatory_School "Seton Hall Preparatory School") in [West Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Orange,_New_Jersey "West Orange, New Jersey"), New Jersey. Three days after a chorus performance at Carnegie Hall, Hathaway was cast as Meghan Green in the short-lived [Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company "Fox Broadcasting Company") television series *[Get Real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Get_Real_(American_TV_series) "Get Real (American TV series)")*, alongside [Jon Tenney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Tenney "Jon Tenney"), [Debrah Farentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debrah_Farentino "Debrah Farentino") and [Jesse Eisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Eisenberg "Jesse Eisenberg"). Hathaway suffered from depression and anxiety as a teenager; in 2008, she stated she had "grown from" it.

Hathaway spent several semesters as an English major and [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science "Political science") minor at [Vassar College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassar_College "Vassar College") in [Poughkeepsie, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poughkeepsie_(town),_New_York "Poughkeepsie (town), New York"); she missed her first semester due to the production schedule of her first film, *[The Princess Diaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Princess_Diaries_(film) "The Princess Diaries (film)")* (2001). She later transferred to [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University "New York University")'s [Gallatin School of Individualized Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallatin_School_of_Individualized_Study "Gallatin School of Individualized Study"). Hathaway said she never regretted not completing her degree, as she enjoyed being with others who "were trying to grow up". Early in Hathaway's film career, her acting style and appearance were likened to [Judy Garland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judy_Garland "Judy Garland")—whom she cites as one of her favorite actresses—and [Audrey Hepburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audrey_Hepburn "Audrey Hepburn").

## Career

### 2001–2004: Early roles and breakthrough

In 2001, Hathaway starred in the [Disney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Pictures "Walt Disney Pictures") comedy *[The Princess Diaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Princess_Diaries_(film) "The Princess Diaries (film)")*, based on [Meg Cabot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meg_Cabot "Meg Cabot")'s novel *[The Princess Diaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Princess_Diaries "The Princess Diaries")*. She portrayed the teenager [Mia Thermopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mia_Thermopolis "Mia Thermopolis"), who discovers that she is the heiress to the throne of the fictional Kingdom of Genovia. Hathaway auditioned for the role during a flight layover on the way to New Zealand. Director [Garry Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Marshall "Garry Marshall") initially considered [Liv Tyler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liv_Tyler "Liv Tyler") for the role, but cast Hathaway after his granddaughters suggested that she had the best "princess" hair. The film received mixed reviews from critics but became a major commercial success,[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] grossing $165 million worldwide. Ben Falk of the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") praised Hathaway's chemistry with her co-star [Julie Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julie_Andrews "Julie Andrews"), while [Elvis Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_Mitchell "Elvis Mitchell") of *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* lauded her comic talent and called her "royalty in the making". For her performance, Hathaway was nominated for the [MTV Movie Award for Best Breakthrough Female Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Movie_Award_for_Best_Breakthrough_Female_Performance "MTV Movie Award for Best Breakthrough Female Performance"). The same year, she starred with [Christopher Gorham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Gorham "Christopher Gorham") in [Mitch Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitch_Davis "Mitch Davis")'s adventure drama *[The Other Side of Heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Other_Side_of_Heaven "The Other Side of Heaven")*, which was also distributed by Disney. Inspired by [John H. Groberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_H._Groberg "John H. Groberg")'s memoir *In the Eye of the Storm*, the film met with mostly negative reviews and was a box-office failure.

> In terms of the princess role, there is only so long that you can play those as a young lady before you start feeling really ridiculous. They are so much fun to do, I figure I might as well get the most out of them while I can. Then [I'll] go off and play all the drug addicts and the prostitutes, and all the good ones you win Oscars for a little bit later on.

— Hathaway, 2004

Due to the success of *The Princess Diaries*, *[People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_(magazine) "People (magazine)")* magazine named Hathaway one of its breakthrough stars of 2001. In 2002, Hathaway starred in the City Center Encores! concert production of *[Carnival!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival! "Carnival!")* in her New York City stage debut. She played Lili, an optimistic orphan who falls in love with a magician. She memorized almost all her lines and songs at the first read-through. Critics generally praised her for holding her own against well-known actors and heralded her as a new star. In a review of the musical, [Charles Isherwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Isherwood "Charles Isherwood") of *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* praised Hathaway's convincing performance, calling her the highlight of the show and "remarkably unaffected and winning". Hathaway won a [Clarence Derwent Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarence_Derwent_Award "Clarence Derwent Award") for Most Promising Female. Later, she voiced the audiobook release of the first three books in *The Princess Diaries* novel series.

Over the next three years, Hathaway portrayed princesses and appeared in family-oriented films, subsequently becoming known in mainstream media as a children's role model. After voicing Haru Yoshioka for the English version of *[The Cat Returns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cat_Returns "The Cat Returns")* (2002), she starred in [Douglas McGrath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_McGrath "Douglas McGrath")'s comedy-drama *[Nicholas Nickleby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Nickleby_(2002_film) "Nicholas Nickleby (2002 film)")* (2002), which opened to generally positive reviews. However, the film did not enter wide release and failed at the North American box office, grossing less than $4 million in ticket sales. Hathaway next starred as Ella in the fantasy romantic comedy *[Ella Enchanted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ella_Enchanted_(film) "Ella Enchanted (film)")* (2004), which was based on the novel *[Ella Enchanted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ella_Enchanted "Ella Enchanted")*. Hathaway had read the book when she was 16, and said the initial version of the script was much closer to the source material but did not work as a film. *Ella Enchanted* opened to mixed reviews and performed poorly at the box office, although many critics found Hathaway "charming" in her role, according to the review aggregator [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes"). Hathaway sang three songs on the [film's soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ella_Enchanted_(soundtrack) "Ella Enchanted (soundtrack)"), including a duet with singer [Jesse McCartney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_McCartney "Jesse McCartney").

In 2003, Hathaway turned down the role of Christine Daaé in [Joel Schumacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Schumacher "Joel Schumacher")'s *[The Phantom of the Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Phantom_of_the_Opera_(2004_film) "The Phantom of the Opera (2004 film)")* (2004), because the production schedule of the film overlapped with that of *[The Princess Diaries 2: Royal Engagement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Princess_Diaries_2:_Royal_Engagement "The Princess Diaries 2: Royal Engagement")* (2004). When Hathaway expressed hesitation about starring in the *Princess Diaries* sequel, director Gary Marshall encouraged her by saying that the film would bring happiness to young girls around the world. The film earned mostly negative reviews but grossed $134.7 million against a $45 million budget.

### 2005–2008: Rise to prominence

Hathaway began taking on more mature roles to avoid [typecasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typecasting "Typecasting"). She also said she needed a reprieve from being a role model for children.[*[new archival link needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Archiving_a_source "Help:Archiving a source")*] After replacing [Tara Strong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tara_Strong "Tara Strong") for the voice role of [Red Puckett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Red_Riding_Hood "Little Red Riding Hood") in *[Hoodwinked!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoodwinked! "Hoodwinked!")*, she starred in the drama *[Havoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havoc_(2005_film) "Havoc (2005 film)")* (2005) as a spoiled socialite, appearing partially nude in some scenes. Hathaway denied that taking the role was an overt attempt to be seen as a more mature actress. The film was not released in theaters in the United States due to unfavorable critical reception.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

In the 2005 drama *[Brokeback Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brokeback_Mountain "Brokeback Mountain")*, which depicts the emotional and sexual relationship between two men, [Ennis Del Mar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ennis_Del_Mar "Ennis Del Mar") ([Heath Ledger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heath_Ledger "Heath Ledger")) and [Jack Twist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Twist "Jack Twist") ([Jake Gyllenhaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Gyllenhaal "Jake Gyllenhaal")), Hathaway played Lureen, the wife of Jack. She had been sent the script with the part of Ennis' wife in mind, but decided to audition for Lureen instead. She lied during the audition about her knowledge of horseback riding so that director [Ang Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ang_Lee "Ang Lee") would cast her, but she did subsequently take lessons. The film received critical acclaim and several [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award "Academy Award") nominations. [Peter Travers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Travers "Peter Travers") of *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")* wrote that Hathaway "excels at showing Lureen's journey from cutie-pie to hard case", while [Todd McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_McCarthy "Todd McCarthy") of *Variety* credited her for providing "an entertaining contrast in wifely disappointment". Hathaway stated that the content of *Brokeback Mountain* was more important than its award count, and that making the film made her more aware of the kind of stories she wanted to tell as an actor. At this point, she realized she wanted to play roles that would move audiences or entertain them so thoroughly that they would forget about their own lives.

Hathaway starred in *[The Devil Wears Prada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Devil_Wears_Prada_(film) "The Devil Wears Prada (film)")* (2006), based on [Lauren Weisberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauren_Weisberger "Lauren Weisberger")'s [2003 novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Devil_Wears_Prada_(novel) "The Devil Wears Prada (novel)"). Her character is a recent college graduate who becomes an assistant to a powerful [fashion magazine editor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miranda_Priestly "Miranda Priestly") ([Meryl Streep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meryl_Streep "Meryl Streep")). Hathaway said she was "the ninth choice" for the role, which she prepared for by volunteering as an assistant at an [auction house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auction_house "Auction house"). She also followed a weight-loss regimen which led to crying breakdowns. She stated that working on the film made her respect the fashion industry a great deal more than she did previously. *The Devil Wears Prada* received generally positive reviews; [Roger Ebert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ebert "Roger Ebert") said Hathaway "makes a convincing career girl" while the critical consensus on [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes") found "Streep in top form and Anne Hathaway more than holding her own". The film became Hathaway's most popular film at the time, with a worldwide gross of over $326.5 million.

Hathaway was cast as the female lead in the comedy *[Knocked Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knocked_Up "Knocked Up")* (2007), but dropped out because she did not agree with director Judd Apatow's decision to use real footage of a woman giving birth, according to Apatow and the film's male star [Seth Rogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Rogen "Seth Rogen"). In 2007, Hathaway portrayed the English author [Jane Austen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Austen "Jane Austen") in the biographical romantic drama *[Becoming Jane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Becoming_Jane "Becoming Jane")*. A fan of Austen since the age of 14, Hathaway prepared for the role by learning [sign language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sign_language "Sign language"), [calligraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calligraphy "Calligraphy"), dance choreography and piano, and by examining Austen's letters. She moved to England a month before filming to improve her English accent. She believed that if she did not perfect her accent, people would dismiss her performance in the first five minutes of the film. She was nominated for a [British Independent Film Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Independent_Film_Award "British Independent Film Award") for Best Actress for the film.

In 2008, Hathaway starred as [Agent 99](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent_99 "Agent 99") in [Peter Segal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Segal "Peter Segal")'s [film adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Get_Smart_(film) "Get Smart (film)") of [Mel Brooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mel_Brooks "Mel Brooks")' television series *[Get Smart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Get_Smart "Get Smart")*. Calling the role "a childhood dream come true", Hathaway learned martial arts and dancing techniques in preparation for her performance. While filming an action sequence, she suffered a shin injury that required 15 stitches. *Get Smart* was a box office success. The same year, Hathaway starred in [Jonathan Demme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Demme "Jonathan Demme")'s drama *[Rachel Getting Married](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Getting_Married "Rachel Getting Married")* (2008) as a young woman who returns home for her sister's wedding after being released from drug rehabilitation. Hathaway described her character as narcissistic, and earned critical acclaim for her performance. [Peter Travers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Travers "Peter Travers") of *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")* described her as "raw and riveting" in the role, writing that she "acts the hell out of it, achieving a state of sorrowful grace". Hathaway received [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress "Academy Award for Best Actress"), [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Drama "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Drama"), [Critics' Choice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Movie_Award_for_Best_Actress "Critics' Choice Movie Award for Best Actress"), and [SAG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Female_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role "Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Female Actor in a Leading Role") nominations for Best Actress, winning the Critics' Choice award. The same year, she starred in the mystery thriller *Passengers* and hosted an episode of the sketch comedy television show *[Saturday Night Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live "Saturday Night Live")*.

### 2009–2011: Romantic comedies and hosting events

In 2009, Hathaway was invited to join the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences "Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences"). The same year, she starred in the romantic comedy *[Bride Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bride_Wars "Bride Wars")*, which she described as "hideously commercial—gloriously so". The film, in which she and [Kate Hudson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Hudson "Kate Hudson") play best friends who become rivals after their weddings are scheduled on the same day, was a critical failure but was successful[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] at the box office, and earned Hathaway a nomination for the [MTV Movie Award for Best Female Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Movie_Award_for_Best_Female_Performance "MTV Movie Award for Best Female Performance"). During the summer of 2010, Hathaway played the heroine [Viola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viola_(Twelfth_Night) "Viola (Twelfth Night)") in a production of Shakespeare's *[Twelfth Night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelfth_Night "Twelfth Night")* at the [Delacorte Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delacorte_Theater "Delacorte Theater") in New York City. For her portrayal of the role, she was nominated for the [Drama Desk Award for Outstanding Actress in a Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama_Desk_Award_for_Outstanding_Actress_in_a_Play "Drama Desk Award for Outstanding Actress in a Play"). In 2010, she won a [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Voice-Over Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Voice-Over_Performance "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Voice-Over Performance") for providing her voice for the episode "[Once Upon a Time in Springfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Once_Upon_a_Time_in_Springfield "Once Upon a Time in Springfield")" of *[The Simpsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simpsons "The Simpsons")*. Hathaway voiced characters in *[Family Guy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Guy "Family Guy")* in 2010 and 2011.

In 2010, Hathaway played a receptionist who dates a clerk (played by [Topher Grace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topher_Grace "Topher Grace")) in the romantic comedy *[Valentine's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentine%27s_Day_(2010_film) "Valentine's Day (2010 film)")*, directed by Garry Marshall. The film was a commercial success,[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] grossing more than $215 million worldwide against a budget of $52 million. The same year, Hathaway played the [White Queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Queen_(Through_the_Looking_Glass) "White Queen (Through the Looking Glass)") in [Tim Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Burton "Tim Burton")'s [*Alice in Wonderland*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_in_Wonderland_(2010_film) "Alice in Wonderland (2010 film)") alongside [Helena Bonham Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helena_Bonham_Carter "Helena Bonham Carter") and [Johnny Depp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Depp "Johnny Depp"). She drew inspiration from [Debbie Harry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debbie_Harry "Debbie Harry") and the artwork of [Dan Flavin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Flavin "Dan Flavin") for her version of the character, which she described as "a punk-rock [vegan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veganism "Veganism") pacifist". *Alice in Wonderland* received mixed reviews from critics but grossed $1 billion at the box office, becoming the [second-highest-grossing film of 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_films "List of highest-grossing films").

Hathaway reunited with her *[Brokeback Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brokeback_Mountain "Brokeback Mountain")* co-star [Jake Gyllenhaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Gyllenhaal "Jake Gyllenhaal") for [Edward Zwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Zwick "Edward Zwick")'s romantic comedy-drama *[Love & Other Drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_%26_Other_Drugs "Love & Other Drugs")* (2010), which was based on the nonfiction book *Hard Sell: The Evolution of a Viagra Salesman* by [Jamie Reidy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Reidy "Jamie Reidy"). Hathaway's character is a free-spirited artist with [Parkinson's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinson%27s_disease "Parkinson's disease"). To prepare for the film's nude scenes, Hathaway watched films featuring [Kate Winslet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Winslet "Kate Winslet") and [Penélope Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pen%C3%A9lope_Cruz "Penélope Cruz"), both of whom she felt had performed nude scenes with sensitivity and dignity. Critics generally praised the film's mature depiction of romance, but were unenthusiastic about its plot. Hathaway's performance, which the critic [Roger Ebert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ebert "Roger Ebert") called "warm" and "lovable", earned her a [Satellite Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_Award_for_Best_Actress_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture "Satellite Award for Best Actress – Motion Picture") and a nomination for the [Golden Globe Award for Best Actress – Comedy or Musical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Comedy_or_Musical "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress – Motion Picture Comedy or Musical").

In December 2010, Hathaway and [Denzel Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denzel_Washington "Denzel Washington") attended the [2010 Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Nobel_Peace_Prize "2010 Nobel Peace Prize") ceremony in [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo "Oslo"), which honored the imprisoned Chinese writer and human rights activist [Liu Xiaobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Xiaobo "Liu Xiaobo"). The following day, they co-hosted the [Nobel Peace Prize Concert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize_Concert "Nobel Peace Prize Concert"). Two months later, Hathaway and [James Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Franco "James Franco") hosted the [83rd Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/83rd_Academy_Awards "83rd Academy Awards"). Although critics were unenthusiastic about their chemistry, the pair were nominated for [Outstanding Variety Special (Live)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Variety_Special_(Live) "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Variety Special (Live)") at the [63rd Primetime Emmy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/63rd_Primetime_Emmy_Awards "63rd Primetime Emmy Awards").

In 2011, Hathaway voiced Jewel, a [Spix's macaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spix%27s_macaw "Spix's macaw") from Rio de Janeiro, in the animated film *[Rio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_(2011_film) "Rio (2011 film)")*. The film received generally positive reviews from critics, who praised its voice acting. A commercial success,[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] it grossed more than $484 million worldwide against a budget of $90 million. Later that year, Hathaway starred alongside [Jim Sturgess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Sturgess "Jim Sturgess") in [Lone Scherfig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lone_Scherfig "Lone Scherfig")'s *[One Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Day_(2011_film) "One Day (2011 film)")*, based on [the 2009 novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Day_(novel) "One Day (novel)") by [David Nicholls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Nicholls_(writer) "David Nicholls (writer)"). The film tells the story of two young people who meet annually for twenty years after sharing a platonic one-night stand. Hathaway was given the script clandestinely, as *One Day* was set in Britain, and Scherfig was not looking for an American actress for the lead role. After an unproductive meeting with Scherfig, Hathaway left a list of songs for her. After listening to them, she cast Hathaway. Hathaway later expressed regret that she may have unwittingly held a [misogynistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misogyny "Misogyny") attitude towards Scherfig and not trusted her fully because of her gender. Hathaway's [Yorkshire accent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire_accent "Yorkshire accent") in the film received criticism, with Suzanne Moore of [BBC Radio 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_4 "BBC Radio 4")'s [Front Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_Row_(radio_programme) "Front Row (radio programme)") saying that it was "all over the shop ... Sometimes she's from Scotland, sometimes she's from New York, you just can't tell". *One Day* received mixed reviews, but became a moderate box office success.

### 2012–2014: *Les Miserables* and Christopher Nolan films

In 2012, Hathaway's audiobook recording of [L. Frank Baum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L._Frank_Baum "L. Frank Baum")'s 1900 novel *[The Wonderful Wizard of Oz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wonderful_Wizard_of_Oz "The Wonderful Wizard of Oz")* was released at [Audible.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audible.com "Audible.com") and garnered her an [Audie Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audie_Award "Audie Award") nomination for Best Solo Narration – Female. She then played the morally ambiguous cat burglar [Selina Kyle / Catwoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catwoman "Catwoman") in *[The Dark Knight Rises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dark_Knight_Rises "The Dark Knight Rises")*, the final film in [Christopher Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan "Christopher Nolan")'s [*The Dark Knight* trilogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dark_Knight_Trilogy "The Dark Knight Trilogy"). Hathaway thought she was auditioning for the role of [Harley Quinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harley_Quinn "Harley Quinn"); after an hour of talking with Nolan, he revealed that he was considering her for Catwoman. Hathaway had to redouble her efforts at the gym to meet the requirements of the role, which she described as her most physically demanding assignment up to that point. She trained extensively in martial arts, and looked to [Hedy Lamarr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedy_Lamarr "Hedy Lamarr") in developing her performance as Catwoman. Jim Vejvoda of *[IGN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IGN "IGN")* called Hathaway "a magnetic presence" who imbues her character with "a wounded spirit and a survivor's edge that makes her feel genuine and sympathetic even when she's being naughty." Hathaway won the [Saturn Award for Best Supporting Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress "Saturn Award for Best Supporting Actress") for her performance. *The Dark Knight Rises* was well-received by critics and grossed more than $1 billion worldwide, becoming the [third-highest-grossing film of 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_in_film "2012 in film").

Hathaway portrayed [Fantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantine "Fantine"), a [prostitute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostitute "Prostitute") dying of [tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculosis "Tuberculosis"), in [Tom Hooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Hooper "Tom Hooper")'s *[Les Misérables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Mis%C3%A9rables_(2012_film) "Les Misérables (2012 film)")* (2012), a film adaptation of the stage musical [*Les Misérables*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Mis%C3%A9rables_(musical) "Les Misérables (musical)"). In preparation for the role, Hathaway consumed fewer than 500 calories a day in order to lose 25 pounds (11 kg). As part of her effort to inhabit Fantine's misery, Hathaway deprived herself of her husband, sending him back to the United States while she was filming in London. Reviewing the film for *[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post"),* [Ann Hornaday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Hornaday "Ann Hornaday") called Hathaway "the centerpiece of a movie composed entirely of centerpieces". Although Hathaway herself felt lukewarm about her performance, she won the [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress "Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress"), [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture "Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actress – Motion Picture"), [Screen Actors Guild Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Female_Actor_in_a_Supporting_Role "Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Female Actor in a Supporting Role") and [BAFTA Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Supporting_Role "BAFTA Award for Best Actress in a Supporting Role") for Best Supporting Actress. In January 2013, Hathaway's rendition of "I Dreamed a Dream" reached number 69 on the [*Billboard* Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100 "Billboard Hot 100").

After briefly appearing in the romantic comedy *[Don Jon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Jon "Don Jon")* (2013), Hathaway starred in and co-produced (with her husband and others) the drama film *[Song One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_One "Song One")*. She plays a student who begins a romantic relationship with her brother's favorite musician. Her character was originally written as a 19-year-old, but Kate Barker-Froyland, the film's writer and director, changed the character to an older woman after casting Hathaway. Hathaway said she decided to produce the film because of its depiction of the healing power of music and second chances. For the film's soundtrack, she provided her voice for the song "Afraid of Heights". *Song One* premiered in the U.S. Dramatic Competition at the [30th Sundance Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Sundance_Film_Festival "2014 Sundance Film Festival") in January 2014. It was released in theaters the following year to mixed reviews from critics, and failed to recoup its $6 million budget.

Hathaway reprised her role as Jewel in the animated film *[Rio 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_2 "Rio 2")* (2014), her third film with [Jamie Foxx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Foxx "Jamie Foxx"). It grossed nearly five times more than its $103 million budget. Hathaway's sole live-action release of 2014 was Nolan's science fiction film *[Interstellar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstellar_(film) "Interstellar (film)")*, which follows a crew of astronauts who travel through a wormhole in search of a new home for mankind. She was drawn to the part of [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA") scientist Amelia Brand due to the character's growth from an arrogant person to a humbler one. Hathaway nearly experienced hypothermia while filming a water scene in Iceland, as the [dry suit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_suit "Dry suit") she was wearing had not been properly secured. Geoffrey Macnab of *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent "The Independent")* described Hathaway as "affecting" in her role, while James Dyer of *[Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_(magazine) "Empire (magazine)")* found "soulful nuance" in her performance. *Interstellar* grossed over $701 million worldwide against a budget of $165 million, and earned Hathaway a nomination for the [Saturn Award for Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn_Award_for_Best_Actress "Saturn Award for Best Actress").

### 2015–2021: Mixed success

Hathaway began 2015 with an appearance in the first season of the musical reality show *[Lip Sync Battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lip_Sync_Battle "Lip Sync Battle")*. In the episode, she competed against her *The Devil Wears Prada* co-star [Emily Blunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Blunt "Emily Blunt"); she lip synced "[Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_More_Drama "No More Drama")" by [Mary J. Blige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_J._Blige "Mary J. Blige") and "[Wrecking Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrecking_Ball_(Miley_Cyrus_song) "Wrecking Ball (Miley Cyrus song)")" by [Miley Cyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miley_Cyrus "Miley Cyrus"). [Nancy Meyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Meyers "Nancy Meyers")' *[The Intern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Intern_(2015_film) "The Intern (2015 film)")* was Hathaway's sole film release of 2015. It tells the story of Ben Whittaker (played by [Robert De Niro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_De_Niro "Robert De Niro")), a 70-year-old widower who becomes a senior intern at an online fashion site run by Jules Ostin (Hathaway). Hathaway had aspired to work with De Niro and Meyers, her favorite actor and director, respectively; impressed with the film's story, she auditioned for the third time for a Meyers film. *The Intern* received mixed reviews. Glenn Kenny of [RogerEbert.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RogerEbert.com "RogerEbert.com") called Hathaway "extremely appealing" in her role, while [Bilge Ebiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilge_Ebiri "Bilge Ebiri") of *[New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_(magazine) "New York (magazine)")* magazine said the film "gets off on De Niro's amiability and Hathaway's sweet energy". *The Intern* grossed $194 million worldwide against a $35 million budget.

Hathaway reprised the role of the White Queen in *[Alice Through the Looking Glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Through_the_Looking_Glass_(2016_film) "Alice Through the Looking Glass (2016 film)")*, the 2016 sequel to *[Alice in Wonderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_in_Wonderland_(2010_film) "Alice in Wonderland (2010 film)")*. That March, it was reported that she would reprise her role for *The Princess Diaries 3*; the project was cancelled after the death of [Garry Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Marshall "Garry Marshall"), who was set to direct. Hathaway is one of several actors featured on [Barbra Streisand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbra_Streisand "Barbra Streisand")'s 2016 album *[Encore: Movie Partners Sing Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encore:_Movie_Partners_Sing_Broadway "Encore: Movie Partners Sing Broadway")*. Along with [Daisy Ridley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisy_Ridley "Daisy Ridley"), Hathaway and Streisand performed the song "[At the Ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/At_the_Ballet "At the Ballet")" from *[A Chorus Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Chorus_Line "A Chorus Line")*; Hathaway played the role of Maggie, one of a trio of dancers hoping to be cast in an upcoming show. Her final film that year was alongside [Jason Sudeikis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Sudeikis "Jason Sudeikis") in [Nacho Vigalondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nacho_Vigalondo "Nacho Vigalondo")'s science fiction black comedy *[Colossal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossal_(film) "Colossal (film)")* (2016). Playing an unemployed young writer, Hathaway was the first actress to sign on at a time when the project had no financial backing. She was drawn to the genre-hopping nature of the script, later comparing it to *[Being John Malkovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Being_John_Malkovich "Being John Malkovich")* (1999), one of her favorite films. *Colossal* received positive reviews from critics and earned $4 million at the box office.

After a two-year absence from the screen, Hathaway starred as a famous actress in *[Ocean's 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean%27s_8 "Ocean's 8")*, an all-female spin-off of the [*Ocean's Eleven* franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean%27s "Ocean's") from director [Gary Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Ross "Gary Ross"). Co-starring [Sandra Bullock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_Bullock "Sandra Bullock") and [Cate Blanchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett "Cate Blanchett"), it follows a group of criminals who plan to rob the [Met Gala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Met_Gala "Met Gala"). Hathaway was drawn to the idea of playing someone with an immense ego and saw the role as an opportunity "to lean into all the ridiculous fame nonsense" that she tried to avoid in her everyday life. She hoped the film would be profitable so that it could debunk claims that female-led films do not succeed commercially.Several critics considered Hathaway a "scene stealer" among the cast. Jason Di Rosso of [ABC Online](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_Online "ABC Online") said the film's best moments "belong to Hathaway as the anxiety-ridden, vain and capricious starlet. She is regarded as a successful combination of comedy and pathos, portraying a celebrity affected by the pressures of fame while also demonstrating the ability to navigate and outsmart them." *Ocean's 8* was a box office success,[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] grossing over $297 million worldwide against a $70 million budget.

Hathaway's first two films of 2019, the thriller *[Serenity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serenity_(2019_film) "Serenity (2019 film)")* and the comedy *[The Hustle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hustle_(film) "The Hustle (film)")*, were poorly received by critics. In *Serenity*, she starred alongside her *Interstellar* co-star [Matthew McConaughey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_McConaughey "Matthew McConaughey") as a woman who asks her ex-husband to kill her current abusive husband. Michael O’Sullivan of *[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")* described her performance as "cartoonish", adding that her *[femme fatale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Femme_fatale "Femme fatale")* character was reminiscent of [Jessica Rabbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Rabbit "Jessica Rabbit") from *[Who Framed Roger Rabbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who_Framed_Roger_Rabbit "Who Framed Roger Rabbit").* Hathaway starred alongside [Rebel Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebel_Wilson "Rebel Wilson") in *The Hustle*, a remake of *[Dirty Rotten Scoundrels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirty_Rotten_Scoundrels_(film) "Dirty Rotten Scoundrels (film)")* (1988). *The Hustle* emerged as a [sleeper hit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleeper_hit "Sleeper hit"). Hathaway next played a woman with [bipolar disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipolar_disorder "Bipolar disorder") in an episode of the [Amazon Prime Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Prime_Video "Amazon Prime Video") romantic anthology series *[Modern Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Love_(TV_series) "Modern Love (TV series)")*. She then played the wife of [Mark Ruffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Ruffalo "Mark Ruffalo")'s character in [Todd Haynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Haynes "Todd Haynes")' legal drama film *[Dark Waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Waters_(2019_film) "Dark Waters (2019 film)")*, about environmental poisoning committed by the chemical company [DuPont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuPont_(1802%E2%80%932017) "DuPont (1802–2017)"). Writing for *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")*, [Owen Gleiberman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Gleiberman "Owen Gleiberman") called her supporting performance "a piercing dance of agony and loyalty".

Hathaway began the new decade with the political thriller *[The Last Thing He Wanted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Thing_He_Wanted_(film) "The Last Thing He Wanted (film)")* (2020), based on [the 1996 novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Thing_He_Wanted "The Last Thing He Wanted") by [Joan Didion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Didion "Joan Didion"). Hathaway considered herself to be an unlikely choice for the role of a headstrong journalist, as it differed from her own "puppy dog" personality. The film received negative reviews from critics. Hathaway then starred as an [evil witch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_High_Witch "Grand High Witch") in *[The Witches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Witches_(2020_film) "The Witches (2020 film)")*, an adaptation of [the 1983 children's novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Witches_(novel) "The Witches (novel)") by [Roald Dahl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roald_Dahl "Roald Dahl"). The film received mixed reviews. Hathaway's performances in both films earned her nominations for Worst Actress at the [41st Golden Raspberry Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/41st_Golden_Raspberry_Awards "41st Golden Raspberry Awards"). In 2021, she starred with [Chiwetel Ejiofor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiwetel_Ejiofor "Chiwetel Ejiofor") in the heist film *[Locked Down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locked_Down_(film) "Locked Down (film)")*, directed by [Doug Liman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Liman "Doug Liman"), which premiered on [HBO Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO_Max "HBO Max"). Set during the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"), it was shot over the course of 18 days with limited resources. Hathaway next appeared in one episode of the Amazon Prime Video anthology series *[Solos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solos_(TV_series) "Solos (TV series)")*.

### 2022–present: Mainstream resurgence

Hathaway executive produced and starred opposite [Jared Leto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jared_Leto "Jared Leto") in the [Apple TV+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_TV%2B "Apple TV+") miniseries *[WeCrashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WeCrashed "WeCrashed")* (2022), about the company [WeWork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WeWork "WeWork"). It received generally favorable reviews, with particular praise for Hathaway's portrayal of [Rebekah Neumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebekah_Neumann "Rebekah Neumann"). Angie Han of *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")* commended her for resisting "the temptation to turn Rebekah into an exaggerated caricature of an entitled woo-woo type". The same year, Hathaway starred in [James Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gray_(director) "James Gray (director)")'s semi-autobiographical period drama *[Armageddon Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armageddon_Time "Armageddon Time")*, portraying a character inspired by Gray's mother. David Rooney of *The Hollywood Reporter* considered it to be her best performance since *Rachel Getting Married*, while [Owen Gleiberman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Gleiberman "Owen Gleiberman") of *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* praised her for making her character "at once affectionate and blinkered".

In her two film releases of 2023, *[Eileen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eileen_(film) "Eileen (film)")* and *[She Came to Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/She_Came_to_Me "She Came to Me")*, Hathaway played emotionally troubled psychiatrists. *Eileen*, based on [Ottessa Moshfegh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottessa_Moshfegh "Ottessa Moshfegh")'s [2015 thriller novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eileen_(novel) "Eileen (novel)"), stars [Thomasin McKenzie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomasin_McKenzie "Thomasin McKenzie") in the title role and premiered at the [2023 Sundance Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Sundance_Film_Festival "2023 Sundance Film Festival"). Hathaway described the film as "*[Carol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_(film) "Carol (film)")* meets *[Reservoir Dogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservoir_Dogs "Reservoir Dogs")*". Ryan Lattanzio of *[IndieWire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndieWire "IndieWire")* felt Hathaway's role was "tailor-made" for her, and thought her performance was the best of her career. At the [2023 Berlin International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/73rd_Berlin_International_Film_Festival "73rd Berlin International Film Festival"), [Rebecca Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_Miller "Rebecca Miller")'s romantic comedy *She Came to Me* was released.

Hathaway's first role of 2024 was a grieving mother coping with the loss of her son in the thriller *[Mothers' Instinct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mothers%27_Instinct_(2024_film) "Mothers' Instinct (2024 film)")*, which is a remake of the [2018 Belgian film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mothers%27_Instinct_(2018_film) "Mothers' Instinct (2018 film)"). Because the loss of a child was her worst fear, Hathaway created a "protective layer" between herself and her character, which included asking the cast and crew to call her by her character's name. Writing for *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")*, [Peter Bradshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bradshaw "Peter Bradshaw") thought her performance was overshadowed by that of her co-star [Jessica Chastain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Chastain "Jessica Chastain"). Hathaway next starred in the romantic comedy *[The Idea of You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Idea_of_You "The Idea of You")*, based on [Robinne Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robinne_Lee "Robinne Lee")'s novel *The Idea of You*, playing a divorced mother who begins a romance with a younger pop star. Alissa Wilkinson of *The New York Times* thought the film "succeeds mostly because of Hathaway's performance", and praised her chemistry with co-star [Nicholas Galitzine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Galitzine "Nicholas Galitzine").

Hathaway appeared in five films in 2026. Her first was [David Lowery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lowery_(director) "David Lowery (director)")'s *[Mother Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_Mary_(2026_film) "Mother Mary (2026 film)")*, in which she played a pop star whose reunion with her former costume designer (played by [Michaela Coel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michaela_Coel "Michaela Coel")) forces her to confront her past. The film received mixed reviews. Hathaway next reprised the role of Andrea "Andy" Sachs in *[The Devil Wears Prada 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Devil_Wears_Prada_2 "The Devil Wears Prada 2")*. She and her co-stars [Meryl Streep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meryl_Streep "Meryl Streep") and [Emily Blunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Blunt "Emily Blunt") each earned a $12.5 million upfront salary for the sequel, with back-end bonuses that could push their total pay past $20 million. The film received generally positive reviews, with David Rooney of *The Hollywood Reporter* calling Hathaway "effortlessly charming in the role". *The Devil Wears Prada 2* and Hathaway's next release—*[The Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Odyssey_(2026_film) "The Odyssey (2026 film)")*, her third film with Christopher Nolan—were among the [highest-grossing films of 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_in_film#Highest-grossing_films "2026 in film"). In *The Odyssey*, she played [Penelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penelope "Penelope"), the Greek queen of [Ithaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ithaca_(island) "Ithaca (island)"), who awaits the return of her husband Odysseus ([Matt Damon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Damon "Matt Damon")) after the [Trojan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_War "Trojan War"). Marlow Stern of *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* described Hathaway's performance as a powerful and gritty portrayal characterized by strength and determination. Hathaway's fourth film of the year was [David Robert Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Robert_Mitchell "David Robert Mitchell")'s science fiction film *[The End of Oak Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_End_of_Oak_Street "The End of Oak Street")*, which she starred in alongside [Ewan McGregor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewan_McGregor "Ewan McGregor").[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] Hathaway's final film of 2026 is [*Verity*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verity_(film) "Verity (film)"), based on [Colleen Hoover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colleen_Hoover "Colleen Hoover")'s [2018 novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verity_(novel) "Verity (novel)"). Hathaway stars in the film as a best-selling author.

## Public image

[Laura Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Brown_(fashion_journalist) "Laura Brown (fashion journalist)") of *[Harper's Bazaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harper%27s_Bazaar "Harper's Bazaar")* has described Hathaway as sincere, warm and funny, while John Hiscock of *[The Daily Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph "The Daily Telegraph")* has called her "well-grounded" and friendly, with a good sense of humor and an easy-going attitude. Nancy Meyers, who directed Hathaway in *The Intern,* referred to her as "wise beyond her years", while the book *365 Style* described her as having a [girl next door](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girl_next_door "Girl next door") image.

After Hathaway's 2013 awards acceptance speeches for *Les Misérables*, *[The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic "The Atlantic")* noted that several media commentators accused her of being "annoying" and making "awkward" jokes. Discussing this, Hathaway explained that she feels anxious when public speaking but has since grown from it and become a more compassionate person. She said regarding her perceived image: "People have this idea of me as just being a very prim, professional girl, which I suppose I am, but I do cut loose and have fun in my life."

Remarking on her performance in *[Twelfth Night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelfth_Night "Twelfth Night")*, [Charles Isherwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Isherwood "Charles Isherwood") wrote that "on screen or onstage Ms. Hathaway possesses the unmistakable glow of a natural star". An *[Esquire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esquire_(magazine) "Esquire (magazine)")* writer wrote in 2018 that many of her good performances had been overlooked, describing her career as "subtle brilliance that has largely gone unnoticed". Discussing her career in 2015, Hathaway said that, after her breakthrough in *The Princess Diaries*, she struggled to find roles that were serious—or were not about princesses. According to Jodi Guglielmi of *[People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_(magazine) "People (magazine)")*, Hathaway used that fear of being typecast as motivation to build a versatile body of work. Gugliemli believed that her ability to extensively research her roles has been the key to her success. A writer for *The Daily Telegraph* commended Hathaway's willingness to appear in different genres, ranging from action comedies to dramas. Hathaway said she would be "lost" without acting and feels lucky to have found it as her profession. A trained stage actress, she said in 2006 that she prefers performing on stage to film roles and is terrified of acting on camera. "I always assume that every film is my last, and I always assume that I have to go out and convince everybody why they have to hire me. I still audition," she said in 2015.

*[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes "Forbes")* reported that Hathaway was one of the world's highest-paid actresses in 2015, and since 2017, she has been among the highest-grossing actresses of the 21st century. In 2009, she was included on *Forbes*' annual [Celebrity 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebrity_100 "Celebrity 100") list with earnings of $7 million. As of July 2018[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anne_Hathaway&action=edit), her films had grossed $6.7 billion worldwide. Profiled as among the world's leading actresses by *[Vanity Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanity_Fair_(magazine) "Vanity Fair (magazine)")*, Hathaway, according to Catherine Elsworth of *The Daily Telegraph* wrote in 2008 that she is pursued by both directors and cosmetics companies. In January 2008, she joined French luxury perfumes and cosmetics house [Lancôme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanc%C3%B4me "Lancôme") as the face of their fragrance "Magnifique". In 2011, she became the new face of the Italian company [Tod's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tod%27s "Tod's").

Hathaway's beauty and sex appeal have been picked up by several media outlets; *[FHM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FHM "FHM")*, *People*, *[Maxim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxim_(magazine) "Maxim (magazine)")*, *[Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_(magazine) "Empire (magazine)")* and *[Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")* have included her on their yearly listings of sexiest women. In 2011, *[Los Angeles Times Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times_Magazine "Los Angeles Times Magazine")* listed her as one of the 50 Most Beautiful Women in Film. Elsworth called her in 2008 "the hottest young actress in Hollywood". Hathaway disagrees with this opinion, insisting that she has a "good girl" image and no sex appeal. She has refused to undergo treatment with [Botox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botox "Botox"), saying she is comfortable in her own skin. In 2026, *[People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_(magazine) "People (magazine)")* magazine featured Hathaway on the cover of their annual "[World's Most Beautiful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Magazine_50_Most_Beautiful_People "People Magazine 50 Most Beautiful People")" issue.

## Advocacy

In 2008, Hathaway won an award from the [Human Rights Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Campaign "Human Rights Campaign") for her philanthropy. The same year, she was honored at *[Elle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elle_(magazine) "Elle (magazine)")*'s Women in Hollywood tribute and was honored for her work with Step Up Women's Network. In 2010, she began working with the [World Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank_Group "World Bank Group") on a two-year development program called *The Girl Effect*, with the mission of empowering girls around the world. In 2013, she narrated *[Girl Rising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girl_Rising "Girl Rising")*, a [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN "CNN") documentary film about the power of women's education. Hathaway was appointed as a [UN Women Goodwill Ambassador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Women_Goodwill_Ambassador "UN Women Goodwill Ambassador") in 2016, with the stated goal of addressing the unequal burden shouldered by women who care for children. She has also worked with the [Nike Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.#Causes "Nike, Inc.") to raise awareness about [child marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_marriage "Child marriage"), helped vaccinate children against [hepatitis A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatitis_A "Hepatitis A") in [Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaragua "Nicaragua"), and promoted the rights of women and girls in [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya "Kenya") and [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia "Ethiopia").

Hathaway serves on the board of the Lollipop Theater Network, which brings entertainment to children in hospitals. She has been involved with the charities [Creative Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Coalition "Creative Coalition"), [Human Rights Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Campaign "Human Rights Campaign"), and [St. Jude Children's Research Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Jude_Children%27s_Research_Hospital "St. Jude Children's Research Hospital"). In 2017, she spoke on [International Women's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Women%27s_Day "International Women's Day") in favor of paid [parental leave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parental_leave "Parental leave") for both men and women. In 2018, she collaborated with 300 women in Hollywood to launch the [Time's Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time%27s_Up_(organization) "Time's Up (organization)") initiative, which aims to protect women from harassment and discrimination. Hathaway has criticized inequality in the U.S. film industry and has advocated for more professional opportunities for women in the industry.

In response to the 2022 [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine "Russian invasion of Ukraine"), Hathaway made donations to the [Ukrainian Red Cross Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Red_Cross_Society "Ukrainian Red Cross Society"), [UNICEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNICEF "UNICEF") and [Save the Children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Children "Save the Children") to help Ukrainians affected by the war. She praised the [Berlin International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_International_Film_Festival "Berlin International Film Festival") in 2023 for featuring Ukrainian president [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volodymyr_Zelenskyy "Volodymyr Zelenskyy"), who gave a televised speech during the opening ceremony.

## Personal life

### Relationships

Hathaway dated the Italian real estate developer Raffaello Follieri from 2004 to 2008. Follieri was investigated by the [Internal Revenue Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_Revenue_Service "Internal Revenue Service") in 2008 for failure to file required non-profit information forms. He was arrested on charges of defrauding investors, including former U.S. president [Bill Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Clinton "Bill Clinton"), out of millions of dollars. The [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI "FBI") confiscated Hathaway's private journals from Follieri's New York City apartment as part of its investigation into his activities. After pleading guilty, Follieri was sentenced to four and a half years in prison.

Hathaway married the actor and businessman Adam Shulman in [Big Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Sur "Big Sur"), California on September 29, 2012. The couple sold their wedding photograph and donated the proceeds to the same-sex marriage advocacy organization [Freedom to Marry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_to_Marry "Freedom to Marry"). They later hosted the organization's National Engagement Party fundraiser, which raised $500,000. Hathaway and Shulman own several properties, including a [chalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalet "Chalet") in Southern California, which serves as their primary residence, and a penthouse on the [Upper West Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_West_Side "Upper West Side") of New York City. Hathaway experienced a miscarriage in 2015. The couple have two sons, born in 2016 and 2019. In June 2026, Hathaway announced that she was pregnant with their third child.

### Politics

Hathaway has supported various [Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)") candidates during U.S. presidential elections, including [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") ([2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election "2008 United States presidential election"), [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_presidential_election "2012 United States presidential election")), [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton "Hillary Clinton") ([2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election "2016 United States presidential election")), [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden") ([2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election "2020 United States presidential election")), and [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris "Kamala Harris") ([2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election "2024 United States presidential election")). She attended the [2008 Democratic National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Democratic_National_Convention "2008 Democratic National Convention"), and appeared at a benefit concert for the Hillary Clinton [campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton_2016_presidential_campaign "Hillary Clinton 2016 presidential campaign") in New York City in 2012.

Hathaway supports [abortion rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion-rights_movement "Abortion-rights movement"), [immigrant rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigrant_rights "Immigrant rights") and [gun control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_control_in_the_United_States "Gun control in the United States"), and has criticized U.S. president [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") for his [anti-immigration policies.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_policy_of_the_second_Trump_administration "Immigration policy of the second Trump administration") She is a supporter of [LGBT rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights "LGBT rights"), and has donated money to organizations which support the legalization of [same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage "Same-sex marriage"). She has spoken out on [homophobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homophobia "Homophobia"), [school bullying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_bullying "School bullying"), [transphobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transphobia "Transphobia") and [white privilege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_privilege "White privilege").

### Health

In 2007, Hathaway discussed experiencing [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_depressive_disorder "Major depressive disorder") during her teenage years, stating that she eventually overcame it without medication. In 2008, she began smoking after a stressful summer and the end of her relationship with Follieri. She eventually quit smoking, and in 2018 she quit drinking alcohol. She has been [vegetarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetarianism "Vegetarianism") and [vegan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veganism "Veganism") at various points in her life.

## Acting credits and awards

Hathaway's most acclaimed and highest-grossing films, according to [Box Office Mojo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box_Office_Mojo "Box Office Mojo") and the review aggregator [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes"), include *The Princess Diaries* (2001), *Brokeback Mountain* (2005), *The Devil Wears Prada* (2006), *Get Smart* (2008), *Rachel Getting Married* (2008), *Valentine's Day* (2010), *Alice in Wonderland* (2010), *Love and Other Drugs* (2010), *The Dark Knight Rises* (2012), *Les Misérables* (2012), *Interstellar* (2014), *The Intern* (2015), *Colossal* (2016), and *Ocean's 8* (2018).

Hathaway has been nominated for two [Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards"), three [Golden Globes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globes "Golden Globes"), and a [British Academy Film Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Awards "British Academy Film Awards") (BAFTA). For her performance in *[Les Misérables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Mis%C3%A9rables_(2012_film) "Les Misérables (2012 film)")*, she won an [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress "Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress"), a [Golden Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture "Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actress – Motion Picture"), a [Screen Actors Guild Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actor_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Female_Actor_in_a_Supporting_Role "Actor Award for Outstanding Performance by a Female Actor in a Supporting Role") and a [BAFTA Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Supporting_Role "BAFTA Award for Best Actress in a Supporting Role") for best supporting actress. She also won a [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Voice-Over Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Voice-Over_Performance "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Voice-Over Performance") for her voice role in a 2010 episode of *[The Simpsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simpsons "The Simpsons")*. In 2018, Hathaway was one of 50 nominees for the [New Jersey Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Hall_of_Fame "New Jersey Hall of Fame"). In 2019, she received a [motion pictures star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_actors_with_Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame_motion_picture_stars "List of actors with Hollywood Walk of Fame motion picture stars") on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame "Hollywood Walk of Fame") for her contributions to the film industry.
