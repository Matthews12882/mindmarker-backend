# Spanish Empire

The **Spanish Empire**, sometimes referred to as the **Hispanic Monarchy** or the **Catholic Monarchy**, was a [colonial empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_empire "Colonial empire") that existed between 1492 and 1976. In conjunction with the [Portuguese Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire "Portuguese Empire"), it ushered in the European [Age of Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery "Age of Discovery"). It achieved a global scale, controlling vast portions of the [Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americas "Americas"), [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa "Africa"), various islands in [Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia "Asia") and [Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania "Oceania"), as well as territory in other parts of Europe. It was one of the most powerful empires of the [early modern period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_period "Early modern period"), becoming known as "[the empire on which the sun never sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_empire_on_which_the_sun_never_sets "The empire on which the sun never sets")". At its greatest extent in the late 1700s and early 1800s, the Spanish Empire covered 13.7 million square kilometres (5.3 million square miles), making it one of the [largest empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_empires "List of largest empires") in history.

Beginning with the 1492 arrival of [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus "Christopher Columbus") and continuing for over three centuries, the Spanish Empire would expand across the [Caribbean Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Islands "Caribbean Islands"), half of [South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_America "South America"), most of [Central America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_America "Central America") and much of [North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_America "North America"). In the beginning, Portugal was the only serious threat to Spanish hegemony in the [New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World "New World"). To end the threat of Portuguese expansion, Spain conquered Portugal and the [Azores Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azores_Islands "Azores Islands") from 1580 to 1582 during the [War of the Portuguese Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Portuguese_Succession "War of the Portuguese Succession"), resulting in the establishment of the [Iberian Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Union "Iberian Union"), a forced union between the two crowns that lasted until 1640 when Portugal [regained its independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Restoration_War "Portuguese Restoration War") from Spain. In 1700, [Philip V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_V_of_Spain "Philip V of Spain") became king of Spain after the death of [Charles II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_Spain "Charles II of Spain"), the last [Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_Spain "Habsburg Spain") monarch of Spain, who died without an heir.

The [Magellan–Elcano circumnavigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magellan_expedition "Magellan expedition")—the first circumnavigation of the Earth—laid the foundation for Spain's [Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific "Pacific") empire and for Spanish control over the [East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_East_Indies "Spanish East Indies"). The influx of gold and silver from the mines in [Zacatecas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zacatecas "Zacatecas") and [Guanajuato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanajuato "Guanajuato") in Mexico and [Potosí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potos%C3%AD "Potosí") in Bolivia enriched the Spanish crown and financed military endeavors and territorial expansion. Spain was largely able to defend its territories in the Americas, with the [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_colonial_empire "Dutch colonial empire"), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_overseas_possessions "English overseas possessions"), and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire "French colonial empire") taking only small Caribbean islands and outposts, using them to engage in [contraband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraband "Contraband") trade with the Spanish populace in the Indies. Another crucial element of the empire's expansion was the financial support provided by [Genoese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa "Republic of Genoa") bankers, who financed royal expeditions and military campaigns.

The Bourbon monarchy implemented [reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourbon_Reforms "Bourbon Reforms") like the *[Nueva Planta decrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Planta_decrees "Nueva Planta decrees")*, which centralized power and abolished regional privileges. Economic policies promoted trade with the colonies, enhancing Spanish influence in the Americas. Socially, tensions emerged between the ruling elite and the rising bourgeoisie, as well as divisions between peninsular Spaniards and Creoles in the Americas. These factors ultimately set the stage for the independence movements that began in the early 19th century, leading to the gradual disintegration of Spanish colonial authority. By the mid-1820s, Spain had lost its territories in Mexico, Central America, and South America. By late 1898, it had also lost [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captaincy_General_of_Cuba "Captaincy General of Cuba"), [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captaincy_General_of_Puerto_Rico "Captaincy General of Puerto Rico"), the [Philippine Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Islands "Philippine Islands"), and [Guam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guam "Guam") in the [Mariana Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariana_Islands "Mariana Islands") following the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War "Spanish–American War").

## Catholic Monarchs and origins of the empire

With the marriage of the heirs apparent to their respective thrones [Ferdinand of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_II_of_Aragon "Ferdinand II of Aragon") and [Isabella of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabella_I_of_Castile "Isabella I of Castile") created a [personal union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_union "Personal union") that most scholars[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] view as the foundation of the Spanish monarchy. The union of the Crowns of [Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castile "Crown of Castile") and [Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon "Crown of Aragon") joined the economic and military power of Iberia under one dynasty, the [House of Trastámara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Trast%C3%A1mara "House of Trastámara"). Their dynastic alliance was important for a number of reasons, ruling jointly over a number of kingdoms and other territories, mostly in the western Mediterranean region, under their respective legal and administrative status. They successfully pursued expansion in Iberia in the Christian conquest of the Muslim [Emirate of Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Granada "Emirate of Granada"), completed in 1492, for which Valencia-born Pope [Alexander VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_VI "Alexander VI") gave them the title of the [Catholic Monarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Monarchs "Catholic Monarchs"). Ferdinand of Aragon was particularly concerned with expansion in France and Italy, as well as conquests in North Africa.

With the [Ottoman Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire "Ottoman Empire") controlling the choke points of the overland trade from Asia and the Middle East, both Spain and Portugal sought alternative routes. The [Kingdom of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Portugal "Kingdom of Portugal") had an advantage over the [Crown of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castile "Crown of Castile"), having earlier retaken territory from the Muslims. Following Portugal's earlier completion of the reconquest and its establishment of settled boundaries, it began to seek overseas expansion, first to the port of [Ceuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceuta "Ceuta") (1415) and then by colonizing the Atlantic islands of [Madeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira "Madeira") (1418) and the [Azores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azores "Azores") (1427–1452); it also began voyages down the west coast of Africa in the fifteenth century. Its rival Castile laid claim to the [Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Islands "Canary Islands") (1402) and retook territory from the Moors in 1462. The Christian rivals Castile and Portugal came to formal agreements over the division of new territories in the [Treaty of Alcaçovas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Alca%C3%A7ovas "Treaty of Alcaçovas") (1479), as well as securing the crown of Castile for Isabella whose accession was challenged militarily by Portugal.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Following the voyage of [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus "Christopher Columbus") in 1492 and first major settlement in the [New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World "New World") in 1493, Portugal and Castile divided the world by the [Treaty of Tordesillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Tordesillas "Treaty of Tordesillas") (1494), which gave Portugal Africa and Asia, and the Western Hemisphere to Spain. The voyage of Columbus, a [Genoese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa "Genoa") mariner, obtained the support of Isabella of Castile, sailing west in 1492, seeking a route to the Indies. Columbus unexpectedly encountered the New World, populated by peoples he named "Indians". Subsequent voyages and full-scale settlements of Spaniards followed, with gold beginning to flow into Castile's coffers. Managing the expanding empire became an administrative issue. The reign of Ferdinand and Isabella began the professionalization of the apparatus of government in Spain, which led to a demand for men of letters (*letrados*) who were university graduates (*licenciados*), of [Salamanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Salamanca "University of Salamanca"), [Valladolid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Valladolid "University of Valladolid"), [Complutense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complutense_University_of_Madrid "Complutense University of Madrid") and [Alcalá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Alcal%C3%A1 "University of Alcalá"). These lawyer-bureaucrats staffed the various councils of state, eventually including the [Council of the Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_the_Indies "Council of the Indies") and [Casa de Contratación](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_de_Contrataci%C3%B3n "Casa de Contratación"), the two highest bodies in metropolitan Spain for the government of the empire in the New World, as well as royal government in the Indies.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Early expansion: Canary Islands

Portugal obtained several [papal bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_bull "Papal bull") that acknowledged Portuguese control over the discovered territories, but Castile also obtained from the Pope the safeguard of its rights to the [Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Islands "Canary Islands") with the bulls *[Romani Pontifex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanus_Pontifex "Romanus Pontifex")* dated 6 November 1436 and *Dominatur Dominus* dated 30 April 1437. The [conquest of the Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_the_Canary_Islands "Conquest of the Canary Islands"), inhabited by [Guanche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanches "Guanches") people, began in 1402 during the reign of [Henry III of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_III_of_Castile "Henry III of Castile"), by [Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normandy "Normandy") nobleman [Jean de Béthencourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_de_B%C3%A9thencourt "Jean de Béthencourt") under a feudal agreement with the crown. The conquest was completed with the campaigns of the armies of the [Crown of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castile "Crown of Castile") between 1478 and 1496, when the islands of [Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria "Gran Canaria") (1478–1483), [La Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Palma "La Palma") (1492–1493), and [Tenerife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenerife "Tenerife") (1494–1496) were subjugated. By 1504, more than 90 percent of the native Canarians had been killed or enslaved.

### Rivalry with Portugal

The Portuguese tried in vain to keep secret their discovery of the [Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Gold_Coast "Portuguese Gold Coast") (1471) in the [Gulf of Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Guinea "Gulf of Guinea"), but the news quickly caused a huge gold rush. Chronicler [Pulgar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hernando_del_Pulgar "Hernando del Pulgar") wrote that the fame of the treasures of Guinea "spread around the ports of [Andalusia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andalusia "Andalusia") in such way that everybody tried to go there". Worthless trinkets, Moorish textiles, and above all, shells from the Canary and [Cape Verde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verde "Cape Verde") islands were exchanged for gold, slaves, ivory and Guinea pepper.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The [War of the Castilian Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Castilian_Succession "War of the Castilian Succession") (1475–79) provided the Catholic Monarchs with the opportunity not only to attack the main source of the Portuguese power, but also to take possession of this lucrative commerce. The Crown officially organized this trade with Guinea: every caravel had to secure a government license and to pay a tax on one-fifth of their profits (a receiver of the customs of Guinea was established in [Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville "Seville") in 1475—the ancestor of the future and famous [Casa de Contratación](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_de_Contrataci%C3%B3n "Casa de Contratación")).

Castilian fleets fought in the Atlantic Ocean, temporarily occupying the [Cape Verde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verde "Cape Verde") islands (1476), conquering the city of [Ceuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceuta "Ceuta") in the [Tingitan Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tingitan_Peninsula "Tingitan Peninsula") in 1476 (but retaken by the Portuguese), and even attacked the [Azores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azores "Azores") islands, being defeated at [Praia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praia_da_Vit%C3%B3ria "Praia da Vitória"). The turning point of the war came in 1478, however, when a Castilian fleet sent by King Ferdinand to conquer [Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria "Gran Canaria") lost men and ships to the Portuguese who expelled the attack, and a large Castilian armada—full of gold—was entirely captured in the decisive [Battle of Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Guinea "Battle of Guinea").

The [Treaty of Alcáçovas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Alc%C3%A1%C3%A7ovas "Treaty of Alcáçovas") (4 September 1479), while assuring the Castilian throne to the Catholic Monarchs, reflected the Castilian naval and colonial defeat: "War with Castile broke out waged savagely in the Gulf [of Guinea] until the Castilian fleet of thirty-five sail was defeated there in 1478. As a result of this naval victory, at the Treaty of Alcáçovas in 1479 Castile, while retaining her rights in the Canaries, recognized the Portuguese monopoly of fishing and navigation along the whole west African coast and Portugal's rights over the [Madeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira "Madeira"), Azores and Cape Verde islands [plus the right to conquer the [Kingdom of Fez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Fez "Kingdom of Fez") ]." The treaty delimited the [spheres of influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spheres_of_influence "Spheres of influence") of the two countries, establishing the principle of the [Mare clausum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mare_clausum "Mare clausum"). It was confirmed in 1481 by the [Pope Sixtus IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Sixtus_IV "Pope Sixtus IV"), in the papal bull [*Æterni regis*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeterni_regis "Aeterni regis") (dated on 21 June 1481).

However, this experience would prove to be profitable for future Spanish overseas expansion, because as the Spaniards were excluded from the lands discovered or to be discovered from the Canaries southward—and consequently from the [road to India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Portuguese_discovery_of_the_sea_route_to_India "The Portuguese discovery of the sea route to India") around Africa—they sponsored the voyage of Columbus towards the west (1492) in search of Asia to trade in its [spices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spices "Spices"), encountering [the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americas "Americas") instead. Thus, the limitations imposed by the Alcáçovas treaty were overcome and a new and more balanced division of the world would be reached in the [Treaty of Tordesillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Tordesillas "Treaty of Tordesillas") between both emerging maritime powers.

### New World voyages and Treaty of Tordesillas

Seven months before the treaty of Alcaçovas, King [John II of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_II_of_Aragon "John II of Aragon") died, and his son [Ferdinand II of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_II_of_Aragon "Ferdinand II of Aragon"), married to [Isabella I of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabella_I_of_Castile "Isabella I of Castile"), inherited the thrones of the [Crown of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon "Crown of Aragon"). The two became known as the [Catholic Monarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Monarchs "Catholic Monarchs"), with their marriage a [personal union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_union "Personal union") that created a relationship between the Crown of Aragon and Castile, each with their own administrations, but ruled jointly by the two monarchs.

Ferdinand and Isabella defeated the last Muslim king out of Granada in 1492 after a [ten-year war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granada_War "Granada War"). The Catholic Monarchs then negotiated with [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus "Christopher Columbus"), a [Genoese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa "Genoa") sailor attempting to reach [Cipangu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cipangu "Cipangu") (Japan) by sailing west. Castile was already engaged in a [race of exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Exploration "Age of Exploration") with Portugal to reach the Far East by sea when Columbus made his bold proposal to Isabella. In the [Capitulations of Santa Fe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitulations_of_Santa_Fe "Capitulations of Santa Fe"), dated on 17 April 1492, Christopher Columbus obtained from the Catholic Monarchs his appointment as viceroy and governor in the lands *already discovered* and that he might discover thenceforth; thereby, it was the first document to establish an administrative organization in the Indies. Columbus' discoveries began the [Spanish colonization of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_colonization_of_the_Americas "Spanish colonization of the Americas"). Spain's claim to these lands was solidified by the *[Inter caetera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_caetera "Inter caetera")* [papal bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_bull "Papal bull") dated 4 May 1493, and *[Dudum siquidem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudum_siquidem "Dudum siquidem")* on 26 September 1493.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Since the Portuguese wanted to keep the line of demarcation of Alcaçovas running east and west along a latitude south of [Cape Bojador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Bojador "Cape Bojador"), a compromise was worked out and incorporated in the [Treaty of Tordesillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Tordesillas "Treaty of Tordesillas"), dated on 7 June 1494, in which the world was split into two dividing Spanish and Portuguese claims. These actions gave Spain exclusive rights to establish colonies in all of the New World from north to south (later with the exception of Brazil, which Portuguese commander [Pedro Álvares Cabral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_%C3%81lvares_Cabral "Pedro Álvares Cabral") encountered in 1500), as well as the easternmost parts of Asia. The Treaty of Tordesillas was confirmed by [Pope Julius II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Julius_II "Pope Julius II") in the bull *[Ea quae pro bono pacis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ea_quae_pro_bono_pacis "Ea quae pro bono pacis")* on 24 January 1506.

The Treaty of Tordesillas and the treaty of Cintra (18 September 1509) established the limits of the Kingdom of Fez for Portugal, and the Castilian expansion was allowed outside these limits, beginning with the [conquest of Melilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_Melilla "Conquest of Melilla") in 1497. Other European powers did not see the treaty between Castile and Portugal as binding on themselves. [Francis I of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_I_of_France "Francis I of France") observed "The sun shines for me as for others and I should very much like to see the clause in Adam's will that excludes *me* from a share of the world."

### First settlements in the Americas

Spanish settlement in the New World was based on a pattern of a large, permanent settlements with the entire complex of institutions and material life to replicate Castilian life in a different venue. Columbus's second voyage in 1493 had a large contingent of settlers and goods to accomplish that. On Hispaniola, the city of [Santo Domingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciudad_Colonial_(Santo_Domingo) "Ciudad Colonial (Santo Domingo)") was founded in 1496 by Christopher Columbus's brother [Bartholomew Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartholomew_Columbus "Bartholomew Columbus") and became a stone-built, permanent city. Non-Castilians, such as [Catalans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalans "Catalans") and [Aragonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aragonese_people "Aragonese people"), were often prohibited from migrating to the New World.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Following the settlement of Hispaniola, Europeans began searching elsewhere to begin new settlements, since there was little apparent wealth and the numbers of indigenous were declining due to the [Taíno genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ta%C3%ADno_genocide "Taíno genocide"). Those from the less prosperous Hispaniola were eager to search for new success in a new settlement. From there [Juan Ponce de León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Ponce_de_Le%C3%B3n "Juan Ponce de León") conquered [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico "Puerto Rico") (1508) and [Diego Velázquez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez_de_Cu%C3%A9llar "Diego Velázquez de Cuéllar") took [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba "Cuba"). The Spanish enslaved and deported the entire [Lucayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucayan_people "Lucayan people") population of the Bahamas by around 1520, leading to their complete extinction.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Columbus encountered the mainland in 1498, and the Catholic Monarchs learned of his discovery in May 1499. The first settlement on the mainland was [Santa María la Antigua del Darién](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Mar%C3%ADa_la_Antigua_del_Dari%C3%A9n "Santa María la Antigua del Darién") in [Castilla de Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castilla_de_Oro "Castilla de Oro") (now [Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaragua "Nicaragua"), [Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rica "Costa Rica"), [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama "Panama") and [Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia "Colombia")), settled by [Vasco Núñez de Balboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_N%C3%BA%C3%B1ez_de_Balboa "Vasco Núñez de Balboa") in 1510. In 1513, Balboa crossed the [Isthmus of Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmus_of_Panama "Isthmus of Panama"), and led the first European expedition to see the Pacific Ocean from the West coast of the New World. In an action with enduring historical import, Balboa claimed the Pacific Ocean and all the lands adjoining it for the Spanish Crown.

### Navarre and struggles for Italy

The Catholic Monarchs had developed a strategy of marriages for their children to isolate their rival, France. The Spanish princesses married the heirs of Portugal, England and the [House of Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Habsburg "House of Habsburg"). Following the same strategy, the Catholic Monarchs decided to support the Aragonese house of the Kingdom of Naples against [Charles VIII of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_VIII_of_France "Charles VIII of France") in the [Italian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Wars "Italian Wars") beginning in 1494. Following Spanish victories at the Battles of [Cerignola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cerignola "Battle of Cerignola") and [Garigliano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Garigliano_(1503) "Battle of Garigliano (1503)") in 1503, France recognized Ferdinand's sovereignty over Naples through a treaty.

After the death of Queen Isabella in 1504, and her exclusion of Ferdinand from a further role in Castile, Ferdinand married [Germaine de Foix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germaine_de_Foix "Germaine de Foix") in 1505, cementing an alliance with France. Had that couple had a surviving heir, probably the [Crown of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon "Crown of Aragon") would have been split from Castile, which was inherited by Charles, Ferdinand and Isabella's grandson. Ferdinand joined the [League of Cambrai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_League_of_Cambrai "War of the League of Cambrai") against [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice "Republic of Venice") in 1508. In 1511, he became part of the [Holy League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_League_(Italian) "Catholic League (Italian)") against France, seeing a chance at taking both [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Milan "Duchy of Milan")—to which he held a dynastic claim—and [Navarre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Navarre "Kingdom of Navarre"). In 1516, France agreed to a truce that left Milan in its control and recognized Spanish control of [Upper Navarre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Navarre "Upper Navarre"), which had effectively been a Spanish protectorate following a series of treaties in 1488, 1491, 1493, and 1495.

### Campaigns in North Africa

With the Christian reconquest completed in the Iberian peninsula, Spain began trying to take territory in Muslim North Africa. It had conquered [Melilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melilla "Melilla") in 1497, and further expansionism policy in North Africa was developed during the regency of Ferdinand the Catholic in Castile, stimulated by [Cardinal Cisneros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Jim%C3%A9nez_de_Cisneros "Francisco Jiménez de Cisneros"). Several towns and outposts in the North African coast were conquered and occupied by Castile between 1505 and 1510: [Mers El Kébir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mers_El_K%C3%A9bir "Mers El Kébir"), [Peñón de Vélez de la Gomera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pe%C3%B1%C3%B3n_de_V%C3%A9lez_de_la_Gomera "Peñón de Vélez de la Gomera"), [Oran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Oran "Spanish Oran"), [Bougie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bejaia "Bejaia"), [Tripoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Tripoli "Spanish Tripoli"), and [Peñón of Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pe%C3%B1%C3%B3n_of_Algiers "Peñón of Algiers"). On the Atlantic coast, Spain took possession of the outpost of [Santa Cruz de la Mar Pequeña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_de_la_Mar_Peque%C3%B1a "Santa Cruz de la Mar Pequeña") (1476) with support from the [Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Islands "Canary Islands"), and it was retained until 1525 with the consent of the Treaty of Cintra (1509).[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

## The Spanish Habsburgs (1516–1700)

As a result of the marriage politics of the [Catholic Monarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Monarchs "Catholic Monarchs") (in Spanish, **Reyes Católicos**), their [Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Habsburg "House of Habsburg") grandson [Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor "Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor") inherited the Castilian empire in the Americas and the possessions of the Crown of Aragon in the Mediterranean (including all of [southern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Italy "Southern Italy")), lands in Germany, the [Low Countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Countries "Low Countries"), [Franche-Comté](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franche-Comt%C3%A9 "Franche-Comté"), and [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erblande "Erblande"), starting the rule of the Spanish Habsburgs. The Austrian hereditary Habsburg domains were transferred to [Ferdinand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor "Ferdinand I, Holy Roman Emperor"), brother of Holy Roman Emperor [Charles V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor "Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor"), whereas Spain and the remaining possessions were inherited by Charles's son, [Philip II of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain "Philip II of Spain"), at the abdication of the former in 1556.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The Habsburgs pursued several goals:

* Undermining the power of France and containing it in its eastern borders
* Defending Europe against [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam"), notably the [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire "Ottoman Empire") in the [Ottoman–Habsburg wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman%E2%80%93Habsburg_wars "Ottoman–Habsburg wars")
* Maintaining Habsburg [hegemony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegemony "Hegemony") in the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire "Holy Roman Empire") and defending the [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church "Roman Catholic Church") against the [Protestant Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformation "Protestant Reformation")
* Spreading (Catholic) Christianity to the unconverted indigenous of the [New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World "New World") and the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines")
* Exploiting the resources of the Americas (gold, silver, sugar) and trading with Asia ([porcelain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcelain "Porcelain"), spices, silk)
* Excluding other European powers from the possessions it claimed in the New World

"I learnt a proverb here", said a French traveler in 1603: "Everything is dear in Spain except silver". The problems caused by inflation were discussed by scholars at the [School of Salamanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Salamanca "School of Salamanca") and the *[arbitristas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitrista "Arbitrista")*. The natural resource abundance provoked a decline in entrepreneurship as profits from resource extraction are less risky. The wealthy preferred to invest their fortunes in [public debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_debt "Public debt") (*juros*). The Habsburg dynasty spent the Castilian and American riches in [wars across Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_wars_involving_Spain "List of wars involving Spain") on behalf of Habsburg interests, and declared moratoriums (bankruptcies) on their debt payments several times. These burdens led to a number of revolts across the Spanish Habsburg's domains, including their Spanish kingdoms.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Territorial expansion in the Americas

During the Habsburg rule, the Spanish Empire significantly expanded its territories in the Americas, beginning with the conquest of the [Aztec Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec_Empire "Aztec Empire"); these conquests were achieved not by the Spanish army, but by small groups of adventurers—artisans, traders, gentry, and peasants—who operated independently under the crown's [encomienda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encomienda "Encomienda") system.

Defying the opposition of [Diego Velázquez de Cuéllar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez_de_Cu%C3%A9llar "Diego Velázquez de Cuéllar"), the governor of Hispaniola, [Hernán Cortés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hern%C3%A1n_Cort%C3%A9s "Hernán Cortés") organized an expedition of 550 [conquistadors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquistadors "Conquistadors") and sailed for the coast of Mexico in March 1519. The Castilians defeated a 10,000-strong [Chontal Mayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chontal_Maya "Chontal Maya") army at [Potonchán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potonch%C3%A1n "Potonchán") on 24 March and emerged triumphant against a larger force of 40,000 [Mayans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_peoples "Maya peoples") three days later. On 2 September, 360 Castilians and 2,300 [Totonac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totonac "Totonac") Indigenous allies defeated a 20,000-strong [Tlaxcalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tlaxcalan "Tlaxcalan") army. Three days later, a 50,000-strong [Otomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otomi "Otomi")-Tlaxcalan force was defeated by Spanish arquebusier and cannon fire, and a Castilian cavalry charge. Thousands of Tlaxcalans joined the invaders against their Aztec rulers. Cortés's forces sacked the city of [Cholula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholula_(Mesoamerican_site) "Cholula (Mesoamerican site)"), massacring 6,000 inhabitants, and later entered Emperor [Moctezuma II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moctezuma_II "Moctezuma II")'s capital, [Tenochtitlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenochtitlan "Tenochtitlan"), on 8 November. Velázquez sent a force led by Pánfilo de Narváez to punish the insubordinate Cortés for his unauthorized invasion of Mexico, but they were defeated at the [Battle of Cempoala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cempoala "Battle of Cempoala") on 29 May 1520. Narváez was wounded and captured and 17 of his troops were killed; the rest joined Cortés. Meanwhile, [Pedro de Alvarado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_de_Alvarado "Pedro de Alvarado") triggered an Aztec uprising following the [massacre in the Great Temple of Tenochtitlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massacre_in_the_Great_Temple_of_Tenochtitlan "Massacre in the Great Temple of Tenochtitlan"), during which 400 Aztec nobles and 2,000 onlookers were killed. The Castilians were driven out of the Aztec capital, suffering heavy losses and losing all of their gold and guns during [La Noche Triste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Noche_Triste "La Noche Triste").[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

On 8 July 1520, at [Otumba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Otumba "Battle of Otumba"), the Castilians and their allies, without artillery or arquebusiers, repelled 100,000 Aztecs armed with obsidian-bladed clubs. In August, 500 Castilians and 40,000 Tlaxcalans conquered the hilltop town of [Tepeaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tepeaca "Tepeaca"), an Aztec ally. Most of the inhabitants were either branded on the face with the letter "G" (for guerra, the Spanish word for "war") and enslaved by the Spanish, or sacrificed and eaten by the Tlaxcalans. Cortés returned to Tenochtitlan in 1521 with a new invasion force and laid siege to the Aztec capital in May, which was suffering from a [smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox "Smallpox") epidemic that killed thousands. The new emperor, [Cuauhtémoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuauht%C3%A9moc "Cuauhtémoc"), defended Tenochtitlan with 100,000 warriors armed with slings, bows, and [obsidian clubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macuahuitl "Macuahuitl"). The first military encounter occurred after an advance along the causeway at [Tlacopan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tlacopan "Tlacopan") by the armies of Alvarado and [Cristóbal de Olid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crist%C3%B3bal_de_Olid "Cristóbal de Olid"). While fighting on the causeway, the Spanish and their allies came under attack from both sides by Aztecs firing arrows from canoes. Thirteen Spanish brigantines sank 300 out of 400 enemy war canoes sent against them. The Aztecs tried to damage the Spanish vessels by hiding spears beneath the shallow water. The attackers breached the city and engaged in fighting with the Aztec defenders in the streets.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The Aztecs defeated the Spanish-Tlaxcalan forces at the [Battle of Colhuacatonco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Colhuacatonco "Battle of Colhuacatonco") on 30 June 1521. Following this Aztec victory, 53 Spanish prisoners were paraded to the tops of [Tlatelolco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tlatelolco_(altepetl) "Tlatelolco (altepetl)")'s highest [pyramids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerican_pyramids "Mesoamerican pyramids") and publicly [sacrificed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_sacrifice_in_Aztec_culture "Human sacrifice in Aztec culture"). In late July, the attackers resumed their assaults, resulting in the massacre of 800 Aztec civilians. By 29 July, the Spanish had reached Tlatelolco's center, raising their new flag atop the city's twin towers. Having exhausted their gunpowder, they attempted a catapult breach but failed. On 3 August, 12,000 more civilians were killed in another city section. Alvarado's destruction of the aqueducts forced the Aztecs to drink from the lake, causing disease and thousands of deaths. Another major assault occurred on 12 August, during which many thousands of non-combatants were massacred in their shelters. The following day, the city fell and Cuauhtémoc was captured. At least 100,000 Aztecs died during the siege, while 100 Spaniards and up to 30,000 of their Indigenous allies were killed or died from disease.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The [fall of Tenochtitlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Tenochtitlan "Fall of Tenochtitlan") marked the beginning of Spanish colonial rule in Mexico, leading to the establishment of the [Viceroyalty of New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_New_Spain "Viceroyalty of New Spain") in 1535. Following the conquest of the Aztec Empire in 1521, Spanish conquistador Pedro de Alvarado commenced the conquest of northern Central America in 1523. By 1528, most of the major [Maya kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_civilization "Maya civilization") had been subjugated, with only the [Petén Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet%C3%A9n_Basin "Petén Basin") remaining outside Spanish control. The last independent Maya kingdoms were finally defeated in 1697 during the [Spanish conquest of Petén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_conquest_of_Pet%C3%A9n "Spanish conquest of Petén").[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

In 1532, [Francisco Pizarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Pizarro "Francisco Pizarro") conquered the [Inca Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_Empire "Inca Empire") by capturing its leader [Atahualpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atahualpa "Atahualpa") during a surprise attack in [Cajamarca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cajamarca "Battle of Cajamarca") that resulted in the massacre of thousands of Incas. This conquest facilitated the establishment of the [Viceroyalty of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_Peru "Viceroyalty of Peru") in 1542, allowing Spain to exert control over territories in western South America, comprising present-day Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador, and parts of Chile and Argentina. In southern Chile, the Spanish faced resistance from the Mapuche people for centuries ([Arauco War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arauco_War "Arauco War"), lasting from the 1540s into the 1800s).[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Reign of Philip II

Philip II of Spain (r. 1556–98) oversaw the colonization of the Philippines, which began in 1565 with the arrival of Spanish explorer [Miguel López de Legazpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_L%C3%B3pez_de_Legazpi "Miguel López de Legazpi"), making him ruler of one of the first true globe-spanning empires. His victory in the [War of the Portuguese Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Portuguese_Succession "War of the Portuguese Succession") led to the annexation of Portugal in 1580, effectively integrating its overseas empire—encompassing coastal Brazil, the [Persian Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf#Colonial_era "Persian Gulf") area, and [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Africa_(disambiguation) "Portuguese Africa (disambiguation)") and [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_India "Portuguese India") coastal enclaves—into Spain's domain. Philip II also reaffirmed Spanish control over the [Kingdom of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples "Kingdom of Naples"), the [Kingdom of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sicily "Kingdom of Sicily"), the [Kingdom of Sardinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sardinia "Kingdom of Sardinia"), and the [Duchy of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Milan "Duchy of Milan") through the [Treaty of Cateau-Cambrésis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Cateau-Cambr%C3%A9sis "Treaty of Cateau-Cambrésis") in 1559. Italy became the core of Spain's power.

### Decline

By the mid-17th century, Spain's global empire burdened its economic, administrative, and military resources. Over the preceding century, Spanish troops had fought in France, Germany, and the Netherlands, suffering heavy casualties. Despite its vast holdings, Spain's military lacked essential modernization and heavily relied on foreign suppliers. Nevertheless, Spain possessed abundant bullion from the Americas, which played a crucial role in both sustaining its military endeavors and meeting the needs of its civilian population. During this period, Spain displayed limited military interest in its overseas colonies. The *Criollo* elites (colonial-born Spaniards) and *mestizo* and *mulatto* militia (of mixed Indigenous-Spanish and African-Spanish descent) provided only minimal protection, often assisted by more influential allies with vested interests in maintaining the balance of power and safeguarding the Spanish Empire from falling into enemy hands.

## The Spanish Bourbons (1700–1833)

With the 1700 death of the childless [Charles II of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_Spain "Charles II of Spain"), the crown of Spain was contested in the [War of the Spanish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession "War of the Spanish Succession").
Under the [Treaties of Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaties_of_Utrecht "Treaties of Utrecht") (11 April 1713) ending the war, the French prince of the [House of Bourbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Bourbon "House of Bourbon"), Philippe of Anjou, grandchild of [Louis XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIV "Louis XIV") of France, became King [Philip V of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_V_of_Spain "Philip V of Spain"). He retained the Spanish overseas empire in the Americas and the Philippines. The settlement gave spoils to those who had backed a Habsburg for the Spanish monarchy, ceding European territory of the [Spanish Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Netherlands "Spanish Netherlands"), [Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples "Kingdom of Naples"), [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Milan "Duchy of Milan"), and [Sardinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sardinia "Kingdom of Sardinia") to [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy "Habsburg monarchy"); [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sicily "Kingdom of Sicily") and parts of Milan to the [Duchy of Savoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Savoy "Duchy of Savoy"), and [Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar "Gibraltar") and [Menorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menorca "Menorca") to the [Kingdom of Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain "Kingdom of Great Britain"). The treaty also granted British merchants the exclusive right to sell [slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_slave_trade "Atlantic slave trade") in [Spanish America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_America "Spanish America") for thirty years, the *[asiento de negros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asiento_de_negros "Asiento de negros")*, as well as licensed voyages to ports in Spanish colonial dominions and openings.

Spain's economic and demographic recovery had begun slowly in the last decades of the Habsburg reign, as was evident from the growth of its trading convoys and the much more rapid growth of illicit trade during the period. (This growth was slower than the growth of illicit trade by northern rivals in the empire's markets.) However, this recovery was not then translated into institutional improvement, rather the "proximate solutions to permanent problems". This legacy of neglect was reflected in the early years of Bourbon rule in which the military was ill-advisedly pitched into battle in the [War of the Quadruple Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Quadruple_Alliance "War of the Quadruple Alliance") (1718–20). Spain was defeated in Italy by an alliance of Britain, France, Savoy, and Austria. Following the war, the new Bourbon monarchy took a much more cautious approach to international relations, relying on a family alliance with Bourbon France, and continuing to follow a program of institutional renewal.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The crown program to enact reforms that promoted administrative control and efficiency in the metropole to the detriment of interests in the colonies, undermined creole elites' loyalty to the crown. When French forces of [Napoleon Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_Bonaparte "Napoleon Bonaparte") [invaded the Iberian peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_War "Peninsular War") in 1808, Napoleon ousted the Spanish Bourbon monarchy, placing his brother [Joseph Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Bonaparte "Joseph Bonaparte") on the Spanish throne. There was a crisis of legitimacy of crown rule in Spanish America, leading to the [Spanish American wars of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_American_wars_of_independence "Spanish American wars of independence") (1808–1826).[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Bourbon reforms

The Spanish Bourbons' broadest intentions were to reorganize the institutions of empire to better administer it for the benefit of Spain and the crown. It sought to increase revenues and to assert greater crown control, including over the Catholic Church. Centralization of power (beginning with the [Nueva Planta decrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Planta_decrees "Nueva Planta decrees") against the realms of the [Crown of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon "Crown of Aragon")) was to be for the benefit of the crown and the metropole and for the defense of its empire against foreign incursions. From the viewpoint of Spain, the structures of colonial rule under the Habsburgs were no longer functioning to the benefit of Spain, with much wealth being retained in Spanish America and going to other European powers. The presence of other European powers in the Caribbean, with the English in [Barbados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbados "Barbados") (1627), [St Kitts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Kitts "St Kitts") (1623–25), and [Jamaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica "Jamaica") (1655); the Dutch in [Curaçao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cura%C3%A7ao "Curaçao"), and the French in Saint Domingue (Haiti) (1697), [Martinique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinique "Martinique"), and [Guadeloupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadeloupe "Guadeloupe") had broken the integrity of the closed Spanish mercantile system and established thriving sugar colonies.

At the beginning of his reign, the first Spanish Bourbon, King Philip V, reorganized the government to strengthen the executive power of the monarch as was done in France, in place of the deliberative, [Polysynodial System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polysynodial_System "Polysynodial System") of Councils.

Philip's government set up a ministry of the Navy and the Indies (1714) and established commercial companies, the [Honduras Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Honduras "History of Honduras") (1714), a Caracas company; the [Guipuzcoana Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guipuzcoana_Company "Guipuzcoana Company") (1728), and the most successful ones, the [Havana Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Cuba "History of Cuba") (1740) and the [Barcelona Trading Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_Trading_Company "Barcelona Trading Company") (1755).[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

In 1717–18, the structures for governing the Indies, the **[Consejo de Indias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consejo_de_Indias "Consejo de Indias")** and the **[Casa de Contratación](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_de_Contrataci%C3%B3n "Casa de Contratación")**, which governed investments in the cumbersome [Spanish treasure fleets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_treasure_fleet "Spanish treasure fleet"), were transferred from [Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville "Seville") to [Cádiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A1diz "Cádiz"), where foreign merchant houses had easier access to the Indies trade. Cádiz became the one port for all Indies trading (see [flota system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flota_system "Flota system")). Individual sailings at regular intervals were slow to displace the traditional armed convoys, but by the 1760s there were regular ships plying the Atlantic from Cádiz to [Havana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havana "Havana") and [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico "Puerto Rico"), and at longer intervals to the [Río de la Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADo_de_la_Plata "Río de la Plata"), where an additional [viceroyalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_the_R%C3%ADo_de_la_Plata "Viceroyalty of the Río de la Plata") was created in 1776. The contraband trade that was the lifeblood of the Habsburg empire declined in proportion to registered shipping (a shipping registry having been established in 1735).[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Two upheavals registered unease within Spanish America and at the same time demonstrated the renewed resiliency of the reformed system: the [Tupac Amaru uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BApac_Amaru_II "Túpac Amaru II") in Peru in 1780 and the [rebellion of the *comuneros*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolt_of_the_Comuneros_(New_Granada) "Revolt of the Comuneros (New Granada)") of [New Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_New_Granada "Viceroyalty of New Granada"), both in part reactions to tighter, more efficient control.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### 18th-century economic conditions

The 18th century was a century of prosperity for the overseas Spanish Empire as trade within grew steadily, particularly in the second half of the century, under the Bourbon reforms. Spain's victory in the [Battle of Cartagena de Indias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cartagena_de_Indias "Battle of Cartagena de Indias") against a British expedition in the Caribbean port of [Cartagena de Indias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartagena_de_Indias "Cartagena de Indias") helped Spain secure its dominance of its possessions in the Americas until the 19th century. But different regions fared differently under Bourbon rule, and even while New Spain was particularly prosperous, it was also marked by steep wealth inequality. Silver production boomed in New Spain during the 18th century, with output more than tripling between the start of the century and the 1750s. The economy and the population both grew, both centered around Mexico City. But while mine owners and the crown benefited from the flourishing silver economy, most of the population in the rural Bajío faced rising land prices, falling wages. Eviction of many from their lands resulted.

With a Bourbon monarchy came a repertory of Bourbon [mercantilist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercantilism "Mercantilism") ideas based on a centralized state, put into effect in the Americas slowly at first but with increasing momentum during the century. Shipping grew rapidly from the mid-1740s until the [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years%27_War "Seven Years' War") (1756–63), reflecting in part the success of the Bourbons in bringing illicit trade under control. With the loosening of trade controls after the Seven Years' War, shipping trade within the empire once again began to expand, reaching an extraordinary rate of growth in the 1780s.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The end of Cádiz's monopoly of trade with the American colonies brought about very important changes, particularly a rebirth of Spanish manufactures. Most notable of those changes were both the beginning of [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalonia "Catalonia") participation in the Spanish [slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transatlantic_Slave_Trade "Transatlantic Slave Trade"), and the rapidly growing textile industry of Catalonia which by the mid-1780s saw the first signs of industrialization. This saw the emergence of a small, politically active commercial class in [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona "Barcelona"). This isolated pocket of advanced economic development stood in stark contrast to the relative backwardness of most of the country. Most of the improvements were in and around some major coastal cities and the major islands such as Cuba, with its tobacco [plantations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantation "Plantation"), and a renewed growth of [precious metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precious_metal "Precious metal") mining in South America.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Agricultural productivity remained low despite efforts to introduce new techniques to what was for the most part an uninterested, exploited peasant and laboring groups. Governments were inconsistent in their policies. Though there were substantial improvements by the late 18th century, Spain was still an economic backwater. Under the [mercantile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercantilism "Mercantilism") trading arrangements it had difficulty in providing the goods being demanded by the strongly growing markets of its empire, and providing adequate outlets for the return trade.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

From an opposing point of view according to the "backwardness" mentioned above the naturalist and explorer [Alexander von Humboldt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Humboldt "Alexander von Humboldt") traveled extensively throughout the Spanish Americas, exploring and describing it for the first time from a modern scientific point of view between 1799 and 1804. In his work *Political essay on the kingdom of New Spain containing researches relative to the geography of Mexico* he says that the Amerindians of [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_New_Spain "Viceroyalty of New Spain") were wealthier than any Russian or German peasant in Europe. According to Humboldt, despite the fact that Indian farmers were poor, under Spanish rule they were free and slavery was non-existent, their conditions were much better than any other peasant or farmer in [northern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Europe "Northern Europe").

Humboldt also published a comparative analysis of bread and meat consumption in New Spain compared to other cities in Europe such as Paris. [Mexico City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico_City "Mexico City") consumed 189 pounds of meat per person per year, in comparison to 163 pounds consumed by the inhabitants of Paris, the Mexicans also consumed almost the same amount of bread as any European city, with 363 kilograms of bread per person per year in comparison to the 377 kilograms consumed in Paris. [Caracas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracas "Caracas") consumed seven times more meat per person than in Paris. Von Humboldt also said that the average income in that period was four times the European income and also that the cities of New Spain were richer than many European cities.

### Contesting with other empires

Bourbon institutional reforms under Philip V bore fruit militarily when Spanish forces easily retook [Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples "Kingdom of Naples") and [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sicily "Kingdom of Sicily") from the Austrians at the [Battle of Bitonto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bitonto "Battle of Bitonto") in 1734 during the [War of the Polish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Polish_Succession "War of the Polish Succession"), and during the [War of Jenkins' Ear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_Jenkins%27_Ear "War of Jenkins' Ear") (1739–42) thwarted [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire") efforts to capture the strategic cities of [Cartagena de Indias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cartagena_de_Indias "Battle of Cartagena de Indias"), [Santiago de Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Cuba_(1741) "Invasion of Cuba (1741)") and [St. Augustine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_St._Augustine_(1740) "Siege of St. Augustine (1740)"), although Spain's [invasion of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Georgia_(1742) "Invasion of Georgia (1742)") also ended in failure. The British suffered approximately 20,000 killed or wounded during the war while the Spanish suffered roughly 10,000.

In 1742, the War of Jenkins' Ear merged with the larger [War of the Austrian Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Austrian_Succession "War of the Austrian Succession"), and [King George's War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_George%27s_War "King George's War") in North America. The British, also occupied with France, were unable to capture Spanish treasure convoys, while Spanish [privateers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privateer "Privateer") targeted British merchant shipping along the [Triangle Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangle_Trade "Triangle Trade") routes and [attacked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Alarm "Spanish Alarm") the coast of [North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_North_Carolina "Province of North Carolina"), levying [tribute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribute "Tribute") on the inhabitants. In Europe, Spain had been trying to divest [Maria Theresa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Theresa "Maria Theresa") of the Duchy of Milan in northern Italy since 1741, but faced the opposition of [Charles Emmanuel III of Sardinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Emmanuel_III_of_Sardinia "Charles Emmanuel III of Sardinia"), and warfare in northern Italy remained indecisive throughout the period up to 1746. By the 1748 [Treaty of Aix-la-Chappelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Aix-la-Chapelle_(1748) "Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle (1748)"), Spain gained (indirectly) [Parma, Piacenza, and Guastalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Parma_and_Piacenza "Duchy of Parma and Piacenza") in northern Italy.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Spain was defeated during the [invasion of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_invasion_of_Portugal_(1762) "Spanish invasion of Portugal (1762)") and lost both [Havana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Havana "Siege of Havana") and [Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Manila_(1762) "Battle of Manila (1762)") to British forces towards the end of the [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years%27_War "Seven Years' War") (1756–63). In response, the [Bourbon Reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourbon_Reforms "Bourbon Reforms") allowed for Spain to recover from these losses and Spanish forces recaptured [Menorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menorca "Menorca"), [West Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Florida "West Florida") (present-day [Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana "Louisiana"), [Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi "Mississippi"), [Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabama "Alabama") and [Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida "Florida")) and temporarily occupied [the Bahamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bahamas "The Bahamas") during the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War "American Revolutionary War") (1775–83). However, a Franco-Spanish attempt to [capture Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Siege_of_Gibraltar "Great Siege of Gibraltar") ended in failure.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

During most of the 18th century, Spanish privateers, particularly from [Santo Domingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santo_Domingo "Santo Domingo"), were the scourge of the [Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antilles "Antilles"), with Dutch, British, French and Danish vessels as their [prizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prize_(law) "Prize (law)").

#### Rival empires in the Pacific Northwest

Spain claimed all of North America in the Age of Discovery, but claims were not translated into occupation until a major resource was discovered and Spanish settlement and crown rule put in place. The French had established an [empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_America "French America") in northern North America and took some islands in the Caribbean. The English established colonies on the eastern seaboard of North America and in northern North America and some Caribbean islands as well. In the eighteenth century, the Spanish crown realized that its territorial claims needed to be defended, particularly in the wake of its visible weakness during the Seven Years' War when Britain captured the important Spanish ports of Havana and Manila. Another important factor was that the Russian Empire had [expanded into North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_America "Russian America") from the mid-eighteenth century, with [fur trading settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_fur_trade "Maritime fur trade") in what is now [Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska "Alaska") and forts as far south as [Fort Ross, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Ross,_California "Fort Ross, California"). Great Britain was also expanding into areas that Spain claimed as its territory on the Pacific coast. Taking steps to shore up its fragile claims to California, Spain began planning [California missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_missions_in_California "Spanish missions in California") in 1769. Spain also began a series of voyages to the Pacific Northwest, where Russia and Great Britain were encroaching on claimed territory. The [Spanish expeditions to the Pacific Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_expeditions_to_the_Pacific_Northwest "Spanish expeditions to the Pacific Northwest"), with [Alessandro Malaspina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Malaspina "Alessandro Malaspina") and others sailing for Spain, came too late for Spain to assert its sovereignty in the Pacific Northwest.

The [Nootka Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nootka_Crisis "Nootka Crisis") (1789–1791) nearly brought Spain and Britain to war. It was a dispute over claims in the Pacific Northwest, where neither nation had established permanent settlements. The crisis could have led to war, but without French support Spain capitulated to British terms and negotiations took place with the [Nootka Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nootka_Convention "Nootka Convention"). Spain and Great Britain agreed to not establish settlements and allowed free access to Nootka Sound on the west coast of what is now [Vancouver Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Island "Vancouver Island"). Nevertheless, the outcome of the crisis was a humiliation for Spain and a triumph for Britain, as Spain had practically renounced all sovereignty on the North Pacific coast.

In 1806, Baron [Nikolai Rezanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Rezanov "Nikolai Rezanov") attempted to negotiate a treaty between the [Russian-American Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian-American_Company "Russian-American Company") and the [Viceroyalty of New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_New_Spain "Viceroyalty of New Spain"), but his unexpected death in 1807 ended any treaty hopes. Spain gave up its claims in the West of North America in the [Adams-Onis Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adams-Onis_Treaty "Adams-Onis Treaty") of 1819, ceding its rights there to the United States, allowing the U.S. to purchase Florida, and establishing a boundary between New Spain and the U.S. When the negotiations between the two nations were taking place, Spain's resources were stretched due to the [Spanish American wars of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_American_wars_of_independence "Spanish American wars of independence"). Much of the present-day [American Southwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Southwest "American Southwest") later became part of Mexico after its independence from Spain; after the [Mexican–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican%E2%80%93American_War "Mexican–American War"), Mexico ceded to the U.S. present-day [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California "California"), [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas "Texas"), [New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mexico "New Mexico"), [Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah "Utah"), [Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada "Nevada"), [Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona "Arizona"), and parts of [Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado "Colorado"), [Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma "Oklahoma"), [Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas "Kansas"), [Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska "Nebraska") and [Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming "Wyoming") for $15 million.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

#### Loss of Spanish Louisiana

The growth of trade and wealth in the colonies caused increasing political tensions as frustration grew with the improving but still restrictive trade with Spain. [Alessandro Malaspina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Malaspina "Alessandro Malaspina")'s recommendation to turn the empire into a looser [confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation "Confederation") to help improve governance and trade so as to quell the growing political tensions between the élites of the empire's periphery and center was suppressed by a monarchy afraid of losing control. All was to be swept away by the tumult that was to overtake Europe at the turn of the 19th century with the [French Revolutionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolutionary_Wars "French Revolutionary Wars") and [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars "Napoleonic Wars").[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The first major territory Spain was to lose in the 19th century was the vast [Louisiana Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_(New_Spain) "Louisiana (New Spain)"), which had few European settlers. It stretched north to Canada and was ceded by France in 1763 under the terms of the [Treaty of Fontainebleau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Fontainebleau_(1762) "Treaty of Fontainebleau (1762)"). The French, under Napoleon, took back possession as part of the [Treaty of San Ildefonso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Treaty_of_San_Ildefonso "Third Treaty of San Ildefonso") in 1800 and sold it to the United States in the [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase "Louisiana Purchase") of 1803. Napoleon's sale of the Louisiana Territory to the United States in 1803 caused border disputes between the United States and Spain that, with rebellions in [West Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Florida "West Florida") (1810) and in the remainder of Louisiana at the mouth of the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River "Mississippi River"), led to their eventual cession to the United States.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Spanish American Wars of Independence

In 1808, [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon "Napoleon") took the Spanish Crown under his control. This action sparked resistance from the Spanish people, leading to the [Peninsular War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_War "Peninsular War"). This conflict created a power vacuum, a transition to liberalism, followed by civil wars, and eventually the establishment of republics in Spanish America. The breakup of Spanish America into around twenty new independent states was one of the most important events in the shaping of the modern world.

By the late 18th century, the Spanish Empire had passed from the Habsburg to the [Bourbon dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Bourbon "House of Bourbon") without major loss of territory. In 1808, however, Napoleon kidnapped the Spanish royal family as part of his [Continental System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_System "Continental System") strategy. He forced the [Abdications of Bayonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdications_of_Bayonne "Abdications of Bayonne"): [Charles IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_IV_of_Spain "Charles IV of Spain") was removed from the throne, [Ferdinand VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_VII "Ferdinand VII") was taken prisoner, and Napoleon's brother, [Joseph Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Bonaparte "Joseph Bonaparte"), was made king, in an attempt to replace the Bourbon dynasty with the [House of Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Bonaparte "House of Bonaparte"). This led to the [Peninsular War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_War "Peninsular War") (1808–1814). Britain, through the [Apodaca–Canning treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apodaca%E2%80%93Canning_treaty "Apodaca–Canning treaty"), supported the Spanish liberal resistance and recognized Ferdinand VII as the rightful king, even in his absence. Rebel Spanish juntas set up a liberal state through the [Cortes of Cádiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cortes_of_C%C3%A1diz "Cortes of Cádiz").
Conflict between Spanish and American Juntas: In America, Napoleon's envoys were turned away, and officials suspected of loyalty to Joseph I were removed from their posts. In America, local juntas began their own revolutions. The final break came in 1810, after a long and destructive civil war: the [Supreme Central Junta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Central_Junta "Supreme Central Junta") was defeated, it dissolved, giving way first to a Regency and then to the Cortes of Cádiz, dominated by peninsular Spaniards. These Cortes refused to recognize the American juntas and sent military expeditions to put down their rebellions. At the same time, neither the American juntas nor the Cortes of Cádiz accepted Ferdinand VII as an authority above their own national interests. Britain, the United States, and Portugal all took advantage of the resulting power vacuum to expand their influence in the Americas.

The Return of Ferdinand VII (1814): After France's defeat in Europe and the [Treaty of Valençay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Valen%C3%A7ay "Treaty of Valençay"), Ferdinand VII returned to Spain as an absolute king. He rejected the [Constitution of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_1812 "Constitution of 1812") and, together with the Cortes of Cádiz, declared the American juntas to be in open rebellion. Once absolutism was restored and Spain's liberal government collapsed, Britain cut off European support for Spain and instead [backed the American rebels with troops, ships, and supplies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_intervention_in_Spanish_American_independence "British intervention in Spanish American independence"). Most of the uprisings turned into republics, which fought openly against the restored absolutist rule. Viceroys [Félix Calleja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Calleja "Félix Calleja") (New Spain), [Fernando de Abascal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Fernando_de_Abascal_y_Sousa "José Fernando de Abascal y Sousa") (Peru), and [Pablo Morillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Morillo "Pablo Morillo") (Costa Firme) led the royalist defense. Throughout the 15 years of fighting, Spain sent around 10,000 troops to Mexico. 20,000 Spanish soldiers were sent to South America. However, disease and combat claimed the lives of two-thirds of these soldiers. Even within the Viceroyalty of Peru, the center of Spanish power in South America, the majority of the Royalist army consisted of Americans, around 80%. After the [Battle of Ayacucho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ayacucho "Battle of Ayacucho") in 1824, the captured Royalist army numbered only 751 Spaniards. Only 6,000 troops were sent to Peru and Upper Peru directly from Spain, although others arrived from neighboring theaters of operation. In 1829, Spain attempted to [reconquer Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_attempts_to_reconquer_Mexico "Spanish attempts to reconquer Mexico") with only 3,000 troops.

Despite warnings about Spain's weakened military, repeated liberal revolts inside Spain led to [Rafael del Riego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_del_Riego "Rafael del Riego")'s uprising in 1820, which brought about the short-lived [Liberal Triennium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Triennium "Liberal Triennium"). Recognized only by Britain, this government halted troop deployments and could only negotiate truces. From 1820 on, [Simón Bolívar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sim%C3%B3n_Bol%C3%ADvar "Simón Bolívar") and [José de San Martín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_de_San_Mart%C3%ADn "José de San Martín") in South America — known as the "Liberators" — and [Agustín Iturbide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agust%C3%ADn_Iturbide "Agustín Iturbide") in North America led the final campaigns of the "patriot" armies against the "royalists." That term became widespread once Ferdinand VII took back the absolute throne in 1823, backed by French troops from the [Holy Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Alliance "Holy Alliance"), who occupied Spain until 1828. The last royalist strongholds held out in coastal forts until 1826, alongside scattered inland guerrillas and naval fighting in the Caribbean. These final efforts fed [Spanish hopes of reconquering Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_attempts_to_reconquer_Mexico "Spanish attempts to reconquer Mexico"), hopes that ended only with Ferdinand VII's death in 1833.

## Last territories in the Americas and the Pacific (1833–1898)

In the 1850s and 1860s, Spain engaged in colonial activities around the world, including on the west coast of South America ([Chincha Islands War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chincha_Islands_War "Chincha Islands War")), in Vietnam ([Cochinchina campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochinchina_campaign "Cochinchina campaign")), and in Mexico. In 1861, Spain [annexed Santo Domingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_annexation_of_the_Dominican_Republic "Spanish annexation of the Dominican Republic"), which had been independent from [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Spanish_Haiti "Republic of Spanish Haiti") since 1821 and from [Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_occupation_of_Santo_Domingo "Haitian occupation of Santo Domingo") since 1844. This led to the outbreak of a guerrilla war in 1863, during which Spanish forces were debilitated by disease and deprived of supplies and weapons as insurgents intercepted mule [supply trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train_(military) "Train (military)"), captured arms depots containing rifles, cannons, and ammunition, and burned towns they could not hold to deny the Spanish access to provisions. By the time Spain withdrew from Santo Domingo in 1865, it had spent over 33 million pesos and suffered over 10,000 combat casualties, with a total of 18,000 Spanish soldiers dead from all causes. Dominicans who had sided with Spain relocated to Cuba, where they later played a key role in helping Cuban rebels defeat Spanish detachments and gain control of much of eastern Cuba during the [Ten Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Years%27_War "Ten Years' War"). In contrast, Puerto Rican rebels had no military tactics, and the [Lares rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grito_de_Lares "Grito de Lares") of 1868 was quickly suppressed.

In Cuba, the First War for Independence was fought from 1868 to 1878, resulting in between 100,000 and 150,000 Cuban deaths. The [Second War for Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_War_of_Independence "Cuban War of Independence") occurred between 1895 and 1898, during which approximately 300,000 Cubans died, with around 200,000 civilian deaths attributed to disease and famine caused by [Spanish concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconcentration_policy "Reconcentration policy"). Two contemporary sources estimated that by December 1895, the rebel army had lost between 29,850 and 42,800 men, and many Cuban generals were killed in combat.

American sympathy for Cuban revolutionaries grew due to reports of atrocities and the sinking of [USS *Maine*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Maine_(1889) "USS Maine (1889)"). On 25 April 1898, the U.S. declared war on Spain, marking the start of the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War "Spanish–American War"). The destruction of Spain's Pacific and Caribbean fleets at [Manila Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Manila_Bay "Battle of Manila Bay") and [Santiago de Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Santiago_de_Cuba "Battle of Santiago de Cuba") severed supply lines, leading to the surrender of Spanish garrisons in the Philippines, Cuba, and Puerto Rico, the last two of which were the [remaining territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonization_of_the_Americas#Timeline "Decolonization of the Americas") of the empire in the [Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americas "Americas"). The war ended with the [Treaty of Paris (1898)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1898) "Treaty of Paris (1898)"), which ceded [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Military_Government_in_Cuba "United States Military Government in Cuba"), [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico "Puerto Rico"), and [Guam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guam "Guam") to the U.S. and sold the Philippines for US$20 million. The following year, Spain then sold its remaining Pacific Ocean possessions to Germany in the [German–Spanish Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German%E2%80%93Spanish_Treaty_(1899) "German–Spanish Treaty (1899)"), retaining only its African territories. On 2 June 1899, the second expeditionary battalion [*Cazadores*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_infantry "Light infantry") of Philippines, the last Spanish garrison in the Philippines, which had been [besieged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Baler "Siege of Baler") in [Baler, Aurora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baler,_Aurora "Baler, Aurora") at war's end, was pulled out, effectively ending around 300 years of Spanish hegemony in the archipelago.

## Territories in Africa (1885–1976)

By the end of the 17th century, only Melilla, [Alhucemas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alhucemas_Islands "Alhucemas Islands"), [Peñón de Vélez de la Gomera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pe%C3%B1%C3%B3n_de_V%C3%A9lez_de_la_Gomera "Peñón de Vélez de la Gomera") (which had been taken again in 1564), and [Ceuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceuta "Ceuta") (part of the [Portuguese Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire "Portuguese Empire") since 1415, chose to retain their links to Spain once the [Iberian Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Union "Iberian Union") ended. The formal allegiance of Ceuta to Spain was recognized by the [Treaty of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lisbon_(1668) "Treaty of Lisbon (1668)") in 1668), and Oran and [Mers El Kébir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mers_El_K%C3%A9bir "Mers El Kébir") remained Spanish territories in Africa. The latter cities were lost in 1708, [reconquered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_conquest_of_Oran_(1732) "Spanish conquest of Oran (1732)") in 1732 and sold by [Charles IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_IV_of_Spain "Charles IV of Spain") in 1792.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

In 1778, [Fernando Pó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioko "Bioko") (now Bioko), adjacent islets, and commercial rights to the mainland between the [Niger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niger_river "Niger river") and [Ogooué](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogoou%C3%A9 "Ogooué") rivers were ceded to Spain by the Portuguese in exchange for territory in South America ([Treaty of El Pardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_El_Pardo_(1778) "Treaty of El Pardo (1778)")). In the 19th century, some Spanish explorers and missionaries would cross this zone, among them [Manuel Iradier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Iradier "Manuel Iradier"). In 1848, Spanish troops occupied the uninhabited [Chafarinas Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chafarinas_Islands "Chafarinas Islands"), anticipating a French move on the rocks located off the North-African coast.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

In 1860, after the [Tetuan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish-Moroccan_War_(1859) "Spanish-Moroccan War (1859)"), [Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco "Morocco") paid Spain 100 million pesetas as [war reparations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_reparations "War reparations") and ceded [Sidi Ifni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidi_Ifni "Sidi Ifni") to Spain as a part of the [Treaty of Tangiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Tangiers "Treaty of Tangiers"), on the basis of the old outpost of Santa Cruz de la Mar Pequeña, thought to be Sidi Ifni. The following decades of Franco-Spanish collaboration resulted in the establishment and extension of Spanish protectorates south of the city, and Spanish influence obtained international recognition in the [Berlin Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Conference "Berlin Conference") of 1884: Spain administered Sidi Ifni and [Spanish Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Sahara "Spanish Sahara") jointly. Spain claimed a [protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate "Protectorate") over the coast of [Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Guinea "Spanish Guinea") from [Cape Bojador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Bojador "Cape Bojador") to [Cap Blanc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ras_Nouadhibou "Ras Nouadhibou"), too, and even try to press a claim over the [Adrar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrar_(region) "Adrar (region)") and [Tiris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiris_Zemmour_Region "Tiris Zemmour Region") regions in [Mauritania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritania "Mauritania"). [Río Muni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADo_Muni "Río Muni") became a protectorate in 1885 and a colony in 1900. Conflicting claims to the Guinea mainland were settled in 1900 by the [Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1900) "Treaty of Paris (1900)"), because of which Spain was left with a mere 26,000 km2 out of the 300,000 stretching east to the [Ubangi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubangi_River "Ubangi River") which they initially claimed.

Following a [brief war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rif_War_(1893) "Rif War (1893)") in 1893, Morocco paid war reparations of 20 million pesetas and Spain expanded its influence south from Melilla. In 1912, Morocco was [divided between the French and Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_conquest_of_Morocco "French conquest of Morocco"). The [Riffians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riffians "Riffians") rebelled, led by [Abdelkrim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ibn_%27Abd_al-Karim_al-Khattabi "Muhammad Ibn 'Abd al-Karim al-Khattabi"), a former officer for the Spanish administration. The [Battle of Annual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Annual "Battle of Annual") (1921) during the [Rif War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rif_War "Rif War") was a major military defeat suffered by the Spanish army against Moroccan insurgents. A leading Spanish politician emphatically declared: "*We are at the most acute period of Spanish decadence*". After the disaster of Annual, Spain began [using German chemical weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_use_of_chemical_weapons_in_the_Rif_War "Spanish use of chemical weapons in the Rif War") against the Moroccans. In September 1925, the [Alhucemas landing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alhucemas_landing "Alhucemas landing") by the Spanish Army and Navy with a small collaboration of an allied French contingent put an end to the Rif War. It is considered the first successful amphibious landing in history supported by seaborne air power and tanks.

In 1923, [Tangier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangier_International_Zone "Tangier International Zone") was declared an international city under French, Spanish, British, and later Italian [joint administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condominium_(international_law) "Condominium (international law)"). In 1926, Bioko and Rio Muni were united as the colony of [Spanish Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Guinea "Spanish Guinea"), a status that would last until 1959. In 1931, following the fall of the monarchy, the African colonies became part of the [Second Spanish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic "Second Spanish Republic"). In 1934, during the government of Prime Minister [Alejandro Lerroux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Lerroux "Alejandro Lerroux"), Spanish troops led by General Osvaldo Capaz landed in Sidi Ifni and carried out the occupation of the territory, ceded *[de jure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_jure "De jure")* by Morocco in 1860. Two years later, [Francisco Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Franco "Francisco Franco"), a general of the [Army of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Africa_(Spain) "Army of Africa (Spain)"), rebelled against the republican government and started the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War "Spanish Civil War") (1936–39). During the Second World War the [Vichy French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_French "Vichy French") presence in Tangier was overcome by that of [Francoist Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francoist_Spain "Francoist Spain").[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Spain lacked the wealth and the interest to develop an extensive economic infrastructure in its African colonies during the first half of the 20th century. However, through a [paternalistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paternalism "Paternalism") system, particularly on [Bioko Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioko_Island "Bioko Island"), Spain developed large [cocoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocoa_bean "Cocoa bean") plantations for which thousands of Nigerian workers were imported as laborers.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

In 1956, when [French Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Morocco "French Morocco") became independent, Spain surrendered [Spanish Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Morocco "Spanish Morocco") to the new nation, but retained control of Sidi Ifni, the [Tarfaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarfaya "Tarfaya") region and [Spanish Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Sahara "Spanish Sahara"). Moroccan [Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan "Sultan") (later King) [Mohammed V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_V_of_Morocco "Mohammed V of Morocco") was interested in these territories and unsuccessfully invaded Spanish Sahara in 1957, in the [Ifni War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ifni_War "Ifni War"), or in Spain, the Forgotten War (**la Guerra Olvidada**). In 1958, Spain ceded Tarfaya to Mohammed V and joined the previously separate districts of [Saguia el-Hamra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saguia_el-Hamra "Saguia el-Hamra") (in the north) and [Río de Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADo_de_Oro "Río de Oro") (in the south) to form the province of Spanish Sahara.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

In 1959, the Spanish territory on the [Gulf of Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Guinea "Gulf of Guinea") was established with a status similar to the provinces of metropolitan Spain. As the Spanish Equatorial Region, it was ruled by a [governor general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_general "Governor general") exercising military and civilian powers. The first local elections were held in 1959, and the first Equatoguinean representatives were seated in the [Spanish parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cortes_Generales "Cortes Generales"). Under the Basic Law of December 1963, limited autonomy was authorized under a joint legislative body for the territory's two provinces. The name of the country was changed to [Equatorial Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equatorial_Guinea "Equatorial Guinea"). In March 1968, under pressure from Equatoguinean nationalists and the United Nations, Spain announced that it would grant the country independence.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

In 1969, under international pressure, Spain returned Sidi Ifni to Morocco. Spanish control of Spanish Sahara endured until the 1975 [Green March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_March "Green March") prompted a withdrawal, under Moroccan military pressure. The future of this former Spanish colony remains uncertain.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The [Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Islands "Canary Islands") and Spanish cities in the African mainland are considered an equal part of Spain and the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union") but have a different tax system.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Morocco still claims Ceuta, Melilla, and **[plazas de soberanía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plazas_de_soberan%C3%ADa "Plazas de soberanía")** even though they are internationally recognized as administrative divisions of Spain. [Isla Perejil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isla_Perejil "Isla Perejil") [was occupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perejil_Island_crisis "Perejil Island crisis") on 11 July 2002 by Moroccan Gendarmerie and troops, who were evicted by [Spanish naval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Navy "Spanish Navy") forces in a bloodless operation.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

## Imperial economic policy

The Spanish Empire benefited from favorable [factor endowments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factor_endowments "Factor endowments") from its overseas possessions with their large, exploitable indigenous populations and rich mining areas. Thus the crown attempted to create and maintain a classic closed [mercantile system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercantile_system "Mercantile system"), warding off competitors and keeping wealth within the empire, specifically within the Crown of Castile. While in theory the Habsburgs were committed to maintaining a state monopoly, the reality was that the empire was a porous economic realm with widespread smuggling. In the 16th and 17th centuries under the Habsburgs, Spain's economic conditions gradually declined, especially in regards to the industrial development of its French, Dutch, and English rivals. Many of the goods being exported to the Empire originated from manufacturers in northwest Europe rather than in Spain. Illicit commercial activities became a part of the Empire's administrative structure. Supported by large flows of silver from the Americas, trade prohibited by Spanish mercantilist restrictions flourished as it provided a source of income to both crown officials and private merchants. The local administrative structure in [Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires "Buenos Aires"), for example, was established through its oversight of both legal and illegal commerce. The crown's pursuit of wars to maintain and expand territory, defend the Catholic faith, stamp out Protestantism, and beat back the Ottoman Turkish strength outstripped its ability to pay for it all, despite the huge production of silver in Peru and New Spain. Most of that flow paid mercenary soldiers in the European religious wars of the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, and paid foreign merchants for the consumer goods manufactured in northern Europe. Paradoxically, the wealth of the Indies impoverished Spain and enriched northern Europe, a course the [Bourbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Bourbon "House of Bourbon") monarchs would later attempt to reverse in the eighteenth century.

This was well acknowledged in Spain, with writers on political economy, the *[arbitristas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitristas "Arbitristas"),* sending the crown lengthy analyses in the form of "memorials, of the perceived problems and with proposed solutions". According to these thinkers, "Royal expenditure must be regulated, the sale of office halted, the growth of the church checked. The tax system must be overhauled, special concessions be made to agricultural laborers, rivers be made navigable and dry lands irrigated. In this way alone could Castile's productivity increase, its commerce restored, and its humiliating dependence on foreigners, on the Dutch and the Genoese, be brought to an end."

Since the early days of the Caribbean and conquest era, the crown attempted to control trade between Spain and the Indies with restrictive policies enforced by the House of Trade (est. 1503) in [Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville "Seville"). Shipping was through particular ports in Castile: Seville, and subsequently Cádiz, Spanish America: [Veracruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veracruz "Veracruz"), [Acapulco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acapulco "Acapulco"), Havana, [Cartagena de Indias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartagena_de_Indias "Cartagena de Indias"), and [Callao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callao "Callao")/[Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima "Lima"), and the Philippines: [Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila "Manila"). There were very few Spanish settlers in the Indies in the very early period and Spain could supply sufficient goods to them. But as the Aztec and Inca empires were conquered in the early sixteenth century, and large deposits of silver found in both Mexico and Peru, Spanish immigration increased and the demand for goods rose far beyond Spain's ability to supply it. Since Spain had little capital to invest in the expanding trade and no significant commercial group, bankers and commercial houses in [Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa "Republic of Genoa"), Germany, the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic "Dutch Republic"), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_France "Kingdom of France"), and [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_England "Kingdom of England") supplied both investment capital and goods in a supposedly closed system. Even in the sixteenth century, Spain recognized that the idealized closed system did not function in reality. Since the crown did not alter its restrictive structure or advocate fiscal prudence, despite the pleas of the *arbitristas*, the Indies trade remained nominally in the hands of Spain, but in fact enriched the other European countries.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The crown established the system of [treasure fleets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_treasure_fleets "Spanish treasure fleets") (Spanish: *flota*) to protect the conveyance of silver to Seville (later Cádiz). Produced in other European countries, Sevillian merchants conveyed consumer goods that were registered and taxed by the House of Trade, and then sent to the Indies. Other European commercial interests came to dominate supply, with Spanish merchant houses and their guilds (*[consulados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consulado "Consulado")*) in both Spain and the Indies acting as mere middlemen, reaping a slice of the profits. However, those profits did not promote a manufacturing sector in Spain's economic development, and its economy continued to be based in agriculture. The wealth of the Indies led to prosperity in northern Europe, particularly in the Netherlands and England, which were both Protestant. As Spain's power weakened in the seventeenth century, England, the Netherlands, and the French took advantage overseas by seizing islands in the Caribbean, which became bases for a burgeoning contraband trade in Spanish America. Crown officials, who were supposed to suppress contraband trade, were quite often in cahoots with the foreigners, since it was a source of personal enrichment. In Spain, the crown itself participated in collusion with foreign merchant houses, since they paid fines "meant to establish a compensation to the state for losses through fraud". It became a calculated risk for merchant houses doing business, and for the crown it gained income that would have otherwise been lost. Foreign merchants were part of the supposed monopoly system of trade. The transfer of the House of Trade from Seville to Cádiz meant foreign merchant houses had even easier access to the Spanish trade.

The Spanish imperial economy's major [global impact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_silver_trade_from_the_16th_to_19th_centuries "Global silver trade from the 16th to 19th centuries") was [silver mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_mining "Silver mining"). The mines in Peru and Mexico were in the hands of a few elite mining entrepreneurs with access to capital and a stomach for the risk that mining entailed. They operated under a system of royal licensing, since the crown held the rights to subsoil wealth. Mining entrepreneurs assumed all the risk of the enterprise, while the crown gained a 20% slice of the profits, the [royal fifth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_fifth "Royal fifth") ("quinto real"). Further adding to the crown's revenues in mining was that it held a monopoly on the mercury supply, used for separating pure silver from silver ore in the [patio process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patio_process "Patio process"). The crown kept the price high, thereby depressing the volume of silver production. Protecting its flow from Mexico and Peru as it transited to ports for shipment to Spain resulted early on in a convoy system (the flota) sailing twice a year. Its success can be judged by the fact that the silver fleet was captured only once, in 1628 by Dutch privateer [Piet Hein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piet_Pieterszoon_Hein "Piet Pieterszoon Hein"). That loss resulted in the bankruptcy of the Spanish crown and an extended period of economic depression in Spain.

One practice the Spanish used to gather workers for the mines was called *[repartimiento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repartimiento "Repartimiento")*. This was a rotational forced labor system where indigenous pueblos were obligated to send laborers to work in Spanish mines and plantations for a set number of days out of the year. Repartimiento was not implemented to replace [slave labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_labor "Slave labor"), but instead existed alongside free wage labor, slavery, and [indentured labor.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indentured_servitude "Indentured servitude") It was, however, a way for the Spanish to procure cheap labor, thus boosting the mining-driven economy.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The men who worked as repartimiento laborers were not always resistant to the practice. Some were drawn to the labor as a way to supplement the wages they earned cultivating fields so as to support their families and, of course, pay [tributes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribute "Tribute"). At first, a Spaniard could get repartimiento laborers to work for them with permission from a crown official, such as a [viceroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy "Viceroy"), only on the basis that this labor was absolutely necessary to provide the country with important resources. This condition became laxer as the years went on, and various enterprises had repartimiento laborers who would work in dangerous conditions for long hours and low wages.

During the Bourbon era, economic reforms sought to reverse the pattern that left Spain impoverished with no manufacturing sector and its colonies' need for manufactured goods supplied by other nations. It attempted to establish a closed trading system, but it was hampered by the terms of the 1713 [Treaty of Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Utrecht "Treaty of Utrecht"). The treaty ending the [War of the Spanish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession "War of the Spanish Succession"), with a victory for the Bourbon French candidate for the throne, had a provision for British merchants to legally sell slaves with a license (*[Asiento de Negros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asiento_de_Negros "Asiento de Negros")*) [slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_slave_trade "Atlantic slave trade") to Spanish America. The provision undermined the possibility of a revamped Spanish monopoly system. The merchants also used the opportunity to engage in contraband trade of their manufactured goods. Crown policy sought to make legal trade more appealing than contraband by instituting free commerce (*comercio libre*) in 1778, whereby Spanish American ports could trade with each other, and they could trade with any port in Spain. It was aimed at revamping a closed Spanish system and outflanking the increasingly powerful British. Silver production revived in the eighteenth century, with production far surpassing the earlier output. The crown reduced the taxes on mercury, meaning that a greater volume of pure silver could be refined. Silver mining absorbed most of the available capital in Mexico and Peru, and the crown emphasized the production of precious metals, which were sent to Spain. There was some economic development in the Indies to supply food, but a diversified economy did not emerge. The economic reforms of the Bourbon era both shaped and were themselves impacted by geopolitical developments in Europe. The [Bourbon Reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourbon_Reforms "Bourbon Reforms") arose out of the War of the Spanish Succession. In turn, the crown's attempt to tighten its control over its colonial markets in the Americas led to further conflict with other European powers who were vying for access to them. After a sparking a series of skirmishes throughout the 1700s over its stricter policies, Spain's reformed trade system led to war with Britain in 1796. In the Americas, meanwhile, economic policies enacted under the Bourbons had different impacts in different regions. On one hand, silver production in New Spain greatly increased and led to economic growth. But much of the profits of the revitalized mining sector went to mining elites and state officials, while in rural areas of New Spain conditions for rural workers deteriorated, contributing to social unrest that would impact subsequent revolts.

## Scientific investigations and expeditions

The [Spanish American Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_American_Enlightenment "Spanish American Enlightenment") produced a huge body of information on Spain's overseas empire via scientific expeditions. The most famous traveler in Spanish America was Prussian scientist Alexander von Humboldt, whose travel writings, especially *Political Essay on the Kingdom of New Spain* and scientific observations remain important sources for the history of Spanish America. Humboldt's expedition was authorized by the crown, but was self-funded from his personal fortune. The Bourbon crown promoted state-funded scientific work prior to the famous Humboldt expedition. Eighteenth-century clerics contributed to the expansion of scientific knowledge. These include [José Antonio de Alzate y Ramírez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Antonio_de_Alzate_y_Ram%C3%ADrez "José Antonio de Alzate y Ramírez"), and [José Celestino Mutis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Celestino_Mutis "José Celestino Mutis").[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The Spanish crown funded a number of important scientific expeditions: [Botanical Expedition to the Viceroyalty of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanical_Expedition_to_the_Viceroyalty_of_Peru "Botanical Expedition to the Viceroyalty of Peru") (1777–78); [Royal Botanical Expedition to New Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Botanical_Expedition_to_New_Granada "Royal Botanical Expedition to New Granada") (1783–1816); the [Royal Botanical Expedition to New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Botanical_Expedition_to_New_Spain "Royal Botanical Expedition to New Spain") (1787–1803); which scholars are now examining afresh. Although the crown funded a number of [Spanish expeditions to the Pacific Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_expeditions_to_the_Pacific_Northwest "Spanish expeditions to the Pacific Northwest") to bolster claims to territory, lengthy transatlantic and transpacific [Malaspina-Bustamante Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaspina_Expedition "Malaspina Expedition") was for scientific purposes. The crown also funded the [Balmis Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balmis_Expedition "Balmis Expedition") in 1804 to vaccinate colonial populations against smallpox.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Much of the research done in the eighteenth century was never published or otherwise disseminated, in part due to budgetary constraints on the crown. Starting in the late twentieth century, research on the [history of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_science "History of science") in Spain and the Spanish empire has blossomed, with primary sources being published in scholarly editions or reissued, as well the publication of a considerable number of important scholarly studies.

## Legacy

Although the Spanish Empire declined from its apogee in the late seventeenth century, it remained a wonder for other Europeans for its sheer geographical span. [Writing in 1738](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_(Samuel_Johnson_poem) "London (Samuel Johnson poem)"), English author [Samuel Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Johnson "Samuel Johnson") questioned, "Has heaven reserved, in pity to the poor,/No pathless waste or undiscovered shore,/No secret island in the boundless main,/No peaceful desert yet unclaimed by Spain?"

The Spanish Empire left a huge linguistic, religious, political, cultural, and urban architectural legacy in the [Western Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Hemisphere "Western Hemisphere"). With over 519 million native speakers today, Spanish is the second [most spoken native language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_languages_by_number_of_native_speakers "List of languages by number of native speakers") in the world, as result of the introduction of the language of Castile—Castilian, "*Castellano*" —from Iberia to Spanish America, later expanded by the governments of successor independent republics. In the Philippines, the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War "Spanish–American War") (1898) brought the islands under U.S. jurisdiction, with English being imposed in schools and Spanish becoming [a secondary official language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_the_Philippines "Languages of the Philippines"). Many indigenous languages throughout the empire were often lost either as indigenous populations were decimated by war and disease, or as indigenous people mixed with colonists, and the Spanish language was taught and spread over time.

An important cultural legacy of the Spanish empire overseas is [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism "Roman Catholicism"), which remains the main religious faith in Spanish America and the Philippines. Christian evangelization of indigenous peoples was a key responsibility of the crown and a justification for its imperial expansion. Although indigenous were considered neophytes and insufficiently mature in their faith for indigenous men to be ordained to the priesthood, the indigenous were part of the Catholic community of faith. Catholic orthodoxy was enforced by the [Inquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Office_of_the_Inquisition "Holy Office of the Inquisition"), particularly targeting [crypto-Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crypto-Jews "Crypto-Jews") and Protestants. Not until after their independence in the nineteenth century did Spanish American republics allow [religious toleration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_toleration "Religious toleration") of other faiths. Observances of Catholic holidays often have strong regional expressions and remain important in many parts of Spanish America. Observances include [Day of the Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_of_the_Dead "Day of the Dead"), [Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnaval_de_Oruro "Carnaval de Oruro"), [Holy Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Week_in_Popay%C3%A1n "Holy Week in Popayán"), [Corpus Christi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_devils_of_Corpus_Christi "Dancing devils of Corpus Christi"), [Epiphany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphany_(holiday) "Epiphany (holiday)"), and national saints' days, such as the [Virgin of Guadalupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_of_Guadalupe "Virgin of Guadalupe") in Mexico.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Politically, the colonial era has strongly influenced modern Spanish America. The territorial divisions of the empire in Spanish America became the basis for boundaries between new republics after independence and for state divisions within countries. It is often argued that the rise of [caudillismo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caudillo "Caudillo") during and after Latin American independence movements created a legacy of authoritarianism in the region. There was no significant development of representative institutions during the colonial era, and the executive power was often made stronger than the legislative power during the national period as a result.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

This has led to a popular misconception that the colonial legacy has caused the region to have an extremely oppressed proletariat. Revolts and riots are often seen as evidence of this supposed extreme oppression. However, the culture of revolting against an unpopular government is not simply a confirmation of widespread authoritarianism. The colonial legacy did leave a political culture of revolt, but not always as a desperate last act. The civil unrest of the region is seen by some as a form of political involvement. While the political context of the political revolutions in Spanish America is understood to be one in which liberal elites competed to form new national political structures, so too were those elites responding to mass lower-class political mobilization and participation.

Hundreds of towns and cities in the Americas were founded during the Spanish rule, with the colonial centers and buildings of many of them now designated as [UNESCO World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_World_Heritage_Sites "UNESCO World Heritage Sites") attracting tourists. The tangible heritage includes universities, forts, cities, cathedrals, schools, hospitals, missions, government buildings and colonial residences, many of which still stand today. A number of present-day roads, canals, ports or bridges sit where Spanish engineers built them centuries ago. The oldest universities in the Americas were founded by Spanish scholars and Catholic missionaries. The Spanish Empire also left a vast [cultural and linguistic legacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic "Hispanic"). The cultural legacy is also present in the [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Latin_America "Music of Latin America"), [cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine "Cuisine"), and fashion, some of which have been granted the status of [UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_Intangible_Cultural_Heritage_Lists "UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage Lists").[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The long colonial period in [Spanish America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_America "Hispanic America") resulted in a mixing of indigenous peoples, Europeans, and Africans that were [classified by race and hierarchically ranked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casta "Casta"), which created a markedly different society than the European colonies of North America. In concert with the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire "Portuguese Empire"), the Spanish Empire laid the foundations of a truly global trade by opening up the great trans-oceanic [trade routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_routes "Trade routes") and the exploration of unknown territories and oceans for the western knowledge. The [Spanish dollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_dollar "Spanish dollar") became the world's first global currency.

One of the features of this trade was the exchange of a great array of domesticated plants and animals between the [Old World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World "Old World") and the [New](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World "New World") in the [Columbian Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbian_Exchange "Columbian Exchange"). Some cultivars that were introduced to the Americas included grapes, wheat, barley, apples and citrous fruits; animals that were introduced to the New World were horses, donkeys, cattle, sheep, goats, pigs, and chickens. The Old World received from the Americas such things as maize, potatoes, chili peppers, tomatoes, tobacco, beans, squash, cacao (chocolate), vanilla, avocados, pineapples, chewing gum, rubber, peanuts, cashews, Brazil nuts, pecans, blueberries, strawberries, quinoa, amaranth, chia, agave and others. The result of these exchanges was to significantly improve the agricultural potential of not only in the Americas, but also that of Europe and Asia. Diseases brought by Europeans and Africans, such as smallpox, measles, typhus, and others, devastated almost all indigenous populations that had no immunity.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

There were also cultural influences, which can be seen in everything from architecture to food, music, art and law, from southern Argentina and Chile to the United States of America together with the Philippines. The complex origins and contacts of different peoples resulted in cultural influences coming together in the varied forms evident today in the former colonial areas.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]
