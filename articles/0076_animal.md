# Animal

**Animals** are [multicellular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multicellular "Multicellular"), [eukaryotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eukaryotic "Eukaryotic") [organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organism "Organism") belonging to the [biological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology "Biology") [kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_(biology) "Kingdom (biology)") **Animalia** ([/ˌænɪˈmeɪliə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English")). Animals [consume organic material](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterotroph "Heterotroph"), [breathe oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_respiration#Aerobic_respiration "Cellular respiration"), have [muscle cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscle_cell "Muscle cell"), can [reproduce sexually](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reproduce_sexually "Reproduce sexually"), and grow from a hollow sphere of [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_(biology) "Cell (biology)"), the [blastula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blastula "Blastula"), during [embryonic development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryonic_development "Embryonic development"). With few exceptions, animals are [able to move](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_locomotion "Animal locomotion"). Animals form a [clade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clade "Clade"), meaning that they arose from a single [common ancestor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_ancestor "Common ancestor"). Over 1.5 million [living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extant_taxon "Extant taxon") animal [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species "Species") have been [described](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species_description "Species description"), of which around 1.05 million are [insects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insect "Insect"), over 85,000 are [molluscs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mollusc "Mollusc"), and around 65,000 are [vertebrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebrate "Vertebrate"). It has been estimated there are as many as 7.77 million animal species on Earth. Animal body lengths range from 8.5 μm (0.00033 in) to 33.6 m (110 ft). They have complex [ecologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecologies "Ecologies") and [interactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_interaction "Biological interaction") with each other and their environments, forming intricate [food webs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_web "Food web"). The scientific study of animals is known as [zoology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoology "Zoology"), and the study of animal behaviour is known as [ethology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethology "Ethology").

The animal kingdom is divided into five major clades, namely [Porifera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porifera "Porifera"), [Ctenophora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ctenophora "Ctenophora"), [Placozoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placozoa "Placozoa"), [Cnidaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cnidaria "Cnidaria") and [Bilateria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilateria "Bilateria"). Most living animal species belong to the clade Bilateria, a highly proliferative clade whose members have a [bilaterally symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilaterally_symmetric "Bilaterally symmetric") and significantly [cephalised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cephalization "Cephalization") [body plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_plan "Body plan"), and the vast majority of bilaterians belong to two large clades: the [protostomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protostome "Protostome"), which include organisms such as [arthropods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthropod "Arthropod"), [molluscs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mollusc "Mollusc"), [flatworms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flatworm "Flatworm"), [annelids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annelid "Annelid") and [nematodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nematode "Nematode"); and the [deuterostomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deuterostome "Deuterostome"), which include [echinoderms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echinoderm "Echinoderm"), [hemichordates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemichordate "Hemichordate") and [chordates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chordate "Chordate"), the last of which contains the [vertebrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebrate "Vertebrate"). The much smaller [basal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basal_(phylogenetics) "Basal (phylogenetics)") [phylum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylum "Phylum") [Xenacoelomorpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenacoelomorpha "Xenacoelomorpha") have an uncertain position within Bilateria.

Animals are first represented in the fossil record in the [Ediacaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ediacaran "Ediacaran") period, with most modern animal phyla appearing as [marine species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_species "Marine species") in the [Cambrian explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian_explosion "Cambrian explosion"), which began around 539 [million years ago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Million_years_ago "Million years ago") (Mya), and most [classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_(biology) "Class (biology)") during the [Ordovician radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordovician_radiation "Ordovician radiation") 485.4 Mya. Common to all living animals, 6,331 groups of [genes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene "Gene") have been identified that may have arisen from a single [common ancestor](#Phylogeny) that lived about 650 Mya during the Cryogenian period.

Historically, [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle "Aristotle") divided animals [into those with blood and those without](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle%27s_biology "Aristotle's biology"). [Carl Linnaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Linnaeus "Carl Linnaeus") created the first hierarchical [biological classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_classification "Biological classification") for animals in 1758 with his *[Systema Naturae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systema_Naturae "Systema Naturae")*, which [Jean-Baptiste Lamarck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Baptiste_Lamarck "Jean-Baptiste Lamarck") expanded into 14 phyla by 1809. In 1874, [Ernst Haeckel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Haeckel "Ernst Haeckel") divided the animal kingdom into the multicellular **Metazoa** (now [synonymous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synonym_(taxonomy) "Synonym (taxonomy)") with Animalia) and the [Protozoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protozoa "Protozoa"), single-celled organisms no longer considered animals. In modern times, the biological classification of animals relies on advanced techniques, such as [molecular phylogenetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_phylogenetics "Molecular phylogenetics"), which are effective at demonstrating the [evolutionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution "Evolution") relationships between [taxa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxa "Taxa").

[Humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human "Human") make [use of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_uses_of_animals "Human uses of animals") many other animal species for [food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_food "Human food") (including [meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meat "Meat"), [eggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eggs_as_food "Eggs as food"), and [dairy products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_product "Dairy product")), for [materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_product "Animal product") (such as [leather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leather "Leather"), [fur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur "Fur"), and [wool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wool "Wool")), as [pets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet "Pet") and as [working animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_animal "Working animal") for [transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation "Transportation"), and [services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_(economics) "Service (economics)"). [Dogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dog "Dog"), the first [domesticated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domesticate "Domesticate") animal, have been used [in hunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunting_dog "Hunting dog"), [in security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guard_dog "Guard dog") and [in warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogs_in_warfare "Dogs in warfare"), as have [horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equestrianism "Equestrianism"), [pigeons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigeon_post "Pigeon post") and [birds of prey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falconry "Falconry"); while other [terrestrial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrestrial_animal "Terrestrial animal") and [aquatic animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquatic_animal "Aquatic animal") are [hunted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunt "Hunt") for sports, trophies or profits. Non-human animals are also an important element of human culture, having appeared in [cave arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_art "Cave art") and [totems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totem "Totem") since the earliest times, and are frequently featured in [mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythology "Mythology"), [religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion "Religion"), [arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art "Art"), [literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature "Literature"), [heraldry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heraldry "Heraldry"), [politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics "Politics"), and [sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport "Sport").

## Etymology

The word *animal* comes from the Latin noun *[animal](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/animal#Latin "wikt:animal")* of the same meaning, which is itself derived from Latin *[animalis](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/animalis#Latin "wikt:animalis")* 'having breath or soul'. The biological definition includes all members of the kingdom Animalia. In colloquial usage, the term *animal* is often used to refer only to nonhuman animals. The term *metazoa* is derived from Ancient Greek μετα *meta* 'after' (in biology, the prefix *meta-* stands for 'later') and ζῷᾰ *zōia* 'animals', plural of ζῷον *zōion* 'animal'. A **metazoan** is any member of the group *Metazoa*.

## Characteristics

Animals have several characteristics that they share with other living things. Animals are [eukaryotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eukaryotic "Eukaryotic"), [multicellular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multicellular "Multicellular"), and [aerobic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerobic_respiration "Aerobic respiration"), as are [plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plants "Plants") and [fungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fungi "Fungi"). Unlike plants and [algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alga "Alga"), which [produce their own food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autotroph "Autotroph"), animals [cannot produce their own food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterotroph "Heterotroph"), a feature they share with fungi. Animals ingest organic material and digest it internally.

### Structural features

Animals have structural characteristics that set them apart from all other living things, such as cells being surrounded by an [extracellular matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extracellular_matrix "Extracellular matrix") composed of [collagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collagen "Collagen") and elastic [glycoproteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycoprotein "Glycoprotein"), [motility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motile "Motile") to spontaneously move their bodies during at least part of their [life cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_life_cycle "Biological life cycle"), and a [blastula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blastula "Blastula") stage during [embryonic development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryogenesis "Embryogenesis").

Typically, there is an internal [digestive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digestion "Digestion") chamber with either one opening (in Ctenophora, Cnidaria, and flatworms) or two openings (in most bilaterians).

### Development

Development in [Bilateria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilateria "Bilateria") and [Cnidaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cnidaria "Cnidaria") is controlled by [Hox genes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hox_genes "Hox genes"), which signal the times and places to develop structures such as body segments and limbs.

During development, the animal extracellular matrix forms a relatively flexible framework upon which cells can move about and be reorganised into specialised tissues and organs, making the formation of complex structures possible, and allowing [cells to be differentiated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_differentiation "Cellular differentiation"). The extracellular matrix may be calcified, forming structures such as [shells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exoskeleton "Exoskeleton"), [bones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone "Bone"), and [spicules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spicule_(sponge) "Spicule (sponge)"). In contrast, the cells of other multicellular organisms (primarily algae, plants, and [fungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fungi "Fungi")) are held in place by cell walls, and so develop by progressive growth.

### Reproduction

Nearly all animals make use of some form of sexual reproduction. They produce [haploid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haploid "Haploid") [gametes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamete "Gamete") by [meiosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiosis "Meiosis"); the smaller, motile gametes are [spermatozoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spermatozoa "Spermatozoa") and the larger, non-motile gametes are [ova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ova "Ova"). These fuse to form [zygotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zygote "Zygote"), which develop via [mitosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitosis "Mitosis") into a hollow sphere, called a blastula. In sponges, blastula larvae swim to a new location, attach to the seabed, and develop into a new sponge. In most other groups, the blastula undergoes more complicated rearrangement. It first [invaginates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invagination "Invagination") to form a [gastrula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastrula "Gastrula") with a digestive chamber and two separate [germ layers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germ_layer "Germ layer"), an external [ectoderm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ectoderm "Ectoderm") and an internal [endoderm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoderm "Endoderm"). In most cases, a third germ layer, the [mesoderm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoderm "Mesoderm"), also develops between them. These germ layers then differentiate to form tissues and organs.

Repeated instances of [mating with a close relative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inbreeding "Inbreeding") during sexual reproduction generally leads to [inbreeding depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inbreeding_depression "Inbreeding depression") within a population due to the increased prevalence of harmful [recessive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recessive "Recessive") traits. Animals have evolved numerous mechanisms for [avoiding close inbreeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inbreeding_avoidance "Inbreeding avoidance").

Some animals are capable of [asexual reproduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asexual_reproduction "Asexual reproduction"), which often results in a genetic clone of the parent. This may take place through [fragmentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fragmentation_(reproduction) "Fragmentation (reproduction)"); [budding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budding "Budding"), such as in [*Hydra*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydra_(genus) "Hydra (genus)") and other [cnidarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cnidaria "Cnidaria"); or [parthenogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthenogenesis "Parthenogenesis"), where fertile eggs are produced without [mating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mating "Mating"), such as in [aphids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aphid "Aphid").

## Ecology

Animals are categorised into ecological groups depending on their [trophic levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trophic_level "Trophic level") and [how they consume organic material](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feeding_behaviour "Feeding behaviour"). Such groupings include [carnivores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnivore "Carnivore") (further divided into subcategories such as [piscivores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piscivore "Piscivore"), [insectivores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insectivore "Insectivore"), [ovivores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovivore "Ovivore"), etc.), [herbivores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbivore "Herbivore") (subcategorised into [folivores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folivore "Folivore"), [graminivores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graminivore "Graminivore"), [frugivores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frugivore "Frugivore"), [granivores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granivore "Granivore"), [nectarivores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nectarivore "Nectarivore"), [algivores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algivore "Algivore"), etc.), [omnivores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnivore "Omnivore"), [fungivores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fungivore "Fungivore"), [scavengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scavenger "Scavenger")/[detritivores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detritivore "Detritivore"), and [parasites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasite "Parasite").

[Interactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_interaction "Biological interaction") between animals of each [biome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biome "Biome") form complex [food webs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_web "Food web") within that [ecosystem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystem "Ecosystem"). In carnivorous or omnivorous species, [predation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predation "Predation") is a [consumer–resource interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer-resource_systems "Consumer-resource systems") where the predator feeds on another organism, its [prey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prey "Prey"), who often evolves [anti-predator adaptations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-predator_adaptation "Anti-predator adaptation") to avoid being fed upon. [Selective pressures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selective_pressure "Selective pressure") imposed on one another lead to an [evolutionary arms race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_arms_race "Evolutionary arms race") between predator and prey, resulting in various antagonistic/[competitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competition_(biology) "Competition (biology)") [coevolutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coevolution "Coevolution"). Almost all multicellular predators are animals. Some [consumers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_(food_chain) "Consumer (food chain)") use multiple methods; for example, in [parasitoid wasps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasitoid_wasp "Parasitoid wasp"), the larvae feed on the hosts' living tissues, killing them in the process, but the adults primarily consume nectar from flowers. Other animals may have very specific feeding behaviours, such as [hawksbill sea turtles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawksbill_sea_turtle "Hawksbill sea turtle") which mainly [eat sponges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spongivore "Spongivore").

Most animals rely on [biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass_(ecology) "Biomass (ecology)") and [bioenergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_energy "Biological energy") produced by [plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant "Plant") and [phytoplanktons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytoplankton "Phytoplankton") (collectively called [producers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Producer_(biology) "Producer (biology)")) through [photosynthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photosynthesis "Photosynthesis"). Herbivores, as [primary consumers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_(food_chain)#Levels_of_the_food_chain "Consumer (food chain)"), eat the plant material directly to digest and absorb the nutrients, while carnivores and other animals on higher [trophic levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trophic_level "Trophic level") indirectly acquire the nutrients by eating the herbivores or other animals that have eaten the herbivores. Animals oxidise [carbohydrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbohydrate "Carbohydrate"), [lipids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipid "Lipid"), [proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein") and other biomolecules in [cellular respiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_respiration "Cellular respiration"), which allows the animal to grow and to sustain [basal metabolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basal_metabolism "Basal metabolism") and fuel other biological processes such as [locomotion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_locomotion "Animal locomotion"). Of the 550 gigatons of carbon biomass on Earth, animals account for 2 gigatons. Some [benthic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benthic "Benthic") animals living close to [hydrothermal vents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrothermal_vent "Hydrothermal vent") and [cold seeps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_seep "Cold seep") on the dark [sea floor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_floor "Sea floor") consume organic matter produced through [chemosynthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemosynthesis "Chemosynthesis") (via [oxidising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidising "Oxidising") [inorganic compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inorganic_compound "Inorganic compound") such as [hydrogen sulfide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_sulfide "Hydrogen sulfide")) by [archaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaea "Archaea") and [bacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteria "Bacteria").

Animals originated in the ocean; all extant animal phyla, except for [Micrognathozoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micrognathozoa "Micrognathozoa") and [Onychophora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onychophora "Onychophora"), feature at least some marine species. However, several lineages of arthropods begun to colonise land around the same time as [land plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_plant "Land plant"), between 510 and 471 million years ago, during the [Late Cambrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Cambrian "Late Cambrian") or Early [Ordovician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordovician "Ordovician") periods. [Vertebrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebrate "Vertebrate") such as the [lobe-finned fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobe-finned_fish "Lobe-finned fish") *[Tiktaalik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiktaalik "Tiktaalik")* started to move on to land in the late [Devonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devonian "Devonian"), about 375 million years ago.

Animals occupy virtually all of earth's [habitats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat "Habitat") and microhabitats, with [faunas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna "Fauna") adapted to salt water, hydrothermal vents, fresh water, hot springs, swamps, forests, pastures, deserts, air, and the interiors of other organisms. Animals are however not particularly [heat tolerant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermophile "Thermophile"); very few of them can survive at constant temperatures above 50 °C (122 °F) or in the most extreme cold deserts of continental [Antarctica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctica "Antarctica").

The collective global geomorphic influence of animals on the processes shaping the Earth's surface remains largely understudied, with most studies limited to individual species and well-known exemplars.

## Diversity

### Size

The [blue whale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_whale "Blue whale") (*Balaenoptera musculus*) is the largest animal that has ever lived, weighing up to 190 [tonnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonne "Tonne") and measuring up to 33.6 metres (110 ft) long. The largest extant terrestrial animal is the [African bush elephant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_bush_elephant "African bush elephant") (*Loxodonta africana*), weighing up to 12.25 tonnes and measuring up to 10.67 metres (35.0 ft) long. The largest terrestrial animals that ever lived were [titanosaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanosaur "Titanosaur") [sauropod dinosaurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauropoda "Sauropoda") such as *[Argentinosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentinosaurus "Argentinosaurus")*, which may have weighed as much as 73 tonnes, and *Supersaurus* which may have reached 39 metres. Several animals are microscopic; some [Myxozoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myxozoa "Myxozoa") ([obligate parasites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obligate_parasite "Obligate parasite") within the Cnidaria) never grow larger than 20 [μm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CE%9Cm "Μm"), and one of the smallest species (*Myxobolus szekeli*) is no more than 8.5 μm when fully grown.

* The [blue whale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_whale "Blue whale") is the largest animal that has ever lived; it can be up to 33.6 metres (110 ft) long.
* [Myxozoans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myxozoa "Myxozoa") such as *[Myxobolus cerebralis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myxobolus_cerebralis "Myxobolus cerebralis")* are single-celled parasites, never more than 20 [μm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CE%9Cm "Μm") across.

### Numbers and habitats of major phyla

The following table lists estimated numbers of described extant species for the major animal phyla, along with their principal habitats (terrestrial, fresh water, and marine), and free-living or parasitic ways of life. Species estimates shown here are based on numbers described scientifically; much larger estimates have been calculated based on various means of prediction, and these can vary wildly. For instance, around 25,000–27,000 species of nematodes have been described, while published estimates of the total number of nematode species include 10,000–20,000; 500,000; 10 million; and 100 million. Using patterns within the [taxonomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxonomy_(biology) "Taxonomy (biology)") hierarchy, the total number of animal species—including those not yet described—was calculated to be about 7.77 million in 2011.

## Evolutionary origin

The first body fossils of animals appear in the [Ediacaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ediacaran "Ediacaran"), represented by forms such as *[Charnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charnia "Charnia")* and *[Spriggina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spriggina "Spriggina")*. It had long been doubted whether these fossils truly represented animals, but the discovery of the animal lipid [cholesterol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholesterol "Cholesterol") in fossils of *[Dickinsonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dickinsonia "Dickinsonia")* suggests that they are. Animals are thought to have originated under low-oxygen conditions, suggesting that they were capable of living entirely by [anaerobic respiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaerobic_respiration "Anaerobic respiration"), but as they became specialised for aerobic metabolism they became fully dependent on oxygen in their environments.

Many animal phyla first appear in the [fossil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil "Fossil") record during the [Cambrian explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian_explosion "Cambrian explosion"), starting about 539 million years ago, in beds such as the [Burgess Shale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgess_Shale "Burgess Shale"). Extant phyla in these rocks include [molluscs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mollusc "Mollusc"), [brachiopods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachiopod "Brachiopod"), [onychophorans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onychophora "Onychophora"), [tardigrades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tardigrade "Tardigrade"), [arthropods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthropod "Arthropod"), [echinoderms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echinoderm "Echinoderm") and [hemichordates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemichordate "Hemichordate"), along with numerous now-extinct forms such as the [predatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predator "Predator") *[Anomalocaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anomalocaris "Anomalocaris")*. The apparent suddenness of the event may however be an artefact of the fossil record, rather than showing that all these animals appeared simultaneously. That view is supported by the discovery of *[Auroralumina attenboroughii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auroralumina_attenboroughii "Auroralumina attenboroughii")*, the earliest known Ediacaran crown-group cnidarian (557–562 mya, some 20 million years before the Cambrian explosion) from [Charnwood Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charnwood_Forest "Charnwood Forest"), England. It is thought to be one of the earliest predators, catching small prey with its [nematocysts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nematocyst "Nematocyst") as modern cnidarians do.

Some palaeontologists have suggested that animals appeared much earlier than the Cambrian explosion, possibly as early as 1 billion years ago. Early fossils that might represent animals appear for example in the 665-million-year-old rocks of the [Trezona Formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trezona_Formation "Trezona Formation") of [South Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Australia "South Australia"). These fossils are interpreted as most probably being early [sponges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sponge "Sponge").
[Trace fossils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace_fossil "Trace fossil") such as tracks and burrows found in the [Tonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonian "Tonian") period (from 1 gya) may indicate the presence of [triploblastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triploblastic "Triploblastic") worm-like animals, roughly as large (about 5 mm wide) and complex as earthworms. However, similar tracks are produced by the giant single-celled protist *[Gromia sphaerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gromia_sphaerica "Gromia sphaerica")*, so the Tonian trace fossils may not indicate early animal evolution. Around the same time, the layered mats of [microorganisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microorganism "Microorganism") called [stromatolites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stromatolite "Stromatolite") decreased in diversity, perhaps due to grazing by newly evolved animals. [Sterane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterane "Sterane") biomarkers, which are usually indicative of [demosponges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demosponge "Demosponge"), are found throughout the late Cryogenian and onward, but may also be produced by [rhizarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhizaria "Rhizaria"). Objects such as sediment-filled tubes that resemble trace fossils of the burrows of wormlike animals have been found in 1.2 gya rocks in North America, in 1.5 gya rocks in Australia and North America, and in 1.7 gya rocks in Australia. Their interpretation as having an animal origin is disputed, as they might be water-escape or other structures.

* *[Dickinsonia costata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dickinsonia_costata "Dickinsonia costata")* from the [Ediacaran biota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ediacaran_biota "Ediacaran biota") (c. 635–542 mya) is one of the earliest animal species known.
* *[Auroralumina attenboroughii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auroralumina_attenboroughii "Auroralumina attenboroughii")*, an Ediacaran predator (c. 560 mya)
* *[Anomalocaris canadensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anomalocaris_canadensis "Anomalocaris canadensis")* is one of the many animal species that emerged in the [Cambrian explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian_explosion "Cambrian explosion"), starting some 539 mya, and found in the fossil beds of the [Burgess Shale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgess_Shale "Burgess Shale").

### Internal phylogeny

The relationships at the base of the animal tree have been debated. Other than Ctenophora, the Bilateria and Cnidaria are the only groups with symmetry, and other evidence shows they are closely related. In addition to sponges, Placozoa has no symmetry and was often considered a "missing link" between protists and multicellular animals. The presence of [hox genes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hox_gene "Hox gene") in Placozoa shows that they were once more complex.

The [Porifera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porifera "Porifera") (sponges) have long been assumed to be sister to the rest of the animals, but there is evidence that the [Ctenophora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ctenophora "Ctenophora") may be in that position. Molecular phylogenetics has supported both the sponge-sister and ctenophore-sister hypotheses. ### Porifera

Sponges are physically very distinct from other animals, and were long thought to have diverged first, representing the oldest animal phylum and forming a sister clade to all other animals. Despite their morphological dissimilarity with all other animals, genetic evidence suggests sponges may be more closely related to other animals than the comb jellies are. Sponges lack the complex organisation found in most other animal phyla; their cells are differentiated, but in most cases not organised into distinct tissues, unlike all other animals. Sponges are almost entirely [sessile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sessility_(motility) "Sessility (motility)") in their adult stage. They lack true nerve cells.

Sponges lack a digestive, [circulatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circulatory_system "Circulatory system"), [respiratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_system "Respiratory system"), or [excretory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excretion "Excretion") system. Instead, they rely on the flow of water through their bodies to perform these functions. Many have adapted a hollow structure to facilitate the flow of water through their bodies. They typically feed by drawing in water through pores, filtering out small particles of food. They absorb oxygen [dissolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solubility "Solubility") in water, and they release [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide "Carbon dioxide") and [nitrogenous waste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolic_waste "Metabolic waste") into water that flows through them. Sponges can reproduce sexually and asexually. Asexual reproduction happens through a variety of mechanisms including by [fragmentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fragmentation_(reproduction) "Fragmentation (reproduction)"), where a piece of the sponge splits off and reestablishes somewhere else. For sexual reproduction, most sponges are [hermaphrodites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermaphrodite "Hermaphrodite"), they produce both egg and sperm cells.

### Ctenophora

Comb jellies, members of Ctenophora, are [radially symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radially_symmetric "Radially symmetric") and have digestive chambers with a single opening, which serves as both mouth and anus. Ctenophora have distinct tissues, but these are not organised into discrete [organs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organs "Organs"). They move using large arrays of [cilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilium "Cilium"), called swimming plates. Almost all comb jellies are predatory. They have are unique in having [colloblasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colloblast "Colloblast"), structures on their tentacles that help them catch prey. They have nerve cells, but lack a brain or [central nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system"). Instead their nerves are arranged into a [nerve net](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve_net "Nerve net"). Their nerves are very different from those of other animals, and evidence suggests that their nerves developed independently of the nerves of other animals.

### Cnidaria

Like Ctenophora, Cnidaria are radially symmetric and have digestive chambers with a single opening, which serves as both mouth and anus. They also have distinct tissues that are not organised into discrete [organs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organs "Organs"). Members of the group [Anthozoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthozoa "Anthozoa"), such as [sea anemones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_anemone "Sea anemone"), [coral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral "Coral"), and [sea pens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_pen "Sea pen") only are sessile [polyps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyp_(zoology) "Polyp (zoology)"). Most members of [Medusozoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medusozoa "Medusozoa"), including [jellyfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jellyfish "Jellyfish") and most [hydrozoans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrozoa "Hydrozoa"), have a polyp form as well, but also have a motile [medusae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medusae "Medusae") stage. The medusae stage are commonly known as jellyfish. Cnidaria are diploblastic, having only two main germ layers, ectoderm and endoderm.

### Placozoa

The tiny placozoans have no permanent digestive chamber and no symmetry; they superficially resemble amoebae. Their phylogeny is poorly defined, and under active research.

### Bilateria

The remaining animals, the great majority—comprising some 29 phyla and over a million species—form the [Bilateria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilateria "Bilateria") [clade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clade "Clade"), which have a bilaterally symmetric [body plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_plan "Body plan"). The Bilateria are [triploblastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triploblasty "Triploblasty"), with three well-developed germ layers, and their tissues [form distinct organs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organogenesis "Organogenesis"). The digestive chamber has two openings, a mouth and an anus, and in the [Nephrozoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nephrozoa "Nephrozoa") there is an internal body cavity, a [coelom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coelom "Coelom") or pseudocoelom. These animals have a head end (anterior) and a tail end (posterior), a back (dorsal) surface and a belly (ventral) surface, and a left and a right side. Having a front end means that this part of the body encounters stimuli, such as food, favouring [cephalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cephalisation "Cephalisation"), the development of a head with [sense organs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sense_organ "Sense organ") and a mouth. Many bilaterians have a combination of circular [muscles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscle "Muscle") that constrict the body, making it longer, and an opposing set of longitudinal muscles, that shorten the body; these enable soft-bodied animals with a [hydrostatic skeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrostatic_skeleton "Hydrostatic skeleton") to move by [peristalsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peristalsis "Peristalsis"). They also have a gut that extends through the basically cylindrical body from mouth to anus. Many bilaterian phyla have primary [larvae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larva "Larva") which swim with [cilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilia "Cilia") and have an apical organ containing sensory cells. However, over evolutionary time, descendant spaces have evolved which have lost one or more of each of these characteristics. For example, adult echinoderms are radially symmetric (unlike their larvae), while some [parasitic worms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasitic_worms "Parasitic worms") have extremely simplified body structures.

Genetic studies have considerably changed zoologists' understanding of the relationships within the Bilateria. Most appear to belong to two major lineages, the [protostomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protostomes "Protostomes") and the [deuterostomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deuterostomes "Deuterostomes"). It is often suggested that the basalmost bilaterians are the [Xenacoelomorpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenacoelomorpha "Xenacoelomorpha"), with all other bilaterians belonging to the subclade [Nephrozoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nephrozoa "Nephrozoa"). However, this suggestion has been contested, with other studies finding that xenacoelomorphs are more closely related to [Ambulacraria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambulacraria "Ambulacraria") than to other bilaterians.

#### Protostomes and deuterostomes

Protostomes and deuterostomes differ in several ways. Early in development, deuterostome embryos undergo radial [cleavage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleavage_(embryo) "Cleavage (embryo)") during cell division, while many protostomes (the [Spiralia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiralia "Spiralia")) undergo spiral cleavage.
Animals from both groups possess a complete digestive tract, but in protostomes the first opening of the [embryonic gut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archenteron "Archenteron") develops into the mouth, and the anus forms secondarily. In deuterostomes, the anus forms first while the mouth develops secondarily. Most protostomes have [schizocoelous development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schizocoely "Schizocoely"), where cells simply fill in the interior of the gastrula to form the mesoderm. In deuterostomes, the mesoderm forms by [enterocoelic pouching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterocoely "Enterocoely"), through invagination of the endoderm.

The main deuterostome taxa are the Ambulacraria and the Chordata. Ambulacraria are exclusively marine and include [acorn worms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acorn_worm "Acorn worm"), [starfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starfish "Starfish"), [sea urchins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_urchin "Sea urchin"), and [sea cucumbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_cucumber "Sea cucumber"). The chordates are dominated by the [vertebrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebrates "Vertebrates") (animals with [backbones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebral_column "Vertebral column")), which consist of [fishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish "Fish"), [amphibians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibia "Amphibia"), [reptiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reptile "Reptile"), [birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird "Bird"), and [mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal "Mammal").

The protostomes include the [Ecdysozoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecdysozoa "Ecdysozoa"), named after their shared [trait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenotypic_trait "Phenotypic trait") of [ecdysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecdysis "Ecdysis"), growth by moulting, Among the largest ecdysozoan phyla are the [arthropods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthropod "Arthropod") and the [nematodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nematode "Nematode"). The rest of the protostomes are in the [Spiralia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiralia "Spiralia"), named for their pattern of developing by spiral cleavage in the early embryo. Major spiralian phyla include the [annelids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annelid "Annelid") and [molluscs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mollusca "Mollusca").

* The bilaterian gut develops in two ways. In many [protostomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protostome "Protostome"), the blastopore develops into the mouth, while in [deuterostomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deuterostome "Deuterostome") it becomes the anus.
* The [Spiralia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiralia "Spiralia") develop with [spiral cleavage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiral_cleavage "Spiral cleavage") in the embryo, as here in a sea snail.

## History of classification

In the [classical era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_era "Classical era"), Aristotle [divided animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle%27s_biology "Aristotle's biology"), based on his own observations, into those with blood (roughly, the vertebrates) and those without. The animals were then [arranged on a scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scala_naturae "Scala naturae") from man (with blood, two legs, rational soul) down through the live-bearing tetrapods (with blood, four legs, sensitive soul) and other groups such as crustaceans (no blood, many legs, sensitive soul) down to spontaneously generating creatures like sponges (no blood, no legs, vegetable soul). [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle "Aristotle") was uncertain whether sponges were animals, which in his system ought to have sensation, appetite, and locomotion, or plants, which did not: he knew that sponges could sense touch and would contract if about to be pulled off their rocks, but that they were rooted like plants and never moved about.

In 1758, [Carl Linnaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Linnaeus "Carl Linnaeus") created the first [hierarchical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hierarchical "Hierarchical") classification in his *[Systema Naturae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systema_Naturae "Systema Naturae")*. In his original scheme, the animals were one of three kingdoms, divided into the classes of [Vermes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vermes_in_the_10th_edition_of_Systema_Naturae "Vermes in the 10th edition of Systema Naturae"), [Insecta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insecta_in_the_10th_edition_of_Systema_Naturae "Insecta in the 10th edition of Systema Naturae"), [Pisces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisces_in_the_10th_edition_of_Systema_Naturae "Pisces in the 10th edition of Systema Naturae"), [Amphibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibia_in_the_10th_edition_of_Systema_Naturae "Amphibia in the 10th edition of Systema Naturae"), [Aves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aves_in_the_10th_edition_of_Systema_Naturae "Aves in the 10th edition of Systema Naturae"), and [Mammalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammalia_in_the_10th_edition_of_Systema_Naturae "Mammalia in the 10th edition of Systema Naturae"). Since then, the last four have all been subsumed into a single phylum, the [Chordata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chordata "Chordata"), while his Insecta (which included the crustaceans and arachnids) and Vermes have been renamed or broken up. The process was begun in 1793 by [Jean-Baptiste de Lamarck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Baptiste_de_Lamarck "Jean-Baptiste de Lamarck"), who called the Vermes *une espèce de chaos* ('a chaotic mess') and split the group into three new phyla: worms, echinoderms, and polyps (which contained corals and jellyfish). By 1809, in his *[Philosophie Zoologique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophie_Zoologique "Philosophie Zoologique")*, Lamarck had created nine phyla apart from vertebrates (where he still had four phyla: mammals, birds, reptiles, and fish) and molluscs, namely [cirripedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cirripede "Cirripede"), annelids, crustaceans, arachnids, insects, worms, [radiates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiates "Radiates"), polyps, and [infusorians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infusoria "Infusoria").

In his 1817 **[Le Règne Animal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_R%C3%A8gne_Animal "Le Règne Animal")**, [Georges Cuvier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Cuvier "Georges Cuvier") used [comparative anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_anatomy "Comparative anatomy") to group the animals into four *embranchements* ('branches' with different body plans, roughly corresponding to phyla), namely vertebrates, molluscs, articulated animals (arthropods and annelids), and [zoophytes (radiata)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoophytes "Zoophytes") (echinoderms, cnidaria and other forms). This division into four was followed by the embryologist [Karl Ernst von Baer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Ernst_von_Baer "Karl Ernst von Baer") in 1828, the zoologist [Louis Agassiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Agassiz "Louis Agassiz") in 1857, and the comparative anatomist [Richard Owen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Owen "Richard Owen") in 1860.

In 1874, [Ernst Haeckel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Haeckel "Ernst Haeckel") divided the animal kingdom into two subkingdoms: Metazoa (multicellular animals, with five phyla: coelenterates, echinoderms, articulates, molluscs, and vertebrates) and Protozoa (single-celled animals), including a sixth animal phylum, sponges. The protozoa were later moved to the former kingdom [Protista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protista "Protista"), leaving only the Metazoa as a synonym of Animalia.

## In human culture

### Practical uses

The human population exploits a large number of other animal species for food, both of [domesticated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestication_of_animals "Domestication of animals") livestock species in [animal husbandry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_husbandry "Animal husbandry") and, mainly at sea, by hunting wild species. Marine fish of many species are [caught commercially](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing "Fishing") for food. A smaller number of species are [farmed commercially](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_farming "Fish farming"). Humans and their [livestock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livestock "Livestock") make up more than 90% of the biomass of all terrestrial vertebrates, and almost as much as all insects combined.

[Invertebrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invertebrates "Invertebrates") including [cephalopods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cephalopod "Cephalopod"), [crustaceans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crustacea "Crustacea"), [insects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insect "Insect")—principally [bees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_honey_bee "Western honey bee") and [silkworms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silkworms "Silkworms")—and [bivalve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bivalve "Bivalve") or [gastropod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastropod "Gastropod") molluscs are hunted or farmed for food, fibres. [Chickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken "Chicken"), [cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle "Cattle"), [sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep "Sheep"), [pigs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig "Pig"), and other animals are raised as livestock for meat across the world. Animal fibres such as wool and silk are used to make textiles, while animal [sinews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinew "Sinew") have been used as lashings and bindings, and leather is widely used to make shoes and other items. Animals have been hunted and farmed for their fur to make items such as coats and hats. Dyestuffs including [carmine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmine "Carmine") ([cochineal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochineal "Cochineal")), [shellac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shellac "Shellac"), and [kermes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kermes_(dye) "Kermes (dye)") have been made from the bodies of insects. [Working animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_animals "Working animals") including cattle and horses have been used for work and transport from the first days of agriculture.

Animals such as the fruit fly *[Drosophila melanogaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drosophila_melanogaster "Drosophila melanogaster")* serve a major role in science as [experimental models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_organism "Model organism"). Animals have been used to create [vaccines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaccine "Vaccine") since their discovery in the 18th century. Some medicines such as the cancer drug [trabectedin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trabectedin "Trabectedin") are based on [toxins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toxin "Toxin") or other molecules of animal origin.

People have used [hunting dogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunting_dog "Hunting dog") to help chase down and retrieve animals, and [birds of prey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birds_of_prey "Birds of prey") to catch birds and mammals, while tethered [cormorants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cormorant "Cormorant") have been [used to catch fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cormorant_fishing "Cormorant fishing"). [Poison dart frogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poison_dart_frog "Poison dart frog") have been used to poison the tips of [blowpipe darts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blowdart "Blowdart").
A wide variety of animals are kept as pets, from invertebrates such as tarantulas, octopuses, and [praying mantises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praying_mantis "Praying mantis"), reptiles such as [snakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake "Snake") and [chameleons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chameleon "Chameleon"), and birds including [canaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_canary "Domestic canary"), [parakeets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parakeet "Parakeet"), and [parrots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parrot "Parrot") all finding a place. However, the most kept pet species are mammals, namely [dogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dog "Dog"), [cats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat "Cat"), and [rabbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbit "Rabbit"). There is a tension between the role of animals as companions to humans, and their existence as [individuals with rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_rights "Animal rights") of their own.

A wide variety of terrestrial and aquatic animals are hunted [for sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animals_in_sport "Animals in sport").

### Symbolic uses

The [signs of the Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signs_of_the_zodiac "Signs of the zodiac") and [Chinese zodiacs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_zodiac "Chinese zodiac") are based on animals. In China and Japan, the [butterfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butterfly "Butterfly") has been seen as the [personification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personification "Personification") of a person's [soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul "Soul"), and in classical representation the butterfly is also the symbol of the soul.

Animals have been the [subjects of art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_style "Animal style") from the earliest times, both historical, as in ancient Egypt, and prehistoric, as in the [cave paintings at Lascaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_paintings_at_Lascaux "Cave paintings at Lascaux"). Major animal paintings include [Albrecht Dürer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albrecht_D%C3%BCrer "Albrecht Dürer")'s 1515 *[The Rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCrer%27s_Rhinoceros "Dürer's Rhinoceros")*, and [George Stubbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Stubbs "George Stubbs")'s c. 1762 horse portrait *[Whistlejacket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whistlejacket "Whistlejacket")*. [Insects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthropods_in_film "Arthropods in film"), birds and mammals play roles in literature and film, such as in [giant bug movies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_bug_movie "Big bug movie").

Animals including [insects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insects_in_mythology "Insects in mythology") and mammals feature in mythology and religion. The [scarab beetle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarabaeidae "Scarabaeidae") was sacred in [ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt "Ancient Egypt"), and the [cow is sacred in Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle_in_religion_and_mythology#Hinduism "Cattle in religion and mythology"). Among other mammals, [deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deer_in_mythology "Deer in mythology"), [horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_worship "Horse worship"), [lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_depictions_of_lions "Cultural depictions of lions"), [bats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bat#Cultural_significance "Bat"), [bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bear_worship "Bear worship"), and [wolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolves_in_folklore,_religion_and_mythology "Wolves in folklore, religion and mythology") are the subjects of myths and worship.
