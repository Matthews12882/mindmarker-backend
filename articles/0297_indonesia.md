# Indonesia

**Indonesia**, officially the **Republic of Indonesia**, is a country in [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia") and [Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania "Oceania"), between the [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean "Indian Ocean") and [Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean "Pacific Ocean") oceans. Comprising between [13,000 and 17,000 islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Indonesia "List of islands of Indonesia")—including [Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatra "Sumatra"), [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java "Java"), [Sulawesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulawesi "Sulawesi"), and parts of [Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo "Borneo") and [New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Guinea "New Guinea")—it is the world's largest [archipelagic state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelagic_state "Archipelagic state") and the [14th-largest country by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area "List of countries and dependencies by area"). Indonesia has extensive tropical forests and marine ecosystems that support some of the [world's highest levels of biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megadiverse_countries "Megadiverse countries").

The [Indonesian archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nusantara_(term) "Nusantara (term)") has been inhabited since prehistoric times, with early human presence evidenced by fossils of *[Homo erectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_erectus "Homo erectus")* and *[Homo sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_sapiens "Homo sapiens")*, and megalithic sites. It became connected to regional and international maritime trade networks by the first millennium BCE. [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Indonesia "Hinduism in Indonesia") and [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_Indonesia "Buddhism in Indonesia") kingdoms developed by early centuries CE, followed by the [spread of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spread_of_Islam_in_Indonesia "Spread of Islam in Indonesia") and the growth of Islamic states from the late medieval period. European powers later competed to monopolise trade in the Spice Islands of [Maluku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maluku_Islands "Maluku Islands") during the [Age of Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery "Age of Discovery"). Dutch commercial and colonial control expanded from the 17th century and was consolidated over most of the archipelago during the 19th and early 20th centuries, before Indonesia [proclaimed its independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_Indonesian_Independence "Proclamation of Indonesian Independence") in the [aftermath of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aftermath_of_World_War_II "Aftermath of World War II").

Since independence, Indonesia has experienced separatist conflicts, corruption, political upheaval and natural disasters, alongside [democratisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_era_in_Indonesia "Reform era in Indonesia") and rapid economic growth. The country today is a [presidential republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system "Presidential system") with an elected [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Consultative_Assembly "People's Consultative Assembly") and consists of [38 provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Indonesia "Provinces of Indonesia"), some of which enjoy [greater autonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_administrative_division "Autonomous administrative division") than others. Home to over 280 million people, Indonesia [ranks fourth in the world by population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population "List of countries and dependencies by population") and has the largest [Muslim population of any country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_by_country "Islam by country"). More than half of Indonesians live on Java, the [most heavily populated island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_by_population "List of islands by population") in the world, while the capital [Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta "Jakarta") is the world's [most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_cities "List of largest cities").

There are hundreds of [ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Indonesia "Ethnic groups in Indonesia") and more than [700 languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Indonesia "Languages of Indonesia"), as well as diverse regional cultures. This diversity is reflected in the national motto, *[Bhinneka Tunggal Ika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhinneka_Tunggal_Ika "Bhinneka Tunggal Ika")*, meaning "unity in diversity". [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language "Indonesian language"), a standardised variety of [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language "Malay language"), is the official and national language and its *[lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca "Lingua franca")*. Indonesia has the largest [economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Indonesia "Economy of Indonesia") in Southeast Asia, with services and industry accounting for most output and economic activity concentrated on Java. Sea and air transport are the primary means of connecting the country's many islands. Indonesia is a [middle power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_power "Middle power") active in regional and global affairs and is a member of the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations"), [G20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G20 "G20"), the [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement "Non-Aligned Movement"), [ASEAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN "ASEAN"), and the [Organisation of Islamic Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_Islamic_Cooperation "Organisation of Islamic Cooperation").

## Etymology

The name *Indonesia* is usually explained as a combination of *India* and the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek "Ancient Greek") word *[nesos](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%CE%BD%E1%BF%86%CF%83%CE%BF%CF%82 "wikt:νῆσος")*, together meaning 'Indian islands'. In 1850, [George Windsor Earl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Windsor_Earl "George Windsor Earl"), an English [ethnologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnology "Ethnology"), proposed the terms *Indunesians* and *Malayunesians* for the inhabitants of the "Indian Archipelago or [Malay Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Archipelago "Malay Archipelago")", preferring the latter because he considered *Indunesians* too general. In the same journal, [James Richardson Logan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Richardson_Logan "James Richardson Logan") adopts *Indonesia* as a geographical term for the *Indian Archipelago*.

Usage remained limited for several decades. German ethnographer [Adolf Bastian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Bastian "Adolf Bastian") from the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin "Humboldt University of Berlin") gave the term greater scholarly currency by using it in the title of his five-volume work *Indonesia, or the Islands of the Malay Archipelago* ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language "German language"): *Indonesien oder die Inseln des Malayischen Archipel*), published from 1884 to 1894. Dutch scholars nevertheless tend to use *Malay Archipelago* ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language "Dutch language"): *Maleische Archipel*) or the *[Netherlands East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_Indies "Dutch East Indies")* (*Nederlandsch Oost Indië*). Popular alternatives include *Indië*, *de Oost* ("the East"), and, less often, *Insulinde*.

After 1900, *Indonesia* became increasingly common in academic and public usage, and Indonesian nationalist organisations adopted it as an expression of national unity and freedom from colonial rule. Among its early Indonesian promoters was [Soewardi Soerjaningrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ki_Hajar_Dewantara "Ki Hajar Dewantara") (Ki Hajar Dewantara), who established the *Indonesisch Persbureau* in [The Hague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hague "The Hague") in November 1918, a press bureau that used *Indonesia* in its name.

## History

### Prehistory, early states, and Islamisation

The island of [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java "Java") has yielded numerous hominin fossils generally attributed to *[Homo erectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_erectus "Homo erectus")*, including the [type specimen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_(biology) "Type (biology)") discovered by [Eugène Dubois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eug%C3%A8ne_Dubois "Eugène Dubois") at [Trinil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinil "Trinil"). Stone artefacts on [Flores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flores "Flores") show that hominins had reached the island by at least 1.02 million years ago. *[Homo sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_sapiens "Homo sapiens")* had dispersed into Southeast Asia by at least 50,000 years ago. In [Sulawesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulawesi "Sulawesi"), narrative cave art has been dated to a minimum age of 51,200 years. Flores also yielded remains of the small-bodied hominin *[Homo floresiensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_floresiensis "Homo floresiensis")*. Megalithic traditions later developed at different times across the archipelago, and stone monuments were used for burial and to honour or commemorate ancestors.

Beginning in the third millennium BCE, [Austronesian-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_languages "Austronesian languages") communities moved south from [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan") through the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines") and into the Indonesian archipelago, where related languages spread across most of the islands. Settlement and subsistence patterns varied across the islands, with communities combining fishing, hunting, gathering, and cultivation. From around 500 BCE, regional trading networks increasingly connected the archipelago with [South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia") and [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia"). Imported goods, knowledge, and technologies circulated through these networks, and during the early centuries CE some coastal communities in [Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo "Borneo"), [Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatra "Sumatra"), and Java developed into trading ports. Indian merchants and religious specialists brought [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit "Sanskrit") literacy, [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Indonesia "Hinduism in Indonesia") and [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_Indonesia "Buddhism in Indonesia") practices, and models of kingship that local rulers adopted.

[Srivijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivijaya "Srivijaya"), founded in southeastern Sumatra in the late 7th century, grew as a maritime trading polity and a centre of Buddhist learning and religious networks. In central Java, rulers supported the construction of major Hindu and Buddhist monuments between the 8th and 10th centuries, including [Borobudur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borobudur "Borobudur") and [Prambanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prambanan "Prambanan"). [Majapahit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majapahit "Majapahit") emerged in eastern Java in the late 13th century after [Raden Wijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raden_Wijaya "Raden Wijaya") used a [Mongol expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Java "Mongol invasion of Java") to defeat his rivals and then forced the Mongols to withdraw. Its rulers claimed suzerainty over a wide network of ports across the archipelago, although beyond Java this authority was often exercised indirectly through tributary, ritual, and commercial relationships rather than continuous territorial control.

By the late 13th century, Muslim rulers were established in parts of northern Sumatra. Perlak, Aceh is described by [Marco Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Polo "Marco Polo") as Muslim in 1292, and a royal tomb at Pasai dated 1297 records a Muslim ruler there. [Samudra-Pasai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samudera_Pasai_Sultanate "Samudera Pasai Sultanate") became an important pepper port and a major centre for Islamic learning. [Islam spread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spread_of_Islam_in_Indonesia "Spread of Islam in Indonesia") further through maritime trading networks, with Muslim communities becoming established at ports along Java's north coast and elsewhere in the archipelago. [Majapahit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majapahit "Majapahit") declined during the 15th century, and [Demak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demak_Sultanate "Demak Sultanate") emerged as an independent north-coast sultanate and an important centre of Javanese Islam. Early conversions were associated with the *[Wali Sanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wali_Sanga "Wali Sanga")*, the nine saints traditionally credited with the spread of Islam in Java. Islamic practice there also incorporated elements of older Javanese culture.

### European contact and colonial rule

Before European arrival, spices from [Maluku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maluku_Islands "Maluku Islands") were already traded through Asian maritime networks in which Javanese and other regional merchants participated. The Portuguese [captured Malacca in 1511](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Malacca_(1511) "Capture of Malacca (1511)") and reached Maluku the following year, where they sought control of the spice trade. [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic "Dutch Republic") expeditions followed in the 1590s, and in 1602 competing Dutch companies were merged into the [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company "Dutch East India Company") (*Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie*, VOC). Its charter authorised it to wage war, build fortresses, and conclude treaties in Asia. In 1619, the VOC [captured Jayakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Jayakarta "Fall of Jayakarta") (present-day [Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta "Jakarta")), renamed it [Batavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batavia,_Dutch_East_Indies "Batavia, Dutch East Indies"), and turned it into its headquarters. It developed as an administrative and commercial centre and attracted Chinese migrants and others from the archipelago. The VOC was dissolved in 1800, and its possessions were passed to the [Dutch state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batavian_Republic "Batavian Republic").

During the 19th century, Dutch power expanded across the archipelago and met sustained [resistance in Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java_War "Java War"), [Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padri_War "Padri War"), [Bali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_intervention_in_Bali_(1908) "Dutch intervention in Bali (1908)"), and [Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aceh_War "Aceh War"). In [western New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_New_Guinea "Western New Guinea"), the Dutch established an outpost in 1828 but abandoned it in 1836 and began permanent occupation only after 1898. By the early 20th century, military campaigns and boundary agreements had established Dutch control over the [Netherlands Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_Indies "Dutch East Indies"). The colonial administration ruled several territories directly and governed others through local states whose rulers retained limited authority under Dutch supremacy.

The [Ethical Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Ethical_Policy "Dutch Ethical Policy") became an official programme in 1901 and expanded vernacular schooling, irrigation, and other public services. Western-style schooling also expanded, but it reached only a small minority of the population. During this period, several political organisations were founded, including [Budi Utomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budi_Utomo "Budi Utomo") in 1908 and [Sarekat Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarekat_Islam "Sarekat Islam") in 1912, followed by the [Communist Party of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Indonesia "Communist Party of Indonesia") (PKI) in 1920 and the [Indonesian National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Party "Indonesian National Party") in 1927. The [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression "Great Depression") reached the colony in the early 1930s, bringing severe economic hardship and increasing fears among colonial authorities that discontent could strengthen radical nationalist movements. Restrictions on nationalist activity became stricter, and by the mid-1930s, [Sukarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukarno "Sukarno"), [Mohammad Hatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Hatta "Mohammad Hatta"), [Sutan Sjahrir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutan_Sjahrir "Sutan Sjahrir"), and other non-cooperative nationalist leaders had been imprisoned or exiled.

### Emergence of Indonesia and independence

A shared national identity began developing through youth associations and a growing literature in the [Malay language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language "Malay language"), which had long been a *[lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca "Lingua franca")* that did not favour any single ethnic group. Nationalists increasingly called the language *[Bahasa Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language "Indonesian language")* and made it a common medium for the emerging nation. At the Second Youth Congress in Batavia in October 1928, delegates pledged allegiance to one homeland and one nation, Indonesia, and recognised [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language "Indonesian language") as the "language of unity".

Dutch colonial rule collapsed when Japan [conquered the colony in 1942](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_Indies_campaign "Dutch East Indies campaign"). [Japanese occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_the_Dutch_East_Indies "Japanese occupation of the Dutch East Indies") imposed martial law, banned political parties, and mobilised Indonesians through Japanese-sponsored civil and quasi-military organisations. Wartime shortages and forced labour caused widespread hardship, but the ban on the [Dutch language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language "Dutch language") strengthened the public use of Indonesian. Japanese-sponsored forces such as [PETA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defenders_of_the_Homeland "Defenders of the Homeland") and the Sumatran *Giyugun* trained tens of thousands of Indonesians. A day after the [atomic bombing of Hiroshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bombings_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki "Atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki"), Japanese authorities permitted Indonesian leaders to begin preparing institutions and a constitution for an independent state planned for September 1945.

However, [Japan surrendered to the Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrender_of_Japan "Surrender of Japan") on 15 August 1945. Two days later, [Sukarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukarno "Sukarno") proclaimed [Indonesian independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_Indonesian_Independence "Proclamation of Indonesian Independence"), with [Hatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Hatta "Mohammad Hatta") co-signing the declaration. They were named president and vice-president on 18 August. Dutch attempts to restore colonial rule led to a four-year [military and diplomatic struggle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Revolution "Indonesian National Revolution"). International opposition to the Netherlands intensified after [its offensive of December 1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Kraai "Operation Kraai"), and the United States subsequently pressed the Dutch into negotiations recommended by the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations"). On 27 December 1949, the Netherlands [transferred sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch%E2%80%93Indonesian_Round_Table_Conference "Dutch–Indonesian Round Table Conference") to the federal [United States of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_Indonesia "United States of Indonesia"), except over [western New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_New_Guinea "Western New Guinea"). The federal states dissolved, and a unitary republic was proclaimed on 17 August 1950.

### Sukarno era and Suharto's New Order

Indonesia adopted parliamentary government under the [1950 provisional constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Constitution_of_1950 "Provisional Constitution of 1950"). The [1955 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1955_Indonesian_legislative_election "1955 Indonesian legislative election") produced no durable governing majority and did not end the persistent political crisis. In February 1958, dissidents in [West Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Sumatra "West Sumatra") proclaimed [its own government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Government_of_the_Republic_of_Indonesia "Revolutionary Government of the Republic of Indonesia") before being joined by [rebels in Sulawesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permesta "Permesta"). They were seeking reforms to the republic rather than separation but were defeated by the central government. After the [Constituent Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Assembly_of_Indonesia "Constitutional Assembly of Indonesia") failed to secure the required majority for a permanent constitution, Sukarno [dissolved it by decree in 1959](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_Sukarno%27s_1959_Decree "President Sukarno's 1959 Decree") and restored the 1945 Constitution. This established the [Guided Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided_Democracy_in_Indonesia "Guided Democracy in Indonesia"), a system characterised by dominant presidential authority, less regional autonomy and reduced role for political parties.

Under this system, Sukarno expanded the military's political role while supporting the growing PKI as a counterweight. His campaign against Dutch rule in western New Guinea led to the [New York Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Agreement "New York Agreement") in 1962 and Indonesian administration of the territory in 1963. After the [30 September Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30_September_Movement "30 September Movement") killed six generals in 1965, army general [Suharto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suharto "Suharto")'s forces took control of Jakarta, and the military presented the movement as a PKI coup. The ensuing [anti-communist campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_mass_killings_of_1965%E2%80%9366 "Indonesian mass killings of 1965–66") spread across several regions, with army units working alongside or directing civilian groups. At least 500,000 people were killed, and hundreds of thousands were detained. The campaign destroyed the PKI and shifted power to the army. The *[Supersemar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersemar "Supersemar")* order in 1966 gave Suharto *de facto* control over key executive and security powers, an effective transfer of power from Sukarno.

Suharto's [New Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Order_(Indonesia) "New Order (Indonesia)") restricted political organisation, embedded the military in government, and used his political party [Golkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golkar "Golkar") and a tightly controlled party system to dominate elections. The [Act of Free Choice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Free_Choice "Act of Free Choice") in 1969 formally incorporated western New Guinea into Indonesia. However, just over a thousand selected community leaders took part in it amid reports of military intimidation. Many [Papuans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Papuans "Indonesian Papuans") opposed this integration and have [waged an armed resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papua_conflict "Papua conflict") since then. In 1975, Indonesia [invaded Portuguese Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_invasion_of_East_Timor "Indonesian invasion of East Timor") and [annexed the territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Timor_(province) "East Timor (province)") the following year. [Hasan di Tiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasan_Tiro "Hasan Tiro") founded the [Free Aceh Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Aceh_Movement "Free Aceh Movement") in 1976 and declared independence from Indonesia. The [insurgency in Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurgency_in_Aceh "Insurgency in Aceh") was largely suppressed by 1979 but revived in 1989 after the return of fighters trained in [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya "Libya"), prompting a decade-long military campaign.

The New Order reopened the economy to foreign capital and pursued industrialisation through investment, foreign aid, and state development plans. Manufacturing expanded, and the number of people below the official poverty line fell substantially by the mid-1990s. At the same time, conglomerates connected to Suharto's family and close associates accumulated wealth through state patronage and political protection. The [Asian financial crisis of 1997–98](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Asian_financial_crisis "1997 Asian financial crisis") caused a sharp economic downturn, followed by protests and [riots in May 1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_1998_Indonesia_riots "May 1998 Indonesia riots"), culminating in [Suharto's resignation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Suharto "Fall of Suharto"). In a [UN-supervised referendum in 1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_East_Timorese_independence_referendum "1999 East Timorese independence referendum"), a majority of people in [East Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Timor_(province) "East Timor (province)") voted against remaining part Indonesia; East Timor became the independent country of [Timor-Leste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timor-Leste "Timor-Leste") in 2002.

### *Reformasi* and contemporary Indonesia

The period after Suharto, known as *[Reformasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_era_in_Indonesia "Reform era in Indonesia")*, brought constitutional amendments, competitive elections, and decentralisation. The military lost its reserved parliamentary seats, and the police became a separate service, but military influence did not disappear. Democratic institutions were established, but patronage and influence of oligarchy persisted. Former general [Susilo Bambang Yudhoyono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susilo_Bambang_Yudhoyono "Susilo Bambang Yudhoyono") won the first direct [presidential election in 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Indonesian_presidential_election "2004 Indonesian presidential election"). His administration reached a [peace agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki_Agreement "Helsinki Agreement") with Aceh separatists in 2005 after the previous year's [Indian Ocean tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Indian_Ocean_earthquake_and_tsunami "2004 Indian Ocean earthquake and tsunami") brought more international attention to the province. [Sporadic bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_in_Indonesia "Terrorism in Indonesia") occurred throughout the 2000s but had declined by the early 2010s.

[Joko Widodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joko_Widodo "Joko Widodo"), popularly called Jokowi, was elected president [in 2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Indonesian_presidential_election "2014 Indonesian presidential election") and prioritised infrastructure and economic development, which he linked to improving welfare. Indonesia remained an electoral democracy, but the quality of its democracy declined amid weakened parliamentary opposition and oversight, curbs on dissent, reduced independence of the [anti-corruption commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_Eradication_Commission "Corruption Eradication Commission"), and Jokowi's intervention in the [2024 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Indonesian_presidential_election "2024 Indonesian presidential election"). Under his successor [Prabowo Subianto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prabowo_Subianto "Prabowo Subianto"), the government pursued a fiscally expansive programme and revised a military law in 2025, which allowed more active officers to serve in civilian posts.

## Geography

Indonesia is an equatorial archipelago of coastal plains, mountains, and volcanic islands. It lies between latitudes [11°S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_parallel_south "11th parallel south") and [6°N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_parallel_north "6th parallel north") and longitudes [95°E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/95th_meridian_east "95th meridian east") and [141°E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/141st_meridian_east "141st meridian east"), and is the world's largest [archipelagic state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelagic_state "Archipelagic state"), extending 5,120 kilometres (3,181 mi) from east to west and 1,760 kilometres (1,094 mi) from north to south. Several straits, including [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca "Strait of Malacca"), [Sunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_Strait "Sunda Strait"), and [Lombok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombok_Strait "Lombok Strait"), carry international traffic between the Indian and Pacific oceans, including ships transporting energy supplies and commercial cargo.

The quantity of islands varies by counting method, with published figures ranging from 13,466 named islands to more than 17,500. About 922 of them are permanently inhabited. Its five main islands are [Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatra "Sumatra"), [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java "Java"), [Sulawesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulawesi "Sulawesi"), [Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo "Borneo"), and [New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Guinea "New Guinea"). Indonesia occupies most of Borneo, where it is known as [Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalimantan "Kalimantan"), and the western part of New Guinea, commonly called [Papua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_New_Guinea "Western New Guinea"). The remainder of those islands is shared with [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei "Brunei") and [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia"), and with [Papua New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papua_New_Guinea "Papua New Guinea"), respectively.

Java accounts for less than 7% of Indonesia's land area but is its most populous island, with intensive farming and extensive urban growth. Forest cover differs widely, with larger forested areas in Papua and Maluku than in Java and Bali. At 4,884 metres (16,024 ft), [Puncak Jaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puncak_Jaya "Puncak Jaya") on New Guinea is the highest peak, while [Lake Toba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Toba "Lake Toba") on Sumatra is the largest lake. In Kalimantan, major waterways like the [Kapuas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapuas_River "Kapuas River"), [Barito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barito_River "Barito River"), and [Mahakam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahakam_River "Mahakam River") rivers have historically served as corridors for movement and trade between communities in the interior and those near the coast.

Indonesia occupies a complex plate-boundary zone where the [Eurasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_plate "Eurasian plate"), [India-Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Australian_plate "Indo-Australian plate"), and [Pacific-Philippine sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Sea_plate "Philippine Sea plate") tectonic plates interact, with [subduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subduction "Subduction") zones along these boundaries producing frequent earthquakes and volcanic activity. The resulting [volcanic belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_belt "Volcanic belt") contains roughly 400 volcanoes, around 130 of which are considered active. This tectonic setting exposes the archipelago to major earthquake and tsunami hazards. The [Sumatra earthquake in 2004 generated a tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Indian_Ocean_earthquake_and_tsunami "2004 Indian Ocean earthquake and tsunami") that caused widespread destruction in Aceh. The [Toba super-eruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youngest_Toba_eruption "Youngest Toba eruption") about 74,000 years ago is one of the [largest known explosive eruptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_volcanic_eruptions "List of largest volcanic eruptions") in Earth's history, and the [1815 eruption of Mount Tambora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1815_eruption_of_Mount_Tambora "1815 eruption of Mount Tambora") was followed by global cooling and the [Year Without a Summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Year_Without_a_Summer "Year Without a Summer") in 1816.

### Climate

Indonesia's equatorial position and [monsoon winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon "Monsoon") give it a tropical climate. Most lowland areas are warm and humid throughout the year, while higher terrain is cooler, and seasonal temperature differences are small. Seasonal changes in the monsoon generally bring wetter conditions from November to April and drier conditions from May to October, but their timing and intensity vary by region. Much of the country has a [tropical rainforest climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest_climate "Tropical rainforest climate"), with tropical monsoon and savanna climates across parts of the islands. Seasonal rainfall helps set rice-planting calendars and influences droughts, floods, and fires.

Western Sumatra, western Java, and portions of Kalimantan and Papua receive the highest annual rainfall, whereas regions in closer proximity to Australia—such as [Nusa Tenggara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Sunda_Islands "Lesser Sunda Islands")—experience a significantly drier climate. The surrounding oceans, island geography, monsoon winds, and topography all affect where and when rain falls. In drought-prone regions of the country, [El Niño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Ni%C3%B1o%E2%80%93Southern_Oscillation "El Niño–Southern Oscillation") can suppress rainfall and prolong dry periods, causing water shortages and crop losses.

Indonesia already faces climate-related hazards. Climate projections indicate further warming and sea-level rise, changes in rainfall patterns, and greater risks from extreme heat and flooding. These changes affect agriculture, water resources, public health, coastal settlements, and wildfire management. [Sea-level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise "Sea level rise") is especially relevant to coastal areas, where much of the population and infrastructure are located. Evidence from [Pekalongan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pekalongan "Pekalongan") in Central Java suggests that households with fewer economic resources and communities with weaker infrastructure may have less capacity to adapt.

### Biodiversity and conservation

Indonesia is among the 17 countries identified as [megadiverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megadiverse_countries "Megadiverse countries") by [Conservation International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_International "Conservation International"). Its many islands and geological past have helped produce a diverse flora and fauna. [Wallacea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallacea "Wallacea") is a transition zone between Asian and Australian fauna and one of the world's largest centres of endemism. In 2015, an estimated 83% of Southeast Asia's old-growth forest was in Indonesia. Mangrove forests, coral reefs and seagrass beds make up much of the coastal ecosystems.

[Large oil-palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_oil_production_in_Indonesia "Palm oil production in Indonesia") and timber plantations were estimated to account for more than 40% of [national deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation_in_Indonesia "Deforestation in Indonesia") between 2001 and 2016. Conversion of forest land for farming, roads, and other development breaks up remaining habitat, which affects species populations, habitat quality, and biodiversity. Peat-swamp forests are important carbon stores and habitat for threatened taxa but are vulnerable to logging, fire and land conversion. In the ocean, coral diversity has been found lower on polluted or anchor-damaged reefs, and coral cover has declined sharply under extreme, prolonged ocean heat.

Indonesia has designated [554 protected areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protected_areas_of_Indonesia "Protected areas of Indonesia") covering 27 million hectares, equivalent to 14% of its land area. Assessments conducted in 2015, 2017 and 2019 found improved management scores, but identified poaching, illegal logging, human settlements, tourism and non-timber cultivation as the main threats to protected areas. In Papua, research around the [Cyclops Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclops_Mountains "Cyclops Mountains") protected area found limited consultation with nearby communities and implementation problems linked to scarce resources.

## Government and politics

Indonesia is a unitary republic with a presidential system under the [1945 Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Indonesia "Constitution of Indonesia"). The constitution's five principles of *[Pancasila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancasila "Pancasila")* are taught through civic education. After the fall of the New Order in 1998, four [constitutional amendments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amendments_to_the_Constitution_of_Indonesia "Amendments to the Constitution of Indonesia") between 1999 and 2002 altered the political system by introducing direct presidential elections, establishing new state institutions, reducing role of the [People's Consultative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Consultative_Assembly "People's Consultative Assembly") (MPR), and giving regional autonomy a constitutional basis. Indonesia remained a unitary state, while decentralisation transferred substantial powers to the provinces, districts, and cities.

The [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Indonesia "President of Indonesia") is head of state, head of government, and commander-in-chief of the [Indonesian National Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Armed_Forces "Indonesian National Armed Forces"), and may serve no more than two five-year terms. The MPR consists of elected members of the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_(Indonesia) "House of Representatives (Indonesia)") (DPR) and the [Regional Representative Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Representative_Council "Regional Representative Council") (DPD). The MPR may amend the constitution, inaugurates the president and vice president, and may dismiss them under constitutional procedures. The DPR makes laws with the president, approves the state budget, and oversees the government. The DPD may propose, review, and advise on bills concerning regional government but has less authority than the DPR.

The legal system belongs primarily to the [civil-law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_(legal_system) "Civil law (legal system)") tradition, while also incorporating customary (*[adat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adat "Adat")*) law and Islamic law, including in marriage and inheritance matters for Muslims. [Judicial power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_of_Indonesia "Judiciary of Indonesia") is exercised by the [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Indonesia "Supreme Court of Indonesia"), the courts beneath it, and the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Indonesia "Constitutional Court of Indonesia"). The Supreme Court acts as the final appellate court and reviews regulations below statutes. The Constitutional Court reviews statutes for constitutionality and decides disputes over state-institution powers, political party dissolution, and election results. It also has a role in presidential impeachment proceedings. The Religious Courts hear marriage, divorce and inheritance cases, as well as certain economic disputes arising under Islamic law. The [Judicial Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_Commission_of_Indonesia "Judicial Commission of Indonesia") proposes candidates for Supreme Court justices and has responsibilities concerning judicial conduct.

### Parties and elections

Since the return to [competitive elections in 1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Indonesian_legislative_election "1999 Indonesian legislative election"), Indonesia has had a fragmented [multi-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system "Multi-party system"). No party has won a majority in [legislative elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Indonesia "Elections in Indonesia"), and presidents have usually governed through broad coalitions that include many parties in the legislature and cabinet.

Political parties are often distinguished by their orientation toward secular nationalism or political Islam, but governing coalitions commonly include both. Party politics often relies more on patronage and elite power-sharing than detailed policy programmes, although the role of Islam in public life is a source of ideological difference. Opposition parties have at times joined presidential power-sharing arrangements after elections.

Indonesia held its first [general election in 1955](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1955_Indonesian_legislative_election "1955 Indonesian legislative election"). Since [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Indonesian_presidential_election "2004 Indonesian presidential election"), voters have directly elected the president, the DPR, and the DPD every five years. Members of the DPR are elected through party-based contests, while DPD candidates run as individuals to represent provincial constituencies. National elections require large-scale logistics, as ballots and other materials must reach remote islands, mountain areas, and places with limited infrastructure.

### Administrative divisions

Indonesia is a unitary state divided into [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Indonesia "Provinces of Indonesia"), [regencies, and cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_regencies_and_cities_in_Indonesia "List of regencies and cities in Indonesia"), each with elected regional authorities regulated by law. Provinces are headed by governors and have provincial legislatures. Regencies and cities are headed by regents and mayors and have local legislatures. Below regencies and cities are [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Indonesia "Districts of Indonesia"), which are divided into [villages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villages_of_Indonesia "Villages of Indonesia"). Rural villages have self-governing status, while urban villages form part of district administration.

Regional autonomy after 1998 transferred substantial powers to subnational governments, including the provinces. Special arrangements apply to nine of them: Aceh may apply aspects of Islamic law, the six provinces in Papua have special autonomy, and [Yogyakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Region_of_Yogyakarta "Special Region of Yogyakarta") retains a hereditary sultanate. Jakarta has autonomy as the national capital. Since 2022, [Nusantara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nusantara "Nusantara") in [East Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Kalimantan "East Kalimantan") has been planned as the future national capital.

### Foreign relations and military

Indonesia follows an "independent and active" (*bebas aktif*) foreign policy, a doctrine formulated by Vice President Mohammad Hatta in 1948. The policy has emphasised non-alignment and independent action, along with a preference for diplomacy over military means in advancing regional objectives. As a middle power, Indonesia works through regional and multilateral institutions while preserving room to manoeuvre in its relations with major powers.

As a founding member of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations ([ASEAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN "ASEAN")), Indonesia places the organisation at the centre of its regional diplomacy, including in managing relations with major powers. Indonesia has broad bilateral ties with [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia%E2%80%93Malaysia_relations "Indonesia–Malaysia relations") and [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia%E2%80%93Singapore_relations "Indonesia–Singapore relations"), including cooperation in economic, defence, and cultural fields. [Relations with Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93Indonesia_relations "Australia–Indonesia relations") are conducted through a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership covering several areas, including economic development and regional stability. On China–United States competition, Indonesia has avoided alliances and taking sides, emphasised an ASEAN-led regional approach, and sought to limit escalation.

Indonesia has been a member of the United Nations since 1950, apart from an [absence in 1965–66](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withdrawal_from_the_United_Nations#Indonesia "Withdrawal from the United Nations"). It participates in multilateral forums including the [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement "Non-Aligned Movement"), the [Organisation of Islamic Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_Islamic_Cooperation "Organisation of Islamic Cooperation"), and the [East Asia Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia_Summit "East Asia Summit"). After decades as a major recipient of foreign aid, Indonesia has been providing development assistance since 2015 and established a foreign aid agency in 2019. Since 1957, it has sent military and police personnel to [UN peacekeeping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_peacekeeping "United Nations peacekeeping") missions.

The [Indonesian National Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Armed_Forces "Indonesian National Armed Forces") consists of the [Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Army "Indonesian Army"), [Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Navy "Indonesian Navy"), and [Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Air_Force "Indonesian Air Force"). The Army's territorial units extend across provincial and regency/city areas. Until its termination in 2000, the military's "dual function" (*[dwifungsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwifungsi "Dwifungsi")*) doctrine provided a basis for involvement in political affairs alongside its security role. By 2004, reforms had ended unelected military representation in Parliament and removed the military faction from the MPR. A 2024 study found that cooperative parliamentary oversight had helped stabilise civil–military relations but also slowed further reform and weakened democratic management of defence.

### Law enforcement and human rights

Law enforcement is primarily carried out by the [Indonesian National Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Police "Indonesian National Police"). Duties include maintaining public order and security, protecting and serving the public, and enforcing the law. The [Chief of the Indonesian National Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Indonesian_National_Police "Chief of the Indonesian National Police") is directly responsible to the president.

The [National Commission on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Commission_on_Human_Rights "National Commission on Human Rights") (Komnas HAM) is authorised to investigate human rights violations. Accountability for past abuses has been limited. In 2024, the [United Nations Human Rights Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Human_Rights_Committee "United Nations Human Rights Committee") noted that only 4 of 16 violations investigated by Komnas HAM had reached the courts. Indonesia [practices the death penalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_Indonesia "Capital punishment in Indonesia") but has not executed anyone since 2016.

[Anti-Chinese racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrimination_against_Chinese_Indonesians "Discrimination against Chinese Indonesians") has persisted despite the removal of most discriminatory policies after the New Order. Since 2016, [anti-LGBTQ campaigns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_rights_in_Indonesia "LGBTQ rights in Indonesia") have included hostile statements by public officials, police raids and proposals for criminal restrictions. Religious minorities have also been affected by the country's [blasphemy law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blasphemy_law_in_Indonesia "Blasphemy law in Indonesia"). In Papua, human rights monitors have reported extrajudicial killings and disappearances, restrictions on peaceful expression, and displacement due to security operations and armed conflict.

## Economy

Indonesia has a [mixed economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_economy "Mixed economy") in which the [private sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_sector "Private sector") and the government both have large roles. It is the only [G20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G20 "G20") member state in Southeast Asia. In 2025, gross domestic product was Rp23.821 quadrillion (US$1.45 trillion) at current prices, making it Southeast Asia's largest economy and placing it among the world's [top 20 by nominal GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(nominal) "List of countries by GDP (nominal)") and [top 10 by GDP at purchasing power parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(PPP) "List of countries by GDP (PPP)"). Services and industry account for the largest shares of gross domestic product, while agriculture remains a major source of employment.

The economy was once largely agricultural, and early post-independence policy centred on agricultural self-sufficiency. Shift towards industrial and urban growth gathered pace from the late 1960s, and sped up in the 1980s as lower oil prices pushed the economy toward manufactured exports. Export-oriented manufacturing expanded from the mid-1980s to the mid-1990s, during a period of strong economic growth and declining poverty. The Asian financial crisis of 1997–98 caused a severe contraction, but growth resumed in 1999 and was followed by reforms to banking, fiscal policy and the exchange-rate regime.

Consumer spending makes up a large share of GDP, along with population growth, urbanisation and an expanding consuming class. Domestic demand helped soften the effect of the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis "2008 financial crisis"), while exports, public consumption and private consumption contributed to recovery after the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"). Informal employment is widespread, particularly in agriculture and low-skilled services. Informal firms tend to have lower productivity and pay, and workers outside the bigger firms have more limited access to social insurance. Businesses also have to contend with corruption, burdensome and sometimes conflicting local regulations, in addition to unreliable infrastructure such as power outages and congested roads. The official poverty rate was about 8% in March 2026.

The many islands affect where economic activity takes place and how goods move across the country. Economic activity is concentrated on Java, with Jakarta in particular accounting for a disproportionate share of national output. Meanwhile, much of eastern Indonesia has weaker infrastructure and economies more dependent on agriculture. High domestic transport costs hinder production and investment outside Java and make it harder to connect producers in the outer islands with major markets.

Tourism is a major service industry, generating about [US$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar "United States dollar")14 billion in foreign-exchange earnings and recording 11.6 million international visitor arrivals in 2023. Tourism-related jobs and enterprises include accommodation, food services, passenger transport, travel agencies and other activities. International tourism is concentrated in [Bali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bali "Bali") and other major gateways, while domestic travellers account for most tourism spending.

Indonesia spends little on research and development, with limited private-sector involvement and weak coordination in government. [Indonesian Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Aerospace "Indonesian Aerospace") is involved in aircraft design and the development and manufacture of civilian and military regional commuter aircraft. The [Palapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palapa "Palapa") domestic satellite system extended communications to remote parts of Indonesia through a network of earth stations and was also used to distribute television and radio programmes.

Natural resource industries affect both investment and economic growth, which can rise or fall with commodity prices. Policies since 2020 have encouraged more processing of commodities at home, particularly [nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel "Nickel"). Exports include coal and petroleum gas, as well as palm oil, coffee and spices. Its main trading partners are mostly in Asia, with the United States also among the largest. Indonesia takes part in regional economic cooperation through ASEAN and [APEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APEC "APEC") and is integrated into the wider global economy.

## Infrastructure

### Transport and connectivity

Road transport is the predominant mode of [transport in Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Indonesia "Transport in Indonesia"). Reliance on cars and motorcycles is high because public transport in many cities has not kept pace with urban growth. Rail transport is concentrated on Java, but new lines have recently begun operating in South Sulawesi. The [Jakarta–Bandung high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail_in_Indonesia "High-speed rail in Indonesia"), the first of its kind in the region, began commercial operations in 2023.

Maritime and air networks are important for linking population centres across the country. Connectivity is weaker in eastern Indonesia, where higher logistics costs contribute to regional price differences. In the telecommunications sector, the Palapa Ring project uses terrestrial and submarine fibre as a core national internet network, but there are service gaps in some remote regions.

### Energy

In 2023, Indonesia produced nearly twice as much primary energy as it consumed, with coal dominating both primary energy production and electricity generation. The country exports large volumes of coal and liquified natural gas and imports substantial amounts of refined petroleum products. The state-owned electricity company [Perusahaan Listrik Negara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perusahaan_Listrik_Negara "Perusahaan Listrik Negara") (PLN) controls transmission and distribution networks and purchases electricity from independent power producers for supply to the national grids.

Renewables supplied a small amount of electricity in 2023, although the country ranked first globally in geothermal generation. Investment in renewable energy has been hindered by regulatory uncertainty and PLN's financial capacity.

## Demographics

The [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Indonesian_census "2020 Indonesian census") recorded 270.2 million people, making it the world's [fourth most populous country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population "List of countries and dependencies by population") after India, China and the United States. The population is concentrated on Java and in urban centres, while many provinces outside Java and Bali are much less densely populated. Java is home to 56.1% of the population, and its population density is far above the national average, reaching 1,171 people per square kilometre (3,030 people/mi2).

Indonesia has a median age of 30.7 years as of 2026, while about 69% of the population was aged 15–64 in 2025. Jakarta is listed as the world's most populous city, with nearly 42 million inhabitants as of 2025. Approximately 59% of Indonesians lived in urban areas as of 2025. Urbanisation has come from rural-to-urban migration, natural increase in urban areas, and the reclassification of rural settlements. It has also expanded metropolitan regions, especially on Java, and increased demand for infrastructure, basic services, and city management. In addition, about 8 million [Indonesians reside overseas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_diaspora "Indonesian diaspora"), with large communities in Malaysia, the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands "Netherlands"), [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia "Saudi Arabia"), [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore"), and [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan").

### Ethnic groups and languages

The [2010 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Indonesian_census "2010 Indonesian census") listed over 1,300 *suku bangsa*, or ethnic groups. A later study in 2015 reclassified them into about 600 groups after noting classification difficulties. [Austronesian-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_languages "Austronesian languages") populations predominate in much of western and central Indonesia. Melanesian populations live mainly in the eastern islands, including parts of Nusa Tenggara, Maluku, and Papua. The [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people "Javanese people") are the largest ethnic group, making up about 40% of the population. Other major groups include the [Sundanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundanese_people "Sundanese people"), [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Indonesians "Malay Indonesians"), [Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak "Batak"), [Madurese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madurese_people "Madurese people"), [Betawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betawi_people "Betawi people"), [Minangkabau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minangkabau_people "Minangkabau people"), and [Bugis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugis_people "Bugis people").

The official language, [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language "Indonesian language"), is a standardized variety of [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language "Malay language") based on the Riau-Johor literary tradition. Malay had long served as a *[lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca "Lingua franca")* in the archipelago before Indonesian nationalists adopted it as a language of unity through the 1928 [Youth Pledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_Pledge "Youth Pledge"). It gained official and formal status in 1945 under the name *Bahasa Indonesia*. Written in the [Latin script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_script "Latin script"), Indonesian has spread through education, government, and mass media, and serves as a common language across ethnic and regional boundaries.

More than 700 languages are spoken across the archipelago. They are divided into two major language groups, [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_languages "Austronesian languages") and [Papuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papuan_languages "Papuan languages"). [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_language "Javanese language") is the most widely spoken local language and has official regional status in [Yogyakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Region_of_Yogyakarta "Special Region of Yogyakarta"). Several regional languages in Indonesia have traditional writing systems of their own, some of which survive in only limited use today. Local languages remain part of regional identity and cultural heritage, even as Indonesian dominates national public life. [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language "Dutch language") never became widely spoken as Malay was already the archipelago's lingua franca. However, it remains important in some legal contexts.

### Religion

Indonesia recognises six religions: [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Indonesia "Islam in Indonesia"), [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism_in_Indonesia "Protestantism in Indonesia"), [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Indonesia "Catholic Church in Indonesia"), [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Indonesia "Hinduism in Indonesia"), [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_Indonesia "Buddhism in Indonesia"), and [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Council_for_the_Confucian_Religion_in_Indonesia "Supreme Council for the Confucian Religion in Indonesia"), while the constitution guarantees freedom of religion. The state regulates religious affiliation and public worship. Recognised religions receive administrative recognition for religious affiliation, marriages, and houses of worship, but followers of [indigenous religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliran_Kepercayaan "Aliran Kepercayaan") often receive weaker recognition or face discrimination. *Pancasila* makes belief in one God one of the state ideology's five principles and is used to support national unity and religious harmony. Religious minorities still face discrimination, including through local religious bylaws, social pressure, and restrictions on worship.

Indonesia has [the largest Muslim population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_by_country "Islam by country") in the world, with 251 million Muslims in 2025, or 87.15% of the population. Christians make up 10.44% of the population and form majorities in several eastern provinces. Hindus account for 1.66% and are concentrated in Bali, while Buddhists account for 0.69% and are found mainly among [Chinese Indonesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Indonesians "Chinese Indonesians"), though not exclusively.

Many local belief systems predate major world religions and continue alongside them. They include beliefs in ancestors, spirits, and powers associated with nature. Traditions such as [Sunda Wiwitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_Wiwitan "Sunda Wiwitan"), [Kejawèn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kejaw%C3%A8n "Kejawèn"), and [Kaharingan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaharingan "Kaharingan") continue in different forms within or alongside the recognised religions. In Java and Bali, Islam and Hinduism also absorbed older customs and ritual practices.

### Education

[Indonesia's education system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Indonesia "Education in Indonesia") includes more than 50 million students and over 250,000 schools. It is managed across ministries responsible for school education, higher education, and religious education, and follows a 6-3-3-4 structure: six years of elementary school, three years each of junior and senior secondary school, and four years of tertiary education.

Since independence, schools have supported national integration through Indonesian-language instruction, national curricula, and civic education. Access to education and school quality vary, particularly between urban and rural areas and among regions with differing levels of development. Teacher quality and distribution are also persistent issues. Student learning outcomes are also low by [OECD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OECD "OECD") benchmarks. In [PISA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programme_for_International_Student_Assessment "Programme for International Student Assessment") 2022, Indonesian 15-year-olds performed below OECD averages in mathematics, reading and science, with most not reaching the minimum proficiency level in each subject.

Government spending accounted for approximately 1.3% of GDP in 2023. In 2022, Indonesia had 4,481 higher education institutions, including universities, Islamic institutions, service colleges, and open universities. The [University of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Indonesia "University of Indonesia"), [Gadjah Mada University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gadjah_Mada_University "Gadjah Mada University"), and the [Bandung Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandung_Institute_of_Technology "Bandung Institute of Technology") are among the highest-ranked public universities in international rankings. Higher education has expanded since 1990 and contributes to better employment, but access, participation, and institutional quality are uneven.

### Healthcare

Primary care is provided mainly through community health centres (*[puskesmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puskesmas "Puskesmas")*) and primary-care hospitals. In 2014, Indonesia launched *[Jaminan Kesehatan Nasional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_Indonesia#Universal_health_coverage "Healthcare in Indonesia")*, one of the world's largest [single-payer health-insurance systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-payer_healthcare "Single-payer healthcare") that covered 98% of the population by 2024. Despite the near-universal coverage, where people live still affects the availability of care and healthcare personnel.

Life expectancy increased between 1990 and 2019 from 62.5 to 69.4 years for men and from 65.7 to 73.5 years for women, but there are substantial differences between provinces. Indonesia faces a double burden of disease. Non-communicable conditions have become increasingly prominent in overall health loss, and infectious diseases continue to contribute substantially to disability and early mortality. Stunting prevalence among children was 21.6% in 2022.

## Culture

[Indonesia's cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Indonesia "Culture of Indonesia") differ across islands, languages, and communities. Older populations and later Austronesian migrations contributed to this diversity, with Papuan and Melanesian populations prominent in the east. Movement by sea placed the archipelago within trading and migration networks involving Indian, Arab, Persian, Chinese, and mainland Southeast Asian peoples, followed later by Europeans. Religious and courtly influences entered through these routes, and European expansion ultimately brought colonial domination. Coastal commercial centres often encountered outside influences before they reached inland communities.

Indonesia has no single uniform cultural tradition. Regional traditions are tied to particular languages and local cultures, while pan-Indonesian forms generally use Indonesian and are intended for audiences across regional and ethnic boundaries. These traditions include performance, visual art, ritual, clothing, food, and literature. Modern popular culture includes cinema, television drama, music, celebrity media, and reality shows, along with imported Asian dramas and global formats adapted for Indonesian audiences.

Indonesia has 16 items on the [UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage Lists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_Intangible_Cultural_Heritage_Lists "UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage Lists"), including *[wayang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayang "Wayang")* puppet theatre, *[batik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batik "Batik")*, *[angklung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angklung "Angklung")*, the *[saman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saman_(dance) "Saman (dance)")* dance, and *[pencak silat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pencak_silat "Pencak silat")*. Since 2024, joint nominations have added *[pantun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun "Pantun")*, *[kebaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kebaya "Kebaya")*, and *[kolintang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolintang "Kolintang")* to the list.

### Visual arts and architecture

[Indonesian art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_art "Indonesian art") include textiles, puppetry, masks, carving, and painting. Textiles are used in rituals and can convey social standing and ethnic affiliation. Colonial rule and nationalism affected the development of modern painting, and tourism and urban art communities became increasingly important later in the 20th century. Regional [painting traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_painting "Indonesian painting") include Bali's classical *[Kamasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamasan "Kamasan")* tradition, a narrative form often described as *[wayang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayang "Wayang")* painting. Sculptural traditions range from megalithic monuments to Ngaju Dayak *hampatong* figures and *Asmat* artefacts, serving commemorative, protective, aesthetic, or spiritual functions.

[Indonesian architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Indonesia "Architecture of Indonesia") ranges from everyday houses to ritual houses (*[rumah adat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumah_adat "Rumah adat")*), whose layouts, posts, roofs, heirlooms, and construction rites can express ancestry, status, and local beliefs. Regional forms include the Javanese *[pendapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pendopo "Pendopo")*, [Dayak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayak_people "Dayak people") longhouses, Minangkabau *[Rumah Gadang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumah_Gadang "Rumah Gadang")*, and Toraja *[Tongkonan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongkonan "Tongkonan")*. Monumental religious architecture includes [Borobudur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borobudur "Borobudur") and [Prambanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prambanan "Prambanan"), while surviving Hindu and Buddhist sculpture from the 8th to 15th centuries includes works in stone, bronze, and gold.

[Clothing traditions differ by region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_costume_of_Indonesia "National costume of Indonesia") and are used in ceremonies, weddings, formal occasions, and as markers of local identity. *[Batik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batik "Batik")* and *[kebaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kebaya "Kebaya")* are widely used in national and formal dress and are especially prominent in Javanese culture. Other regional textile traditions include [Minangkabau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minangkabau "Minangkabau") gold-thread textiles and distinctive *[ikat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikat "Ikat")* traditions among [Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak "Batak") and many eastern Indonesian communities. Such textiles are used at formal events, in bridal exchange, ceremonies, and weddings.

### Performing arts

[Indonesian music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Indonesia "Music of Indonesia") and [dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance_in_Indonesia "Dance in Indonesia") include court, village, ritual, and popular forms. *[Gamelan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamelan "Gamelan")* traditions are centred especially in Java and Bali, while *[angklung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angklung "Angklung")* and other bamboo ensembles are used in western Java, Banyumas, and other regions. Drums, gongs, lutes, singing, and music often accompany dance. Islamic musical forms include *[gambus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qanb%C5%ABs "Qanbūs")* and *[qasida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qasida "Qasida")*, while popular genres include *[keroncong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kroncong "Kroncong")* and *[dangdut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dangdut "Dangdut")*, which grew from Melayu music and draws on Indian, Arabic, and Western pop and rock elements.

Mask dances appear in Javanese court drama and Balinese ritual performance, while Dayak mask dances are performed in agricultural rituals and communal ceremonial settings. Javanese court dance includes forms such as *[bedhaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedhaya "Bedhaya")* and *[serimpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serimpi "Serimpi")*, while other local dances are associated with theatre and music, spirit possession, harvests, temple festivals, cremation ceremonies, and commemorations of the dead. Contemporary performers also adapt global forms.

Traditional [Indonesian theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_of_Indonesia "Theatre of Indonesia") encompasses storytelling, music, movement, puppetry, masks, acting, and dance. *Wayang* is a prominent puppet-theatre tradition in Java that is also practiced in Bali. Led by a *dalang* and accompanied by *gamelan* music, its repertory frequently reworks episodes from the *[Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana "Ramayana")* and *[Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata "Mahabharata")*. It has served ritual, moral, comic, political, and public-messaging functions. Other regional traditions combine drama with music, dance, masks, or martial arts, and modern theatre groups have used their performances for social and political commentary.

### Literature

[Literary traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_literature "Indonesian literature") developed in many regional languages and included both oral and written forms. Oral traditions ranged from storytelling to songs, customs, beliefs, and other community forms, while material could pass between oral and written transmission. Traditional written literature includes court chronicles, religious and legal writing, narrative prose, and verse. Such forms often served ethical, religious, or dynastic purposes rather than entertainment.

Modern literature grew in the early 20th century alongside the expansion of literacy, publishing, and a wider reading public. *[Balai Pustaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balai_Pustaka "Balai Pustaka")*, established in 1917, distributed books and maintained libraries and agencies across the archipelago, which gave writers access to wider circulation. During this period, prose fiction and poetry became more prominent, displacing some older literary forms. From 1933, *[Pujangga Baru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pujangga_Baru "Pujangga Baru")* provided another venue for literary and cultural writing, publishing essays, poetry, short stories, and longer prose before the Japanese occupation. Newspapers, magazines, and other publications carried Indonesian-language prose and poetry during the prewar period.

In the 1970s and 1980s, newspapers and magazines were important outlets, with regular sections carrying poetry, short stories, criticism, and serial fiction. From the late 1990s, some forms of literary activity began to emerge in digital media, with websites, mailing lists, forums, and blogs providing spaces for literary communities and for publication. Post-1998 literary production has faced fewer state restrictions, with writers able to engage more openly with previously sensitive subjects, such as sexuality and stronger religious expressions.

### Cinema and mass media

[Indonesian film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_Indonesia "Cinema of Indonesia") production began during the Dutch colonial era with *[Loetoeng Kasaroeng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loetoeng_Kasaroeng "Loetoeng Kasaroeng")* (1926), and a national film industry developed and expanded after independence. Film became a vehicle for nationalist and anti-colonial politics under Sukarno, while the New Order regulated and censored cinema. The domestic industry of the 1970s and 1980s declined sharply in the early 1990s. After 1998, independent filmmakers, film communities, festivals, and renewed commercial production helped revive Indonesian cinema. However, censorship and pressure from non-state actors still limited expression.

During the New Order, newspapers and broadcasters operated under licensing and censorship, and television was used to advance state-led development and a unified vision of national identity. In the *Reformasi* period, the 1999 Press Law prohibited censorship, publication bans, and licensing requirements, but journalists and media organisations still face legal cases, political pressure, violence, and other forms of intimidation.

[Mainstream media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media_in_Indonesia "Mass media in Indonesia") increasingly uses the same reporting across television, print, and online outlets, either through integrated newsrooms or by sharing content between them. News is also shortened or reformatted for websites and social media.

### Cuisine

[Indonesian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_cuisine "Indonesian cuisine") across regions in its ingredients, methods, and flavours. Trade and migration brought Indian, Chinese, and Middle Eastern influences over centuries, and Portuguese and other European traders arriving from the 16th century, followed by Dutch colonial rule, introduced further ingredients and cooking practices. Indonesian cuisine also spread abroad, particularly [to the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_cuisine#Indonesian "Dutch cuisine"), where cookbooks and migration introduced Indonesian food to Dutch society by the late 19th century.

Rice is the [staple food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staple_food "Staple food") in much of the country and is commonly served with meat or fish, vegetables, *sambal*, and other condiments. Chilli, coconut milk, shrimp paste, peanuts, garlic, shallots, and tamarind are widely used in cooking, along with foods such as chicken and seafood. Peanuts are used to make sauces for dishes such as *[gado-gado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gado-gado "Gado-gado")* and *[sate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satay "Satay")*. *[Tempeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempeh "Tempeh")* and *tahu* ([tofu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tofu "Tofu")) are widely eaten soybean products. *Tempeh* originated in Java and is throughout Indonesia. *Mie* ([noodle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noodle "Noodle")) and *[nasi goreng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasi_goreng "Nasi goreng")* are popular. Regional cuisines include [Minangkabau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padang_cuisine "Padang cuisine") dishes such as *[rendang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendang "Rendang")*. *[Tumpeng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumpeng "Tumpeng")* is a Javanese ceremonial rice dish served for life-cycle events and other communal observances, where it is associated with gratitude and blessing.

### Sports

Popular [sports in Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_Indonesia "Sports in Indonesia") include football, badminton, weightlifting, and basketball, along with local games and [Indonesian martial arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_martial_arts "Indonesian martial arts"). Football is one of the most followed sports, with active club supporters and public screenings of matches. The [national footbal team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia_national_football_team "Indonesia national football team") was the first Asian representative to appear at the [FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup "FIFA World Cup"), taking part in the [1938 tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup "1938 FIFA World Cup") as the Dutch East Indies.

Indonesia has been particularly successful in international badminton, winning both the [Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Cup "Thomas Cup") and [Uber Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uber_Cup "Uber Cup"), the men's and women's world team championships. Most [Olympic medals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia_at_the_Olympics "Indonesia at the Olympics") have come from [badminton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_at_the_Summer_Olympics "Badminton at the Summer Olympics") and [weightlifting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_weightlifting "Olympic weightlifting"). Indonesia first competed at the [Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Games "Olympic Games") in [1952](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1952_Summer_Olympics "1952 Summer Olympics") and has participated in the [Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Games "Asian Games") since the inaugural 1951 edition. Jakarta has hosted the Asian Games twice, first in [1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_Asian_Games "1962 Asian Games") and again in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Asian_Games "2018 Asian Games") with [Palembang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palembang "Palembang").

Traditional sports and games are featured in ceremonies, harvest events, contests, and public entertainment. Examples include *[sepak takraw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepak_takraw "Sepak takraw")*, bull racing (*[karapan sapi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karapan_sapi "Karapan sapi")*) in [Madura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madura "Madura"), and ritual combat traditions such as *caci* in [Flores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flores "Flores") and *[pasola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasola "Pasola")* in [Sumba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumba "Sumba"). *[Pencak silat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pencak_silat "Pencak silat")* is a martial art and was included as an official event at the [2018 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Asian_Games "2018 Asian Games"), where Indonesia won all 14 gold medals contested in the sport.
