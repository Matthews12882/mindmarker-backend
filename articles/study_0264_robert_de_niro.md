# Robert De Niro

**Robert Anthony De Niro** ([/də ˈnɪəroʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*də NEER-roh*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"); Italian: [[de ˈniːro]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Italian "Help:IPA/Italian"); born August 17, 1943) is an American actor and producer. Widely regarded as one of the greatest and most influential actors of his generation, he has received [various accolades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Robert_De_Niro "List of awards and nominations received by Robert De Niro"), including two [Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards") and a [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award "Golden Globe Award") as well as nominations for eight [BAFTA Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Awards "BAFTA Awards") and four [Emmy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Awards "Emmy Awards"). He was honored with the [AFI Life Achievement Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFI_Life_Achievement_Award "AFI Life Achievement Award") in 2003, the [Kennedy Center Honors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Center_Honors "Kennedy Center Honors") in 2009, the [Cecil B. DeMille Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecil_B._DeMille_Award "Cecil B. DeMille Award") in 2011, the [Presidential Medal of Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Medal_of_Freedom "Presidential Medal of Freedom") in 2016, the [Screen Actors Guild Life Achievement Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Life_Achievement_Award "Screen Actors Guild Life Achievement Award") in 2019, and the [Honorary Palme d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_Palme_d%27Or "Honorary Palme d'Or") in 2025.

De Niro studied acting at [HB Studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HB_Studio "HB Studio"), [Stella Adler Conservatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_Adler_Conservatory "Stella Adler Conservatory"), and [Lee Strasberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Strasberg "Lee Strasberg")'s [Actors Studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actors_Studio "Actors Studio"). He won the [Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor "Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor") for his role as [Vito Corleone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vito_Corleone "Vito Corleone") in the crime drama *[The Godfather Part II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Godfather_Part_II "The Godfather Part II")* (1974), becoming the [first male to win for an Italian-language performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_actors_nominated_for_Academy_Awards_for_non-English_performances "List of actors nominated for Academy Awards for non-English performances"). Six years later, he won the [Academy Award for Best Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor "Academy Award for Best Actor") for his portrayal of [Jake LaMotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_LaMotta "Jake LaMotta") in the biopic drama *[Raging Bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raging_Bull "Raging Bull")* (1980). He was further Oscar-nominated for his performances in *[Taxi Driver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxi_Driver "Taxi Driver")* (1976), *[The Deer Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Deer_Hunter "The Deer Hunter")* (1978), *[Awakenings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awakenings "Awakenings")* (1990), *[Cape Fear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Fear_(1991_film) "Cape Fear (1991 film)")* (1991), *[Silver Linings Playbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Linings_Playbook "Silver Linings Playbook")* (2012), and *[Killers of the Flower Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killers_of_the_Flower_Moon "Killers of the Flower Moon")* (2023), while also earning a nomination for [Best Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture") for producing *[The Irishman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Irishman "The Irishman")* (2019).

De Niro is known for his dramatic roles in *[Mean Streets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_Streets "Mean Streets")* (1973), *[1900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1900_(film) "1900 (film)")* (1976), *[The King of Comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_King_of_Comedy_(film) "The King of Comedy (film)")* (1982), *[Once Upon a Time in America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Once_Upon_a_Time_in_America "Once Upon a Time in America")* (1984), *[Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_(1985_film) "Brazil (1985 film)")* (1985), *[The Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mission_(1986_film) "The Mission (1986 film)")* (1986), *[Angel Heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel_Heart "Angel Heart")* (1987), *[The Untouchables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Untouchables_(film) "The Untouchables (film)")* (1987), *[Goodfellas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodfellas "Goodfellas")* (1990), *[This Boy's Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Boy%27s_Life "This Boy's Life")* (1993), *[Heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_(1995_film) "Heat (1995 film)")* (1995), *[Casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casino_(1995_film) "Casino (1995 film)")* (1995), *[Jackie Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_Brown "Jackie Brown")* (1997), *[Ronin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronin_(film) "Ronin (film)")* (1998), and *[Joker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joker_(2019_film) "Joker (2019 film)")* (2019), as well as his comedic roles in *[Midnight Run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnight_Run "Midnight Run")* (1988), *[Wag the Dog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wag_the_Dog "Wag the Dog")* (1997), *[Analyze This](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analyze_This "Analyze This")* (1999), the *[Meet the Parents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meet_the_Parents_(film_series) "Meet the Parents (film series)")* films (2000–present), and *[The Intern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Intern_(2015_film) "The Intern (2015 film)")* (2015). He directed and acted in both the crime drama *[A Bronx Tale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Bronx_Tale "A Bronx Tale")* (1993) and the spy film *[The Good Shepherd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Good_Shepherd_(film) "The Good Shepherd (film)")* (2006). On television, he portrayed [Bernie Madoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Madoff "Bernie Madoff") in the [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO "HBO") film *[The Wizard of Lies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wizard_of_Lies "The Wizard of Lies")* (2017).

De Niro and producer [Jane Rosenthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Rosenthal "Jane Rosenthal") founded the film and television production company [TriBeCa Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TriBeCa_Productions "TriBeCa Productions") in 1989, which has produced several films alongside his own. Also with Rosenthal, he founded the [Tribeca Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribeca_Film_Festival "Tribeca Film Festival") in 2002. Many of De Niro's films are considered classics of American cinema. Six of De Niro's films have been inducted into the [United States National Film Registry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Film_Registry "United States National Film Registry") by the [Library of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress "Library of Congress") as "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant" as of 2023. Five films were listed on the [AFI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Film_Institute "American Film Institute")'s [100 greatest American films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFI%27s_100_Years...100_Movies "AFI's 100 Years...100 Movies") of all time.

## Early life and education

Robert Anthony De Niro was born in the [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan "Manhattan"), New York City, on August 17, 1943, the only child of painters [Virginia Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Admiral "Virginia Admiral") and [Robert De Niro Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_De_Niro_Sr. "Robert De Niro Sr.") His father was of Irish and Italian descent, while his mother had Dutch, English, French, and German ancestry. His parents, who had met at the painting classes of [Hans Hofmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Hofmann "Hans Hofmann") in [Provincetown, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincetown,_Massachusetts "Provincetown, Massachusetts"), separated when he was two years old after his father announced that he was gay. De Niro was raised by his mother in the [Greenwich Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich_Village "Greenwich Village") and [Little Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Italy,_Manhattan "Little Italy, Manhattan") neighborhoods of Manhattan. His father lived nearby, and remained close with De Niro during his childhood. Nicknamed "Bobby Milk" because of his pale complexion, De Niro befriended many street kids in Little Italy, much to the disapproval of his father. Some, however, have remained his lifelong friends. His mother was raised [Presbyterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian "Presbyterian") but became an [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist "Atheist") as an adult, while his father had been a [lapsed Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapsed_Catholic "Lapsed Catholic") since the age of 12. Against his parents' wishes, his grandparents had De Niro secretly baptized into the [Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church "Catholic Church") while he was staying with them during his parents' divorce.

De Niro attended [PS 41](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PS_41 "PS 41"), a public elementary school in Manhattan, through the sixth grade. He began acting classes at the [Dramatic Workshop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dramatic_Workshop "Dramatic Workshop") and made his stage debut in school at age 10, playing the [Cowardly Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowardly_Lion "Cowardly Lion") in *[The Wizard of Oz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wonderful_Wizard_of_Oz "The Wonderful Wizard of Oz")*. He later went to [Elisabeth Irwin High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisabeth_Irwin_High_School "Elisabeth Irwin High School"), the upper school of the [Little Red School House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Red_School_House "Little Red School House"), for the seventh and eighth grades. He was then accepted into the [High School of Music & Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_School_of_Music_%26_Art "High School of Music & Art") for the ninth grade, but attended for only a short time before transferring to a public junior high school: IS 71, Charles Evans Hughes Junior High School. De Niro attended high school at [McBurney School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McBurney_School "McBurney School") and later, [Rhodes Preparatory School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes_Preparatory_School "Rhodes Preparatory School"). He found performing to be a way to relieve his shyness, and became fascinated by cinema, so he dropped out of high school at 16 to pursue acting. He later said, "When I was around 18, I was looking at a TV show and I said, 'If these actors are making a living at it, and they're not really that good, I can't do any worse than them.'" He studied acting at HB Studio and Lee Strasberg's Actors Studio. De Niro also studied with [Stella Adler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_Adler "Stella Adler"), of the Stella Adler Conservatory, where he was exposed to the techniques of the [Stanislavski system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislavski%27s_system "Stanislavski's system"). As a young actor, De Niro was inspired by the work of [Marlon Brando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlon_Brando "Marlon Brando"), [Montgomery Clift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery_Clift "Montgomery Clift"), [James Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Dean "James Dean"), [Greta Garbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greta_Garbo "Greta Garbo"), [Geraldine Page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geraldine_Page "Geraldine Page"), and [Kim Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Stanley "Kim Stanley").

## Career

### 1963–1973: Early roles and breakthrough

De Niro had minor film roles in *Encounter, [Three Rooms in Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Rooms_in_Manhattan "Three Rooms in Manhattan")* (both released in 1965) and *Les Jeunes Loups* (1968). Shortly afterwards, De Niro landed a major role in [*Greetings*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greetings_(1968_film) "Greetings (1968 film)") (1968), a [satirical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satirical "Satirical") film about men avoiding the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War") [draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription "Conscription"). The film marked the first of a series of early collaborations between De Niro and director [Brian De Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_De_Palma "Brian De Palma").

In July 1968, De Niro made his stage debut in [Jackie Curtis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_Curtis "Jackie Curtis")' [Off-Off Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-off-Broadway "Off-off-Broadway") production *Glamour, Glory, and Gold: The Life and Legend of Nola Noonan, Goddess and Star*, at Playwright's Workshop in Manhattan's [East Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Village,_Manhattan "East Village, Manhattan"). De Niro played several roles and received a favorable review in [*The* *Village Voice*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Village_Voice "The Village Voice").

In 1969, De Niro appeared in the drama *[Sam's Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam%27s_Song "Sam's Song")* in which he portrays a New York City filmmaker. Also that year, he appeared in De Palma's comedy *[The Wedding Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wedding_Party_(1969_film) "The Wedding Party (1969 film)")*; although it was filmed in 1963, it was kept unreleased for six years. De Niro, who was still unknown at the time, gained a favorable review from *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")*' [Howard Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Thompson_(film_critic) "Howard Thompson (film critic)"): "This farcical comedy, modestly produced by a trio of young people and utilizing some unfamiliar faces, is great fun."

He then appeared in [Roger Corman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Corman "Roger Corman")'s low-budget crime drama *[Bloody Mama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloody_Mama "Bloody Mama")* (1970), a loose adaptation of [Ma Barker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma_Barker "Ma Barker")'s life, who was the mother of four American criminals, of which De Niro portrayed one: Lloyd Barker. Thompson praised the film and thought the cast gave "fine performances". Next, De Niro starred in De Palma's comedy *[Hi, Mom!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hi,_Mom! "Hi, Mom!")* (1970), a sequel to *Greetings.* Writing for *[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker "The New Yorker")*, [Richard Brody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Brody "Richard Brody") opined that De Niro "brings unhinged spontaneity" to his character. He also had a small role in *[Jennifer on My Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_on_My_Mind "Jennifer on My Mind")* (1971) and in [Ivan Passer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Passer "Ivan Passer")'s *[Born to Win](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Born_to_Win "Born to Win")* (1971). His last film appearance of 1971 was in *[The Gang That Couldn't Shoot Straight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gang_That_Couldn%27t_Shoot_Straight "The Gang That Couldn't Shoot Straight")*, a crime-comedy based on [the 1969 novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gang_That_Couldn%27t_Shoot_Straight "The Gang That Couldn't Shoot Straight") by [Jimmy Breslin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Breslin "Jimmy Breslin").

In 1972, De Niro starred in two performances at [The American Place Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_American_Place_Theatre "The American Place Theatre"), directed by Charles Maryan. He then returned to the big screen with *[Bang the Drum Slowly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bang_the_Drum_Slowly_(film) "Bang the Drum Slowly (film)")* (1973), in which he played the lead role as Bruce Pearson, a [Major League Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Baseball "Major League Baseball") player with [Hodgkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodgkin_lymphoma "Hodgkin lymphoma") disease. His co-stars were [Michael Moriarty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Moriarty "Michael Moriarty") and [Vincent Gardenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Gardenia "Vincent Gardenia"). Adapted from the 1956 [novel of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bang_the_Drum_Slowly "Bang the Drum Slowly") by [Mark Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Harris_(author) "Mark Harris (author)"), the film received critical acclaim and helped De Niro gain further recognition. *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")* wrote, "De Niro proves himself to be one of the best and most likable young character actors in movies with this performance." *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* magazine's Alex Belth also took note of De Niro's "touching" portrayal, while Gardenia was nominated for an Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor. Harris later wrote about De Niro, "He learned only as much baseball as he needed for his role [...] I doubt that he ever cared to touch a baseball again."

In 1973, De Niro began collaborating with Martin Scorsese when he appeared in the crime film *[Mean Streets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_Streets "Mean Streets")*, co-starring [Harvey Keitel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Keitel "Harvey Keitel"). Although De Niro was offered a choice of roles, Scorsese wanted De Niro to play "Johnny Boy" Civello, a small time criminal working his way up into a local [mob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mafia "American Mafia"). While De Niro and Keitel were given freedom to improvise certain scenes, assistant director Ron Satlof recalls De Niro was "extremely serious, extremely involved in his role and preparation", and became isolated from the rest of the cast and crew. *Mean Streets* debuted at the [Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes_Film_Festival "Cannes Film Festival"), followed by the [New York Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Film_Festival "New York Film Festival") five months later, to a generally warm response. Film critic [Roger Ebert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ebert "Roger Ebert") thought De Niro gave a "marvelous performance, filled with urgency and restless desperation." [Pauline Kael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauline_Kael "Pauline Kael") of *The New York Times* was equally impressed by De Niro, writing he is "a bravura actor, and those who have registered him only as the grinning, tobacco-chewing dolt of that hunk of inept whimsey *Bang the Drum Slowly* will be unprepared for his volatile performance. De Niro does something like what [Dustin Hoffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dustin_Hoffman "Dustin Hoffman") was doing in *[Midnight Cowboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnight_Cowboy "Midnight Cowboy")*, but wilder; this kid doesn't just act – he takes off into the vapors." In 1997, *Mean Streets* was selected for preservation in the U.S. [National Film Registry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Film_Registry "National Film Registry") by the [Library of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress "Library of Congress") as being "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant".

### 1974–1980: Collaboration with Martin Scorsese and acclaim

De Niro had a pivotal role in [Francis Ford Coppola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Ford_Coppola "Francis Ford Coppola")'s crime [epic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_film "Epic film") *[The Godfather Part II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Godfather_Part_II "The Godfather Part II")* (1974), playing the young [Vito Corleone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vito_Corleone "Vito Corleone"). De Niro had previously auditioned for the first installment, *[The Godfather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Godfather "The Godfather")* (1972), but quit the project in favor of doing *The Gang That Couldn't Shoot Straight*. Coppola, having remembered him, gave De Niro a role in *Part II* instead. To portray his character, De Niro spoke mainly in several [Sicilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_language "Sicilian language") dialects, although he delivered a few lines in English. The film was a commercial success and grossed $48 million at the worldwide box office. *The Godfather Part II* received eleven nominations at the [47th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/47th_Academy_Awards "47th Academy Awards"), winning six, including one for De Niro as [Best Supporting Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor "Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor"), making him the [third-youngest winner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oldest_and_youngest_Academy_Award_winners_and_nominees#Youngest_winners_4 "List of oldest and youngest Academy Award winners and nominees") in the category at the time. It was De Niro's first Academy win; Coppola accepted the award on his behalf as he did not attend the ceremony. De Niro and [Marlon Brando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlon_Brando "Marlon Brando"), who played the older Vito Corleone in the first film, were the first pair of actors to win Academy Awards for [portraying the same fictional character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_superlative_Academy_Award_winners_and_nominees#Performances_of_the_same_character "List of superlative Academy Award winners and nominees").

After working with Scorsese in *Mean Streets*, De Niro collaborated with him again for the psychological drama *[Taxi Driver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxi_Driver "Taxi Driver")* (1976). Set in gritty and morally bankrupt New York City following the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War"), the film tells the story of [Travis Bickle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_Bickle "Travis Bickle"), a lonely taxi driver who descends into insanity. In preparation for the role, De Niro spent time with members of a U.S. army base to learn their [Midwestern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern "Midwestern") accent and mannerisms. He also lost 30 pounds (13 kg) in weight, took firearm training and studied the behavior of taxi drivers. The film was critically acclaimed, in particular for De Niro's performance; *[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")* critic hailed it as his "landmark performance", and the *[San Francisco Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Chronicle "San Francisco Chronicle")* wrote "De Niro is dazzling in one of his signature roles." The film was nominated for four Academy Awards, including [Best Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor "Academy Award for Best Actor") for De Niro. His "[You talkin' to me?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_talkin%27_to_me%3F "You talkin' to me?")" quote, which he improvised, was selected as the 10th most memorable quote in the [AFI's 100 Years...100 Movie Quotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFI%27s_100_Years...100_Movie_Quotes "AFI's 100 Years...100 Movie Quotes") by the [American Film Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Film_Institute "American Film Institute"). In 2005, the film was chosen by *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")* magazine as one of the [100 best films of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time%27s_All-TIME_100_Movies "Time's All-TIME 100 Movies").

De Niro had two other film releases in 1976. He starred in [*1900*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1900_(film) "1900 (film)"), a historical drama directed by [Bernardo Bertolucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_Bertolucci "Bernardo Bertolucci"). Starring an [ensemble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensemble_cast "Ensemble cast") cast, the film is set in the [Emilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilia-Romagna "Emilia-Romagna") region of Italy, and tells the story of two men, the landowner Alfredo Berlinghieri (De Niro) and the [peasant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peasant "Peasant") Olmo Dalcò ([Gérard Depardieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9rard_Depardieu "Gérard Depardieu")), as they witness and participate in the political conflicts between [fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism "Fascism") and [communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism "Communism") in the first half of the twentieth century. Next, he played a [CEO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEO "CEO") in *[The Last Tycoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Tycoon_(1976_film) "The Last Tycoon (1976 film)")*, based on [F. Scott Fitzgerald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._Scott_Fitzgerald "F. Scott Fitzgerald")'s [novel of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Tycoon "The Last Tycoon"), as adapted by British [screenwriter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screenwriter "Screenwriter") [Harold Pinter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Pinter "Harold Pinter"). De Niro lost 42 pounds (19 kg) for the role, and director [Elia Kazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elia_Kazan "Elia Kazan") observed that De Niro would rehearse on Sundays, adding "Bobby and I would go over the scenes to be shot. Bobby is more meticulous ... he's very imaginative. He's very precise. He figures everything out both inside and outside. He has good emotion. He's a [character actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Character_actor "Character actor"): everything he does he calculates. In a good way, but he calculates." The film received mixed reviews; *Variety* magazine's critic opined that the film was "unfocused" and called De Niro's performance "mildly intriguing". Film critic [Marie Brenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Brenner "Marie Brenner") wrote, "it is a role that surpasses even his brilliant and daring portrayal of Vito Corleone in *The Godfather Part II*... his performance deserves to be compared with the very finest."

For De Niro's sole project of 1977, he starred in Scorsese's musical drama [*New York, New York*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York,_New_York_(1977_film) "New York, New York (1977 film)") opposite [Liza Minnelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liza_Minnelli "Liza Minnelli"). De Niro learned to play the [saxophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxophone "Saxophone") from musician [Georgie Auld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgie_Auld "Georgie Auld"), to portray saxophonist Jimmy, who falls in love with a pop singer (Minnelli). The film received generally mixed reception, although critics were kinder to De Niro. The film was nominated for four [Golden Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe "Golden Globe") awards including [Best Actor in a Motion Picture Musical or Comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actor_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Musical_or_Comedy "Golden Globe Award for Best Actor – Motion Picture Musical or Comedy") for De Niro.

In 1978, De Niro starred in [Michael Cimino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Cimino "Michael Cimino")'s epic war film *[The Deer Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Deer_Hunter "The Deer Hunter"),* in which he played a steelworker whose life was changed after serving in the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War"). He co-starred with [Christopher Walken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Walken "Christopher Walken"), [John Savage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Savage_(actor) "John Savage (actor)"), [John Cazale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cazale "John Cazale"), [Meryl Streep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meryl_Streep "Meryl Streep"), and [George Dzundza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Dzundza "George Dzundza"). The story takes place in [Clairton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clairton "Clairton"), Pennsylvania, a [working-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working-class "Working-class") town on the [Monongahela River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monongahela_River "Monongahela River") south of [Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh "Pittsburgh"), and in Vietnam. Producer [Michael Deeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Deeley "Michael Deeley") pursued De Niro for the role, because the fame of his previous films would help make a "gruesome-sounding storyline and a barely known director" marketable. De Niro, impressed by the script and director's preparation, was among the first to sign on to the film. Reviews for *The Deer Hunter* were generally positive, and the cast attracted strong praise for their performances. The film received nominations at the Academy Awards, Golden Globes and [British Academy Film Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Awards "British Academy Film Awards") (BAFTAs), and earned De Niro a nomination for Best Actor at the Academy Awards. In 2007, the American Film Institute ranked it as the 53rd-greatest American film of all time in their [10th Anniversary Edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFI%27s_100_Years...100_Movies_(10th_Anniversary_Edition) "AFI's 100 Years...100 Movies (10th Anniversary Edition)") of the [AFI's 100 Years...100 Movies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFI%27s_100_Years...100_Movies "AFI's 100 Years...100 Movies") list.

The fourth collaboration between De Niro and Scorsese was in 1980, with the biographical drama *[Raging Bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raging_Bull "Raging Bull").* Adapted from [Jake LaMotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_LaMotta "Jake LaMotta")'s memoir *[Raging Bull: My Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raging_Bull:_My_Story "Raging Bull: My Story"),* De Niro portrays LaMotta, the Italian-American [middleweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middleweight "Middleweight") [boxer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxing "Boxing") whose violent behavior and temper destroyed his relationship with his wife and family. Co-starring [Joe Pesci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Pesci "Joe Pesci") and [Cathy Moriarty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathy_Moriarty "Cathy Moriarty"), De Niro later said it was one of the toughest roles to prepare for because he had to gain 60 pounds (27 kg), and had to learn to box. "The book's not great literature, but it's got a lot of heart", De Niro told Scorsese at the time. Although the film received critical acclaim, some reviewers were divided and criticized its "exceedingly violent" content; however, De Niro garnered praise for his realistic portrayal. The critic from *The Hollywood Reporter* declared that "De Niro is incredible and makes the actor almost unrecognizable as himself; he looks amazingly like La Motta. De Niro's appearance is also astonishing in the final scenes." Michael Thomson of the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") observed "the power of Scorsese is matched by the intensity of De Niro who delves deep into the soul of the boxer." At the [53rd Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/53rd_Academy_Awards "53rd Academy Awards"), the film received eight nominations, including Best Actor for De Niro for which he won. *Raging Bull* has since been regarded as one of the greatest films of the 1980s by American critics. De Niro was strongly considered for the role of [Jack Torrance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Torrance "Jack Torrance") in [Stanley Kubrick's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Kubrick%27s "Stanley Kubrick's") *[The Shining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Shining_(film) "The Shining (film)")*, but it ended up going to [Jack Nicholson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Nicholson "Jack Nicholson"), the director's first choice for the role.

### 1981–1991: Dramas, comedies, and awards success

De Niro returned to the crime genre with *[True Confessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Confessions_(film) "True Confessions (film)")* (1981), adapted from the 1977 [novel of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Confessions_(novel) "True Confessions (novel)") by [John Gregory Dunne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Gregory_Dunne "John Gregory Dunne"). Less challenging than his previous film, De Niro played a priest who clashes with his brother ([Robert Duvall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Duvall "Robert Duvall")), a detective investigating the murder of a prostitute. [Vincent Canby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Canby "Vincent Canby") of *The New York Times* thought the plot was hard to follow at times but praised the actors who "work so beautifully together it sometimes seems like a single performance." To expand his range of acting roles and to prove his acting abilities, De Niro sought out films with a comedic tone throughout the 1980s. He found it in *[The King of Comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_King_of_Comedy_(film) "The King of Comedy (film)")* (1982), in which he played the struggling [stand-up comedian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand-up_comedian "Stand-up comedian") Rupert Pupkin. De Niro was first to bring the script to the attention of Scorsese, who then gave it a New York setting and darker tone. The film failed to find an audience, and was a [box office disappointment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box_office_disappointment "Box office disappointment"), grossing only $2.5 million from a budget of $19 million. However, most critics praised De Niro's performance. His next film credit was in [Sergio Leone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Leone "Sergio Leone")'s *[Once Upon a Time in America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Once_Upon_a_Time_in_America "Once Upon a Time in America")* (1984), in which he plays David "Noodles" Aaronson, a New York City Jewish gangster. The theatrical cut, with a runtime of 229 minutes, premiered at the [1984 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Cannes_Film_Festival "1984 Cannes Film Festival") and received a 15-minute standing ovation. The film was shortened for theaters in the U.S. (139 minutes), but this proved to be highly unpopular with critics. After seeing the full cut, [Kenneth Turan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Turan "Kenneth Turan") of the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")* described the film as "excessive as well as tightly controlled" with the actors showing "impressive restraint and power".

*[Falling in Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falling_in_Love_(1984_film) "Falling in Love (1984 film)")*, a romantic comedy starring opposite Meryl Streep, was his last release of 1984. One year later, De Niro starred in a [science fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction "Science fiction") for the first time, *[Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_(1985_film) "Brazil (1985 film)"),* about a daydreaming man living in a [dystopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dystopia "Dystopia") society. Although the film was unsuccessful at the box office, *Brazil* was included in [The Criterion Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Criterion_Collection "The Criterion Collection"). In May 1986, De Niro returned to the stage at [Longacre Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longacre_Theatre "Longacre Theatre"), playing the lead role in the production *Cuba and His Teddy Bear.* For his next feature film, he co-starred in *[The Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mission_(1986_film) "The Mission (1986 film)")* (1986) with [Jeremy Irons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Irons "Jeremy Irons"), a [period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_drama "Historical drama") drama about the experiences of a [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit "Jesuit") missionary in eighteenth century South America. Vincent Canby reviewed the film negatively, and was critical of De Niro's casting: "De Niro, who was very fine as the street-wise priest in *True Confessions*, is all right here until he opens his mouth." However, the film won an Academy Award for [Best Cinematography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Cinematography "Academy Award for Best Cinematography"), three BAFTAs, including [Best Editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Editing "BAFTA Award for Best Editing"), and two Golden Globes for [Best Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Screenplay "Golden Globe Award for Best Screenplay") and [Best Original Score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Original_Score "Golden Globe Award for Best Original Score").

In 1987, De Niro had two minor film roles. In the first, he was cast as Louis Cyphre in [Alan Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Parker "Alan Parker")'s horror *[Angel Heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel_Heart "Angel Heart")*, an adaptation of [William Hjortsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Hjortsberg "William Hjortsberg")'s 1978 novel *[Falling Angel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falling_Angel "Falling Angel").* In the second, he portrayed [Al Capone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Capone "Al Capone") in De Palma's crime drama, *[The Untouchables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Untouchables_(film) "The Untouchables (film)").* While Pauline Kael opined that De Niro was "lazy" for undertaking small roles, De Palma defended him by saying he was "experimenting with those characters". In July 1987, he traveled to Russia to serve as president of the jury at the [15th Moscow International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15th_Moscow_International_Film_Festival "15th Moscow International Film Festival"). Finally that year, he provided a [voice-over](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice-over "Voice-over") for the documentary *[Dear America: Letters Home from Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dear_America:_Letters_Home_from_Vietnam "Dear America: Letters Home from Vietnam").* The [buddy cop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddy_cop "Buddy cop") film, *[Midnight Run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnight_Run "Midnight Run")*, was his next effort in 1988. Starring opposite [Charles Grodin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Grodin "Charles Grodin"), De Niro played [bounty hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounty_hunter "Bounty hunter") Jack Walsh. The film received amicable reception and was a commercial success, grossing $81 million worldwide. In his mixed review, [Hal Hinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hal_Hinson "Hal Hinson") of *The Washington Post* wrote of De Niro:

> De Niro has reduced himself in scale here, too, and it's a relief to see him drop the great-actor mantle, and the theatricality. As a result, he hasn't seemed as fresh since *Mean Streets* or *New York, New York.* Walsh is more of a character role than the ones he played in those films; there's less specificity in the conception – he's more of a type – but the actor fits into him snugly, effortlessly, and the chance to play comedy, particularly opposite a comic foil as ideal as Grodin, appears to have revitalized him.

He turned down an opportunity to play [Jesus Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Christ "Jesus Christ") in Scorsese's *[The Last Temptation of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Temptation_of_Christ_(film) "The Last Temptation of Christ (film)")* (1988), although he told the director that he would do it as a favor if needed. Scorsese cast [Willem Dafoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_Dafoe "Willem Dafoe") instead. In 1989, De Niro starred in several films that were not widely seen. He starred alongside [Ed Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Harris "Ed Harris") and [Kathy Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathy_Baker "Kathy Baker") in the drama *[Jacknife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacknife "Jacknife").* The film revolves around the complex relationship between a [Vietnam veteran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_veteran "Vietnam veteran"), his sister and fellow army buddy. Next, he starred in the crime comedy [*We're No Angels*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We%27re_No_Angels_(1989_film) "We're No Angels (1989 film)") (1989) with [Sean Penn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Penn "Sean Penn") and [Demi Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demi_Moore "Demi Moore"), a remake of the 1955 [film of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We%27re_No_Angels_(1955_film) "We're No Angels (1955 film)"). The pair play escaped convicts who go on the run towards Canada. A year later, he starred in the romantic drama *[Stanley & Iris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_%26_Iris "Stanley & Iris")* opposite [Jane Fonda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Fonda "Jane Fonda"). Film critics did not receive *We're No Angels* or *Stanley & Iris* positively; modern review aggregator [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes") gives them approval ratings of 47% and 33%, respectively.

De Niro and Scorsese soon reunited for their sixth collaboration in 1990, with the crime film *[Goodfellas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodfellas "Goodfellas").* It is an adaptation of the 1985 non-fiction book *[Wiseguy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiseguy_(book) "Wiseguy (book)")* by [Nicholas Pileggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Pileggi "Nicholas Pileggi"). The film narrates the life of mob associate [Henry Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Hill "Henry Hill") ([Ray Liotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Liotta "Ray Liotta")) and his friends and family from 1955 to 1980. De Niro played James Conway, an Irish truck [carjacker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carjacker "Carjacker") and gangster. *Goodfellas* premiered at the [47th Venice International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/47th_Venice_International_Film_Festival "47th Venice International Film Festival") to an "enthusiastic" response from Italian critics, although it grossed a moderate $46 million upon its wider release. [Peter Travers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Travers "Peter Travers"), writing for *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")* magazine, praised the cast performances, and called De Niro's character "a smooth killer acted with riveting restraint". *[Chicago Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Tribune "Chicago Tribune")*'s [Gene Siskel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_Siskel "Gene Siskel") was equally impressed by their improvised performances and concluded "easily one of the year's best films." In the awards season, the film was nominated for six Academy Awards, and De Niro was nominated for [Best Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role "BAFTA Award for Best Actor in a Leading Role") at the BAFTAs. In 2007, the American Film Institute ranked it as the 92nd-greatest American film of all time in their 10th Anniversary Edition of the AFI's 100 Years...100 Movies list. Also in 1990, De Niro appeared in the lead role for *[Awakenings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awakenings "Awakenings")*, directed by [Penny Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penny_Marshall "Penny Marshall"). The drama, based on [Oliver Sacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Sacks "Oliver Sacks")' 1973 [book of the same title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awakenings_(book) "Awakenings (book)"), tells the story of Dr. Malcolm Sayer ([Robin Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Williams "Robin Williams")), who discovers benefits of the drug [L-Dopa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-Dopa "L-Dopa") in 1969 and administers it to [catatonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catatonic "Catatonic") patients. The film was nominated for three Academy Awards, including Best Actor for De Niro. Sacks later remarked of the film: "I was pleased with a great deal of it. I think in an uncanny way, De Niro did somehow feel his way into being [Parkinsonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinsonian "Parkinsonian"). [...] At other levels I think things were sort of sentimentalized and simplified somewhat."

De Niro's next film project was the drama *[Guilty by Suspicion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilty_by_Suspicion "Guilty by Suspicion")* (1991) in which he plays David Merrill, a fictitious film director, returning to the U.S. during the [McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCarthyism "McCarthyism") era and [Hollywood blacklist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_blacklist "Hollywood blacklist"). The film received generally favorable reviews. He then had a minor role in the [mystery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mystery_film "Mystery film") drama [*Backdraft*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backdraft_(film) "Backdraft (film)") (1991), playing a veteran fire inspector. De Niro's biggest success of 1991 was *[Cape Fear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Fear_(1991_film) "Cape Fear (1991 film)")*, his seventh film with Scorsese and a remake of the 1962 [film of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Fear_(1962_film) "Cape Fear (1962 film)"). De Niro portrays convicted rapist [Max Cady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Cady "Max Cady"), who seeks revenge against a former [public defender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_defender "Public defender") who originally defended him. De Niro's performance was widely lauded. [David Ansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ansen "David Ansen") of *[Newsweek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsweek "Newsweek")* remarked that De Niro "dominates the film with his lip-smacking, blackly comic and terrifying portrayal of psychopathic self-righteousness." The film grossed $182 million at the box office and earned De Niro a Best Actor nomination at the [64th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/64th_Academy_Awards "64th Academy Awards").

### 1992–1997: Directorial debut and crime dramas

In 1992, De Niro appeared in two films. The first, *[Mistress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mistress_(1992_film) "Mistress (1992 film)"),* is a comedy-drama in which he played ruthless businessman Evan Wright. Of his performance, the critic from *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent "The Independent")* called De Niro "more urbane and coherent than we've seen him for a while." [Irwin Winkler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irwin_Winkler "Irwin Winkler")'s *[Night and the City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_and_the_City_(1992_film) "Night and the City (1992 film)")* was his second release, a crime drama remake of the 1950 [film noir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_noir "Film noir") [of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_and_the_City "Night and the City"). He was cast as New York lawyer Harry Fabian. [Owen Gleiberman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Gleiberman "Owen Gleiberman") of *[Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")* gave the film a rating of "B−" and was critical of De Niro: "The actor who once got so far inside his roles that he just about detonated the screen – now plays characters who don't seem to have any inner life at all." Next, he served as a producer for the mystery thriller *[Thunderheart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunderheart "Thunderheart")* (1992)*.* In 1993, he played crime scene photographer Wayne Dobie in the comedy drama *[Mad Dog and Glory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mad_Dog_and_Glory "Mad Dog and Glory")* with co-stars [Uma Thurman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uma_Thurman "Uma Thurman") and [Bill Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Murray "Bill Murray"). The feature received reasonable reviews and was lauded for the chemistry between De Niro and Murray; *The Washington Post* critic noted that their "real-life friendship spills over into this jittery, very funny look at the male bonding experience." Next, De Niro starred in the [coming-of-age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coming-of-age_story "Coming-of-age story") film *[This Boy's Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Boy%27s_Life "This Boy's Life")* (1993), based on the memoir of the same name by [Tobias Wolff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobias_Wolff "Tobias Wolff"). It features [Ellen Barkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellen_Barkin "Ellen Barkin") and [Leonardo DiCaprio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_DiCaprio "Leonardo DiCaprio"). Playing stepfather Dwight Hansen of Wolff (DiCaprio), the film was mostly well received, although *[Timeout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Out_(magazine) "Time Out (magazine)")* magazine believed that "DiCaprio steals the show".

De Niro starred in his directorial debut, *[A Bronx Tale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Bronx_Tale "A Bronx Tale")* (1993), a coming-of-age story about an Italian-American boy who is torn between the temptations of organized crime, racism in his community, and the values of his decent father. The film also stars [Chazz Palminteri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chazz_Palminteri "Chazz Palminteri"), who wrote the [play of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Bronx_Tale_(play) "A Bronx Tale (play)"), and is based on his childhood. *A Bronx Tale* premiered at the [Toronto International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_International_Film_Festival "Toronto International Film Festival") to a positive response; Marjorie Baumgarten of *[The Austin Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Austin_Chronicle "The Austin Chronicle")* wrote "De Niro's choices as a director all seem prudent and un-showy, designed to draw attention to the characters and the story rather than its technical assemblage and much-lauded star." *Variety* magazine's [Todd McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_McCarthy "Todd McCarthy") took issue with the film's slow start but complimented De Niro's "impressive sensitivity to the irrational roots of racism and violence." A year later, De Niro was cast in the lead role of [*Mary Shelley's Frankenstein*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Shelley%27s_Frankenstein_(film) "Mary Shelley's Frankenstein (film)"), an adaptation of [Mary Shelley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Shelley "Mary Shelley")'s 1818 novel *[Frankenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankenstein "Frankenstein").* Although the film was commercially successful, earning $112 million worldwide, the general consensus of reviews were largely negative. Film critic [James Berardinelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Berardinelli "James Berardinelli") opined that it was entertaining and De Niro gave a strong performance, despite the film's "frantic" pace.

*[Casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casino_(1995_film) "Casino (1995 film)")* (1995) marked De Niro's return to the crime genre with Scorsese in their eighth collaboration. Co-starring [Sharon Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Stone "Sharon Stone") and Joe Pesci, the film is based on the book *[Casino: Love and Honor in Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casino:_Love_and_Honor_in_Las_Vegas "Casino: Love and Honor in Las Vegas")* by [Nicholas Pileggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Pileggi "Nicholas Pileggi"). De Niro portrays Sam "Ace" Rothstein, a mob-connected casino operator in Las Vegas. The film's themes revolve around greed, betrayal, wealth, status, and murder that occur between two mobsters, Sam "Ace" Rothstein (De Niro) and Nicky Santoro (Pesci), and a [trophy wife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trophy_wife "Trophy wife") (Stone) over a gambling empire. *Casino* was released to mostly positive critical reception, and was a success at the worldwide box office. Roger Ebert was impressed with the lead performers' abilities to "inhabit their roles with unconscious assurance", and *[The Globe and Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Globe_and_Mail "The Globe and Mail")*'s critic thought "De Niro does an extraordinarily subtle job of capturing the [paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradox "Paradox") [...] that lie at the heart of this picture." Shortly afterwards, he starred in 1995's crime thriller [*Heat*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_(1995_film) "Heat (1995 film)"), about a group of professional bank robbers. [Art Linson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Linson "Art Linson"), who had previously produced films starring De Niro, sent him the script first. "It was very good, very strong, had a particular feel to it, a reality and authenticity", De Niro said. Co-starring [Al Pacino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Pacino "Al Pacino"), [Val Kilmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val_Kilmer "Val Kilmer"), [Tom Sizemore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Sizemore "Tom Sizemore"), and [Jon Voight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Voight "Jon Voight") among others, the film was released to wide acclaim; Michael Wilmington of the *Chicago Tribune* wrote:

> De Niro and Pacino redeem everything. In *Heat*, they represent a high postwar tradition for movie actors – the ones inspired by [Marlon Brando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlon_Brando "Marlon Brando"), [John Cassavetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cassavetes "John Cassavetes") and [James Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Dean "James Dean") – who aren't afraid of emotion, who run right into the jaws of a scene to grab it. Like others from their generation – [Jack Nicholson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Nicholson "Jack Nicholson"), [Gene Hackman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_Hackman "Gene Hackman"), [Harvey Keitel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Keitel "Harvey Keitel") – they have a keener slant on machismo. They easily explore its darker strata.

*Heat* marked the first time that Pacino and De Niro appeared on-screen together; they were both in *The Godfather Part II* but were never in any scenes together because the film takes place in two separate timelines. De Niro as Vito Corleone was in the past and Pacino as his son Michael was in the present.

In 1995, De Niro had minor roles in the French comedy *[One Hundred and One Nights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Hundred_and_One_Nights "One Hundred and One Nights")* and in the drama *[Panther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panther_(film) "Panther (film)").* In 1996, De Niro starred in the sports thriller *[The Fan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fan_(1996_film) "The Fan (1996 film)")*, based on the [novel of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fan_(Abrahams_novel) "The Fan (Abrahams novel)") by [Peter Abrahams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Abrahams_(American_author) "Peter Abrahams (American author)"). De Niro plays Gil Renard, a baseball fanatic who loses his sanity. His fiftieth film credit was in the crime drama *[Sleepers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleepers_(film) "Sleepers (film)")* (1996), about four boys who become involved with crime, and are sentenced to a detention center where they are abused by guards, and seek vengeance upon release. De Niro plays priest Bobby Carillo, a father figure to the four boys. Afterwards, he appeared in [*Marvin's Room*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvin%27s_Room_(film) "Marvin's Room (film)") (1996) as Dr. Wallace Carter, who treats a woman (Diane Keaton) with [leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia "Leukemia"). Writing for the British *[Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_(film_magazine) "Empire (film magazine)")* magazine, Bob McCabe opined that "Performances are all eminently watchable [...] but the truncated feel robs the film of anything more than perfunctory pleasures." Also in 1996, De Niro co-produced the crime-comedy [*Faithful*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faithful_(1996_film) "Faithful (1996 film)").
In 1996 a video game produced by De Niro called [9: The Last Resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9:_The_Last_Resort "9: The Last Resort") was released. A surreal point and click puzzle game about a hotel filled with strange characters. De Niro met the game's director Buzz Hayes when Hayes worked at [Lucasfilm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucasfilm "Lucasfilm"). The game did not do well at launch and Hayes is quoted as saying "I wouldn't call it a failure. it was just kind of a quiet landing."

The following year, he appeared in [James Mangold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Mangold "James Mangold")'s *[Cop Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cop_Land "Cop Land")* (1997), a crime-drama co-starring [Sylvester Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone "Sylvester Stallone"), [Harvey Keitel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Keitel "Harvey Keitel") and [Ray Liotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Liotta "Ray Liotta"). De Niro plays [Internal Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_affairs_(law_enforcement) "Internal affairs (law enforcement)") investigator Lt. Moe Tilden, who explores police corruption in a New Jersey town. The film opened to a generally warm response, although Barbara Shulgasser of *[San Francisco Examiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Examiner "San Francisco Examiner")* criticized De Niro's acting in certain scenes, suggesting Mangold put De Niro in a "manufactured situation", preventing him from realizing his full potential. De Niro co-starred and co-produced *[Wag the Dog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wag_the_Dog "Wag the Dog")* (1997). The film is a [political satire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_satire "Political satire") about a biased publicist (De Niro) and a Hollywood producer (Dustin Hoffman) who fabricate a war in Albania to cover up a U.S. president's sex scandal. In January 1998, a month after its release, the [Clinton–Lewinsky scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton%E2%80%93Lewinsky_scandal "Clinton–Lewinsky scandal") dominated the headlines, which helped the film generate publicity. As a result, *Wag the Dog* was well-received and made the list of Roger Ebert's ten best films of 1997. De Niro also had a supporting role in [Quentin Tarantino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quentin_Tarantino "Quentin Tarantino")'s *[Jackie Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_Brown "Jackie Brown")* in that same year.

### 1998–2006: Comic roles, thrillers, and career slump

De Niro began 1998 with an appearance in [*Great Expectations*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Expectations_(1998_film) "Great Expectations (1998 film)"), a modern adaptation of [Charles Dickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens "Charles Dickens")' 1861 [novel of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Expectations "Great Expectations"), in which he played Arthur Lustig. Later that year, his next major role came in [*Ronin*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronin_(film) "Ronin (film)") (1998), about a team of former [special operatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_forces "Special forces") that are hired to steal a mysterious briefcase while navigating a maze of shifting loyalties. De Niro plays Sam, an American [mercenary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercenary "Mercenary") formerly associated with the [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA "CIA"). *Ronin* premiered at the [1998 Venice Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Venice_Film_Festival "1998 Venice Film Festival") to favorable response; [Janet Maslin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Maslin "Janet Maslin") of *The New York Times* praised De Niro's confident portrayal as an action hero. In 1999, De Niro ventured back into crime-comedy; he was cast as an insecure mob boss opposite [Billy Crystal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Crystal "Billy Crystal") and [Lisa Kudrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Kudrow "Lisa Kudrow") in [Harold Ramis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Ramis "Harold Ramis")' *[Analyze This](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analyze_This "Analyze This").* The film was a box office hit, earning $176 million worldwide, and De Niro was nominated for Best Actor at the Golden Globes. In *[Flawless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flawless_(1999_film) "Flawless (1999 film)")* (1999), De Niro appeared as a homophobic police officer, who suffers a stroke, and is assigned to a rehabilitative program with a gay singer. The critic from the BBC gave the film 3 out of 5 stars, and thought De Niro gave a "refreshingly low-key" performance, in comparison to his previous work.

In 2000, De Niro produced and starred in his first [live-action animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live-action_animation "Live-action animation") comedy, *[The Adventures of Rocky and Bullwinkle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Adventures_of_Rocky_and_Bullwinkle_(film) "The Adventures of Rocky and Bullwinkle (film)").* He voiced the character [Fearless Leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fearless_Leader "Fearless Leader"), who is a dictator and employer of two mobsters. The film was critically panned, with Rotten Tomatoes giving the film a 43% approval rating. De Niro played [Master Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_chief_petty_officer "Master chief petty officer") 'Billy' Sunday in the biographical drama *[Men of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_of_Honor "Men of Honor")* (2000), based on the life of [Carl Brashear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Brashear "Carl Brashear"), the first African-American to become a [U.S. Navy Master Diver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_diver_(United_States_Navy) "Master diver (United States Navy)"). Although the film garnered mixed reviews, [Bob Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Thomas_(reporter) "Bob Thomas (reporter)") of the *[Associated Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_Press "Associated Press")* wrote "De Niro infuses the role with all his dynamism. It is his best performance in years." That same year, he starred in the comedy *[Meet the Parents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meet_the_Parents "Meet the Parents")* opposite [Ben Stiller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Stiller "Ben Stiller") as Jack Byrnes, a former CIA operative who takes a dislike to Stiller's character. De Niro, who had been seeking comic roles at the time, was encouraged by his producing partner Jane Rosenthal, to take on the role. The film was a high earner at the box office, with $330 million in receipts. Film critics welcomed De Niro's transition as a comic actor and ability to make audiences laugh.

After several comedies, De Niro landed a lead role in the crime thriller *[15 Minutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15_Minutes "15 Minutes")* (2001), a story about a [homicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homicide "Homicide") detective (De Niro) and a [fire marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_fire_marshals "New York City fire marshals") ([Edward Burns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Burns "Edward Burns")) who join forces to apprehend a pair of Eastern European murderers. The film's reception was generally unfavorable; William Arnold of the *[Seattle Post-Intelligencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Post-Intelligencer "Seattle Post-Intelligencer")* took issue with the "in-your-face exaggeration", but he thought De Niro delivered "his usual edgy flair, [...] on the mean streets of his native Manhattan." De Niro followed up with a [heist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heist_film "Heist film"), in [Frank Oz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Oz "Frank Oz")'s *[The Score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Score_(2001_film) "The Score (2001 film)")* (2001), starring [Edward Norton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Norton "Edward Norton"), [Angela Bassett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Bassett "Angela Bassett") and [Marlon Brando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlon_Brando "Marlon Brando"). He plays a retiring thief when a young man (Norton) persuades him into doing one last heist together. Upon release, *The Score* fared well with critics, although Peter Rainer of *[New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_(magazine) "New York (magazine)")* magazine did not think the film challenged De Niro or fully utilize his talents. The next year, he played an [LAPD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LAPD "LAPD") detective opposite [Eddie Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Murphy "Eddie Murphy") in the action-comedy [*Showtime*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showtime_(film) "Showtime (film)"). The reviewer from *[LA Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LA_Weekly "LA Weekly")* remarked "De Niro isn't actually playing a part but riffing on his own legend", and thought the references to *Taxi Driver* were "cheap".

Also in 2002, he collaborated with [Michael Caton-Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Caton-Jones "Michael Caton-Jones") in *[City by the Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_by_the_Sea "City by the Sea")*, who had previously directed De Niro in *This Boy's Life*. Starring opposite [Frances McDormand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_McDormand "Frances McDormand") and [James Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Franco "James Franco"), he portrayed another police detective in the drama. The film received mixed reviews and under-performed at the theaters. He appeared in *[Analyze That](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analyze_That "Analyze That")* (2002)*,* a sequel to 1999's *Analyze This.* Filming began in New York City, seven months after the [September 11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_attacks "September 11 attacks"). De Niro insisted on filming there, stating "It's a New York story, a New York movie. We always intended to keep it there and I'm glad we were able to do it." Upon release, most critics thought the sequel was weak; [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN "CNN")'s [Paul Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Clinton "Paul Clinton") remarked "Unfortunately the result is just a bunch of one-liners strung together, of which some work and some don't. The actual story never gets off the ground." Despite these failures, De Niro served as a producer for the critically acclaimed romantic-comedy *[About a Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/About_a_Boy_(film) "About a Boy (film)")* (2002), and appeared in *[9/11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9/11_(2002_film) "9/11 (2002 film)")* (2002), a [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS "CBS") documentary about the September 11 attacks, told from the New York City fire department's point of view.

Several critics consider De Niro's career as having begun to slump in the early 2000s, with De Niro starring in roles that were less dramatic than those in the previous decade. He returned to the screen in 2004, playing a doctor in the [fantasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasy_film "Fantasy film") drama [*Godsend*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godsend_(2004_film) "Godsend (2004 film)"). As of 2020, the film is De Niro's poorest-performing work; Rotten Tomatoes gave the film an approval rating of 4% based on 139 critic reviews. He voiced a character in [DreamWorks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DreamWorks_Animation "DreamWorks Animation")' animation of *[Shark Tale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shark_Tale "Shark Tale")* (2004). Most critics were also unimpressed, but it was a high earner at the box office. After co-producing *[Stage Beauty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stage_Beauty "Stage Beauty")* (2004), De Niro reprised his role of Jack Byrnes in 2004's *[Meet the Fockers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meet_the_Fockers "Meet the Fockers"),* the sequel to *Meet the Parents*. In a scathing review of De Niro, the critic from *Slant Magazine* wrote "In self-parody mode for the umpteenth time, De Niro mugs for the camera with a series of overblown grimaces and faux-menacing glares." [*The Bridge of San Luis Rey*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bridge_of_San_Luis_Rey_(2004_film) "The Bridge of San Luis Rey (2004 film)"), was De Niro's last release of 2004, based on [Thornton Wilder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thornton_Wilder "Thornton Wilder")'s [novel of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bridge_of_San_Luis_Rey "The Bridge of San Luis Rey"). It was also critically panned.

In 2005, De Niro starred in the horror [*Hide and Seek*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hide_and_Seek_(2005_film) "Hide and Seek (2005 film)") opposite [Dakota Fanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_Fanning "Dakota Fanning"), playing Dr. David Callaway who leaves the city with his traumatized daughter after the mother's suicide. Although the film was a financial success, some critics thought De Niro had been miscast, and queried his decision to star in a mediocre feature. In 2006, De Niro turned down a role in *[The Departed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Departed "The Departed")* to direct his second film, the [spy thriller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spy_film "Spy film") *[The Good Shepherd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Good_Shepherd_(film) "The Good Shepherd (film)")*, a fictional account about the growth of the CIA during its formative years*.* The film reunited him onscreen with Joe Pesci, co-star from *Raging Bull*, *Goodfellas*, *A Bronx Tale*, *Casino*, among others*.* Based on the screenplay by [Eric Roth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Roth "Eric Roth"), the project was personal for De Niro, who was raised during the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War") and fascinated by it. Despite starring some of Hollywood's leading actors; [Matt Damon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Damon "Matt Damon"), [Angelina Jolie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelina_Jolie "Angelina Jolie") and [Alec Baldwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alec_Baldwin "Alec Baldwin"), the film garnered a mixed reception. Writing for *[The Sydney Morning Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sydney_Morning_Herald "The Sydney Morning Herald")*, [Sandra Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_Hall_(writer) "Sandra Hall (writer)") noted its slow pace, stating "There's a potentially fascinating slice of [American history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_history "American history") here, but De Niro has carved it up with an excruciatingly dull knife." The critic from *USA Today* found the plot initially hard to follow, but praises De Niro for "creating a stirring personal tale". *The Good Shepherd* was nominated for [Best Art Direction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Art_Direction "Best Art Direction") at the [79th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/79th_Academy_Awards "79th Academy Awards"). Finally in 2006, he voiced the character Emperor Sifrat XVI in *[Arthur and the Invisibles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_and_the_Invisibles "Arthur and the Invisibles").*

### 2007–2016: Further film roles

His sole project in 2007 was [Matthew Vaughn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Vaughn "Matthew Vaughn")'s [*Stardust*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stardust_(2007_film) "Stardust (2007 film)"), a fantasy adventure, based on [Neil Gaiman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Gaiman "Neil Gaiman")'s 1999 [novel of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stardust_(Gaiman_novel) "Stardust (Gaiman novel)"). He plays Captain Shakespeare, the leader of a ship. The film was generally well received, although one critic from *New York* magazine thought De Niro's performance was "god-awful – yet his [gung-ho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gung-ho "Gung-ho") spirit wins him Brownie points." The following year, he starred in the [police procedural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_procedural "Police procedural") thriller *[Righteous Kill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Righteous_Kill "Righteous Kill")* opposite Al Pacino, both playing New York City detectives who investigate serial executions of criminals who escaped justice. The film's response was mainly disappointing; Peter Hartlaub of *San Francisco Chronicle* thought the story was unoriginal and De Niro lacked energy. The film grossed $78 million from a budget of $60 million. Next, he starred in [*What Just Happened*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_Just_Happened_(2008_film) "What Just Happened (2008 film)") (2008), a satirical comedy based on Art Linson's experiences as a producer in Hollywood. The film was screened at the [2008 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Cannes_Film_Festival "2008 Cannes Film Festival") as an out-of-competition entry. *The Sydney Morning Herald* opined that most reviewers gave the film a lukewarm reception because of the character he plays, which is "sympathetic" and quieter than his earlier roles. In 2009, he was cast as Frank Goode in the drama [*Everybody's Fine*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everybody%27s_Fine_(2009_film) "Everybody's Fine (2009 film)"), a remake of [Giuseppe Tornatore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Tornatore "Giuseppe Tornatore")'s Italian film [of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everybody%27s_Fine_(1990_film) "Everybody's Fine (1990 film)"). Although the film's response was equally mixed, *The Guardian*'s critic praised De Niro for a "his first decent, watchable performance in quite a while."

In 2010, he had a minor part as Senator John McLaughlin in the action film *[Machete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machete_(2010_film) "Machete (2010 film)")*. That same year, he starred in *[Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_(2010_film) "Stone (2010 film)")* opposite [Milla Jovovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milla_Jovovich "Milla Jovovich") and Edward Norton, co-star from *The Score*. It is a crime drama where De Niro plays a manipulated [parole officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parole_officer "Parole officer"). The film was met with a divided reception; *Toronto Star*'s critic thought De Niro delivered a respectable performance due to Jovovich's support. Another critic, Jesse Cataldo from *Slant Magazine* noted the film's restraint and thought De Niro is repeating himself by playing the same basic characters. Next he starred in *[Little Fockers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Fockers "Little Fockers")* (2010), the second sequel to *Meet the Parents* and *Meet the Fockers*. Despite universally negative reviews from critics, the film was a box office success, grossing over $310 million worldwide. In one review, *The Daily Telegraph* wrote "Despite the farcical script, De Niro in particular has his [paterfamilias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paterfamilias "Paterfamilias") character sensitively tuned". That year, De Niro was cast in *[Edge of Darkness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_of_Darkness_(2010_film) "Edge of Darkness (2010 film)")*, but he left the project citing creative differences. He was replaced by [Ray Winstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Winstone "Ray Winstone").

In 2011, De Niro starred in the Italian comedy *[Manuale d'amore 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuale_d%27amore_3 "Manuale d'amore 3")*. He also appeared in three other films: *[Killer Elite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killer_Elite_(film) "Killer Elite (film)")*, *[Limitless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limitless_(film) "Limitless (film)")*, and *[New Year's Eve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Year%27s_Eve_(2011_film) "New Year's Eve (2011 film)")*. Except for *Limitless*, which received an approval rating of 69% from Rotten Tomatoes, the other two films were met with mixed-to-negative reviews. De Niro was also appointed president of the jury for the [2011 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Cannes_Film_Festival "2011 Cannes Film Festival"), making it the second time he has served. Continuing into 2012, he starred in the drama *[Being Flynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Being_Flynn "Being Flynn"),* based on *[Another Bullshit Night in Suck City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Another_Bullshit_Night_in_Suck_City "Another Bullshit Night in Suck City"),* a memoir by [Nick Flynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Flynn "Nick Flynn"). It was met with a mixed response; critic [A. O. Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._O._Scott "A. O. Scott") complimented De Niro's ability for playing an estranged father (opposite [Paul Dano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Dano "Paul Dano")), calling him "unpredictable and subtle", despite an uncertain plot. De Niro also appeared in the thrillers *[Red Lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Lights_(2012_film) "Red Lights (2012 film)")* and *[Freelancers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freelancers_(film) "Freelancers (film)")* (both 2012).

De Niro made his first appearance in a [David O. Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_O._Russell "David O. Russell") film, in the romantic comedy *[Silver Linings Playbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Linings_Playbook "Silver Linings Playbook")* (2012), as the father of Pat Solatano ([Bradley Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradley_Cooper "Bradley Cooper")), who is released from a psychiatric hospital and moves back in with his parents to rebuild his life. The film was a critical and commercial success, earning eight [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award "Academy Award") nominations, including Best Supporting Actor for De Niro. The film grossed $236 million worldwide. Critics lauded the entire cast; *Variety* magazine's [Justin Chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Chang "Justin Chang") noted De Niro's calm performance, writing "it's hard to remember the last time De Niro was this effortlessly endearing and relaxed onscreen." In 2013, he played a one-scene uncredited cameo role in Russell's ten-time Oscar-nominated *[American Hustle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Hustle "American Hustle")* as mob boss Victor Tellegio. In 2012, De Niro served as an executive producer for the television series *[NYC 22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NYC_22 "NYC 22").*

Next, he was cast in 2013's *[The Big Wedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Big_Wedding "The Big Wedding")*, [*Killing Season*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_Season_(film) "Killing Season (film)"), and *[The Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Family_(2013_film) "The Family (2013 film)")*; all three were met with mainly a negative response. His other 2013 release, *[Last Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Vegas "Last Vegas"),* received some respectable reviews. Co-starring [Michael Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Douglas "Michael Douglas"), [Morgan Freeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Freeman "Morgan Freeman"), [Kevin Kline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Kline "Kevin Kline") and [Mary Steenburgen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Steenburgen "Mary Steenburgen"), the film is about three retirees who travel to [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas "Las Vegas") to have a [bachelor party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_party "Bachelor party") for their last remaining single friend. In a harsh assessment of De Niro's performance, the *[A.V. Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.V._Club "A.V. Club")*'s critic considered it "arguably the low point of De Niro's career." Shortly afterwards, he starred in *[Grudge Match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grudge_Match "Grudge Match")* (2013) opposite [Sylvester Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone "Sylvester Stallone"), as aging boxers stepping into the [ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxing_ring "Boxing ring") for one last match. They had previously worked together in 1997's *Cop Land*. That same year he starred in the crime thriller, *[The Bag Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bag_Man "The Bag Man")*. In 2014, De Niro appeared in a documentary about his father, Robert De Niro Sr., titled *Remembering the Artist Robert De Niro Sr.* which aired on [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO "HBO"). In 2015, he starred in [Nancy Meyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Meyers "Nancy Meyers")' comedy *[The Intern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Intern_(2015_film) "The Intern (2015 film)")* alongside [Anne Hathaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hathaway "Anne Hathaway"). The latter fared better with critics; Mark Olsen of the *Los Angeles Times* cordially remarked "De Niro brings a fresh, relaxed lightness to his performance, tinged with the gruff charm of [Spencer Tracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spencer_Tracy "Spencer Tracy")." His performance won him a nomination from the [Critics Choice Movie Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critics_Choice_Movie_Awards "Critics Choice Movie Awards") for [Best Actor in a Comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Movie_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Comedy "Critics' Choice Movie Award for Best Actor in a Comedy").

Also in 2015, he appeared in two short films, Scorsese's [*The Audition*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Audition_(2015_film) "The Audition (2015 film)") and [JR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JR_(artist) "JR (artist)")'s [*Ellis*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellis_(film) "Ellis (film)"). Returning to the heist genre, he starred in *[Heist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heist_(2015_film) "Heist (2015 film)")*, playing Francis "The Pope" Silva, a gangster casino owner who is targeted by criminals. The film was not a box office success. ## Reception and legacy

Several journalists consider De Niro to be among the best actors of his generation. John Naughton of *[GQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GQ "GQ")* magazine believes that De Niro has "redefined what we can expect of an actor." [A. O. Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._O._Scott "A. O. Scott") said that De Niro "was transforming himself – physically, vocally, psychologically – with each new role. And in the process, before our eyes, reinventing the art of acting." As early as 1977, *Newsweek* remarked that the actor "gives you the shock of becoming, of a metamorphosis that can be thrilling, moving, or frightening." Biographer Douglas Brode praises De Niro's versatility and ability to inhabit any role, although [Pauline Kael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauline_Kael "Pauline Kael") once said in 1983 that she did not like how the actor was "disfiguring" himself in films such as *Raging Bull*. When asked why he undertook such roles, De Niro responded, "To totally submerge into another character and experience life through him, without having to risk the real-life consequences—well it's a cheap way to do doing things that you would never dare to do yourself."

In 2009, he was announced as one of the [Kennedy Center Honorees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Center_Honors "Kennedy Center Honors") with the commemoration: "One of America's greatest cinematic actors, Robert De Niro has demonstrated a legendary commitment to his characters and has co-founded one of the world's major film festivals." [Martin Scorsese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Scorsese "Martin Scorsese") and [Meryl Streep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meryl_Streep "Meryl Streep") honored him at the event. In 2016, he received the [Presidential Medal of Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Medal_of_Freedom "Presidential Medal of Freedom") from President Barack Obama. Obama said "Everybody on this stage has touched me in a very powerful, very personal way [...] These are folks who have helped make me who I am." [White House Press Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Press_Secretary "White House Press Secretary") [Josh Earnest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Earnest "Josh Earnest") added, "There is no arguing that the individuals who will be honored today are richly deserving", he said.

Many of De Niro's films have become classics of American cinema, in which six of them have been inducted into the [U.S. National Film Registry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Film_Registry "National Film Registry") as of 2022. Five films are featured on the American Film Institute's (AFI) list of the [100 greatest American films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFI%27s_100_Years...100_Movies "AFI's 100 Years...100 Movies") of all time. De Niro and [James Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Stewart "James Stewart") share the title for the most films represented on the AFI list. In 2006, De Niro donated his collection of film-related materials, such as scripts, wardrobe pieces and [props](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Props "Props"), to the [Harry Ransom Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Ransom_Center "Harry Ransom Center") at the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin "University of Texas at Austin"). The collection, which took more than two years to process and catalog, opened to the public in 2009. *[Time Out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Out_(magazine) "Time Out (magazine)")* magazine's list of 100 best movies included seven of De Niro's films, as chosen by actors in the industry. In May 2025, De Niro received the [Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes_Film_Festival "Cannes Film Festival")'s [Honorary Palme d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_Palme_d%27Or "Honorary Palme d'Or").

## Business interests

In 1989, De Niro and partner Jane Rosenthal co-founded the film production company TriBeCa Productions, which also organizes the Tribeca Film Festival. De Niro owned [Tribeca Grill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribeca_Grill "Tribeca Grill") (co-owned with Broadway producer [Stewart F. Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewart_F._Lane "Stewart F. Lane")), a [New American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_American_cuisine "New American cuisine") restaurant located at 375 Greenwich Street (at Franklin Street) in Tribeca, Manhattan. It opened in 1990. He is also the owner of the Greenwich Hotel in Tribeca. De Niro co-owns [Nobu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobu "Nobu") restaurants and hotels with partners Meir Teper and Chef [Nobu Matsuhisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobu_Matsuhisa "Nobu Matsuhisa"). The first Nobu Hotel opened inside [Caesars Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesars_Palace "Caesars Palace"), Las Vegas, in 2013. Two years later, the second Nobu Hotel opened at [City of Dreams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Dreams_Manila "City of Dreams Manila") in Manila, Philippines. In 2015, De Niro partnered with [James Packer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Packer "James Packer") after the billionaire acquired a 20 percent stake in Nobu for $100 million. He is a stakeholder in Paradise Found Nobu Resort, a company planning to build a luxury resort on the island of [Barbuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbuda "Barbuda").

## Personal life

De Niro is a long-term resident of New York City, and has been investing in Manhattan's [Tribeca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribeca "Tribeca") neighborhood since 1989. He has properties on the [east](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Side_(Manhattan) "East Side (Manhattan)") and [west](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Side_(Manhattan) "West Side (Manhattan)") sides of Manhattan. He also has a 78-acre (32-hectare) estate in [Gardiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardiner,_New_York "Gardiner, New York"), New York, which serves as his primary residence.

In 1998, De Niro lobbied [U.S. Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Congress "U.S. Congress") against [impeaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeachment_of_Bill_Clinton "Impeachment of Bill Clinton") President [Bill Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Clinton "Bill Clinton").

In October 2003, De Niro was diagnosed with [prostate cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostate_cancer "Prostate cancer"). He underwent surgery at the [Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_Sloan_Kettering_Cancer_Center "Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center") in December 2003.

In 2006, De Niro received Italian citizenship, despite opposition by the [Sons of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sons_of_Italy "Sons of Italy"), who believe that De Niro damaged the public image of Italians by portraying criminals.

In 2012, De Niro joined the anti-[fracking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fracking "Fracking") campaign [Artists Against Fracking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artists_Against_Fracking "Artists Against Fracking").

In 2013, De Niro attended [Israeli president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_president "Israeli president") [Shimon Peres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimon_Peres "Shimon Peres")'s 90th birthday celebration, as well as the [Israeli Presidential conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Presidential_Conference "Israeli Presidential Conference"). He praised Peres as "a great person, [and] a great statesman."

In 2016, De Niro initially defended the inclusion of a controversial documentary, *[Vaxxed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaxxed "Vaxxed")*, at the 2016 Tribeca Film Festival; the film was directed and co-written by discredited [anti-vaccine activist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-vaccine_activist "Anti-vaccine activist") [Andrew Wakefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Wakefield "Andrew Wakefield") and echoed Wakefield's fraudulent claim that there was a causal link between the [MMR vaccine and autism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MMR_vaccine_and_autism "MMR vaccine and autism"). De Niro explained that his interest in the film was from his personal experience with his [autistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autistic "Autistic") son, Elliot. The film was withdrawn from the schedule after consultation with the festival organizers and scientific community. During a subsequent appearance on [NBC's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC%27s "NBC's") *[Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Today_(American_TV_program) "Today (American TV program)")*, De Niro expressed regret that he agreed to exclude *Vaxxed* from the festival and stated, "I think the movie is something that people should see." Also during the appearance, De Niro promoted the 2014 anti-vaccination film *[Trace Amounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace_Amounts "Trace Amounts")* and stated that he doubted the scientific consensus that there was no link between [autism and vaccines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autism_and_vaccines "Autism and vaccines"). In February 2017, De Niro took part in a joint presentation with [Robert F. Kennedy Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_F._Kennedy_Jr. "Robert F. Kennedy Jr."), chairman of the anti-vaccine non-profit [Children's Health Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children%27s_Health_Defense "Children's Health Defense"), to discuss their concerns with vaccine safety. De Niro has stated that he is not anti-vaccination, but does question their efficacy.

Later in 2016, De Niro attended an annual celebrity gala for the [Friends of the Israel Defense Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends_of_the_Israel_Defense_Forces "Friends of the Israel Defense Forces") at the [Beverly Hilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beverly_Hilton "Beverly Hilton") in Los Angeles. The event raised $38 million for the organization.

In October 2018, De Niro was [targeted by an explosive device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_2018_United_States_mail_bombing_attempts "October 2018 United States mail bombing attempts"). The device was found at the Tribeca Grill, which also houses his production company in Manhattan. According to the [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI "FBI"), similar devices were sent to high-profile politicians including [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama"), [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton "Hillary Clinton"), [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden"), former Attorney General [Eric Holder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Holder "Eric Holder"), and former [CIA Director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA_Director "CIA Director") [John Brennan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Brennan_(CIA_officer) "John Brennan (CIA officer)").

De Niro was a supporter of President [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden") for re-election in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_presidential_election,_2024 "United States presidential election, 2024"), and backed Vice President [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris "Kamala Harris")'s [campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris_presidential_campaign,_2024 "Kamala Harris presidential campaign, 2024") after Biden dropped out of the race. He had previously criticized President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump"), calling him a "buffoon", believing that he could become a "vicious dictator" and [comparing his politics to fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_and_fascism "Donald Trump and fascism").

### Relationships

De Niro married actress [Diahnne Abbott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diahnne_Abbott "Diahnne Abbott") in 1976. They have a son, [Raphael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael_De_Niro "Raphael De Niro"), a former actor who works in New York real estate. De Niro also adopted Abbott's daughter, [Drena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drena_De_Niro "Drena De Niro"), from a previous relationship. He and Abbott divorced in 1988.

De Niro was in a relationship with model [Toukie Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toukie_Smith "Toukie Smith") from 1988 to 1996. The couple has twins, son Julian and daughter Airyn, conceived by [*in vitro* fertilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_vitro_fertilization "In vitro fertilization") and delivered by a [surrogate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrogacy "Surrogacy") mother in 1995. Airyn [came out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Came_out "Came out") as a [trans woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans_woman "Trans woman") in April 2025, and Robert stated that he supported her identity.

In 1997, De Niro married actress [Grace Hightower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Hightower "Grace Hightower"). Their son, Elliot, was born in 1998, and the couple split in 1999. A divorce was never finalized, and in 2004 they renewed their vows. In December 2011, their daughter, Helen, was born via surrogate. In 2014, he and Hightower moved into a 6,000-square-foot, five-bedroom apartment at [15 Central Park West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15_Central_Park_West "15 Central Park West"). Four years later, De Niro and Hightower separated after 20 years of marriage. De Niro has four grandchildren: one from his daughter Drena and three from his son Raphael. On April 19, 2021, De Niro's lawyer argued in a virtual divorce hearing presided by a Manhattan judge that he is "working at an unsustainable pace" in order "to support Hightower and pay off all his back taxes." Hightower's lawyer claimed that since the pair filed for divorce in 2018, De Niro had been "unfairly decreasing" the agreed-upon payments to her.

In April 2023, De Niro welcomed his seventh child, a daughter, Gia, with his girlfriend, Tiffany Chen. Aged 79 at the time, De Niro became one of the [oldest fathers on record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oldest_fathers "List of oldest fathers").

In July 2023, it was announced that De Niro's grandson through his daughter Drena, Leandro De Niro Rodriguez, had died at age 19. The cause of death was determined as a [combined drug intoxication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_drug_intoxication "Combined drug intoxication") involving [fentanyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fentanyl "Fentanyl") and [cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine "Cocaine").

### Legal issues

In February 1998, De Niro was held for questioning by French police in connection with an international prostitution ring. De Niro denied any involvement and later filed a complaint against the [examining magistrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Examining_magistrate "Examining magistrate") for "violation of secrecy in an investigation". He stated he would not return to France but has since traveled there several times, including for the [2011 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Cannes_Film_Festival "2011 Cannes Film Festival").

In 1999, De Niro threatened to sue the owners of "De Niro's Supper Club" in [Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver "Vancouver") under section 3 of the [BC Privacy Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_Information_and_Protection_of_Privacy_Act_(British_Columbia) "Freedom of Information and Protection of Privacy Act (British Columbia)"). The restaurant subsequently changed its name to "Section (3)".

In 2006, the [trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_trust "Land trust") that owns De Niro's Gardiner estate sued the town to have its [property tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_tax "Property tax") [assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_assessment "Tax assessment") reduced, arguing that $6 million was too high and should be compared only with similar properties in [Ulster County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_County "Ulster County"), where Gardiner is located. The town, which had been comparing its value with similar estates in [Dutchess County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutchess_County "Dutchess County"), across the [Hudson River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_River "Hudson River"), and [Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut "Connecticut")'s [Litchfield County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litchfield_County "Litchfield County"), where many other affluent New York residents maintain estates on large properties, won in [State Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Supreme_Court "New York State Supreme Court"). In 2014, the trust's lawyers appealed the decision, and the town was unsure whether it should continue to defend the suit, because of financial limitations in that it would have earned far less in payments on the increased taxes than it had spent on legal costs. This angered many residents, who initially sympathized with De Niro, and some proposed to raise money privately to help the town continue the suit. The dispute was publicized by *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")*. "When he (De Niro) read about it on [Election Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_Day_(United_States) "Election Day (United States)"), he went bananas" due to the negative publicity, said Gardiner town councilman Warren Wiegand. He was unaware that a lawsuit had been filed; the trust's accountants took responsibility, citing [fiduciary duty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiduciary_duty "Fiduciary duty"). Shortly afterward, De Niro's lawyer, Tom Harvey, contacted the town to settle the case, and the trust's accounting firm agreed to pay the town's $129,000 legal bill.

In August 2019, De Niro's company Canal Productions filed a $6-million lawsuit against former employee Graham Chase Robinson for breaching her fiduciary duties and violating New York's [faithless servant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faithless_servant "Faithless servant") doctrine by misusing company funds and watching hours of [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") during work hours. In October 2019, Robinson filed a lawsuit against De Niro, claiming harassment and [gender discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_discrimination "Gender discrimination"). In November 2023, a jury found De Niro not personally liable for gender discrimination, but his production company was ordered to pay Robinson $1.2 million in damages.

## Filmography and accolades

Prolific in film since the 1970s, De Niro's most critically acclaimed films, according to review aggregate site [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes"), are [*Bang the Drum Slowly*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bang_the_Drum_Slowly_(film) "Bang the Drum Slowly (film)") (1973), *[Mean Streets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_Streets "Mean Streets")* (1973), *[The Godfather Part II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Godfather_Part_II "The Godfather Part II")* (1974), *[Taxi Driver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxi_Driver "Taxi Driver")* (1976), *[The Deer Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Deer_Hunter "The Deer Hunter")* (1978), *[Raging Bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raging_Bull "Raging Bull")* (1980), *[The King of Comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_King_of_Comedy_(film) "The King of Comedy (film)")* (1983), *[Once Upon a Time in America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Once_Upon_a_Time_in_America "Once Upon a Time in America")* (1984), *[Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_(1985_film) "Brazil (1985 film)")* (1985), *[The Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mission_(1986_film) "The Mission (1986 film)")* (1986), *[Midnight Run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnight_Run "Midnight Run")* (1988), *[Goodfellas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodfellas "Goodfellas")* (1990), *[Casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casino_(1995_film) "Casino (1995 film)")* (1995), *[Heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_(1995_film) "Heat (1995 film)")* (1995), *[Meet the Parents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meet_the_Parents "Meet the Parents")* (2000), *[Silver Linings Playbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Linings_Playbook "Silver Linings Playbook")* (2012), *[The Irishman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Irishman "The Irishman")* (2019), and *[Killers of the Flower Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killers_of_the_Flower_Moon "Killers of the Flower Moon")* (2023).

De Niro has been recognized by the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences "Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences") for the following performances:

* [47th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/47th_Academy_Awards "47th Academy Awards") (1974): [Best Supporting Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor "Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor"), **win**, for *The Godfather Part II*
* [49th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/49th_Academy_Awards "49th Academy Awards") (1976): [Best Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor "Academy Award for Best Actor"), nomination, for *Taxi Driver*
* [51st Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/51st_Academy_Awards "51st Academy Awards") (1978): Best Actor, nomination, for *The Deer Hunter*
* [53rd Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/53rd_Academy_Awards "53rd Academy Awards") (1980): Best Actor, **win**, for *Raging Bull*
* [63rd Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/63rd_Academy_Awards "63rd Academy Awards") (1990): Best Actor, nomination, for *[Awakenings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awakenings "Awakenings")*
* [64th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/64th_Academy_Awards "64th Academy Awards") (1991): Best Actor, nomination, for *[Cape Fear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Fear_(1991_film) "Cape Fear (1991 film)")*
* [85th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/85th_Academy_Awards "85th Academy Awards") (2012): Best Supporting Actor, nomination, for *Silver Linings Playbook*
* [92nd Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/92nd_Academy_Awards "92nd Academy Awards") (2019): [Best Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture"), nomination, for *The Irishman*
* [96th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/96th_Academy_Awards "96th Academy Awards") (2023): Best Supporting Actor, nomination, for *Killers of the Flower Moon*

De Niro has won two [Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards "Golden Globe Awards"): [Best Actor – Motion Picture Drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Actor_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Drama "Best Actor – Motion Picture Drama") for *Raging Bull* and a [Cecil B. DeMille Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecil_B._DeMille_Award "Cecil B. DeMille Award") for "outstanding contributions to the world of entertainment." He was also the 56th recipient of [SAG-AFTRA Life Achievement Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAG-AFTRA_Life_Achievement_Award "SAG-AFTRA Life Achievement Award"); [Leonardo DiCaprio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_DiCaprio "Leonardo DiCaprio"), who co-starred with De Niro in *This Boy's Life*, presented him the award, citing him as an inspiration and influence.

On May 13, 2025, De Niro received the [Honorary Palme d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palme_d%27Or "Palme d'Or") at the [Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes_Film_Festival "Cannes Film Festival"), celebrating his five-decade career in cinema.
