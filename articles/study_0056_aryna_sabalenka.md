# Aryna Sabalenka

**Aryna Siarhiejeŭna Sabalenka** (born 5 May 1998) is a Belarusian professional [tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis "Tennis") player. She has been ranked the world No. 1 in women's [singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_WTA_number_1_ranked_singles_tennis_players "List of WTA number 1 ranked singles tennis players") by the [Women's Tennis Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_Tennis_Association "Women's Tennis Association") (WTA) for a total of 107 weeks ([tenth-most of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_WTA_number_1_ranked_singles_tennis_players#Weeks_at_No._1 "List of WTA number 1 ranked singles tennis players")), and finished as the [year-end No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_WTA_number_1_ranked_singles_tennis_players#Year-end_No._1_player "List of WTA number 1 ranked singles tennis players") twice ([2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_WTA_Tour "2024 WTA Tour"), [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_WTA_Tour "2025 WTA Tour")). Sabalenka has won 24 career singles titles, including four [majors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis)#Tournaments "Grand Slam (tennis)") (two titles at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Open "Australian Open") and two at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Open_(tennis) "US Open (tennis)")), as well as eleven [WTA 1000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_1000 "WTA 1000") events. She is also a former [world No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_WTA_number_1_ranked_doubles_tennis_players "List of WTA number 1 ranked doubles tennis players") in women's doubles and has won six doubles titles, including the [2019 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2019 US Open – Women's doubles") and [2021 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2021 Australian Open – Women's doubles"), both with [Elise Mertens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elise_Mertens "Elise Mertens").

Sabalenka came to prominence in 2017 when she and [Aliaksandra Sasnovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliaksandra_Sasnovich "Aliaksandra Sasnovich") led the [Belarus Fed Cup team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus_Fed_Cup_team "Belarus Fed Cup team") to a runner-up finish despite both being ranked outside the top 75 at the time. She finished 2018 and 2019 ranked world No. 11 in singles. Following two major singles semifinal appearances in 2021, Sabalenka rose to world No. 2 but struggled to maintain that success in 2022 consistently. In 2023, she won her first major singles title at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 Australian Open – Women's singles"), finished runner-up at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 US Open – Women's singles"), and obtained the world No. 1 ranking for the first time.

In 2024, Sabalenka defended her [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 Australian Open – Women's singles") title and won a third major singles title at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 US Open – Women's singles"), finishing as the year-end No. 1 for the first time. In 2025, she defended her title at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2025 US Open – Women's singles") and was runner-up at the [Australian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2025 Australian Open – Women's singles") and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2025 French Open – Women's singles") Opens, holding the No. 1 position for the entire season. In 2026, she reached two major singles finals ([Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2026 Australian Open – Women's singles") and [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2026 US Open – Women's singles")) and completed the [Sunshine Double](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunshine_Double "Sunshine Double") (winning [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2026 BNP Paribas Open – Women's singles") and [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2026 Miami Open – Women's singles") back-to-back). Also in 2026, Sabalenka won the [Laureus World Sports Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laureus_World_Sports_Award "Laureus World Sports Award") for [Sportswoman of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laureus_World_Sports_Award_for_Sportswoman_of_the_Year "Laureus World Sports Award for Sportswoman of the Year").

Sabalenka began playing doubles regularly in 2019. With Mertens as her partner, she found instant success by winning the [Sunshine Double](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunshine_Double "Sunshine Double") ([Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2019 BNP Paribas Open – Women's doubles") and [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2019 Miami Open – Women's doubles")) that same year. They won the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2019 US Open – Women's doubles") later in the season, and Sabalenka qualified for the [WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Doubles "2019 WTA Finals – Doubles") for the first time. After she and Mertens won their second major title at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2021 Australian Open – Women's doubles") in 2021, Sabalenka became the doubles world No. 1, after which she shifted to playing exclusively singles.

## Early life and background

Sabalenka was born in [Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minsk "Minsk"), the capital of [Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus "Belarus"). Her father, Sergey (died 2019), was an [ice hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey "Ice hockey") player. Sabalenka started playing tennis by chance. She said, "One day, my dad was just driving me somewhere in the car, and he saw tennis courts on the way. So he took me to the courts. I liked it and enjoyed it, and that's how it was. That's how it started." She began training at the National Tennis Academy in Minsk when it opened in 2014. In 2015, the Belarusian Tennis Federation persuaded Sabalenka and her team to focus on playing low-level professional events instead of junior tournaments, even though she was still eligible to compete at the junior level.

## Career

### Juniors

Sabalenka had a late start on the [ITF Junior Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITF_Junior_Circuit "ITF Junior Circuit"), instead competing on the U14 and U16 Tennis Europe tours at a younger age. She did not compete in the main draw of any ITF events until 2013 at the low-level Grade-4 Tallink Cup in Estonia at the age of 15. She never played in the junior Grand Slam tournaments or any other high-level Grade-A and Grade-1 events. Without the higher point levels from these bigger tournaments, she had a career-high ranking of just No. 225. Sabalenka won her first ITF title in doubles at the lowest-level Grade-5 Alatan Tour Cup in Belarus in late 2013 with compatriot [Vera Lapko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vera_Lapko "Vera Lapko") as her partner. In 2014, she excelled at Grade 4 events. She reached her first singles final at the Estonian Junior Open in June and won her first singles title at the MTV Total Junior Cup in Finland in October. At the end of the season, Sabalenka defended her Alatan Tour Cup doubles title, this time with compatriot Nika Shytkouskaya, and also won the singles title. She only played in one tournament in 2015, the European Junior Championships. As a Grade B1 event, this was the highest-level junior tournament she played in. She lost in the second round to top seed [Markéta Vondroušová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark%C3%A9ta_Vondrou%C5%A1ov%C3%A1 "Markéta Vondroušová").

### 2012–2016: Professional & Fed Cup debut

Sabalenka began playing on the [ITF Women's Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITF_Women%27s_World_Tennis_Tour "ITF Women's World Tennis Tour") in 2012, even before she competed on the [ITF Junior Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITF_Junior_Circuit "ITF Junior Circuit"). Her first five tournaments were in her hometown of Minsk and spread out over two years, but she did not win a main draw match in any of them. She won her first professional match at the very end of 2014 in Istanbul. The following season in October, she won her first two titles in back-to-back weeks in [Antalya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antalya "Antalya"), both at the $10k level. Sabalenka also won a $25k title the last week of the year. This title put her into the top 300 WTA rankings for the first time at the start of 2016. That year, she made her Fed Cup debut in April, losing her only match. She also won her two biggest titles to date at the $50k level. The first in [Tianjin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Tianjin_Health_Industry_Park "2016 Tianjin Health Industry Park") put her into the top 200 in May and the second in [Toyota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Dunlop_World_Challenge "2016 Dunlop World Challenge") in November helped her finish the year ranked at No. 137 in the world.

### 2017: WTA 125 title, top 100

Despite some early season success in the Fed Cup, Sabalenka had a quiet start to the year. She played in her first WTA Tour main draw in February as a qualifier at the [Dubai Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Dubai_Tennis_Championships "2017 Dubai Tennis Championships"); however, she did not win her first WTA Tour match until [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Wimbledon_Championships "2017 Wimbledon Championships") in July. In her Grand Slam debut, she again reached the main draw through qualifying and defeated [Irina Khromacheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irina_Khromacheva "Irina Khromacheva") in the opening round. Sabalenka followed up this achievement with another win at the [Washington Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Citi_Open "2017 Citi Open") over No. 34 [Lauren Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauren_Davis "Lauren Davis"), the 2016 runner-up and the highest-ranked player she had defeated at the time.

After losing in qualifying at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_US_Open_(tennis) "2017 US Open (tennis)"), Sabalenka reached her first ever WTA semifinal at the [Tashkent Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Tashkent_Open "2017 Tashkent Open"), defeating third seed and world No. 53, [Tatjana Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatjana_Maria "Tatjana Maria"), along the way. A few weeks later, she entered the [Tianjin Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Tianjin_Open "2017 Tianjin Open") as the 119th-ranked player in the world, but managed to reach her first WTA tournament final. There, she faced her childhood idol [Maria Sharapova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Sharapova "Maria Sharapova"), but ultimately lost in two tight sets. With this performance, she rose to No. 76 in the rankings, entering the top 100 for the first time. After losing a tight Fed Cup final to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Fed_Cup_team "United States Fed Cup team"), Sabalenka finished the season by winning the biggest title of her career at the time at the [Mumbai Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Mumbai_Open "2017 Mumbai Open"), a [WTA 125](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_125_tournaments "WTA 125 tournaments") event. The title cemented her at No. 78 at the end of the year.

### 2018: Premier 5 title, top 20

After playing relatively few WTA events in 2017, Sabalenka used her higher ranking to play exclusively on the WTA Tour 2018. She reached two quarterfinals to begin the year. At the [Shenzhen Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Shenzhen_Longhua_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2018 Shenzhen Longhua Open – Women's singles"), she defeated [Monica Niculescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monica_Niculescu "Monica Niculescu") and qualifier [Danka Kovinić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danka_Kovini%C4%87 "Danka Kovinić") to reach the quarterfinals, where she lost to world No. 1 [Simona Halep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simona_Halep "Simona Halep") in straight sets. At the [Hobart International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Hobart_International_%E2%80%93_Singles "2018 Hobart International – Singles"), she defeated wildcard entrant [Eugenie Bouchard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenie_Bouchard "Eugenie Bouchard") and [Zhang Shuai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Shuai "Zhang Shuai") to reach the quarterfinals, where she lost to [Lesia Tsurenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesia_Tsurenko "Lesia Tsurenko") in straight sets. At the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2018 Australian Open – Women's singles"), however, she lost her opening-round match to No. 17 [Ashleigh Barty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashleigh_Barty "Ashleigh Barty"). At the [Indian Wells Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2018 BNP Paribas Open – Women's singles"), she defeated [Varvara Lepchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varvara_Lepchenko "Varvara Lepchenko") and No. 18 [Svetlana Kuznetsova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svetlana_Kuznetsova "Svetlana Kuznetsova") before losing to [Markéta Vondroušová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark%C3%A9ta_Vondrou%C5%A1ov%C3%A1 "Markéta Vondroušová").

Sabalenka began the clay-court season at the [Ladies Open Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Ladies_Open_Lugano_%E2%80%93_Singles "2018 Ladies Open Lugano – Singles"). She defeated [Mihaela Buzărnescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihaela_Buz%C4%83rnescu "Mihaela Buzărnescu"), [Polona Hercog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polona_Hercog "Polona Hercog"), [Camila Giorgi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camila_Giorgi "Camila Giorgi"), and wildcard entrant [Stefanie Vögele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefanie_V%C3%B6gele "Stefanie Vögele") to reach her second career final, where she lost to No. 20 [Elise Mertens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elise_Mertens "Elise Mertens") in straight sets. As a result, her ranking rose to the top 50. At the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2018 French Open – Women's singles"), however, she lost in the first round to No. 22 [Kiki Bertens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiki_Bertens "Kiki Bertens").

Sabalenka had stronger results on grass. At the [Libéma Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Lib%C3%A9ma_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2018 Libéma Open – Women's singles"), she defeated Markéta Vondroušová and [Ajla Tomljanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajla_Tomljanovi%C4%87 "Ajla Tomljanović") to make it to the quarterfinals, where she lost to [Kirsten Flipkens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirsten_Flipkens "Kirsten Flipkens") in straight sets. At the [Eastbourne International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Eastbourne_International_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2018 Eastbourne International – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated No. 13 [Julia Görges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_G%C3%B6rges "Julia Görges") and No. 15 Elise Mertens to reach the quarterfinals. In the quarterfinals, she upset No. 7 and defending champion [Karolína Plíšková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol%C3%ADna_Pl%C3%AD%C5%A1kov%C3%A1 "Karolína Plíšková") for her first victory over a top 10 player. She defeated [Agnieszka Radwańska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnieszka_Radwa%C5%84ska "Agnieszka Radwańska") to reach her first WTA Premier final, where she lost to world No. 2 [Caroline Wozniacki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Wozniacki "Caroline Wozniacki") in straight sets. As a result, her ranking climbed to No. 32. At [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2018 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles"), however, she lost in the first round to No. 28 Mihaela Buzărnescu.

During the North American hardcourt summer season, Sabalenka rose through the rankings. At the [Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Rogers_Cup_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2018 Rogers Cup – Women's singles"), she stunned world No. 2 Caroline Wozniacki for the biggest win of her career, but lost in the third round to No. 15 Elise Mertens. At the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Western_%26_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2018 Western & Southern Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka recorded two more top-ten wins over No. 8 Karolína Plíšková and No. 5 [Caroline Garcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Garcia "Caroline Garcia") to reach her first Premier 5 quarterfinal. She stunned No. 13 [Madison Keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Keys "Madison Keys") to reach her first Premier 5 semifinal, where she lost to world No. 1 Simona Halep in straight sets. At the [Connecticut Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Connecticut_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2018 Connecticut Open – Singles"), Sabalenka defeated lucky loser [Samantha Stosur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samantha_Stosur "Samantha Stosur"), No. 24 [Daria Saville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daria_Saville "Daria Saville"), lucky loser [Belinda Bencic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belinda_Bencic "Belinda Bencic"), and No. 9 Julia Görges to reach the final. In the final, she defeated [Carla Suárez Navarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carla_Su%C3%A1rez_Navarro "Carla Suárez Navarro") in straight sets to claim her first Premier-tier title and jump into the top 20. Sabalenka closed out this part of the season with her best result at a Grand Slam tournament to date. At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2018 US Open – Women's singles"), she defeated [Danielle Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danielle_Collins "Danielle Collins"), qualifier [Vera Zvonareva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vera_Zvonareva "Vera Zvonareva"), and No. 5 [Petra Kvitová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petra_Kvitov%C3%A1 "Petra Kvitová") to make it to the fourth round, where she lost to No. 19 and eventual champion [Naomi Osaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Osaka "Naomi Osaka") in three sets. She was the only player to win a set against Osaka in the tournament.

At the [Wuhan Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Wuhan_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2018 Wuhan Open – Singles"), Sabalenka defeated Carla Suárez Navarro in the first round. In the second round, she upset No. 6 [Elina Svitolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elina_Svitolina "Elina Svitolina"). She defeated qualifier [Sofia Kenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia_Kenin "Sofia Kenin") and [Dominika Cibulková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominika_Cibulkov%C3%A1 "Dominika Cibulková") to reach her second Premier 5 semifinal. She defeated No. 17 Ashleigh Barty to reach her first Premier 5 final. In the final, she defeated [Anett Kontaveit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anett_Kontaveit "Anett Kontaveit") in straight sets to win her first Premier 5 title. At the [China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_China_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2018 China Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated No. 15 [Garbiñe Muguruza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garbi%C3%B1e_Muguruza "Garbiñe Muguruza") and No. 4 Caroline Garcia to reach the quarterfinals, where she lost to wildcard entrant [Wang Qiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Qiang_(tennis) "Wang Qiang (tennis)") in straight sets. The success in China helped her reach a new career-high singles ranking of No. 11. At the end of the season, Sabalenka qualified for the [WTA Elite Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_WTA_Elite_Trophy_%E2%80%93_Singles "2018 WTA Elite Trophy – Singles"). She defeated Ashleigh Barty but lost to Caroline Garcia in the group stage. Barty, having defeated Garcia with fewer games lost, advanced out of the group to end Sabalenka's season. Sabalenka was named the [WTA Newcomer of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_Newcomer_of_the_Year "WTA Newcomer of the Year").

### 2019: Premier 5 title, singles top 10, doubles No. 2

#### Singles: Elite Trophy, three titles

Sabalenka began the season at the [Shenzhen Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_WTA_Shenzhen_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2019 WTA Shenzhen Open – Singles"). She defeated Tatjana Maria and [Ekaterina Alexandrova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekaterina_Alexandrova "Ekaterina Alexandrova") to reach the quarterfinals. She reached the semifinals as No. 29 Maria Sharapova retired. She defeated [Wang Yafan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Yafan "Wang Yafan") to reach the final. In the final, she came from a set down to defeated [Alison Riske](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alison_Riske "Alison Riske") and win the title. However, she could not build on this success in the rest of the first half of the year. At the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2019 Australian Open – Women's singles"), she defeated qualifier [Anna Kalinskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Kalinskaya "Anna Kalinskaya") and [Katie Boulter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Boulter "Katie Boulter") before losing to [Amanda Anisimova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Anisimova "Amanda Anisimova"). Nonetheless, she made her top-10 debut following the event. At the [St. Petersburg Ladies Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_St._Petersburg_Ladies%27_Trophy_%E2%80%93_Singles "2019 St. Petersburg Ladies' Trophy – Singles"), Sabalenka defeated [Alison Van Uytvanck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alison_Van_Uytvanck "Alison Van Uytvanck") and qualifier Ekaterina Alexandrova to reach the semifinals, where she lost to No. 8 Kiki Bertens in straight sets. At the [Dubai Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Dubai_Tennis_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2019 Dubai Tennis Championships – Women's singles"), she defeated qualifier [Ivana Jorović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivana_Jorovi%C4%87 "Ivana Jorović") before losing to Belinda Bencic. At the [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2019 BNP Paribas Open – Women's singles"), she defeated Ajla Tomljanović and No. 28 Lesia Tsurenko before losing to No. 8 [Angelique Kerber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelique_Kerber "Angelique Kerber"). Following early exits in [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2019 Miami Open – Women's singles"), [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2019 Mutua Madrid Open – Women's singles"), and [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2019 Italian Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka reached the semifinals at the [Internationaux de Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Internationaux_de_Strasbourg_%E2%80%93_Singles "2019 Internationaux de Strasbourg – Singles"), where she lost to [Dayana Yastremska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayana_Yastremska "Dayana Yastremska") in straight sets. At the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2019 French Open – Women's singles"), she lost to Amanda Anisimova again in the second round. At the [Eastbourne International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Eastbourne_International_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2019 Eastbourne International – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated No. 14 and defending champion Caroline Wozniacki to reach the quarterfinals, where she lost to No. 4 Kiki Bertens in three sets. At [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2019 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles"), she lost in the first round to [Magdaléna Rybáriková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdal%C3%A9na_Ryb%C3%A1rikov%C3%A1 "Magdaléna Rybáriková").

Sabalenka had a better second half of the season. At the [Silicon Valley Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Silicon_Valley_Classic_%E2%80%93_Singles "2019 Silicon Valley Classic – Singles"), she defeated wildcard entrant [CoCo Vandeweghe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CoCo_Vandeweghe "CoCo Vandeweghe"), No. 29 Carla Suárez Navarro, and No. 25 [Donna Vekić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donna_Veki%C4%87 "Donna Vekić") to reach the final, where she lost to [Zheng Saisai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zheng_Saisai "Zheng Saisai") in straight sets. At the [Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Rogers_Cup_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2019 Rogers Cup – Women's singles"), she lost in the first round to [Anastasia Pavlyuchenkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasia_Pavlyuchenkova "Anastasia Pavlyuchenkova"). At the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Western_%26_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2019 Western & Southern Open – Women's singles"), she defeated [Petra Martić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petra_Marti%C4%87 "Petra Martić") and Zheng Saisai before losing to [Maria Sakkari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Sakkari "Maria Sakkari"). At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2019 US Open – Women's singles"), she defeated [Victoria Azarenka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Azarenka "Victoria Azarenka") before losing to [Yulia Putintseva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yulia_Putintseva "Yulia Putintseva"). She returned to China following the US Open, and produced stronger results. At the [Zhengzhou Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Zhengzhou_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2019 Zhengzhou Open – Singles"), Sabalenka defeated [Jeļena Ostapenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Je%C4%BCena_Ostapenko "Jeļena Ostapenko") to reach the quarterfinals, where she lost to No. 23 Petra Martić in three sets. At the [Wuhan Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Wuhan_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2019 Wuhan Open – Singles"), Sabalenka defeated [Aliaksandra Sasnovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliaksandra_Sasnovich "Aliaksandra Sasnovich"), Danielle Collins, and No. 8 Kiki Bertens to reach the quarterfinals, without dropping a set. She defeated wildcard entrant [Elena Rybakina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elena_Rybakina "Elena Rybakina") in three sets to reach the semifinals. She upset world No. 1 Ashleigh Barty in straight sets to reach her second straight Wuhan Open final. In the final, she defeated Alison Riske in three sets to defend her title. At the end of the season, Sabalenka qualified for the [WTA Elite Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_WTA_Elite_Trophy_%E2%80%93_Singles "2019 WTA Elite Trophy – Singles") for the second consecutive year. In her round-robin group, she swept Maria Sakkari and Elise Mertens. In the semifinals, she defeated [Karolína Muchová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol%C3%ADna_Muchov%C3%A1 "Karolína Muchová"). In the final, she defeated Kiki Bertens in straight sets to take her third title of the year in China. She finished the year for a ranking of world No. 11, the same as she ended 2018.

#### Doubles: US Open title, Sunshine Double

Sabalenka started the year ranked No. 73 in doubles. She began partnering with Elise Mertens in January, when the pair lost to top seeds [Barbora Krejčíková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbora_Krej%C4%8D%C3%ADkov%C3%A1 "Barbora Krejčíková") and [Kateřina Siniaková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate%C5%99ina_Siniakov%C3%A1 "Kateřina Siniaková") in the third round of the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Australian_Open "2019 Australian Open"). They had their breakthrough in March at the two Premier Mandatory tournaments. In only their second tournament together, Sabalenka and Mertens won the [Indian Wells Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Indian_Wells_Masters "2019 Indian Wells Masters"). They defeated three of the top five seeds in the event, including second seeds [Tímea Babos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%ADmea_Babos "Tímea Babos") and [Kristina Mladenovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristina_Mladenovic "Kristina Mladenovic") in the first round and top seeds Krejčíková and Siniaková in the final. The pair matched this success at their next event by winning the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Miami_Open "2019 Miami Open") to complete the Sunshine Double. They defeated three of the top six seeds, including third seeds [Hsieh Su-wei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hsieh_Su-wei "Hsieh Su-wei") and [Barbora Strýcová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbora_Str%C3%BDcov%C3%A1 "Barbora Strýcová") in the second round. They won the final against sixth seeds Samantha Stosur and Zhang Shuai. With these two titles, Sabalenka rose to No. 21 in the world.

Sabalenka and Mertens continued to partner together throughout the year, entering eleven events before the year-end championships. Sabalenka did not have much success in the Grand Slam singles events, but she produced much better results in doubles. Sabalenka and Mertens reached the semifinals at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_French_Open "2019 French Open"), losing to second seeds Babos and Mladenovic. They then reached the quarterfinals at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Wimbledon_Championships "2019 Wimbledon Championships"), losing to third seeds Hsieh and Strýcová. These were Sabalenka's first two appearances in at least the quarterfinals at a Grand Slam event. Sabalenka and Mertens had their best result of the year at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_US_Open_(tennis) "2019 US Open (tennis)"). As the fourth seed at the event, they made it to the final without playing another top-ten-seeded team. In the final, they faced eighth seeds Victoria Azarenka and Ashleigh Barty, the latter of whom had won the title a year earlier with CoCo Vandeweghe. Sabalenka and Mertens defeated Azarenka and Barty in straight sets for their first Grand Slam title in any discipline. With this title, Sabalenka made her top 10 debut in doubles at No. 6 in the world.

Sabalenka and Mertens made one more final during the year, finishing runner-up at the [Wuhan Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Wuhan_Open "2019 Wuhan Open"), where Sabalenka won the singles title. Their three big titles helped them win the Race to Shenzhen and qualify for the [WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_WTA_Finals "2019 WTA Finals") as the top seeds. Before the event, Sabalenka and Mertens moved up to No. 2 and No. 3 in the rankings, respectively, behind only world No. 1, Barbora Strýcová. At the WTA Finals, the pair were placed in a round robin group with third seeds Babos and Mladenovic, fifth seeds [Chan Hao-ching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chan_Hao-ching "Chan Hao-ching") and [Latisha Chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latisha_Chan "Latisha Chan"), as well as eighth seeds [Anna-Lena Grönefeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna-Lena_Gr%C3%B6nefeld "Anna-Lena Grönefeld") and [Demi Schuurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demi_Schuurs "Demi Schuurs"). In their opening match, they were upset by Grönefeld and Schuurs in a match tiebreak. After defeating the Chan sisters, Sabalenka and Mertens lost to Babos and Mladenovic in another match tiebreak. They did not advance out of their group.

### 2020: Premier 5 title

Sabalenka started the season at the [Shenzhen Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_WTA_Shenzhen_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2020 WTA Shenzhen Open – Singles"). She defeated qualifier [Margarita Gasparyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margarita_Betova "Margarita Betova") before losing to [Kristýna Plíšková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krist%C3%BDna_Pl%C3%AD%C5%A1kov%C3%A1 "Kristýna Plíšková"). At the [Adelaide International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Adelaide_International_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2020 Adelaide International – Women's singles"), Sabalenka upset No. 4 Simona Halep to reach the semifinals, where she lost to Dayana Yastremska in straight sets. At the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2020 Australian Open – Women's singles"), she lost in the first round to Carla Suárez Navarro. She enjoyed moderate success in [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2020 Australian Open – Women's doubles"), reaching the quarterfinals with Elise Mertens before losing to the Chan sisters. At the [Dubai Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Dubai_Tennis_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2020 Dubai Tennis Championships – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated Maria Sakkari and Elise Mertens to reach the quarterfinals, where she lost to No. 2 Simona Halep in three sets. At the [Qatar Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Qatar_Total_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2020 Qatar Total Open – Singles"), Sabalenka defeated Anett Kontaveit, No. 20 Maria Sakkari, Zheng Saisai and Svetlana Kuznetsova to reach the final. In the final, she defeated No. 11 Petra Kvitová in straight sets to win her third Premier 5 title.

After the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic") shut down the WTA Tour, Sabalenka entered the [Top Seed Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Top_Seed_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2020 Top Seed Open – Singles") in August. She defeated [Madison Brengle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Brengle "Madison Brengle") before falling to [Coco Gauff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coco_Gauff "Coco Gauff"). In [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Western_%26_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2020 Western & Southern Open – Women's singles"), she defeated qualifier [CiCi Bellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CiCi_Bellis "CiCi Bellis") before losing to qualifier [Jessica Pegula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pegula "Jessica Pegula"). At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2020 US Open – Women's singles"), she defeated [Océane Dodin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oc%C3%A9ane_Dodin "Océane Dodin") before losing to Victoria Azarenka. She had moderate success in doubles, reaching the quarterfinals of both events. Her results began to improve on clay. At the [Internationaux de Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Internationaux_de_Strasbourg_%E2%80%93_Singles "2020 Internationaux de Strasbourg – Singles"), Sabalenka defeated qualifier [Ellen Perez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellen_Perez "Ellen Perez"), [Anna Blinkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Blinkova "Anna Blinkova"), and [Kateřina Siniaková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate%C5%99ina_Siniakov%C3%A1 "Kateřina Siniaková") to reach the semifinals, where she lost to No. 5 Elina Svitolina in three sets. At the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2020 French Open – Women's singles"), she defeated Jessica Pegula and [Daria Kasatkina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daria_Kasatkina "Daria Kasatkina") before losing to [Ons Jabeur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ons_Jabeur "Ons Jabeur").

Sabalenka entered two indoor hard-court events at the end of 2020. At the [Ostrava Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_J%26T_Banka_Ostrava_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2020 J&T Banka Ostrava Open – Singles"), she came from a set down to defeat qualifiers Coco Gauff and [Sara Sorribes Tormo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Sorribes_Tormo "Sara Sorribes Tormo"). She defeated [Jennifer Brady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Brady "Jennifer Brady") and No. 14 Victoria Azarenka in straight sets to win her second title of the season. At the same tournament, she also won the [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_J%26T_Banka_Ostrava_Open_%E2%80%93_Doubles "2020 J&T Banka Ostrava Open – Doubles") event with Elise Mertens, defeating [Gabriela Dabrowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriela_Dabrowski "Gabriela Dabrowski") and [Luisa Stefani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luisa_Stefani "Luisa Stefani") in the final. At the [Linz Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Upper_Austria_Ladies_Linz_%E2%80%93_Singles "2020 Upper Austria Ladies Linz – Singles"), Sabalenka defeated [Jasmine Paolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasmine_Paolini "Jasmine Paolini") and qualifiers Stefanie Vögele and Océane Dodin to reach the semifinals. She defeated [Barbora Krejčíková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbora_Krej%C4%8D%C3%ADkov%C3%A1 "Barbora Krejčíková") to reach the final. In the final, she defeated No. 21 Elise Mertens in straight sets to capture her third title of the season. For the first time, she finished the year at the top 10.

### 2021: Major semifinals, singles No. 2, doubles No. 1

Sabalenka entered her first tournament in 2021 at the [Abu Dhabi Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Abu_Dhabi_Women%27s_Tennis_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 Abu Dhabi Women's Tennis Open – Singles"). She defeated Polona Hercog, Ajla Tomljanović, and Ons Jabeur, without dropping a set, to reach the quarterfinals. She defeated No. 19 Elena Rybakina in three sets to reach the semifinals. She defeated No. 22 Maria Sakkari and [Veronika Kudermetova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veronika_Kudermetova "Veronika Kudermetova") in straight sets to win her third straight WTA title, extending her winning streak to 15 matches and catapulting her to a new ranking of No. 7.

At the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2021 Australian Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated [Viktória Hrunčáková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikt%C3%B3ria_Hrun%C4%8D%C3%A1kov%C3%A1 "Viktória Hrunčáková"), Daria Kasatkina, and [Ann Li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Li_(tennis) "Ann Li (tennis)") to reach the fourth round, where she lost to No. 11 [Serena Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serena_Williams "Serena Williams") in three sets. Nonetheless, at the same tournament, Sabalenka won her second major [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2021 Australian Open – Women's doubles") title with Elise Mertens, defeating Barbora Krejčíková and Kateřina Siniaková in the final. By winning the title, she ascended to world No. 1 in the doubles rankings for the first time in her career.

As the defending champion at the [Qatar Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Qatar_Total_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 Qatar Total Open – Singles"), Sabalenka lost her opening match to Garbiñe Muguruza. At the [Dubai Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Dubai_Tennis_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2021 Dubai Tennis Championships – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated [Alizé Cornet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliz%C3%A9_Cornet "Alizé Cornet") and No. 24 Anett Kontaveit to reach the quarterfinals, where she lost again to No. 16 Garbiñe Muguruza in three sets.

At the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2021 Miami Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka came from a set down to defeat qualifier [Tsvetana Pironkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsvetana_Pironkova "Tsvetana Pironkova") in her opening match. She defeated Veronika Kudermetova and No. 20 Markéta Vondroušová in straight sets to reach the quarterfinals. In the quarterfinals, she lost to world No. 1 Ashleigh Barty in three sets.

Sabalenka started her clay season at the [Stuttgart Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 Porsche Tennis Grand Prix – Singles"). She defeated Zhang Shuai and qualifier [Anna-Lena Friedsam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna-Lena_Friedsam "Anna-Lena Friedsam"), without dropping a set, to reach the quarterfinals. She defeated Anett Kontaveit in three sets to reach the semifinals. She upset No. 3 Simona Halep in straight sets to reach the final. In the final, she lost again to world No. 1 Ashleigh Barty in three sets.

At the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2021 Mutua Madrid Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated qualifier Vera Zvonareva, Daria Kasatkina, and Jessica Pegula, without dropping a set, to reach the quarterfinals. She reached the semifinals as No. 16 Elise Mertens retired while trailing by a set. She defeated [Anastasia Pavlyuchenkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasia_Pavlyuchenkova "Anastasia Pavlyuchenkova") in straight sets to reach her second consecutive final on clay. In the final, she upset world No. 1 Ashleigh Barty in three sets to win her tenth WTA title and first on clay. As a result, her ranking moved to world No. 4.

At the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2021 Italian Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated Sara Sorribes Tormo before losing to Coco Gauff. At the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2021 French Open – Women's singles"), she defeated qualifier [Ana Konjuh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ana_Konjuh "Ana Konjuh") and Aliaksandra Sasnovich before losing to Anastasia Pavlyuchenkova.

Sabalenka started her grass season at the [German Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_WTA_German_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 WTA German Open – Singles"). She lost her opening match against Madison Keys. Nonetheless, Sabalenka and her compatriot Victoria Azarenka won the [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_WTA_German_Open_%E2%80%93_Doubles "2021 WTA German Open – Doubles") event, defeating the top-seeded pair of Demi Schuurs and [Nicole Melichar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Melichar "Nicole Melichar") in the final. At the [Eastbourne International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Eastbourne_International_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2021 Eastbourne International – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated qualifier [Bernarda Pera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernarda_Pera "Bernarda Pera") and Alison Riske-Amritraj, without dropping a set, to reach the quarterfinals. In the quarterfinals, she lost to qualifier Camila Giorgi in three sets.

At the [Wimbledon Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_Singles "2021 Wimbledon Championships – Women's Singles"), Sabalenka defeated qualifier Monica Niculescu in the first round. In the second round, she came from a set down to defeat wildcard entrant Katie Boulter. In the third round, she defeated qualifier [Camila Osorio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camila_Osorio "Camila Osorio"). She defeated No. 20 Elena Rybakina in three sets to reach her first major quarterfinal. She defeated No. 24 Ons Jabeur in straight sets to reach her first major semifinal, becoming the third Belarusian woman to reach Wimbledon semifinals following [Natasha Zvereva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natasha_Zvereva "Natasha Zvereva") in 1998 and Victoria Azarenka in 2011 and 2012. In the semifinals, she lost to No. 13 [Karolína Plíšková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol%C3%ADna_Pl%C3%AD%C5%A1kov%C3%A1 "Karolína Plíšková") in three sets. As a result, she reached a career-high ranking of No. 3.

Sabalenka started her North American hard court season at the [Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2021 National Bank Open – Women's singles"). She defeated wildcard entrants [Sloane Stephens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloane_Stephens "Sloane Stephens") and [Rebecca Marino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_Marino "Rebecca Marino") to reach the quarterfinals. She defeated No. 15 Victoria Azarenka to reach the semifinals, where she lost again to No. 6 Karolína Plíšková in straight sets. At the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Western_%26_Southern_Open_-_Women%27s_Singles "2021 Western & Southern Open - Women's Singles"), she lost her opening-round match against [Paula Badosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paula_Badosa "Paula Badosa"). Despite the loss, she reached a career-high ranking of No. 2.

At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2021 US Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated [Nina Stojanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Stojanovi%C4%87 "Nina Stojanović") in the first round in three sets. She defeated [Tamara Zidanšek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamara_Zidan%C5%A1ek "Tamara Zidanšek"), No. 29 Danielle Collins, No. 16 Elise Mertens, and No. 9 Barbora Krejčíková, without dropping a set, to reach her second major semifinal. In the semifinals, she lost to [Leylah Fernandez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leylah_Fernandez "Leylah Fernandez") in three sets.

Due to a positive COVID-19 test, Sabalenka was not able to play at [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_BNP_Paribas_Open_-_Women%27s_Singles "2021 BNP Paribas Open - Women's Singles"). Sabalenka qualified for the [WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 WTA Finals – Singles") in singles for the first time. She defeated [Iga Świątek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iga_%C5%9Awi%C4%85tek "Iga Świątek"), but was eliminated by Paula Badosa and Maria Sakkari in the round-robin stage.

### 2022: US Open semifinal

Sabalenka struggled with her serve at the beginning of 2022. At the [Adelaide International 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Adelaide_International_1_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 Adelaide International 1 – Women's singles"), she lost her opening match against [Kaja Juvan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaja_Juvan "Kaja Juvan"); at the [Adelaide International 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Adelaide_International_2_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 Adelaide International 2 – Women's singles"), she lost her first round to qualifier [Rebecca Peterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_Peterson "Rebecca Peterson"). At the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 Australian Open – Women's singles"), she managed to dig out three-set wins against wildcard entrant [Storm Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storm_Sanders "Storm Sanders"), [Wang Xinyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Xinyu "Wang Xinyu"), and Markéta Vondroušová, but lost in the fourth round to [Kaia Kanepi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaia_Kanepi "Kaia Kanepi") in a three-set match, which ended in a third-set super-tiebreak.

At the [Dubai Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Dubai_Tennis_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 Dubai Tennis Championships – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated qualifier [Marta Kostyuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marta_Kostyuk "Marta Kostyuk") before losing to Petra Kvitová. At the [Qatar Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Qatar_Total_Open_-_Singles "2022 Qatar Total Open - Singles"), Sabalenka beat wildcard entrant Alizé Cornet and [Jil Teichmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jil_Teichmann "Jil Teichmann"), without dropping a set, to reach the quarterfinals. In the quarterfinals, she lost to No. 8 and eventual champion Iga Świątek in straight sets.

At the [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 BNP Paribas Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka lost her opening match against Jasmine Paolini. In [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 Miami Open – Women's singles"), she lost her opening match against [Irina-Camelia Begu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irina-Camelia_Begu "Irina-Camelia Begu").

At the [Charleston Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Credit_One_Charleston_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 Credit One Charleston Open – Singles"), Sabalenka lost in the third round to Amanda Anisimova. At the [Stuttgart Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix_-_Singles "2022 Porsche Tennis Grand Prix - Singles"), Sabalenka defeated [Bianca Andreescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bianca_Andreescu "Bianca Andreescu") and No. 6 Anett Kontaveit in three sets to reach the semifinals. She defeated No. 3 Paula Badosa in straight sets to reach her second straight Stuttgart final. In the final, she lost again to world No. 1 Iga Świątek in straight sets.

As the defending champion at the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 Mutua Madrid Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka was knocked out by Amanda Anisimova again in the first round. At the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 Italian Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated Zhang Shuai and No. 11 Jessica Pegula, without dropping a set, to reach the quarterfinals. She defeated Amanda Anisimova in three sets to reach the semifinals. In the semifinals, she lost to world No. 1 Iga Świątek for the third time in 2022. At the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 French Open – Women's singles"), she defeated [Chloé Paquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlo%C3%A9_Paquet "Chloé Paquet") and Madison Brengle before losing to Camila Giorgi.

Sabalenka began the grass-court season at the [Libéma Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Lib%C3%A9ma_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 Libéma Open – Women's singles"). She defeated [Kateryna Baindl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kateryna_Baindl "Kateryna Baindl") and wildcard entrant [Arianne Hartono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arianne_Hartono "Arianne Hartono"), without dropping a set, to reach the quarterfinals. She defeated Alison Van Uytvanck in three sets to reach the semifinals. She defeated [Shelby Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shelby_Rogers "Shelby Rogers") in straight sets to make the final. In the final, she lost to Ekaterina Alexandrova in straight sets. Due to Wimbledon's [ruling on Russian and Belarusian players](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wimbledon_ban_on_Russian_and_Belarusian_players "Wimbledon ban on Russian and Belarusian players"), Sabalenka was banned from participating in the event, cutting her grass season short.

Sabalenka opened the US hardcourt swing in [Silicon Valley Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Silicon_Valley_Classic_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 Silicon Valley Classic – Singles"). She came from a set down to defeat lucky loser [Caroline Dolehide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Dolehide "Caroline Dolehide") in her opening match. In the quarterfinals, she lost to No. 12 and eventual champion Daria Kasatkina in three sets. At the [Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 National Bank Open – Women's singles"), she defeated Sara Sorribes Tormo before losing to No. 11 Coco Gauff. At the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Western_%26_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 Western & Southern Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka reached the last 16 as qualifier Anna Kalinskaya retired while trailing by a set. She defeated wildcard entrant Shelby Rogers in three sets to reach the quarterfinals. She defeated Zhang Shuai in straight sets to reach the semifinals. In the semifinals, she lost to qualifier and eventual champion [Caroline Garcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Garcia "Caroline Garcia") in three sets.

At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 US Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated qualifier [Catherine Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_Harrison_(tennis) "Catherine Harrison (tennis)") in the first round. In the second round, she came from a set down to defeat Kaia Kanepi. In the third round, she defeated qualifier [Clara Burel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clara_Burel "Clara Burel") in straight sets. In the fourth round, she came from a set down to defeat No. 19 Danielle Collins. She defeated No. 22 Karolína Plíšková in straight sets to reach her second consecutive US Open semifinal. In the semifinals, she was defeated by world No. 1 Iga Świątek for the fourth time this season.

At the [San Diego Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_San_Diego_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 San Diego Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated wildcard entrant Sloane Stephens before losing to qualifier Donna Vekić. At the [Guadalajara Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Guadalajara_Open_Akron_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 Guadalajara Open Akron – Singles"), she lost her opening match against [Liudmila Samsonova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liudmila_Samsonova "Liudmila Samsonova").

Sabalenka qualified for the [WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 WTA Finals – Singles") for a second straight year. She defeated world No. 2 Ons Jabeur and No. 3 Jessica Pegula in the round-robin stage, and No. 1 Iga Świątek in the semifinals. She became the fourth woman to defeat the top 3 players at the same tournament, joining [Steffi Graf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steffi_Graf "Steffi Graf") (1999 French Open), Serena Williams (2002 Miami Open), and [Venus Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus_Williams "Venus Williams") (2008 WTA Finals). She lost her group match against Maria Sakkari and lost in the final to Caroline Garcia in straight sets.

### 2023: Australian Open champion, No. 1

Sabalenka began her 2023 season at the [Adelaide International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Adelaide_International_1_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 Adelaide International 1 – Women's singles"). She defeated Liudmila Samsonova, Markéta Vondroušová, and Irina-Camelia Begu to reach the final. She defeated qualifier [Linda Nosková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Noskov%C3%A1 "Linda Nosková") to win the title, without dropping a set en route.

Seeded fifth at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 Australian Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated [Tereza Martincová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tereza_Martincov%C3%A1 "Tereza Martincová"), Shelby Rogers, Elise Mertens, and No. 10 Belinda Bencic to reach her first-ever Australian Open quarterfinal. She then beat Donna Vekić to reach her fourth Grand Slam semifinal. She beat [Magda Linette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magda_Linette "Magda Linette") to secure her 10th straight-set victory of 2023 and make her first ever major final. In the final, she defeated No. 25 [Elena Rybakina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elena_Rybakina "Elena Rybakina") in three sets to win her first major title. She became the second Belarusian to ever win a major singles title after Victoria Azarenka in 2012 and 2013. After winning the title in Melbourne, Sabalenka rose back to No. 2 in the WTA rankings.

In [Dubai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Dubai_Tennis_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 Dubai Tennis Championships – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated lucky loser [Lauren Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauren_Davis "Lauren Davis") in her opening match. In the third round, she came from a set down to defeat Jeļena Ostapenko. In the quarterfinals, she fell to the eventual champion Barbora Krejčíková in three sets.

At the [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 BNP Paribas Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated [Evgeniya Rodina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgeniya_Rodina "Evgeniya Rodina") in her opening match. She reached the fourth round as qualifier Lesia Tsurenko withdrew. She defeated Barbora Krejčíková in three sets to reach the quarterfinals. She defeated sixth seed Coco Gauff and seventh seed Maria Sakkari in straight sets to reach the final. In a rematch of their Australian Open final, she lost to tenth seed Elena Rybakina in straight sets.

In [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 Miami Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated Shelby Rogers, [Marie Bouzková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Bouzkov%C3%A1 "Marie Bouzková"), and Barbora Krejčíková, without dropping a set, to reach the quarterfinals. In the quarterfinals, she was defeated by [Sorana Cîrstea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorana_C%C3%AErstea "Sorana Cîrstea") in straight sets.

Sabalenka started her clay court season at the [Stuttgart Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 Porsche Tennis Grand Prix – Singles"). She defeated Barbora Krejčíková in straight sets to reach the quarterfinals. She defeated wildcard entrant Paula Badosa in three sets to reach the semifinals. She defeated [Anastasia Potapova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasia_Potapova "Anastasia Potapova") in straight sets to make her third straight Stuttgart final. In a rematch of [last year's final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 Porsche Tennis Grand Prix – Singles"), she was defeated by world No. 1 Iga Świątek in straight sets.

In [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 Mutua Madrid Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated Sorana Cîrstea and wildcard entrants Camila Osorio and [Mirra Andreeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirra_Andreeva "Mirra Andreeva"), without dropping a set, to reach the quarterfinals. In the quarterfinals, she came from a set down to beat [Mayar Sherif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayar_Sherif "Mayar Sherif"). She defeated ninth seed Maria Sakkari in straight sets to reach her second Madrid final. In the final, she defeated world No. 1 Iga Świątek in three sets to win her second Madrid title and fifth WTA 1000 title.

In [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 Italian Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka suffered her first early defeat of the year, losing her opening match to Sofia Kenin. At the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 French Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated Marta Kostyuk, qualifier [Iryna Shymanovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iryna_Shymanovich "Iryna Shymanovich"), [Kamilla Rakhimova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamilla_Rakhimova "Kamilla Rakhimova"), and Sloane Stephens, without dropping a set, to reach her first French Open quarterfinal. She defeated Elina Svitolina in straight sets to reach her first French Open semifinal. In the semifinals, she lost to [Karolína Muchová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol%C3%ADna_Muchov%C3%A1 "Karolína Muchová") in three sets, despite leading 5–2 and holding match point.

Sabalenka began her grass court season at the [German Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_WTA_German_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 WTA German Open – Singles"). She defeated qualifier Vera Zvonareva before losing to wildcard entrant Veronika Kudermetova. At the [Wimbledon Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated [Panna Udvardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panna_Udvardy "Panna Udvardy") in the first round. In the second round, she came from a set down to defeat [Varvara Gracheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varvara_Gracheva "Varvara Gracheva"). She defeated Anna Blinkova, Ekaterina Alexandrova, and Madison Keys, without dropping a set, to reach her fourth consecutive major semifinal. In the semifinals, she lost to sixth seed Ons Jabeur in three sets.

Sabalenka started her North American hard court season in [Montréal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 National Bank Open – Women's singles"). She defeated Petra Martić before losing to Liudmila Samsonova. In [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Western_%26_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 Western & Southern Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated qualifier Ann Li in three sets in her opening match. She defeated Daria Kasatkina in straight sets to reach the quarterfinals. In a rematch of their Wimbledon semifinal, she defeated fifth seed Ons Jabeur in straight sets. In a rematch of their French Open semifinal, she fell again to Karolína Muchová in three sets.

At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 US Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated [Maryna Zanevska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryna_Zanevska "Maryna Zanevska"), [Jodie Burrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jodie_Burrage "Jodie Burrage"), Clara Burel, Daria Kasatkina, and [Zheng Qinwen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zheng_Qinwen "Zheng Qinwen"), without dropping a set, to reach her third consecutive US Open semifinal. She became the first player to reach the semifinals of all four major events in one year since Serena Williams in 2016. She outlasted Madison Keys after losing the first set 6–0 to reach her first US Open final. In the final, she lost to sixth seed Coco Gauff in three sets. Due to the loss of No. 1 Iga Świątek in the fourth round, Sabalenka became, for the first time, the [world No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_WTA_number_1_ranked_singles_tennis_players "List of WTA number 1 ranked singles tennis players") tennis player. She became the 29th player to be ranked No. 1 in the world on the WTA Tour, and the second Belarusian, after Victoria Azarenka. She was also the eighth female player to have been ranked No. 1 in the Open Era in both singles and doubles at some point in their careers.

After the US Open, Sabalenka played at the [China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_China_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 China Open – Women's singles"). She defeated wildcard entrant Sofia Kenin, qualifier Katie Boulter, and Jasmine Paolini, without dropping a set, to reach the quarterfinals. In the quarterfinals, she lost to fifth seed Elena Rybakina in straight sets.

Sabalenka made her third consecutive appearance at the [WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 WTA Finals – Singles"). She defeated Maria Sakkari and Elena Rybakina but lost to Jessica Pegula in the round-robin stage. In the semifinals, she lost to Iga Świątek in straight sets, who would go on to win the event. As a result, Świątek reclaimed the world No. 1 ranking and Sabalenka finished as year-end No. 2.

### 2024: Two major titles, year-end No. 1

Sabalenka started her 2024 tennis season at the [Brisbane International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Brisbane_International_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 Brisbane International – Women's singles"). She defeated [Lucia Bronzetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucia_Bronzetti "Lucia Bronzetti"), [Zhu Lin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhu_Lin_(tennis) "Zhu Lin (tennis)"), Daria Kasatkina, and Victoria Azarenka without dropping a set, to reach the final. In the final, she lost to second seed Elena Rybakina in straight sets.

At the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 Australian Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka overcame qualifier [Ella Seidel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ella_Seidel "Ella Seidel"), qualifier [Brenda Fruhvirtová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenda_Fruhvirtov%C3%A1 "Brenda Fruhvirtová"), Lesia Tsurenko, Amanda Anisimova, ninth seed Barbora Krejčíková, without dropping a set, to reach the semifinals. In a rematch of their [US Open final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 US Open – Women's singles"), she defeated fourth seed Coco Gauff in straight sets to reach her second consecutive Australian Open final. In the final, she defeated Zheng Qinwen in straight sets, becoming the first woman to successfully defend her title at the Australian Open since Victoria Azarenka in 2013.

At the [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 BNP Paribas Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated [Peyton Stearns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peyton_Stearns "Peyton Stearns") and wildcard entrant [Emma Raducanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Raducanu "Emma Raducanu") before losing to [Emma Navarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Navarro "Emma Navarro"). In [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 Miami Open – Women's singles"), she defeated Paula Badosa before losing to [Anhelina Kalinina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anhelina_Kalinina "Anhelina Kalinina").

Sabalenka started her clay season in [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix_%E2%80%93_Singles "2024 Porsche Tennis Grand Prix – Singles"). She reached the quarterfinals when Paula Badosa retired, but was eliminated by sixth seed Markéta Vondroušová in three sets. As the defending champion in [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 Mutua Madrid Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka reached the quarterfinals after three-set wins over Magda Linette, wildcard entrant [Robin Montgomery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Montgomery "Robin Montgomery"), and Danielle Collins. She defeated Mirra Andreeva in straight sets to reach the semifinals. In the semifinals, she edged an epic three-set victory over fourth seed Elena Rybakina. In a rematch of the [previous year's final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 Mutua Madrid Open – Women's singles"), she faced world No. 1 Iga Świątek. Sabalenka had three championship points during the match, but eventually lost.

In [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 Italian Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka came from a set down to defeat qualifier [Katie Volynets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Volynets "Katie Volynets") in her opening match. In the third round, she defeated Dayana Yastremska in straight sets. In the fourth round, she came from a set down to defeat Elina Svitolina. She defeated ninth seed Jeļena Ostapenko and Danielle Collins in straight sets to reach the final. In the final, she faced again Iga Świątek, and lost in straight sets.

At the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 French Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated [Erika Andreeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erika_Andreeva "Erika Andreeva"), qualifier [Moyuka Uchijima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moyuka_Uchijima "Moyuka Uchijima"), Paula Badosa and Emma Navarro, without dropping a set, to reach the quarterfinals. In the quarterfinals, she was hampered by stomach issues and lost to Mirra Andreeva in three sets. Due to her loss, her ranking dropped to No. 3.

Sabalenka suffered a right shoulder injury that forced her to retire during her quarterfinal match against Anna Kalinskaya at the [Berlin Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Berlin_Ladies_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2024 Berlin Ladies Open – Singles") in June and she withdrew from the [Wimbledon Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles"), having failed to recover in time to play.

Sabalenka returned from injury at the [Washington DC Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Mubadala_Citi_DC_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 Mubadala Citi DC Open – Women's singles"). She defeated qualifier Kamilla Rakhimova and Victoria Azarenka to reach the semifinals, where she lost to Marie Bouzková in three sets. At the [Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 National Bank Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated [Yuan Yue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_Yue "Yuan Yue") and Katie Boulter to reach the quarterfinals, where she lost to Amanda Anisimova in straight sets.

At the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Cincinnati_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 Cincinnati Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated [Elisabetta Cocciaretto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisabetta_Cocciaretto "Elisabetta Cocciaretto"), Elina Svitolina, and Liudmila Samsonova to reach the semifinals. She defeated world No. 1 Iga Świątek in the semifinals, and defeated sixth seed Jessica Pegula to win the title, without dropping a set en route. Her ranking moved back to No. 2.

At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 US Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated qualifier [Priscilla Hon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priscilla_Hon "Priscilla Hon") and Lucia Bronzetti in straight sets. In the third round, she came from a set down to defeat Ekaterina Alexandrova. She defeated Elise Mertens, seventh seed Zheng Qinwen and Emma Navarro, without dropping a set, to reach her second straight US Open final. She defeated sixth seed Jessica Pegula in straight sets to win her first US Open singles title and third major singles title. She became the fifth woman to win the Australian Open and the US Open in the same year since 1988.

Sabalenka continued her great form in China. At the [China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_China_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 China Open – Women's singles"), she defeated qualifier [Mananchaya Sawangkaew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mananchaya_Sawangkaew "Mananchaya Sawangkaew"), [Ashlyn Krueger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashlyn_Krueger "Ashlyn Krueger") and Madison Keys, without dropping a set, to reach the quarterfinals. In the quarterfinals, she lost to Karolína Muchová in three sets. At the [Wuhan Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Wuhan_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2024 Wuhan Open – Singles"), where she won back-to-back in 2018 and 2019, before the tournament was suspended due to COVID-19 from 2020 until 2023, Sabalenka defeated Kateřina Siniaková in straight sets in her opening match. In the third round, she came back from a set down against Yulia Putintseva. In the quarterfinals, she defeated [Magdalena Fręch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalena_Fr%C4%99ch "Magdalena Fręch"). In the semifinals, she mounted a comeback from a set and a break down against fourth seed Coco Gauff. In the final, she defeated fifth seed Zheng Qinwen in three sets to lift her third Wuhan Open title and her second WTA 1000 title for the 2024 season.

After 11 months off the top in October, Sabalenka regained the WTA world number-one ranking. Following wins against Zheng Qinwen and Jasmine Paolini in her first two group matches at the [WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2024 WTA Finals – Singles"), combined with Świątek's loss to Coco Gauff, Sabalenka for the first time would finish as [year-end No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_WTA_number_1_ranked_singles_tennis_players "List of WTA number 1 ranked singles tennis players"). She lost her last group match to Elena Rybakina and was defeated by Coco Gauff in the semifinals.

In December, Sabalenka was named [WTA Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_Awards#Player_of_the_Year "WTA Awards").

### 2025: US Open title, year-end No. 1

Sabalenka started 2025 at the [Brisbane International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Brisbane_International_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2025 Brisbane International – Women's singles"). She defeated [Renata Zarazúa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renata_Zaraz%C3%BAa "Renata Zarazúa"), Yulia Putintseva, Marie Bouzková, and No. 16 Mirra Andreeva, without dropping a set, to reach the final. In the final, she came back from a set down against qualifier [Polina Kudermetova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polina_Kudermetova "Polina Kudermetova") to win the title. As the two-time defending champion at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2025 Australian Open – Women's singles"), Sabalenka defeated Sloane Stephens, [Jéssica Bouzas Maneiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%A9ssica_Bouzas_Maneiro "Jéssica Bouzas Maneiro"), [Clara Tauson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clara_Tauson "Clara Tauson"), and No. 15 Mirra Andreeva, without dropping a set, to reach the quarterfinals. She defeated Anastasia Pavlyuchenkova in three sets to reach the semifinals. She defeated No.12 Paula Badosa in straight sets to advance to her third straight Australian Open final. In the final, she lost to No. 14 Madison Keys in three sets.

On 28 December 2025, Sabalenka (women's singles world No. 1) and [Nick Kyrgios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Kyrgios "Nick Kyrgios") (men's singles world No. 671) contested an exhibition match promoted as the *[Battle of the Sexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Sexes_(tennis) "Battle of the Sexes (tennis)")* at the [Coca-Cola Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca-Cola_Arena "Coca-Cola Arena") in [Dubai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai "Dubai"). The event was organized by their shared management agency, Evolve. The match was played as a three-set contest under modified rules: the dimensions of Sabalenka's side of the court were reduced by 9%, and both players were restricted to a single serve per point. In the event of a tie, a 10-point match tiebreak would have been played. According to Evolve, the court-size adjustment was intended to "reflect average movement-speed differences between men and women". Kyrgios defeated Sabalenka in straight sets, 6–3, 6–3.

## National representation

### Fed Cup

#### Early appearances

Sabalenka represented Belarus at the Junior Fed Cup in 2014, with the team finishing in sixth place. She then made her senior [Fed Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fed_Cup "Fed Cup") debut for [Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus_Fed_Cup_team "Belarus Fed Cup team") in April 2016, losing a [dead rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_rubber "Dead rubber") doubles match against [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_Fed_Cup_team "Russia Fed Cup team"). Nonetheless, the Belarusian team led by [Victoria Azarenka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Azarenka "Victoria Azarenka") and [Aliaksandra Sasnovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliaksandra_Sasnovich "Aliaksandra Sasnovich") won the tie to qualify for the top-tier World Group the following season for the first time in their history.

#### 2017: Surprise runner-up in World Group debut

The Belarus Fed Cup team made their debut in the World Group and ultimately reached the final, despite being the underdogs in all three ties. Little was expected from the team because they were without their veteran leader Azarenka, who missed the first two ties on maternity leave and the last because of a custody battle. Without her, Belarus was led by Sabalenka and Sasnovich, neither of whom had ever been ranked above No. 76 by the time of the final. However, they did have the advantage of playing all of their ties at home in Minsk.

The ties in the quarterfinals against the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Fed_Cup_team "Netherlands Fed Cup team") in February and the semi-finals against [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland_Fed_Cup_team "Switzerland Fed Cup team") in April both played out in the same way. While Sabalenka lost her opening matches to their opponents' respective top-ranked players of [Kiki Bertens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiki_Bertens "Kiki Bertens") and [Timea Bacsinszky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timea_Bacsinszky "Timea Bacsinszky"), Sasnovich was able to give Belarus a 2–1 lead in each instance. Sabalenka then clinched both ties, with wins over [Michaëlla Krajicek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micha%C3%ABlla_Krajicek "Michaëlla Krajicek") and No. 54 [Viktorija Golubic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktorija_Golubic "Viktorija Golubic"), respectively. She was only ranked No. 125 at the time of the semifinal, with no career tour match wins outside of Fed Cup.

> "I've never felt so much emotion in a match. When you play at home and you are down 0–1 and you have to win and you fight with yourself... I just started crying because it was such an important match."

— Sabalenka on her Fed Cup rubber win over Stephens

On the opening day of the final against the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Fed_Cup_team "United States Fed Cup team"), Sabalenka upset the reigning US Open champion and world No. 13, [Sloane Stephens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloane_Stephens "Sloane Stephens"), to level the tie after Sasnovich lost her first rubber to No. 10, [CoCo Vandeweghe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CoCo_Vandeweghe "CoCo Vandeweghe"). The next day began with Sabalenka losing to Vandeweghe, before Sasnovich again levelled the tie by defeating Stephens. Sabalenka and Sasnovich were then selected for the decisive doubles rubber for the Fed Cup crown, but the duo were comprehensively defeated by Vandeweghe and [Shelby Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shelby_Rogers "Shelby Rogers").

Despite finishing as runner-up, Belarus's Fed Cup success helped popularize women's tennis in Belarus, and vaulted Sabalenka and Sasnovich into international prominence. Sasnovich said, "When we played the quarterfinals and semi-finals in Minsk, a lot of people were coming to see our matches. They finally saw tennis in life, and it's like a popularization... I want my country to improve even more in tennis, because I think we can have even more from Belarus."

#### 2018–19: Avoiding demotion, another semifinal

Belarus was unable to repeat their 2017 Fed Cup success in 2018. Their quarterfinal tie was held in Minsk against [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_Fed_Cup_team "Germany Fed Cup team"). Although Sabalenka won both of her singles rubbers, Sasnovich and [Vera Lapko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vera_Lapko "Vera Lapko") lost each of theirs to set up a decisive doubles rubber. Sabalenka and doubles specialist [Lidziya Marozava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lidziya_Marozava "Lidziya Marozava") were selected for the match, with Sabalenka playing on short rest directly after her last singles match. After taking the first set against [Anna-Lena Grönefeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna-Lena_Gr%C3%B6nefeld "Anna-Lena Grönefeld") and [Tatjana Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatjana_Maria "Tatjana Maria"), they ultimately lost the rubber and the tie.

Their next tie was again contested in Minsk as part of the [World Group play-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Fed_Cup_World_Group_play-offs "2018 Fed Cup World Group play-offs"), with [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovakia_Fed_Cup_team "Slovakia Fed Cup team") competing to take Belarus's place in the World Group the following season. Sabalenka and Sasnovich each split their two singles rubbers, with Sabalenka being upset by [Viktória Kužmová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikt%C3%B3ria_Ku%C5%BEmov%C3%A1 "Viktória Kužmová"). Doubles specialists Lapko and Marozava were chosen for the final rubber and the pair won the match to keep Belarus in the World Group for 2019.

In the [2019 Fed Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Fed_Cup "2019 Fed Cup"), Belarus were drawn against Germany in the quarterfinals for the second consecutive year. After Sasnovich won the opening rubber against Maria, Sabalenka won both of her singles rubbers against [Andrea Petkovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Petkovic "Andrea Petkovic") and [Laura Siegemund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Siegemund "Laura Siegemund") to clinch the tie. They advanced to face [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_Fed_Cup_team "Australia Fed Cup team") in the semi-finals. Only two players from each team participated: Sabalenka and Azarenka for Belarus, and Ashleigh Barty and [Samantha Stosur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samantha_Stosur "Samantha Stosur") for Australia. Both Sabalenka and Azarenka defeated Stosur, but lost to Barty. In the decisive doubles rubber, Barty and Stosur won in three sets to eliminate Belarus.

## Rivalries

### Elena Rybakina

Sabalenka and [Elena Rybakina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elena_Rybakina "Elena Rybakina") have played each other 18 times since 2019, with Sabalenka leading their head-to-head 10–8 whereas Rybakina leads the finals 5–2. Both players are known for their aggressive baseline play, resulting in fast-paced and intense matches.

They have faced off in three major finals with the [2023 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Australian_Open_-_Women%27s_singles "2023 Australian Open - Women's singles") won by Sabalenka, and the [2026 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Australian_Open_-_Women%27s_singles "2026 Australian Open - Women's singles") and [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2026 US Open – Women's singles") both won by Rybakina.

Their other notable encounters include the [2023 BNP Paribas Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 BNP Paribas Open – Women's singles") finals, [2024 Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 Mutua Madrid Open – Women's singles") semifinal, [2025 WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 WTA Finals – Singles") finals, the [2026 BNP Paribas Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2026 BNP Paribas Open – Women's singles") final, and the [2026 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_US_Open_(tennis) "2026 US Open (tennis)") final.

### Iga Świątek

Sabalenka and [Iga Świątek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iga_%C5%9Awi%C4%85tek "Iga Świątek") have met 13 times since 2021, with Świątek in control of the head-to-head at 8–5. Their rivalry has been seen as becoming one of the greatest in women's tennis. Their most praised matches include the [2024 Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 Mutua Madrid Open – Women's singles") final, where Świątek defeated defending champion Sabalenka in three sets in 3 hours and 11 minutes, saving three championship points in the process, and the [2023 Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 Mutua Madrid Open – Women's singles") final, where Sabalenka won in three sets in 2 hours and 25 minutes against Świątek to claim her second Madrid title. Sabalenka and Świątek played each other at the [2025 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2025 French Open – Women's singles"), which Sabalenka won in three sets.

### Coco Gauff

Sabalenka and [Coco Gauff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coco_Gauff "Coco Gauff") have met 13 times since 2020, with Sabalenka edging the head-to-head at 7–6. They have played each other multiple times at [majors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis) "Grand Slam (tennis)"), the first being the [2023 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 US Open – Women's singles") final. Sabalenka lost that match despite winning the first set. Sabalenka defeated Gauff at the [2024 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 Australian Open – Women's singles") semifinals on her way to defending the title. Sabalenka and Gauff faced off again in the final of the [2025 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open "2025 French Open"), with Gauff overtaking Sabalenka for her first French Open Major Championship win and second Grand Slam overall. Sabalenka would avenge that loss by knocking Gauff out of the [2025 WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 WTA Finals – Singles") where Gauff was the defending champion. Sabalenka would meet Gauff for the first time in 2026 in the [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2026 Miami Open – Women's singles") finals, where Sabalenka battled through Gauff in three sets to win Miami for the second time, as well as the [Sunshine Double](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunshine_Double "Sunshine Double").

### Jessica Pegula

Sabalenka has developed a fairly storied rivalry with [Jessica Pegula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pegula "Jessica Pegula"). Their consistency on hard courts leads to them frequently facing each other in the latter stages of events. The two have met 14 times since 2020, with Sabalenka controlling the head-to-head 10–4. Pegula would win their first meeting in the third round of the [2020 Western & Southern Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Western_%26_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2020 Western & Southern Open – Women's singles") in three sets. They would face off at the [2024 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 US Open – Women's singles") final in which Sabalenka would win 7–5, 7–5 to earn her first US Open title. The two would have a rematch of their US Open final in the [2025 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2025 US Open – Women's singles") semifinals, where Sabalenka would win a close three-set match 4–6, 6–3, 6–4 on her way to defending her US Open title. Pegula would snap Sabalenka's 20-match winning streak as well as her 2025 tiebreak record in their following match in [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Wuhan_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 Wuhan Open – Singles"), before pulling out a three-set win 2–6, 6–4, 7–6(2). They faced each other once more in the Riyadh [2025 WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 WTA Finals – Singles"), where Sabalenka defeated Pegula in three sets. The pair's first meeting on grass came at the [2026 Berlin Tennis Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Berlin_Tennis_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2026 Berlin Tennis Open – Singles"), which Pegula won in three sets, bageling Sabalenka in the final set.

### Jasmine Paolini

Sabalenka has developed a recurring rivalry with [Jasmine Paolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasmine_Paolini "Jasmine Paolini"), which she leads 6–2. The pair first met at the [2017 Ilkley Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Aegon_Ilkley_Trophy_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2017 Aegon Ilkley Trophy – Women's singles"), where Paolini defeated Sabalenka in three sets. Paolini later earned another notable win at the [2022 Indian Wells Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Indian_Wells_Open "2022 Indian Wells Open"), defeating Sabalenka 2–6, 6–3, 6–3. Sabalenka has otherwise controlled the matchup, winning their later meetings at events including [Beijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Open_(tennis) "China Open (tennis)"), the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Open_(tennis) "Miami Open (tennis)"), the [Stuttgart Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix "Porsche Tennis Grand Prix"), and the [WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_Finals "WTA Finals"). Their matches have contrasted Sabalenka's power-based baseline game with Paolini's speed, court coverage, and ability to extend rallies.

### Amanda Anisimova

Sabalenka has also developed a rivalry with [Amanda Anisimova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Anisimova "Amanda Anisimova"). The pair have played 11 times with Sabalenka trailing 5–6. They've met six times in Grand Slams including 3 consecutive major contests in 2025 where they're tied 3–3. Anisimova defeated Sabalenka en route to her first major semifinal at [2019 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2019 French Open – Women's singles") and also in the semifinal of [2025 Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2025 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles") to reach her first major final.
They met in the final of [2025 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2025 US Open – Women's singles") where Sabalenka won 6–3, 7–6(7–3) to win her fourth major. In 2025, they met again in the semifinal to [WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 WTA Finals – Singles") won by Sabalenka.

## Playing style

Sabalenka is an [aggressive baseliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aggressive_baseliner "Aggressive baseliner") whose game is characterised by a powerful [serve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serve_(tennis) "Serve (tennis)") and equally powerful [groundstrokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundstroke "Groundstroke"). Her game is based around taking control of points, with a knack for turning defensive positions into offensive opportunities. Due to her aggression, she usually hits a high number of both winners and unforced errors off her groundstrokes, which are hit with relentless pace and depth. At the [2024 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 US Open – Women's singles"), she struck her forehand at an average speed of 129 km/h (80 mph). Sabalenka's big serve is central to her game: in 2024 she won 62.7% of points on her serve, ranking fifth among the players on tour, and won 78.5% of her service games.

Sabalenka's improved consistency on serve was the key which allowed her to ascend to the top of the WTA rankings. In the past, she struggled with an inconsistent serve, leading to a high double fault count; she served 166 double faults in 2020, the most of any player. She notably suffered from the [yips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yips "Yips") from the [2021 WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_WTA_Finals "2021 WTA Finals") and into 2022, serving 152 double faults in 11 matches, an average of 14 double faults a match. Following the [2022 Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Canadian_Open_(tennis) "2022 Canadian Open (tennis)"), she hired biomechanics expert Gavin Macmillan to revamp her serve. Sabalenka served a total of 440 double faults in 2022, but her serve quickly saw improvements in 2023 under Macmillan's guidance, and has since become a reliable weapon.

As Sabalenka's game has matured, she has also developed better shot selection and more variety. When she first arrived on tour, she was sometimes described as a player who hit everything as hard as she could. In the summer of 2018, her coach [Dmitry Tursunov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Tursunov "Dmitry Tursunov") said her game was already evolving, and, "The major thing is she stopped trying [to] hit a winner with every shot." Sabalenka has become increasingly willing to experiment with variety on her groundstrokes, and with finishing more points at the net. During the 2024 clay season, she began to incorporate more [drop shots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drop_shot "Drop shot") into her game, which pair well with her power from the baseline.

Sabalenka is primarily known for her success on [hardcourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcourt "Hardcourt"). Nineteen of her twenty-two singles titles, including all four of her major titles, have been won on hardcourt, which suits her game due to its consistent bounce and fast-paced play. However, she is also recognised as a consistent contender in the late stages of [grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grass_court "Grass court") and [clay court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_court "Clay court") tournaments. She has been particularly successful on faster clay courts, such as the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_Open_(tennis) "Madrid Open (tennis)"), where she won the title in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2021 Mutua Madrid Open – Women's singles"), [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 Mutua Madrid Open – Women's singles"), and [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2025 Mutua Madrid Open – Women's singles"). She also reached her first major final on clay at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2025 French Open – Women's singles") in 2025.

Sabalenka has become increasingly notable for her ability to win tiebreaks. In 2025, she set an [Open Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Era "Open Era") record for most tiebreaks won by a woman in a single season, winning 22 out of 25 played. This included a streak of nineteen consecutive tiebreaks won between February and October. In 2026, Sabalenka won her twentieth consecutive tiebreak. While Sabalenka is known for her clutch factor in tiebreaks, she has also lost a large number of closely-contested prominent matches, such as the [2025 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2025 French Open – Women's singles") final and [2026 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2026 Australian Open – Women's singles") final, and holds a 22–20 (52%) record in singles finals overall.

Sabalenka frequently accompanies her shots with loud [grunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grunting_in_tennis "Grunting in tennis"). She said, "Honestly, I don't even hear myself when I am playing." She expressed her hope that her grunting does not disturb her opponents. At the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Open "Australian Open") in 2018, the crowd mocked her habit in a match against Ashleigh Barty. She is an expressive personality on court.

## Coaches

Sabalenka had worked with Khalil Ibrahimov for two years up until early 2018. At this point, she began working with Swedish former professional tennis players [Magnus Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnus_Norman "Magnus Norman") and [Magnus Tideman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnus_Tideman "Magnus Tideman"). [Dmitry Tursunov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Tursunov "Dmitry Tursunov") became her primary coach in time for the grass court season in 2018. Sabalenka briefly split with Tursunov after the 2019 US Open. Although they reunited later in the year, she made the split permanent at the end of the season. Sabalenka briefly worked with [Dieter Kindlmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieter_Kindlmann "Dieter Kindlmann") before switching coaches to her longtime hitting partner and compatriot [Anton Dubrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Dubrov "Anton Dubrov"), aged 25 at the time. In August 2022, Sabalenka hired biomechanics specialist Gavin MacMillan to improve on her serve. In 2025, Belarusian former professional tennis player and 10-time [major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis)#Tournaments "Grand Slam (tennis)") doubles champion [Max Mirnyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Mirnyi "Max Mirnyi") joined Sabalenka's coaching team as a consultant.

As of 2025[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Aryna_Sabalenka&action=edit), her coaching team includes Dubrov; Mirnyi; fitness trainer Jason Stacy, a martial arts expert; and hitting partner [Andrei Vasilevski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Vasilevski_(tennis) "Andrei Vasilevski (tennis)").

## Personal life

Sabalenka has a tiger tattoo on her left arm that has earned her the nickname "The Tiger", which she has used to refer to herself. She studied at the [Belarusian State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_State_University "Belarusian State University") in a sports-related program. Her tennis idols growing up were [Serena Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serena_Williams "Serena Williams") and [Maria Sharapova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Sharapova "Maria Sharapova"). Her father Sergey, a former ice hockey player, died suddenly in 2019 at age 43. He had [meningitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meningitis "Meningitis").

Sabalenka's ex-boyfriend, [Konstantin Koltsov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Koltsov "Konstantin Koltsov"), died of an apparent suicide in March 2024 at age 42. She became engaged to Brazilian entrepreneur Georgios Frangulis on March 3, 2026.

### Political views

In August 2020, during the [2020–2021 Belarusian protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%932021_Belarusian_protests "2020–2021 Belarusian protests"), Sabalenka criticised the government of [Alexander Lukashenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Lukashenko "Alexander Lukashenko") for its dispersal of peaceful protests and called for no violence, but was criticised by members of the [Belarusian opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_opposition "Belarusian opposition") for not using her platform to advocate the protest movement's goals. She separately praised the Belarusian government for "everything that is done for the country in general and for sports in particular". In 2020, during widespread protests after the disputed election, Sabalenka signed an open letter that said that sport should remain outside of politics. Belarusian media considered the letter supportive of Lukashenko, as it followed an open letter by Belarusian sporting figures demanding the election be invalidated.

Following the start of the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine "Russian invasion of Ukraine"), Sabalenka expressed support for the Ukrainian people and the use of Ukraine-coloured ribbons: "I feel like people need our support. I just hope they understand that we're all really concerned. I think even 'sad' isn't even the right word." In January 2023, with regard to the war and the banning of Russian and Belarusian players from the [2022 Wimbledon Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Wimbledon_Championships "2022 Wimbledon Championships"), Sabalenka said, "I just understand that it's not my fault." Her comments on the Russian invasion of Ukraine and the situation of Ukrainian tennis players were criticised by [Lesia Tsurenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesia_Tsurenko "Lesia Tsurenko")'s coach Nikita Vlasov and former Ukrainian player [Sergiy Stakhovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergiy_Stakhovsky "Sergiy Stakhovsky") for lacking substance and compassion. Sabalenka later indicated support for ending the war in Ukraine, saying: "If I could stop the war I would do it but unfortunately that doesn't lie in my hands. I just hope for peace."

During a press conference at the [2023 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_French_Open "2023 French Open"), when asked about the war by Ukrainian journalist [Daria Meshcheriakova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daria_Meshcheriakova "Daria Meshcheriakova"), she said: "Nobody in this world, Russian athletes or Belarusian athletes, support the war. Nobody. How can we support the war? Nobody, normal people will never support it." She later said: "I don't want my country to be in any conflict, I don't support war, meaning I don't support Lukashenko right now."

### Comments on transgender athletes

In December 2025, Sabalenka stated in an interview with [Piers Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piers_Morgan "Piers Morgan") that she opposed the participation of [transgender women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender_women "Transgender women") in women's tennis. While saying she had "nothing against" transgender people, she argued that transgender women retained a biological advantage over female players and that it was unfair for women to compete against "biological men." She also said that female athletes train throughout their lives to reach the highest level and should not have to compete against opponents whom she believed possessed inherent physical advantages.

In August 2026, Sabalenka reiterated her views on transgender athletes saying "it would be not really fair for the woman to compete against biological man" and voiced support for the WTA's new gender-testing policy that bans trans women from participating in the WTA Tour.
