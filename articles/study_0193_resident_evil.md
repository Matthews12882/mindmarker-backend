# Resident Evil

***Resident Evil***, also known as ***Biohazard*** (バイオハザード, *Baiohazādo*) in Japan, is a Japanese [horror game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horror_game "Horror game") series and [media franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_franchise "Media franchise") created by [Capcom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capcom "Capcom"). It consists of [survival horror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survival_horror "Survival horror"), [third-person shooter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third-person_shooter "Third-person shooter"), and [first-person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-person_(video_games) "First-person (video games)") games, with players typically surviving in environments inhabited by [zombies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zombies_in_Resident_Evil "Zombies in Resident Evil") and other mutated creatures. The franchise has expanded into media including a [live-action film series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_(film_series) "Resident Evil (film series)"), animated films, television series, [comic books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic_book "Comic book"), [novels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novel "Novel"), audio dramas, and merchandise. With over 213 million video game copies sold worldwide as of June 2026[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Resident_Evil&action=edit), *Resident Evil* is Capcom's best-selling franchise, the tenth [best-selling video game franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_best-selling_video_game_franchises "List of best-selling video game franchises"), the best‑selling horror game franchise, and one of the [highest-grossing horror media franchises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_media_franchises "List of highest-grossing media franchises").

The first *[Resident Evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_(1996_video_game) "Resident Evil (1996 video game)")* game was created by [Shinji Mikami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinji_Mikami "Shinji Mikami") and [Tokuro Fujiwara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokuro_Fujiwara "Tokuro Fujiwara") for [PlayStation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_(console) "PlayStation (console)"), and was released in 1996. It is credited with defining the survival horror genre and returning zombies to popular culture. With *[Resident Evil 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_4 "Resident Evil 4")* (2005), the franchise shifted to more dynamic shooting action, popularizing the now-ubiquitous "over-the-shoulder" [third-person view](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third-person_(video_games) "Third-person (video games)") in action-adventure games. The franchise returned to survival horror with *[Resident Evil 7: Biohazard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_7:_Biohazard "Resident Evil 7: Biohazard")* (2017) and *[Resident Evil Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_Village "Resident Evil Village")* (2021), which both used a [first-person perspective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-person_(video_games) "First-person (video games)"). Capcom has also released four *Resident Evil* [remakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_remake "Video game remake"): *[Resident Evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_(2002_video_game) "Resident Evil (2002 video game)")* (2002), *[Resident Evil 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_2_(2019_video_game) "Resident Evil 2 (2019 video game)")* (2019), *[Resident Evil 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_3_(2020_video_game) "Resident Evil 3 (2020 video game)")* (2020) and *[Resident Evil 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_4_(2023_video_game) "Resident Evil 4 (2023 video game)")* (2023), with a [remake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_Veronica "Resident Evil Veronica") of *[Resident Evil – Code: Veronica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_%E2%80%93_Code:_Veronica "Resident Evil – Code: Veronica")* (2000) releasing in 2027. The ninth and most recent main installment, *[Resident Evil Requiem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_Requiem "Resident Evil Requiem")*, was released in 2026.

A live-action film adaptation, similarly titled *[Resident Evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_(film_series) "Resident Evil (film series)")* was released in 2002, written and directed by [Paul W. S. Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_W._S._Anderson "Paul W. S. Anderson"). It spawned a [franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_(film_series) "Resident Evil (film series)") comprising five sequels and two reboots, *[Welcome to Raccoon City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welcome_to_Raccoon_City "Welcome to Raccoon City")* (2021) directed by [Johannes Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Roberts "Johannes Roberts") and *[Resident Evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_(2026_film) "Resident Evil (2026 film)")* (2026) written and directed by [Zach Cregger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zach_Cregger "Zach Cregger"). [A television series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_(TV_series) "Resident Evil (TV series)") was developed by [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") and aired for one season in 2022. The first seven films received negative reviews, while the 2026 film garnered critical acclaim and became the highest-rated [film based on a video game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_films_based_on_video_games "List of films based on video games") on both [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes") and [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"). The films have altogether grossed more than $1.2 billion, making *Resident Evil* the third [highest-grossing video game film series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_films_based_on_video_games "List of highest-grossing films based on video games") and the third [highest-grossing horror film series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_horror_films "List of highest-grossing horror films").

The development of the first *[Resident Evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_(1996_video_game) "Resident Evil (1996 video game)")*, released as *Biohazard* in Japan, began in 1993 when [Capcom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capcom "Capcom")'s [Tokuro Fujiwara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokuro_Fujiwara "Tokuro Fujiwara") told [Shinji Mikami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinji_Mikami "Shinji Mikami") and other co-workers to create a game using elements from Fujiwara's 1989 game *[Sweet Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_Home_(video_game) "Sweet Home (video game)")* on the [Family Computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Computer "Family Computer") (Famicom) in Japan. When in late 1994 marketing executives were setting up to release *Biohazard* in the United States, it was pointed out that securing the rights to the name *Biohazard* would be very difficult as a DOS game had been [registered under that name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bio_Menace#Development "Bio Menace"), as well as a New York hardcore punk band called [Biohazard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biohazard_(band) "Biohazard (band)"). A contest was held among company personnel to choose a new name; this competition turned up *Resident Evil*, the name under which it was released in the west. *Resident Evil* made its debut on the [PlayStation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_(console) "PlayStation (console)") in 1996 and was ported to the [Sega Saturn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sega_Saturn "Sega Saturn").

The first entry in the series was the first game to be dubbed a "[survival horror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survival_horror "Survival horror")", a term coined for the new genre it initiated, and its critical and commercial success led to the production of two sequels, *[Resident Evil 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_2 "Resident Evil 2")* in 1998 and *[Resident Evil 3: Nemesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_3:_Nemesis "Resident Evil 3: Nemesis")* in 1999, both for the PlayStation. A port of *Resident Evil 2* was released for the [Nintendo 64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_64 "Nintendo 64"). In addition, ports of all three were released for [Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows "Windows"). The next big game in the series, *[Resident Evil – Code: Veronica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_%E2%80%93_Code:_Veronica "Resident Evil – Code: Veronica")*, was developed for the [Dreamcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreamcast "Dreamcast") and released in 2000, followed by ports of *Resident Evil 2* and *Resident Evil 3: Nemesis*. *Resident Evil – Code: Veronica* was rereleased for Dreamcast in Japan in an updated form as *Code: Veronica Complete*, which included slight changes, many of which revolved around story cutscenes. This updated version was ported to the [PlayStation 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_2 "PlayStation 2") and [GameCube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GameCube "GameCube") as *Code: Veronica X*.

Despite earlier announcements that the next game in the series would be released for the PlayStation 2, which resulted in the creation of an unrelated game, *[Devil May Cry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devil_May_Cry "Devil May Cry")*, Mikami decided to make the series exclusively for the GameCube. The next three games in the series—a [remake of the original *Resident Evil*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_(2002_video_game) "Resident Evil (2002 video game)") and the prequel *[Resident Evil Zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_Zero "Resident Evil Zero")*, both released in 2002, as well as *[Resident Evil 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_4 "Resident Evil 4")* (2005)—were all released initially for GameCube. *Resident Evil 4* was later released for Windows, PlayStation 2, and [Wii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wii "Wii").

A trilogy of [GunCon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GunCon "GunCon")-compatible [light gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_gun "Light gun") games, the *Gun Survivor* series, featured [first-person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_person_(video_games) "First person (video games)") gameplay. The first, *[Resident Evil Survivor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_Survivor "Resident Evil Survivor")*, was released in 2000 for the PlayStation and PC but received mediocre reviews. The subsequent games, *[Resident Evil Survivor 2 – Code: Veronica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_Survivor_2_%E2%80%93_Code:_Veronica "Resident Evil Survivor 2 – Code: Veronica")* and *[Resident Evil: Dead Aim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil:_Dead_Aim "Resident Evil: Dead Aim")*, fared somewhat better. *Dead Aim* is the fourth *Gun Survivor* game in Japan, with *Gun Survivor 3* being the *[Dino Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dino_Crisis "Dino Crisis")* spin-off *[Dino Stalker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dino_Stalker "Dino Stalker")*. In a similar vein, the *Chronicles* series features first-person gameplay, albeit on an on-rails path. *[Resident Evil: The Umbrella Chronicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil:_The_Umbrella_Chronicles "Resident Evil: The Umbrella Chronicles")* was released in 2007 for the Wii, with a sequel, *[Resident Evil: The Darkside Chronicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil:_The_Darkside_Chronicles "Resident Evil: The Darkside Chronicles")*, released in 2009 (both were ported to the PlayStation 3 in 2012).

*[Resident Evil Outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_Outbreak "Resident Evil Outbreak")* is an [online game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_game "Online game") for the PlayStation 2, released in 2003, depicting a series of episodic storylines in Raccoon City set during the same period as *Resident Evil 2* and *Resident Evil 3: Nemesis*. It was the first in the series and the first survival horror game to feature [cooperative gameplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative_video_game "Cooperative video game"). It was followed by a sequel, *[Resident Evil Outbreak: File #2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_Outbreak:_File_2 "Resident Evil Outbreak: File 2")*. Raccoon City is a metropolis located in the Arklay Mountains of the Midwestern United States that succumbed to the deadly T-virus outbreak and was consequently destroyed via a nuclear missile attack issued by the United States government. The town served as a critical junction for the series' progression as one of the main catalysts to [Umbrella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbrella_(Resident_Evil) "Umbrella (Resident Evil)")'s downfall and the entry point for some of the series' most notable characters.

*[Resident Evil Gaiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_Gaiden "Resident Evil Gaiden")* is an [action-adventure game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action-adventure_game "Action-adventure game") for the [Game Boy Color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Boy_Color "Game Boy Color") featuring a [role-playing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Role-playing_video_game "Role-playing video game")-style combat system. There have been several downloadable [mobile games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_games_in_the_Resident_Evil_series "Mobile games in the Resident Evil series") based on the *Resident Evil* series in Japan. Some of these mobile games have been released in North America and Europe through T-Mobile. At the [Sony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Computer_Entertainment "Sony Computer Entertainment") press conference during [E3 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E3_2009 "E3 2009"), *Resident Evil Portable* was announced for the [PlayStation Portable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Portable "PlayStation Portable"), described as a new game being developed with "the [PSP Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSP_Go "PSP Go") in mind" and "totally different for a *Resident Evil* game". No further announcements have been made, and the game is considered to have been canceled.

In 2009, *[Resident Evil 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_5 "Resident Evil 5")* was released for [PlayStation 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_3 "PlayStation 3"), [Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows "Windows") and [Xbox 360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_360 "Xbox 360"), becoming the best selling game of the franchise despite mixed fan reception. Capcom revealed the [third-person shooter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third-person_shooter "Third-person shooter") *[Resident Evil: Operation Raccoon City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil:_Operation_Raccoon_City "Resident Evil: Operation Raccoon City")*, which was developed by [Slant Six Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slant_Six_Games "Slant Six Games") for the PlayStation 3, Xbox 360 and Windows and released in March 2012. A [survival horror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survival_horror "Survival horror") game for the [Nintendo 3DS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_3DS "Nintendo 3DS"), *[Resident Evil: Revelations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil:_Revelations "Resident Evil: Revelations")*, was released in February 2012. In October of the same year, the next numbered entry in the main series, *[Resident Evil 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_6 "Resident Evil 6")*, was released to mixed reviews, but enthusiastic pre-order sales.

In 2013, producer Masachika Kawata said the *Resident Evil* franchise would return to focus on elements of horror and suspense over action, adding that "survival horror as a genre is never going to be on the same level, financially, as shooters and much more popular, mainstream games. At the same time, I think we need to have the confidence to put money behind these projects, and it doesn't mean we can't focus on what we need to do as a survival horror game to meet fan's needs." *[Resident Evil: Revelations 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil:_Revelations_2 "Resident Evil: Revelations 2")*, an episodic game set between *Resident Evil 5* and *Resident Evil 6*, was released in March 2015. A series of team-based multiplayer games were developed beginning with the [poorly received](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_video_games_notable_for_negative_reception "List of video games notable for negative reception") *[Umbrella Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbrella_Corps "Umbrella Corps")*, which was released in June 2016. *[Resident Evil: Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil:_Resistance "Resident Evil: Resistance")* was released in April 2020, followed by *Resident Evil Re:Verse* in October 2022, with both available for free to those who bought *Resident Evil 3* and *Village* respectively.

Built on the newly developed [RE Engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RE_Engine "RE Engine"), the series continued its shift back towards more horror elements. The next main installment, *[Resident Evil 7: Biohazard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_7:_Biohazard "Resident Evil 7: Biohazard")*, was released for [Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows "Windows"), [PlayStation 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4 "PlayStation 4") and [Xbox One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_One "Xbox One") in January 2017. Set in a dilapidated mansion in [Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana "Louisiana"), the game uses a [first-person perspective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-person_narrative "First-person narrative") and emphasizes horror and exploration over action, unlike previous installments. The first-person perspective continued in the eighth main installment, *[Resident Evil Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_Village "Resident Evil Village").* Released in May 2021, the game, set in a mysterious European village, is a direct sequel to *Resident Evil 7: Biohazard* although it incorporates more action elements inspired from *Resident Evil 4*. The game also marked the franchise's debut on [PlayStation 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_5 "PlayStation 5") and [Xbox Series X/S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_Series_X_and_Series_S "Xbox Series X and Series S"). The ninth main installment, *[Resident Evil Requiem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_Requiem "Resident Evil Requiem")*, was released in February 2026.

A series of remakes of older entries began in 2019 with a [remake of *Resident Evil 2*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_2_(2019_video_game) "Resident Evil 2 (2019 video game)") being released for the PlayStation 4, Windows, and Xbox One. The remake outsold the original game within a year, selling over five million copies. Capcom revealed a remake of *Resident Evil 3: Nemesis* in December 2019, known as *[Resident Evil 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_3_(2020_video_game) "Resident Evil 3 (2020 video game)")*. It was released in April 2020. A [remake of *Resident Evil 4*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_4_(2023_video_game) "Resident Evil 4 (2023 video game)") was released on March 24, 2023, for PlayStation 4, PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S and Windows. A remake of *Code: Veronica*, *[Resident Evil Veronica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_Veronica "Resident Evil Veronica")*, is set for release in 2027. *Resident Evil: Survival Unit*, a strategy game by [Aniplex Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aniplex "Aniplex") and Joycity Corporation, was released for [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS "IOS") and [Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_(operating_system) "Android (operating system)") in November 2025.

## Story overview

The early *Resident Evil* games focused on the [Umbrella Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbrella_(Resident_Evil) "Umbrella (Resident Evil)"), an international pharmaceutical company that secretly develops mutagenic viruses to further their "bio-organic weapons" (BOW) research. The company's viruses can transform humans into mindless zombies while also mutating plants and animals into horrifying monstrosities. The Umbrella Corporation uses its vast resources to effectively control Raccoon City, a fictional [midwestern American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States "Midwestern United States") city. In the original *[Resident Evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_(1996_video_game) "Resident Evil (1996 video game)")*, members of an elite police task force, Special Tactics and Rescue Service (STARS), are lured to a derelict mansion on the outskirts of Raccoon City. The STARS team is mostly decimated by zombies and other BOWs, leaving only a handful of survivors, including [Chris Redfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Redfield "Chris Redfield"), [Jill Valentine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jill_Valentine "Jill Valentine"), and [Albert Wesker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Wesker "Albert Wesker"). Chris and Jill explore the zombie-infested mansion and uncover a secret underground Umbrella research facility. Wesker reveals himself to be a double agent for Umbrella and betrays his comrades. However, Wesker is seemingly murdered by a Tyrant, a special BOW that is the culmination of the Umbrella Corporation's research.

Chris and Jill escape the mansion, but Raccoon City's officials ridicule their testimony due to Umbrella's influence. Meanwhile, a separate viral outbreak occurs in another Umbrella research facility underneath Raccoon City. Most of the city's residents are infected and become zombies. *[Resident Evil 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_2 "Resident Evil 2")* introduces two new protagonists, [Leon S. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_S._Kennedy "Leon S. Kennedy"), a rookie police officer and [Claire Redfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claire_Redfield "Claire Redfield"), the younger sister of Chris. Leon and Claire arrive in Raccoon City amidst the chaos of the viral outbreak. Leon is aided by [Ada Wong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada_Wong "Ada Wong"), a spy posing as an [FBI agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Investigation "Federal Bureau of Investigation"), while Claire rescues [Sherry Birkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherry_Birkin "Sherry Birkin"), the daughter of two prominent Umbrella researchers. At the same time, Jill makes her escape from the city in *[Resident Evil 3: Nemesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_3:_Nemesis "Resident Evil 3: Nemesis")*. A new Tyrant, [Nemesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemesis_(Resident_Evil) "Nemesis (Resident Evil)"), who Umbrella deploys to eliminate all surviving STARS members, relentlessly pursues her. The U.S. Government destroys Raccoon City with a nuclear missile strike to sterilize the viral outbreak. Leon, Claire, Sherry, Ada, and Jill escape the city before its eradication. Claire continues to look for Chris, whereas Leon is recruited to work for the U.S. Government. *[Resident Evil – Code: Veronica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_%E2%80%93_Code:_Veronica "Resident Evil – Code: Veronica")* follows Claire as she escapes from a prison camp in the [Southern Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Ocean "Southern Ocean") and reunites with Chris at an Umbrella research facility in Antarctica, while Wesker is revealed to be alive. *[Resident Evil 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_4 "Resident Evil 4")*, set six years after the Raccoon City incident, focuses on Leon as he tries to rescue the [U.S. President's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States "President of the United States") daughter from a cult in Spain.

A government investigation into the Umbrella Corporation reveals its involvement in the Raccoon City disaster and leads to the company's dissolution. Despite the downfall of the Umbrella Corporation, its research and BOWs continue to proliferate across the black market, fueling the rise of bioterrorism. Chris and Jill establish the Bioterrorism Security Assessment Alliance (BSAA) to combat these ever-growing threats on a global scale. Wesker is involved in the development of new potent viral agents and BOWs. In *[Resident Evil 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_5 "Resident Evil 5")*, Wesker seeks to unleash a highly mutagenic virus that will infect all of humanity. Chris and the BSAA confront and kill Wesker in [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa "Africa") before he can fulfill his mission. *[Resident Evil 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_6 "Resident Evil 6")* features Leon and Chris meeting for the first time in the video game series. The two work separately to triage bioterrorist attacks in the United States, [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe "Eastern Europe"), and [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China"). They are assisted by Sherry, Wesker's illegitimate son Jake Muller, Ada, and members of the BSAA and the U.S. government.

*[Resident Evil 7: Biohazard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_7:_Biohazard "Resident Evil 7: Biohazard")* and *[Resident Evil Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_Village "Resident Evil Village")* feature a new protagonist, [Ethan Winters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethan_Winters "Ethan Winters"), who becomes entangled in a bioterrorism incident while searching for his missing wife. He encounters Chris and the BSAA, who help him rescue his wife and defeat Eveline, a powerful BOW. Ethan, Mia, and their newborn daughter, Rosemary, are relocated to Eastern Europe but are abducted by a cult. Ethan ultimately sacrifices himself to destroy a fungal colony being weaponized by bioterrorists and save his family. *[Resident Evil Requiem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_Requiem "Resident Evil Requiem")* introduces a new protagonist, [Grace Ashcroft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Ashcroft "Grace Ashcroft"), whose investigation into a syndrome affecting survivors of the Raccoon City incident leads her into the city's ruins to uncover a new conspiracy, aided by Leon and Sherry.

## Gameplay

The *Resident Evil* franchise has had a variety of control schemes and gameplay mechanics throughout its history. Puzzle-solving has figured prominently throughout the series.

### Tank controls

Early *Resident Evil* games use "tank controls", whereby pressing up on the [game controller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_controller "Game controller") moves the character in the direction they face, down reverses them, and left and right rotates them. This differs from many games, in which characters move in the direction players push from the perspective of the [camera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_camera_system "Virtual camera system"). Some critics have posited that the control scheme is intentionally clumsy, meant to enhance stress and exacerbate difficulty. Mikami felt the use of fixed camera perspectives and tank controls made the game scarier.

*Resident Evil 3: Nemesis* saw some action-oriented additions. These included a 180 degree turn and dodge command that, according to *[GameSpot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GameSpot "GameSpot")*, "hinted at a new direction that the series would go in". Later games in the series, like *[Resident Evil 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_4 "Resident Evil 4")*, introduced a more fluid over-the-shoulder third-person camera instead of a fixed camera for each room, while *[Resident Evil 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_7 "Resident Evil 7")* and *[Resident Evil Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_Village "Resident Evil Village")* are played from the [first-person perspective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-person_(video_games) "First-person (video games)").

### Third-person shooter gameplay

*Resident Evil 4* saw significant changes to the established gameplay, including switching from [fixed camera perspectives to a tracking camera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third-person_(video_games) "Third-person (video games)"), and more action-oriented gameplay and mechanics. This was complemented by an abundance of ammunition and revised aiming and melee mechanics. Some critics claimed that this overhauled control scheme "made the game less scary." The next two games in the franchise furthered the action-oriented mechanics: *Resident Evil 5* featured cooperative play and added strafing, while *Resident Evil 6* allowed players to move while aiming and shooting for the first time, fully abandoning the series' signature tank controls.

### First-person shooter gameplay and VR

*Resident Evil 7* is the first main *Resident Evil* game to use the first-person perspective and to use [virtual reality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_reality "Virtual reality"). It drew comparisons to modern survival horror games such as *[Outlast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlast "Outlast")* and *[PT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P.T._(video_game) "P.T. (video game)")*. The eighth main installment, *[Resident Evil Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_Village "Resident Evil Village")*, also features a first-person perspective and included a VR mode added post launch. A VR version of *[Resident Evil 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_4 "Resident Evil 4")* was released on the [Oculus Quest 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oculus_Quest_2 "Oculus Quest 2") on October 21, 2021. On December 8, 2023 a free DLC for the [remake of *Resident Evil 4*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_4_(2023_video_game) "Resident Evil 4 (2023 video game)") was released that added a VR mode using [PlayStation VR2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_VR2 "PlayStation VR2") to the [PlayStation 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_5 "PlayStation 5") version of the game.

## Other media

The *Resident Evil* franchise features video games and tie-in merchandise and products, including various [live-action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live-action "Live-action") and animated films, comic books, and novels.

### Films

#### Live-action films

From 2002 to 2016, six live-action *Resident Evil* films were produced, all written and produced by [Paul W. S. Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_W._S._Anderson "Paul W. S. Anderson"). The films do not follow the games' premise but feature some game characters. The series' protagonist is [Alice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_(Resident_Evil) "Alice (Resident Evil)"), an original character created for the films portrayed by [Milla Jovovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milla_Jovovich "Milla Jovovich"). Despite a negative reaction from critics, the live-action film series has made over $1 billion worldwide. They are, to date, the only video game adaptations to increase the amount of money made with each successive film. The series holds the record for the "Most Live-Action Film Adaptations of a Video Game" in the 2012 *[Guinness World Records Gamer's Edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Records_Gamer%27s_Edition "Guinness World Records Gamer's Edition")*, which also described it as "the most successful movie series to be based on a video game."

A reboot, *[Resident Evil: Welcome to Raccoon City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil:_Welcome_to_Raccoon_City "Resident Evil: Welcome to Raccoon City"),* was released on November 24, 2021, with [Johannes Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Roberts "Johannes Roberts") as writer and director. In January 2025, it was reported that [Zach Cregger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zach_Cregger "Zach Cregger") was writing and directing a film reboot, *[Resident Evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_(2026_film) "Resident Evil (2026 film)"),* co-produced by [Constantin Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantin_Film "Constantin Film") and [PlayStation Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Productions "PlayStation Productions"). In March 2025, [Sony Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Pictures "Sony Pictures") won a bidding war to secure the rights to the *Resident Evil* franchise, including its upcoming untitled project, with [Columbia Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Pictures "Columbia Pictures") serving as the film's new distributor. Sony has set a release date of September 18, 2026, for the project. In June 2025, it was reported that the project had registered to film in the [Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Republic "Czech Republic"). On August 11, 2025, [Austin Abrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Abrams "Austin Abrams") was confirmed to star in the film. Filming is scheduled to begin in Prague in November 2025. Cregger said that he had not seen the previous *Resident Evil* films and that his film will be more closely based on the video games, particularly *[Resident Evil 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_2 "Resident Evil 2")*, *[Resident Evil 3: Nemesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_3:_Nemesis "Resident Evil 3: Nemesis")*, and *[Resident Evil 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_4 "Resident Evil 4")*.

#### Animated films

The first computer animated film for the franchise was *[Biohazard 4D-Executer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biohazard_4D-Executer "Biohazard 4D-Executer")*. It was a short 3D film produced for Japanese theme parks and did not feature any characters from the game. Starting in 2008, a series of feature-length computer-animated films have been released. These films take place in the same continuity as the games and feature characters such as [Leon Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_S._Kennedy "Leon S. Kennedy"), [Claire Redfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claire_Redfield "Claire Redfield"), [Ada Wong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada_Wong "Ada Wong"), [Chris Redfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Redfield "Chris Redfield"), [Jill Valentine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jill_Valentine "Jill Valentine") and [Rebecca Chambers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_Chambers_(character) "Rebecca Chambers (character)").

*[Resident Evil: Infinite Darkness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil:_Infinite_Darkness "Resident Evil: Infinite Darkness")*, a four-part CG [anime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anime "Anime") series, premiered on July 8, 2021, on [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix"). *Resident Evil Archives* is a reference guide of the *Resident Evil* series written by staff members of Capcom. The second Archives volume was also translated by Capcom and published by BradyGames.

### Novels

The first *Resident Evil* novel was Hiroyuki Ariga's novella *Biohazard: The Beginning*, published in 1997 as a portion of the book *The True Story of Biohazard*, which was given away as a pre-order bonus with the Sega Saturn version of *Biohazard*. The story serves as a prelude to the original *Resident Evil*, in which Chris investigates the disappearance of his missing friend, Billy Rabbitson.

[S. D. Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._D._Perry "S. D. Perry") has written novelizations of the first five games, as well as two original novels taking place between games. The novels often take liberties with the games' plot by exploring events occurring outside and beyond the games. This often meant that the games would later contradict the books on a few occasions. One notable addition from the novels is the original character Trent, who often served as a mysterious behind-the-scenes string-puller who aided the main characters. Perry's novels were translated and released in Japan with new cover arts by Wolfina. Perry's novels, particularly *The Umbrella Conspiracy*, also alluded to events in *Biohazard: The Beginning*, such as the disappearance of Billy Rabbitson and Brian Irons' bid to run for Mayor. A reprinting of Perry's novels with new cover artwork began in 2012 to coincide with the release of *[Resident Evil: Retribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil:_Retribution "Resident Evil: Retribution")* and its respective novelization.

There are a trilogy of original *Biohazard* novels in Japan.  *Hokkai no Yōjū* (北海の妖獣; lit. "The Strange Beast of the North Sea") was published in 1998 and was written by Kyū Asakura and the staff of Flagship. Two additional novels were published in 2002, *To the Liberty* by Sudan Kimura and *Rose Blank* by Tadashi Aizawa. While no official English translation of these novels has been published yet, the last two books were translated into German and published in 2006.

Novelizations of the films *Genesis*, *Apocalypse*, and *Extinction* were written by [Keith DeCandido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_DeCandido "Keith DeCandido"). *Afterlife* did not receive a novelization due to Capcom's decision to discontinue working with [Pocket Books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocket_Books "Pocket Books"), who had been their primary source of publishing books up to that point, Capcom would later make [Titan Books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titan_Books "Titan Books") their primary publisher going forth. *Retribution* was written by [John Shirley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Shirley "John Shirley"), while *The Final Chapter* was written by [Tim Waggoner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Waggoner "Tim Waggoner"). *Genesis* was published over two years after that film's release and coincided with the publication of *Apocalypse*, *Genesis* being marketed as a prequel to *Apocalypse*, while the *Extinction* novel was released in late July 2007, two months before the film's release. *The Final Chapter* was published in December 2016 alongside the film's theatrical release. There was also a Japanese novelization of the first film, unrelated to DeCandido's version, written by Osamu Makino. Makino also wrote two novels based on the game *Resident Evil: The Umbrella Chronicles*. The books are a two-part direct novelization of the game and are published in Japanese and German only. The first novel, titled *Biohazard: The Umbrella Chronicles Side A* in Japan and *Resident Evil: The Umbrella Chronicles 1* in Germany, was released on December 22, 2007. The second novel, titled *Biohazard: The Umbrella Chronicles Side B* in Japan and *Resident Evil: The Umbrella Chronicles 2* in Germany, was published in January 2008.

### Comics

In 1997, [Marvel Comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Comics "Marvel Comics") published a single-issue prologue comic based on the original *Resident Evil*, released through a promotional giveaway alongside the original PlayStation game.

In 1998, [WildStorm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WildStorm "WildStorm") began producing a monthly comic book series based on the first two games, *Resident Evil: The Official Comic Magazine*, which lasted five issues. The first four issues were published by [Image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_Comics "Image Comics"), while Wildstorm themselves published the fifth and final issue. Each issue was a compilation of short stories that were both adaptations of events from the games and related side stories. Like the Perry novels, the comics also explored events occurring beyond *Resident Evil 2* (the latest game during the series' publication) and thus were contradicted by later games. Wildstorm also published a four-issue miniseries, *Resident Evil: Fire & Ice*, which depicted the ordeal of Charlie Team, a third STARS team created specifically for the comic. In 2009, Wildstorm reprinted *Fire & Ice* in a trade paperback collection.

In Hong Kong, there has been officially licensed *Biohazard* [manhua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhua "Manhua") adaptations of *Biohazard 0* by publisher Yulang Group, *Biohazard 2* by Kings Fountain, *Biohazard 3 Supplemental Edition* by Cao Zhihao and, *Biohazard 3 The Last Escape*, and *Biohazard Code: Veronica* by Lee Chung Hing published by Tinhangse Publishing. The Code: Veronica manhua was translated into English, formatted to look like an American comic and distributed by WildStorm as a series of four graphic novel collections.

In 2009, Wildstorm began publishing a *Resident Evil* comic book prequel to *Resident Evil 5*, which centers on two original members of the BSAA, Mina Gere and Holiday Sugarman. Written by Ricardo Sanchez and illustrated by Kevin Sharpe and Jim Clark, the first issue was published on March 11, 2009. On November 11, 2009, the third issue was released, and the fourth was released March 24, 2010. The sixth and final book was finally published in February 2011.

### Plays

In the summer of 2000, *Bioroid: Year Zero* was performed in Japan. It was a musical horror-comedy but took the perspective of the infected. Super Eccentric Theater put on the production under the direction of Osamu Yagihashi. The stage play was performed from early July to late August. *Biohazard The Stage* was released in Japan in 2015. The play focused on Chris Redfield and Rebecca Chambers as Philosophy University in Australia is experiencing a bioterrorist attack. The production was handled by Avex Live Creative and Ace Crew Entertainment, under supervision from Capcom. The following year, *Musical Biohazard ~Voice of Gaia~* was released in September. The story is set in 2015 and follows a cast of thirteen survivors who were abducted and woke up in a mansion during an outbreak.

## Reception and legacy

Most of the games in the prominent *Resident Evil* series have been released to positive reviews. Some of the games, most notably *[Resident Evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_(1996_video_game) "Resident Evil (1996 video game)")*, *[Resident Evil 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_2 "Resident Evil 2")* and *[Resident Evil 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_4 "Resident Evil 4")*, have been bestowed with multiple [Game of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Game_of_the_Year_awards "List of Game of the Year awards") honors and often placed on lists of the [best video games ever made](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_video_games_ever_made "Best video games ever made").

In 1999, *[Next Generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Generation_(magazine) "Next Generation (magazine)")* listed the *Resident Evil* series as number 13 on their "Top 50 Games of All Time", commenting that, "Flawless graphics, excellent music, and a top-notch storyline all combined to make a game of unparalleled atmosphere and suspense." In 2012, *[Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_(magazine) "Complex (magazine)")* ranked *Resident Evil* at number 22 on the list of the best video game franchises. That same year, [G4tv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G4tv "G4tv") called it "one of the most successful series in gaming history."

### Commercial performance

By December 2022, around 135 million *Resident Evil* games had been sold. The first two *Resident Evil* games had collectively sold approximately 11 million units worldwide by March 1999. By early 2001, the series had sold 17 million units worldwide, earning more than $600 million. By 2011, it had sold about 46 million copies and was estimated to have grossed at least $1.3 billion. It is recognized by *[Guinness World Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Records "Guinness World Records")* as the best-selling survival horror series, with *[Resident Evil 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_2_(2019_video_game) "Resident Evil 2 (2019 video game)")* remake being the best-selling survival horror game as of 2023[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Resident_Evil&action=edit). Seven of the top ten best-selling horror games in North America are *Resident Evil* games.

The 2023 *Resident Evil 4* remake sold more than three million copies in its first two days of release. It sold four million copies in its first two weeks, making it one of the fastest-selling *Resident Evil* games. In Japan, it was the best-selling retail game in its first week, selling 89,662 copies on PlayStation 5 and 85,371 on PlayStation 4.

The *Resident Evil* film series was the highest-grossing [film series based on video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_films_based_on_video_games "List of films based on video games") by 2012. By 2011, the films had grossed over $600 million at the box office, bringing the franchise's estimated revenue to at least more than $1.9 billion in combined video game sales and box office gross up until then. As of 2020[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Resident_Evil&action=edit), the films have grossed more than $1.3 billion in box office and home video sales. The success of the video games and films have made *Resident Evil* the [highest-grossing franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_media_franchises "List of highest-grossing media franchises") in the [horror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horror_fiction "Horror fiction") and zombie genres.

### Cultural impact

*[GameSpot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GameSpot "GameSpot")* listed the original *Resident Evil* as one of the fifteen most influential video games of all time. It is credited with defining and popularizing the [survival horror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survival_horror "Survival horror") genre of games. It is also credited with taking [video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_games "Video games") in a cinematic direction with its [B-movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-movie "B-movie") style [cut-scenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cut-scenes "Cut-scenes"), including live-action [full-motion video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full-motion_video "Full-motion video") (FMV) footage. Its live-action opening, however, was controversial; it became one of the first [action games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_games "Action games") to receive the "Mature 17+" (M) rating from the [Entertainment Software Rating Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Software_Rating_Board "Entertainment Software Rating Board") (ESRB), despite the opening cutscene being censored in North America.

The *Resident Evil* franchise is credited with sparking a revival of the [zombie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zombie "Zombie") genre in popular culture, leading to a renewed interest in [zombie films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zombie_films "Zombie films") during the 2000s. *Resident Evil* also helped redefine the zombie genre, playing an important role in its shift from supernatural themes to scientific themes by using science to explain the origins of zombies. According to [Kim Newman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Newman "Kim Newman") in the book *[Nightmare Movies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightmare_Movies "Nightmare Movies")* (2011), "the zombie revival began in the Far East" mainly due to the 1996 Japanese zombie games *Resident Evil* and *[The House of the Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_House_of_the_Dead "The House of the Dead")*. [George A. Romero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_A._Romero "George A. Romero"), in 2013, said it was the video games *Resident Evil* and *House of the Dead* "more than anything else" that popularised his zombie concept in early 21st-century popular culture. In a 2015 interview with *[Huffington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huffington_Post "Huffington Post")*, screenwriter-director [Alex Garland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Garland "Alex Garland") credited the *Resident Evil* series as a primary influence on his script for the [horror film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horror_film "Horror film") *[28 Days Later](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/28_Days_Later "28 Days Later")* (2002), and credited the first *Resident Evil* game for revitalizing the zombie genre. Screenwriter [Edgar Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Wright "Edgar Wright") cited *Resident Evil 2* as a primary influence on his [zombie comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zombie_comedy "Zombie comedy") film *[Shaun of the Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaun_of_the_Dead "Shaun of the Dead")* (2004), with the film's star and co-writer [Simon Pegg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Pegg "Simon Pegg") also crediting the first game with starting the zombie revival in popular culture. [*The Walking Dead* comic book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walking_Dead_(comic_book) "The Walking Dead (comic book)") creator [Robert Kirkman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Kirkman "Robert Kirkman") cited *Resident Evil* as his favorite zombie game, while [*The Walking Dead* television series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walking_Dead_(TV_series) "The Walking Dead (TV series)") director [Greg Nicotero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Nicotero "Greg Nicotero") credited *Resident Evil* and *The House of the Dead* with introducing the zombie genre "to a whole generation of younger people who didn't grow up watching *Night of the Living Dead* and *Dawn of the Dead*."

The *Resident Evil Apocalypse* zombies were conceptualized and choreographed by Sharon B. Moore and Derek Aasland.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] Through script analysis and movement research, a "scientific logic" was devised for the T-virus accounting for each Zombie behavior envisioned in [Paul W. S. Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_W._S._Anderson "Paul W. S. Anderson")'s script. Sharon B. Moore and Derek Aasland then wrote the so-called Undead Bible - a Handbook for the Undead - used as the guide for the nearly 1000 cast under the choreographic department (stunt performers, actors, dancers, extras) to ensure the Undead physicality was performed in a unified way across the picture.  The Stunt and Core teams participated in the "Undead Bootcamp".  See also 2007 Documentary *Undead Bootcamp* starring producer [Jeremy Bolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Bolt "Jeremy Bolt"), director [Alexander Witt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Witt "Alexander Witt"), and choreographers Sharon B. Moore and Derek Aasland.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

On the DVD Featurette '*Resident Evil; Game Over*' *Apocalypse* director [Alexander Witt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Witt "Alexander Witt") said the zombies needed to be "more aggressive and more dangerous" than the original film, so they were created by the film's choreographers Sharon B. Moore and Derek Aasland as "liquid zombie[s]' in terms of their relentless forward motion: unstoppable, flowing around any kind of resistance, and then rushing in on the final attack. This is also detailed in the book *Biopunk Dystopias: Genetic Engineering, Society, and Science Fiction* (Lars Schmeink, 2016, p. 214).

Additionally, the first *Resident Evil* film adaptation also contributed to the revival of zombie films, with the success of the film and the games resulting in zombies achieving greater mainstream prominence and several zombie films being greenlit, such as the video game film adaptation *[House of the Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_the_Dead_(film) "House of the Dead (film)")* (2003), the remake *[Dawn of the Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawn_of_the_Dead_(2004_film) "Dawn of the Dead (2004 film)")* (2004) and Romero's *[Land of the Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_of_the_Dead "Land of the Dead")* (2005). The *Resident Evil* films, *28 Days Later* and the *Dawn of the Dead* remake all set box office records for the zombie genre, reaching levels of commercial success not seen since the original *[Dawn of the Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawn_of_the_Dead_(1978_film) "Dawn of the Dead (1978 film)")* (1978). They were followed by other zombie films such as *[28 Weeks Later](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/28_Weeks_Later "28 Weeks Later")* (2007), *[Zombieland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zombieland "Zombieland")* (2009), *[Cockneys vs Zombies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockneys_vs_Zombies "Cockneys vs Zombies")* (2012), and *[World War Z](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_Z_(film) "World War Z (film)")* (2013), as well as zombie-themed graphic novels and television shows such as *[The Walking Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walking_Dead_(franchise) "The Walking Dead (franchise)")* and *[The Returned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Returned_(French_TV_series) "The Returned (French TV series)")*, and books such as *[World War Z](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_Z "World War Z")* (2006), *[Pride and Prejudice and Zombies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pride_and_Prejudice_and_Zombies "Pride and Prejudice and Zombies")* (2009) and *[Warm Bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warm_Bodies "Warm Bodies")* (2010). The zombie revival trend was popular across different media up until the mid-2010s. Since then, zombie films have declined in popularity during the late 2010s, but [zombie video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_zombie_video_games "List of zombie video games") have remained popular, as seen with the commercial success of the *Resident Evil 2* remake and *[Days Gone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Days_Gone "Days Gone")* in 2019.
