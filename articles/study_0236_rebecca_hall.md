# Rebecca Hall

**Rebecca Maria Hall** (born 3 May 1982) is an English actress and filmmaker. She made her first onscreen appearance at the age of 10 in the 1992 [television adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Camomile_Lawn_(TV_serial) "The Camomile Lawn (TV serial)") of *[The Camomile Lawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Camomile_Lawn "The Camomile Lawn")*, directed by her father, [Peter Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Hall_(director) "Peter Hall (director)"). Her professional stage debut came in her father's 2002 production of *[Mrs. Warren's Profession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mrs._Warren%27s_Profession "Mrs. Warren's Profession")*, which earned her an [Ian Charleson Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Charleson_Award "Ian Charleson Award"). In 2006, following her film debut in *[Starter for 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starter_for_10_(film) "Starter for 10 (film)")*, Hall got her breakthrough role in [Christopher Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan "Christopher Nolan")'s thriller film *[The Prestige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prestige "The Prestige")*. She starred in [Woody Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Allen "Woody Allen")'s romantic comedy *[Vicky Cristina Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicky_Cristina_Barcelona "Vicky Cristina Barcelona")* in 2008, for which she received a [Golden Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe "Golden Globe") nomination for [Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Actress_in_a_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Musical_or_Comedy "Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy").

Hall has appeared in a wide array of films, including [Ron Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Howard "Ron Howard")'s historical drama *[Frost/Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frost/Nixon_(film) "Frost/Nixon (film)")* (2008), [Ben Affleck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Affleck "Ben Affleck")'s crime drama *[The Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Town_(2010_film) "The Town (2010 film)")* (2010), the horror thriller *[The Awakening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Awakening_(2011_film) "The Awakening (2011 film)")* (2011), the [Marvel Cinematic Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe "Marvel Cinematic Universe") film *[Iron Man 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Man_3 "Iron Man 3")* (2013), the thriller *[The Gift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gift_(2015_American_film) "The Gift (2015 American film)")* (2015), and the fantasy film *[The BFG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_BFG_(2016_film) "The BFG (2016 film)")* (2016). In 2016, Hall was praised by critics for her portrayal of news reporter [Christine Chubbuck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christine_Chubbuck "Christine Chubbuck") in the biographical drama *[Christine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christine_(2016_film) "Christine (2016 film)")*. She has since starred in the [MonsterVerse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MonsterVerse "MonsterVerse") films *[Godzilla vs. Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godzilla_vs._Kong "Godzilla vs. Kong")* (2021) and *[Godzilla x Kong: The New Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godzilla_x_Kong:_The_New_Empire "Godzilla x Kong: The New Empire")* (2024). Hall has also had several notable performances on British television. She won the [British Academy Television Award for Best Supporting Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Television_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress "British Academy Television Award for Best Supporting Actress") for the 2009 [Channel 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_4 "Channel 4") miniseries *[Red Riding: 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Riding#Red_Riding_1974 "Red Riding")*. In 2013, she was nominated for the [British Academy Television Award for Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Television_Award_for_Best_Actress "British Academy Television Award for Best Actress") for her performance in [BBC Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Two "BBC Two")'s *[Parade's End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parade%27s_End_(TV_series) "Parade's End (TV series)")*.

In 2021, she made her feature directorial debut with the period drama *[Passing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passing_(film) "Passing (film)")*, which she also wrote and co-produced, adapted from [Nella Larsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nella_Larsen "Nella Larsen")'s [1929 novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passing_(novel) "Passing (novel)"). It received critical acclaim, and she received [BAFTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Awards "British Academy Film Awards") nominations for [Outstanding British Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outstanding_British_Film "Outstanding British Film") and [Outstanding Debut by a British Writer, Director or Producer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outstanding_Debut_by_a_British_Writer,_Director_or_Producer "Outstanding Debut by a British Writer, Director or Producer").

## Early life and education

Rebecca Maria Hall was born in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London") on 3 May 1982, the daughter of American opera singer [Maria Ewing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Ewing "Maria Ewing") and [Sir Peter Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Hall_(director) "Peter Hall (director)"), an English stage director and founder of the [Royal Shakespeare Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Shakespeare_Company "Royal Shakespeare Company"). Her mother was born in [Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit "Detroit"), the daughter of an [African-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African-American "African-American") [mixed-race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed-race "Mixed-race") father and a Dutch mother. She is a descendant of [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War "American Revolutionary War") veteran [Bazabeel Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bazabeel_Norman "Bazabeel Norman"), a free African man.

Years later, as a guest on *[Finding Your Roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finding_Your_Roots "Finding Your Roots")*, Hall discovered that, while her maternal grandfather, Norman Isaac Ewing, had performed as a [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American "Native American") figure and was reported as a [Sioux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sioux "Sioux") chief in newspapers, he was the son of mixed-race African-American parents, and had no Native American ancestry. His father, Hall's great-grandfather John William Ewing, had been born into slavery. After the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War"), he became [a prominent figure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African-American_upper_class "African-American upper class") in the black community of [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C.") Hall has 91% European DNA and 9% sub-Saharan African DNA, according to an [Ancestry.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestry.com "Ancestry.com") DNA test. Hall's parents separated when she was still young, eventually divorcing in 1990. Hall has five paternal half-siblings: stage director [Edward Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Hall_(director) "Edward Hall (director)"), producer [Christopher Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Hall_(producer) "Christopher Hall (producer)"), actresses [Jennifer Caron Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Caron_Hall "Jennifer Caron Hall") and Emma Hall, and set designer Lucy Hall. Additionally, on the April 7, 2026 episode of *Finding Your Roots*, it was revealed that Hall is a distant cousin of the American actress [Danielle Deadwyler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danielle_Deadwyler "Danielle Deadwyler"), who shares her date of birth.

Hall attended [Roedean School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roedean_School "Roedean School"), where she became [head girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_girl "Head girl"). She studied [English literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_literature "English literature") at [St Catharine's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Catharine%27s_College,_Cambridge "St Catharine's College, Cambridge"), before dropping out in 2002, just before her final year. During her time at Cambridge, she was active in the student theatre scene and also set up her own theatre company. She was a member of the [Marlowe Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlowe_Society "Marlowe Society") and performed in several productions alongside her housemate [Dan Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Stevens "Dan Stevens"), an English literature student at [Emmanuel College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_College,_Cambridge "Emmanuel College, Cambridge").

## Career

### Film and television

Hall's first professional role came in 1992, when at the age of nine she appeared as young Sophy in her father's television adaptation of [Mary Wesley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Wesley "Mary Wesley")'s *[The Camomile Lawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Camomile_Lawn_(TV_serial) "The Camomile Lawn (TV serial)")*. Her feature film debut came in 2006 as Rebecca Epstein in [the film adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starter_for_10_(film) "Starter for 10 (film)") of [David Nicholls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Nicholls_(writer) "David Nicholls (writer)")'s *[Starter for Ten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starter_for_Ten_(novel) "Starter for Ten (novel)")*.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

She got her breakthrough with the role of Sarah Borden in [Christopher Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan "Christopher Nolan")'s film *[The Prestige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prestige "The Prestige")* (2006). She appeared in [Stephen Poliakoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Poliakoff "Stephen Poliakoff")'s *[Joe's Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe%27s_Palace "Joe's Palace")* in 2007, in addition to appearing in several other television films, including *[Wide Sargasso Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide_Sargasso_Sea_(2006_film) "Wide Sargasso Sea (2006 film)")* and *Rubberheart*.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Hall's Hollywood fame was sparked when she starred in the [Woody Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Allen "Woody Allen") film *[Vicky Cristina Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicky_Cristina_Barcelona "Vicky Cristina Barcelona")* (2008) as one of the title characters, Vicky. Her performance was well received, and she was nominated for the [Golden Globe Award for Best Actress – Motion Picture Musical or Comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Musical_or_Comedy "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress – Motion Picture Musical or Comedy").[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] Also in the same year, she appeared in [Ron Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Howard "Ron Howard")'s historical drama *[Frost/Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frost/Nixon_(film) "Frost/Nixon (film)")* as the girlfriend of [David Frost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Frost "David Frost"), played by [Michael Sheen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Sheen "Michael Sheen").[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] The following year, she was cast in the British fantasy-horror film *[Dorian Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorian_Gray_(2009_film) "Dorian Gray (2009 film)")*, based on [Oscar Wilde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Wilde "Oscar Wilde")'s 1890 novel *[The Picture of Dorian Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Picture_of_Dorian_Gray "The Picture of Dorian Gray")*.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Following a small role in the indie film *[Please Give](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Please_Give "Please Give")*, Hall starred in [Ben Affleck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Affleck "Ben Affleck")'s crime drama *[The Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Town_(2010_film) "The Town (2010 film)")* (2010) opposite Affleck and [Jon Hamm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Hamm "Jon Hamm"). In June 2010, she won the [British Academy Television Award for Best Supporting Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Television_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress "British Academy Television Award for Best Supporting Actress") for her portrayal of Paula Garland in the 2009 [Channel 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_4 "Channel 4") production *[Red Riding: In the Year of Our Lord 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Riding#In_the_Year_of_Our_Lord_1974 "Red Riding")*. The following year, she played the female lead in the British ghost film *[The Awakening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Awakening_(2011_film) "The Awakening (2011 film)")*, released in September 2011.

In 2012, she took on the role of [Beth Raymer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beth_Raymer "Beth Raymer") in the comedy-drama film *[Lay the Favorite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lay_the_Favorite "Lay the Favorite")*, based on Raymer's memoir of the same title. One review commented that she "plays Raymer as an endearing force of nature who somehow manages to survive in a dangerous world through sheer force of character". She starred in the BBC/HBO/VRT production of *[Parade's End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parade%27s_End_(TV_series) "Parade's End (TV series)")* (2012) opposite [Benedict Cumberbatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedict_Cumberbatch "Benedict Cumberbatch"), which earned her a [BAFTA Television Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Television_Award "BAFTA Television Award") nomination for [Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Television_Award_for_Best_Actress "British Academy Television Award for Best Actress"). In 2013, Hall replaced [Jessica Chastain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Chastain "Jessica Chastain") as [Maya Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_Hansen_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Maya Hansen (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") in the superhero film *[Iron Man 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Man_3 "Iron Man 3")*. The same year, she appeared in the political thriller *[Closed Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_Circuit_(2013_film) "Closed Circuit (2013 film)")* (2013). In 2014, she starred opposite [Johnny Depp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Depp "Johnny Depp") in [Wally Pfister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wally_Pfister "Wally Pfister")'s directorial debut *[Transcendence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendence_(2014_film) "Transcendence (2014 film)")* (2014). In 2015, Hall starred in the romantic comedy *[Tumbledown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumbledown_(2015_film) "Tumbledown (2015 film)")* and [Joel Edgerton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Edgerton "Joel Edgerton")'s directorial debut *[The Gift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gift_(2015_American_film) "The Gift (2015 American film)")*.

In the 2016 biographical drama *[Christine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christine_(2016_film) "Christine (2016 film)")*, Hall played [Christine Chubbuck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christine_Chubbuck "Christine Chubbuck"), a real-life TV news reporter. Her performance was praised by critics. In 2017, she portrayed [Elizabeth Holloway Marston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Holloway_Marston "Elizabeth Holloway Marston"), a psychologist who inspired the character of [Wonder Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonder_Woman "Wonder Woman"), in *[Professor Marston and the Wonder Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_Marston_and_the_Wonder_Women "Professor Marston and the Wonder Women")*. The same year, Hall joined the cast of *[A Rainy Day in New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Rainy_Day_in_New_York "A Rainy Day in New York")*, directed by Allen. After re-reading accounts related to allegations of [sexual abuse against Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Allen_sexual_abuse_allegation "Woody Allen sexual abuse allegation"), Hall, in January 2018, donated her salary to [Time's Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time%27s_Up_(organization) "Time's Up (organization)"). She said, "I see not only how complicated this matter is, but that my actions have made another woman feel silenced and dismissed. I regret this decision and wouldn't make the same one today." However, Hall expressed regret for her apology in 2024, saying "I don’t talk to him any more, but I don’t think that [actors] should be the ones who are doing judge and jury on this."

In 2018, Hall provided English dubbing for the character Mother in *[Mirai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirai_(2018_film) "Mirai (2018 film)")*. In 2021, Hall starred in and co-executive produced the well-reviewed horror-thriller *[The Night House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Night_House_(2020_film) "The Night House (2020 film)")*. The same year, she appeared in the [monster film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monster_film "Monster film") *[Godzilla vs. Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godzilla_vs._Kong "Godzilla vs. Kong")*[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] and made her directorial debut with the drama *[Passing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passing_(film) "Passing (film)")*, based on the 1929 American novel of the same name by [Nella Larsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nella_Larsen "Nella Larsen"). Hall wrote the screenplay and co-produced the film, which was largely inspired by her own mixed-race heritage, as well as her African-American ancestors who passed as white. *Passing* premiered at the [2021 Sundance Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Sundance_Film_Festival "2021 Sundance Film Festival") and received critical acclaim. Its distribution rights were acquired by [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") for approximately $16 million.

Hall is a signatory of the [Film Workers for Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_Workers_for_Palestine "Film Workers for Palestine") boycott pledge that was published in September 2025.

### Stage

Hall made her professional stage debut in 2002 when she starred as Vivie in her father's production of *[Mrs. Warren's Profession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mrs._Warren%27s_Profession "Mrs. Warren's Profession")* at the [Strand Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novello_Theatre "Novello Theatre") in London. Her performance, described as "admirable" and "accomplished", earned her the [Ian Charleson Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Charleson_Award "Ian Charleson Award") in 2003.

In 2003, Hall's father celebrated 50 years as a theatre director by staging a season of five plays at the [Theatre Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_Royal,_Bath "Theatre Royal, Bath") in [Bath, Somerset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bath,_Somerset "Bath, Somerset"). Hall starred in two of these plays; she appeared as Rosalind in her father's production of *[As You Like It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As_You_Like_It "As You Like It")*, which gained her a second Charleson nomination and starred in the title role of [Thea Sharrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thea_Sharrock "Thea Sharrock")'s revival of [D. H. Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._H._Lawrence "D. H. Lawrence")'s *The Fight for Barbara*.

In 2004, Hall appeared in three plays for the Peter Hall Company at the Theatre Royal Bath, two of which her father directed: *[Man and Superman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_and_Superman "Man and Superman")* in which she played Ann, and *[Galileo's Daughter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo%27s_Daughter "Galileo's Daughter")* in which she played Sister Maria Celeste. The third, [Molière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moli%C3%A8re "Molière")'s *[Don Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dom_Juan "Dom Juan")*, in which she played the part of Elvira, was directed by Sharrock.

In 2005, Hall reprised the role of Rosalind in a touring production of *As You Like It*, again under the direction of her father. This tour played the Rose Theatre in [Kingston upon Thames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston_upon_Thames "Kingston upon Thames"), the [Brooklyn Academy of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Academy_of_Music "Brooklyn Academy of Music") in New York, the Curran Theatre in San Francisco and the Ahmanson Theatre in Los Angeles. This was a second leg of the US tour that began in 2003 with venues at the Shubert Theater [New Haven, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Haven,_Connecticut "New Haven, Connecticut"), [Columbus, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Ohio "Columbus, Ohio"), and the [Wilbur Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilbur_Theater "Wilbur Theater") in Boston.

In 2008–2009, Hall appeared in [Sam Mendes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Mendes "Sam Mendes")'s first instalment of the Bridge Project as Hermione in *[The Winter's Tale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Winter%27s_Tale "The Winter's Tale")* and as Varya in *[The Cherry Orchard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cherry_Orchard "The Cherry Orchard")*. The project gave performances with the same cast in Germany, Greece, New Zealand, Singapore, Spain, the United Kingdom and the United States. In 2010–2011, she played Viola in a production of *[Twelfth Night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelfth_Night "Twelfth Night")* at London's [National Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_National_Theatre "Royal National Theatre"), directed by her father.

Hall made her [Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_theatre "Broadway theatre") debut in 2013 in [Sophie Treadwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophie_Treadwell "Sophie Treadwell")'s expressionist play *[Machinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machinal "Machinal")*. The Roundabout Theater production, directed by Lyndsey Turner, began previews on 20 December 2013, with the official opening on 16 January 2014 at the [American Airlines Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines_Theatre "American Airlines Theatre").

## Personal life

Hall and director [Sam Mendes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Mendes "Sam Mendes") were in a relationship from 2011 to 2013. In 2014, Hall met actor [Morgan Spector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Spector "Morgan Spector") while co-starring in a Broadway production. They married in 2015. Their daughter was born in 2018.
