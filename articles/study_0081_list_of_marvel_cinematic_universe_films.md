# List of Marvel Cinematic Universe films

The [Marvel Cinematic Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe "Marvel Cinematic Universe") (MCU) centers on American [superhero films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superhero_film "Superhero film") produced by [Marvel Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Studios "Marvel Studios"), based on characters from [Marvel Comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Comics "Marvel Comics") publications. The MCU is the [shared universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shared_universe "Shared universe") in which all of the films are set. Marvel Studios has released 38 films since 2008 and has at least eight more in various stages of development.

Marvel Studios president [Kevin Feige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Feige "Kevin Feige") has produced every MCU film, with some films having additional producers and production companies. The [*Spider-Man* films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man_(2017_film_series) "Spider-Man (2017 film series)") are owned, financed, and distributed by [Sony Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Pictures "Sony Pictures") and co-produced by Sony's [Columbia Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Pictures "Columbia Pictures") and [Pascal Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_Pictures "Pascal Pictures"). The MCU films are written and directed by various individuals and feature large, often [ensemble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensemble_cast "Ensemble cast"), casts. They are released in groups called "Phases", with three phases making up a "Saga". The MCU is the [highest-grossing film franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_films#Highest-grossing_franchises_and_film_series "List of highest-grossing films") of all time, grossing over $35.1 billion at the global box office. This includes *[Avengers: Endgame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers:_Endgame "Avengers: Endgame")*, which is the [highest-grossing film ever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_films#Highest-grossing_films "List of highest-grossing films").

The films from [Phases One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe:_Phase_One "Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase One"), [Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe:_Phase_Two "Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Two"), and [Three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe:_Phase_Three "Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Three") are collectively known as "[The Infinity Saga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Infinity_Saga "The Infinity Saga")". The first MCU film, *[Iron Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Man_(2008_film) "Iron Man (2008 film)")* (2008), was distributed by [Paramount Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures "Paramount Pictures"). Paramount also distributed *[Iron Man 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Man_2 "Iron Man 2")* (2010), *[Thor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thor_(film) "Thor (film)")* (2011), and *[Captain America: The First Avenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_America:_The_First_Avenger "Captain America: The First Avenger")* (2011), while [Universal Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures "Universal Pictures") distributed *[The Incredible Hulk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Incredible_Hulk_(film) "The Incredible Hulk (film)")* (2008). [Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Studios_Motion_Pictures "Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures") began distributing the series with the [crossover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossover_(fiction) "Crossover (fiction)") film *[The Avengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Avengers_(2012_film) "The Avengers (2012 film)")* (2012), which concluded Phase One. Phase Two comprises *[Iron Man 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Man_3 "Iron Man 3")* (2013), *[Thor: The Dark World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thor:_The_Dark_World "Thor: The Dark World")* (2013), *[Captain America: The Winter Soldier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_America:_The_Winter_Soldier "Captain America: The Winter Soldier")* (2014), *[Guardians of the Galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardians_of_the_Galaxy_(film) "Guardians of the Galaxy (film)")* (2014), *[Avengers: Age of Ultron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers:_Age_of_Ultron "Avengers: Age of Ultron")* (2015), and *[Ant-Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant-Man_(film) "Ant-Man (film)")* (2015). *[Captain America: Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_America:_Civil_War "Captain America: Civil War")* (2016) started Phase Three, followed by *[Doctor Strange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Strange_(2016_film) "Doctor Strange (2016 film)")* (2016), *[Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardians_of_the_Galaxy_Vol._2 "Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 2")* (2017), *[Spider-Man: Homecoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man:_Homecoming "Spider-Man: Homecoming")* (2017), *[Thor: Ragnarok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thor:_Ragnarok "Thor: Ragnarok")* (2017), *[Black Panther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Panther_(film) "Black Panther (film)")* (2018), *[Avengers: Infinity War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers:_Infinity_War "Avengers: Infinity War")* (2018), *[Ant-Man and the Wasp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant-Man_and_the_Wasp "Ant-Man and the Wasp")* (2018), *[Captain Marvel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Marvel_(film) "Captain Marvel (film)")* (2019), *Avengers: Endgame*, and *[Spider-Man: Far From Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man:_Far_From_Home "Spider-Man: Far From Home")* (2019).

The films from [Phases Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe:_Phase_Four "Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Four"), [Five](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe:_Phase_Five "Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Five"), and [Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe:_Phase_Six "Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Six") are collectively known as "[The Multiverse Saga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Multiverse_Saga "The Multiverse Saga")". These phases also include [television series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Marvel_Cinematic_Universe_television_series_(Marvel_Studios) "List of Marvel Cinematic Universe television series (Marvel Studios)") and [television specials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel%27s_Special_Presentations "Marvel's Special Presentations") streaming on [Disney+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney%2B "Disney+"). The Phase Four films are *[Black Widow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Widow_(2021_film) "Black Widow (2021 film)")* (2021), *[Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang-Chi_and_the_Legend_of_the_Ten_Rings "Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings")* (2021), *[Eternals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternals_(film) "Eternals (film)")* (2021), *[Spider-Man: No Way Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man:_No_Way_Home "Spider-Man: No Way Home")* (2021), *[Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Strange_in_the_Multiverse_of_Madness "Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness")* (2022), *[Thor: Love and Thunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thor:_Love_and_Thunder "Thor: Love and Thunder")* (2022), and *[Black Panther: Wakanda Forever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Panther:_Wakanda_Forever "Black Panther: Wakanda Forever")* (2022). Phase Five features *[Ant-Man and the Wasp: Quantumania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant-Man_and_the_Wasp:_Quantumania "Ant-Man and the Wasp: Quantumania")* (2023), *[Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardians_of_the_Galaxy_Vol._3 "Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 3")* (2023), *[The Marvels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Marvels "The Marvels")* (2023), *[Deadpool & Wolverine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadpool_%26_Wolverine "Deadpool & Wolverine")* (2024), *[Captain America: Brave New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_America:_Brave_New_World "Captain America: Brave New World")* (2025), and *[Thunderbolts\*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunderbolts* "Thunderbolts*")* (2025). Phase Six began with *[The Fantastic Four: First Steps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fantastic_Four:_First_Steps "The Fantastic Four: First Steps")* (2025), and also includes *[Spider-Man: Brand New Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man:_Brand_New_Day "Spider-Man: Brand New Day")* (2026), *[Avengers: Doomsday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers:_Doomsday "Avengers: Doomsday")* (2026), and *[Avengers: Secret Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers:_Secret_Wars "Avengers: Secret Wars")* (2027).

The next MCU saga will start with [an *X-Men* film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-Men_(2028_film) "X-Men (2028 film)") in 2028, followed by *[Ghost Rider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_Rider_(2028_film) "Ghost Rider (2028 film)")* and *[Black Panther III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Panther_III "Black Panther III")* later that year.

## Development

### Initial development work for the Infinity Saga

By 2005, [Marvel Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Entertainment "Marvel Entertainment") had begun planning to produce its own films independently and distribute them through [Paramount Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures "Paramount Pictures"). In June 2007, [Marvel Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Studios "Marvel Studios") secured funding from a $525 million revolving credit facility with [Merrill Lynch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merrill_Lynch "Merrill Lynch"). Marvel planned to release individual films for their main characters and then merge them in a [crossover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossover_(fiction) "Crossover (fiction)") film.

Marvel Studios president [Kevin Feige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Feige "Kevin Feige") said in November 2013 that releases each year would ideally include one film based on an existing character and one featuring a new character, feeling that would be "a nice rhythm", although, this was not always the case as shown by the sequels *[Iron Man 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Man_3 "Iron Man 3")* and *[Thor: The Dark World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thor:_The_Dark_World "Thor: The Dark World")* being released in 2013. Feige elaborated in July 2014 that this model was being followed for 2014 and 2015, and he felt it would be fun to continue. After the reveal that month of multiple release dates for films through 2019, in which some years had three films scheduled, Feige said there was no "number cruncher" telling the studio to increase their film output and the change was based on them "managing [existing] franchises, film to film, and when we have a team ready to go, why tell them to go away for four years just because we don't have a slot? We'd rather find a way to keep that going." After the titles for these films were revealed in October 2014, Feige said the studio was "firing on all cylinders right now" and this made them comfortable with increasing to three films a year in 2017 and 2018 without changing their production approach. On the potential for so-called "[superhero fatigue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superhero_fatigue "Superhero fatigue")", Feige stated that, although each film is based on Marvel Comics and features the "Marvel Studios" logo, he believed each film had unique qualities that differentiated them from the others and from non-[Marvel Cinematic Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe "Marvel Cinematic Universe") (MCU) superhero films. For example, he noted how the studio's 2016 releases, *[Captain America: Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_America:_Civil_War "Captain America: Civil War")* and *[Doctor Strange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Strange_(2016_film) "Doctor Strange (2016 film)")*, were "completely different movies". The studio hoped to continue to surprise audiences and "not [fall] into things becoming too similar".

In February 2014, Feige said Marvel Studios wanted to mimic the "rhythm" of comic book releases by having characters appear in their own films and then come together for crossover events, with *Avengers* films acting as "big, giant linchpins" within the [shared universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shared_universe "Shared universe"). On expanding the number of characters in the universe and letting individual films breathe and work on their own, as opposed to having Avenger team-ups outside of *Avengers* films, Feige said they planned to teach general audiences "about the notion of the characters existing separately, coming together for specific events and going away and existing separately in their own worlds again. Just like comic readers have been doing for decades... people sort of are accepting that there's just a time when they should be together and there's a time when they're not." Discussing how much story is developed for future MCU films, Feige said in September 2015 that "broad strokes" and occasionally "super-specific things" are determined far in advance. He said there was enough leeway to "have room to sway and to move and to go and to surprise ourselves in places that we end up" and that each film would feel satisfying on its own, but still interconnected to the larger universe and as if it had been planned years ahead of time. The studio has contingency plans for times when they are unable to secure a certain actor to reprise a role, and can respond to surprises such as the [film rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_rights "Film rights") to use [Spider-Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man "Spider-Man") becoming available in February 2015.

Feige discussed moving the MCU to [Phase Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe:_Phase_Four "Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Four") in April 2016, reflecting on the first three phases of films and saying, "I think there will be a finality to moments of [Phase Three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe:_Phase_Three "Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Three"), as well as new beginnings that will mark a different, a *very* different, a distinctively different chapter in what will someday be a complete first saga made up of three Phases." Frequent MCU director [Joe Russo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo_brothers "Russo brothers") added that Phase Three was the "deconstruction Phase" of the MCU, beginning with *Civil War* and leading into "the culmination films" of *[Avengers: Infinity War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers:_Infinity_War "Avengers: Infinity War")* (2018) and *[Avengers: Endgame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers:_Endgame "Avengers: Endgame")* (2019). A year later, Feige felt after the conclusion of Phase Three, Marvel might abandon grouping the films by Phases, saying, "it might be a new thing". Feige mentioned that *Avengers: Endgame* would provide "a definitive end" to the films and storylines preceding it, with the franchise having "two distinct periods. Everything before [*Endgame*] and everything after". Many of the films that were planned to follow *Endgame* were intentionally different from the films in "[The Infinity Saga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Infinity_Saga "The Infinity Saga")", which includes [Phase One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe:_Phase_One "Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase One"), [Phase Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe:_Phase_Two "Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Two"), and Phase Three.

### Expansion to the Multiverse Saga and shifting approach

In July 2019, Feige announced the Phase Four slate at San Diego Comic-Con, consisting of films and television event series for the streaming service [Disney+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney%2B "Disney+"). In December 2020, at Disney's Investor Day, Marvel provided updates to previously announced films for the Phase. In late June 2022, Feige said audiences would begin to see where the next saga of the MCU would be heading as Phase Four neared its conclusion, adding that there had been many clues throughout the Phase to what that would be. He said Marvel Studios would be a "little more direct" on their future plans in the following months to provide audiences with "the bigger picture". In July 2022, Feige unveiled the [Phase Five](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe:_Phase_Five "Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Five") and [Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe:_Phase_Six "Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Six") slates at San Diego Comic-Con, similarly consisting of films and Disney+ series, and revealed that these three Phases would make up "[The Multiverse Saga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Multiverse_Saga "The Multiverse Saga")".

In May 2024, Disney CEO [Bob Iger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Iger "Bob Iger") said the company planned to release two, or at most three, Marvel films a year moving forward, down from four films being released in some recent years, as part of Disney's larger strategy to reduce its content output and focus on quality. At that time, four films were still expected to be released in both 2025 and 2026. Iger said Marvel content would continue to balance sequels with new franchises. Later in 2024, Disney removed the long-in-development film *[Blade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blade_(unproduced_film) "Blade (unproduced film)")* from its 2025 release date and also removed an unspecified Marvel film that was scheduled for July 2026, which was replaced by [Sony Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Pictures "Sony Pictures")'s *[Spider-Man: Brand New Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man:_Brand_New_Day "Spider-Man: Brand New Day")*. Feige said the three films that were still scheduled for 2025 had been in development for a long time and were ready for release. He expected Marvel Studios to start releasing two films a year from 2026, but said there was potential for anywhere between one and three films in some years. In May 2025, Iger called *Thunderbolts\** the "first and best example" of Marvel Studios' refocusing efforts.

Further changes to Marvel Studios' release slate resulted in over a year gap between *[The Fantastic Four: First Steps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fantastic_Four:_First_Steps "The Fantastic Four: First Steps")* (2025) and *Brand New Day*; this would be the longest gap between MCU film releases since the gap between *[Spider-Man: Far From Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man:_Far_From_Home "Spider-Man: Far From Home")* (2019) and *[Black Widow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Widow_(2021_film) "Black Widow (2021 film)")* (2021), which was impacted by the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"). In July 2025, Feige said the studio was working to reduce its film budgets, which had increased since *Endgame* in part due to the pandemic. He said the budgets for their 2024 and 2025 films were a third lower than those for 2022 and 2023. Marvel Studios executives met with the creative team behind the film *[The Creator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Creator_(2023_film) "The Creator (2023 film)")* (2023) to understand how that film was made with a relatively low $80 million budget. Additionally, Feige stated that Marvel Studios had made a deal to film many of their then-upcoming films at [Pinewood Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinewood_Studios "Pinewood Studios") in the United Kingdom, where several of their other films had previously been shot. He said Marvel Studios anticipated utilizing studio space in Georgia and New York as well, rather than California, because of those states' production tax credits. However, *[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")* reported that Marvel would largely forego shooting in Georgia because of its rising costs, instead choosing to shoot in the United Kingdom because it had become cheaper to do so.

Feige called *[Deadpool & Wolverine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadpool_%26_Wolverine "Deadpool & Wolverine")* (2024) the true start to Marvel Studios' exploration and use of the Fox characters following [Disney's acquisition of assets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acquisition_of_21st_Century_Fox_by_Disney "Acquisition of 21st Century Fox by Disney") from [21st Century Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_Century_Fox "21st Century Fox") in 2019, and said every project after it would be part of the MCU's "Mutant era". The next MCU saga after the Multiverse Saga is expected to center on the Fox characters, including the [X-Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-Men "X-Men"). Feige reportedly had a 10-year plan for the X-Men in the MCU by May 2025. In July 2025, Feige said Marvel Studios was "already well into development" on the three phases of their next saga following the conclusion of the Multiverse Saga with *Secret Wars*. He said Phase Seven would be "directly impacted" by the films of Phase Six. A year later, Feige said *Secret Wars* would "establish a streamlined, simplified, single universe that audiences can keep up with. Some things will be familiar, but many, many things will be entirely new." He said the X-Men would be core to the next era of the MCU, which he called the "Mutant Saga", and noted that those characters have their own universe and "multi-stage saga" in the comics.

Following several critical and financial failures, the studio began testing its films through test screenings organized by the National Research Group (NRG). The studio previously relied on "friends-and-family" test screenings at [Walt Disney Studios in Burbank, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Studios_(Burbank) "Walt Disney Studios (Burbank)"), to help maintain secrecy. Feige said the NRG tests yielded results similar to their own.

#### Untitled X-Men film (2028)

At SDCC in July 2019, Kevin Feige said [mutants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutant_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Mutant (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"), which include the X-Men, would be introduced to the MCU, and they would differ from [20th Century Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Century_Fox "20th Century Fox")'s *X-Men* film series. [Michael Lesslie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Lesslie "Michael Lesslie") entered negotiations to write a new X-Men film in May 2024, and was confirmed to be writing it a year later. [Jake Schreier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Schreier "Jake Schreier") was announced as director following positive responses to his film *[Thunderbolts\*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunderbolts* "Thunderbolts*")* (2025). After bringing back actors from Fox's *X-Men* films for the Multiverse Saga, Feige said this film would feature a recast X-Men team following the MCU's "reset" in *Secret Wars*. He said it would be "a very youth-oriented, focused and cast movie". Schreier revealed in April 2026 that [Joanna Calo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joanna_Calo "Joanna Calo") and [Lee Sung Jin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Sung_Jin "Lee Sung Jin") were writing a new draft of the script after they collaborated with him on *Thunderbolts\** and the television series *[Beef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beef_(TV_series) "Beef (TV series)")* (2023–present). Following an active casting process, the first actors were announced in August 2026, including [Sadie Sink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadie_Sink "Sadie Sink") reprising her role as [Jean Grey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Grey_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Jean Grey (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") from the film *[Spider-Man: Brand New Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man:_Brand_New_Day "Spider-Man: Brand New Day")* (2026). The film is scheduled for release on May 5, 2028.

#### *Ghost Rider* (2028)

The film rights to the character [Ghost Rider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_Rider "Ghost Rider") reverted to Marvel Studios from [Sony Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Pictures "Sony Pictures") and [Columbia Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Pictures "Columbia Pictures") in May 2013. Those companies produced the films *[Ghost Rider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_Rider_(2007_film) "Ghost Rider (2007 film)")* (2007) and *[Ghost Rider: Spirit of Vengeance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_Rider:_Spirit_of_Vengeance "Ghost Rider: Spirit of Vengeance")* (2011), both starring [Nicolas Cage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Cage "Nicolas Cage") as the [Johnny Blaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Blaze "Johnny Blaze") incarnation of the character. After [Ryan Gosling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Gosling "Ryan Gosling") publicly expressed interest in portraying Ghost Rider in the MCU in 2022, Feige suggested that he was open to that. At SDCC in July 2026, Gosling was confirmed to be starring in a new *Ghost Rider* film to be directed by [Shawn Levy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawn_Levy "Shawn Levy") and written by [Jonathan Tropper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Tropper "Jonathan Tropper"). Levy previously directed *Deadpool & Wolverine* for Marvel Studios, and worked with Gosling and Tropper on the film *[Star Wars: Starfighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars:_Starfighter "Star Wars: Starfighter")* (2027). Gosling was reported to be portraying [Johnny Blaze / Ghost Rider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Blaze_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Johnny Blaze (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"). The film is scheduled to be released on July 28, 2028.

#### *Black Panther III* (2028)

By November 2022, writer and director [Ryan Coogler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Coogler "Ryan Coogler") had discussed a potential third *Black Panther* film with Feige. Former Marvel Studios executive Nate Moore–who left the studio in March 2025–returned to produce the film alongside Marvel Studios. In November 2025, Coogler confirmed that *Black Panther III* would be his next film and said development had begun. At SDCC in July 2026, Feige and Coogler announced that [David Jonsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Jonsson "David Jonsson") had been cast as an adult version of [T'Challa II / Toussaint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%27Challa_II "T'Challa II"), who was introduced as a child in the film *[Black Panther: Wakanda Forever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Panther:_Wakanda_Forever "Black Panther: Wakanda Forever")* (2022) and would take on the mantle of Black Panther in *Black Panther III*. The film is scheduled for release on December 15, 2028.

#### *Armor Wars*

[James Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Rhodes_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "James Rhodes (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") must confront one of [Tony Stark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Stark_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Tony Stark (Marvel Cinematic Universe)")'s greatest fears when Stark's tech falls into the wrong hands.

In December 2020, Marvel Studios announced *Armor Wars* as a [Disney+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney%2B "Disney+") series based on the 1987–1988 comic book storyline "[Armor Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armor_Wars "Armor Wars")", with [Don Cheadle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Cheadle "Don Cheadle") reprising his role as James Rhodes / War Machine. In August 2021, [Yassir Lester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yassir_Lester "Yassir Lester") was hired as the series' [head writer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_writer "Head writer"). In September 2022, Marvel Studios decided to rework the series into a feature film, with Cheadle and Lester remaining with the project. Development had slowed down by February 2025.

*Armor Wars* is set after the events of *[Secret Invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_Invasion_(miniseries) "Secret Invasion (miniseries)")* (2023). [Walton Goggins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walton_Goggins "Walton Goggins") was set to reprise his role as [Sonny Burch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonny_Burch_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Sonny Burch (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") from *[Ant-Man and the Wasp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant-Man_and_the_Wasp "Ant-Man and the Wasp")* (2018).

#### Untitled Nova film

A television series featuring the character [Richard Rider / Nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_(Richard_Rider) "Nova (Richard Rider)") was revealed to be in development in March 2022, with [Sabir Pirzada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabir_Pirzada "Sabir Pirzada") writing. Development had slowed down by February 2023 amid Disney's larger decision to scale back content output and costs. In July 2024, Feige confirmed the series would feature Rider and anticipated that it would be released three or four years later. That December, [Ed Bernero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Bernero "Ed Bernero") was hired as writer and showrunner, but the series was put on pause in February 2025. [Michael Waldron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Waldron "Michael Waldron") was revealed in July 2026 to be writing a script for a Nova film that was in early development as a reworking of the series. Waldron was also considering directing the film if it were to move forward.

#### Untitled *Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings* sequel

In December 2021, a sequel to *[Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang-Chi_and_the_Legend_of_the_Ten_Rings "Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings")* (2021) was announced to be in development, with [Destin Daniel Cretton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destin_Daniel_Cretton "Destin Daniel Cretton") returning to write and direct. [Simu Liu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simu_Liu "Simu Liu") was expected to return as [Shang-Chi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang-Chi_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Shang-Chi (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") by the following month. In September 2024, Cretton was chosen to direct *Brand New Day*, which was a higher priority for Marvel Studios than the *Shang-Chi* sequel. In June 2026, Cretton said that they were still developing the sequel and attributed its long development to [the impacts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_on_cinema "Impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on cinema") of the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"). The following month, Cretton confirmed that [Dave Callaham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Callaham "Dave Callaham"), who was a writer on the first film, was also writing on the sequel.

## Release

### Theatrical distribution

Over time, the [distribution rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_rights "Distribution rights") to Marvel Studios' films have changed hands on multiple occasions. In November 2006, [Universal Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures "Universal Pictures") announced that it would distribute *[The Incredible Hulk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Incredible_Hulk_(film) "The Incredible Hulk (film)")* (2008), in an arrangement separate from Marvel's 2005 deal with Paramount, which was distributing Marvel's other films. In September 2008, after the international success of *[Iron Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Man_(2008_film) "Iron Man (2008 film)")* (2008), Paramount signed a deal to have worldwide distribution rights for *[Iron Man 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Man_2 "Iron Man 2")* (2010), *[Iron Man 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Man_3 "Iron Man 3")* (2013), *[Thor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thor_(film) "Thor (film)")* (2011), *[Captain America: The First Avenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_America:_The_First_Avenger "Captain America: The First Avenger")* (2011), and *[The Avengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Avengers_(2012_film) "The Avengers (2012 film)")* (2012).

In late December 2009, [the Walt Disney Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company "The Walt Disney Company") purchased Marvel Entertainment for $4 billion. Additionally, in October 2010, [Walt Disney Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Studios_(division) "Walt Disney Studios (division)") bought the distribution rights for *The Avengers* and *Iron Man 3* from Paramount Pictures, with Paramount's logo remaining on the films, as well as for promotional material and merchandise, although [Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Studios_Motion_Pictures "Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures") is the only studio credited at the end of these films. Disney has distributed all subsequent Marvel Studios films. In July 2013, Disney purchased the distribution rights to *Iron Man*, *Iron Man 2*, *Thor* and *Captain America: The First Avenger* from Paramount. *The Incredible Hulk* was not part of the deal at the time, due to an agreement between Marvel and Universal, where Marvel owns the [film rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_rights "Film rights") and Universal owns the distribution rights, for this film as well as the [right of first refusal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_of_first_refusal "Right of first refusal") to distribute future [Hulk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulk "Hulk") films. According to *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")*, a potential reason why Marvel has not bought the film distribution rights to the Hulk as they did with Paramount for the Iron Man, Thor, and Captain America films is that Universal holds [the theme park rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Parks_%26_Resorts "Universal Parks & Resorts") to several Marvel characters that Disney wants for [its own theme parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Parks_and_Resorts "Walt Disney Parks and Resorts"). In June 2023, the distribution rights to *The Incredible Hulk* reverted from Universal back to Marvel Studios and Disney.

In April 2026, Disney announced their "[Infinity Vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_Vision "Infinity Vision")" certification for [premium large formats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premium_large_format "Premium large format"), informing audiences which theaters offer "the biggest, brightest, and most immersive cinematic experiences" and meet certain technical standards. The re-release of *Endgame* in September 2026 will be Marvel Studios' first film in Infinity Vision, followed by *Doomsday* in December 2026.

#### Spider-Man films

In February 2015, [Sony Pictures Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Pictures_Entertainment "Sony Pictures Entertainment") and Marvel Studios announced a licensing deal that would allow Spider-Man to appear in the Marvel Cinematic Universe, with the character first appearing in *Captain America: Civil War*. *Spider-Man* films produced by Marvel Studios would continue to be financed, distributed, and controlled by Sony Pictures. In June 2015, Feige clarified that the initial Sony deal does not apply to the [MCU television series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Marvel_Cinematic_Universe_television_series_(Marvel_Studios) "List of Marvel Cinematic Universe television series (Marvel Studios)"), as it was "very specific ... with a certain amount of back and forth allowed". Both studios can terminate the agreement at any point, and no money was exchanged with the deal. However, a small adjustment was made to a 2011 deal formed between the two studios (where Marvel gained full control of Spider-Man's merchandising rights, in exchange for making a one-time payment of $175 million to Sony and paying up to $35 million for each future Spider-Man film, and forgoing receiving their previous 5% of any Spider-Man film's revenue), with Marvel getting to reduce their $35 million payment to Sony if *Spider-Man: Homecoming* grossed more than $750 million. Marvel Studios still received 5% of first dollar gross for the film. Sony also paid Marvel Studios an undisclosed producer fee for *Homecoming*.

In August 2019, it was reported that Disney and Sony could not reach a new agreement regarding Spider-Man films, with Marvel Studios and Feige said to no longer have any involvement in future films. *[Deadline Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadline_Hollywood "Deadline Hollywood")* noted that Disney had hoped future films would be a "50/50 co-financing arrangement between the studios", with the possibility to extend the deal to other Spider-Man-related films, an offer Sony rejected and did not counter. Instead, Sony hoped to keep the terms of the previous agreement (Marvel receiving 5% of the film's first dollar gross), with Disney refusing. *The Hollywood Reporter* added that the lack of a new agreement would see the end of Holland's [Spider-Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Parker_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Peter Parker (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") in the MCU. *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* cited unnamed sources claiming negotiations had "hit an impasse" and that a new deal could still be reached. In September 2019, it was announced that Disney and Sony had reached a new agreement allowing for Spider-Man to appear in *[Spider-Man: No Way Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man:_No_Way_Home "Spider-Man: No Way Home")* (2021) as the third film [co-produced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-production_(media) "Co-production (media)") by Marvel Studios and Sony Pictures and a future Marvel Studios film. Disney was reported to be co-financing 25% of the film in exchange for 25% of the film's profits in the new agreement, while retaining the merchandising rights to the character.

In November 2021, producer [Amy Pascal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Pascal "Amy Pascal") revealed that Sony and Marvel Studios were planning to make at least three more Spider-Man films starring Holland, with work on the first of those films getting ready to begin. However, *The Hollywood Reporter* noted that there were no official plans for a new trilogy, despite the strong working relationship between the studios. The following month, Feige said that he, Pascal, Disney, and Sony were "actively beginning to develop" the next Spider-Man story, assuring that there would not be any "separation trauma" that occurred between *Far From Home* and *No Way Home*. Sony's agreement specifies that production has to start on a film within three years and nine months of the previous one, and release within five years and nine months, otherwise the rights revert to Marvel. Sony and Marvel's agreement occurs on a film-by-film basis.

Marvel Studios explored opportunities to integrate other characters of the Marvel Cinematic Universe into their *Spider-Man* films, with [Robert Downey Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Downey_Jr. "Robert Downey Jr.") reprising his role as [Tony Stark / Iron Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Stark_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Tony Stark (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") in *[Spider-Man: Homecoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man:_Homecoming "Spider-Man: Homecoming")* (2017), [Samuel L. Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_L._Jackson "Samuel L. Jackson") reprising his role as [Nick Fury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Fury_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Nick Fury (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") in *[Spider-Man: Far From Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man:_Far_From_Home "Spider-Man: Far From Home")* (2019), and [Benedict Cumberbatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedict_Cumberbatch "Benedict Cumberbatch") as Dr. [Stephen Strange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Strange_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Stephen Strange (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") in *No Way Home*. ### Home media

#### Physical

In June 2012, Marvel announced a 10-disc [box set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box_set "Box set") titled "Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase One – Avengers Assembled", for release on September 25, 2012. The box set includes all six of the Phase One films—*Iron Man*, *The Incredible Hulk*, *Iron Man 2*, *Thor*, *Captain America: The First Avenger*, and *The Avengers*—on [Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blu-ray_Disc "Blu-ray Disc") and Blu-ray 3D, in a replica of Nick Fury's briefcase from *The Avengers*. In August 2012, luggage company Rimowa GmbH, who developed the briefcase for *The Avengers*, filed suit against Marvel Studios and [Buena Vista Home Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Studios_Home_Entertainment "Walt Disney Studios Home Entertainment") in U.S. federal court, complaining that "Marvel did not obtain any license or authorization from Rimowa to make replica copies of the cases for any purpose." The set was delayed to early 2013 for the packaging to be redesigned. The box set, with a redesigned case, was released on April 2, 2013. In addition, the box set included a featurette on the then-upcoming Phase Two films, showing footage and concept art, as well as previously unreleased deleted scenes from all of the Phase One films.

In July 2015, Marvel announced a 13-disc box set titled "Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Two Collection", for release on December 8, 2015, exclusive to [Amazon.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon.com "Amazon.com"). The box set includes all six of the Phase Two films—*Iron Man 3*, *Thor: The Dark World*, *Captain America: The Winter Soldier*, *Guardians of the Galaxy*, *Avengers: Age of Ultron*, and *Ant-Man*—on Blu-ray, Blu-ray 3D and a digital copy, in a replica of the Orb from *Guardians of the Galaxy*, plus a bonus disc and exclusive memorabilia. Material on the bonus disc includes all of the [Marvel One-Shots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_One-Shots "Marvel One-Shots") with commentary, deleted scenes and pre-production creative features for each of the films, featurettes on the making of the post-credit scenes for the films, and first looks at *Captain America: Civil War*, *Doctor Strange*, and *Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 2*.

In September 2019, Feige indicated a box set with all 23 films of "The Infinity Saga" would be released, with the set including previously unreleased deleted scenes and other footage, such as an alternate take of the Nick Fury post-credits scene from *Iron Man* which references Spider-Man, the Hulk, and the X-Men. The box set, featuring all 23 films on Ultra HD Blu-ray and Blu-ray, a bonus disc, a letter from Feige, and a lithograph art piece by Matt Ferguson, was released on November 15, 2019, exclusively at [Best Buy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Buy "Best Buy").

#### Streaming and cable

In March 2008, Marvel Studios presold the US cable broadcast rights to [FX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FX_(TV_channel) "FX (TV channel)") for five of their films, including *Iron Man* and *The Incredible Hulk*, for four years. FX also acquired the rights to *Iron Man 3* in May 2013. In September 2014, [TNT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNT_(American_TV_network) "TNT (American TV network)") acquired the US cable broadcast rights to five Marvel Studios films, beginning with *Avengers: Age of Ultron*, for broadcast two years after their theatrical release.

Every Marvel Studios release from January 2016 to December 2018 was available on [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix"). *Captain Marvel* was the first [Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Studios_Motion_Pictures "Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures")-distributed film not to stream on Netflix, after Disney let their licensing deal with them expire. It became the first theatrical Disney release to stream exclusively on [Disney+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney%2B "Disney+"), which launched on November 12, 2019. *[Bloomberg News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_News "Bloomberg News")* reported that the films part of Disney's agreement with Netflix would return to Netflix starting in 2026, while being removed from Disney+.

In April 2021, Sony signed a deal with Disney for its theatrical releases from 2022 to 2026 to stream on Disney+ and [Hulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulu "Hulu") and appear on Disney's linear television networks for their "pay 2 window". As well, Sony's legacy content, including past Spider-Man films and Marvel content in [Sony's Spider-Man Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony%27s_Spider-Man_Universe "Sony's Spider-Man Universe") (SSU), would be able to be streamed on Disney+ and Hulu. Disney's access to Sony's titles would come following their availability on Netflix for their "pay 1 window". *Homecoming* and *[Spider-Man: Far From Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man:_Far_From_Home "Spider-Man: Far From Home")* (2019) had previously been available on [Starz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starz "Starz") and FX. *Homecoming* became available on Disney+ in the United States on May 12, 2023, *Far From Home* became available on November 3, 2023, and *No Way Home* became available on April 15, 2026. *The Incredible Hulk* became available on Disney+ in the United States on June 16, 2023, following the film's distribution rights reverting to Marvel Studios.

In January 2026, Sony and Netflix signed a new multi-year agreement for Sony titles to stream on Netflix in their "pay 1 window". The new agreement gives Netflix global rights, an increase from the "pay 1" rights for the United States, Germany, and Southeast Asia in the previous deal, with the new agreement beginning later in 2026 as territory rights become available before being fully in effect in early 2029.

### IMAX 10th anniversary festival

From August 30 to September 6, 2018, in conjunction with Marvel Studios' 10-year anniversary celebrations, all 20 films released at the time (*Iron Man* through *Ant-Man and the Wasp*) were screened in [IMAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAX "IMAX"). The films were shown in release order, with four films per day. The final days of the festival were theme-related, with one showing "origin" films (*Iron Man*, *Spider-Man: Homecoming*, *Black Panther*, and *Doctor Strange*), one showing "team-ups" (*Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 2*, *Captain America: Civil War*, *The Avengers*, and *Avengers: Infinity War*), and the final day showing *Iron Man* and *The Avengers* as chosen by the fans via a [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter") poll. The festival also saw *Iron Man*, *The Incredible Hulk*, and *Captain America: The First Avenger* released in IMAX for the first time.

## Reception

### Box office performance

The Marvel Cinematic Universe is the highest-grossing film franchise of all time worldwide, both unadjusted and [adjusted-for-inflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjusted-for-inflation "Adjusted-for-inflation"), having grossed over $35.1 billion at the global box office. Several of its sub-series such as the *[Avengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers_(comics)_in_other_media "Avengers (comics) in other media")*, *[Iron Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Man_in_other_media "Iron Man in other media")*, *[Captain America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_America_in_other_media "Captain America in other media")*, *[Thor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thor_(Marvel_Comics)_in_other_media "Thor (Marvel Comics) in other media")*, and *[Spider-Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man_in_other_media "Spider-Man in other media")* film series are among the [most successful film series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_films#Highest-grossing_franchises_and_film_series "List of highest-grossing films") of all time. From July 2019 to March 2021, *Avengers: Endgame* was the [highest-grossing film of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_films#Highest-grossing_films "List of highest-grossing films"). With the release of *[Deadpool & Wolverine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadpool_%26_Wolverine "Deadpool & Wolverine")* (2024), the MCU became the first film franchise to cross $30 billion.

### Accolades

The films of the Marvel Cinematic Universe have been nominated for numerous awards, including 27 [Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards") (winning four).

## Repurposed and abandoned projects

The following projects were in development as films from Marvel Studios before becoming television series under [Marvel Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Television "Marvel Television"):

* ***Runaways***: A film based on the *[Runaways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runaways_(comics) "Runaways (comics)")* went through a number of iterations. [Brian K. Vaughan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_K._Vaughan "Brian K. Vaughan") was originally hired to write a screenplay based on the property in May 2008. Feige and Marvel Studios producer Jodi Hildebrand envisioned the film as a [coming-of-age story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coming-of-age_story "Coming-of-age story") in the style of director [John Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hughes_(filmmaker) "John Hughes (filmmaker)"). In April 2010, Marvel hired [Peter Sollett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Sollett "Peter Sollett") to direct the film, and [Drew Pearce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drew_Pearce "Drew Pearce") was hired to write a script in May. The film was developed under the working title *Small Faces*, referencing the 1960s rock band [Small Faces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_Faces "Small Faces"). In October 2010, development on the film was put on hold, with Vaughan later noting Marvel Studios had decided to focus their efforts on a *Guardians of the Galaxy* film instead. Pearce revealed in September 2013 that the *Runaways* film had been shelved in favor of *The Avengers*, with the earliest it could release being Phase Three. Speaking in the book *MCU: The Reign of Marvel Studios* (2023), producer [Craig Kyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Kyle "Craig Kyle") noted that the film had been shelved because the New York-based Creative Committee did not believe the film's character make up fit their ideal demographic of what would help sell toys. In October 2014, after announcing all of Marvel's Phase Three films without *Runaways*, Feige stated the project was "still an awesome script that exists in our script vault", adding, "We'd love to do something with *Runaways* some day. In our television and future film discussions, it's always one that we talk about, because we have a solid draft there. But again, we can't make them all." In August 2016, Marvel Television announced *[Marvel's Runaways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runaways_(TV_series) "Runaways (TV series)")* from the streaming service [Hulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulu "Hulu"), with the series receiving a full season order in May 2017. It premiered in November 2017. Hulu announced in November 2019 that the third season of *Runaways* would be its last.
* ***Inhumans***: In April 2013, Feige mentioned the [Inhumans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhumans "Inhumans") as a property out of which he was "confident" a film would be made. Inhumans as a concept would first be introduced to the MCU in 2014 through the [second season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agents_of_S.H.I.E.L.D._season_2 "Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D. season 2") of the television series *[Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agents_of_S.H.I.E.L.D. "Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D.")* By August 2014, the studio was ready to move forward in development with the film, with a screenplay written by Joe Robert Cole. In October 2014, the film was announced for Phase Three and scheduled for release in July 2019. By October 2015, Cole was no longer involved with the film and any potential drafts that he may have written would not be used. In April 2016, *Inhumans* was removed from the release schedule, and would no longer be a part of Phase Three. In July 2016, Feige said *Inhumans* would "certainly" be a part of the discussion regarding the film ideas for 2020 and 2021, adding the following November that he was still optimistic the film could be released in Phase Four. In November 2016, Marvel Television announced the series *[Marvel's Inhumans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhumans_(TV_series) "Inhumans (TV series)")*, which premiered on [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company "American Broadcasting Company") in September 2017, after [the first two episodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhumans_premiere "Inhumans premiere") were screened in [IMAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAX "IMAX"). The series was not intended to be a reworking of the film. ABC canceled *Inhumans* after one season in May 2018.

Additionally, this film was in development from Marvel Studios but has since been canceled:

* ***Blade***: Following [New Line Cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Line_Cinema "New Line Cinema")'s [*Blade* film trilogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blade_(franchise) "Blade (franchise)") (1998–2004), Marvel Studios regained the film rights to the character [Blade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blade_(character) "Blade (character)") by July 2011 and was developing a new version by May 2013. [Mahershala Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahershala_Ali "Mahershala Ali"), who played [Cornell "Cottonmouth" Stokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_Stokes_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Cornell Stokes (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") in [Marvel Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Television "Marvel Television")'s *[Luke Cage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke_Cage_(TV_series) "Luke Cage (TV series)")* (2016–18), approached Marvel Studios in February 2019 about starring in a new Blade film, which Feige announced as *[Blade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blade_(unproduced_film) "Blade (unproduced film)")* at SDCC in July, with Ali as [Blade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blade_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Blade (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"); he first had an uncredited voice-only cameo in the film *[Eternals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternals_(film) "Eternals (film)")* (2021). Multiple filmmakers were attached in the following years—including directors [Bassam Tariq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bassam_Tariq "Bassam Tariq") and [Yann Demange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yann_Demange "Yann Demange"), and writers [Stacy Osei-Kuffour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stacy_Osei-Kuffour "Stacy Osei-Kuffour"), [Beau DeMayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beau_DeMayo "Beau DeMayo"), [Michael Starrbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Starrbury "Michael Starrbury"), [Nic Pizzolatto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nic_Pizzolatto "Nic Pizzolatto"), [Michael Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Green_(writer) "Michael Green (writer)"), and Eric Pearson—while the production experienced several delays. *Blade* was removed from the release schedule in October 2024, but remained in development. By July 2026, Ali had moved on from the role and it was believed to have been canceled.

## Connections with other *Spider-Man* franchises

Following Marvel Studios and Sony Pictures' September 2019 agreement, Feige noted that as Sony continued to separately build their own shared universe, Sony's Spider-Man Universe (SSU), it was possible the MCU version of Spider-Man could appear in that universe. This interaction was said to be "a 'call and answer' between the two franchises as they acknowledge details between the two in what ... would loosely be described as a shared detailed universe". In May 2021, Adam B. Vary of *Variety* called the connections between the two universes perplexing, specifically because if Holland were to appear in an SSU film it would retroactively make any previous SSU films part of the MCU, and because a teaser trailer for the SSU film *[Morbius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morbius_(film) "Morbius (film)")* (2022) had featured [Michael Keaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Keaton "Michael Keaton"), who previously played [Adrian Toomes / Vulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Toomes_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Adrian Toomes (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") in *Spider-Man: Homecoming*. Sony Pictures Group President [Sanford Panitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanford_Panitch "Sanford Panitch") acknowledged this confusion and said there was a plan to clarify the relationship between the two universes. He believed it was already "getting a little more clear for people [as to] where we're headed" at that time and added that the release of *Spider-Man: No Way Home* in December 2021 would reveal more of this plan. Vary commented that the apparent introduction of multiverse elements in *No Way Home* could be what would allow Holland to appear in both the MCU and the SSU. The following month, Feige said he would not "rule anything out completely" in terms of additional Sony-controlled characters appearing in Marvel Studios films.

In *No Way Home*, Stephen Strange casts two spells: one that brings characters from other universes into the MCU and one that sends them back to their own universes. These characters, as depicted in the film, are [Tobey Maguire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobey_Maguire "Tobey Maguire") and [Andrew Garfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Garfield "Andrew Garfield") returning as their versions of Spider-Man from [Sam Raimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Raimi "Sam Raimi")'s [*Spider-Man* trilogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Parker_(2002_film_series_character) "Peter Parker (2002 film series character)") and [Marc Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Webb "Marc Webb")'s [*The Amazing Spider-Man* films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Parker_(The_Amazing_Spider-Man_film_series) "Peter Parker (The Amazing Spider-Man film series)"), respectively, alongside [Willem Dafoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_Dafoe "Willem Dafoe") as [Norman Osborn / Green Goblin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Osborn_(2002_film_series_character) "Norman Osborn (2002 film series character)"), [Alfred Molina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Molina "Alfred Molina") as [Otto Octavius / Doctor Octopus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Octavius_(film_character) "Otto Octavius (film character)"), and [Thomas Haden Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Haden_Church "Thomas Haden Church") as [Flint Marko / Sandman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flint_Marko_(Sam_Raimi_film_series) "Flint Marko (Sam Raimi film series)") from the Raimi films, as well as [Rhys Ifans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhys_Ifans "Rhys Ifans") as [Curt Connors / Lizard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curt_Connors_(The_Amazing_Spider-Man_film_series) "Curt Connors (The Amazing Spider-Man film series)") and [Jamie Foxx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Foxx "Jamie Foxx") as [Max Dillon / Electro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Dillon_(film_character) "Max Dillon (film character)") from the Webb films. The mid-credits scene of the SSU film *[Venom: Let There Be Carnage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venom:_Let_There_Be_Carnage "Venom: Let There Be Carnage")* (2021) shows [Eddie Brock and Venom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venom_(Sony%27s_Spider-Man_Universe) "Venom (Sony's Spider-Man Universe)") ([Tom Hardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Hardy "Tom Hardy")) being transported into the MCU from their universe by the first spell and the mid-credits scene of *No Way Home* shows them being transported back to their own universe by the second spell. A small part of the Venom symbiote is left in the MCU. Feige said there was a lot of coordination between the *Let There Be Carnage* and *No Way Home* teams to create the two scenes, with *No Way Home* director Jon Watts directing both scenes during production of that film. The mid-credits scenes of *Morbius* revealed that Toomes was accidentally transported from the MCU to the SSU following Strange's second spell. The events of *No Way Home* are referenced in the animated film *[Spider-Man: Across the Spider-Verse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man:_Across_the_Spider-Verse "Spider-Man: Across the Spider-Verse")* (2023), which refers to the MCU as "Earth-199999", its designation in the Marvel Comics multiverse. In March 2025, Hardy said a crossover between his Venom and Holland's Spider-Man had been discussed beyond his appearance in *No Way Home*, but this did not eventuate and was never in development as a film. Holland echoed Hardy's comments in July 2026, saying there had been plans to merge the SSU with the MCU, but Sony never found the proper time to attempt that. Holland added that Sony Pictures chairman [Tom Rothman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Rothman "Tom Rothman") saw the success of the MCU films and decided for Marvel Studios to "continue on our path" with the character.

In July 2025, Feige said Sony had told Marvel Studios to "stay away" from [Miles Morales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Morales "Miles Morales"), the main character of the animated [*Spider-Verse* film series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Verse_(franchise) "Spider-Verse (franchise)"), until Sony had finished making those films.
