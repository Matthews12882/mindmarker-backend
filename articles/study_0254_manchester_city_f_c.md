# Manchester City F.C.

**Manchester City Football Club**, commonly referred to as **Man City** or simply **City**, is a professional [football club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_club_(association_football) "Football club (association football)") based in [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester "Manchester"), England, that competes in the [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_League "Premier League"), the [top flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_football_league_system "English football league system") of [English football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_England "Football in England"). Founded in 1880 as St. Mark's (West Gorton), they became Ardwick Association Football Club in 1887 and Manchester City in 1894. The club's home ground is the [City of Manchester Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Manchester_Stadium "City of Manchester Stadium") in east Manchester, to which they moved in 2003, having played at [Maine Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine_Road "Maine Road") since 1923. Manchester City adopted their [sky blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_blue "Sky blue") home shirts in 1894, the first season with the current name.

The club joined the [Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Football_League "English Football League") in [1892](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1892%E2%80%9393_Football_League "1892–93 Football League"), and won their first major honour, the [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1903%E2%80%9304_FA_Cup "1903–04 FA Cup"), in [1904](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1904_FA_Cup_final "1904 FA Cup final"). Manchester City had its first major period of success in the late 1960s and early 1970s, winning the [league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967%E2%80%9368_Football_League_First_Division "1967–68 Football League First Division") title, [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968%E2%80%9369_FA_Cup "1968–69 FA Cup"), [League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969%E2%80%9370_Football_League_Cup "1969–70 Football League Cup"), and [European Cup Winners Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969%E2%80%9370_European_Cup_Winners%27_Cup "1969–70 European Cup Winners' Cup") under the management of [Joe Mercer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Mercer "Joe Mercer") and [Malcolm Allison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Allison "Malcolm Allison"). After losing the [1981 FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_FA_Cup_final "1981 FA Cup final"), the club went through a period of decline, being [relegated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promotion_and_relegation "Promotion and relegation") to the third tier of English football for the only time in their history in [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997%E2%80%9398_Football_League_First_Division "1997–98 Football League First Division"). They regained promotion to the top tier in [1999–2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999%E2%80%932000_Football_League#First_Division "1999–2000 Football League") and have remained in the Premier League since [2002–03](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002%E2%80%9303_FA_Premier_League "2002–03 FA Premier League").

Manchester City received major financial investment after its August 2008 takeover by [Sheikh Mansour bin Zayed Al Nahyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansour_bin_Zayed_Al_Nahyan "Mansour bin Zayed Al Nahyan") through the [Abu Dhabi United Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Dhabi_United_Group "Abu Dhabi United Group"), starting a new era of success, winning the FA Cup in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_FA_Cup_final "2011 FA Cup final") and the Premier League in [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_Premier_League "2011–12 Premier League"). Under the stewardship of [Pep Guardiola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pep_Guardiola "Pep Guardiola") between 2016 and 2026, the club won 20 honours, including the Premier League in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Premier_League "2017–18 Premier League") with a record [100 points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_League_records_and_statistics#Points "Premier League records and statistics"), becoming the first English men's team to win a domestic treble, claiming the three main domestic trophies as well as the [Community Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Community_Shield "FA Community Shield"). The club also won a [continental treble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treble_(association_football)#Continental_trebles "Treble (association football)") in 2023, and four consecutive league titles between the [2020–21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Premier_League "2020–21 Premier League") and [2023–24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Premier_League "2023–24 Premier League") seasons – an English football record.

Manchester City topped the [Deloitte Football Money League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deloitte_Football_Money_League "Deloitte Football Money League") at the end of the 2021–22 season, making it the football club with the highest revenue in the world, approximated at €731 million. In 2022, *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes "Forbes")* estimated the club was the [sixth-most valuable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_list_of_the_most_valuable_football_clubs "Forbes list of the most valuable football clubs") in the world, worth [$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar "United States dollar")4.250 billion. Manchester City are owned by [City Football Group Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Football_Group "City Football Group"), a [holding company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holding_company "Holding company") valued at £3.73 ($4.8) billion in November 2019 and majority-owned by the Abu Dhabi United Group.

Manchester City have been accused of [breaching financial rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_City_F.C._financial_breaches_scandal "Manchester City F.C. financial breaches scandal") set by UEFA and the Premier League since 2018. A total of 115 breaches over nine seasons from 2009 to 2018 were investigated by the Premier League, which formed an independent commission. The independent commission found the club guilty of all but one of the charges in 2026.

## History

### Early years and first trophies

City gained their first honours by winning the [Second Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Second_Division "Football League Second Division") in [1899](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1898%E2%80%9399_Football_League#Second_Division "1898–99 Football League"); with it came promotion to the highest level in English football, the [First Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_First_Division "Football League First Division"). They went on to claim their first major honour on [23 April 1904](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1904_FA_Cup_final "1904 FA Cup final"), beating [Bolton Wanderers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolton_Wanderers_F.C. "Bolton Wanderers F.C.") 1–0 at [Crystal Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_Palace_National_Sports_Centre "Crystal Palace National Sports Centre") to win the [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup "FA Cup"); the Blues narrowly missed out on a [League and Cup double](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_(association_football)#England "Double (association football)") that season after finishing runners-up in the [league campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1903%E2%80%9304_Football_League#First_Division "1903–04 Football League"), but they still became the first club in Manchester to win a major honour. In the seasons following the FA Cup triumph, the club was dogged by allegations of financial irregularities, culminating in the suspension of seventeen players in 1906, including captain [Billy Meredith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Meredith "Billy Meredith"), who subsequently moved across town to [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C. "Manchester United F.C."). A fire at [Hyde Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyde_Road_(stadium) "Hyde Road (stadium)") destroyed the main stand in 1920, and in 1923 the club moved to their new purpose-built stadium at [Maine Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine_Road "Maine Road") in [Moss Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moss_Side "Moss Side").

In the 1930s, Manchester City reached two consecutive FA Cup finals, losing to [Everton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everton_F.C. "Everton F.C.") in [1933](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1933_FA_Cup_final "1933 FA Cup final"), before claiming the Cup by beating [Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_F.C. "Portsmouth F.C.") in [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FA_Cup_final "1934 FA Cup final"). During the 1934 run, the club broke the record for the [highest home attendance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_record_home_attendances_of_English_football_clubs "List of record home attendances of English football clubs") of any club in English football history, as 84,569 fans packed Maine Road for a sixth-round FA Cup tie against [Stoke City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoke_City_F.C. "Stoke City F.C.") – a record which stood until 2016. The club won the First Division title for the first time in 1937, but were relegated the following season, despite scoring more goals than any other team in the division. Twenty years later, a City team inspired by a tactical system known as the [Revie Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revie_Plan "Revie Plan") reached consecutive FA Cup finals again, in [1955](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1955_FA_Cup_final "1955 FA Cup final") and [1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_FA_Cup_final "1956 FA Cup final"); just as in the 1930s, they lost the first one, to [Newcastle United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_United_F.C. "Newcastle United F.C."), and won the second. The 1956 final, in which the Blues defeated [Birmingham City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_City_F.C. "Birmingham City F.C.") 3–1, saw City goalkeeper [Bert Trautmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bert_Trautmann "Bert Trautmann") continuing to play on after unknowingly breaking his neck.

### First golden era and subsequent decline

After being relegated to the Second Division in [1963](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962%E2%80%9363_Football_League_First_Division "1962–63 Football League First Division"), the future looked bleak with a record low home attendance of 8,015 against [Swindon Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swindon_Town_F.C. "Swindon Town F.C.") in January 1965. In the summer of 1965, the management team of [Joe Mercer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Mercer "Joe Mercer") and [Malcolm Allison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Allison "Malcolm Allison") was appointed. In the first season under Mercer, Manchester City won the [Second Division title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965%E2%80%9366_Football_League#Second_Division "1965–66 Football League") and made important signings in [Mike Summerbee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Summerbee "Mike Summerbee") and [Colin Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Bell_(footballer,_born_1946) "Colin Bell (footballer, born 1946)"). Two seasons later, in [1967–68](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967%E2%80%9368_Manchester_City_F.C._season "1967–68 Manchester City F.C. season"), City claimed the [league championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967%E2%80%9368_Football_League_First_Division "1967–68 Football League First Division") for the second time, beating their close neighbours Manchester United to the title on the final day of the season with a 4–3 victory at Newcastle United. Further trophies followed: City won the FA Cup in [1969](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_FA_Cup_final "1969 FA Cup final") and a year later triumphed in the [European Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969%E2%80%9370_European_Cup_Winners%27_Cup "1969–70 European Cup Winners' Cup"), defeating [Górnik Zabrze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B3rnik_Zabrze "Górnik Zabrze") 2–1 in the [1970 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_European_Cup_Winners%27_Cup_final "1970 European Cup Winners' Cup final"). This was the club's only European honour until their triumph in the [2022–23 UEFA Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_UEFA_Champions_League "2022–23 UEFA Champions League"). The Blues also won the [League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969%E2%80%9370_Football_League_Cup "1969–70 Football League Cup") that year, becoming the second English team to win a European trophy and a domestic trophy in the same season.

The club continued to challenge for honours throughout the 1970s, finishing one point behind the league champions on two occasions and reaching the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Football_League_Cup_final "1974 Football League Cup final") of the [1974 League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973%E2%80%9374_Football_League_Cup "1973–74 Football League Cup"). One of the matches from this period that is most fondly remembered by supporters of Manchester City is the final match of the [1973–74 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973%E2%80%9374_Football_League_First_Division "1973–74 Football League First Division") against [arch-rivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_derby "Manchester derby") Manchester United, who needed to win to have any hope of avoiding relegation. Former United player [Denis Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Law "Denis Law") scored with a backheel to give City a 1–0 win at Old Trafford and confirm the relegation of their rivals. The final trophy of the club's most successful period of the 20th century was won in 1976, when [Newcastle United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_United_F.C. "Newcastle United F.C.") were beaten 2–1 in the [League Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Football_League_Cup_final "1976 Football League Cup final").

A long period of decline followed the success of the 1960s and 1970s. Malcolm Allison rejoined the club to become manager for the second time in 1979, but squandered large sums of money on several unsuccessful signings, such as [Steve Daley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Daley "Steve Daley"). A succession of managers then followed – seven in the 1980s alone. Under [John Bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bond_(footballer) "John Bond (footballer)"), City reached the [1981 FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_FA_Cup_final "1981 FA Cup final") but lost in a replay to [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C. "Tottenham Hotspur F.C."). The club were twice relegated from the top flight in the 1980s (in [1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982%E2%80%9383_Football_League#First_Division "1982–83 Football League") and [1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986%E2%80%9387_Football_League#First_Division "1986–87 Football League")), but returned to the top flight again in [1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988%E2%80%9389_Football_League#Second_Division "1988–89 Football League") under [Mel Machin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mel_Machin "Mel Machin"). [Howard Kendall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Kendall "Howard Kendall") guided the club to top flight safety in [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989%E2%80%9390_Football_League_First_Division "1989–90 Football League First Division") and the club finished fifth in [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990%E2%80%9391_Football_League_First_Division "1990–91 Football League First Division") and [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991%E2%80%9392_Football_League_First_Division "1991–92 Football League First Division") under the management of active player [Peter Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Reid "Peter Reid"). However, this was only a temporary respite, and following Reid's departure Manchester City's fortunes continued to fade. City were co-founders of the [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_League "Premier League") upon its creation in [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992%E2%80%9393_FA_Premier_League "1992–93 FA Premier League"), but after finishing ninth in its first season, [Peter Swales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Swales "Peter Swales"), club chairman since 1973, was replaced by club legend [Francis Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Lee_(footballer) "Francis Lee (footballer)") in February 1994 in a movement supported by fans. Despite this, they endured three years of struggle under [Brian Horton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Horton "Brian Horton") and [Alan Ball Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Ball_Jr. "Alan Ball Jr.") before being relegated in dramatic fashion in [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995%E2%80%9396_FA_Premier_League "1995–96 FA Premier League"). After two seasons in the First Division and four different permanent managers, Lee resigned from his role as chairman midway through the [1998 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997%E2%80%9398_Football_League_First_Division "1997–98 Football League First Division"), although remained as a shareholder, as City fell to the lowest point in their history, becoming the second ever European trophy winners to be relegated to their country's third-tier league after [1. FC Magdeburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1._FC_Magdeburg "1. FC Magdeburg") of [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany").

### Recovery and two takeovers

After relegation, the club underwent off-the-field upheaval, with new chairman [David Bernstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bernstein_(executive) "David Bernstein (executive)") introducing greater fiscal discipline. Under manager [Joe Royle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Royle "Joe Royle"), City were promoted at the first attempt, achieved in dramatic fashion in the [Second Division play-off final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Football_League_Second_Division_play-off_final "1999 Football League Second Division play-off final") against [Gillingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillingham_F.C. "Gillingham F.C."). A [second successive promotion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999%E2%80%932000_Football_League#First_Division "1999–2000 Football League") saw City return to the top division, but this proved to have been a step too far for the recovering club, and in [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000%E2%80%9301_FA_Premier_League "2000–01 FA Premier League") City were relegated once more. [Kevin Keegan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Keegan "Kevin Keegan") replaced Royle as manager in the close season, and achieved an immediate return to the top division as the club won the [2001–02 First Division championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001%E2%80%9302_Football_League#First_Division "2001–02 Football League"), breaking club records for the number of points gained and goals scored in a single season in the process. The [2002–03 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002%E2%80%9303_Manchester_City_F.C._season "2002–03 Manchester City F.C. season") was the last at Maine Road and included a 3–1 derby victory over rivals Manchester United, ending a 13-year run without a [derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_derby "Manchester derby") win. Additionally, City qualified for European competition for the first time in 25 years via [UEFA fair play ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Respect_Fair_Play_ranking#Qualification_system "UEFA Respect Fair Play ranking"). In the close [2003–04 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003%E2%80%9304_Manchester_City_F.C._season "2003–04 Manchester City F.C. season"), the club moved to the new [City of Manchester Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Manchester_Stadium "City of Manchester Stadium"). The first four seasons at the stadium all resulted in mid-table finishes. Former [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_national_football_team "England national football team") manager [Sven-Göran Eriksson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sven-G%C3%B6ran_Eriksson "Sven-Göran Eriksson") became the club's first foreign manager when appointed in [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_Manchester_City_F.C._season "2007–08 Manchester City F.C. season"). After a bright start, performances faded in the second half of the season, and Eriksson was sacked on 2 June 2008; he was replaced by [Mark Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Hughes "Mark Hughes") two days later.

By 2008, Manchester City were in a financially precarious position. Former Thai prime minister [Thaksin Shinawatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thaksin_Shinawatra "Thaksin Shinawatra") had taken control of the club the year before, but his political travails saw his assets frozen. Then, in August 2008, City were purchased by the [Abu Dhabi United Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Dhabi_United_Group "Abu Dhabi United Group"). The takeover was immediately followed by a flurry of bids for high-profile players; the club broke the British transfer record by signing [Brazilian international](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_national_football_team "Brazil national football team") [Robinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robinho "Robinho") from [Real Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Madrid_CF "Real Madrid CF") for £32.5 million (€42.5 million). There was not a huge improvement in performance compared to the previous season despite the influx of money however, with the team [finishing tenth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_Premier_League "2008–09 Premier League"), although they did well to reach the quarter-finals of the [UEFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_UEFA_Cup "2008–09 UEFA Cup"). During the summer of 2009, the club took transfer spending to an unprecedented level, with an outlay of over £100 million on players [Gareth Barry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gareth_Barry "Gareth Barry"), [Roque Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_Santa_Cruz "Roque Santa Cruz"), [Kolo Touré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolo_Tour%C3%A9 "Kolo Touré"), [Emmanuel Adebayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Adebayor "Emmanuel Adebayor"), [Carlos Tevez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Tevez "Carlos Tevez"), and [Joleon Lescott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joleon_Lescott "Joleon Lescott"). In December 2009, Mark Hughes – who had been hired shortly before the change in ownership but was originally retained by the new board – was replaced as manager by [Roberto Mancini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Mancini "Roberto Mancini"). City finished the [season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_Manchester_City_F.C._season "2009–10 Manchester City F.C. season") in fifth position in the [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_Premier_League "2009–10 Premier League"), narrowly missing out on a place in the [Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_UEFA_Champions_League "2010–11 UEFA Champions League") but qualifying for the [UEFA Europa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_UEFA_Europa_League "2010–11 UEFA Europa League").

### Second golden era: Pep Guardiola years, financial and non-cooperation charges

Continued investment in players followed in successive seasons, and results began to match the upturn in player quality. After heavy speculation, Roberto Mancini confirmed that a move of [Edin Džeko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edin_D%C5%BEeko "Edin Džeko") from [Wolfsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VfL_Wolfsburg "VfL Wolfsburg") for a fee of £27 million (€32 million) had been agreed on 3 January 2011. This was City's second highest transfer figure, after Robinho's move from Real Madrid for £32.5 million in 2008. The transfer fee was the sixth highest in Premier League history at the time. City reached the [FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_FA_Cup_final "2011 FA Cup final") in 2011, their first major final in over 30 years, after defeating derby rivals Manchester United in the semi-finals, the first time they had knocked their rival out of a cup competition since 1975. The Blues defeated [Stoke City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoke_City_F.C. "Stoke City F.C.") 1–0 in the final, securing their fifth FA Cup and the club's first major trophy since winning the 1976 League Cup. On the last day of the [2010–11 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_Manchester_City_F.C._season "2010–11 Manchester City F.C. season"), City beat out [Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_F.C. "Arsenal F.C.") for the third place, thereby securing qualification directly into the Champions League group stage.

Strong performances continued to follow in the [2011–12 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_Manchester_City_F.C._season "2011–12 Manchester City F.C. season"), including a 5–1 victory over Tottenham at [White Hart Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Hart_Lane "White Hart Lane") and a record-equalling [6–1 win](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C._1%E2%80%936_Manchester_City_F.C. "Manchester United F.C. 1–6 Manchester City F.C.") over Manchester United at [Old Trafford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Trafford "Old Trafford"), but a poor run of form in the second half of the season left City in second place, eight points behind United with only six games left to play. At this point, United suffered their own loss of form, dropping eight points in the space of four games, while City began a run of successive wins which saw both teams level on points with two games to go. Despite the Blues only needing a home win against [Queens Park Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_Park_Rangers_F.C. "Queens Park Rangers F.C."), a team in the relegation zone, they fell 1–2 behind by the end of normal time. However, two goals in injury time – [the second by Sergio Agüero in the fourth added minute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_City_F.C._3%E2%80%932_Queens_Park_Rangers_F.C.#"That_Aguero_goal" "Manchester City F.C. 3–2 Queens Park Rangers F.C.") – settled the title in City's favour, making them the first team to win the Premier League on [goal difference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal_difference "Goal difference") alone.

The [following season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_Manchester_City_F.C._season "2012–13 Manchester City F.C. season"), City were unable to replicate the previous year's success. After finishing second in the [league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_Premier_League "2012–13 Premier League"), eleven points behind Manchester United, and losing the [FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_FA_Cup_final "2013 FA Cup final") 0–1 to relegated [Wigan Athletic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wigan_Athletic_A.F.C. "Wigan Athletic A.F.C."), Mancini was sacked. He was replaced by Chilean manager [Manuel Pellegrini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Pellegrini "Manuel Pellegrini"). In Pellegrini's [first year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Manchester_City_F.C._season "2013–14 Manchester City F.C. season") in charge, City won the [League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Football_League_Cup "2013–14 Football League Cup") and regained the [Premier League title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Premier_League "2013–14 Premier League") on the last matchday of the season. The team's league form then slowly declined over the next couple of years, as the Blues finished second in [2014–15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_Premier_League "2014–15 Premier League") and then dropped to fourth in [2015–16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Premier_League "2015–16 Premier League"), although the [2015–16 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Manchester_City_F.C._season "2015–16 Manchester City F.C. season") would see City win [another League Cup title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Football_League_Cup "2015–16 Football League Cup") and reach the [Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_UEFA_Champions_League "2015–16 UEFA Champions League") semi-finals for the first time.

[Pep Guardiola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pep_Guardiola "Pep Guardiola"), former head coach of [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Barcelona "FC Barcelona") and [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich "FC Bayern Munich"), was confirmed to become Manchester City's new manager on 1 February 2016, with the announcement having been made several months before Manuel Pellegrini left his position. Guardiola's [first season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_Manchester_City_F.C._season "2016–17 Manchester City F.C. season") in Manchester would end trophyless, with the Blues placing third in the [league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_Premier_League "2016–17 Premier League") standings, but the [following season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Manchester_City_F.C._season "2017–18 Manchester City F.C. season") proved far more successful, as City won the [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Premier_League "2017–18 Premier League") title with the highest points total in history and broke numerous other [club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Manchester_City_F.C._records_and_statistics "List of Manchester City F.C. records and statistics") and [English league records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_records_and_statistics_in_England "Football records and statistics in England") along the way.

This would prove to be the start of a period of unprecedented success for Manchester City under Guardiola. Between the 2017–18 and [2023–24 Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Premier_League "2023–24 Premier League") seasons, City won six out of possible seven league titles, only finishing second behind [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C. "Liverpool F.C.") in the [2019–20 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Premier_League "2019–20 Premier League"). Guardiola also guided the Blues to silverware in domestic cup competitions, highlighted by four consecutive League Cup triumphs in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_EFL_Cup_final "2018 EFL Cup final")–[2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_EFL_Cup_final "2021 EFL Cup final"). During the [2018–19 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Manchester_City_F.C._season "2018–19 Manchester City F.C. season"), City completed an unprecedented [domestic treble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treble_(association_football)#Domestic_trebles "Treble (association football)") of English men's titles. Apart from winning all three of the major English football tournaments, they also won the [Community Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FA_Community_Shield "2018 FA Community Shield"), the first time any team has ever held all four of England's primary football trophies at the same time. On the continental stage, the club achieved breakthrough in [2020–21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Manchester_City_F.C._season "2020–21 Manchester City F.C. season"), reaching their first-ever [Champions League final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_UEFA_Champions_League_final "2021 UEFA Champions League final"). In an all-English affair, City lost 0–1 to [Chelsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_F.C. "Chelsea F.C.") at the [Estádio do Dragão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_do_Drag%C3%A3o "Estádio do Dragão") in [Porto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto "Porto").

The [2022–23 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Manchester_City_F.C._season "2022–23 Manchester City F.C. season") turned out to be the greatest in the club's history, as Manchester City won their third consecutive [Premier League title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Premier_League "2022–23 Premier League"), the [FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_FA_Cup_final "2023 FA Cup final") against rivals Manchester United, and their maiden [Champions League title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_UEFA_Champions_League "2022–23 UEFA Champions League") at the [Atatürk Olympic Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atat%C3%BCrk_Olympic_Stadium "Atatürk Olympic Stadium") in [Istanbul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul "Istanbul") against [Inter Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Milan "Inter Milan"), thereby assembling a rare feat – the [continental treble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treble_(association_football)#Continental_trebles "Treble (association football)"). The road to the Champions League victory included wins over European giants Bayern Munich, who were defeated 4–1 on aggregate, and Real Madrid, who suffered a 1–5 aggregate loss at the hands of City. In the [following season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Manchester_City_F.C._season "2023–24 Manchester City F.C. season") City won the [UEFA Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_UEFA_Super_Cup "2023 UEFA Super Cup") and [FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_FIFA_Club_World_Cup "2023 FIFA Club World Cup") for the first time and became the first English men's club to claim four consecutive league titles with another [Premier League title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Premier_League "2023–24 Premier League"). City also advanced to the [FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_FA_Cup_final "2024 FA Cup final") for the second straight year but lost in a rematch to rivals Manchester United.

In the [2024–25 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Manchester_City_F.C._season "2024–25 Manchester City F.C. season") City managed to win only the [Community Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_FA_Community_Shield "2024 FA Community Shield") in another rematch against United. They lost that season's [FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FA_Cup_final "2025 FA Cup final") to [Crystal Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_Palace_F.C. "Crystal Palace F.C.") and finished third in the Premier League. In the following season, Manchester City finished runners-up in the [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Premier_League "2025–26 Premier League") in Guardiola's final season at the club.

Manchester City's era of sustained competitive excellence coincided with several charges of breaching [Financial Fair Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Financial_Fair_Play_Regulations "UEFA Financial Fair Play Regulations") (FFP) regulations. In 2020, the [Court of Arbitration for Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Arbitration_for_Sport "Court of Arbitration for Sport") (CAS) overturned a two-year ban from European competition placed on the club by [UEFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA "UEFA") for violations of FFP. In 2023, the Premier League announced it had charged the club with [115 breaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_City_F.C._financial_breaches_scandal "Manchester City F.C. financial breaches scandal") of its FFP rules, from 2009 until 2018, with all the charges, financial and non-cooperation with the investigation, running from 2009–10 to 2022–23; they were found guilty on all but one of the charges in 2026, with the Premier League announcing that the independent commission had found they had arranged "sham contracts" and "sham agreements to artificially inflate the club's revenues and reduce its costs"; — accounts which facilitated their extensive transfer spend on players. The club lodged an appeal against the guilty verdict, stating the commission's ruling contained "clear material errors, of law, principle and fact and is unsafe".

## Club badge and colours

Manchester City's home colours are sky blue and white. Traditional away [kit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kit_(football) "Kit (football)") colours have been either maroon or (from the 1960s) red and black; however, in recent years several colours have been used. The origins of the club's home colours are unclear, but there is evidence that the club has worn blue since 1892 or earlier. A booklet entitled *Famous Football Clubs – Manchester City* published in the 1940s indicates that West Gorton (St. Marks) originally played in scarlet and black, and reports dating from 1884 describe the team wearing black jerseys bearing a white cross, showing the club's origins as a church side. The infrequent yet recurrent use of red and black away colours comes from former assistant manager [Malcolm Allison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Allison "Malcolm Allison")'s belief that adopting the colours of [AC Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.C._Milan "A.C. Milan") would inspire City to glory. Allison's theory seemingly took effect, with City winning the [1969 FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_FA_Cup_final "1969 FA Cup final"), [1970 League Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_Football_League_Cup_final "1970 Football League Cup final"), and [1970 Cup Winners' Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_European_Cup_Winners%27_Cup_final "1970 European Cup Winners' Cup final") in red and black stripes as opposed to the club's home kit of sky blue.

City had previously worn three other [badges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crest_(sports) "Crest (sports)") on their shirts, prior to their current badge being implemented in 2016. The first, introduced in 1970, was based on designs which had been used on official club documentation since the mid-1960s. It consisted of a circular badge which used the same shield as the present badge (including a ship, based on the [City of Manchester coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbols_of_Manchester#Manchester_City_Council_coat_of_arms "Symbols of Manchester")), inside a circle bearing the name of the club. In 1972, this was replaced by a variation which replaced the lower half of the shield with the red rose of Lancashire. In 1976, a [heraldic badge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heraldic_badge "Heraldic badge") was granted by the [College of Arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Arms "College of Arms") to the [English Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Football_League "English Football League") for use by City. The badge consisted of the familiar ship above a red rose but on a circular device instead of a shield (blazoned as "*A roundel per fess azure and argent in chief a three masted ship sails set pennons flying or in base a rose gules barbed and seeded proper*").

On occasions when Manchester City played in a major cup final, the club wore shirts bearing the City of Manchester coat of arms, as a symbol of pride in representing the city at a major event. This practice originated from a time when the players' shirts did not normally bear a badge of any kind. The club has since abandoned the practice; for the [2011 FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_FA_Cup_final "2011 FA Cup final"), its first in the 21st century, City used the usual badge with a special legend, but the Manchester coat of arms was included as a small monochrome logo in the numbers on the back of players' shirts.

A new club badge was adopted in 1997, as a result of the previous badge being ineligible for registration as a trademark. This badge was based on the [arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms "Coat of arms") of the city of [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester "Manchester"), and consisted of a shield in front of a [golden eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_eagle "Golden eagle"). The eagle is an old heraldic symbol of the city of Manchester; a golden eagle was added to the city's badge in 1958 (but had since been removed), representing the growing aviation industry. The shield featured a ship on its upper half representing the [Manchester Ship Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Ship_Canal "Manchester Ship Canal"), and three diagonal stripes in the lower half symbolised the city's three rivers – the [Irwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Irwell "River Irwell"), the [Irk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Irk "River Irk") and the [Medlock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Medlock "River Medlock"). The bottom of the badge bore the motto "*Superbia in Proelio*", which translates as "Pride in Battle" in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language "Latin language"). Above the eagle and shield were the three stars, added for decorative purposes.

On 16 October 2015, following years of criticism from the fans over the design of the 1997 badge, the club announced they intended to carry out a fan consultation on whether to discontinue the current badge and institute a new design. After the consultation, the club announced in late November 2015 the badge would be replaced in due course by a new version which would be designed in the style of the older, circular variants. A design purporting to be the new badge was unintentionally leaked two days early prior to the official unveiling on 26 December 2015 by the [IPO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_Property_Office_(United_Kingdom) "Intellectual Property Office (United Kingdom)") when the design was trademarked on 22 December. The new badge was officially unveiled at Manchester City's home match against [Sunderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunderland_A.F.C. "Sunderland A.F.C.") on 26 December.

## Supporters

Since moving to the City of Manchester Stadium, the club's average attendances have been in the top six in England, usually in excess of 40,000. Even in the late 1990s, when City were relegated twice in three seasons and playing in the third tier of English football (then the [Second Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Second_Division "Football League Second Division"), now the [EFL League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_League_One "EFL League One")), home attendances were in the region of 30,000, compared to an average of fewer than 8,000 for the division. Research carried out by Manchester City in 2005 estimated a fanbase of 886,000 in the United Kingdom and a total in excess of 2 million worldwide, although since the purchase of the club by Sheikh Mansour, and City's recent achievements, that figure has since ballooned to many times that size.

Manchester City's officially recognised supporters club is the Manchester City F.C. Supporters Club (1949), formed by a merger of two existing organisations in 2010: the Official Supporters Club (OSC) and the Centenary Supporters Association (CSA). City fans' song of choice is a rendition of "[Blue Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Moon_(1934_song) "Blue Moon (1934 song)")", which despite its melancholic theme is belted out with gusto as though it were a heroic anthem. City supporters tend to believe that unpredictability is an inherent trait of their team, and label unexpected results "typical City". Events that fans regard as "typical City" include the club being the only reigning English champions ever to be relegated (in [1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1937%E2%80%9338_Football_League#First_Division "1937–38 Football League")), the only team to score and concede over 100 goals in the same season ([1957–58](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1957%E2%80%9358_Football_League#First_Division "1957–58 Football League")), or the more recent example where Manchester City were the only team to beat [Chelsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_F.C. "Chelsea F.C.") in the latter's record-breaking [2004–05 Premier League season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_FA_Premier_League "2004–05 FA Premier League"), yet in the same season City were knocked out of the [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_FA_Cup "2004–05 FA Cup") by [Oldham Athletic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldham_Athletic_A.F.C. "Oldham Athletic A.F.C."), a team two divisions lower.

In the late 1980s, City fans started a craze of bringing [inflatable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflatable "Inflatable") objects to matches, primarily oversized bananas. One disputed explanation for the phenomenon is that in a match against [West Bromwich Albion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bromwich_Albion_F.C. "West Bromwich Albion F.C."), chants from fans calling for the introduction of [Imre Varadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imre_Varadi "Imre Varadi") as a substitute mutated into "Imre Banana". Terraces packed with inflatable-waving supporters became a frequent sight in the [1988–89 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988%E2%80%9389_Football_League#First_Division "1988–89 Football League"), as the craze spread to other clubs (inflatable fish were seen at [Grimsby Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grimsby_Town_F.C. "Grimsby Town F.C.")), with the craze reaching its peak at City's match at [Stoke City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoke_City_F.C. "Stoke City F.C.") on 26 December 1988, a game declared by fanzines as a fancy dress party. In 2010, Manchester City supporters adopted an exuberant dance, dubbed [The Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pozna%C5%84 "The Poznań"), from fans of Polish club [Lech Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lech_Pozna%C5%84 "Lech Poznań") that they played in the [Europa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_UEFA_Europa_League "2010–11 UEFA Europa League"). In 2022, Manchester City proposed the release of the [Connected Scarf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_Scarf "Connected Scarf"), that would contain a sensor tracking physiological and emotional data of the wearer, for supporters in 2023.

## Rivalries

Manchester City's biggest rivalry is with neighbours [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C. "Manchester United F.C."), against whom they contest the [Manchester derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_derby "Manchester derby"). Before the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War "Second World War"), when travel to away games was rare, many Mancunian football fans regularly watched both teams even if considering themselves "supporters" of only one. This practice continued into the early 1960s but as travel became easier, and the cost of entry to matches rose, watching both teams became unusual and the rivalry intensified. A common stereotype is that City fans come from Manchester proper, while United fans come from elsewhere. A 2002 report by a researcher at [Manchester Metropolitan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Metropolitan_University "Manchester Metropolitan University") found that while it was true that a higher proportion of City season ticket holders came from Manchester postcode areas (40% compared to United's 29%), there were more United season ticket holders, the lower percentage being due to United's higher overall number of season ticket holders (27,667 compared to City's 16,481). The report noted that since the compiling of data in 2001, the number of both City and United season ticket holders had risen; expansion of United's ground and City's move to the City of Manchester Stadium have caused season ticket sales to increase further.

Over the last few years, Manchester City has also developed a [notable rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.%E2%80%93Manchester_City_F.C._rivalry "Liverpool F.C.–Manchester City F.C. rivalry") with [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C. "Liverpool F.C."), currently considered one of the biggest in association football. Though the two clubs had been involved in a title race in the [1976–77 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976%E2%80%9377_Football_League_First_Division "1976–77 Football League First Division"), Liverpool and City's modern rivalry began in the 2010s, with the Blues beating Liverpool to the [2013–14 title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Premier_League "2013–14 Premier League") by just two points on the final day of the season. In the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Football_League_Cup_final "2016 Football League Cup final") of the [2015–16 League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Football_League_Cup "2015–16 Football League Cup"), City defeated Liverpool on [penalties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_shoot-out_(association_football) "Penalty shoot-out (association football)") after a 1–1 draw. The two clubs met in European competition for the first time in the [2017–18 Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_UEFA_Champions_League "2017–18 UEFA Champions League") quarter-finals, where Liverpool won [5–1 on aggregate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_UEFA_Champions_League_knockout_phase#Liverpool_v_Manchester_City "2017–18 UEFA Champions League knockout phase"), ultimately reaching the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_UEFA_Champions_League_final "2018 UEFA Champions League final") and then [winning the competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_UEFA_Champions_League "2018–19 UEFA Champions League") a year later. In the [2018–19 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Premier_League "2018–19 Premier League"), City again won the title on the final day, with the Blues' 98 points and Liverpool's 97 being the third- and fourth-highest Premier League points totals ever. The [following season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Premier_League "2019–20 Premier League"), Liverpool clinched the title, recording 99 points (the second-highest Premier League total ever after Manchester City's 100 in [2017–18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Premier_League "2017–18 Premier League")) to finish 18 points above runners-up City. The Blues then regained the title in [2020–21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Premier_League "2020–21 Premier League") and outgunned Liverpool in another closely-fought title race in [2021–22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Premier_League "2021–22 Premier League"), to finish with 93 points to Liverpool's 92.

The success of the two teams in the 2010s and 2020s has led to the development of a rivalry between [Jürgen Klopp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%BCrgen_Klopp "Jürgen Klopp") and [Pep Guardiola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pep_Guardiola "Pep Guardiola"), the managers of Liverpool and Manchester City, with the two previously having been the respective managers of *[Der Klassiker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Klassiker "Der Klassiker")* rivals [Borussia Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund "Borussia Dortmund") and [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich "FC Bayern Munich") in the [Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesliga "Bundesliga"). At the end of the 2018–19 season, Guardiola described his relationship with Klopp as a "beautiful rivalry" and called Klopp's Liverpool team "the strongest opponents I have faced in my career as a manager". In September 2019, Klopp hailed Guardiola for being his 'greatest rival ever', after both were nominated for the [FIFA Men's Coach of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Best_FIFA_Football_Coach "The Best FIFA Football Coach") award in 2019, which Klopp ultimately won. In a 2019 survey, City fans answered that Liverpool, and not Manchester United, are the club's biggest rivals.

Manchester City also have long established local rivalries with [Bolton Wanderers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolton_Wanderers_F.C. "Bolton Wanderers F.C."), [Oldham Athletic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldham_Athletic_A.F.C. "Oldham Athletic A.F.C."), and [Stockport County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockport_County_F.C. "Stockport County F.C."), and more recent competitive Premier League rivalries with [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C. "Tottenham Hotspur F.C."), [Chelsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_F.C. "Chelsea F.C.") and [Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_F.C. "Arsenal F.C."), and Champions League rivalries with [Real Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Madrid_CF "Real Madrid CF").

## Ownership and finances

The holding company of Manchester City, Manchester City Limited, is a [private limited company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_limited_company "Private limited company"), with approximately 54 million shares in issue. The club has been in private hands since 2007, when the major shareholders agreed to sell their holdings to UK Sports Investments Limited (UKSIL), a company controlled by [Thaksin Shinawatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thaksin_Shinawatra "Thaksin Shinawatra"). UKSIL then made a formal offer to buy the shares held by several thousands of small shareholders.

Prior to Thaksin's takeover, the club had been listed on the specialist independent equity market PLUS (formerly OFEX) since 1995. On 6 July 2007, after acquiring 75% of the shares, Thaksin de-listed the club and re-registered it as a private company. By August, UKSIL had obtained over 90% of the shares and invoked its rights under the Companies Act to "squeeze out" the remaining shareholders, securing full ownership of the club. Thaksin became chairman, while his children, [Panthongtae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panthongtae_Shinawatra "Panthongtae Shinawatra") and [Pintongta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pintongta_Shinawatra "Pintongta Shinawatra"), were appointed as directors. Former chairman John Wardle remained on the board for a year, but resigned in July 2008 following the appointment of [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc. "Nike, Inc.") executive [Garry Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Cook_(executive) "Garry Cook (executive)") as executive chairman in May. The club made a pre-tax loss of £11m in the fiscal year ending 31 May 2007, the final year for which the club published accounts as a public company.

Thaksin's purchase prompted a period of transfer spending at the club, in total around £30 million, whereas over the several previous seasons Manchester City's net spending had been among the lowest in the Premier League. A year later, this investment was dwarfed by an influx of money derived from the club's takeover. On 1 September 2008, [Abu Dhabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Dhabi "Abu Dhabi")-based [Abu Dhabi United Group Investment and Development Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Dhabi_United_Group "Abu Dhabi United Group") completed the takeover of Manchester City. The deal, worth a reported £200 million, was announced on the morning of 1 September. It sparked various transfer "deadline-day" rumours and bids such as the club's attempt to [gazump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazump "Gazump") [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C. "Manchester United F.C.")'s protracted bid to sign [Dimitar Berbatov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitar_Berbatov "Dimitar Berbatov") from [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C. "Tottenham Hotspur F.C.") for a fee in excess of £30 million. Minutes before the transfer window closed, the club signed [Robinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robinho "Robinho") from [Real Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Madrid_CF "Real Madrid CF") for a [British record transfer fee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progression_of_British_football_transfer_record "Progression of British football transfer record") of £32.5 million. The wealth of the new owners meant that, in the summer of 2009, City were able to finance the purchase of experienced international players prior to the [new season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_Manchester_City_F.C._season "2009–10 Manchester City F.C. season"), spending more than any other club in the Premier League.

## Stadium

The City of Manchester Stadium in east Manchester, known as the Etihad Stadium since 2011 for sponsorship reasons, is on a 200-year [lease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lease "Lease") from [Manchester City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_City_Council "Manchester City Council") to Manchester City. It has been the club's home since the end of the [2002–03 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002%E2%80%9303_Manchester_City_F.C._season "2002–03 Manchester City F.C. season"), when City moved from [Maine Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine_Road "Maine Road"). Before moving to the stadium, the club spent in excess of £30 million to convert it to football use: the pitch was lowered, adding another tier of seating around it, and a new North Stand was constructed. The inaugural match at the new stadium was a 2–1 win over [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Barcelona "FC Barcelona") in a [friendly match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friendly_match "Friendly match").

After playing home matches at five stadiums between 1880 and 1887, the club settled at [Hyde Road Football Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyde_Road_(stadium) "Hyde Road (stadium)"), its home for 36 years. A fire destroyed the Main Stand in 1920, and the club moved to the 84,000 capacity Maine Road three years later. Maine Road, nicknamed the "Wembley of the North" by its designers, hosted the largest-ever crowd at an English club ground when 84,569 attended an [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1933%E2%80%9334_FA_Cup "1933–34 FA Cup") tie against Stoke City on 3 March 1934. Though Maine Road was redeveloped several times over its 80-year lifespan, by 1995 its capacity was restricted to 32,000, prompting the search for a new ground which culminated in the move to the City of Manchester Stadium in 2003; it was renamed the Etihad Stadium in 2011.

A 7,000-seat third tier on the South Stand was completed in time for the start of the [2015–16 football season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Manchester_City_F.C._season "2015–16 Manchester City F.C. season"), increasing the stadium's capacity to 55,097. In September 2024, Manchester City revealed plans to expand the North Stand, increasing the stadium's total capacity to over 61,000. The project also included the construction of a hotel, office space for club staff, and a new fan zone. The expanded stand opened for the final game of the [2025–26 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Manchester_City_F.C._season "2025–26 Manchester City F.C. season") and was named in honour of departing manager Pep Guardiola.

## Honours

Based on trophy count, Manchester City are one of [the most successful teams in England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_football_clubs_in_England_by_competitive_honours_won "List of football clubs in England by competitive honours won") – their thirty-eight major domestic, European and worldwide honours rank them fourth on the list of most decorated clubs.

The club's first major trophy was the [1904 FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1904_FA_Cup_final "1904 FA Cup final"), though they had previously won three regional [Manchester Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Cup "Manchester Cup") before that point. Their first top division league title came in the [1936–37 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936%E2%80%9337_Manchester_City_F.C._season "1936–37 Manchester City F.C. season"), with the first [Charity Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Community_Shield "FA Community Shield") won in the following August. City's first [League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Cup "EFL Cup") and [European trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_club_competitions "UEFA club competitions") both came at the end of the [1969–70 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969%E2%80%9370_Manchester_City_F.C._season "1969–70 Manchester City F.C. season"), the two trophies also constituting the team's first double of any kind. In the [2018–19 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Manchester_City_F.C._season "2018–19 Manchester City F.C. season"), City became the first team to claim all of the major English trophies available in a single season, winning not just the [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Premier_League "2018–19 Premier League"), [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_FA_Cup_final "2019 FA Cup final"), and [League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_EFL_Cup_final "2019 EFL Cup final"), but also the [Community Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_FA_Community_Shield "2019 FA Community Shield").

The [1970 Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_European_Cup_Winners%27_Cup_final "1970 European Cup Winners' Cup final") victory remained City's only European trophy until their triumph in the [2022–23 UEFA Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_UEFA_Champions_League "2022–23 UEFA Champions League"). They have reached the semi-finals of the [Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League "UEFA Champions League") four times overall, losing in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_UEFA_Champions_League#Semi-finals "2015–16 UEFA Champions League"), then winning en route to their first-ever [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_UEFA_Champions_League_final "2021 UEFA Champions League final") in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_UEFA_Champions_League#Semi-finals "2020–21 UEFA Champions League"), losing in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_UEFA_Champions_League#Semi-finals "2021–22 UEFA Champions League"), and winning en route to their maiden [European Cup title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_UEFA_Champions_League_final "2023 UEFA Champions League final") in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_UEFA_Champions_League#Semi-finals "2022–23 UEFA Champions League").

Manchester City jointly held the record for [most second division titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_teams_promoted_from_the_English_Football_League_Championship_and_predecessors "List of teams promoted from the English Football League Championship and predecessors") with [Leicester City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicester_City_F.C. "Leicester City F.C."), both clubs having won the league on seven occasions, before Leicester clinched their eighth title in [2023–24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_EFL_Championship "2023–24 EFL Championship"). Manchester City's first victory occurred in [1898–99](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1898%E2%80%9399_Football_League#Second_Division "1898–99 Football League") and the most recent came in [2001–02](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001%E2%80%9302_Football_League#First_Division "2001–02 Football League").
