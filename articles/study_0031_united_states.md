# United States

The **United States of America** (**USA**), also known as the **United States** (**U.S.**) or **America**, is a country primarily located in [North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_America "North America"). It is a [federal republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_republic "Federal republic") consisting of 50 [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._state "U.S. state") and a [federal capital district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_districts_and_territories "Capital districts and territories"), [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C.") The 48 [contiguous states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contiguous_states "Contiguous states") border [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada "Canada") to the north and [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico "Mexico") to the south, with the [semi-exclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-exclave "Semi-exclave") of [Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska "Alaska") in the northwest and the [archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago "Archipelago") of [Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii "Hawaii") in the [Pacific Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean "Pacific Ocean"). The United States also asserts sovereignty over five [major island territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territories_of_the_United_States "Territories of the United States") and [various uninhabited islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Minor_Outlying_Islands "United States Minor Outlying Islands") in [Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania "Oceania") and the [Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean "Caribbean"). It is a [megadiverse country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megadiverse_country "Megadiverse country"), with the world's [fourth-largest total area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area "List of countries and dependencies by area") and [third-largest population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population "List of countries and dependencies by population"), exceeding 341 million.

[Paleo-Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleo-Indians "Paleo-Indians") migrated from North Asia to North America at least 15,000 years ago and formed various civilizations. Beginning in 1607, [British colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_colonization_of_the_Americas "British colonization of the Americas") established the [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies "Thirteen Colonies"). The 18th-century [American Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Enlightenment "American Enlightenment") spread values of [republicanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republicanism "Republicanism") and [liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism_in_the_United_States "Liberalism in the United States") there, and clashes with the [British Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crown "The Crown") over [taxation without parliamentary representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_taxation_without_representation "No taxation without representation"), among other denied [rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rights_of_Englishmen "Rights of Englishmen"), sparked the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution "American Revolution"). Independence from Great Britain was formally declared in the [Lee Resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Resolution "Lee Resolution") of July 2, 1776, two days before the [Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Declaration_of_Independence "United States Declaration of Independence"). Victory in the 1775–1783 [Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War "American Revolutionary War") brought [international recognition of U.S. sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1783) "Treaty of Paris (1783)").

The U.S. [expanded westward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_evolution_of_the_United_States "Territorial evolution of the United States") through the purchase, settlement, and conquest of European- and Indigenous-controlled territory. As more states [joined the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admission_to_the_Union "Admission to the Union"), a [sectional division over slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_states_and_free_states "Slave states and free states") led 11 Southern states [to declare secession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinance_of_Secession "Ordinance of Secession") and form the [Confederate States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America "Confederate States of America"), fighting the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_(American_Civil_War) "Union (American Civil War)") in the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War") of 1861–1865. With the United States' victory and reunification, slavery [was abolished](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_of_slavery_in_the_United_States "End of slavery in the United States"). By 1900, the U.S. emerged as a [great power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_power "Great power"), a status solidified after [its involvement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_in_World_War_I "United States in World War I") in [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"). Following [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan "Empire of Japan")'s [attack on Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor "Attack on Pearl Harbor") in 1941, it [entered World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_entry_into_World_War_II "American entry into World War II") on the [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II "Allies of World War II") side. [The war's aftermath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aftermath_of_World_War_II "Aftermath of World War II") left the U.S. and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") as rival [superpowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superpower "Superpower"), vying for [geopolitical dominance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geopolitical_dominance "Geopolitical dominance") during the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War"). The [Soviet Union's collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union "Dissolution of the Soviet Union") in 1991 left the U.S. as the world's [sole superpower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sole_superpower "Sole superpower"), although it has faced competition from [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") in the 2020s.

The [U.S. federal government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_government_of_the_United_States "Federal government of the United States") is a [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic "Republic") under the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution "United States Constitution"), which created [a separation of powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers_under_the_United_States_Constitution "Separation of powers under the United States Constitution") among [legislative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative "Legislative"), [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_federal_executive_departments "United States federal executive departments"), and [judicial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_judiciary_of_the_United_States "Federal judiciary of the United States") branches. Executive authority is vested in [a president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States "President of the United States"), while [Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Congress "United States Congress") is a [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameralism "Bicameralism") national legislature composed of the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives "United States House of Representatives") (a [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house "Lower house") based on population) and the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate "United States Senate") (an [upper house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_house "Upper house") based on equal representation for each state). The [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_United_States "Supreme Court of the United States") exercises [judicial review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_review_in_the_United_States "Judicial review in the United States"), under which it may hold laws or governmental actions unconstitutional. [Federalism grants substantial autonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism_in_the_United_States "Federalism in the United States") to the 50 states.

A [developed country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_country "Developed country"), the [United States ranks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_rankings_of_the_United_States "International rankings of the United States") high in economic competitiveness, innovation, and [higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education_in_the_United_States "Higher education in the United States"). [Its economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_United_States "Economy of the United States") accounts for [over a quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(nominal) "List of countries by GDP (nominal)") of nominal global GDP and has been the world's largest [since about 1890](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_largest_historical_GDP "List of countries by largest historical GDP"). The U.S. is the [wealthiest country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_total_wealth "List of countries by total wealth"), with the [highest disposable household income per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disposable_household_and_per_capita_income#Household_disposable_income_per_capita_(OECD) "Disposable household and per capita income") among [OECD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OECD "OECD") members, though [its wealth inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth_inequality_in_the_United_States "Wealth inequality in the United States") is highly pronounced. [American culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_the_United_States "Culture of the United States"), shaped by [centuries of immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_immigration_to_the_United_States "History of immigration to the United States"), is diverse and [globally influential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americanization "Americanization"). The U.S. makes up [nearly a third](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_with_highest_military_expenditures "List of countries with highest military expenditures") of global military spending and is widely considered to have [the most powerful armed forces in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Armed_Forces "United States Armed Forces"). A member of [numerous international organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_organization_membership_of_the_United_States "International organization membership of the United States"), it plays a dominant role in global political, cultural, economic, and military affairs.

## Etymology

Documented use of the phrase "United States of America" dates back to January 2, 1776. On that day, [Stephen Moylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Moylan "Stephen Moylan"), a [Continental Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Army "Continental Army") aide to General [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington "George Washington"), wrote a letter to [Joseph Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Reed_(politician) "Joseph Reed (politician)"), Washington's [aide-de-camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aide-de-camp "Aide-de-camp"), seeking to go "with full and ample powers from the United States of America to Spain" to seek assistance in the [Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War "American Revolutionary War") effort. The first known public usage is an [anonymous essay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anonymous_work "Anonymous work") published in the [Williamsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamsburg,_Virginia "Williamsburg, Virginia") newspaper *[The Virginia Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Virginia_Gazette "The Virginia Gazette")* on April 6, 1776. Sometime on or after June 11, 1776, [Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson "Thomas Jefferson") wrote "United States of America" in a rough draft of the [Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Declaration_of_Independence "United States Declaration of Independence"), which was adopted by the [Second Continental Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Continental_Congress "Second Continental Congress") on July 4, 1776.

The term "United States" and its initialism "U.S.", used as nouns or as adjectives in English, are common short names for the country. The initialism "USA", a noun, is also common. "United States" and "U.S." are the established terms throughout the [U.S. federal government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_government_of_the_United_States "Federal government of the United States"), with prescribed rules. "The States" is an established colloquial shortening of the name, used particularly from abroad; "stateside" is the corresponding adjective or adverb.

"America" is the feminine form of the first name of *Americus Vesputius*, the Latinized name of Italian explorer [Amerigo Vespucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerigo_Vespucci "Amerigo Vespucci") (1454–1512); it was first used as a place name by the German cartographers [Martin Waldseemüller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Waldseem%C3%BCller "Martin Waldseemüller") and [Matthias Ringmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Ringmann "Matthias Ringmann") in 1507. Vespucci proposed that the [West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies "West Indies") discovered by [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus "Christopher Columbus") in 1492 were part of a previously unknown landmass and not among the Indies at the eastern limit of Asia. In English, [the term "America"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americas_(terminology) "Americas (terminology)") (used without a qualifier) seldom refers to topics unrelated to the United States. "The [Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americas "Americas")" is the general term to describe the totality of the continents of [North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_America "North America") and [South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_America "South America").

## History

### Indigenous peoples

The [first inhabitants of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleo-Indians "Paleo-Indians") migrated from [Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberia "Siberia") at least 15,000 years ago, either across the [Bering land bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bering_land_bridge "Bering land bridge") or along the [now-submerged Ice Age coastline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_migration_(Americas) "Coastal migration (Americas)"). The [Clovis culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clovis_culture "Clovis culture"), which appeared around 11,000 BCE in North America, is believed to be the first widespread culture in the Americas. Over time, Indigenous North American cultures grew increasingly sophisticated, and some, such as the [Mississippian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippian_culture "Mississippian culture"), developed [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Agricultural_Complex "Eastern Agricultural Complex"), [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeastern_Ceremonial_Complex "Southeastern Ceremonial Complex"), and [complex societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_societies "Complex societies"). In the [post-archaic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Native_Americans_in_the_United_States#Post-Archaic_stage "History of Native Americans in the United States"), the Mississippian cultures were located in the [midwestern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States "Midwestern United States"), [eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_United_States "Eastern United States"), and [southern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_United_States "Southern United States") regions, and the [Algonquian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algonquian_peoples "Algonquian peoples") in the [Great Lakes region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_region "Great Lakes region") and along the [Eastern Seaboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast_of_the_United_States "East Coast of the United States"), while the [Hohokam culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohokam_culture "Hohokam culture") and [Ancestral Puebloans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestral_Puebloans "Ancestral Puebloans") inhabited the [Southwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Southwest "American Southwest"). [Native population estimates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_history_of_the_Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas "Population history of the Indigenous peoples of the Americas") of what is now the United States before the arrival of European colonizers range from around 500,000 to nearly 10 million. In 2024, an estimated 3.43 million people in the United States identified as [American Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Indian "American Indian") or [Alaska Native](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Natives "Alaska Natives") alone (thus, identifying with no other racial category) and represented approximately 1% of the U.S. population. An additional 5.68 million people identified as American Indian or Alaska Native in combination with one or more other races, bringing the total of those identifying as American Indian or Alaska Native to approximately 9.11 million.

### European exploration, colonization and conflict (1513–1765)

[Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus "Christopher Columbus") began exploring the [Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean "Caribbean") for Spain in 1492, leading to [Spanish-speaking settlements and missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire "Spanish Empire") from what are now [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico "Puerto Rico") and Florida to [New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe_de_Nuevo_M%C3%A9xico "Santa Fe de Nuevo México") and [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alta_California "Alta California"). The first Spanish colony in the present-day continental United States was [Spanish Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Florida "Spanish Florida"), chartered in 1513. After several settlements failed there due to starvation and disease, Spain's first permanent town, [Saint Augustine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Augustine,_Florida "Saint Augustine, Florida"), was founded in 1565.

France established its own settlements in [French Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Florida "French Florida") in 1562, but they were either abandoned (Charlesfort, 1563) or destroyed by Spanish raids ([Fort Caroline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Caroline "Fort Caroline"), 1565). [Permanent French settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_France "New France") were founded much later along the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes "Great Lakes") ([Fort Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Pontchartrain_du_D%C3%A9troit "Fort Pontchartrain du Détroit"), 1701), the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River "Mississippi River") ([St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis "St. Louis"), 1764) and especially the [Gulf of Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Mexico "Gulf of Mexico") ([New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans "New Orleans"), 1718). Early European colonies also included the thriving Dutch colony of [New Nederland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Nederland "New Nederland") (settled 1626, present-day New York) and the small Swedish colony of [New Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Sweden "New Sweden") (settled 1638 in what became Delaware). [British colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_colonization_of_the_Americas "British colonization of the Americas") of the [East Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast_of_the_United_States "East Coast of the United States") began with the [Virginia Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Virginia "Colony of Virginia") (1607) and the [Plymouth Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plymouth_Colony "Plymouth Colony") (Massachusetts, 1620).

The [Mayflower Compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayflower_Compact "Mayflower Compact") in Massachusetts and the [Fundamental Orders of Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_Orders_of_Connecticut "Fundamental Orders of Connecticut") established precedents for local representative [self-governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-governance "Self-governance") and [constitutionalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutionalism "Constitutionalism") that would develop throughout the American colonies. While European settlers in what is now the United States experienced conflicts with Native Americans, they also engaged in trade, exchanging European tools for food and animal pelts. Relations ranged from close cooperation to warfare and massacres. The colonial authorities often pursued policies that forced Native Americans to adopt European lifestyles, including conversion to Christianity. Along the eastern seaboard, settlers [trafficked Africans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_colonial_history_of_the_United_States "Slavery in the colonial history of the United States") through the [Atlantic slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_slave_trade "Atlantic slave trade"), largely to provide [manual labor on plantations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_plantation "Slave plantation").

The original [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies "Thirteen Colonies") that would later found the United States were administered as [possessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dependent_territory "Dependent territory") of the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire") by Crown-appointed governors, though [local governments held elections open to most white male property owners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_government_in_the_Thirteen_Colonies "Colonial government in the Thirteen Colonies"). The colonial population grew rapidly from Maine to Georgia, eclipsing Native American populations; by the 1770s, the natural increase of the population was such that only a small minority of Americans had been born overseas. The colonies' distance from Britain facilitated the entrenchment of self-governance, and the [First Great Awakening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Great_Awakening "First Great Awakening"), a series of [Christian revivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_revival "Christian revival"), fueled colonial interest in guaranteed [religious liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_religion "Freedom of religion").

### American Revolution and the early republic (1765–1800)

Following its victory in the [French and Indian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_and_Indian_War "French and Indian War"), Britain began to assert greater control over local affairs in the Thirteen Colonies, resulting in [growing political resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution "American Revolution"). One of the primary grievances of the colonists was the denial of their [rights as Englishmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rights_of_Englishmen "Rights of Englishmen"), particularly the right to [representation in the British government that taxed them](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_taxation_without_representation "No taxation without representation"). To demonstrate their dissatisfaction and resolve, the [First Continental Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Continental_Congress "First Continental Congress") met in 1774 and passed the [Continental Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Association "Continental Association"), a colonial boycott of British goods enforced by local "[committees of safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_safety_(American_Revolution) "Committee of safety (American Revolution)")" that proved effective. The British attempt to then disarm the colonists resulted in the 1775 [Battles of Lexington and Concord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_of_Lexington_and_Concord "Battles of Lexington and Concord"), igniting the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War "American Revolutionary War"). At the [Second Continental Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Continental_Congress "Second Continental Congress"), the colonies appointed [George Washington Commander-in-Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_in_the_American_Revolution "George Washington in the American Revolution") of the [Continental Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Army "Continental Army"), and created [a committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_Five "Committee of Five") that named [Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson "Thomas Jefferson") to draft the [Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Declaration_of_Independence "United States Declaration of Independence"). Two days after the Second Continental Congress passed the [Lee Resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Resolution "Lee Resolution") to create an independent, [sovereign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereignty "Sovereignty") nation, the Declaration was adopted on July 4, 1776. The [political values of the American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Enlightenment "American Enlightenment") evolved from an armed rebellion demanding reform within an empire to a revolution that created a new social and governing system founded on the defense of [liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty "Liberty") and the protection of [inalienable natural rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_law "Natural law"); [equality under the law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equality_under_the_law "Equality under the law"); [sovereignty of the people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_sovereignty "Popular sovereignty"); [republicanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republicanism_in_the_United_States "Republicanism in the United States") over [monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy "Monarchy"), [aristocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristocracy "Aristocracy"), and other hereditary political power; [civic virtue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_virtue "Civic virtue"); and an intolerance of [political corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_corruption "Political corruption"). The [Founding Fathers of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founding_Fathers_of_the_United_States "Founding Fathers of the United States"), who included Washington, Jefferson, [John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams "John Adams"), [Benjamin Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin "Benjamin Franklin"), [Alexander Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Hamilton "Alexander Hamilton"), [John Jay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Jay "John Jay"), [James Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Madison "James Madison"), [Thomas Paine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Paine "Thomas Paine"), and many others, were inspired by [Classical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_republicanism#Classical_antecedents "Classical republicanism"), [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_philosophy "Renaissance philosophy"), and [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment#Politics "Age of Enlightenment") philosophies and ideas.

Though in practical effect since its drafting in 1777, the [Articles of Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articles_of_Confederation "Articles of Confederation") were ratified in 1781 and formally established a decentralized government that operated until 1789. After the British surrender at the [siege of Yorktown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Yorktown "Siege of Yorktown") in 1781, American sovereignty was internationally recognized by the [Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1783) "Treaty of Paris (1783)") (1783), through which the U.S. also gained territory stretching west to the Mississippi River, north to present-day Canada, and south to [Spanish Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Florida "Spanish Florida"). The [Northwest Ordinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Ordinance "Northwest Ordinance") (1787) established the precedent by which the country's territory would expand with the [admission of new states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admission_to_the_Union "Admission to the Union"), rather than the expansion of existing states.

The [U.S. Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_United_States "Constitution of the United States") was drafted at the 1787 [Constitutional Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Convention_(United_States) "Constitutional Convention (United States)") to overcome certain limitations of the Articles. It went into effect in 1789, creating a [federal republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_republic "Federal republic") governed by [three separate branches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers_under_the_United_States_Constitution "Separation of powers under the United States Constitution") that together formed a system of [checks and balances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checks_and_balances "Checks and balances"). George Washington [was elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1788%E2%80%9389_United_States_presidential_election "1788–89 United States presidential election") the country's first president under the Constitution, and the [Bill of Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Bill_of_Rights "United States Bill of Rights") (a series of ten amendments to the Constitution) was adopted in 1791 to allay skeptics' concerns about the power of the more centralized government. [Washington's resignation as Commander-in-Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington%27s_resignation_as_commander-in-chief "George Washington's resignation as commander-in-chief") after the Revolutionary War and his later refusal to run for a third term as the country's first president established a precedent for the [supremacy of civil authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_control_of_the_military "Civil control of the military") in the United States and the [peaceful transfer of power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peaceful_transition_of_power "Peaceful transition of power").

### Westward expansion and Civil War (1800–1865)

In the late 18th century, American settlers began to [expand westward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_evolution_of_the_United_States "Territorial evolution of the United States") in larger numbers, many with a sense of [manifest destiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifest_destiny "Manifest destiny"). The [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase "Louisiana Purchase") of 1803 from France nearly doubled the territory of the United States. [Lingering issues with Britain remained](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origins_of_the_War_of_1812 "Origins of the War of 1812"), leading to the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812 "War of 1812"), which was fought to a draw. [Spain ceded Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adams%E2%80%93On%C3%ADs_Treaty "Adams–Onís Treaty") and its Gulf Coast territory in 1819.

The [Missouri Compromise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Compromise "Missouri Compromise") of 1820, which admitted [Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri "Missouri") as a [slave state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_states_and_free_states "Slave states and free states") and [Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine#Statehood "Maine") as a free state, attempted to balance the desire of northern states to prevent the expansion of slavery into new territories with that of southern states to extend it there. Primarily, the compromise prohibited slavery in all other lands of the Louisiana Purchase north of the [36°30′ parallel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_36%C2%B030%E2%80%B2_north "Parallel 36°30′ north").

As Americans expanded further into territory inhabited by Native Americans, the [federal government implemented policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Indian_Policy "Federal Indian Policy") of [Indian removal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_removal "Indian removal") or [assimilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_assimilation_of_Native_Americans "Cultural assimilation of Native Americans"). The most significant such legislation was the [Indian Removal Act of 1830](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Removal_Act "Indian Removal Act"), a key policy of President [Andrew Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Jackson "Andrew Jackson"). It resulted in the [Trail of Tears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trail_of_Tears "Trail of Tears") (1830–1850), in which an estimated 60,000 Native Americans living east of the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River "Mississippi River") were forcibly removed and displaced to lands far to the west, causing 13,200 to 16,700 deaths along the [forced march](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_march "Death march"). Settler expansion as well as this influx of Indigenous peoples from the East resulted in the [American Indian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Indian_Wars "American Indian Wars") west of the Mississippi.

During the colonial period, [slavery became legal in all the Thirteen colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_colonial_history_of_the_United_States "Slavery in the colonial history of the United States"), and by 1770 it provided the main labor force in the [large-scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantation_complexes_in_the_Southern_United_States "Plantation complexes in the Southern United States"), [agriculture-dependent economies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antebellum_South#Economic_structure "Antebellum South") of the [Southern Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Colonies "Southern Colonies") from Maryland to Georgia. The practice began to be significantly questioned during the American Revolution, and spurred by an active [abolitionist movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism_in_the_United_States "Abolitionism in the United States") that had reemerged in the 1830s, states in [the North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_United_States "Northern United States") enacted laws to prohibit slavery within their boundaries. At the same time, [support for slavery had strengthened in Southern states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-slavery_ideology_in_the_United_States "Pro-slavery ideology in the United States"), with widespread use of inventions such as the [cotton gin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton_gin "Cotton gin") (1793) having made slavery immensely profitable for [Southern elites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planter_class "Planter class").

The United States [annexed the Republic of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_annexation "Texas annexation") in 1845, and the 1846 [Oregon Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Treaty "Oregon Treaty") led to U.S. control of the present-day [American Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_United_States "Northwestern United States"). Dispute with Mexico over Texas led to the [Mexican–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican%E2%80%93American_War "Mexican–American War") (1846–1848). After the victory of the U.S., Mexico recognized U.S. sovereignty over Texas, [New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mexico "New Mexico"), and [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California "California") in the 1848 [Mexican Cession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Cession "Mexican Cession"); the cession's lands also included the future states of [Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada "Nevada"), [Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado "Colorado") and [Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah "Utah"). The [California gold rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_gold_rush "California gold rush") of 1848–1849 spurred a huge migration of white settlers to the Pacific coast, leading to even more confrontations with Native populations. One of the most violent, the [California genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_genocide "California genocide") of thousands of Native inhabitants, lasted into the mid-1870s. Additional western territories and states were created.

Throughout the 1850s, the [sectional conflict regarding slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_states_and_free_states "Slave states and free states") was further inflamed by national legislation in the U.S. Congress and decisions of the Supreme Court. In Congress, the [Fugitive Slave Act of 1850](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fugitive_Slave_Act_of_1850 "Fugitive Slave Act of 1850") mandated the forcible return to their enslavers in the South of persons taking refuge in non-slave states, while the [Kansas–Nebraska Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas%E2%80%93Nebraska_Act "Kansas–Nebraska Act") of 1854 effectively gutted the anti-slavery requirements of the Missouri Compromise. In its [Dred Scott decision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dred_Scott_v._Sandford "Dred Scott v. Sandford") of 1857, the Supreme Court ruled against an enslaved person brought into non-slave territory, simultaneously declaring the entire Missouri Compromise to be unconstitutional. These and other [events exacerbated tensions between North and South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origins_of_the_American_Civil_War "Origins of the American Civil War") that would culminate in the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War") (1861–1865).

[Beginning with South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_in_the_American_Civil_War "South Carolina in the American Civil War"), 11 slave-state governments voted to [secede from the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secession_in_the_United_States "Secession in the United States") in 1860–1861, joining to create the [Confederate States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America "Confederate States of America"). All other state governments remained loyal to the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_(American_Civil_War) "Union (American Civil War)"). War broke out in April 1861 after the Confederacy [bombarded Fort Sumter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Fort_Sumter "Battle of Fort Sumter"). Following the [Emancipation Proclamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emancipation_Proclamation "Emancipation Proclamation") on January 1, 1863, many freed slaves joined the [Union army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_army "Union army"). The war [began to turn in the Union's favor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turning_point_of_the_American_Civil_War "Turning point of the American Civil War") following the 1863 [Siege of Vicksburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Vicksburg "Siege of Vicksburg") and [Battle of Gettysburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gettysburg "Battle of Gettysburg"), and the Confederates surrendered in 1865 after the Union's victory in the [Battle of Appomattox Court House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Appomattox_Court_House "Battle of Appomattox Court House").

### Reconstruction, Gilded Age, and Progressive Era (1866–1917)

Efforts toward [reconstruction in the secessionist South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_era "Reconstruction era") had begun as early as 1862, but it was only after [President Lincoln's assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Abraham_Lincoln "Assassination of Abraham Lincoln") that the three [Reconstruction Amendments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_Amendments "Reconstruction Amendments") to the Constitution were ratified [to protect civil rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_rights_movement_(1865%E2%80%931896) "Civil rights movement (1865–1896)"). The amendments codified nationally the abolition of slavery and involuntary servitude except as punishment for crimes, promised equal protection under the law for all persons, and prohibited denial or abridgment of voting rights on the basis of race, color, or previous enslavement. As a result, African Americans took an active political role in ex-Confederate states in the decade following the Civil War. The former Confederate states were readmitted to the Union, beginning with Tennessee in 1866 and ending with Georgia in 1870.

National infrastructure, including [transcontinental telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_transcontinental_telegraph "First transcontinental telegraph") and [railroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_transcontinental_railroad "First transcontinental railroad"), spurred growth in the [American frontier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_frontier "American frontier"). This was accelerated by the [Homestead Acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead_Acts "Homestead Acts"), through which nearly 10 percent of the total land area of the United States was given away free to some 1.6 million homesteaders. From 1865 through 1917, an unprecedented stream of [immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigrants "Immigrants") arrived in the United States, including 24.4 million from Europe. Most came through the [Port of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_New_York_and_New_Jersey "Port of New York and New Jersey"), as New York City and other major cities on the [East Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast_of_the_United_States "East Coast of the United States") became home to large [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_the_United_States "History of the Jews in the United States"), [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Americans "Irish Americans"), and [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Americans "Italian Americans") populations. Many [Northern Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Europe "Northern Europe") as well as significant numbers of [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans "German Americans") and other [Central Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Europe "Central Europe") moved to the [Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States "Midwestern United States"). At the same time, about one million [French Canadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French-Canadian_Americans "French-Canadian Americans") migrated from [Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec "Quebec") to [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England "New England"). During the [Great Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Migration_(African_American) "Great Migration (African American)"), millions of African Americans [left the rural South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Crow_economy "Jim Crow economy") for urban areas in the North. [Alaska was purchased](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Purchase "Alaska Purchase") from [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire") in 1867.

The [Compromise of 1877](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compromise_of_1877 "Compromise of 1877") is generally considered the end of the Reconstruction era, as it resolved the electoral crisis following the [1876 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1876_United_States_presidential_election "1876 United States presidential election") and led President [Rutherford B. Hayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutherford_B._Hayes "Rutherford B. Hayes") to reduce the role of federal troops in the South. Immediately, the [Redeemers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redeemers "Redeemers") began evicting the [Carpetbaggers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpetbaggers "Carpetbaggers") and quickly regained local control of Southern politics in the name of [white supremacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_supremacy "White supremacy"). African Americans endured a period of heightened, overt racism following Reconstruction, a time often considered the [nadir of American race relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadir_of_American_race_relations "Nadir of American race relations"). A series of Supreme Court decisions, including *[Plessy v. Ferguson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plessy_v._Ferguson "Plessy v. Ferguson")*, emptied the [Fourteenth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourteenth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "Fourteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution") and [Fifteenth Amendments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifteenth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "Fifteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution") of their force, allowing [Jim Crow laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Crow_laws "Jim Crow laws") in the South to remain unchecked, [sundown towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundown_town "Sundown town") in the Midwest, and [segregation in communities across the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation_in_the_United_States "Racial segregation in the United States"), which would be reinforced in part by the policy of [redlining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redlining "Redlining") later adopted by the federal [Home Owners' Loan Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Owners%27_Loan_Corporation "Home Owners' Loan Corporation").

[An explosion of technological advancement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Industrial_Revolution "Second Industrial Revolution"), accompanied by the [exploitation of cheap immigrant labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploitation_of_labour "Exploitation of labour"), led to rapid economic expansion during the [Gilded Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilded_Age "Gilded Age") of the late 19th century. It continued into the early 20th, when the United States already outpaced the economies of Britain, France, and Germany combined. [Tycoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tycoon "Tycoon") led the nation's expansion in the [railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_rail_transportation_in_the_United_States "History of rail transportation in the United States"), [petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_petroleum_industry_in_the_United_States "History of the petroleum industry in the United States"), and [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_steel_industry_(1850%E2%80%931970) "History of the steel industry (1850–1970)") industries, as the United States emerged as a pioneer of the [automotive industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_industry_in_the_United_States "Automotive industry in the United States"). This fostered the amassing of enormous economic and political power by [a few prominent industrialists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robber_baron_(industrialist) "Robber baron (industrialist)"), largely through the formation of [trusts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trust_(business) "Trust (business)") and [monopolies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopoly "Monopoly") to prevent competition. This resulted in significant increases in [economic inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_inequality "Economic inequality"), [slum conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_the_Other_Half_Lives "How the Other Half Lives"), and [civil unrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_incidents_of_civil_unrest_in_the_United_States "List of incidents of civil unrest in the United States"), creating a fertile environment for [labor unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_history_of_the_United_States "Labor history of the United States") to flourish and, to a more limited extent, [socialist movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism_in_the_United_States "Socialism in the United States") to emerge. This period eventually ended with the advent of the [Progressive Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Era "Progressive Era"), which was characterized by significant economic, legislative and social reforms.

Pro-American elements in Hawaii [overthrew the Hawaiian monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overthrow_of_the_Hawaiian_Kingdom "Overthrow of the Hawaiian Kingdom"); the islands [were annexed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newlands_Resolution "Newlands Resolution") in 1898. That same year, [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico "Puerto Rico"), [the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Philippines "The Philippines"), and [Guam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guam "Guam") were ceded to the U.S. by Spain after the latter's defeat in the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War "Spanish–American War"). (The Philippines was granted full independence from the U.S. on July 4, 1946, following World War II. Puerto Rico and Guam have remained U.S. territories.) [American Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Samoa "American Samoa") was acquired by the United States in 1900 after the [Second Samoan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Samoan_Civil_War "Second Samoan Civil War"). The [U.S. Virgin Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Virgin_Islands "United States Virgin Islands") were purchased from [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark "Denmark") in 1917 after Danish voters approved the sale in [a 1916 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1916_Danish_West_Indies_status_referendum "1916 Danish West Indies status referendum").

### World War I, Great Depression, and World War II (1917–1945)

The United States [entered World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_entry_into_World_War_I "American entry into World War I") alongside the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I "Allies of World War I") in 1917, helping to turn the tide against the [Central Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Powers "Central Powers"). In 1920, [a constitutional amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteenth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "Nineteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution") granted nationwide [women's suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_suffrage_in_the_United_States "Women's suffrage in the United States"). During the 1920s and 1930s, radio for [mass communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_communication "Mass communication") and early television transformed communications nationwide. The [Wall Street Crash of 1929](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street_Crash_of_1929 "Wall Street Crash of 1929") had triggered the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression_in_the_United_States "Great Depression in the United States"), to which President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt "Franklin D. Roosevelt") responded with the [New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Deal "New Deal") plan of "reform, recovery and relief", a series of unprecedented and sweeping [recovery programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphabet_agencies "Alphabet agencies") and [employment relief projects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Works_Progress_Administration "Works Progress Administration") combined with [financial reforms and regulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_economics "Regulatory economics"). During the 1930s, [severe drought and dust storms caused or exacerbated by poor agricultural practices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dust_Bowl "Dust Bowl") forced hundreds of thousands of displaced farming families (often called ["Okies"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okie "Okie") because many originated from [Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma "Oklahoma")) to migrate from the [Great Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains "Great Plains") to California in search of subsistence.

[Initially neutral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_non-interventionism_before_entering_World_War_II "United States non-interventionism before entering World War II") during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_the_United_States_during_World_War_II "Military history of the United States during World War II"), the U.S. began [supplying war materiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lend-Lease "Lend-Lease") to the [Allies of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II "Allies of World War II") in March 1941 and [entered the war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_entry_into_World_War_II "American entry into World War II") in December after [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan "Empire of Japan")'s [attack on Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor "Attack on Pearl Harbor"). Agreeing to a "[Europe first](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe_first "Europe first")" policy, the U.S. concentrated its wartime efforts on Japan's allies [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_Italy "Fascist Italy") and [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany") until their final defeat in May 1945. The U.S. [developed the first nuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project "Manhattan Project") and [used them against the Japanese cities of Hiroshima and Nagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bombings_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki "Atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki") in August 1945, which [historians consider central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debate_over_the_atomic_bombings_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki "Debate over the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki") to the [end of World War II in Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_of_World_War_II_in_Asia "End of World War II in Asia"). The United States was one of the "[Big Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Four_(World_War_II) "Big Four (World War II)")" allied powers during the war, alongside the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom"), the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"), and [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_(1912%E2%80%931949) "Republic of China (1912–1949)"); they later became the "Four Policemen" that met to plan the [postwar world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aftermath_of_World_War_II "Aftermath of World War II"). The U.S. emerged relatively unscathed from the war, with even greater [economic power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_power "Economic power") and [international political influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphere_of_influence "Sphere of influence").

### Cold War and social revolution (1945–1991)

The end of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") in 1945 left the U.S. and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") as [superpowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superpower "Superpower"), each with its own political, military, and economic [sphere of influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphere_of_influence "Sphere of influence"). Geopolitical tensions between the two superpowers soon led to the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War"). The Soviets' development of their own atomic weapons led to a fierce [nuclear arms race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_arms_race "Nuclear arms race") with America. The U.S. implemented a policy of [containment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Containment "Containment") intended to limit the Soviet Union's sphere of influence; engaged in [regime change against governments perceived to be aligned with the Soviets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_involvement_in_regime_change#1945–1991:_Cold_War "United States involvement in regime change"); and prevailed in the [Space Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Race "Space Race"), which culminated with the [first crewed Moon landing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_11 "Apollo 11") in 1969.

Domestically, the U.S. [experienced drastic economic growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post%E2%80%93World_War_II_economic_expansion "Post–World War II economic expansion"), [urbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization_in_the_United_States "Urbanization in the United States"), and [population growth following World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-20th_century_baby_boom "Mid-20th century baby boom"). The [civil rights movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_rights_movement "Civil rights movement") emerged, with [Martin Luther King Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther_King_Jr. "Martin Luther King Jr.") becoming a prominent leader in the early 1960s. The [Great Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Society "Great Society") plan of President [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson "Lyndon B. Johnson")'s administration resulted in groundbreaking and broad-reaching laws, policies and [a constitutional amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-fourth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "Twenty-fourth Amendment to the United States Constitution") to counteract some of the worst effects of lingering [institutional racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutional_racism "Institutional racism").

The [counterculture movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterculture_of_the_1960s "Counterculture of the 1960s") in the U.S. brought significant social changes, including the liberalization of attitudes toward [recreational drug use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreational_drug_use "Recreational drug use") and [sexuality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_revolution "Sexual revolution"). It also encouraged [open defiance of the military draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draft_evasion_in_the_Vietnam_War "Draft evasion in the Vietnam War") (leading to the [end of conscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription_in_the_United_States "Conscription in the United States") in 1973) and [wide opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposition_to_United_States_involvement_in_the_Vietnam_War "Opposition to United States involvement in the Vietnam War") to [U.S. intervention in Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_in_the_Vietnam_War "United States in the Vietnam War"), with the U.S. totally withdrawing in 1975. [A societal shift in the roles of women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_liberation_movement_in_the_United_States "Women's liberation movement in the United States") was significantly responsible for the large increase in female paid labor participation starting in the 1970s, and by 1985 the majority of American women aged 16 and older were employed.

The [Fall of Communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1989 "Revolutions of 1989") and the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union "Dissolution of the Soviet Union") from 1989 to 1991 marked the [end of the Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_of_the_Cold_War "End of the Cold War") and [left the United States as the world's sole superpower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superpower#Post–Cold_War_era "Superpower"). This cemented the United States' global influence, reinforcing the concept of the "[American Century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Century "American Century")" as the U.S. dominated international political, cultural, economic, and military affairs.

### Contemporary (1991–present)

The 1990s saw the [longest recorded economic expansion in American history up to that time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990s_United_States_boom "1990s United States boom"), a dramatic [decline in U.S. crime rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_in_the_United_States#Crime_over_time "Crime in the United States"), and [advances in technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_and_industrial_history_of_the_United_States#Computers_and_information_networks "Technological and industrial history of the United States"). Throughout this decade, technological innovations such as the [World Wide Web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Web "World Wide Web"), the evolution of the [Pentium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentium_(original) "Pentium (original)") microprocessor in accordance with [Moore's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moore%27s_law "Moore's law"), rechargeable [lithium-ion batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium-ion_battery "Lithium-ion battery"), the first [gene therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_therapy "Gene therapy") trial, and [cloning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloning "Cloning") either emerged in the U.S. or were improved upon there. In the 1990s, the [Human Genome Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Genome_Project "Human Genome Project") was launched, while [Nasdaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasdaq "Nasdaq") became the first stock market in the United States to trade online. In the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War "Gulf War") of 1991, [an American-led international coalition of states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_of_the_Gulf_War "Coalition of the Gulf War") expelled an [Iraqi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27athist_Iraq "Ba'athist Iraq") invasion force that had occupied neighboring [Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuwait "Kuwait"). The [September 11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_attacks "September 11 attacks") on the United States in 2001 by the [pan-Islamist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Islamism "Pan-Islamism") militant organization [al-Qaeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda "Al-Qaeda") led to the U.S. launching the [war on terror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_on_terror "War on terror") and military interventions [in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Afghanistan_(2001%E2%80%932021) "War in Afghanistan (2001–2021)") and [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_War "Iraq War"). The [U.S. housing bubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000s_United_States_housing_bubble "2000s United States housing bubble") culminated in 2007 with the [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession "Great Recession"), the largest economic contraction since the Great Depression.

Beginning in the 2010s, the United States has experienced [increased political polarization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_polarization_in_the_United_States "Political polarization in the United States") and [significant democratic backsliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_backsliding_in_the_United_States "Democratic backsliding in the United States"). The country's polarization was violently reflected in the [January 2021 Capitol attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_6_United_States_Capitol_attack "January 6 United States Capitol attack"), when a mob of insurrectionists supporting President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") entered the [U.S. Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Capitol "United States Capitol") and sought to prevent his successor, [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden"), from taking office. It was the first time that a U.S. president has refused the [peaceful transfer of power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peaceful_transition_of_power "Peaceful transition of power"). Some scholars have described the United States as transitioning from a [liberal democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_democracy "Liberal democracy") polity to a [hybrid regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_regime "Hybrid regime") since 2025.
In the 2020s, academics and commentators have noted China's economic rise and its growing challenge to the U.S. as the world's sole superpower.

## Geography

The United States is the [third or fourth largest country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area "List of countries and dependencies by area") by total area, with figures ranging between 3,717,813 and 3,809,525 square miles (9,629,091 and 9,866,624 km2).

In 2021, the United States held 8% of the Earth's permanent meadows and pastures and 10% of its cropland. Starting in the east, [the coastal plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Plain "Atlantic Plain") of the [Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Ocean "Atlantic Ocean") seaboard gives way to inland forests and rolling hills in the [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_(plateau) "Piedmont (plateau)") plateau region. The [Appalachian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Mountains "Appalachian Mountains") and the [Adirondack Massif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adirondack_Mountains "Adirondack Mountains") separate the [East Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast_of_the_United_States "East Coast of the United States") from the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes "Great Lakes") and the grasslands of [the Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States "Midwestern United States"). The [Mississippi River System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River_System "Mississippi River System"), the world's [fourth-longest river system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rivers_by_length "List of rivers by length"), runs predominantly north–south through the center of the country. The flat and fertile [prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie "Prairie") of the [Great Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains "Great Plains") stretches to the west, interrupted by [a highland region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Interior_Highlands "U.S. Interior Highlands") in the southeast.

The [Rocky Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains "Rocky Mountains"), west of the Great Plains, extend north to south across the country, peaking at over 14,000 feet (4,300 m) in [Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado "Colorado"). The [supervolcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supervolcano "Supervolcano") underlying [Yellowstone National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellowstone_National_Park "Yellowstone National Park") in the Rocky Mountains, the [Yellowstone Caldera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellowstone_Caldera "Yellowstone Caldera"), is the continent's largest volcanic feature. Farther west are the rocky [Great Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Basin "Great Basin") and the [Chihuahuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chihuahuan_Desert "Chihuahuan Desert"), [Sonoran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonoran_Desert "Sonoran Desert"), and [Mojave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojave_Desert "Mojave Desert") deserts. In the northwest corner of [Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona "Arizona"), carved by the [Colorado River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_River "Colorado River"), is the [Grand Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Canyon "Grand Canyon"), a steep-sided canyon and popular tourist destination known for its overwhelming visual size and intricate, colorful landscape. The [Cascade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascade_Range "Cascade Range") and [Sierra Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada "Sierra Nevada") mountain ranges run close to the [Pacific coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_of_the_United_States "West Coast of the United States"). The [lowest and highest points in the contiguous United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_points_of_the_United_States "Extreme points of the United States") are in the state of California, about 84 miles (135 km) apart.

[Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska "Alaska") is a state in the northwestern regions of [North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_America "North America"). It is a [semi-exclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-exclave "Semi-exclave") and separated from the [contiguous United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contiguous_United_States "Contiguous United States"). At an elevation of 20,310 feet (6,190.5 m), Alaska's [Denali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denali "Denali") (also called Mount McKinley) is the highest peak in the country and on the continent. Active [volcanoes in the U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_volcanoes_in_the_United_States "List of volcanoes in the United States") are common throughout Alaska's [Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Archipelago "Alexander Archipelago") and [Aleutian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleutian_Islands "Aleutian Islands"), while [Hawaii has six active volcanoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_volcanoes_in_the_Hawaiian%E2%80%93Emperor_seamount_chain "List of volcanoes in the Hawaiian–Emperor seamount chain"), including [Kilauea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilauea "Kilauea") and [Mauna Loa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauna_Loa "Mauna Loa"). Located entirely outside North America, the Hawaiian archipelago consists of volcanic islands, all [physiographically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_geography "Physical geography") and [ethnologically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnology "Ethnology") part of the [Polynesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesia "Polynesia") subregion of [Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania "Oceania").

In addition to its total land area, the United States has one of the world's largest marine [exclusive economic zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zones "Exclusive economic zones"), spanning approximately 4.5 million square miles (11.7 million km2) of ocean.

### Climate

With its large size and geographic variety, the United States includes most climate types. East of the [100th meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100th_meridian_west "100th meridian west"), the climate ranges from [humid continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate "Humid continental climate") in the north to [humid subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate "Humid subtropical climate") in the south. The western Great Plains are [semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate "Semi-arid climate"). Many mountainous areas of the American West have an [alpine climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_climate "Alpine climate"). The climate is [arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_climate "Desert climate") in the Southwest, [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate "Mediterranean climate") in [coastal California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_California "Coastal California"), and [oceanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate "Oceanic climate") in coastal [Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon "Oregon"), [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_(state) "Washington (state)"), and southern [Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska "Alaska"). Most of Alaska is [subarctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarctic_climate "Subarctic climate") or [polar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_climate "Polar climate"). [Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii "Hawaii"), the [southern tip of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Florida "South Florida") and U.S. territories in the [Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean "Caribbean") and [Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean "Pacific Ocean") are [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_climate "Tropical climate").

The United States receives more high-impact [extreme weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_weather "Extreme weather") incidents than any other country. States bordering the [Gulf of Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Mexico "Gulf of Mexico") are prone to hurricanes, and most of the world's tornadoes [occur in the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornadoes_in_the_United_States "Tornadoes in the United States"), mainly in [Tornado Alley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_Alley "Tornado Alley"). Due to [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change "Climate change"), extreme weather has become more frequent in the U.S. in the 21st century, with three times the number of reported [heat waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_waves "Heat waves") compared to the 1960s. Since the 1990s, droughts in the [American Southwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_United_States "Southwestern United States") have become more persistent and more severe. The regions considered as the most attractive to the population are the most vulnerable.

### Biodiversity and conservation

The U.S. is one of 17 [megadiverse countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megadiverse_countries "Megadiverse countries") containing large numbers of [endemic species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_endangered_animals_of_North_America "List of endangered animals of North America"): about 17,000 species of [vascular plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vascular_plant "Vascular plant") occur in the contiguous United States and Alaska, and over 1,800 species of [flowering plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flowering_plant "Flowering plant") are found in Hawaii, few of which occur on the mainland. The United States is home to 428 [mammal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal "Mammal") species, 784 birds, 311 reptiles, 295 [amphibians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibian "Amphibian"), and around 91,000 insect species.

There are [63 national parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_the_United_States "List of national parks of the United States"), and [hundreds of other federally managed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_lands "Federal lands") monuments, forests, and [wilderness areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Wilderness_Preservation_System "National Wilderness Preservation System"), administered by the [National Park Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Park_Service "National Park Service") and other agencies. About 28% of the country's land is publicly owned and federally managed, primarily in the [Western States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_United_States "Western United States"). [Most of this land is protected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protected_areas_of_the_United_States "Protected areas of the United States"), though some is leased for commercial use, and less than one percent is used for military purposes.

[Environmental issues in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_issues_in_the_United_States "Environmental issues in the United States") include debates on [non-renewable resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-renewable_resource "Non-renewable resource") and [nuclear energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_debate "Nuclear power debate"), [air and water pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution_prevention_in_the_US "Pollution prevention in the US"), [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity "Biodiversity"), logging and [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation_in_the_United_States "Deforestation in the United States"), and [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_the_United_States "Climate change in the United States"). The [U.S. Environmental Protection Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Environmental_Protection_Agency "United States Environmental Protection Agency") (EPA) is the federal agency charged with [addressing most environmental-related issues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_policy_of_the_United_States "Environmental policy of the United States"). The [idea of wilderness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Wilderness_Preservation_System "National Wilderness Preservation System") has shaped the management of public lands since 1964, with the [Wilderness Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilderness_Act "Wilderness Act"). The [Endangered Species Act of 1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endangered_Species_Act_of_1973 "Endangered Species Act of 1973") provides a way to protect threatened and endangered species and their habitats. The [United States Fish and Wildlife Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Fish_and_Wildlife_Service "United States Fish and Wildlife Service") implements and enforces the Act. In 2024, the U.S. ranked 35th among 180 countries in the [Environmental Performance Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_Performance_Index "Environmental Performance Index").

## Government and politics

The United States is a federal republic consisting of 50 states and a federal capital district, Washington, D.C. The U.S. asserts sovereignty over five unincorporated territories and several uninhabited island possessions. It is the world's oldest surviving federation, and its [presidential system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system "Presidential system") of federal government has been adopted, in whole or in part, by many newly independent states worldwide following their [decolonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonization "Decolonization"). The Constitution of the United States serves as the country's [supreme legal document](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremacy_Clause "Supremacy Clause").

### Federal government

Composed of three branches, all headquartered in Washington, D.C., the federal government is the national government of the United States. The U.S. Constitution establishes a [separation of powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers "Separation of powers") intended to provide a system of [checks and balances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checks_and_balances "Checks and balances") to prevent any of the three branches from becoming supreme. The three-branch system is known as the [presidential system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system "Presidential system"), in contrast to the [parliamentary system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system "Parliamentary system") where the executive is part of the legislative body. Many countries around the world adopted this aspect of the 1789 Constitution of the United States, especially in the [postcolonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postcolonialism "Postcolonialism") Americas.

#### Legislature

The [U.S. Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Congress "United States Congress") is a [bicameral legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral_legislature "Bicameral legislature") made up of the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate "United States Senate") and the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives "United States House of Representatives"). The Senate has 100 members—two residents from each state and elected by that state's voters for a six-year term. The House of Representatives has 435 voting members, elected for a two-year term by the constituency of the [congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_congressional_districts "List of United States congressional districts") in which they reside. District boundaries are generally drawn by [state legislatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_legislature_(United_States) "State legislature (United States)") or redistricting commissions under state law. Every U.S. congressional district must be as nearly equal in population as practicable; each sends one representative to Congress. Election years for senators are staggered so that only one-third of them will be up for election every two years. U.S. representatives are all up for election at the same time every two years.

The U.S. Congress makes [federal law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_law "Federal law"), [declares war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_war "Declaration of war"), has the [power of the purse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_of_the_purse "Power of the purse"), and has [the power of impeachment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeachment_in_the_United_States "Impeachment in the United States"). The Senate gives advice and consent to the ratification of treaties by a two-thirds vote of senators present. One of the foremost non-legislative functions of Congress is [the power to investigate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_investigation "Congressional investigation") and oversee the executive branch. [Congressional oversight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_oversight "Congressional oversight") is usually delegated to committees and is facilitated by [Congress' power to issue subpoenas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contempt_of_Congress "Contempt of Congress"). Much of the work of Congress is performed by [a collection of committees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_congressional_committee "United States congressional committee"), each appointed for a specific purpose or function. Committee membership is by tradition and statute [bipartisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipartisan "Bipartisan"), but all committees are [chaired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chair_(officer) "Chair (officer)") by a member of the [majority party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority_party "Majority party"), who sets the committee agenda.

#### Executive

The U.S. president is the [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state "Head of state"), [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief "Commander-in-chief") of the military, and [chief executive of the federal government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_government "Head of government"). The president appoints the [members of the Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_United_States "Cabinet of the United States"), subject to Senate approval, and names other officials who administer and enforce federal law and policy through [their respective agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_federal_agencies_in_the_United_States "List of federal agencies in the United States"). The president has the ability to veto [legislative bills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_(law) "Bill (law)") from the U.S. Congress before they become law. However, [presidential vetoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veto_power_in_the_United_States "Veto power in the United States") can be overridden by a two-thirds [supermajority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermajority#Use_in_governments_around_the_world "Supermajority") vote in both chambers of Congress. The president also has clemency power for federal crimes and [can issue pardons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_pardons_in_the_United_States "Federal pardons in the United States"). Finally, the president has the authority to issue expansive "[executive orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_orders "Executive orders")" in a number of policy areas, subject to [judicial review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_review_in_the_United_States "Judicial review in the United States").

Candidates for president campaign with a vice-presidential [running mate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_mate "Running mate"), and both candidates are elected or defeated together in a presidential election. Unlike other votes in American politics, this is technically an [indirect election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indirect_election "Indirect election") in which the winner will be determined by the [U.S. Electoral College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Electoral_College "United States Electoral College"). Each state is allocated two electors plus one additional elector for every congressional district in the state, which in effect combines to equal the number of elected officials that state sends to Congress. The District of Columbia, with no voting representation in Congress, is allocated three electoral votes. Electoral College votes are officially cast by individual electors selected under procedures established by [state law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_legislature_(United_States) "State legislature (United States)"). In practice, 48 states and the District of Columbia require their electors to vote for the statewide popular-vote winner; Maine and Nebraska award two votes to the statewide winner and one to the winner in each congressional district. Both the president and the vice president serve a four-year term, and the president may be [reelected to the office only once](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-second_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "Twenty-second Amendment to the United States Constitution"), for one additional four-year term.

#### Judiciary

The [U.S. federal judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_judiciary_of_the_United_States "Federal judiciary of the United States") consists primarily of the [U.S. Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_United_States "Supreme Court of the United States"), the [U.S. courts of appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_courts_of_appeals "United States courts of appeals"), and the [U.S. district courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_district_court "United States district court"). Judges for these courts are appointed under [Article III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_Three_of_the_United_States_Constitution "Article Three of the United States Constitution"): They are nominated by the president, confirmed by the Senate, and hold office subject to good behavior (generally resulting in life tenure). To limit interference from other branches, their compensation may not be reduced while they remain in office. Congress has also created [Article I courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_I_tribunal "Article I tribunal") under its legislative powers, largely to handle [administrative law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_law "Administrative law"); unlike Article III judges, whose tenure and compensation are protected by the Constitution, Article I judges generally serve fixed statutory terms.

Federal district courts are the primary trial courts of the federal judiciary and hear civil and criminal cases within their jurisdiction, primarily cases arising under federal statutes, the Constitution, or [international treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_treaties "International treaties"). Decisions of federal district courts may generally be challenged to a federal court of appeals. The U.S. judicial system's 12 regional circuits divide the country into geographic regions for appellate purposes, while the [Federal Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Federal_Circuit "United States Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit") has nationwide jurisdiction over specified types of cases.

The Supreme Court is the highest court in the federal judiciary. It has both original and appellate jurisdiction and exercises [judicial review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_review_in_the_United_States "Judicial review in the United States"), under which it may hold laws or governmental actions unconstitutional. On average, the Supreme Court receives about 7,000 petitions for [writs of certiorari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writ_of_certiorari "Writ of certiorari") each year but grants review in about 80 cases.

The Supreme Court consists of nine justices, including the [chief justice of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_the_United_States "Chief Justice of the United States"), and decides cases before it by majority vote. When the Senate conducts an impeachment trial of a sitting president, the chief justice presides. Like other Article III judges, Supreme Court justices are nominated by the president and confirmed by the Senate and hold office during good behavior.

### Subdivisions

In the [U.S. federal system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism_in_the_United_States "Federalism in the United States"), sovereign powers are shared between three levels of government specified in the Constitution: the federal government, the states, and Indian tribes. The U.S. also asserts sovereignty over five permanently inhabited territories: [American Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Samoa "American Samoa"), [Guam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guam "Guam"), the [Northern Mariana Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Mariana_Islands "Northern Mariana Islands"), [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico "Puerto Rico"), and the [U.S. Virgin Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Virgin_Islands "United States Virgin Islands").

Residents of the 50 states are governed by their elected state government, under [state constitutions compatible with the national constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_constitutions_in_the_United_States "State constitutions in the United States"), and by elected [local governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_the_United_States "Local government in the United States") that are administrative divisions of a state. States are subdivided into counties or county equivalents, which (except in Hawaii) can permit the formation of municipalities administered by their own elected representatives. The District of Columbia is [a federal district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_district_of_the_United_States "Federal district of the United States") containing the U.S. capital, [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C.") The federal district is an administrative division of the federal government.

As of January 2026, there were 575 [federally recognized tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federally_recognized_tribe "Federally recognized tribe"). There are approximately [326 Indian reservations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_reservations_in_the_United_States "List of Indian reservations in the United States"). The tribes hold a government-to-government relationship with the U.S. federal government in Washington and are legally defined as [domestic dependent nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_dependent_nations "Domestic dependent nations") with [inherent tribal sovereignty rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribal_sovereignty_in_the_United_States "Tribal sovereignty in the United States").

In addition to the five major territories, the U.S. also asserts sovereignty over the [United States Minor Outlying Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Minor_Outlying_Islands "United States Minor Outlying Islands") in the [Pacific Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean "Pacific Ocean") and the [Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean "Caribbean"). The seven undisputed islands without permanent populations are [Baker Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baker_Island "Baker Island"), [Howland Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howland_Island "Howland Island"), [Jarvis Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarvis_Island "Jarvis Island"), [Johnston Atoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnston_Atoll "Johnston Atoll"), [Kingman Reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingman_Reef "Kingman Reef"), [Midway Atoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midway_Atoll "Midway Atoll"), and [Palmyra Atoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmyra_Atoll "Palmyra Atoll"). U.S. sovereignty over the unpopulated [Bajo Nuevo Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bajo_Nuevo_Bank "Bajo Nuevo Bank"), [Navassa Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navassa_Island "Navassa Island"), [Serranilla Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serranilla_Bank "Serranilla Bank"), and [Wake Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_Island "Wake Island") is disputed.

### Political parties

The Constitution is silent on political parties. However, they developed independently in the 18th century with the [Federalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalist_Party "Federalist Party") and [Anti-Federalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Federalist_Party "Anti-Federalist Party") parties. Since then, the United States has operated as a *de facto* [two-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-party_system "Two-party system"), though the parties have changed over time.

Since the mid-19th century, the two main national parties have been the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)") and the [Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States) "Republican Party (United States)"). The Democratic Party is generally described as sitting on the [center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrism "Centrism") to [center-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-left_politics "Centre-left politics") of the [political spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_spectrum "Political spectrum"), with [a platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_positions_of_the_Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Political positions of the Democratic Party (United States)") rooted in [modern American liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_liberalism_in_the_United_States "Modern liberalism in the United States"), while the Republican Party is generally described as [right-wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-wing_politics "Right-wing politics"), with [a platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_positions_of_the_Republican_Party_(United_States) "Political positions of the Republican Party (United States)") rooted in [American conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States "Conservatism in the United States"). Since the 2010s, a number of political scientists have characterized the Republican Party as having shifted toward [right-wing populism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-wing_populism "Right-wing populism") and [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism "Nationalism"), with some [comparative political](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_politics "Comparative politics") studies classifying it as a [far-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-right_politics "Far-right politics") party. Surveys have also found that Democrats have become more consistently liberal and Republicans more consistently conservative, reducing ideological overlap between the two parties.

### Foreign relations

The United States has the world's [second-largest diplomatic corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_number_of_diplomatic_missions "List of countries by number of diplomatic missions") and maintains full diplomatic relations with 195 states. The [history of U.S. foreign policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_foreign_policy_of_the_United_States "History of the foreign policy of the United States") began with [isolationism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_non-interventionism "United States non-interventionism") but gradually shifted towards [interventionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_interventions_by_the_United_States "Foreign interventions by the United States"); the U.S. emerged as the *de facto* leader of the post–World War II [liberal international order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_international_order "Liberal international order") as a founding member of [numerous international organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_organization_membership_of_the_United_States "International organization membership of the United States"), including the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations"). The U.S. is a permanent member of the [UN Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council "United Nations Security Council") and member of the [G7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G7 "G7"), [G20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G20 "G20"), [OECD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OECD "OECD"), and [Organization of American States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_of_American_States "Organization of American States").

On national security, the United States works closely with [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO")'s 31 other members and designates 21 additional countries as "[major non-NATO allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_non-NATO_ally "Major non-NATO ally")". As a present [superpower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superpower "Superpower"), the U.S. has increasingly focused on the [Indo-Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pacific "Indo-Pacific") region, [seeking to counter China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_foreign_policy_toward_the_People%27s_Republic_of_China "United States foreign policy toward the People's Republic of China").

The U.S. has a "[Special Relationship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Relationship "Special Relationship")" with the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom") and historically strong ties to [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada "Canada"), [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia"), and [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand "New Zealand"); together, they form the [Five Eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Eyes "Five Eyes") intelligence alliance. Through the [Compact of Free Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_of_Free_Association "Compact of Free Association"), the U.S. has authority over security and defense for [Micronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federated_States_of_Micronesia "Federated States of Micronesia"), the [Marshall Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Islands "Marshall Islands"), and [Palau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau "Palau"). In 2024, the largest purchasers of U.S. [arms exports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arms_industry "Arms industry") were [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey "Turkey"), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland "Poland"), and [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan"); meanwhile, the largest recipients of [U.S. military aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_military_aid "United States military aid") were [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel"), [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine "Ukraine"), and [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan").

### Military

The president is the [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-Chief_of_the_United_States "Commander-in-Chief of the United States") of the United States Armed Forces and appoints its leaders, the [secretary of defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_Defense "United States Secretary of Defense") and the [Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff "Joint Chiefs of Staff"). The [Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Defense "United States Department of Defense"), headquartered at [the Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon "The Pentagon") near Washington, D.C., administers five of the six service branches, which are made up of the [U.S. Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army "United States Army"), [Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marine_Corps "United States Marine Corps"), [Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy "United States Navy"), [Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force "United States Air Force"), and [Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force "United States Space Force"). The [Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard "United States Coast Guard") is administered by the [Department of Homeland Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Homeland_Security "United States Department of Homeland Security") in peacetime and can be transferred to the [Department of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy "United States Department of the Navy") in wartime. Total strength of the entire military is about 1.3 million active duty with an additional 400,000 in reserve.

The United States military is widely regarded as the most powerful and advanced in the world. The [U.S. military budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._military_budget "U.S. military budget") was $954 billion in 2025, which is by far the [largest of any country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_with_highest_military_expenditures "List of countries with highest military expenditures"), making up 33% of global military spending and accounting for 3.1% of total U.S. GDP. The [United States possesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapons_of_the_United_States "Nuclear weapons of the United States") 42% of the world's nuclear weapons—the second-largest stockpile after [that of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_and_weapons_of_mass_destruction "Russia and weapons of mass destruction"). The U.S. has the [third-largest combined armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_number_of_military_and_paramilitary_personnel "List of countries by number of military and paramilitary personnel") in the world, behind the [Chinese People's Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Liberation_Army "People's Liberation Army") and [Indian Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Armed_Forces "Indian Armed Forces"). In addition to the vast network of military bases on its soil, the U.S. maintains approximately 800 other bases and installations around the world, and it [deploys greater than 100 active-duty personnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_military_deployments "United States military deployments") in each of 25 foreign countries. The United States has engaged in over 400 military interventions since its founding in 1776, with over half of these occurring between 1950 and 2019 and 25% occurring in the post–Cold War era.

[State defense forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_defense_forces "State defense forces") (SDFs) are military units that operate under the sole authority of a state government. SDFs are authorized by state and federal law but are under the command of [the state's governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_(United_States) "Governor (United States)").
By contrast, the 54 [U.S. National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Guard_(United_States) "National Guard (United States)") organizations fall under the dual control of [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_governments_of_the_United_States "State governments of the United States") or [territorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territories_of_the_United_States "Territories of the United States") governments and the [federal government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_government_of_the_United_States "Federal government of the United States"). The National Guard personnel of a state or territory can be federalized by the president under the [National Defense Act Amendments of 1933](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Act_of_1916#National_Defense_Act_Amendments_of_1933 "National Defense Act of 1916"); this legislation created the Guard and provides for the integration of [Army National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_National_Guard "Army National Guard") and [Air National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_National_Guard "Air National Guard") units and personnel into the U.S. Army and (since 1947) the U.S. Air Force. SDFs cannot be federalized. The total number of National Guard members is about 430,000, while the estimated combined strength of SDFs is less than 10,000.

### Law enforcement and criminal justice

There are about 18,000 U.S. police agencies from local to national level in the United States. Law in the United States is mainly enforced by local police departments and [sheriff departments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheriffs_in_the_United_States "Sheriffs in the United States") in their municipal or county jurisdictions. [The state police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_police_(United_States) "State police (United States)") departments [have authority in their respective state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_power_(United_States_constitutional_law) "Police power (United States constitutional law)"), and [federal agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_law_enforcement_in_the_United_States "Federal law enforcement in the United States") such as the [Federal Bureau of Investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Investigation "Federal Bureau of Investigation") (FBI) and the [U.S. Marshals Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marshals_Service "United States Marshals Service") have national jurisdiction and specialized duties, such as protecting [civil rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_rights "Civil rights"), [national security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_security_of_the_United_States "National security of the United States"), enforcing [U.S. federal courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._federal_courts "U.S. federal courts")' rulings and federal laws, and interstate criminal activity. [State courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_court_(United_States) "State court (United States)") conduct almost all civil and criminal trials, while federal courts [adjudicate the much smaller number of civil and criminal cases that relate to federal law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subject-matter_jurisdiction "Subject-matter jurisdiction").

There is no unified "criminal justice system" in the United States. The [American prison system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incarceration_in_the_United_States "Incarceration in the United States") is largely heterogenous, with thousands of relatively independent systems operating across federal, state, local, and tribal levels. In 2026, "these systems hold [nearly 2 million people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_incarceration_rate "United States incarceration rate") in 1,566 state prisons, 98 federal prisons, 3,116 local jails, 1,277 juvenile correctional facilities, 220 immigration detention facilities, and 77 Indian country jails, as well as in military prisons, civil commitment centers, state psychiatric hospitals, and prisons in the U.S. territories—at a system-wide cost of at least $445 billion each year."

Despite disparate systems of confinement, four main institutions dominate: [federal prisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_federal_prisons "List of United States federal prisons"), [state prisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lists_of_United_States_state_prisons "Lists of United States state prisons"), local jails, and [juvenile correctional facilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_juvenile_justice_system "American juvenile justice system"). Federal prisons are run by the [Federal Bureau of Prisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Prisons "Federal Bureau of Prisons") and hold pretrial detainees as well as people who have been convicted of federal crimes. State prisons, run by the [department of corrections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corrections "Corrections") of each state, hold people sentenced and serving prison time (usually longer than one year) for felony offenses. Local jails are county or municipal facilities that incarcerate defendants prior to trial; they also hold those serving short sentences (typically under a year). Juvenile correctional facilities are operated by local or state governments and serve as longer-term placements for any [minor adjudicated as delinquent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juvenile_delinquency "Juvenile delinquency") and ordered by a judge to be confined.

In January 2023, the United States had the [sixth-highest per capita incarceration rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_incarceration_rate "List of countries by incarceration rate") in the world—531 people per 100,000 inhabitants—and the largest prison and jail population in the world, with more than 1.9 million people incarcerated. An analysis of the [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization "World Health Organization") Mortality Database from 2010 showed U.S. homicide rates "were 7 times higher than in other high-income countries, driven by [a gun homicide rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_deaths_in_the_United_States "Gun deaths in the United States") that was 25 times higher".

The country's legal system is classified by some political scientists and legal scholars as operating under a "partial [rule of law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_law "Rule of law")" system, according to two 2026 surveys conducted by [Bright Line Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Line_Watch "Bright Line Watch") and the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics "London School of Economics").

## Economy

The U.S. has a highly developed [mixed economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_economy "Mixed economy") that has been the world's largest nominally [since about 1890](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_largest_historical_GDP "List of countries by largest historical GDP"). Its 2024 [gross domestic product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_domestic_product "Gross domestic product") (GDP) of more than $29 trillion constituted over 25% of nominal [global economic output](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_world_product "Gross world product"), or 15% at [purchasing power parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity "Purchasing power parity") (PPP). The country ranks [first in the world by nominal GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(nominal) "List of countries by GDP (nominal)"), [second when adjusted for purchasing power parities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(PPP) "List of countries by GDP (PPP)") (PPP), and [ninth by PPP-adjusted GDP per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(PPP)_per_capita "List of countries by GDP (PPP) per capita"). In August 2026, the total [U.S. federal government debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_debt_of_the_United_States "National debt of the United States") passed $40 trillion, having more than doubled in the previous decade.

Of the world's [500 largest companies by revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_Global_500 "Fortune Global 500"), [138 were headquartered in the U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_companies_in_the_United_States_by_revenue "List of largest companies in the United States by revenue") in 2025, the highest number of any country. The [U.S. dollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar "United States dollar") is the currency most used [in international transactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_use_of_the_U.S._dollar "International use of the U.S. dollar") and the world's foremost [reserve currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_currency "Reserve currency"), backed by the country's dominant economy, [its military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Armed_Forces "United States Armed Forces"), the [petrodollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrodollar "Petrodollar") system, its large [U.S. treasuries market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Treasury "U.S. Treasury"), and its linked [eurodollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurodollar "Eurodollar"). [Several countries use it as their official currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar#Countries_that_use_US_dollar "United States dollar"), and in others it is the [*de facto* currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_facto_currency "De facto currency"). The U.S. has [free trade agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_trade_agreements "Free trade agreements") with [several countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_trade_agreements_of_the_United_States "Free trade agreements of the United States"), including the [USMCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States%E2%80%93Mexico%E2%80%93Canada_Agreement "United States–Mexico–Canada Agreement"). Although the United States has reached a [post-industrial level of economic development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-industrial_economy "Post-industrial economy") and is often described as having a [service economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_economy "Service economy"), it remains a major industrial power; in 2024, the [U.S. manufacturing sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._manufacturing_sector "U.S. manufacturing sector") was the world's [second-largest by value output](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_manufacturing_output "List of countries by manufacturing output") after [China's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing_in_China "Manufacturing in China").

[New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") is the world's principal [financial center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_center "Financial center"), and [its metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area "New York metropolitan area") is the world's [largest metropolitan economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_GDP "List of cities by GDP"). The [New York Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Stock_Exchange "New York Stock Exchange") and [Nasdaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasdaq "Nasdaq"), both located in New York City, are the world's two [largest stock exchanges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_stock_exchanges "List of stock exchanges") by [market capitalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_capitalization "Market capitalization") and [trade volume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_volume "Trade volume"). The United States is at the forefront of [technological advancement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_technology_in_the_United_States "Science and technology in the United States") and [innovation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innovation "Innovation") in many economic fields, especially in [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence"); [electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics "Electronics") and [computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer "Computer"); [pharmaceuticals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmaceuticals "Pharmaceuticals"); and medical, [aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace "Aerospace") and [military equipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_equipment "Military equipment"). The country's economy is fueled by abundant [natural resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_resource "Natural resource"), a well-developed [infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrastructure_policy_of_the_United_States "Infrastructure policy of the United States"), and [high productivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_labour_productivity "List of countries by labour productivity"). The [largest trading partners of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_largest_trading_partners_of_the_United_States "List of the largest trading partners of the United States") are the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union"), Mexico, Canada, China, Japan, South Korea, the United Kingdom, Vietnam, India, and Taiwan. The United States is the world's [largest importer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_imports "List of countries by imports") and [second-largest exporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_exports "List of countries by exports"). It is by far the world's [largest exporter of services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_service_exports "List of countries by service exports").

Americans have the highest average [household](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Household_income "Household income") and [employee income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_average_wage "List of countries by average wage") among OECD member states, and the fourth-highest [median household income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_income "Median income") in 2023, up from sixth-highest in 2013. With personal [consumption expenditures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_spending "Consumer spending") of over $18.5 trillion in 2023, the U.S. has a heavily [consumer-driven economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_economy "Consumer economy") and is the world's [largest consumer market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_consumer_markets "List of largest consumer markets"). The U.S. [ranked first in the number of dollar billionaires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_number_of_billionaires "List of countries by number of billionaires") and [millionaires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_number_of_millionaires "List of countries by number of millionaires") in 2023, with 735 billionaires and nearly 22 million millionaires.

[Wealth in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth_in_the_United_States "Wealth in the United States") is highly concentrated; in 2011, the richest 10% of the adult population owned 72% of the country's household wealth, while the bottom 50% owned just 2%. [U.S. wealth inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth_inequality_in_the_United_States "Wealth inequality in the United States") increased substantially since the late 1980s, and [income inequality in the U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_inequality_in_the_United_States "Income inequality in the United States") reached a record high in 2019. In 2024, the country had some of the highest wealth and income inequality levels among [OECD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OECD "OECD") countries. Since the 1970s, there has been a decoupling of U.S. wage gains from worker productivity, while the economy has become more dominated by [financial services and stock trading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_capitalism "Financial capitalism"). In 2016, the top fifth of earners took home more than half of all income, giving the U.S. one of the widest income distributions among OECD countries. There were about 771,480 [homeless persons in the U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homelessness_in_the_United_States "Homelessness in the United States") on a single night in January 2024. In 2022, 6.4 million children experienced food insecurity. [Feeding America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feeding_America "Feeding America") estimates that around one in five, or approximately 13 million, [children experience hunger in the U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunger_in_the_United_States#Children "Hunger in the United States") and do not know where or when they will get their next meal. Also in 2022, about 37.9 million people, or 11.5% of the U.S. population, were [living in poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_the_United_States "Poverty in the United States").

The United States has a smaller [welfare state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_state "Welfare state") and redistributes less income through government action than most other [high-income countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank_high-income_economy "World Bank high-income economy"). It is the only [advanced economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_economy "Advanced economy") that does not [guarantee its workers paid vacation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_statutory_minimum_employment_leave_by_country "List of statutory minimum employment leave by country") nationally and one of a few countries in the world without [paid family leave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parental_leave_in_the_United_States "Parental leave in the United States") as a legal right. The United States has a higher percentage of low-income [workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_class_in_the_United_States "Working class in the United States") than almost any other developed country, largely because of a weak [collective bargaining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_bargaining "Collective bargaining") system and lack of government support for at-risk workers.

### Science and technology

The United States [has been a leader in technological innovation since the late 19th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_and_industrial_history_of_the_United_States "Technological and industrial history of the United States") and scientific research since the mid-20th century. Methods for producing [interchangeable parts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interchangeable_parts "Interchangeable parts") and the establishment of a [machine tool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_tool "Machine tool") industry enabled [the large-scale manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_system_of_manufacturing "American system of manufacturing") of U.S. consumer products in the late 19th century. By the early 20th century, factory [electrification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrification "Electrification"), the introduction of the [assembly line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_line "Assembly line"), and other [labor-saving techniques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automation "Automation") created the system of [mass production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_production "Mass production").

In the 21st century, the United States continues to be one of the world's foremost scientific powers, though China has emerged as a major competitor in many fields. The U.S. has the [highest research and development expenditures of any country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states_by_research_and_development_spending "List of sovereign states by research and development spending") and ranks ninth as a percentage of GDP. In 2022, the United States was (after China) the country with the [second-highest number of published scientific papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_number_of_scientific_and_technical_journal_articles "List of countries by number of scientific and technical journal articles"). The United States is considered to be a world leader in the development of [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence") technology.

#### Spaceflight

The United States has maintained a space program since the late 1950s, beginning with the establishment of the [National Aeronautics and Space Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA") (NASA) in 1958. In 1961, the United States became the second country (after the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union")) to successfully [launch a human into space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_spaceflight "Human spaceflight"). NASA's [Apollo program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_program "Apollo program") (1961–1972) achieved the first crewed [Moon landing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_landing "Moon landing") with the 1969 [Apollo 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_11 "Apollo 11") mission; it remains one of the agency's most significant milestones. Other major endeavors by NASA include the [Space Shuttle program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_program "Space Shuttle program") (1981–2011), the [Voyager program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyager_program "Voyager program") (1972–present), the [Hubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubble_Space_Telescope "Hubble Space Telescope") and [James Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope "James Webb Space Telescope") [space telescopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_telescope "Space telescope") (launched in 1990 and 2021, respectively), and the multi-mission [Mars Exploration Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_Exploration_Program "Mars Exploration Program") (*[Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_(rover) "Spirit (rover)")*, *[Opportunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opportunity_(rover) "Opportunity (rover)")*, *[Curiosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curiosity_(rover) "Curiosity (rover)")*, and *[Perseverance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perseverance_(rover) "Perseverance (rover)")*). NASA is one of five agencies collaborating on the [International Space Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station "International Space Station") (ISS); U.S. contributions to the ISS include several modules, including *[Destiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destiny_(ISS_module) "Destiny (ISS module)")* (2001), *[Harmony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmony_(ISS_module) "Harmony (ISS module)")* (2007), and *[Tranquility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tranquility_(ISS_module) "Tranquility (ISS module)")* (2010), as well as ongoing logistical and operational support.

The United States [private sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_sector "Private sector") dominates the global [commercial spaceflight industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_spaceflight "Private spaceflight"). Prominent American spaceflight contractors include [Blue Origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Origin "Blue Origin"), [Boeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing "Boeing"), [Lockheed Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin "Lockheed Martin"), [Northrop Grumman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman "Northrop Grumman"), and [SpaceX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX "SpaceX"). NASA programs such as the [Commercial Crew Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_Crew_Program "Commercial Crew Program"), [Commercial Resupply Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_Resupply_Services "Commercial Resupply Services"), [Commercial Lunar Payload Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_Lunar_Payload_Services "Commercial Lunar Payload Services"), and [NextSTEP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Space_Technologies_for_Exploration_Partnerships "Next Space Technologies for Exploration Partnerships") have facilitated growing private-sector involvement in American spaceflight.

### Energy

In 2023, the United States received approximately 84% of its energy from fossil fuel, and its largest source of energy was [petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_in_the_United_States "Petroleum in the United States") (38%), followed by [natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas_in_the_United_States "Natural gas in the United States") (36%), [renewable sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_in_the_United_States "Renewable energy in the United States") (9%), [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_in_the_United_States "Coal in the United States") (9%), and [nuclear power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_in_the_United_States "Nuclear power in the United States") (9%). In 2022, the United States constituted about 4% of the [world's population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_population "World population"), but [consumed around 16%](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_energy_consumption "List of countries by energy consumption") of the [world's energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_energy_consumption "World energy consumption"). The U.S. ranks as the [second-highest emitter of greenhouse gases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_greenhouse_gas_emissions "List of countries by greenhouse gas emissions") behind China.

The U.S. is the world's [largest producer of nuclear power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_by_country "Nuclear power by country"), generating around 30% of the world's nuclear electricity. It also has the highest number of nuclear power reactors of any country. From 2024, the U.S. plans to triple its nuclear power capacity by 2050.

### Transportation

The United States' 4 million miles (6.4 million kilometers) of road network, owned almost entirely by state and local governments, is the [longest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_road_network_size "List of countries by road network size") in the world. The extensive [Interstate Highway System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_Highway_System "Interstate Highway System") that connects all major U.S. cities is funded mostly by the federal government but maintained by [state departments of transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._state_and_insular_area_departments_of_transportation "List of U.S. state and insular area departments of transportation"). The system is further extended by [state highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_highway#United_States "State highway") and some private [toll roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toll_road "Toll road").

The U.S. ranks among the ten countries with the [highest vehicle ownership per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_vehicles_per_capita "List of countries by vehicles per capita"), with 850 vehicles per 1,000 residents in 2022. A 2024 survey reported 58.3% of households with two or more vehicles, and 21.9% with three or more. According to the 2022 [American Community Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Community_Survey "American Community Survey"), 68.7% of U.S. workers aged 16 and older drove alone to work, 2.9% walked or cycled, and 3.1% used public transportation.

[Public transportation in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transportation_in_the_United_States "Public transportation in the United States") is well developed in the largest urban areas, notably New York City, Washington, D.C., Boston, Philadelphia, Chicago, and the San Francisco Bay Area where [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit "Rapid transit") and/or [light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail "Light rail") is a major component, however most metropolitan areas rely on bus for public transportation; otherwise, coverage is generally less extensive than in most other developed countries and many U.S. localities are [car-dependent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car-dependent "Car-dependent").

Long-distance intercity travel is provided primarily by airlines, but travel by rail is more common along the [Northeast Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Corridor "Northeast Corridor"), where the only [high-speed rail in the U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail_in_the_United_States "High-speed rail in the United States") that meets international standards operates. [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak "Amtrak"), the country's government-sponsored national passenger rail company, has a relatively sparse network compared to that of Western European countries. Service is concentrated in the Northeast, California, the Midwest, and the Pacific Northwest. [The country's rail transport network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_the_United_States "Rail transport in the United States"), the [longest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_rail_transport_network_size "List of countries by rail transport network size") in the world at 182,412.3 mi (293,564.2 km), handles mostly [freight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freight_transport "Freight transport") (in contrast to more passenger-centered rail in Europe). Because they are often privately owned, U.S. freight railroads lag behind much of the rest of the world in terms of electrification.

The United States has an extensive air transportation network. [U.S. civilian airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airlines_of_the_United_States "List of airlines of the United States") are all privately owned. The three largest airlines in the world, by total number of passengers carried, are U.S.-based; [American Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines "American Airlines") became the global leader after its 2013 merger with [US Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Airways "US Airways"). Of the 50 busiest airports in the world, 16 are in the United States, as well as five of the top 10. The world's busiest airport by passenger volume is [Hartsfield–Jackson Atlanta International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartsfield%E2%80%93Jackson_Atlanta_International_Airport "Hartsfield–Jackson Atlanta International Airport") in [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta "Atlanta"), Georgia. In 2022, most of the [19,969 U.S. airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_the_United_States "List of airports in the United States") were owned and operated by local government authorities, and there are also some private airports. Some 5,193 are designated as "public use", including for [general aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_aviation "General aviation"). The [Transportation Security Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_Security_Administration "Transportation Security Administration") (TSA) has provided security at most major airports since 2001.

[The country's inland waterways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inland_waterways_of_the_United_States "Inland waterways of the United States") are the world's [fifth-longest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_waterways_length "List of countries by waterways length"), totaling 25,482 mi (41,009 km). They are used extensively for freight, recreation, and a small amount of passenger traffic. Of the world's [50 busiest container ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_container_ports "List of busiest container ports"), four are located in the United States, with the busiest in the country being the [Port of Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Los_Angeles "Port of Los Angeles").

### Urbanization

About 82% of Americans live in [metropolitan areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_area "Metropolitan statistical area"), particularly in [suburbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburb "Suburb") and outer-ring [exurbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exurb "Exurb"); about half of those reside in cities with populations over 50,000. In 2024, 346 [incorporated U.S. municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_cities_by_population#50_states_and_District_of_Columbia "List of United States cities by population") had populations over 100,000, 11 cities had more than one million residents, and four cities—[New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"), [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles"), [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago"), and [Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston "Houston")—had populations exceeding two million. Some 56 U.S. metropolitan areas [have one million or more residents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_areas#United_States "Metropolitan statistical areas").

More recently, the fastest-growing metropolitan areas were in the South, while southern metros along the Mexican border and Gulf Coast metros susceptible to hurricanes declined the most in 2025. The [New York metro area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metro_area "New York metro area"), which gained the most new residents in 2024, fell to 13th in 2025, due to a fall in immigrants. The top metro areas with rising populations in 2025 were [Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_metropolitan_area "Houston metropolitan area") and [Dallas–Fort Worth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Metroplex "Dallas Metroplex"), followed by [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta_metropolitan_area "Atlanta metropolitan area"), [Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_metropolitan_area "Phoenix metropolitan area") and [Charlotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_metropolitan_area "Charlotte metropolitan area").

### Language

While many languages and [dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialects "Dialects") are spoken in the United States, [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English "American English") is by far the most commonly spoken and written. English was designated the [official language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language "Official language") of the United States by [Executive Order 14224](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Order_14224 "Executive Order 14224") in March 2025. However, the U.S. has never had a statutory official language, as Congress has never passed a law to designate English as official for all three federal branches. Some laws, such as [U.S. naturalization requirements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalized_citizen_of_the_United_States "Naturalized citizen of the United States"), nonetheless standardize English. Twenty-eight states and the [United States Virgin Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Virgin_Islands "United States Virgin Islands") have laws that designate English as the sole official language; 19 states and the [District of Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Columbia "District of Columbia") have no official language. Three states and four U.S. territories have recognized local or indigenous languages in addition to English: Hawaii ([Hawaiian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiian_language "Hawaiian language")), Alaska ([twenty Native languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Native_languages "Alaska Native languages")), South Dakota ([Sioux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sioux_language "Sioux language")), American Samoa ([Samoan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samoan_language "Samoan language")), Puerto Rico ([Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rican_Spanish "Puerto Rican Spanish")), Guam ([Chamorro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamorro_language "Chamorro language")), and the Northern Mariana Islands ([Carolinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolinian_language "Carolinian language") and Chamorro). In total, 169 Native American languages are spoken in the United States. In Puerto Rico, Spanish is more widely spoken than English.

According to the [American Community Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Community_Survey "American Community Survey") (2020), some 245.4 million people in the U.S. age five and older spoke only English at home. About 41.2 million spoke [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_language_in_the_United_States "Spanish language in the United States") at home, making it the second most commonly used language. Other languages spoken at home by one million people or more include [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language_in_the_United_States "Chinese language in the United States") (3.40 million), [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language_in_the_United_States "Tagalog language in the United States") (1.71 million), [Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_language_in_the_United_States "Vietnamese language in the United States") (1.52 million), [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language_in_the_United_States "Arabic language in the United States") (1.39 million), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language_in_the_United_States "French language in the United States") (1.18 million), [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language_in_the_United_States "Korean language in the United States") (1.07 million), and [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language_in_the_United_States "Russian language in the United States") (1.04 million). [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language_in_the_United_States "German language in the United States"), spoken by 1 million people at home in 2010, fell to 881,000 estimated total speakers in 2020.

### Immigration

America's immigrant population is by far the world's [largest in absolute terms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states_by_immigrant_and_emigrant_population "List of sovereign states by immigrant and emigrant population"). In 2022, there were 87.7 million immigrants and [U.S.-born children of immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-generation_immigrants_in_the_United_States "Second-generation immigrants in the United States") in the United States, accounting for nearly 27% of the overall U.S. population. In 2017, out of the U.S. foreign-born population, some 45% (20.7 million) were naturalized citizens, 27% (12.3 million) were lawful permanent residents, 6% (2.2 million) were temporary lawful residents, and 23% (10.5 million) were [unauthorized immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undocumented_immigrant_population_of_the_United_States "Undocumented immigrant population of the United States"). In 2019, the top countries of origin for immigrants were Mexico (24% of immigrants), India (6%), China (5%), the Philippines (4.5%), and El Salvador (3%). In fiscal year 2022, over one million immigrants (most of whom entered through [family reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_migration#Legislation "Chain migration")) were granted [legal residence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_residence_(United_States) "Permanent residence (United States)"). The undocumented immigrant population in the U.S. reached a record high of 14 million in 2023.

### Religion

The [First Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "First Amendment to the United States Constitution") guarantees the [free exercise of religion in the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Exercise_Clause "Free Exercise Clause") and forbids Congress from passing laws respecting [its establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Establishment_Clause "Establishment Clause"). Religious practice is widespread, among the [most diverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_ethnic_and_cultural_diversity_level "List of countries by ethnic and cultural diversity level") in the world, and profoundly vibrant.

The country has the world's [largest Christian population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_by_country "Christianity by country"), which includes the [fourth-largest population of Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_by_country "Catholic Church by country"). Other notable faiths include [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism "Judaism"), [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"), [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism"), [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam"), [New Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Age "New Age"), and [Native American religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_religions "Native American religions"). Religious practice varies significantly by region. "[Ceremonial deism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_deism "Ceremonial deism")" is common in American culture.

The overwhelming majority of [Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans "Americans") believe in a [higher power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_Power "Higher Power") or spiritual force, engage in [spiritual practices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritual_practice "Spiritual practice") such as prayer, and consider themselves religious or [spiritual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirituality "Spirituality"). In the Southern United States' "[Bible Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_Belt "Bible Belt")", [evangelical Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism "Evangelicalism") plays a significant role culturally; [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England "New England") and the Western United States [tend to be more secular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unchurched_Belt "Unchurched Belt"). [Mormonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormonism "Mormonism"), a [Restorationist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restorationism "Restorationism") movement founded in the U.S. in 1830, is the predominant religion in Utah and a major religion in Idaho.

### Health

According to the [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centers_for_Disease_Control_and_Prevention "Centers for Disease Control and Prevention") (CDC), average U.S. life expectancy at birth reached 79.0 years in 2024, its highest recorded level and an increase of 0.6 years over 2023. The CDC attributed the improvement mainly to lower mortality from unintentional injuries, COVID-19, heart disease, [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer "Cancer"), and homicide. Heart disease, cancer, and unintentional injuries remained the leading causes of death. In 2024, life expectancy at birth for American men rose to 76.5 years (+0.7 years compared to 2023), while life expectancy for women was 81.4 years (+0.3 years). Starting in 1998, life expectancy in the U.S. fell [behind that of other wealthy industrialized countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_life_expectancy "List of countries by life expectancy"), and Americans' "health disadvantage" gap has been increasing ever since.

The Commonwealth Fund reported in 2020 that the U.S. had the [highest suicide rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_suicide_rate "List of countries by suicide rate") among the 11 [high-income countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-income_countries "High-income countries") included in its study. [Obesity affected 40.3% of U.S. adults aged 20 and older](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obesity_in_the_United_States "Obesity in the United States") in August 2021–August 2023. The U.S. healthcare system far [outspends that of any other country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_total_health_expenditure_(PPP)_per_capita "List of countries by total health expenditure (PPP) per capita"), measured both in per capita spending and as a percentage of GDP, but attains worse healthcare outcomes when compared to peer countries for reasons that are debated. The United States is the only developed country [without a system of universal healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_reform_in_the_United_States "Healthcare reform in the United States"), and [a significant proportion of the population that does not carry health insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_insurance_coverage_in_the_United_States "Health insurance coverage in the United States"). Government-funded healthcare coverage for the poor ([Medicaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicaid "Medicaid")) and for those age 65 and older ([Medicare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicare_(United_States) "Medicare (United States)")) is available to Americans who meet the programs' income or age qualifications. In 2010, President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") signed the [Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patient_Protection_and_Affordable_Care_Act "Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act") into law. Since the 2022 U.S. Supreme Court decision *[Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobbs_v._Jackson_Women%27s_Health_Organization "Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization")*, which effectively overruled *[Roe v. Wade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roe_v._Wade "Roe v. Wade")* (1973), [abortion in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion_in_the_United_States "Abortion in the United States") is no longer federally protected but is subject to the laws of each state or territory.

### Education

American primary and secondary education, known in the U.S. as [K–12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%E2%80%9312_education_in_the_United_States "K–12 education in the United States") ("kindergarten through 12th grade"), is decentralized. School systems are operated and largely funded by state, territorial, and sometimes municipal governments and regulated by the [U.S. Department of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Education "United States Department of Education"). In general, children are required to attend school or [an approved homeschool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeschooling_in_the_United_States "Homeschooling in the United States") from the age of five or six ([kindergarten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindergarten "Kindergarten") or [first grade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_grade "First grade")) until they are 18 years old. This often brings students through the [12th grade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelfth_grade "Twelfth grade"), the final year of a U.S. high school, but some states and territories allow them to withdraw earlier without earning a diploma, at age 16 or 17. The U.S. spends more on education per student than any other country, an average of $18,614 per year per public elementary and secondary school student in 2020–2021, though actual spending on individual students varied greatly depending on the community. Among Americans age 25 and older, 92.2% graduated from high school, 62.7% attended some college, 37.7% earned a [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor%27s_degree "Bachelor's degree"), and 14.2% earned a graduate degree. The [U.S. literacy rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy_in_the_United_States "Literacy in the United States") is near-universal. The U.S. has produced the [most Nobel Prize winners of any country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_laureates_by_country "List of Nobel laureates by country"), with [411](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_American_Nobel_laureates "List of American Nobel laureates") (having won 413 awards).

[U.S. tertiary or higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education_in_the_United_States "Higher education in the United States") has earned a global reputation. Many of the world's top universities, as listed by various ranking organizations, are in the United States, including 19 of the top 25. American higher education is dominated by [state university systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_university_system "State university system"), although [the country's many private universities and colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_universities_in_the_United_States "Private universities in the United States") enroll about 20% of all American students. Local [community colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_colleges_in_the_United_States "Community colleges in the United States") generally offer [open admissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_admissions "Open admissions"), lower tuition, and coursework leading to an [associate degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_degree "Associate degree") or a [non-degree certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Certificate_program "Certificate program").

As for [public expenditures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_expenditure "Public expenditure") on higher education, the U.S. spends more per student than the [OECD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OECD "OECD") average, and Americans spend more than all nations in combined public and private spending. Colleges and universities directly funded by the federal government do not charge tuition and are limited to military personnel and government employees, including: the [U.S. service academies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_service_academies "United States service academies"), the [Naval Postgraduate School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Postgraduate_School "Naval Postgraduate School"), and [military staff colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_military_staff_colleges "US military staff colleges"). Despite some student [loan forgiveness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loan_forgiveness "Loan forgiveness") programs in place, [student loan debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_debt "Student debt") increased by 102% between 2010 and 2020, and exceeded $1.7 trillion in 2022.

## Culture and society

The United States is home to [a wide variety of ethnic groups, traditions, and customs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiculturalism_in_the_United_States "Multiculturalism in the United States"). The country has been described as having [the values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americanism_(ideology) "Americanism (ideology)") of [individualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individualism "Individualism") and [personal autonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-libertarianism "Left-libertarianism"), as well as a strong [work ethic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Work_ethic "Work ethic") and [competitiveness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competition "Competition"). Voluntary [altruism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altruism "Altruism") toward others also plays a major role; according to a 2016 study by the [Charities Aid Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charities_Aid_Foundation "Charities Aid Foundation"), Americans donated 1.44% of total GDP to charity—the [highest rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_charitable_donation "List of countries by charitable donation") in the world by a large margin. Americans have traditionally [been characterized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereotypes_of_Americans "Stereotypes of Americans") by a unifying political belief in an "[American Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Creed "American Creed")" that emphasizes [consent of the governed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consent_of_the_governed "Consent of the governed"), [liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty "Liberty"), [equality under the law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equality_under_the_law "Equality under the law"), [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy "Democracy"), [social equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_equality "Social equality"), [property rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_rights "Property rights"), and a preference for [limited government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_government "Limited government"). The U.S. has acquired significant [hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_power "Hard power") and [soft power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_power "Soft power") through [its diplomatic influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_the_United_States "Foreign relations of the United States"), [economic power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_power "Economic power"), [military alliances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_alliance "Military alliance"), and [cultural exports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_exports "Cultural exports") such as [American movies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_the_United_States "Cinema of the United States"), [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_the_United_States "Music of the United States"), [video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_games_in_the_United_States "Video games in the United States"), [sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_the_United_States "Sports in the United States"), and [food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_cuisine "American cuisine"). The influence that the United States exerts on other countries through soft power is referred to as [Americanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americanization "Americanization").

Nearly all present Americans or their ancestors came from [Europe, Africa, or Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Eurasia "Afro-Eurasia") (the "[Old World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World "Old World")") within the past five centuries. [Mainstream](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/mainstream "wikt:mainstream") American culture is a [Western culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_culture "Western culture") largely derived from the [traditions of European immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_American#Culture "European American") with influences from many other sources, such as [traditions brought by slaves from Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African-American_culture "African-American culture"). More recent immigration from [Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_American "Asian American") and especially [Latin America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_culture "Latin American culture") has added to a cultural mix that has been described as a homogenizing [melting pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melting_pot "Melting pot"), and a heterogeneous [salad bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salad_bowl_(cultural_idea) "Salad bowl (cultural idea)"), with immigrants contributing to, and often [assimilating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assimilation_(phonology) "Assimilation (phonology)") into, mainstream American culture.

Under the [First Amendment to the Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "First Amendment to the United States Constitution"), the United States is considered to have the [strongest protections of free speech of any country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_speech_in_the_United_States "Freedom of speech in the United States"). [Flag desecration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_desecration_in_the_United_States "Flag desecration in the United States"), [hate speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hate_speech_in_the_United_States "Hate speech in the United States"), [blasphemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blasphemy_law_in_the_United_States "Blasphemy law in the United States"), and [lese majesty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A8se-majest%C3%A9 "Lèse-majesté") are all forms of protected expression. A 2016 [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center "Pew Research Center") poll found that Americans were the most supportive of free expression of any polity measured. Additionally, they are the "most supportive of [freedom of the press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_the_press_in_the_United_States "Freedom of the press in the United States") and the [right to use the Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_Internet_access "Right to Internet access") without government censorship". The U.S. is a [socially progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_liberalism "Cultural liberalism") country with [permissive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permissive_society "Permissive society") attitudes surrounding [human sexuality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_sexuality "Human sexuality"). [LGBTQ rights in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_rights_in_the_United_States "LGBTQ rights in the United States") are among the most advanced by global standards.

The [American Dream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Dream "American Dream"), or the perception that Americans enjoy high levels of [social mobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socio-economic_mobility_in_the_United_States "Socio-economic mobility in the United States"), plays a key role in attracting immigrants. Whether this perception is accurate has been a topic of debate. While mainstream culture holds that the United States is a [classless society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classless_society "Classless society"), scholars identify significant differences between [the country's social classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_class_in_the_United_States "Social class in the United States"), affecting [socialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialization "Socialization"), language, and values. Americans tend to greatly value [socioeconomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socioeconomics "Socioeconomics") achievement, but [being ordinary or average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Average_Joe "Average Joe") is promoted by some as a noble condition as well.

The [National Foundation on the Arts and the Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Foundation_on_the_Arts_and_the_Humanities "National Foundation on the Arts and the Humanities") is an agency of the United States federal government that was established in 1965 with the purpose to "develop and promote a broadly conceived national policy of support for the humanities and the arts in the United States, and for institutions which preserve the cultural heritage of the United States."

### Literature

Colonial American authors were influenced by [John Locke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Locke "John Locke") and other [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment "Age of Enlightenment") philosophers. The [American Revolutionary Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution "American Revolution") (1765–1783) is notable for the political writings of [Benjamin Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin "Benjamin Franklin"), [Alexander Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Hamilton "Alexander Hamilton"), [Thomas Paine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Paine "Thomas Paine"), and [Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson "Thomas Jefferson"). Shortly before and after the [Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War "American Revolutionary War"), the newspaper rose to prominence, filling a demand for anti-British national literature. An early novel is [William Hill Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Hill_Brown "William Hill Brown")'s *[The Power of Sympathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Power_of_Sympathy "The Power of Sympathy")*, published in 1789. Writer and critic [John Neal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Neal "John Neal") in the early- to mid-19th century helped advance America toward a unique literature and culture by criticizing predecessors such as [Washington Irving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Irving "Washington Irving") for imitating their British counterparts, and by influencing writers such as [Edgar Allan Poe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Allan_Poe "Edgar Allan Poe"), who took American poetry and short fiction in new directions. [Ralph Waldo Emerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Waldo_Emerson "Ralph Waldo Emerson") and [Margaret Fuller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Fuller "Margaret Fuller") pioneered the influential [Transcendentalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendentalism "Transcendentalism") movement; [Henry David Thoreau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_David_Thoreau "Henry David Thoreau"), author of *[Walden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walden "Walden")*, was influenced by this movement.

The conflict surrounding [abolitionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism_in_the_United_States "Abolitionism in the United States") inspired writers, like [Harriet Beecher Stowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harriet_Beecher_Stowe "Harriet Beecher Stowe"), and authors of slave narratives, such as [Frederick Douglass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Douglass "Frederick Douglass"). [Nathaniel Hawthorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathaniel_Hawthorne "Nathaniel Hawthorne")'s *[The Scarlet Letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Scarlet_Letter "The Scarlet Letter")* (1850) explored the dark side of American history, as did [Herman Melville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herman_Melville "Herman Melville")'s *[Moby-Dick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moby-Dick "Moby-Dick")* (1851). Major American poets of the 19th century [American Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Renaissance_(literature) "American Renaissance (literature)") include [Walt Whitman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Whitman "Walt Whitman"), Melville, and [Emily Dickinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Dickinson "Emily Dickinson"). [Mark Twain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Twain "Mark Twain") was the first major American writer to be born in the West. [Henry James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_James "Henry James") achieved international recognition with novels like *[The Portrait of a Lady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Portrait_of_a_Lady "The Portrait of a Lady")* (1881). As literacy rates rose, periodicals published more stories centered around industrial workers, women, and the rural poor. [Naturalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalism_(literature) "Naturalism (literature)"), [regionalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_literary_regionalism "American literary regionalism"), and [realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_realism#UnitedStates "Literary realism") were the major literary movements of the period.

While [modernism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_modernism "Literary modernism") generally took on an international character, modernist authors working within the United States more often rooted their work in specific regions, peoples, and cultures. Following the Great Migration to northern cities, African-American and black [West Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indian_Americans "West Indian Americans") authors of the [Harlem Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlem_Renaissance "Harlem Renaissance") developed an independent tradition of literature that rebuked a history of inequality and celebrated black culture. An important cultural export during the [Jazz Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_Age "Jazz Age"), these writings were a key influence on *[Négritude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%A9gritude "Négritude")*, a philosophy emerging in the 1930s among francophone writers of the [African diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_diaspora "African diaspora"). In the 1950s, an ideal of homogeneity led many authors to attempt to write the [Great American Novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_American_Novel "Great American Novel"), while the [Beat Generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beat_Generation "Beat Generation") rejected this conformity, using styles that elevated the impact of the [spoken word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spoken_word "Spoken word") over mechanics to describe drug use, sexuality, and the failings of society. Contemporary literature is more pluralistic than in previous eras, with the closest thing to a unifying feature being a trend toward self-conscious [experiments with language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_literature "Experimental literature"). Twelve American laureates have won the [Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "Nobel Prize in Literature").

### Mass media

The four major broadcasters in the U.S. are the [National Broadcasting Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Broadcasting_Company "National Broadcasting Company") (NBC), [Columbia Broadcasting System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Broadcasting_System "Columbia Broadcasting System") (CBS), [American Broadcasting Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company "American Broadcasting Company") (ABC), and [Fox Broadcasting Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company "Fox Broadcasting Company") (Fox). The four major broadcast television networks are all commercial entities. The [Public Broadcasting Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS "PBS") (PBS) is the country's major non-commercial [public broadcast network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_broadcaster "Public broadcaster"); it also provides [educational programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_television "Educational television") through local PBS stations. The [U.S. cable television system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_television_in_the_United_States "Cable television in the United States") offers hundreds of channels catering to a variety of niches. In 2021, about 83% of Americans over age 12 listened to [broadcast radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_broadcasting "Radio broadcasting"), while about 40% listened to [podcasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podcast "Podcast"). In the prior year, there were 15,460 licensed full-power radio stations in the U.S. according to the [Federal Communications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission "Federal Communications Commission") (FCC). Public radio broadcasting is largely supplied by [National Public Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Public_Radio "National Public Radio") (NPR), incorporated in February 1970 under the [Public Broadcasting Act of 1967](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Broadcasting_Act_of_1967 "Public Broadcasting Act of 1967").

U.S. newspapers with a global reach and reputation include *[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")*, *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")*, *[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")*, and *[USA Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Today "USA Today")*. [About 800 publications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Spanish-language_newspapers_published_in_the_United_States "List of Spanish-language newspapers published in the United States") are produced in Spanish. With few exceptions, newspapers are privately owned, either by large chains such as [Gannett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gannett_Company "Gannett Company") or [McClatchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_McClatchy_Company "The McClatchy Company"), which own dozens or even hundreds of newspapers; by small chains that own a handful of papers; or, in an increasingly rare situation, by individuals or families. Major cities often have [alternative newspapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_newspaper "Alternative newspaper") to complement the mainstream daily papers, such as *[The Village Voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Village_Voice "The Village Voice")* in New York City and *[LA Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LA_Weekly "LA Weekly")* in Los Angeles. The five [most-visited websites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most-visited_websites "List of most-visited websites") in the world are [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Search "Google Search"), [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube"), [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook"), [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram "Instagram"), and [ChatGPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChatGPT "ChatGPT")—all of them American-owned. Other popular platforms used include [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_(social_network) "X (social network)") (formerly Twitter) and [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_(company) "Amazon (company)"). In 2025, the U.S. was the world's [second-largest video game market by revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_video_games_markets_by_country "List of video games markets by country") (after China). In 2015, the U.S. video game industry consisted of 2,457 companies that employed around 220,000 jobs and generated $30.4 billion in revenue. There are 444 game publishers, developers, and hardware companies in California alone. In the [Game Developers Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Developers_Conference "Game Developers Conference") (GDC) 2025 survey, 58% of responding [game developers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_developer "Video game developer") were based in the U.S.

[Media freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censorship_in_the_United_States "Censorship in the United States") was classified as "problematic" by [Reporters Without Borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reporters_Without_Borders "Reporters Without Borders") in 2026, and scholars have noted a significant rise in [censorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censorship "Censorship") and [self-censorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-censorship "Self-censorship") in recent years.

### Theater

The United States is well known for its theater. Mainstream theater in the United States derives from the old European theatrical tradition and has been heavily influenced by the [British theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_of_the_United_Kingdom "Theatre of the United Kingdom"). By the middle of the 19th century, America had created new distinct dramatic forms in the [Tom Shows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Shows "Tom Shows"), the [showboat theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showboat "Showboat") and the [minstrel show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minstrel_show "Minstrel show"). The central hub of the American theater scene is the [Theater District in Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_District,_Manhattan "Theater District, Manhattan"), with its divisions of [Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_theatre "Broadway theatre"), [off-Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-Broadway "Off-Broadway"), and [off-off-Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-off-Broadway "Off-off-Broadway").

Many movie and television [celebrities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebrity "Celebrity") have gotten their big break working in New York productions. Outside New York City, many cities have professional [regional or resident theater companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_theater_in_the_United_States "Regional theater in the United States") that produce their own seasons. The biggest-budget theatrical productions are musicals. U.S. theater has an active [community theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_theater "Community theater") culture.

The [Tony Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Awards "Tony Awards") recognize excellence in live Broadway theater and are presented at an annual ceremony in [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan "Manhattan"). The awards are given for Broadway productions and performances. One is also given for [regional theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_theatre_in_the_United_States "Regional theatre in the United States").

### Visual arts

[Folk art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_art_of_the_United_States "Folk art of the United States") in [colonial America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_america "Colonial america") grew out of artisanal [craftsmanship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workmanship "Workmanship") in communities that allowed commonly trained people to individually express themselves. It was distinct from Europe's tradition of [high art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_culture "High culture"), which was less accessible and generally less relevant to early American settlers. Cultural movements in art and craftsmanship in colonial America generally lagged behind those of Western Europe. For example, the prevailing medieval style of [woodworking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodworking "Woodworking") and primitive [sculpture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sculpture_of_the_United_States "Sculpture of the United States") became integral to early American folk art, despite the emergence of [Renaissance styles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_art "Renaissance art") in England in the late 16th and early 17th centuries. The new English styles would have been early enough to make a considerable impact on American folk art, but American styles and forms had already been firmly adopted. Not only did styles change slowly in early America, but there was a tendency for rural artisans there to continue their traditional forms longer than their urban counterparts did—and far longer than those in Western Europe.

The [Hudson River School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_River_School "Hudson River School") was a mid-19th-century movement in the visual arts tradition of European [naturalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realism_(arts) "Realism (arts)"). The 1913 [Armory Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armory_Show "Armory Show") in New York City, an exhibition of European [modernist art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_art "Modern art"), shocked the public and transformed the U.S. art scene.

[American Realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Realism "American Realism") and [American Regionalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Regionalism "American Regionalism") sought to reflect and give America new ways of looking at itself. [Georgia O'Keeffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_O%27Keeffe "Georgia O'Keeffe"), [Marsden Hartley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsden_Hartley "Marsden Hartley"), and others experimented with new and individualistic styles, which would become known as [American modernism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_modernism "American modernism"). Major artistic movements such as the [abstract expressionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_expressionism "Abstract expressionism") of [Jackson Pollock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson_Pollock "Jackson Pollock") and [Willem de Kooning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_de_Kooning "Willem de Kooning") and the [pop art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_art "Pop art") of [Andy Warhol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Warhol "Andy Warhol") and [Roy Lichtenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Lichtenstein "Roy Lichtenstein") developed largely in the United States. Major photographers include [Alfred Stieglitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Stieglitz "Alfred Stieglitz"), [Edward Steichen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Steichen "Edward Steichen"), [Dorothea Lange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothea_Lange "Dorothea Lange"), [Edward Weston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Weston "Edward Weston"), [James Van Der Zee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Van_Der_Zee "James Van Der Zee"), [Ansel Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansel_Adams "Ansel Adams"), and [Gordon Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Parks "Gordon Parks").

The tide of [modernism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernism "Modernism") and then [postmodernism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmodernism "Postmodernism") has brought global fame to American architects, including [Frank Lloyd Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Lloyd_Wright "Frank Lloyd Wright"), [Philip Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Johnson "Philip Johnson"), and [Frank Gehry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Gehry "Frank Gehry"). The [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Museum_of_Art "Metropolitan Museum of Art") in [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan "Manhattan") is the largest [art museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_museum "Art museum") in the United States and the [fourth-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_art_museums "List of largest art museums") in the world.

### Music

[American folk music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_folk_music "American folk music") encompasses numerous music genres, variously known as traditional music, traditional [folk music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music"), contemporary folk music, or roots music. Many traditional songs have been sung within the same family or folk group for generations, and sometimes trace back to such origins as the [British Isles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Isles "British Isles"), [mainland Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Europe "Mainland Europe"), or [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African-American_music "African-American music"). The rhythmic and lyrical styles of African-American music in particular have influenced American music. [Banjos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo "Banjo") were brought to America through the slave trade. [Minstrel shows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minstrel_show "Minstrel show") incorporating the instrument into their acts led to its increased popularity and widespread production in the 19th century. The [electric guitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_guitar "Electric guitar"), first invented in the 1930s, and mass-produced by the 1940s, had an enormous influence on popular music, in particular due to the development of [rock and roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_roll "Rock and roll"). The [synthesizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthesizer "Synthesizer"), [turntablism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turntablism "Turntablism"), and [electronic music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_music "Electronic music") were also largely developed in the U.S.

Elements from folk idioms such as the [blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues "Blues") and [old-time music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old-time_music "Old-time music") were adopted and transformed into [popular genres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_music "Popular music") with global audiences. [Jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz") grew from blues and [ragtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragtime "Ragtime") in the early 20th century, developing from the innovations and recordings of composers such as [W.C. Handy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W.C._Handy "W.C. Handy") and [Jelly Roll Morton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jelly_Roll_Morton "Jelly Roll Morton"). [Louis Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Armstrong "Louis Armstrong") and [Duke Ellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_Ellington "Duke Ellington") increased its popularity early in the 20th century. [Country music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music") developed in the 1920s, [bluegrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluegrass_music "Bluegrass music") and [rhythm and blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhythm_and_blues "Rhythm and blues") in the 1940s, and rock and roll in the 1950s. In the 1960s, [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan "Bob Dylan") emerged from the [folk revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_folk_music_revival "American folk music revival") to become one of the country's most celebrated songwriters. The musical forms of [punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock "Punk rock") and [hip hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip_hop "Hip hop") both originated in the United States in the 1970s.

The United States has the world's [largest music market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_recorded_music_markets "List of largest recorded music markets"), with a total retail value of $15.9 billion in 2022, and is the largest exporter of music. Most of the world's [major record companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_label#Major_labels "Record label") are based in the U.S.; they are represented by the [Recording Industry Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America "Recording Industry Association of America") (RIAA). Mid-20th-century American pop stars, such as [Frank Sinatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Sinatra "Frank Sinatra") and [Elvis Presley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_Presley "Elvis Presley"), became [global celebrities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superstar "Superstar") and [best-selling music artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_best-selling_music_artists "List of best-selling music artists"), as have artists of the late 20th century, such as [Michael Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jackson "Michael Jackson"), [Madonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna "Madonna"), [Whitney Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Houston "Whitney Houston"), and [Mariah Carey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariah_Carey "Mariah Carey"), and of the early 21st century, such as [Eminem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eminem "Eminem"), [Britney Spears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britney_Spears "Britney Spears"), [Lady Gaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Gaga "Lady Gaga"), [Katy Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katy_Perry "Katy Perry"), [Taylor Swift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift "Taylor Swift") and [Beyoncé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyonc%C3%A9 "Beyoncé").

### Fashion

The United States has the world's largest [apparel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apparel "Apparel") market by revenue. Apart from professional [business attire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_attire "Business attire"), American fashion is eclectic and predominantly informal. Americans' diverse cultural roots are reflected in their clothing; however, [sneakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sneaker "Sneaker"), [jeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeans "Jeans"), [T-shirts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-shirts "T-shirts"), and [baseball caps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_cap "Baseball cap") are emblematic of American styles. New York, with [its Fashion Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Fashion_Week "New York Fashion Week"), is considered to be one of the "Big Four" global [fashion capitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_capital "Fashion capital"), along with [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Fashion_Week "Paris Fashion Week"), [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Fashion_Week "Milan Fashion Week"), and [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Fashion_Week "London Fashion Week"). A study demonstrated that general proximity to [Manhattan's Garment District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garment_District,_Manhattan "Garment District, Manhattan") has been synonymous with American fashion since its inception in the early 20th century.

A number of well-known [designer labels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Designer_label "Designer label"), among them [Tommy Hilfiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Hilfiger_(company) "Tommy Hilfiger (company)"), [Ralph Lauren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Lauren_Corporation "Ralph Lauren Corporation"), [Tom Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Ford_(brand) "Tom Ford (brand)") and [Calvin Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Klein_(fashion_house) "Calvin Klein (fashion house)"), are headquartered in [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan "Manhattan"). Labels cater to [niche markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niche_market "Niche market"), such as preteens. [New York Fashion Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Fashion_Week "New York Fashion Week") is one of the most influential fashion shows in the world, and is held twice each year in Manhattan; the annual [Met Gala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Met_Gala "Met Gala"), also in Manhattan, has been called the fashion world's "biggest night".

### Cinema

The U.S. film industry has [a worldwide influence and following](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Hollywood "Global Hollywood"). [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood,_Los_Angeles "Hollywood, Los Angeles"), a district in central Los Angeles, the nation's second-most populous city, is also [metonymous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metonymous "Metonymous") for the American filmmaking industry. The [major film studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_film_studios "Major film studios") of the United States are the primary source of the [most commercially successful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_films "List of highest-grossing films") movies selling the most tickets in the world.

Largely centered in the New York City region from its beginnings in the late 19th century through the first decades of the 20th century, the U.S. film industry has since been primarily based in and around Hollywood. Nonetheless, American film companies have been subject to the forces of [globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization "Globalization") in the 21st century, and an increasing number of films are made elsewhere. The [Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards"), popularly known as "the Oscars", have been held annually by the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences "Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences") since 1929, and the [Golden Globes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globes "Golden Globes") have been held annually since January 1944.

The industry peaked in what is commonly referred to as the "[Golden Age of Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Hollywood_cinema "Classical Hollywood cinema")", from the early sound period until the early 1960s, with screen actors such as [John Wayne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wayne "John Wayne") and [Marilyn Monroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marilyn_Monroe "Marilyn Monroe") becoming iconic figures. In the 1970s, "[New Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hollywood "New Hollywood")", or the "Hollywood Renaissance", was defined by grittier films influenced by French and Italian realist pictures of the [post-war period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aftermath_of_World_War_II "Aftermath of World War II"). The 21st century has been marked by the rise of American [streaming platforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_platform "Streaming platform"), which came to rival traditional cinema.

### Cuisine

Early settlers were introduced by Native Americans to foods such as [turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_as_food "Turkey as food"), [sweet potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_potato "Sweet potato"), [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize "Maize"), [squash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cucurbita "Cucurbita"), and [maple syrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple_syrup "Maple syrup"). Of the most enduring and pervasive examples are variations of the native dish called [succotash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Succotash "Succotash"). Early settlers and later immigrants combined these with foods they were familiar with, such as [wheat flour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat_flour "Wheat flour"), beef, and milk, to create a distinctive American cuisine. [New World crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World_crops "New World crops"), especially [pumpkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pumpkin "Pumpkin"), corn, [potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potatoes "Potatoes"), and turkey as the main course are part of a shared national menu on [Thanksgiving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanksgiving_(United_States) "Thanksgiving (United States)"), when many Americans prepare or purchase traditional dishes to celebrate the occasion.

Characteristic American dishes such as [apple pie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_pie "Apple pie"), [fried chicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fried_chicken "Fried chicken"), [doughnuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doughnut "Doughnut"), [french fries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_fries "French fries"), [macaroni and cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macaroni_and_cheese "Macaroni and cheese"), [ice cream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_cream "Ice cream"), [hamburgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburger "Hamburger"), [hot dogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_dog "Hot dog"), and [American pizza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pizza_in_the_United_States "Pizza in the United States") derive from the recipes of various immigrant groups. [Mexican dishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican-American_cuisine "Mexican-American cuisine") such as [burritos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burritos "Burritos") and [tacos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacos "Tacos") preexisted the United States in areas later annexed from Mexico, and [adaptations of Chinese cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Chinese_cuisine "American Chinese cuisine") as well as [pasta dishes freely adapted from Italian sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian-American_cuisine "Italian-American cuisine") are all widely consumed.

American [chefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chef "Chef") have had a significant impact on society both domestically and internationally. In 1946, the [Culinary Institute of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culinary_Institute_of_America "Culinary Institute of America") was founded by [Katharine Angell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katharine_Cramer_Angell "Katharine Cramer Angell") and [Frances Roth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Roth "Frances Roth"). This would become the United States' most prestigious culinary school, where many of the most talented American chefs would study prior to successful careers. The [United States restaurant industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_restaurant_industry "United States restaurant industry") was projected at $899 billion in sales for 2020, and employed more than 15 million people, representing 10% of the nation's workforce directly. It is the country's second-largest private employer and the third-largest employer overall. The United States is home to over 220 [Michelin-starred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelin_star "Michelin star") restaurants, [14 of which were awarded three stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Michelin_3-star_restaurants_in_the_United_States "List of Michelin 3-star restaurants in the United States").

[Wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_wine "American wine") has been produced in what is now the United States since the 1500s, with the [first widespread production beginning in what is now New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mexico_wine "New Mexico wine") in 1628. In the modern U.S., wine production is undertaken in all fifty states, with [California producing 84 percent of all U.S. wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_wine "California wine"). With more than 1,100,000 acres (4,500 km2) under vine, the United States is the [fourth-largest wine-producing country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_wine_production "List of countries by wine production") in the world, after [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_wine "Italian wine"), [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_wine "Spanish wine"), and [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_wine "French wine").

The [classic American diner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diner "Diner"), a casual restaurant type originally intended for the working class, emerged during the 19th century from converted railroad [dining cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dining_car "Dining car") made stationary. The diner soon evolved into purpose-built structures whose number expanded greatly in the 20th century. The American [fast-food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast-food "Fast-food") industry developed alongside the nation's [car culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950s_American_automobile_culture "1950s American automobile culture"). American restaurants developed the [drive-in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drive-in "Drive-in") format in the 1920s, which they began to replace with the [drive-through](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drive-through "Drive-through") format by the 1940s. American [fast-food restaurant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast-food_restaurant "Fast-food restaurant") chains, such as [McDonald's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonald%27s "McDonald's"), [Burger King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burger_King "Burger King"), [Chick-fil-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chick-fil-A "Chick-fil-A"), [Kentucky Fried Chicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_Fried_Chicken "Kentucky Fried Chicken"), [Dunkin' Donuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunkin%27_Donuts "Dunkin' Donuts") and [many others](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fast_food_restaurant_chains#United_States "List of fast food restaurant chains"), have numerous outlets around the world.

### Sports

The most popular spectator sports in the U.S. are [American football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_football_in_the_United_States "American football in the United States"), [basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball_in_the_United_States "Basketball in the United States"), [baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_in_the_United_States "Baseball in the United States"), [soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer_in_the_United_States "Soccer in the United States"), and [ice hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_in_the_United_States "Ice hockey in the United States"). Their [premier leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_in_the_United_States_and_Canada "Major professional sports leagues in the United States and Canada") are, respectively, the [National Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League "National Football League"), [National Basketball Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Basketball_Association "National Basketball Association"), [Major League Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Baseball "Major League Baseball"), [Major League Soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Soccer "Major League Soccer"), and the [National Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Hockey_League "National Hockey League"). All these leagues, excluding soccer, are considered to be preeminent in their respective sports worldwide.

While most major U.S. sports such as baseball and American football have evolved out of European practices, basketball, [volleyball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volleyball_in_the_United_States "Volleyball in the United States"), [skateboarding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skateboarding "Skateboarding"), and [snowboarding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowboarding "Snowboarding") are American inventions, many of which have become popular worldwide. [Lacrosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacrosse_in_the_United_States "Lacrosse in the United States") and [surfing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfing_in_the_United_States "Surfing in the United States") arose from Native American and Native Hawaiian activities that predate European contact. The [U.S. professional sports market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_sports_market_in_the_United_States "Professional sports market in the United States") was approximately $69 billion in July 2013, roughly 50% larger than that of Europe, the Middle East, and Africa combined.

American football is by several measures the most popular spectator sport in the United States; the NFL has the highest average attendance of any professional sports league in the world. Baseball has been regarded as the U.S. "[national sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_sport "National sport")" since the late 19th century. The most-watched [individual sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_sport "Individual sport") in the U.S. are [golf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golf_in_the_United_States "Golf in the United States") and [auto racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auto_racing "Auto racing"), particularly [NASCAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR "NASCAR") and [IndyCar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndyCar "IndyCar").

On the [collegiate level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_athletics_in_the_United_States "College athletics in the United States"), the [NCAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Collegiate_Athletic_Association "National Collegiate Athletic Association") reported $1.1 billion in revenue for the 2017 fiscal year, and [college football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_football "College football") and [basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_basketball "College basketball") attract large audiences, as the [NCAA March Madness tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_men%27s_basketball_tournament "NCAA Division I men's basketball tournament") and the [College Football Playoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_Football_Playoff "College Football Playoff") are some of the most watched national sporting events. In the U.S., the intercollegiate sports level serves as the main feeder system for professional and Olympic sports, with significant exceptions such as [Minor League Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_League_Baseball "Minor League Baseball"). This differs greatly from practices in nearly all other countries, where publicly and privately funded sports organizations serve this function.

Eight [Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Games "Olympic Games") have taken place in the United States, beginning with the [1904 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1904_Summer_Olympics "1904 Summer Olympics") in [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis "St. Louis"), Missouri. The U.S. is scheduled to host the [2028 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2028_Summer_Olympics "2028 Summer Olympics") in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles") and the [2034 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2034_Winter_Olympics "2034 Winter Olympics") in [Salt Lake City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_City "Salt Lake City"). [U.S. athletes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_at_the_Olympics "United States at the Olympics") have won a total of 2,968 medals (1,179 gold) at the Olympic Games, the most of any country.

In other international competition, the United States is the home of a number of prestigious events, including the [America's Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America%27s_Cup "America's Cup"), [World Baseball Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Baseball_Classic "World Baseball Classic"), the [U.S. Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Open_(tennis) "U.S. Open (tennis)"), and the [Masters Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masters_Tournament "Masters Tournament"). The [U.S. men's national soccer team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_men%27s_national_soccer_team "United States men's national soccer team") has qualified for [eleven World Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_at_the_FIFA_World_Cup "United States at the FIFA World Cup"), while the [women's national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_women%27s_national_soccer_team "United States women's national soccer team") has [won](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_at_the_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "United States at the FIFA Women's World Cup") the [FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "FIFA Women's World Cup") and [Olympic soccer tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_Summer_Olympics "Football at the Summer Olympics") four and five times, respectively. The U.S. hosted the World Cup in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup "1994 FIFA World Cup") and co-hosted in [2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup "2026 FIFA World Cup"); it hosted the Women's World Cup in [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "1999 FIFA Women's World Cup") and will co-host in [2031](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2031_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2031 FIFA Women's World Cup").
