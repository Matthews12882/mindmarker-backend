# The Odyssey (2026 film)

***The Odyssey*** is a 2026 epic action fantasy film written and directed by [Christopher Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan "Christopher Nolan"), who produced it with his wife [Emma Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Thomas "Emma Thomas"). An adaptation of [Homer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer "Homer")'s ancient Greek epic poem the *[Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odyssey "Odyssey")*, it stars an ensemble cast including [Matt Damon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Damon "Matt Damon"), [Tom Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Holland "Tom Holland"), [Anne Hathaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hathaway "Anne Hathaway"), [Robert Pattinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pattinson "Robert Pattinson"), [Lupita Nyong'o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupita_Nyong%27o "Lupita Nyong'o"), [Samantha Morton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samantha_Morton "Samantha Morton"), [Zendaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zendaya "Zendaya"), and [Charlize Theron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlize_Theron "Charlize Theron"). In the film, [Odysseus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odysseus "Odysseus") (Damon), the Greek king of [Ithaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer%27s_Ithaca "Homer's Ithaca"), undergoes a long and perilous journey home after the [Trojan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_War "Trojan War") and encounters mythical beings as he attempts to reunite with his wife, [Penelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penelope "Penelope") (Hathaway).

Nolan began writing the script in March 2024 and secured the project with [Universal Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures "Universal Pictures") by October. *The Odyssey* was announced in December. Casting took place from late 2024 through early 2025, and Damon was confirmed for the lead role in February 2025. Nolan studied several [translations of the *Odyssey*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translations_of_the_Odyssey "Translations of the Odyssey") before deciding upon a realistic interpretation of [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology "Greek mythology"), drawing inspiration from the epic historical films *[Andrei Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Rublev_(film) "Andrei Rublev (film)")* (1966) and *[Ran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ran_(film) "Ran (film)")* (1985), as well as the films of special effects artist [Ray Harryhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Harryhausen "Ray Harryhausen"). Filming took place on location from February to August 2025 in Morocco, Greece, Italy, Scotland, Iceland, Western Sahara, and Malta, as well as at the [Universal Studios Lot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Studios_Lot "Universal Studios Lot") in Los Angeles. With an estimated budget of [US$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar "United States dollar")250 million, it is among the most expensive films of [Nolan's career](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan_filmography "Christopher Nolan filmography"), and the first feature film to be shot entirely on [IMAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAX "IMAX")'s [70 mm film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/70_mm_film#IMAX "70 mm film") cameras.

*The Odyssey* premiered at the [Odeon Luxe Leicester Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odeon_Luxe_Leicester_Square "Odeon Luxe Leicester Square") in London on July 6, 2026, ahead of a theatrical release by Universal Pictures on July 17 in the United States and the United Kingdom. It was made available in IMAX and multiple premium large formats. The film received acclaim for its direction, performances, and set pieces, though some criticized Nolan's [deviations from the source material](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan%27s_interpretation_of_the_Odyssey "Christopher Nolan's interpretation of the Odyssey"). It has grossed $1.784 billion worldwide, becoming Nolan's highest-grossing film, the [second-highest-grossing film of 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_in_film#Highest-grossing_films "2026 in film"), the [highest-grossing R-rated film ever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_R-rated_films "List of highest-grossing R-rated films"), the [highest-grossing film distributed by Universal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures#Highest-grossing_films "Universal Pictures"), and the [eleventh-highest grossing film of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_films#Highest-grossing_films "List of highest-grossing films").

## Plot

For twenty years, [Ithaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer%27s_Ithaca "Homer's Ithaca") has been kingless since [Odysseus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odysseus "Odysseus") did not return from the [Trojan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_War "Trojan War"). Several [suitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suitors_of_Penelope "Suitors of Penelope"), including nobleman [Antinous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antinous_of_Ithaca "Antinous of Ithaca"), aggressively court Odysseus's wife [Penelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penelope "Penelope") to claim the throne from Odysseus and Penelope's son [Telemachus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telemachus "Telemachus"). Only Penelope, Telemachus, and Odysseus' half-blind servant [Eumaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eumaeus "Eumaeus") still believe Odysseus will return. Penelope tells Telemachus she fears attacks by murderous "[Sea Peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Peoples "Sea Peoples")" who violate [Zeus's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenia_(Greek) "Xenia (Greek)"), the custom of respect between guests and hosts. Telemachus travels with Odysseus's friend [Mentor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mentor_(Odyssey) "Mentor (Odyssey)") to [Menelaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menelaus "Menelaus"), King of [Sparta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparta "Sparta"), to ascertain his father's whereabouts. Antinous learns about this and conspires to kill Telemachus upon his return and plunder Ithaca. Menelaus and his wife, [Helen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_of_Troy "Helen of Troy"), tell Telemachus about the war, but express uncertainty about Odysseus's fate.

Meanwhile, the goddess [Athena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athena "Athena") appears to Odysseus on the island of [Ogygia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogygia "Ogygia"), where the nymph [Calypso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calypso_(mythology) "Calypso (mythology)") is infatuated with Odysseus. Athena commands Odysseus to return home; Calypso dislikes this, but encourages Odysseus to remember his past. Years earlier, the Greek army, led by Menelaus' brother [Agamemnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agamemnon "Agamemnon"), conquered [Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy "Troy") using Odysseus' stratagem, the [Trojan Horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_Horse "Trojan Horse"), hiding Greeks inside, with the soldier [Sinon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinon "Sinon") sacrificed as a decoy. While Menelaus and Agamemnon returned home, Odysseus and his crew, including second-in-command [Eurylochus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurylochus "Eurylochus"), searched an island for supplies. However, the cyclops [Polyphemus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyphemus "Polyphemus") traps them in a cave and devours several soldiers. They blind Polyphemus and escape, but hear him cry out to his father, the sea god [Poseidon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poseidon "Poseidon"). Consequently, the crew suffers harsh sea conditions. They land on [Telepylus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telepylus "Telepylus") where [Laestrygonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laestrygonians "Laestrygonians") (man-eating giants), kill most of them and sink two of their three ships. The survivors rebel, leaving Odysseus with their ship as they seek refuge on [Aeaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeaea "Aeaea") with the witch [Circe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circe "Circe"), who turns them into pigs. After Odysseus persuades her to change his men back, Circe advises that to reach Ithaca, Odysseus must cross the river [Oceanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanus#Geography "Oceanus") into [Hades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_underworld "Greek underworld").

There, Odysseus meets his fallen soldiers, including Sinon's [shade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shade_(mythology) "Shade (mythology)"), who reproaches him for deceiving him, and reveals that Antinous manipulated him into taking his place in the Greek army. Next, Agamemnon's shade reveals that his wife [Clytemnestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clytemnestra "Clytemnestra") murdered him upon his return as revenge for sacrificing their daughter [Iphigenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iphigenia "Iphigenia") to [Artemis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemis "Artemis") for fair seas. Finally, Odysseus speaks with the shade of the prophet [Tiresias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiresias "Tiresias"), who predicts that, by divine will, only Odysseus will return home. Chased from the underworld by Odysseus' fallen army, Odysseus follows Tiresias' guidance to survive encounters with the [Sirens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siren_(mythology) "Siren (mythology)") and sea monsters [Charybdis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charybdis "Charybdis") and [Scylla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scylla "Scylla"). On [Thrinacia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrinacia "Thrinacia"), Odysseus warns his men not to kill the [cattle of Apollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle_of_Helios "Cattle of Helios"), as Tiresias foretold Odysseus' men would die if they did, but the starving men disobey. [Zeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeus "Zeus") sends a storm that sinks their ship, killing all but Odysseus. He washes ashore unconscious on Ogygia, where Calypso's lotus flowers render him [amnesic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retrograde_amnesia "Retrograde amnesia").

After seven years, Odysseus regains his memories, builds a raft, and surrenders his fate to the storm. He washes ashore in Ithaca, where he disguises himself and bears the alias "Sinon". Eumaeus tells him that the suitors sent bandits to assassinate Telemachus and Mentor. The bandits kill Mentor, but Odysseus rescues Telemachus and kills the bandits. Odysseus's elderly dog [Argos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argos_(dog) "Argos (dog)") dies as he recognizes Odysseus, and Telemachus deduces Odysseus's identity. During a feast, Odysseus confronts Antinous about betraying Sinon. Antinous thrashes Odysseus before Telemachus intervenes.

After the meal, Odysseus, still disguised, confesses to Penelope his guilt for violating Zeus's law during the [sacking of Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_War#Sack_of_Troy "Trojan War"), desecrating [Athena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athena "Athena")'s statue after killing her priestess (whose form Athena assumed in visions), and initiating the [Late Bronze Age collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Bronze_Age_collapse "Late Bronze Age collapse"). He reveals that the "sea people" were the Greeks themselves, and begs Penelope's forgiveness. Penelope announces her readiness to remarry and instructs the suitors to return at dawn.

Penelope declares she will marry whomever can string Odysseus' bow and shoot an arrow through a row of axe heads. All the suitors fail, but Odysseus succeeds, thereby revealing his identity. The hall erupts in battle. Telemachus aids Odysseus in killing many of the challenging suitors, including Antinous. After the remaining suitors surrender, Odysseus cedes his throne to Telemachus and leaves with Penelope to seek peace in exile. Sailing west to honor Odysseus' fallen soldiers, the couple expect civilization's renewal and muse that time will forget Odysseus' mistakes.

## Production

### Development and writing

By November 2023, director [Christopher Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan "Christopher Nolan") had yet to decide what his next film would be after *[Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppenheimer_(film) "Oppenheimer (film)")* (2023). While he was receptive to various ideas, he felt he needed to "own it completely". After winning several [Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards") for *Oppenheimer* in March 2024, including [Best Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture") and [Best Director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Director "Academy Award for Best Director"), Nolan and [Emma Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Thomas "Emma Thomas"), his wife and producing partner, felt the success provided them the opportunity to explore what Nolan's next film would be. Nolan began writing the script shortly after. Nolan was revealed in October to be developing his next film at [Universal Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures "Universal Pictures"), after working with that studio on *Oppenheimer*. He was confirmed to be writing the script and producing once again with Thomas through their production company, [Syncopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncopy_Inc. "Syncopy Inc."), and filming was scheduled to begin in early 2025. Unlike *Oppenheimer*, which Universal acquired at auction, *The Odyssey* was set up at the studio and given a release date of July 17, 2026.

The [logline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logline "Logline") and additional details of its premise were kept largely secret compared to Nolan's previous films, which prompted much speculation about its subject matter. This included rumors that it would either be a vampire [period piece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Period_piece "Period piece"), a [reboot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reboot_(fiction) "Reboot (fiction)") of the 1983 helicopter action-thriller film *[Blue Thunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Thunder "Blue Thunder")*, or an adaptation of the British spy television series *[The Prisoner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prisoner "The Prisoner")* (1967–1968), which Nolan was previously attached to in 2009, but all of these were debunked. Nolan later reflected the "disadvantage of never telling people what we're doing" was the invention and circulation of rumors, though he nonetheless enjoyed them. In December 2024, Universal announced Nolan's new film as *The Odyssey*, an adaptation of [Homer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer "Homer")'s ancient Greek [epic poem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_poem "Epic poem"), the *[Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odyssey "Odyssey")*. The studio described it as a "mythic action epic" that would be filmed worldwide. Nolan was previously attached to direct *[Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy_(film) "Troy (film)")* (2004)—based on Homer's *Odyssey* predecessor the *[Iliad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iliad "Iliad")*—that [Wolfgang Petersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Petersen "Wolfgang Petersen") had developed for [Warner Bros. Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures "Warner Bros. Pictures"), before Petersen chose to direct *Troy* and Nolan subsequently made *[Batman Begins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batman_Begins "Batman Begins")* (2005). Nolan believed he was "in a little over [his] head" for *Troy*, but felt he had become more qualified to adapt the "sweeping story" of the *Odyssey* after working on several large-scale productions during the following 20 years. *The Odyssey* was expected to be the most expensive film of [Nolan's career](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan_filmography "Christopher Nolan filmography") with a net budget of $250 million.

Nolan described *The Odyssey* as the largest and most challenging production of his career due to its scale. He explained that he chose to adapt the *Odyssey* because it had not been adapted into a large-scale production from a major Hollywood studio, calling it "an odd gap in movie history". Nolan and Thomas described *The Odyssey* as "foundational" because it incorporates aspects of the horror, [mystery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mystery_film "Mystery film"), romance, and thriller genres of stories. Nolan also said that *The Odyssey* had influenced his previous films. He read and studied *The Odyssey* and [several of its translations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translations_of_the_Odyssey "Translations of the Odyssey"), including those by classicists [Emily Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Wilson_(classicist) "Emily Wilson (classicist)"), [E. V. Rieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._V._Rieu "E. V. Rieu"), and [Robert Fagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Fagles "Robert Fagles"). He wrote the script in a [non-linear fashion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_narrative "Nonlinear narrative"). While the script was said to be "very faithful" to Homer's text, Nolan made some alterations, such as more prominently featuring Odysseus's faithful dog [Argos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argos_(dog) "Argos (dog)"); more interactions between Odysseus and Telemachus; humanizing the goddess and witch [Circe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circe "Circe") to be unsettling yet sympathetic; and complicating the reunion between [Helen of Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_of_Troy "Helen of Troy") and Spartan king [Menelaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menelaus "Menelaus"). He was interested in portraying Odysseus's cleverness and inventiveness, particularly citing his characterization in Wilson's [2017 translation of the *Odyssey*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odyssey_(Emily_Wilson_translation) "Odyssey (Emily Wilson translation)").

To combine the *Odyssey*'s fantastical elements with Nolan's use of "tactile realism", the director chose to take a realistic approach in depicting the evidence and actions of the gods through natural phenomena once considered "supernatural" during the story's time period. Nolan called this a major creative breakthrough. Nolan cited [Andrei Tarkovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Tarkovsky "Andrei Tarkovsky")'s *[Andrei Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Rublev_(film) "Andrei Rublev (film)")* (1966) and [Akira Kurosawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akira_Kurosawa "Akira Kurosawa")'s *[Ran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ran_(film) "Ran (film)")* (1985) as major inspirations, particularly regarding its visual style and atmospheric tone. Nolan also drew inspiration from various [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology "Greek mythology")–inspired films he watched in his youth, particularly the works of animator and special effects artist [Ray Harryhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Harryhausen "Ray Harryhausen"). Nolan had considered casting actors to portray [Greek gods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_gods "Greek gods") throwing thunderbolts from [Mount Olympus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Olympus "Mount Olympus"), but found depicting the characters' fear of the gods' actions provided a more immersive experience than outright showing the gods.

### Design and special effects

Production designer [Ruth De Jong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_De_Jong "Ruth De Jong") and costume designer [Ellen Mirojnick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellen_Mirojnick "Ellen Mirojnick") returned to work with Nolan from *Oppenheimer*. Mirojnick discussed her emphasis "on tactility—giving every piece of linen, leather, armour and sandal the texture and dimensions of lived history." Her costume design extended to the [Mycenaean Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenaean_Greece "Mycenaean Greece") costumes for both kings and soldiers, and the draping costumes of both [Athena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athena "Athena") and [Calypso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calypso_(mythology) "Calypso (mythology)"). De Jong designed the sets and ornaments, including the 36 foot (11 metres) tall and 8,800-pound model of the [Trojan Horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_Horse "Trojan Horse") that was transported in 27 crates to the seashore.

To depict the armor, weapons, and ships, Nolan said that the production thoroughly researched the "very fragmentary archeological records" of the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age "Bronze Age") and [Ancient Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece "Ancient Greece") during Homer's life. On the production choices, Nolan explained that the "oldest depictions of Homeric characters tend to be depicted in the manner of people living in Homer's time. So there's a pretty strong case there for portraying things that way because that's the way the first audience received the story." Nolan added that Mirojnick sought to convey through the armor worn by [Agamemnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agamemnon "Agamemnon") that he had an elevated status by using materials more expensive to obtain, such as [blackened bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatizon "Hepatizon"), silver, and [sulfur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur "Sulfur").

The Cyclops was achieved via a combination of [animatronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animatronics "Animatronics"), [puppetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppetry "Puppetry"), and an on-set performance by Bill Irwin, with its design taking influence from the [Francisco Goya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Goya "Francisco Goya") painting *[Saturn Devouring His Son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn_Devouring_His_Son "Saturn Devouring His Son")*. The soldiers eaten by the Cyclops were small semi-edible models with an inner skeleton and burstable bags of blood. Likewise, the sequence where [Circe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circe "Circe") transforms Odysseus' crew into pigs was achieved through a combination of animatronics, puppetry, [makeup effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosthetic_makeup "Prosthetic makeup") and [stage magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stage_magic "Stage magic") techniques. [Wētā Workshop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C4%93t%C4%81_Workshop "Wētā Workshop") provided special effects for the production.

### Filming

[Principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography "Principal photography") lasted for 91 days between February 25 and August 5, 2025,[*[clarification needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")*] using the [working title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_title "Working title") *Charlie's Tale*, and [wrapped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrap_(filmmaking) "Wrap (filmmaking)") nine days ahead of schedule. [Location shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Location_shooting "Location shooting") primarily took place in six countries: Morocco, Western Sahara, Greece, Italy, Iceland, Scotland, and the United States. Nolan sought to embrace the "physicality of the real world" by filming on location to help inform the storytelling and capture "how hard those journeys would have been for people. And the leap of faith that was being made in an unmapped, uncharted world." The director felt that the shifting weather conditions amplified this practical approach due to the challenges they posed. [Hoyte van Hoytema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoyte_van_Hoytema "Hoyte van Hoytema") reunited with Nolan as cinematographer.

After using [IMAX 70 mm film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/70_mm_film#IMAX "70 mm film") extensively since *[The Dark Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dark_Knight "The Dark Knight")* (2008), *The Odyssey* became the first feature film shot entirely with [IMAX film cameras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAX_film_cameras "IMAX film cameras"), primarily using a newly manufactured lighter and quieter version Nolan had specifically developed for the production alongside older versions. For close-up scenes requiring audible dialogue, the IMAX cameras were often concealed in large [sound blimps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_blimp "Sound blimp") on set, while a system of mirrors was set up to help the actors maintain eye contact when the cameras obscured their view. Over 2 million feet (610 kilometres) of IMAX 70 mm film was used to shoot *The Odyssey*. As a single IMAX camera can only hold roughly three minutes of film, actors had to pause during longer scenes while the camera was reloaded before resuming their performances.

The first scenes were filmed in late February at the [Aït Benhaddou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%AFt_Benhaddou "Aït Benhaddou") village near [Ouarzazate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouarzazate "Ouarzazate"), Morocco, to depict the city of [Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy "Troy") at the end of the [Trojan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_War "Trojan War") in an appetizer sequence before the bulk of *The Odyssey*, as well as in the cities of [Essaouira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essaouira "Essaouira") and [Marrakesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marrakesh "Marrakesh"). Van Hoytema rigged numerous portable LEDs to emulate the specific firelight color temperature for the midnight raid of Troy, allowing Nolan to film in any direction. Filming took place in the [Messenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messenia "Messenia") unit of the [Peloponnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnese_(region) "Peloponnese (region)") region in Greece, from March 10 to 21, at [Pylos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pylos "Pylos"), the [Methoni Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methoni_Castle "Methoni Castle"), the Almyrolakkos beach in [Yialova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gialova "Gialova"), and Nestor's Cave in the [Voidokilia beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voidokilia_beach "Voidokilia beach") for scenes featuring the [Cyclops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclops "Cyclops") [Polyphemus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyphemus "Polyphemus"), as well as at an archaeological palace site in [Acrocorinth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acrocorinth "Acrocorinth"), [Corinth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corinth_(modern_city) "Corinth (modern city)"). The production partnered with [Faliro House Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faliro_House_Productions "Faliro House Productions")'s NAF subsidiary for filming in Greece, which lasted three weeks. The production received over [€](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro "Euro")6.5 million ($7.5 million) in funding from Greece's Hellenic Film and Audiovisual Center S.A. (Creative Greece), as part of its approximate [€](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro "Euro")140 million ($160 million) investment in over 200 local and international productions, as well as a 40% cash [rebate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebate_(marketing) "Rebate (marketing)").

By March 27, the production moved to the [Aegadian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegadian_Islands "Aegadian Islands") in Sicily, Italy, to film on the island of [Favignana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Favignana "Favignana"), believed to be the location known as the "goat island" in the poem. Castello di Santa Caterina and the surrounding area stood in for the exteriors of Ithaca. Water-borne filming subsequently took place from April 15 to May 15 throughout the [Aeolian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeolian_Islands "Aeolian Islands") of [Lipari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipari "Lipari"), [Basiluzzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basiluzzo "Basiluzzo"), and [Vulcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulcano "Vulcano") for scenes featuring the mythological island of [Aeolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeolia_(mythical_island) "Aeolia (mythical island)"). Filming in these regions was subject to certain safeguarding restrictions from [local ordinances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_ordinance "Local ordinance"), with [Fremantle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremantle_(company) "Fremantle (company)")'s Italian production company Wildside servicing the shoot. For the maritime sequences, the production used the largest modern [Viking longship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_longship "Viking longship"), the *[Draken Harald Hårfagre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draken_Harald_H%C3%A5rfagre "Draken Harald Hårfagre")*, which doubled as an ancient Greek warship that the actors portraying Odysseus's crew commanded, and was sailed to each of the filming locations. Nolan described filming at sea as a particularly primal experience.

Halfway through shooting by early May, filming took place at the Falls Lake [water tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_tank "Water tank") on the [Universal Studios Lot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Studios_Lot "Universal Studios Lot") in Los Angeles, the production's only set constructed on a studio [sound stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_stage "Sound stage"), before shooting at [Findlater Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Findlater_Castle "Findlater Castle") in [Moray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moray "Moray"), Scotland, in early June. For ten days in late June, the production partnered with [Truenorth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truenorth_(production_company) "Truenorth (production company)") in remote regions of Iceland, including the harbor [Landeyjahöfn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landeyjah%C3%B6fn "Landeyjahöfn"), the [Hjörleifshöfði](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hj%C3%B6rleifsh%C3%B6f%C3%B0i "Hjörleifshöfði") mountain, the river [Markarfljót](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markarflj%C3%B3t "Markarfljót"), and the [Snæfellsnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sn%C3%A6fellsnes "Snæfellsnes") peninsula, primarily to shoot scenes on their [black sand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_sand "Black sand") beaches for the [Greek underworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_underworld "Greek underworld") of Hades, before returning to Scotland for scenes at the [Buckie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckie "Buckie") Harbour along the [Moray Firth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moray_Firth "Moray Firth") coast and at the secluded Sunnyside Beach near [Cullen, Moray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cullen,_Moray "Cullen, Moray"). Filming took place in the [Culbin Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culbin_Forest "Culbin Forest") from July 3 to 16, where the giant [Laestrygonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laestrygonians "Laestrygonians") were portrayed using a variety of practical techniques including [forced perspective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_perspective "Forced perspective"), [wire work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wire-flying "Wire-flying"), and having seven-foot tall [stuntmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuntmen "Stuntmen") play the giants opposite five-foot tall [body doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_doubles "Body doubles") for the other members of the cast. Damon's double for the scene was four-foot-six stuntwoman [Devyn Dalton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devyn_Dalton "Devyn Dalton"), and an image of her from behind appears in one of the film posters. The Laestrygonians are portrayed as having more technologically sophisticated armor and weaponry than anything previously encountered by Odysseus to emphasize the brutality of the encounter and test his leadership. Filming returned to Findlater Castle through July 25, with Theron filming her scenes during the last two weeks in July. Some filming also took place in Malta for [Calypso's Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calypso%27s_Cave "Calypso's Cave"), before concluding back at the Universal lot for most of the last week of filming. Filming was reportedly set to occur in Ireland and the United Kingdom.

Filming took place between July 17 and 22, with Damon and Zendaya, at the White Dune near [Dakhla, Western Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakhla,_Western_Sahara "Dakhla, Western Sahara"), a territory under [Moroccan occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan-occupied_Western_Sahara "Moroccan-occupied Western Sahara") since 1975. The UN-recognized [Polisario Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polisario_Front "Polisario Front") and organizers of the [Sahara International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara_International_Film_Festival "Sahara International Film Festival") decried the decision to film in Western Sahara as [whitewashing](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/whitewash#Verb "wikt:whitewash") Morocco's colonialism. The festival's organizers called for the production to be halted, but filming in the territory had already concluded. They subsequently released a statement calling on Nolan to obtain consent from "the legal representatives of the [Sahrawi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahrawi_people "Sahrawi people")" or otherwise remove the scenes shot in Dakhla. This statement was signed by several prominent figures, including Spanish actors [Carlos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Bardem "Carlos Bardem") and [Javier Bardem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_Bardem "Javier Bardem"), [Luis Tosar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Tosar "Luis Tosar"), [Carolina Yuste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolina_Yuste "Carolina Yuste"), and [Juan Diego Botto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Diego_Botto "Juan Diego Botto"), Spanish filmmakers [Rodrigo Sorogoyen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigo_Sorogoyen "Rodrigo Sorogoyen") and [Icíar Bollaín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ic%C3%ADar_Bolla%C3%ADn "Icíar Bollaín"), and Sahrawi human rights defender [Elghalia Djimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elghalia_Djimi "Elghalia Djimi"). Meanwhile, the [Moroccan Cinematographic Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan_Cinematographic_Center "Moroccan Cinematographic Center") called *The Odyssey* an important production to promote the [Moroccan film industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_Morocco "Cinema of Morocco") and said that it was the first major American film to be filmed in that territory.

### Editing and visual effects

Over 2 million feet of 15-perf 65mm [negative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_(photography) "Negative (photography)") film stock shot for *The Odyssey* was developed and processed at [FotoKem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FotoKem "FotoKem") in [Burbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burbank,_California "Burbank, California"), the last film laboratory producing 70mm prints. The cost of the film stock alone totaled roughly $3 million. The film stock was scanned and converted into high-resolution digital files for editor [Jennifer Lame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Lame "Jennifer Lame") to make initial cuts. The film negative would then be cut and spliced by hand. [Color grading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_grading "Color grading") was done through the analog method of color filters on a [light table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_table "Light table") with numerical values assigned to each shot for the final print.

[DNEG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNEG "DNEG") provided the [visual effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_effects "Visual effects") for *The Odyssey*, with [Andrew Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Jackson_(visual_effects) "Andrew Jackson (visual effects)") serving as visual effects supervisor and editor [Jennifer Lame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Lame "Jennifer Lame") returning from previous Nolan productions. Unlike the Cyclops and Laestrygonians, the six headed [Scylla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scylla "Scylla") was a predominantly digital effect, with Nolan stating the creature was inspired by the works of [Guillermo del Toro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillermo_del_Toro "Guillermo del Toro"), saying, "What I learned from him is that a monster is not a monster. You have to approach them the way you approach any other character." A ratchet system was devised to lift stunt performers into the air for the Scylla's attack, while the nearby [whirlpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whirlpool "Whirlpool") was created by having [jet skis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_ski "Jet ski") ride in circles as reference material to be replaced later by digital effects.

## Music

By April 2025, [Ludwig Göransson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_G%C3%B6ransson "Ludwig Göransson") was composing the musical score for *The Odyssey* after previously working with Nolan on *Tenet* and *Oppenheimer*. Nolan wanted to avoid the typical orchestral music commonly heard in other [sword-and-sandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sword-and-sandal "Sword-and-sandal") films. To "evoke the materiality" and setting of the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age "Bronze Age"), Göransson included in the score: various bronze [gongs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gong "Gong") layered with synthesizers; bronze instruments; at Nolan's suggestion, the sound of the ancient [lyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyre "Lyre") for when Odysseus plucks his bow, and the ancient [aulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aulos "Aulos"); [Albanian iso-polyphony folk chorals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_iso-polyphony "Albanian iso-polyphony") and flutes recorded by Albanian shepherds; and cacophonies of sheet metal alarm drums.

Musicians Callum Armstrong and Rosa Fragorapti played replicas of an ancient [aulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aulos "Aulos") and [lyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyre "Lyre"). Due to the obsolescence of the instruments, they had to research ancient source material to use them properly. English electronic musician [James Blake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Blake_(musician) "James Blake (musician)") and American rapper [Travis Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_Scott "Travis Scott") (who appears in the film) also contributed vocals, and the two collaborated with Göransson on an original song, "When I'm Home", which plays over the closing credits. Nolan is credited as a co-writer of the song.

The soundtrack album was released digitally by Universal's [Back Lot Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_Lot_Music "Back Lot Music") label on July 17, 2026, coinciding with the release date. Record manufacturer Mutant released a triple [vinyl LP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinyl_LP "Vinyl LP") version of the soundtrack on July 31, 2026, followed by a [double album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_album "Double album") [CD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD "CD") release on August 14, 2026.

## Marketing

Before the start of filming in February 2025, Universal Pictures released a "surprise" first-look still image of Damon in costume as Odysseus. Jen Juneau at *[People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_(magazine) "People (magazine)")* described Damon's side-profile pose as "brooding" and "intimidating". The costume—particularly its [Corinthian helmet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corinthian_helmet "Corinthian helmet") with a red [plume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plume_(feather) "Plume (feather)") in place of a [boar's tusk helmet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boar%27s_tusk_helmet "Boar's tusk helmet")—was labeled by various commentators and historians as historically inaccurate, both in terms of the armor Odysseus would have worn around the time the *Odyssey* takes place and of what is described in the *Iliad*. *[/Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki//Film "/Film")*'s Jeremy Mathai felt the image did not provide much insight into the tone or Nolan's approach to the source material, and acknowledged the costume's similarities to ancient Greek attire typically depicted in films. Joe George at *[Den of Geek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Den_of_Geek "Den of Geek")* felt Nolan paid homage to the [New Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hollywood "New Hollywood") films from his childhood, such as *[The 300 Spartans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_300_Spartans "The 300 Spartans")* (1962) and *[Jason and the Argonauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_and_the_Argonauts_(film) "Jason and the Argonauts (film)")* (1963), especially animator [Ray Harryhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Harryhausen "Ray Harryhausen")'s stop-motion visual effects work on the latter. ## Release

### Theatrical

*The Odyssey* held its [world premiere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_premiere "World premiere") on July 6, 2026, at the [Odeon Luxe Leicester Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odeon_Luxe_Leicester_Square "Odeon Luxe Leicester Square") in London. As part of the global promotional tour, a two-day promotional showcase and official premiere event was held from July 10 to 11, 2026, in Mumbai, marking the first time a film directed by Christopher Nolan held an official premiere event in India. Another premiere was held on July 14, 2026, at the [AMC Theatres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMC_Theatres "AMC Theatres") venue on [Lincoln Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Square,_Manhattan "Lincoln Square, Manhattan") in New York City.

*The Odyssey* was released theatrically by [Universal Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures "Universal Pictures") on July 17, 2026, in the United States and the United Kingdom. It has been presented in a variety of [aspect ratios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspect_ratio_(image) "Aspect ratio (image)") depending on the exhibition format: 1.43:1 on [IMAX 70 mm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/70_mm_film#IMAX "70 mm film") film prints, 1.90:1 on [IMAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAX "IMAX") digital screens, 2.20:1 on [70 mm film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/70_mm_film "70 mm film") prints, 2.39:1 on [35 mm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/35_mm_movie_film "35 mm movie film") prints, and either 2.39:1 or 1.85:1 for other [premium large format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premium_large_format "Premium large format") (PLF) screens, such as [D-Box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-Box "D-Box") and RX, depending on the specifications of individual auditoriums. Only 41 screens worldwide can present the film in IMAX 70mm, 25 in the United States, 9 in Canada, 3 in the UK, and 1 each in Australia, Belgium, France and the Czech Republic, with the [IMAX Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAX_Melbourne "IMAX Melbourne") screen in Australia being the only venue in the [Southern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Hemisphere "Southern Hemisphere") capable of doing so. This is an increase from the 30 IMAX 70mm prints for Nolan's previous film *Oppenheimer*, due to an effort from the [IMAX Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAX_Corporation "IMAX Corporation") to locate, refurbish and install disused IMAX 70mm projectors, as well as train 60 new projectionists. *The Odyssey* was released theatrically in China on nearly 800 IMAX screens on August 14, 2026, following early previews beginning on August 1 and 2 in Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou and Shenzhen.

### Home media

*The Odyssey* will be available on digital platforms and [Ultra HD Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_HD_Blu-ray "Ultra HD Blu-ray") from November 17, 2026, 120 days after its theatrical release. Presented in a 4K [HDR transfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-dynamic-range_television "High-dynamic-range television") in a 1.78:1 aspect ratio with a [DTS-HD MA 5.1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DTS-HD_Master_Audio "DTS-HD Master Audio") soundtrack, the decision to simultaneously release on UHD Blu-ray and digital has been interpreted as part of Nolan's longstanding proponency for [physical media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_media "Physical media") over [streaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming "Streaming").

## Reception

### Box office

As of October 14, 2026[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=The_Odyssey_(2026_film)&action=edit), *The Odyssey* has grossed $624 million in the United States and Canada, and $1.16 billion in other territories, for a worldwide total of $1.784 billion. Produced on a $250 million budget, *The Odyssey* is among the most expensive of [Nolan's career](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan_filmography "Christopher Nolan filmography") and is [one of the most expensive films ever made](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_expensive_films "List of most expensive films"). The film became Nolan's first to surpass $1 billion since *The Dark Knight Rises.*

#### Pre-sale ticket records

Tickets for select opening weekend IMAX 70 mm screenings were made available on July 17, 2025, one year before its release, which was considered an unprecedented move by a major film distributor. Some of these showings sold out within the first 12 hours of their availability, including half of the 22 theaters available in the United States, earning approximately $1.5 million.

Tickets for the remainder of IMAX, IMAX 70 mm, and premium format screenings went on sale on June 4, 2026, ahead of general admission tickets being made available later that month. Ticketing websites [Fandango Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fandango_Media "Fandango Media") and [AMC Theatres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMC_Theatres "AMC Theatres") experienced delayed queues, with initial wait times lasting up to an hour due to the high influx of users attempting to purchase tickets, comparable to that of the 2023 concert film *[Taylor Swift: The Eras Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift:_The_Eras_Tour "Taylor Swift: The Eras Tour")*. Emily St. Martin at the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")* described attempts to purchase tickets as "an epic journey of its own". $3.4 million was accumulated from approximately 150,000 pre-sale tickets in the first 24 hours, making it the highest pre-sale gross in four years at that time. The first-day PLF pre-sales were also the highest pre-sales for a major studio release for AMC since 2022, surpassing the first-day pre-sales for any film from 2025 and early 2026. *The Odyssey* sold 28,000 presale tickets at the [BFI IMAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BFI_IMAX "BFI IMAX") in 24 hours, earning £750,000 ($1 million). This more than doubled the £366,000 ($490,000) gross of the venue's previous record holder, *[Dune: Part Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dune:_Part_Two "Dune: Part Two")* (2024), and surpassed the £254,000 ($340,000) gross of *Oppenheimer* during the same timeframe. [Richard Gelfond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Gelfond "Richard Gelfond") confirmed that by July 16, 2026, many IMAX venues had already sold out into the film's fifth weekend.

#### Performance

In France, *The Odyssey* opened on July 15, 2026, in 730 theaters and drew 230,000 viewers on its opening day, surpassing the opening of *Oppenheimer* (160,000 viewers). In the United States and Canada, it earned $51.2 million from its opening day, including $17.6 million from Thursday night previews, which was the highest for any film during that time frame for the year, surpassing *[Toy Story 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toy_Story_5 "Toy Story 5")* ($17.5 million). It went on to gross $41.66 million and $31.7 million during its opening weekend, for a total of $123.5 million from 3,919 theaters, ranking first ahead of *[Moana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moana_(2026_film) "Moana (2026 film)")*. This was the best opening for Canada and the United States within a year; the third-best for a Nolan film, behind *The Dark Knight Rises* ($190.9 million in 2012) and *[The Dark Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dark_Knight "The Dark Knight")* ($158.4 million in 2008); as well as the highest debut for Damon, surpassing *[The Bourne Ultimatum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bourne_Ultimatum_(film) "The Bourne Ultimatum (film)")* ($69.2 million in 2007); an R-rated Universal film, surpassing *[Fifty Shades of Grey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifty_Shades_of_Grey_(film) "Fifty Shades of Grey (film)")* ($85.1 million in 2015) and *Oppenheimer* ($180 million); and the fourth highest opening for an R-rated film overall after *[Deadpool & Wolverine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadpool_%26_Wolverine "Deadpool & Wolverine")* ($211.4 million in 2024), *[Deadpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadpool_(film) "Deadpool (film)")* ($132.4 million in 2016), and *[Deadpool 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadpool_2 "Deadpool 2")* ($125.5 million in 2018). It achieved the highest live-action opening weekend for a 2026 film, beating *[Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_(2026_film) "Michael (2026 film)")* ($97.2 million), as well as the third-highest opening weekend of that year, only behind *Toy Story 5* ($159.6 million) and *[The Super Mario Galaxy Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Super_Mario_Galaxy_Movie "The Super Mario Galaxy Movie")* ($131.2 million). The film also generated the second-highest IMAX opening weekend for the United States and Canada with $29.6 million, after *[Star Wars: The Force Awakens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars:_The_Force_Awakens "Star Wars: The Force Awakens")* ($30.1 million in 2015). Within other countries, it debuted to $130.6 million from 12,700 theaters in 73 markets, marking the largest foreign debut for Nolan, with the highest markets including France, Italy, India, Spain, Brazil, the Netherlands, and Poland, as well as the biggest opening for Universal in 11 markets. The film delivered the biggest opening weekend at the BFI IMAX with £219,867 ($296,147), surpassing the previous record gross of £216,454 ($290,979) held by *[Star Wars: The Last Jedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars:_The_Last_Jedi "Star Wars: The Last Jedi")* (2017). The combined opening grosses resulted in a worldwide opening total of $264.1 million, the highest ever for Nolan, surpassing *The Dark Knight Rises* ($249 million).

In the United States and Canada, *The Odyssey* earned $90 million from its second weekend, marking the 14th all-time highest second-weekend gross for a film in the region and the second-best second-weekend gross for an R-rated film, behind *Deadpool & Wolverine*. After the second weekend, *The Odyssey* has grossed $639.9 million globally, including $286.3 million in the United States and Canada, where it became the highest grossing R-rated film of the year and the second highest grossing R-rated Universal film, behind *Oppenheimer*. It managed to hold well in its third weekend with $51 million despite being dethroned by *[Spider-Man: Brand New Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man:_Brand_New_Day "Spider-Man: Brand New Day")*. Originally finishing August 17, IMAX 70mm screenings were extended until September 16, due to high demand selling out screens for seven weeks in a row, sending IMAX stock prices to an all time high. By August 10, *The Odyssey* had surpassed  *[Avatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avatar_(2009_film) "Avatar (2009 film)")* (2009) to become the highest-grossing IMAX film, earning $289 million globally, $37.5 million of which came from the 41 IMAX 70mm screens.

Following the commercial success, Greek Prime Minister [Kyriakos Mitsotakis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyriakos_Mitsotakis "Kyriakos Mitsotakis") congratulated Nolan in a video call, thanking him for promoting ancient Greek culture internationally and for choosing Greece as the principal shooting location. He said productions of this scale serve as a bridge between Greece's cultural heritage and 2020s filmmaking.

### Critical response

The film received widespread critical acclaim, with praise for the performances, sets, scale, and Nolan's direction. On [review aggregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregation "Review aggregation") website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes"), 94% of 515 critics' reviews are positive, with an average rating of 8.7/10. The website's consensus reads: "Reinvigorating an ancient adventure with majestic sweep and sterling work by its colossal ensemble, Christopher Nolan's *The Odyssey* imbues myth with primal human feeling." It is Nolan's highest-rated film on Rotten Tomatoes. [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), which uses a weighted average, assigned it a score of 88 out of 100, based on 63 critics, indicating "universal acclaim". Audiences polled by [CinemaScore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CinemaScore "CinemaScore") gave it an average grade of "A" on an A+ to F scale, while 81% of those surveyed at [PostTrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PostTrak "PostTrak") said they would definitely recommend it.

Randy Myers of *[The Mercury News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mercury_News "The Mercury News")* called *The Odyssey* "another feather in Nolan's masterpiece cap, a grandiose achievement that fills audiences with the same child-like glee experienced when watching *[Jason and the Argonauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_and_the_Argonauts_(film) "Jason and the Argonauts (film)")* and *[Clash of the Titans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clash_of_the_Titans_(1981_film) "Clash of the Titans (1981 film)")* for the first time". Jake Wilson of *[The Sydney Morning Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sydney_Morning_Herald "The Sydney Morning Herald")* wrote that Nolan "has a way with a time paradox" and that "he can take one of the oldest stories in Western culture and make it feel new". In *[The Daily Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph "The Daily Telegraph")*, [Robbie Collin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Collin "Robbie Collin") wrote: "Nolan and his collaborators have constructed a strange, fearsome and trailblazing machine of a movie – by some distance, the best of the year so far." Alistair Harkness of *[The Scotsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Scotsman "The Scotsman")* called it "a thrilling, thoughtful, bloody epic", writing that it "feels like Nolan's angriest film" and one of his best. Clarisse Loughrey of *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent "The Independent")* described it as "a blockbuster of literally unprecedented scale", and Nolan's best film. In *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")*, [Manohla Dargis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manohla_Dargis "Manohla Dargis") wrote: "It is an anomalous big-studio entertainment that Nolan has filled with stars and polished to a high gleam, turning a 3,000-year-old poem into a smart, thoughtful film with Old Hollywood allure." [Justin Chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Chang "Justin Chang") of *[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker "The New Yorker")* praised Nolan's realistic approach and wrote that his rationalism provides "just the note of real-world groundedness that a great fantasy needs to achieve liftoff".

However, there was some criticism for the [sound mixing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_mixing "Sound mixing"), dialogue, and deviations from its source material. [Richard Brody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Brody "Richard Brody") of *The New Yorker* felt that Nolan "turns an epic poem prosaic". In *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")*, [Stephanie Zacharek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephanie_Zacharek "Stephanie Zacharek") called it an "eye-glazing dud". Odie Henderson of *[The Boston Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Boston_Globe "The Boston Globe")* wrote: "Pattinson has fun channeling [Roddy McDowall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roddy_McDowall "Roddy McDowall")'s snarky performance in *[Cleopatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleopatra_(1963_film) "Cleopatra (1963 film)")*. But like that epic flop, *The Odyssey* takes itself too seriously. The film ultimately sinks under its bleakness like a lead balloon." Mohamed Sleiman Labat in *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* criticized "the decision to shoot in [Moroccan-occupied Western Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan-occupied_Western_Sahara "Moroccan-occupied Western Sahara"), where the Indigenous [[Sahrawis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahrawis "Sahrawis")] people can't tell their stories without fear of imprisonment", and accused Nolan of participating in a "state-sponsored PR campaign designed to legitimise an illegal occupation". [Angelica Jade Bastién](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelica_Jade_Basti%C3%A9n "Angelica Jade Bastién") of [Vulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulture_(website) "Vulture (website)") felt *The Odyssey* was "limited by cinematic sexism" and uninterested in its female characters.

### Views of Nolan's interpretation of Homer's poem

Critics have noted multiple differences between Nolan's and Homer's versions of *The Odyssey*. Some have questioned whether Nolan accurately depicts [Mycenaean Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenaean_Greece "Mycenaean Greece"); others have argued that Homer's epic was simply a myth, and others again have stated that while *The Odyssey* is a story, it was rooted in history. The film has been described as both the "most anticipated" and the "most contested" of 2026.

The process of adapting an ancient book-length poem to a single 172-minute film makes changes and omissions inevitable. Nolan cut some plot elements and characters, including several gods, while attempting to preserve an "appropriate tone". He decided to portray Odysseus as a veteran tired of war and haunted by slaughter. That offers a modern explanation for the length of Odysseus's homecoming—that he was avoiding going home. The historian Andrew E. Larsen admired the visuals, but considered the Greek costumes unhistorically drab, and found Agamemnon's armour silly.
The classicist [Armand D'Angour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armand_D%27Angour "Armand D'Angour") liked the use of an ancient musical instrument, the *[aulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aulos "Aulos")*. Others critiqued the armour and ship designs. Some critics argued that the adaptation reshaped the poem's ancient moral framework to align with modern attitudes. The casting of the Kenyan-Mexican actress [Lupita Nyong'o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupita_Nyong%27o "Lupita Nyong'o") as [Helen of Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_of_Troy "Helen of Troy") was criticised by [conservative American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States "Conservatism in the United States") commentators as [blackwashing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackwashing "Blackwashing"). Several commentators called this criticism racist. Larsen commented that Greek literature accepted the black princess [Andromeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andromeda_(mythology) "Andromeda (mythology)") as extremely beautiful.

The English classicist [Mary Beard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Beard "Mary Beard") criticised *The Odyssey* as lacking the sex and humour of the poem. She said it failed to capture the poem's nuance and called the character dynamics "feebly handled". [Emily Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Wilson_(classicist) "Emily Wilson (classicist)") called the film a simple action film lacking "psychological, emotional, political and ethical depth", abysmally written, and with "nothing convincing to say" about the *Odyssey's* major themes.

Others argued that *The Odyssey* succeeds on its own terms, creating a popular film matching the expectations of a modern audience. The British critic [Peter Bradshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bradshaw "Peter Bradshaw") praised its ambition and scope, offering a vision of grim resolution in the face of loss. Guy Lodge of *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* described it as a "vast, thrilling, slightly aloof epic", offering the audience so much that the chilliness works. For [Victor Davis Hanson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Davis_Hanson "Victor Davis Hanson"), Nolan "diminished the dynamic hero Odysseus and the exhilarating age in which he lived" in favor of "a gloomy experience that emphasizes the moral ambiguity, self-absorption, and monotony of our own age."

### Casting choices

The casting of Kenyan-Mexican actress [Lupita Nyong'o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupita_Nyong%27o "Lupita Nyong'o") as [Helen of Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_of_Troy "Helen of Troy")—often described as the most beautiful woman in the world—was criticized by [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States "Conservatism in the United States") commentators, such as [Matt Walsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Walsh_(political_commentator) "Matt Walsh (political commentator)") and [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk "Elon Musk"), as [blackwashing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackwashing "Blackwashing"). Several commentators called this criticism racist, comparing it to similar casting criticisms of the docudrama *[Queen Cleopatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Cleopatra_(Netflix) "Queen Cleopatra (Netflix)")* (2023) and the [Disney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney "Disney") film *[The Little Mermaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Little_Mermaid_(2023_film) "The Little Mermaid (2023 film)")* (2023). Referencing Greek professor [Dimitris Liantinis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitris_Liantinis "Dimitris Liantinis"), Philip Chrysopoulos of the *Greek Reporter* acknowledged that Homer described Helen as pale or "white-armed" but noted that he did not describe her in detail, allowing everyone to imagine her for themselves. *[Salon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salon.com "Salon.com")*'s CK Smith wrote that the backlash was "selective" and more about social issues over minority representation than fidelity to the source material. Nolan defended Nyong'o's casting, saying he believed she conveyed the "strength and the poise" essential for the role. Nyong'o opted to emphasize Helen's character beyond her appearance, saying, "You can't perform beauty." She added that the cast was meant to be "representative of the world" while "occupying the epic narrative of our time". Writing for *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")*, Greek film critic Chris Cotonou called this an "honourable intention", but argued that it made the absence of Greek actors "even more glaring", as they are the people "most authentically connected to the source".

The casting of [Elliot Page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliot_Page "Elliot Page") was criticized. Page's role was not initially announced but was rumored to be [Achilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achilles "Achilles"). *[Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")* described some of the online backlash as "[transphobic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transphobic "Transphobic") memes about Page playing a role that may not even be his".

### Linguistic register

The use of [American accents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_accent "American accent") and 21st-century [American English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English "American English") dialogue from the cast, including British actors Holland and Pattinson, was also criticized. [James Hibberd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hibberd_(writer) "James Hibberd (writer)") of *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")* called this decision a "striking departure from the unwritten Hollywood rule of [characters in historical epics employing British accents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen%27s_Latin "Queen's Latin")". Hibberd and Nell Geraets at *[The Sydney Morning Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sydney_Morning_Herald "The Sydney Morning Herald")* contended that using [ancient Greek dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_dialects "Ancient Greek dialects") would not be accessible for contemporary audiences, unlike an American accent. This change was perceived as [anachronistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anachronistic "Anachronistic") for an adaptation of ancient [Greek literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_literature "Greek literature"), with Geraets noting neither British nor American accents were linguistically authentic to the ancient Greek setting, while Hibberd said using American accents "risks sounding a bit silly at times", unlike the "'just foreign enough'" modern British accent. Nolan insisted on the cast using American accents rather than British or Greek, which *[IGN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IGN "IGN")*'s Scott Collura defended, noting that Wilson's translation of the poem similarly translated the text into [plain English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plain_English "Plain English"), quoting her rationale: "Translation always, necessarily, involves interpretation." The accents and everyday-language dialogue were mocked by some online commenters.

## Themes

### Memory, time, and autonomy

In discussing the themes with [MS NOW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MS_NOW "MS NOW"), Nolan stated that "There are so many different themes, so many different elements of Homer's original poem that appear in movies and have been the sort of bedrock of Hollywood for so long... It hasn't really been told as a modern film for modern audiences. And so, for a director, that's a pretty thrilling opportunity to try and challenge yourself and try to bring something new to the table." For Daniel Chin, the central themes are its emphasis on the nonlinear experience of time and the exploration of memory in the principal characters, especially of Odysseus and his alternating experience of remembering and forgetting his homeland in Ithaca. In his review for *The Gospel Coalition*, Brett McCracken saw the central theme as that of human autonomy struggling with divine intervention, writing: "*The Odyssey*'s marketing campaign tagline, 'Defy the gods,' is an extension of this theme (human autonomy). It's not that Nolan dislikes Greek mythology. He seems to appreciate it as a beautiful story that helped build Western civilization. But Damon's version of Odysseus doesn't particularly need the gods. He often feels skeptical of them ('Why can't God [*sic*] speak in ways we understand?') and puts faith more in himself than in deities."

### Breakdown of societal norms and decorum

Critics interpreted *The Odyssey* as a reframing of the poem into a meditation on the moral consequences of war and the erosion of the [social norms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_norm "Social norm") and [decorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decorum "Decorum") that underpin civilization. It explores the Greek concept of [xenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenia_(Greek) "Xenia (Greek)")—the mutual respect between hosts and guests—using it to reframe the Trojan Horse and Odysseus' journey as explorations of [post-traumatic stress disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-traumatic_stress_disorder "Post-traumatic stress disorder"), war guilt, [moral injury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_injury "Moral injury"), and the breakdown of trust in [civic norms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civics "Civics"), with some interpreting it as a 21st-century political allegory.
