# Nicole Kidman

**Nicole Mary Kidman** (born 20 June 1967) is an Australian and American actress and producer. Known for [her work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Kidman_on_screen_and_stage "Nicole Kidman on screen and stage") in [blockbusters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockbuster_(entertainment) "Blockbuster (entertainment)") and [independent films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_films "Independent films") across many genres, she has consistently ranked among the world's highest-paid actresses since the late 1990s. [Her accolades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Nicole_Kidman "List of awards and nominations received by Nicole Kidman") include an [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award "Academy Award"), an [Actor Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actor_Award "Actor Award"), a [British Academy Film Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Award "British Academy Film Award"), six [Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards "Golden Globe Awards"), two [Primetime Emmy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Awards "Primetime Emmy Awards"), and a [Volpi Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volpi_Cup_for_Best_Actress "Volpi Cup for Best Actress").

Kidman began her career in Australia with the 1983 films *[Bush Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bush_Christmas "Bush Christmas")* and *[BMX Bandits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMX_Bandits_(film) "BMX Bandits (film)")*. In 1989, her breakthrough came with lead roles in *[Dead Calm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Calm_(film) "Dead Calm (film)")* and the miniseries *[Bangkok Hilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangkok_Hilton "Bangkok Hilton")*. She rose to international prominence with a supporting role in *[Days of Thunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Days_of_Thunder "Days of Thunder")* (1990) followed by leading roles in *[Far and Away](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_and_Away "Far and Away")* (1992), *[To Die For](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Die_For "To Die For")* (1995), *[Batman Forever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batman_Forever "Batman Forever")* (1995), *[Practical Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Practical_Magic "Practical Magic")* (1998), and *[Eyes Wide Shut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyes_Wide_Shut "Eyes Wide Shut")* (1999). Kidman earned Academy Award nominations for her performances in *[Moulin Rouge!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moulin_Rouge! "Moulin Rouge!")* (2001), *[Rabbit Hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbit_Hole_(2010_film) "Rabbit Hole (2010 film)")* (2010), *[Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_(2016_film) "Lion (2016 film)")* (2016), and *[Being the Ricardos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Being_the_Ricardos "Being the Ricardos")* (2021). For her portrayal of [Virginia Woolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Woolf "Virginia Woolf") in *[The Hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hours_(film) "The Hours (film)")* (2002), she received the [Academy Award for Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress "Academy Award for Best Actress") and a shared [Silver Bear for Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Bear_for_Best_Actress "Silver Bear for Best Actress"). She also starred in acclaimed films such as *[Cold Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_Mountain_(film) "Cold Mountain (film)")* (2003), *[The Killing of a Sacred Deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Killing_of_a_Sacred_Deer "The Killing of a Sacred Deer")* (2017), *[The Beguiled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beguiled_(2017_film) "The Beguiled (2017 film)")* (2017), *[Bombshell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombshell_(2019_film) "Bombshell (2019 film)")* (2019), *[The Northman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Northman "The Northman")* (2022), and *[Babygirl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babygirl "Babygirl")* (2024), the latter of which won her the [Volpi Cup for Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volpi_Cup_for_Best_Actress "Volpi Cup for Best Actress"). She achieved commercial success with the films *[The Others](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Others_(2001_film) "The Others (2001 film)")* (2001), *[The Golden Compass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Golden_Compass_(film) "The Golden Compass (film)")* (2007), *[Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_(2008_film) "Australia (2008 film)")* (2008), *[Paddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddington_(film) "Paddington (film)")* (2014), and *[Aquaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquaman_(film) "Aquaman (film)")* (2018).

On television, she produced and starred in the [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO "HBO") series *[Big Little Lies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Little_Lies_(TV_series) "Big Little Lies (TV series)")* (2017–2019), for which she won the [Primetime Emmy Awards for Outstanding Limited Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Limited_or_Anthology_Series "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Limited or Anthology Series") and [Outstanding Lead Actress in a Limited Series or Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actress_in_a_Limited_or_Anthology_Series_or_Movie "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Limited or Anthology Series or Movie"). She also starred in the television movie *[Hemingway & Gellhorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemingway_%26_Gellhorn "Hemingway & Gellhorn")*  (2012), as well as television series *[Top of the Lake: China Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_of_the_Lake:_China_Girl "Top of the Lake: China Girl")* (2017), *[The Undoing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Undoing "The Undoing")* (2020), *[Nine Perfect Strangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Perfect_Strangers_(TV_series) "Nine Perfect Strangers (TV series)")* (2021–present), *[Special Ops: Lioness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Ops:_Lioness "Special Ops: Lioness")* (2023–present), *[Expats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expats_(miniseries) "Expats (miniseries)")* (2024), *[The Perfect Couple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Perfect_Couple_(TV_series) "The Perfect Couple (TV series)")* (2024), and [*Scarpetta*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarpetta_(TV_series) "Scarpetta (TV series)") (2026–present).

Kidman has served as a [goodwill ambassador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodwill_ambassador "Goodwill ambassador") for [UNICEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNICEF "UNICEF") since 1994 and [UNWomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Development_Fund_for_Women "United Nations Development Fund for Women") since 2006. She was appointed Companion of the [Order of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Australia "Order of Australia") in 2006. Divorced from actor [Tom Cruise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cruise "Tom Cruise") and country musician [Keith Urban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Urban "Keith Urban"), she has two children from each marriage. In 2010, she founded the production company [Blossom Films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blossom_Films "Blossom Films"). In 2004 and 2018, *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")* included her on its list of the [100 most influential people in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100 "Time 100"), and in 2020, *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* named her one of the greatest actors of the 21st century. She was also honored with [a star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_actors_with_Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame_motion_picture_stars "List of actors with Hollywood Walk of Fame motion picture stars") on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame "Hollywood Walk of Fame") in 2003, and in 2024, became the first Australian actor to receive the [AFI Life Achievement Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFI_Life_Achievement_Award "AFI Life Achievement Award").

## Early life

Nicole Mary Kidman was born on 20 June 1967 in [Honolulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu "Honolulu"), Hawaii, while her Australian parents were temporarily in the United States on student visas. Her mother, Janelle Ann ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") Glenny), a [nursing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nursing "Nursing") instructor and member of the [Women's Electoral Lobby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_Electoral_Lobby_(Australia) "Women's Electoral Lobby (Australia)"), edited her husband's books; her father, [Antony Kidman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antony_Kidman "Antony Kidman"), was a [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist"), [clinical psychologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_psychologist "Clinical psychologist"), and author. She has a younger sister, [Antonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonia_Kidman "Antonia Kidman"), who is a journalist and television presenter. Having been born in the US to Australian parents, Kidman holds [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_citizenship "Multiple citizenship") Australian and US citizenship. She has English, Irish, and Scottish ancestry. Being born in Hawaii, she was given the Hawaiian name "Hōkūlani" ([[hoːkuːˈlɐni]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Hawaiian "Help:IPA/Hawaiian")), meaning "heavenly star". The inspiration came from a baby elephant born around the same time at the [Honolulu Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu_Zoo "Honolulu Zoo").

When Kidman was born, her father was a graduate student at the [University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hawai%CA%BBi_at_M%C4%81noa "University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa"). He became a visiting fellow at the [National Institute of Mental Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Mental_Health "National Institute of Mental Health"). While living in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C."), following Kidman's birth during the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War"), her parents participated in [anti-Vietnam War protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposition_to_United_States_involvement_in_the_Vietnam_War "Opposition to United States involvement in the Vietnam War"). Her family eventually returned to Australia three years later. She grew up in [Longueville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longueville,_New_South_Wales "Longueville, New South Wales"), a suburb on Sydney's [Lower North Shore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_North_Shore "Lower North Shore"), where she attended [Lane Cove Public School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lane_Cove_Public_School "Lane Cove Public School") and [North Sydney Girls High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sydney_Girls_High_School "North Sydney Girls High School"). She was enrolled in [ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballet "Ballet") at the age of three and showed her natural talent for acting during her primary and high school years.

Kidman has said she first aspired to become an actress upon watching [Margaret Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Hamilton_(actress) "Margaret Hamilton (actress)")'s performance as the [Wicked Witch of the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wicked_Witch_of_the_West "Wicked Witch of the West") in *[The Wizard of Oz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wizard_of_Oz "The Wizard of Oz")*. She revealed that she was timid as a child, saying, "I am very shy – really shy – I even had a [stutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stutter "Stutter") as a kid, which I slowly got over, but I still regress into that shyness. So I don't like walking into a crowded restaurant by myself; I don't like going to a party by myself." During her teenage years, she attended the [Phillip Street Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillip_Street_Theatre "Phillip Street Theatre"), alongside fellow actress [Naomi Watts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Watts "Naomi Watts"), and the [Australian Theatre for Young People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Theatre_for_Young_People "Australian Theatre for Young People"), where she took up drama and mime as she found acting to be a refuge. Owing to her fair skin and naturally red hair, the sun drove her to rehearse in the halls of the theatre. A regular at the Phillip Street Theatre, she was encouraged to pursue acting full-time, which she did by dropping out of high school.

## Career

### Early work and breakthrough (1983–1994)

In 1983, 16-year-old Kidman made her film debut in a remake of the Australian holiday classic *[Bush Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bush_Christmas "Bush Christmas")*. By the end of that year, she had a supporting role in the television series *[Five Mile Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Mile_Creek "Five Mile Creek")*. In 1984, her mother was diagnosed with breast cancer, which caused Kidman to halt her acting work temporarily while she studied [massage therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massage_therapy "Massage therapy") to help her mother with physical therapy. She began gaining recognition during this decade after appearing in several Australian films, such as the action comedy *[BMX Bandits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMX_Bandits_(film) "BMX Bandits (film)")* (1983) and the romantic comedy *[Windrider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windrider "Windrider")* (1986). Throughout the rest of the 1980s, she appeared in various Australian television programs, including the 1987 miniseries *[Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_(miniseries) "Vietnam (miniseries)")*, for which she won her first [Australian Film Institute Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Film_Institute_Award "Australian Film Institute Award").

Kidman next appeared in the Australian film *[Emerald City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerald_City_(film) "Emerald City (film)")* (1988), based on the [play of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerald_City_(play) "Emerald City (play)"), which earned her a second Australian Film Institute Award. She then starred alongside [Sam Neill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Neill "Sam Neill") in the 1989 thriller *[Dead Calm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Calm_(film) "Dead Calm (film)")* as Rae Ingram, the wife of a naval officer who is menaced by a castaway at sea, played by [Billy Zane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Zane "Billy Zane"). The film proved to be her [breakthrough role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_role "Breakthrough role"), one of the first films for which she gained international recognition. Regarding her performance, *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* commented how "throughout the film, Kidman is excellent. She gives the character of Rae real tenacity and energy." Meanwhile, critic [Roger Ebert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ebert "Roger Ebert") noted the excellent chemistry between the leads, stating, "Kidman and Zane do generate real, palpable hatred in their scenes together." She followed that up with the Australian miniseries *[Bangkok Hilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangkok_Hilton "Bangkok Hilton")* before moving on to star alongside her then-boyfriend and future husband, [Tom Cruise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cruise "Tom Cruise"), in the 1990 sports action film *[Days of Thunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Days_of_Thunder "Days of Thunder")*, as a young doctor who falls in love with a [NASCAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR "NASCAR") driver. It was considered her international breakout film and was among the year's highest-grossing films.

In 1991, Kidman co-starred alongside [Thandiwe Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thandiwe_Newton "Thandiwe Newton") and former classmate Naomi Watts in the Australian independent film *[Flirting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flirting_(film) "Flirting (film)")*. They portrayed high school girls in this [coming of age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coming_of_age "Coming of age") story, which won the [Australian Film Institute Award for Best Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Film_Institute_Award_for_Best_Film "Australian Film Institute Award for Best Film"). That same year, her work in the film *[Billy Bathgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Bathgate_(film) "Billy Bathgate (film)")* earned Kidman her first [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award "Golden Globe Award") nomination, for [Best Supporting Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture "Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actress – Motion Picture"). *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")*, in its film review, called her "a beauty with, it seems, a sense of humor". The following year, she and Cruise re-teamed for [Ron Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Howard "Ron Howard")'s Irish epic *[Far and Away](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_and_Away "Far and Away")* (1992), which was a modest critical and commercial success. In 1993, she starred in the thriller *[Malice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malice_(1993_film) "Malice (1993 film)")*, opposite [Alec Baldwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alec_Baldwin "Alec Baldwin"), and the drama *[My Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Life_(film) "My Life (film)")*, opposite [Michael Keaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Keaton "Michael Keaton").

### Critical acclaim and worldwide recognition (1995–2003)

In 1995, Kidman played [Dr. Chase Meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Batman_supporting_characters#Dr._Chase_Meridian "List of Batman supporting characters"), the [damsel in distress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damsel_in_distress "Damsel in distress"), in the superhero film *[Batman Forever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batman_Forever "Batman Forever")*, opposite [Val Kilmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val_Kilmer "Val Kilmer") as the film's [title character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batman "Batman"). That same year, she starred in [Gus Van Sant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gus_Van_Sant "Gus Van Sant")'s critically acclaimed dark comedy *[To Die For](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Die_For "To Die For")*, in which she played the murderous newscaster Suzanne Stone. Regarding her performance, [Mick LaSalle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mick_LaSalle "Mick LaSalle") of the *[San Francisco Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Chronicle "San Francisco Chronicle")* said "[she] brings to the role layers of meaning, intention and impulse. Telling her story in close-up – as she does throughout the film – Kidman lets you see the calculation, the wheels turning, the transparent efforts to charm that succeed in charming all the same." For her performance in the film, she received the [Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Motion Picture - Musical or Comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Motion_Picture_-_Musical_or_Comedy "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Motion Picture - Musical or Comedy"). In the following years, she appeared alongside [Barbara Hershey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Hershey "Barbara Hershey") and [John Malkovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Malkovich "John Malkovich") in *[The Portrait of a Lady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Portrait_of_a_Lady_(film) "The Portrait of a Lady (film)")* (1996), based on the [novel of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Portrait_of_a_Lady "The Portrait of a Lady"), and starred in *[The Peacemaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Peacemaker_(1997_film) "The Peacemaker (1997 film)")* (1997) as nuclear expert Dr. Julia Kelly, opposite [George Clooney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Clooney "George Clooney"). The latter film grossed $110 million worldwide. In 1998, she starred alongside [Sandra Bullock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_Bullock "Sandra Bullock") in the romantic comedy *[Practical Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Practical_Magic "Practical Magic")* as two witch sisters who face a threatening curse that prevents them from finding lasting love. While the film opened at the top of the charts during its North American opening weekend, it was a commercial failure at the box office. She returned to the stage that same year for the [David Hare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hare_(playwright) "David Hare (playwright)") play *[The Blue Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Blue_Room_(play) "The Blue Room (play)")*, which opened in London. For her performance, she received a [Laurence Olivier Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurence_Olivier_Award "Laurence Olivier Award") nomination for Best Actress.

In 1999, Kidman reunited with then-husband [Tom Cruise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cruise "Tom Cruise") to portray a couple on a sexual odyssey in *[Eyes Wide Shut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyes_Wide_Shut "Eyes Wide Shut")*, their third film together and the final film of director [Stanley Kubrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Kubrick "Stanley Kubrick"). It was subject to censorship controversies due to the explicit nature of its sex scenes. After a brief hiatus and a highly publicized divorce from Cruise, Kidman returned to the screen to play a [mail-order bride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mail-order_bride "Mail-order bride") in the British-American drama *[Birthday Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birthday_Girl_(2001_film) "Birthday Girl (2001 film)")*. In 2001, she took on the role of [cabaret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabaret "Cabaret") actress and [courtesan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtesan "Courtesan") Satine in [Baz Luhrmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baz_Luhrmann "Baz Luhrmann")'s musical *[Moulin Rouge!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moulin_Rouge! "Moulin Rouge!")*, opposite [Ewan McGregor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewan_McGregor "Ewan McGregor"). Her performance and her singing received positive reviews; Paul Clinton of [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN "CNN") called it her best work since *To Die For*, and wrote "[she] is smoldering and stunning as Satine. She moves with total confidence throughout the film ... Kidman seems to specialize in 'ice queen' characters, but with Satine, she allows herself to thaw, just a bit." She subsequently received her second Golden Globe Award for [Best Actress – Motion Picture Comedy or Musical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Comedy_or_Musical "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress – Motion Picture Comedy or Musical"), among several other awards and nominations, including her first nomination for the [Academy Award for Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress "Academy Award for Best Actress").

Also in 2001, Kidman starred in [Alejandro Amenábar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Amen%C3%A1bar "Alejandro Amenábar")'s psychological horror film *[The Others](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Others_(2001_film) "The Others (2001 film)")* (2001) as Grace Stewart, a mother living in the [Channel Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_Islands "Channel Islands") during World War II who suspects her house is haunted. Grossing over $210 million worldwide, her performance earned her several award nominations, including for her second [BAFTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Leading_Role "BAFTA Award for Best Actress in a Leading Role") and fifth [Golden Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Drama "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Drama"). [Roger Ebert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ebert "Roger Ebert") commented that "Alejandro Amenábar has the patience to create a languorous, dreamy atmosphere, and Nicole Kidman succeeds in convincing us that she is a normal person in a disturbing situation, and not just a standard-issue horror movie hysteric." [A. O. Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._O._Scott "A. O. Scott") of *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* highlighted Kidman's performance, writing that she "embodies this unstable amalgam with a conviction that is in itself terrifying. The icy reserve that sometimes stands in the way of her expressive gifts here becomes the foundation of her most emotionally layered performance to date."

The following year, Kidman garnered critical acclaim for her portrayal of [Virginia Woolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Woolf "Virginia Woolf") in [Stephen Daldry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Daldry "Stephen Daldry")'s *[The Hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hours_(film) "The Hours (film)")*, co-starring alongside [Meryl Streep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meryl_Streep "Meryl Streep") and [Julianne Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julianne_Moore "Julianne Moore"). Kidman wore prosthetics, which were applied to her nose, to portray the author during the 1920s in England, making her look almost unrecognizable. The film was a critical success, earning several awards and nominations, including a nomination for the [Academy Award for Best Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture"). *The New York Times* wrote that "Ms. Kidman, in a performance of astounding bravery, evokes the savage inner war waged by a brilliant mind against a system of faulty wiring that transmits a searing, crazy static into her brain." She won numerous critic and industry awards for her performance, including her first [BAFTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Leading_Role "BAFTA Award for Best Actress in a Leading Role"), third [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Drama "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Drama"), and the [Academy Award for Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress "Academy Award for Best Actress"), becoming the first Australian to win the award. During her Oscar's acceptance speech, she referenced the currently-ongoing [Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_invasion_of_Iraq "2003 invasion of Iraq") when speaking about the importance of art saying, "Why do you come to the Academy Awards when the world is in such turmoil? Because art is important. And because you believe in what you do and you want to honour that, and it is a tradition that needs to be upheld." That same year, she was named the World's Most Beautiful Person by *[People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_(magazine) "People (magazine)")* magazine.

Following her Oscar win, Kidman appeared in three distinctly different films in 2003. The first of those, a leading role in director [Lars von Trier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_von_Trier "Lars von Trier")'s *[Dogville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogville "Dogville")*, was an [experimental film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_film "Experimental film") set on a bare [soundstage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soundstage "Soundstage"). Though the film divided critics in the United States, Kidman earned praise for her performance. [Peter Travers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Travers "Peter Travers") of *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")* stated, "Kidman gives the most emotionally bruising performance of her career in *Dogville*, a movie that never met a cliche it didn't stomp on." The second film was an adaptation of [Philip Roth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Roth "Philip Roth")'s novel *[The Human Stain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Human_Stain_(film) "The Human Stain (film)")*, opposite [Anthony Hopkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Hopkins "Anthony Hopkins"). Her third film that year was [Anthony Minghella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Minghella "Anthony Minghella")'s war drama *[Cold Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_Mountain_(film) "Cold Mountain (film)")*, where she starred opposite [Jude Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jude_Law "Jude Law") and [Renée Zellweger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9e_Zellweger "Renée Zellweger"), playing [Southerner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_United_States "Southern United States") Ada Monroe, a woman who falls in love with Law's character during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War"). Regarding her performance, *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")* magazine wrote, "Kidman takes strength from Ada's plight and grows steadily, literally luminous. Her sculptural pallor gives way to warm radiance in the firelight." The film garnered several awards and nominations, most notably for the performances of the cast, with Kidman receiving her sixth [Golden Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Musical_or_Comedy "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy") nomination.

### Established actress (2004–2009)

In 2004, Kidman starred in the drama film *[Birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_(2004_film) "Birth (2004 film)")*, which sparked controversy over a scene in which she shares a bath with her co-star [Cameron Bright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Bright "Cameron Bright"), then aged ten. During a press conference at the [61st Venice International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/61st_Venice_International_Film_Festival "61st Venice International Film Festival"), she addressed the controversy, saying, "It wasn't that I wanted to make a film where I kiss a 10-year-old boy. I wanted to make a film where you understand love." For her performance, she received her seventh Golden Globe nomination. That same year, she starred alongside [Matthew Broderick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Broderick "Matthew Broderick"), [Bette Midler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bette_Midler "Bette Midler"), [Christopher Walken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Walken "Christopher Walken") and [Glenn Close](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Close "Glenn Close") in the [black comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_comedy "Black comedy") science-fiction film *[The Stepford Wives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stepford_Wives_(2004_film) "The Stepford Wives (2004 film)")*, a remake of the [1975 film of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stepford_Wives_(1975_film) "The Stepford Wives (1975 film)"), directed by [Frank Oz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Oz "Frank Oz"). The following year, she starred opposite [Sean Penn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Penn "Sean Penn") in the [Sydney Pollack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Pollack "Sydney Pollack") thriller *[The Interpreter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Interpreter_(2005_film) "The Interpreter (2005 film)")*, playing UN translator Silvia Broome, and starred alongside [Will Ferrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Ferrell "Will Ferrell") in the romantic comedy *[Bewitched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bewitched_(2005_film) "Bewitched (2005 film)")*, based on the [1960s TV sitcom of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bewitched "Bewitched"). While neither film performed well in the United States, both were international successes., she and Ferrell won the [Razzie Award for Worst Screen Couple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Razzie_Award_for_Worst_Screen_Couple "Razzie Award for Worst Screen Couple").

In conjunction with her success within the film industry, Kidman became the face of the [Chanel No. 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanel_No._5 "Chanel No. 5") perfume brand. She starred in a television and print ads campaign with [Rodrigo Santoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigo_Santoro "Rodrigo Santoro"), directed by *Moulin Rouge!* director Baz Luhrmann, to promote the fragrance during the holiday seasons of 2004, 2005, 2006, and 2008. *[No. 5 the Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._5_the_Film "No. 5 the Film")*, a three-minute commercial produced for Chanel No. 5, made Kidman the record holder for the most money paid per minute to an actor after she reportedly earned $12 million for the three-minute advert. During this time, she was also featured as the 45th Most Powerful Celebrity on *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes "Forbes")*' 2005 Celebrity 100 List. She reportedly earned $14.5 million between 2004 and 2005. On *[People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_(American_magazine) "People (American magazine)")* magazine's list of 2005's highest-paid actresses, Kidman came in second behind [Julia Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Roberts "Julia Roberts"), with a $16–17 million per-film price tag.

In 2006, Kidman portrayed photographer [Diane Arbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diane_Arbus "Diane Arbus") in the biographical film *[Fur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_(movie) "Fur (movie)")*, opposite [Robert Downey Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Downey_Jr. "Robert Downey Jr."), and lent her voice to the animated film *[Happy Feet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happy_Feet "Happy Feet")*, which grossed over $384 million worldwide, becoming her highest-grossing film at the time. The following year, she starred in the science-fiction film *[The Invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Invasion_(film) "The Invasion (film)")*, a remake of the 1956 *[Invasion of the Body Snatchers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_the_Body_Snatchers "Invasion of the Body Snatchers")*, directed by [Oliver Hirschbiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Hirschbiegel "Oliver Hirschbiegel"), and starred opposite [Jennifer Jason Leigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Jason_Leigh "Jennifer Jason Leigh") and [Jack Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Black "Jack Black") in [Noah Baumbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah_Baumbach "Noah Baumbach")'s comedy-drama *[Margot at the Wedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margot_at_the_Wedding "Margot at the Wedding")*, which earned her a [Satellite Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_Award "Satellite Award") nomination for Best Actress – Musical or Comedy. In 2007, she starred as the main antagonist [Marisa Coulter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marisa_Coulter "Marisa Coulter") in the fantasy-adventure film *[The Golden Compass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Golden_Compass_(film) "The Golden Compass (film)")*, which grossed over $370 million worldwide, also becoming one of her highest-grossing films to date.

The following year, Kidman reunited with *Moulin Rouge!* director Baz Luhrmann for the Australian period film *[Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_(2008_film) "Australia (2008 film)")* (2008), set in the remote [Northern Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Territory "Northern Territory") during the [Japanese attack on Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_attack_on_Darwin "Japanese attack on Darwin") during World War II. Starring opposite [Hugh Jackman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Jackman "Hugh Jackman"), she played an Englishwoman feeling overwhelmed by the continent. Though the film received mixed reviews from critics, it turned out to be a box office success, grossing over $211 million worldwide against a budget of $130 million. In 2009, she appeared in the [Rob Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Marshall "Rob Marshall") musical *[Nine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_(2009_live-action_film) "Nine (2009 live-action film)")*, portraying the [muse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muse_(person) "Muse (person)") Claudia Jenssen, alongside an [ensemble cast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensemble_cast "Ensemble cast") consisting of [Daniel Day-Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Day-Lewis "Daniel Day-Lewis"), [Marion Cotillard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marion_Cotillard "Marion Cotillard"), [Penélope Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pen%C3%A9lope_Cruz "Penélope Cruz"), [Judi Dench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judi_Dench "Judi Dench"), [Fergie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fergie_(singer) "Fergie (singer)"), [Kate Hudson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Hudson "Kate Hudson") and [Sophia Loren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophia_Loren "Sophia Loren"). Kidman, whose screen time was brief in comparison to the other actresses, performed the musical number "Unusual Way" alongside Day-Lewis. The film received several Golden Globe Award and Academy Award nominations, with Kidman earning her fourth [Screen Actors Guild Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award "Screen Actors Guild Award") nomination, as part of the [Outstanding Performance by a Cast in a Motion Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Cast_in_a_Motion_Picture "Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Cast in a Motion Picture") award.

### Biographical and independent films (2010–2016)

Kidman began the 2010s by producing and starring in the [film adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbit_Hole_(2010_film) "Rabbit Hole (2010 film)") of the [Pulitzer Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize "Pulitzer Prize")-winning play *[Rabbit Hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbit_Hole_(play) "Rabbit Hole (play)")*, alongside [Aaron Eckhart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Eckhart "Aaron Eckhart"). Her performance as a grieving mother coping with the death of her son earned her critical acclaim, and she received [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress "Academy Award for Best Actress"), [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Musical_or_Comedy "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy") and [Screen Actors Guild Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Female_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role "Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Female Actor in a Leading Role") nominations. The following year, she appeared with [Adam Sandler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Sandler "Adam Sandler") and [Jennifer Aniston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Aniston "Jennifer Aniston") in [Dennis Dugan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Dugan "Dennis Dugan")'s romantic comedy *[Just Go with It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_Go_with_It "Just Go with It")*, as a [trophy wife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trophy_wife "Trophy wife"), and subsequently starred alongside [Nicolas Cage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Cage "Nicolas Cage") in director [Joel Schumacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Schumacher "Joel Schumacher")'s action-thriller *[Trespass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trespass_(2011_film) "Trespass (2011 film)")*, with the stars playing a married couple taken hostage.

In 2012, Kidman starred alongside [Clive Owen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clive_Owen "Clive Owen") in the [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO "HBO") film *[Hemingway & Gellhorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemingway_%26_Gellhorn "Hemingway & Gellhorn")*, which depicted the relationship between journalist couple [Ernest Hemingway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Hemingway "Ernest Hemingway") and [Martha Gellhorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Gellhorn "Martha Gellhorn"). For her performance as Gellhorn, she received her first [Primetime Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actress_in_a_Limited_or_Anthology_Series_or_Movie "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Limited or Anthology Series or Movie") nomination. That same year, she portrayed death row groupie Charlotte Bless in [Lee Daniels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Daniels "Lee Daniels")' adaptation of the [Pete Dexter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Dexter "Pete Dexter") novel, *[The Paperboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Paperboy_(novel) "The Paperboy (novel)")* (2012). The film competed at the [2012 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Cannes_Film_Festival "2012 Cannes Film Festival") and Kidman's performance garnered her[Screen Actors Guild Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Female_Actor_in_a_Supporting_Role "Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Female Actor in a Supporting Role") and [Saturn Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress "Saturn Award for Best Supporting Actress") nominations, in addition to her tenth-overall [Golden Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture "Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actress – Motion Picture") nomination. Also in 2012, her audiobook recording of Virginia Woolf's *[To the Lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_the_Lighthouse "To the Lighthouse")* was released through [Audible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audible_(service) "Audible (service)"). The following year she starred as an unstable mother in [Park Chan-wook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Chan-wook "Park Chan-wook")'s *[Stoker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoker_(film) "Stoker (film)")*, which was released to positive reception and a [Saturn Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress "Saturn Award for Best Supporting Actress") nomination. In April 2013, she was selected as a member of the main competition jury at the [2013 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Cannes_Film_Festival "2013 Cannes Film Festival").

In 2014, Kidman starred as the [titular character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Kelly "Grace Kelly") in the biographical film *[Grace of Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_of_Monaco_(film) "Grace of Monaco (film)")*, which chronicles the 1962 crisis in which [Charles de Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle "Charles de Gaulle") blockaded the tiny principality, angered by Monaco's status as a tax haven for wealthy French subjects and Kelly's contemplative Hollywood return to star in [Alfred Hitchcock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Hitchcock "Alfred Hitchcock")'s *[Marnie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marnie_(film) "Marnie (film)")*. Opening out of competition at the [2014 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Cannes_Film_Festival "2014 Cannes Film Festival"), the film received largely negative reviews. She also starred in two films with [Colin Firth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Firth "Colin Firth") that year, the first being the British-Australian historical drama *[The Railway Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Railway_Man_(film) "The Railway Man (film)")*, in which she played an officer's wife. Katherine Monk of the [Montreal Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Gazette "Montreal Gazette") said of Kidman's performance, "It's a truly masterful piece of acting that transcends Teplitzky's store-bought framing, but it's Kidman who delivers the biggest surprise: For the first time since her eyebrows turned into solid marble arches, the Australian Oscar winner is truly terrific". Her second film with Firth was the British thriller film *[Before I Go to Sleep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Before_I_Go_to_Sleep "Before I Go to Sleep")*, portraying a car crash survivor with brain damage. Also in 2014, she appeared in the live-action animated comedy film *[Paddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddington_(film) "Paddington (film)")* as the film's main antagonist.

In 2015, Kidman starred in the drama *[Strangerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strangerland "Strangerland")*, which opened at the [2015 Sundance Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Sundance_Film_Festival "2015 Sundance Film Festival"), and the [Jason Bateman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Bateman "Jason Bateman")-directed *[The Family Fang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Family_Fang_(film) "The Family Fang (film)")*, produced by Kidman's production company, [Blossom Films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blossom_Films "Blossom Films"), which premiered at the [2015 Toronto International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Toronto_International_Film_Festival "2015 Toronto International Film Festival"). In her other 2015 film release, the biographical drama *[Queen of the Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_of_the_Desert_(film) "Queen of the Desert (film)")*, she portrayed writer, traveller, political officer, administrator and archaeologist [Gertrude Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gertrude_Bell "Gertrude Bell"). That same year, she played a [district attorney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_attorney "District attorney"), opposite [Julia Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Roberts "Julia Roberts") and [Chiwetel Ejiofor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiwetel_Ejiofor "Chiwetel Ejiofor"), in the film *[Secret in Their Eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_in_Their_Eyes "Secret in Their Eyes")*, a remake of the [2009 Argentine film of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Secret_in_Their_Eyes "The Secret in Their Eyes"), both based on the novel *La pregunta de sus ojos* by author [Eduardo Sacheri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_Sacheri "Eduardo Sacheri"). After more than 15 years, she returned to the [West End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_End_theatre "West End theatre") in the UK premiere of *[Photograph 51](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photograph_51_(play) "Photograph 51 (play)")* at the [Noël Coward Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No%C3%ABl_Coward_Theatre "Noël Coward Theatre"). She starred as British scientist [Rosalind Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosalind_Franklin "Rosalind Franklin"), working for the discovery of the structure of DNA, in the [Michael Grandage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Grandage "Michael Grandage")-directed production from September 5 to November 21, 2015. The production was met with considerable praise from critics, particularly for Kidman, and her return to the West End was hailed a success. For her performance, she won an [Evening Standard Theatre Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evening_Standard_Theatre_Award "Evening Standard Theatre Award") and received a second [Laurence Olivier Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurence_Olivier_Award_for_Best_Actress "Laurence Olivier Award for Best Actress") nomination.

In 2016's *[Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_(2016_film) "Lion (2016 film)")*, Kidman portrayed Sue, the adoptive mother of [Saroo Brierley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saroo_Brierley "Saroo Brierley"), an Indian boy who was separated from his birth family, a role she felt connected to as she is the mother of adopted children. She received positive reviews for her performance, in addition to her first nomination for the [Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress "Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress") (her fourth nomination overall), her eleventh [Golden Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture "Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actress – Motion Picture") nomination, among several others. [Richard Roeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Roeper "Richard Roeper") of the *[Chicago Sun-Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Sun-Times "Chicago Sun-Times")* thought that "Kidman gives a powerful and moving performance as Saroo's adoptive mother, who loves her son with every molecule of her being, but comes to understand his quest. It's as good as anything she's done in the last decade." Budgeted at $12 million, *Lion* earned over $140 million globally. She also gave a voice-over performance for the English version of the animated film *[The Guardian Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian_Brothers "The Guardian Brothers")*.

### Television expansion and continued acclaim (2017–2020)

In 2017, Kidman returned to television for *[Big Little Lies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Little_Lies_(TV_series) "Big Little Lies (TV series)")*, a drama series based on [Liane Moriarty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liane_Moriarty "Liane Moriarty")'s [novel of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Little_Lies_(novel) "Big Little Lies (novel)"), which premiered on HBO. She also served as executive producer alongside her co-star, [Reese Witherspoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reese_Witherspoon "Reese Witherspoon"), and the show's director, [Jean-Marc Vallée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Marc_Vall%C3%A9e "Jean-Marc Vallée"). She played Celeste Wright, a former lawyer and housewife, who conceals an abusive relationship with her husband, played by [Alexander Skarsgård](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Skarsg%C3%A5rd "Alexander Skarsgård"). Matthew Jacobs of *[The Huffington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Huffington_Post "The Huffington Post")* considered that she "delivered a career-defining performance", while Ann Hornaday of *[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")* wrote that "Kidman belongs in the pantheon of great actresses". She won the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Limited or Anthology Series or Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actress_in_a_Limited_or_Anthology_Series_or_Movie "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Limited or Anthology Series or Movie") for her performance, as well as the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Limited Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Limited_Series "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Limited Series") as a producer. She also won a [Screen Actors Guild Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Female_Actor_in_a_Miniseries_or_Television_Movie "Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Female Actor in a Miniseries or Television Movie"), two [Critics' Choice Television Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Television_Awards "Critics' Choice Television Awards") and two Golden Globes for her work in the show.

Kidman next played Martha Farnsworth, the headmistress of an all-girls school during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War"), in [Sofia Coppola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia_Coppola "Sofia Coppola")'s drama *[The Beguiled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beguiled_(2017_film) "The Beguiled (2017 film)")*, a remake of the [1971 film of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beguiled_(1971_film) "The Beguiled (1971 film)"), which premiered at the [2017 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Cannes_Film_Festival "2017 Cannes Film Festival"), competing for the [Palme d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palme_d%27Or "Palme d'Or"). Both films were adaptations of a novel by [Thomas P. Cullinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_P._Cullinan "Thomas P. Cullinan"). The film was an arthouse success, and Katie Walsh of the *Tribune News Service* found Kidman "particularly, unsurprisingly excellent in her performance as the steely Miss Martha. She is controlled and in control, unflappable. Her genteel manners and femininity co-exist easily with her toughness." Kidman had two other films premiere at the festival: the science-fiction romantic comedy *[How to Talk to Girls at Parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_to_Talk_to_Girls_at_Parties_(film) "How to Talk to Girls at Parties (film)")*, reuniting her with director [John Cameron Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cameron_Mitchell "John Cameron Mitchell"), and the [psychological thriller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological_thriller "Psychological thriller") *[The Killing of a Sacred Deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Killing_of_a_Sacred_Deer "The Killing of a Sacred Deer")*, directed by [Yorgos Lanthimos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorgos_Lanthimos "Yorgos Lanthimos"), which also competed for the Palme d'Or. Also in 2017, she played supporting roles in the [BBC Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Two "BBC Two") television series *[Top of the Lake: China Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_of_the_Lake#Top_of_the_Lake:_China_Girl_3 "Top of the Lake")* and in the comedy-drama *[The Upside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Upside "The Upside")*, a remake of the 2011 French comedy *[The Intouchables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Intouchables "The Intouchables")*, starring [Bryan Cranston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Cranston "Bryan Cranston") and [Kevin Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Hart "Kevin Hart").

In 2018, Kidman starred in two dramas, *[Destroyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destroyer_(2018_film) "Destroyer (2018 film)")* and *[Boy Erased](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boy_Erased "Boy Erased")*. In the former, she played a detective troubled by a case for two decades. Peter Debruge of *Variety* and Brooke Marine of *W* both found her "unrecognizable" in the role and Debruge added that "she disappears into an entirely new skin, rearranging her insides to fit the character's tough hide", whereas Marine highlighted Kidman's method acting. The latter film is based on Garrard Conley's *[Boy Erased: A Memoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boy_Erased:_A_Memoir "Boy Erased: A Memoir")*, and features [Russell Crowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Crowe "Russell Crowe") and Kidman as socially conservative parents who send their son (played by [Lucas Hedges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_Hedges "Lucas Hedges")) to a [gay conversion program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversion_therapy "Conversion therapy"). [Richard Lawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Lawson_(writer) "Richard Lawson (writer)") of *[Vanity Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanity_Fair_(magazine) "Vanity Fair (magazine)")* credited all three performers for "elevating the fairly standard-issue material to poignant highs". That same year, Kidman took on the role of [Queen Atlanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanna "Atlanna"), the mother of the [title character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquaman "Aquaman"), in the [DC Extended Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Extended_Universe "DC Extended Universe") superhero film *[Aquaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquaman_(film) "Aquaman (film)")*, which grossed over $1.1 billion worldwide, becoming her highest-grossing film to date. *Forbes* ranked her as the fourth highest-paid actress in the world in 2019, with an annual income of $34 million. Kidman kicked off 2019 by reprising her role in the second season of the hit series *Big Little Lies*, which premiered in June. The second season not only drew a larger audience than the first but also became the most-watched night of viewing for an HBO original series that year. In September 2019, she took on the supporting part of a rich socialite in [John Crowley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Crowley_(director) "John Crowley (director)")'s drama *[The Goldfinch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Goldfinch_(film) "The Goldfinch (film)")*, an adaptation of the [novel of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Goldfinch_(novel) "The Goldfinch (novel)") by [Donna Tartt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donna_Tartt "Donna Tartt"), starring [Ansel Elgort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansel_Elgort "Ansel Elgort"). For her performance, she received an additional Screen Actors Guild Award nomination for [Outstanding Performance by a Female Actor in a Supporting Role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Female_Actor_in_a_Supporting_Role "Outstanding Performance by a Female Actor in a Supporting Role").

## Reception and legacy

Kidman is often regarded to be among the finest actresses of her generation. She is also considered one of Hollywood’s bravest performers. She has been noted for seeking eccentric roles in risky projects helmed by auteurs, as well as for her volatile performances and versatile work, having appeared in a variety of eclectic films from several genres throughout her extensive career spanning nearly four decades. *[Vanity Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanity_Fair_(magazine) "Vanity Fair (magazine)")* stated that, despite struggling with her personal life being publicly scrutinized by the media during the early years of her career, "[Kidman] has shown herself to be a major talent, a remarkable actress who can get in there with the best of them, go toe-to-toe, and come out with her credibility intact. What's more, she's proved herself to be a star with a capital S, the one-in-a-generation kind who, like [Elizabeth Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Taylor "Elizabeth Taylor"), is bigger than the Hollywood system, and is also unafraid to be human and real, which only makes her more popular." According to *The New York Times*, "the plucky, disciplined indomitability she brings to her performances, even more than the artistry she displays within them, may be the secret of her appeal, the source of her bond with the audience."

[Emily Nussbaum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Nussbaum "Emily Nussbaum") of *[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker "The New Yorker")* commented how "in each role, there is something waxen and watchful and self-possessed about Kidman, so that, even when she's smiling, she never seems liberated. While other actors specialize in transparency, Kidman has a different gift: she can wear a mask and simultaneously let you feel what it's like to hide behind it." Kidman’s most remarkable accomplishment lies in her ability to consistently deliver surprising performances, often standing out as the highlight even in otherwise lackluster projects. She remains a captivating figure, sparking more conversation and strong opinions than nearly anyone else in the industry. Leila Latif of *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* commented that Kidman continuously proved again and again "why she is one of Hollywood’s best and subtly most transgressive stars." In 2004 and 2018, *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")* magazine named Kidman one of the 100 most influential people in the world on their annual [*Time* 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100 "Time 100") list. In 2020, *The New York Times* ranked her fifth on its list of the greatest actors of the 21st century, and in a 2022 readers' poll by *[Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_(magazine) "Empire (magazine)")* magazine, she was voted one of the 50 greatest actors of all time. In 2025, Kidman was named one of the [Women of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Person_of_the_Year "Time Person of the Year") of *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")* magazine.

> "I'm a character [actor]. I was trained as a character actor. That's what I want to do. I believe in changing the way you look, the way you move, the way you speak. I'm not great at playing myself, so what really gives me the greatest satisfaction is changing into something else."

— Kidman on her approach to acting

Some media outlets have commended Kidman for continuing to defy age-related stereotypes in Hollywood and thriving in an industry often criticized for its challenges for women over 50. Some have commented on her ability to secure leading roles and produce compelling work highlighting her resilience and determination and proving that age is no barrier to success in her career, and have also said that her journey inspires, challenging the industry’s norms and paving the way for other women in Hollywood.

Kidman is known to practice [method acting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_acting "Method acting") for many of her roles. It has been noted that she oftentimes transforms herself physically, mentally and emotionally to resemble her characters, to the point where it has adversely affected her health. Mark Caro from the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")* stated that "to Nicole Kidman, acting isn't a mere technical feat; it's the art of transformation. To hear her tell it, the change can be as dramatic as a caterpillar-into-butterfly metamorphosis. She'll be working and working to get under the skin of a character." *[W](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W_(magazine) "W (magazine)")* described her as a "cipher", and pointed out how "she gets under her character's skin so thoroughly, it's nearly impossible to distinguish the actress from the role. It's why she has become so synonymous with a few key roles ... and why those films are so defined by Kidman's presence in them."

Scholars have also commented on her acting style and approach to roles. Sharon Marie Carnicke, a professor of critical studies and acting at the [USC School of Dramatic Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USC_School_of_Dramatic_Arts "USC School of Dramatic Arts"), mentioned that "Kidman's [acting] choices are believable and natural as reactions to the specific circumstances in her world" and described her work as "kinetic". Dennis Bingham, a professor of English and director of film studies at [Indiana University–Purdue University Indianapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University%E2%80%93Purdue_University_Indianapolis "Indiana University–Purdue University Indianapolis"), stated that "Kidman acts always a step or two outside the character, telegraphing her reactions, elongating the time she takes to articulate her decisions and conclusions. Even her emotional responses are presented as signs." [Pam Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pam_Cook "Pam Cook"), a professor of film at the [University of Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southampton "University of Southampton"), suggests in her biography of Kidman that "her emphasis on artifice and technique points to a conception of screen acting that looks to cinematic expression rather than to the actor's body and intentions for the realization of character." [Mary Luckhurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Luckhurst "Mary Luckhurst"), a professor and head of the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol "University of Bristol") School of Arts with credentials in theatre and performance, stated how "she has strategically pursued a high-risk mutability and versatility, and regularly traverses between naturalist and non-naturalist roles and artforms." She continues saying that "she can continually test her own emotional limits, physical skills, politics, values and frames of reference" and mentions how "her conception of character acting involves metamorphosing gradually into something that she feels is so 'other' that she frequently speaks of losing herself or getting lost in the role, and her preparedness to challenge herself in this respect has continually surprised other actors, directors and producers."

After nearly four decades in the industry and over 80 film and TV projects, Kidman emphasizes her acting approach, saying, "I still approach acting like I’ve just come out of drama school."

[Arte France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arte_France "Arte France") released a 53-minute documentary in 2023, *Nicole Kidman – Eyes Wide Open*.

Kidman has also been described as a fashion icon. The [chartreuse Dior gown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chartreuse_Dior_dress_of_Nicole_Kidman "Chartreuse Dior dress of Nicole Kidman") she wore to the [1997 Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/69th_Academy_Awards "69th Academy Awards") is regarded as one of the greatest dresses in Oscar history and has been credited with changing red carpet fashion forever. *[Vogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vogue_(magazine) "Vogue (magazine)")* described how "from her embroidered chartreuse [John Galliano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Galliano "John Galliano") for [Christian Dior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Dior_(fashion_house) "Christian Dior (fashion house)") gown in 1997, at the side of then-husband Tom Cruise, to that impeccable red [Balenciaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balenciaga "Balenciaga") moment at the [2007 Oscars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Oscars "2007 Oscars"), to the unforgettable [Calvin Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Klein_(fashion_house) "Calvin Klein (fashion house)") ballerina dress she wore to the [2017 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Cannes_Film_Festival "2017 Cannes Film Festival"), the Australian native has mastered the art of red carpet dressing, always piquing our [interest] and taking risks while never overdoing it." *[Insider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insider_Inc. "Insider Inc.")* stated that "over the years, Kidman has experimented with all sorts of trends, including bold colors, statement jewelry, and everything in between, making herself one of the most iconic celebrities when it comes to her fashion choices." In 2003, she received the Fashion Icon Award, which was awarded to her by the [Council of Fashion Designers of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Fashion_Designers_of_America "Council of Fashion Designers of America"). Regarding her bestowal, Peter Arnold, executive director of the CFDA, said, "Nicole Kidman's style, both on and off the screen, has had an undeniable impact on fashion. As an actress, she has developed her many memorable characters with an innate understanding of the artistry of clothes. At the same time, she has elegantly established her personal style and own iconic presence worldwide."

## Working with female filmmakers

In 2017, Kidman openly pledged to collaborate with a female director every 18 months to address gender disparities in the film industry. This commitment led to her involvement in numerous projects, including *[The Perfect Couple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Perfect_Couple_(TV_series) "The Perfect Couple (TV series)")*, portraying Greer Garrison Winbury, a role reminiscent of her previous characters in *[Big Little Lies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Little_Lies_(TV_series) "Big Little Lies (TV series)")* and *[The Undoing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Undoing "The Undoing")*. Some critics argued that such choices resulted in repetitive portrayals, potentially diminishing the impact of her performances. Kidman was recognized for her continuing efforts in upholding her promise (often collaborating with a female director more frequently than every 18 months) to support female filmmakers, contributing to increased representation in the industry and creating more jobs.

[Halina Reijn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halina_Reijn "Halina Reijn"), director of *[Babygirl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babygirl "Babygirl")*, highlighted her experience with Kidman, stating, "She is one of the few people who practices what she preaches when it comes to feminism and empowering women." Mimi Cave, director of *[Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holland_(film) "Holland (film)")*, reflected on working with Kidman, saying that she "makes herself completely available to you as a director" and praising her collaborative spirit. She added, "She's really incredible to watch and she's someone I've learned a great deal from. It was a pretty magnificent experience for me," underscoring Kidman's professionalism and the lasting impression she leaves on collaborators. [Lulu Wang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lulu_Wang_(filmmaker) "Lulu Wang (filmmaker)"), director of *[Expats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expats_(miniseries) "Expats (miniseries)")*, praised Kidman, stating, "Nicole, being able to support me, created this entire network and world of filmmakers, women, people of color who’ve never had the opportunity to be in this space." [Jamie Lee Curtis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Lee_Curtis "Jamie Lee Curtis"), Kidman's co-star in *[Scarpetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarpetta_(TV_series) "Scarpetta (TV series)")*, shared that "People work when Nicole works. I’m working because Nicole is working."

As of February 2025, Kidman has worked with 19 female directors, either in an acting or producing capacity, over the past eight years to champion gender equality in Hollywood.

## Personal life

### Relationships and family

Kidman met American actor [Tom Cruise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cruise "Tom Cruise") in 1989 while working on the set of *[Days of Thunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Days_of_Thunder "Days of Thunder")*, a film in which both starred, and they married on Christmas Eve of 1990 in Colorado. While married, the couple adopted a daughter and a son. On 5 February 2001, the couple's spokesperson announced their separation. Cruise filed for divorce two days later, and their marriage was dissolved later that year, with Cruise citing irreconcilable differences. In 2013, Kidman said their marriage failed because of her young age when they married. "I was a child, really, when I got married," she said. "And I needed to grow up." In a 2007 interview with *[Marie Claire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Claire "Marie Claire")*, Kidman noted the incorrect reporting of a [miscarriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscarriage "Miscarriage") early in her marriage: "It was wrongly reported as miscarriage by everyone who picked up the story. So it's huge news, and it didn't happen. I had a miscarriage at the end of my marriage, but I had an [ectopic pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ectopic_pregnancy "Ectopic pregnancy") at the beginning of my marriage."

Kidman described the divorce as a "major shock" and said of Cruise: "He was huge; still is. To me, he was just Tom, but to everybody else, he is huge. But he was lovely to me and I loved him. I still love him." In 2015, former [Church of Scientology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Scientology "Church of Scientology") executive [Mark Rathbun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Rathbun "Mark Rathbun") said [in a documentary film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Going_Clear_(film) "Going Clear (film)") that he was instructed to "facilitate [Cruise's] break-up with Nicole Kidman". Cruise's Scientology [auditor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auditing_(Scientology) "Auditing (Scientology)") said Kidman had been wiretapped on Cruise's suggestion. In 2015, Kidman said her divorce from Cruise led her to focus her attention on her career. She stated, "Out of my divorce came work that was applauded, so that was an interesting thing for me."

Before marrying Cruise, Kidman had been in relationships with Australian actor [Marcus Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Graham "Marcus Graham") and *Windrider* co-star [Tom Burlinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Burlinson "Tom Burlinson"). The film *Cold Mountain* brought rumors that an affair between Kidman and co-star [Jude Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jude_Law "Jude Law") was responsible for the break-up of his marriage. Both denied the allegations, and Kidman won an undisclosed sum from the British tabloids that published the story. She began dating musician [Lenny Kravitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenny_Kravitz "Lenny Kravitz") in 2003. According to Kidman, they were engaged before deciding to end their relationship. However, they remain on good terms. Kravitz's daughter, [Zoë Kravitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zo%C3%AB_Kravitz "Zoë Kravitz"), later said that Kidman was a loving potential stepmother to her. In 2003, she briefly dated [A Tribe Called Quest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Tribe_Called_Quest "A Tribe Called Quest") rapper [Q-Tip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q-Tip_(musician) "Q-Tip (musician)").

In January 2005, Kidman met Australian-American country singer [Keith Urban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Urban "Keith Urban") at [G'Day LA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%27Day_LA "G'Day LA"), an event honoring Australians. In May 2006, she revealed they were engaged. Kidman married Urban on 25 June 2006 at Cardinal Cerretti Memorial Chapel on the grounds of [St Patrick's Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Patrick%27s_Estate "St Patrick's Estate"), [Manly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manly,_New_South_Wales "Manly, New South Wales"), in Sydney. For their honeymoon, they went to [French Polynesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Polynesia "French Polynesia").

In a 2015 interview, regarding her relationship with Urban, Kidman said, "We didn't really know each other – we got to know each other during our marriage." Urban has credited Kidman for helping him overcome his struggles with [alcohol addiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_addiction "Alcohol addiction"). They maintained homes in Nashville (Tennessee, US), Beverly Hills (California, US), two apartments in Sydney (New South Wales, Australia), a farmhouse in [Sutton Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutton_Forest "Sutton Forest") (New South Wales, Australia), and an apartment in Manhattan (New York, US). The couple's first daughter was born in 2008, in Nashville. In 2010, their second daughter was born via [gestational surrogacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestational_surrogacy "Gestational surrogacy").

In September 2025, it was revealed that Kidman and Urban had separated and that Kidman had filed for divorce, citing irreconcilable differences; the divorce was finalized on 6 January 2026.

### Religious beliefs and political views

Kidman was brought up in a [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic "Catholic") family. She attended [Mary Mackillop Memorial Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Mackillop_Memorial_Chapel_and_Museum "Mary Mackillop Memorial Chapel and Museum") in [North Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sydney "North Sydney"). Following criticism by Catholic leaders that her role in *The Golden Compass* was [anti-Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Catholic "Anti-Catholic"), Kidman told *[Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")* that the source material had been "watered down a little" and that her religious beliefs would prevent her from taking a role in a film she perceived as anti-Catholic. Since her divorce from Tom Cruise, she has been reluctant to discuss Scientology.

A supporter of [women's rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_rights "Women's rights"), Kidman testified before the [United States House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives "United States House of Representatives") Committee on Foreign Affairs to support the [International Violence Against Women Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Violence_Against_Women_Act "International Violence Against Women Act") in 2009. In January 2017, she stated her support for the legalization of [same-sex marriage in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Australia "Same-sex marriage in Australia"). Kidman has also donated to [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)") candidates.

### Net worth

Kidman has featured in annual rankings of the world's highest-paid actors multiple times, including the leading place for a female actor in 2006. In 2002, she first appeared on the *[Australian Financial Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Financial_Review "Australian Financial Review")* [Rich List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Review_Rich_List "Financial Review Rich List"), following her divorce from Tom Cruise, with an estimated [net worth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_worth "Net worth") of A$122 million. As of May 2023[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Nicole_Kidman&action=edit), Kidman's net worth, listed jointly with Urban's, was assessed at A$596 million by the *Financial Review*, after several years of not meeting the threshold for inclusion on the Rich List. In 2021 it was reported that *[Celebrity Net Worth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebrity_Net_Worth "Celebrity Net Worth")* had assessed Kidman's net worth, not including that of Urban's, at US$250 million.

Nicole Kidman applied for Portuguese residency in July 2025 in order to buy a house in the luxury resort Costa Terra Golf & Ocean Club in Melides. She had previously purchased an apartment in Lisbon.

### Philanthropy

Kidman has raised money for, and drawn attention to, disadvantaged children around the world. In 1994, she was appointed a [Goodwill ambassador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodwill_ambassador "Goodwill ambassador") for [UNICEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNICEF "UNICEF"). She also joined the Little Tee Campaign for breast cancer care to design T-shirts or vests to raise money to fight the disease; motivated by her mother's battle with breast cancer in 1984. Kidman was also appointed Goodwill ambassador of the [United Nations Development Fund for Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Development_Fund_for_Women "United Nations Development Fund for Women") (UNIFEM) in 2006.

She visited Kosovo in 2006 to learn about women's experiences of conflict and UNIFEM's support efforts. She is also the international spokesperson for UNIFEM's Say NO – UNiTE to End Violence against Women initiative. Kidman and the UNIFEM executive director presented over five million signatures collected during the first phase of this to the UN Secretary-General on 25 November 2008. On 8 January 2010, alongside [Nancy Pelosi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Pelosi "Nancy Pelosi"), [Joan Chen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Chen "Joan Chen") and [Joe Torre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Torre "Joe Torre"), Kidman attended the ceremony to help the [Family Violence Prevention Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Violence_Prevention_Fund "Family Violence Prevention Fund") break ground on a new international centre located in the [Presidio of San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidio_of_San_Francisco "Presidio of San Francisco").

In 2014, Kidman designed a gold colored [Paddington Bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddington_Bear "Paddington Bear") statue, one of fifty located around London prior to the release of the film *[Paddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddington_(film) "Paddington (film)")*, which was auctioned to raise funds for the [National Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Society_for_the_Prevention_of_Cruelty_to_Children "National Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Children") (NSPCC). In 2016, she donated $50,000 to [UN Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Women "UN Women").

## Discography

Kidman's [discography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discography "Discography") consists of several audio recordings, including one [spoken word album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spoken_word_album "Spoken word album"), one [extended play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_play "Extended play") and three [singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_(music) "Single (music)"). Kidman, primarily known for her acting career, entered the [music industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_industry "Music industry") during the early 2000s after recording a number of tracks for the original motion picture soundtrack to Baz Luhrmann's 2001 musical film *Moulin Rouge!*, which she starred in. Her duet with Ewan McGregor entitled "[Come What May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Come_What_May_(2001_song) "Come What May (2001 song)")" was released as her debut single and the second single from the film's original soundtrack album through [Interscope Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interscope_Records "Interscope Records") on 24 September 2001. The composition became the eighth-highest selling single by an Australian artist that year, being certified Gold by the [Australian Recording Industry Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Recording_Industry_Association "Australian Recording Industry Association"), while peaking at number twenty-seven on the [UK singles chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_singles_chart "UK singles chart"). In addition, the song received a [Best Original Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Original_Song "Golden Globe Award for Best Original Song") nomination at the 59th Golden Globe Awards and was listed at eighty-fifth within [AFI's 100 Years...100 Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFI%27s_100_Years...100_Songs "AFI's 100 Years...100 Songs") by the [American Film Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Film_Institute "American Film Institute").

"[Somethin' Stupid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somethin%27_Stupid#Robbie_Williams_and_Nicole_Kidman_version "Somethin' Stupid")", a [cover version](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cover_version "Cover version") of [Frank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Sinatra "Frank Sinatra") and [Nancy Sinatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Sinatra "Nancy Sinatra")'s duet, followed soon after. The track, recorded as a duet with English singer-songwriter [Robbie Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Williams "Robbie Williams"), was issued on 14 December 2001 by [Chrysalis Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysalis_Records "Chrysalis Records") as the [lead single](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead_single "Lead single") off his fourth studio album, *[Swing When You're Winning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swing_When_You%27re_Winning "Swing When You're Winning")*. Kidman's second single topped the official music charts in New Zealand, Portugal, and the UK, in addition to reaching top ten placings all over Europe, including Austria, Belgium, Denmark, Germany, the Netherlands, Norway and Switzerland, as well as Australia. Apart from being [certified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_certification "Record certification") in a number of countries, it was ranked as the thirteenth best-selling single of 2002 in the UK, the fifty-ninth in Australia and the ninety-third in France, respectively. The song peaked at No. 8 on the Australian ARIAnet Singles Chart and at No. 1, for three weeks, in the UK.

On 5 April 2002, Kidman released through Interscope Records her third single, a cover of [Randy Crawford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Crawford "Randy Crawford")'s "[One Day I'll Fly Away](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Day_I%27ll_Fly_Away#Nicole_Kidman "One Day I'll Fly Away")". The song, a Tony Philips remix, was promoted as the pilot single for the follow-up to the *Moulin Rouge!* original soundtrack, titled *[Moulin Rouge! Vol. 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moulin_Rouge!_Music_from_Baz_Luhrmann%27s_Film,_Vol._2 "Moulin Rouge! Music from Baz Luhrmann's Film, Vol. 2")*. In 2006, she contributed to the original motion picture soundtrack of *[Happy Feet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happy_Feet_(soundtrack) "Happy Feet (soundtrack)")*, recording a rendition of the [Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_(musician) "Prince (musician)") song "[Kiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiss_(Prince_song)#Other_cover_versions/appearances_in_media "Kiss (Prince song)")" for the film. In 2009, she was featured on the [original soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_(soundtrack) "Nine (soundtrack)") of Rob Marshall's 2009 musical film *Nine*, recording the song "Unusual Way". In 2012, she narrated an [audiobook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audiobook "Audiobook") and in 2017, she contributed with background vocals to her husband's, [country music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music") singer Keith Urban, song titled "[Female](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Female_(song) "Female (song)")". In 2022, Kidman joined [Luke Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke_Evans "Luke Evans") to release a cover of "[Say Something](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Say_Something_(A_Great_Big_World_song) "Say Something (A Great Big World song)")", originally performed by [A Great Big World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Great_Big_World "A Great Big World") and [Christina Aguilera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christina_Aguilera "Christina Aguilera").
