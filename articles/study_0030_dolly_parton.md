# Dolly Parton

**Dolly Rebecca Parton Dean** ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") **Parton**; January 19, 1946 – August 25, 2026) was an American singer-songwriter, actress, philanthropist, and businesswoman. Dubbed the "[Queen of Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_of_Country "Queen of Country")", Parton was one of the most successful performers in the history of [country music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music"). She was also known for [her cultural influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_impact_of_Dolly_Parton "Cultural impact of Dolly Parton"), particularly through [Dollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dollywood "Dollywood") and [the Dollywood Foundation](#Education_and_literacy). With more than a hundred million records sold, Parton was among the [best-selling music artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best-selling_music_artists "Best-selling music artists") in history.

Born and raised in [East Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Tennessee "East Tennessee"), Parton wrote her first song at age six, performed regularly on television by age ten, made her first recording a year later, and became a professional songwriter at sixteen. After writing songs for others, she released her debut album, *[Hello, I'm Dolly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hello,_I%27m_Dolly "Hello, I'm Dolly")* in 1967, starting her nearly sixty-year career. Parton released [49 studio albums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_Parton_albums_discography "Dolly Parton albums discography") and wrote more than 3,000 songs, including "[I Will Always Love You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Will_Always_Love_You "I Will Always Love You")" – a two-time US country chart-topper and [an international success](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Will_Always_Love_You_(Whitney_Houston_recording) "I Will Always Love You (Whitney Houston recording)") for [Whitney Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Houston "Whitney Houston") that became the [best-selling single](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best-selling_single "Best-selling single") by a woman – "[Jolene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolene "Jolene")", "[Coat of Many Colors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_Many_Colors_(song) "Coat of Many Colors (song)")", and "[9 to 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_to_5_(Dolly_Parton_song) "9 to 5 (Dolly Parton song)")". The lattermost, as well as the [Kenny Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenny_Rogers "Kenny Rogers") duet "[Islands in the Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islands_in_the_Stream_(song) "Islands in the Stream (song)")", reached No. 1 on the [*Billboard* Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100 "Billboard Hot 100"). Her final solo studio album, *[Rockstar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockstar_(Dolly_Parton_album) "Rockstar (Dolly Parton album)")* (2023), became her highest-charting album on the [*Billboard* 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200"), reaching No. 3.

Parton starred in the comedy films *[9 to 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_to_5_(film) "9 to 5 (film)")* (1980), *[The Best Little Whorehouse in Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Best_Little_Whorehouse_in_Texas_(film) "The Best Little Whorehouse in Texas (film)")* (1982), *[Rhinestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinestone_(film) "Rhinestone (film)")* (1984), *[Steel Magnolias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_Magnolias "Steel Magnolias")* (1989), *[Straight Talk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight_Talk "Straight Talk")* (1992), and *[Joyful Noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyful_Noise_(film) "Joyful Noise (film)")* (2012). She produced and starred in a series of [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") features including *[Christmas on the Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_on_the_Square "Christmas on the Square")* (2021), which earned her a [Primetime Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Movie "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Movie"). She also played [Aunt Dolly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aunt_Dolly "Aunt Dolly") in *[Hannah Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannah_Montana "Hannah Montana")* (2006–2010), hosted *[Saturday Night Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live "Saturday Night Live")* (1989), and guest-starred on *[Reba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reba_(TV_series) "Reba (TV series)")* (2005) and *[Grace and Frankie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_and_Frankie "Grace and Frankie")* (2022). She wrote the music and lyrics for [Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_theatre "Broadway theatre")'s *[9 to 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_to_5_(musical) "9 to 5 (musical)")* (2009), which earned her a [Tony Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award "Tony Award") nomination. Her life will be depicted in *[Dolly: A True Original Musical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly:_A_True_Original_Musical "Dolly: A True Original Musical")* (2026).

Parton's music received 52 gold, platinum, and multi-platinum certifications from the [Recording Industry Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America "Recording Industry Association of America") (RIAA). [Her accolades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Dolly_Parton "List of awards and nominations received by Dolly Parton") include eleven [Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Awards "Grammy Awards") and three [Emmy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Awards "Emmy Awards"). She logged 25 [No. 1 *Billboard* country singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Billboard_number-one_country_songs "Timeline of Billboard number-one country songs") (tied with [Reba McEntire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reba_McEntire "Reba McEntire") for the most by a woman), 44 career [Top 10 country albums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Country_Albums "Top Country Albums") (a record for any artist), and 110 career-charted singles over forty years. Parton was honored with a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame "Hollywood Walk of Fame") (1984), the [National Medal of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Arts "National Medal of Arts") (2004), the [Kennedy Center Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Center_Honor "Kennedy Center Honor") (2006), the [Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Lifetime_Achievement_Award "Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award") (2011), and the [Jean Hersholt Humanitarian Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Hersholt_Humanitarian_Award "Jean Hersholt Humanitarian Award") (2025).

## Early life

Dolly Rebecca Parton was born on January 19, 1946, in a one-room cabin on the banks of the [Little Pigeon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Pigeon_River_(Tennessee) "Little Pigeon River (Tennessee)") in [Pittman Center, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittman_Center,_Tennessee "Pittman Center, Tennessee"). She was the fourth of twelve children born to Avie Lee Carolina ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") Owens) and Robert Lee Parton. Her three elder siblings are Willadeene, David Wilburn, and Coy Denver; her younger siblings are Robert Lee "Bobby" Jr., [Stella Mae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_Parton "Stella Parton"), Cassie Nan, [Randle Huston "Randy"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Parton "Randy Parton"), Larry Garrold (who died shortly after birth in 1955), twins Frieda Estelle and Floyd Estel, and [Rachel Ann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Parton_George "Rachel Parton George"). Parton's middle name came from her maternal great-great-grandmother, Rebecca Whitted (née Dunn).

Parton's father, who went by his middle name "Lee", worked in the mountains of [East Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Tennessee "East Tennessee"), first as a [sharecropper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharecropper "Sharecropper") and later tending his own small [tobacco farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_farm "Tobacco farm") and acreage. He also worked construction jobs to supplement the farm's small income. Despite his [illiteracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illiteracy "Illiteracy"), Parton often said her father was one of the smartest people she had ever known with regard to business and making a profit.

Parton credited her musical abilities to her mother's influence. Avie Lee Parton's many pregnancies often occurred while still breastfeeding her current infant at the time. This caused her to be ill and weak for many years. However, she still managed to keep house with the help of Parton's eldest sibling, Willadeene. Mrs. Parton entertained her children with [Great Smoky Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Smoky_Mountains "Great Smoky Mountains") [folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore "Folklore") and old world [ballads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballads "Ballads"). The ballads had been brought to the early United States, particularly southern [Appalachia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachia "Appalachia"), by immigrants from the [British Isles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Isles "British Isles"). Having descended from parents with [Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_people "Welsh people") ancestry, Avie Lee grew up singing the ballads. Avie Lee's father, Jake Robert Owens, was a [Pentecostal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostal "Pentecostal") preacher who pastored the [Church of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_God_(Cleveland,_Tennessee) "Church of God (Cleveland, Tennessee)"), and Parton and her siblings attended church there. Music played an important role in Parton's early life. Her earliest public performances were in the church, beginning at age six. At seven, she started playing a homemade guitar. When she was eight, her uncle bought her first guitar.

When Parton was still young, her family moved to a two‑room cabin and farm on nearby [Locust Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locust_Ridge,_Tennessee "Locust Ridge, Tennessee"). Most of her cherished childhood memories happened there. A replica of the Locust Ridge cabin is displayed at her namesake theme park. The Partons' small [subsistence farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsistence_farm "Subsistence farm") on Locust Ridge was in a predominantly Pentecostal area located north of the [Greenbrier Valley of the Great Smoky Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenbrier_(Great_Smoky_Mountains) "Greenbrier (Great Smoky Mountains)"). The farm acreage and surrounding woodland inspired her to write "[My Tennessee Mountain Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Tennessee_Mountain_Home_(song) "My Tennessee Mountain Home (song)")" in the 1970s. Years after the farm was sold, Parton bought it back in the late 1980s. Her brother Bobby helped with the restoration of the building as well as new construction.

Parton said her family was "dirt poor". Her father paid [missionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionary "Missionary") Robert F. Thomas with a sack of cornmeal for delivering her. Later in life, Parton wrote a song about Thomas. She also outlined her family's poverty in her early songs "[Coat of Many Colors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_Many_Colors_(song) "Coat of Many Colors (song)")" and "[In the Good Old Days (When Times Were Bad)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Good_Old_Days_(When_Times_Were_Bad) "In the Good Old Days (When Times Were Bad)")". The Parton family was well-fed despite their poverty, and her 2024 cookbook, *Good Lookin' Cookin'* (co-written with her sister Rachel), recalls numerous family meals. The day after graduating from [Sevier County High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevier_County_High_School "Sevier County High School") in [Sevierville, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevierville,_Tennessee "Sevierville, Tennessee"), in 1964, Parton moved to [Nashville, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville,_Tennessee "Nashville, Tennessee").

## Music career

### 1956–1966: Early work and songwriting

Parton began performing as a child, singing on local radio and television programs in the [East Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Tennessee "East Tennessee") area. By age ten she had appeared on *[The Cas Walker Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cas_Walker#Radio_and_television "Cas Walker")* on both [WIVK Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WVLZ_(AM) "WVLZ (AM)") and [WBIR‑TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WBIR-TV "WBIR-TV") in [Knoxville, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knoxville,_Tennessee "Knoxville, Tennessee"). At thirteen, she recorded the single "Puppy Love" for the Louisiana label [Goldband Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldband_Records "Goldband Records") and appeared at the [Grand Ole Opry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Ole_Opry "Grand Ole Opry"), where she first met [Johnny Cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Cash "Johnny Cash"). He introduced her as "a little girl here from up in East Tennessee" and encouraged her to follow her own instincts regarding her career.

Parton's initial success came as a songwriter. She signed with [Combine Publishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combine_Music "Combine Music") shortly after her arrival in Nashville. Working with her frequent songwriting partner, her uncle [Bill Owens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Owens_(songwriter) "Bill Owens (songwriter)"), she wrote several charting singles, including two Top 10 hits for [Bill Phillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Phillips_(singer) "Bill Phillips (singer)"): "[Put It Off Until Tomorrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Put_It_Off_Until_Tomorrow "Put It Off Until Tomorrow")" and "The Company You Keep" (1966). She also wrote [Skeeter Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeeter_Davis "Skeeter Davis")'s No. 11 hit "[Fuel to the Flame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_to_the_Flame "Fuel to the Flame")" (1967). Her songs were recorded by many other artists during this period, including [Kitty Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitty_Wells "Kitty Wells") and [Hank Williams Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hank_Williams_Jr. "Hank Williams Jr.")

At 19, she signed with [Monument Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_Records "Monument Records") in 1965, where she was initially pitched as a [bubblegum pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubblegum_pop "Bubblegum pop") singer. She released several singles, but only "[Happy, Happy Birthday Baby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happy,_Happy_Birthday_Baby "Happy, Happy Birthday Baby")" charted, and it failed to reach the *Billboard* Hot 100. Although she wanted to record country material, Monument resisted, thinking her unusually high [soprano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soprano "Soprano") voice was not suited to the genre.

After Bill Phillips' recording of her composition "Put It Off Until Tomorrow" reached No. 6 on the country chart in 1966, the label relented and allowed her to record country. Her first country single, "[Dumb Blonde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumb_Blonde_(Dolly_Parton_song) "Dumb Blonde (Dolly Parton song)")", written by [Curly Putman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curly_Putman "Curly Putman"), reached No. 24 on the country chart in 1967. It was followed by "[Something Fishy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Something_Fishy_(song) "Something Fishy (song)")", which reached No. 17. Both singles appeared on her first full-length album, *[Hello, I'm Dolly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hello,_I%27m_Dolly "Hello, I'm Dolly")*.

### 1967–1975: Country music success

In 1967, musician and country music entertainer [Porter Wagoner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porter_Wagoner "Porter Wagoner") invited Parton to join *The Porter Wagoner Show*, offering her a regular spot on his weekly [syndicated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_syndication "Broadcast syndication") television program and in his road show. As documented in her 1994 autobiography, much of Wagoner's audience was initially unhappy that Parton had replaced previous performer [Norma Jean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norma_Jean_(singer) "Norma Jean (singer)") who had left the show, sometimes chanting loudly for Norma Jean from the audience. With Wagoner's assistance, Parton was eventually accepted. Wagoner convinced his label, [RCA Victor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCA_Victor "RCA Victor"), to sign her. RCA decided to protect its investment by releasing her first single as a [duet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duet "Duet") with Wagoner. That song, a remake of [Tom Paxton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Paxton "Tom Paxton")'s "[The Last Thing on My Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Thing_on_My_Mind "The Last Thing on My Mind")", released in late 1967, reached the country Top 10 in January 1968, launching a six-year run of uninterrupted Top 10 singles for the pair.

Parton's first solo single for RCA Victor, "[Just Because I'm a Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_Because_I%27m_a_Woman_(1968_album)#Album_information "Just Because I'm a Woman (1968 album)")", was released in the summer of 1968 and reached No. 17. For the next two years, none of her solo efforts – even "[In the Good Old Days (When Times Were Bad)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Good_Old_Days_(When_Times_Were_Bad) "In the Good Old Days (When Times Were Bad)")", which later became a standard – were as successful as her duets with Wagoner. The duo was named [Vocal Group of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Music_Association_Awards "Country Music Association Awards") in 1968 by the [Country Music Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Music_Association "Country Music Association"), but Parton's solo records continued to be ignored. Wagoner had a significant financial stake in her future; from 1969 he was her co-producer and owned nearly half of Owe-Par, the publishing company Parton had founded with Bill Owens. Parton joined the [Nashville Association of Musicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Federation_of_Musicians "American Federation of Musicians"), a labor union representing professional musicians, in 1968 and remained a dues-paying member until her death in 2026.

By 1970, both Parton and Wagoner had grown frustrated by her lack of solo chart success. Wagoner persuaded Parton to record [Jimmie Rodgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmie_Rodgers "Jimmie Rodgers")' "[Mule Skinner Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mule_Skinner_Blues "Mule Skinner Blues")". The record reached No. 3, followed by her first No. 1 single, "[Joshua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_(song) "Joshua (song)")" in February 1971. For the next two years she had numerous solo hits in addition to her duets, including her signature "[Coat of Many Colors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_Many_Colors_(song) "Coat of Many Colors (song)")". Further Top 20 singles included "The Right Combination" and "Burning the Midnight Oil" (both duets with Wagoner, 1971); "Lost Forever in Your Kiss" (with Wagoner), "[Touch Your Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touch_Your_Woman "Touch Your Woman")" (1972); "[My Tennessee Mountain Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Tennessee_Mountain_Home "My Tennessee Mountain Home")", and "Travelin' Man" (1973).

While her solo singles and the Wagoner duets were successful, her biggest success from this period was "[Jolene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolene "Jolene")". Released in late 1973, the song topped the country chart in February 1974 and reached the lower regions of the Hot 100. It also eventually charted in the UK, reaching No. 7 in 1976, Parton's first UK success. Parton, who had always envisioned a solo career, decided to leave Wagoner's show and stopped appearing in mid-1974. The pair performed their last duet concert in April 1974 although they remained affiliated, with Wagoner helping to produce her records through 1975. Their final release as a duo was 1975's *[Say Forever You'll Be Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Say_Forever_You%27ll_Be_Mine "Say Forever You'll Be Mine")*.

In 1974, Parton's song, "[I Will Always Love You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Will_Always_Love_You "I Will Always Love You")", written about her professional break from Wagoner, went to No. 1 on the country chart. Around the same time, [Elvis Presley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_Presley "Elvis Presley") indicated that he wanted to record the song. Parton was interested until Presley's manager, [Colonel Tom Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_Tom_Parker "Colonel Tom Parker"), told her that it was standard procedure for the songwriter to sign over half the publishing rights to any song recorded by Presley. Parton refused. That decision was later credited with earning her many millions of dollars in royalties from the song. That year, Parton had three solo singles reach No. 1 on the country chart, "Jolene", "I Will Always Love You", and "[Love Is Like a Butterfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Is_Like_a_Butterfly_(song) "Love Is Like a Butterfly (song)")", and the duet with Wagoner, "[Please Don't Stop Loving Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Please_Don%27t_Stop_Loving_Me "Please Don't Stop Loving Me")". She again topped the country chart in 1975 with "[The Bargain Store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bargain_Store_(song) "The Bargain Store (song)")".

### 1976–1986: A broader audience

Between 1974 and 1980, Parton had a series of country hits with eight singles reaching No. 1. Her influence on pop culture was reflected in the many performers who covered her songs, including mainstream and crossover artists such as [Olivia Newton-John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_Newton-John "Olivia Newton-John"), [Emmylou Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmylou_Harris "Emmylou Harris"), and [Linda Ronstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Ronstadt "Linda Ronstadt").

Parton began a high-profile crossover campaign, aiming her music in a more mainstream direction and increasing her visibility outside country music. In 1976, she began working closely with [Sandy Gallin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandy_Gallin "Sandy Gallin"), who became her personal manager for the next 25 years. With her 1976 album *[All I Can Do](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_I_Can_Do_(album) "All I Can Do (album)")*, which she co-produced with Wagoner, Parton took a more active role in production and began aiming her music at a pop audience. Her first self-produced effort, *[New Harvest...First Gathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Harvest...First_Gathering "New Harvest...First Gathering")* (1977), highlighted her pop sensibilities in both song choice and production; the album included covers of the pop and [R&B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%26B "R&B") classics "[My Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Girl_(The_Temptations_song) "My Girl (The Temptations song)")" and "[Higher and Higher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/(Your_Love_Keeps_Lifting_Me)_Higher_and_Higher "(Your Love Keeps Lifting Me) Higher and Higher")". Though well received and topping the US country albums chart, neither the album nor its single "[Light of a Clear Blue Morning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_of_a_Clear_Blue_Morning "Light of a Clear Blue Morning")" made much impact on the pop charts.

After *New Harvest*'s unsuccessful crossover performance, Parton turned to pop producer [Gary Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Klein_(producer) "Gary Klein (producer)") for her next album. The result, 1977's *[Here You Come Again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Here_You_Come_Again "Here You Come Again")*, became her first million-seller, topping the country album chart and reaching No. 20 on the pop chart. The [Barry Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Mann "Barry Mann")-[Cynthia Weil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cynthia_Weil "Cynthia Weil")-penned [title track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Here_You_Come_Again_(song) "Here You Come Again (song)") topped the country singles chart and became Parton's first Top 10 single on the pop chart (No. 3). A second single, the double A‑sided "[Two Doors Down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Doors_Down_(Dolly_Parton_song) "Two Doors Down (Dolly Parton song)")"/"[It's All Wrong, But It's All Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%27s_All_Wrong,_But_It%27s_All_Right "It's All Wrong, But It's All Right")", topped the country chart and crossed into the pop Top 20. For the remainder of the 1970s and into the early 1980s, many of her singles climbed both charts simultaneously. Her albums during this period were developed specifically for pop-[crossover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossover_(music) "Crossover (music)").

In 1978, Parton won a [Grammy Award for Best Female Country Vocal Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Female_Country_Vocal_Performance "Grammy Award for Best Female Country Vocal Performance") for her album *Here You Come Again*. She continued to have success with "[Heartbreaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heartbreaker_(Dolly_Parton_song) "Heartbreaker (Dolly Parton song)")" (1978), "[Baby I'm Burnin'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby_I%27m_Burnin%27 "Baby I'm Burnin'")" (1979), and "[You're the Only One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You%27re_the_Only_One_(Dolly_Parton_song) "You're the Only One (Dolly Parton song)")" (1979), all of which charted in the pop [Top 40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_40 "Top 40") and topped the country chart. "[Sweet Summer Lovin'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_Summer_Lovin%27 "Sweet Summer Lovin'")" (1979) became her first single in two years not to top the country chart, though it reached the Top 10. Her visibility increased with numerous television appearances. Parton submitted to a highly publicized candid interview on a *[Barbara Walters Special](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Walters_Special "Barbara Walters Special")* in 1977, timed to coincide with *Here You Come Again*'s release, against her friends' advice. Walters was dismissive, saying "You don't have to look like this," and asking "Do you ever feel that you're a joke?" Parton indicated that she was deliberately in control of her image and replied, smiling, "The joke's on the public. I know exactly what I'm doing and I can change it at any time ... I'm sure of myself. I'm sure of my talent."

In 1978 she appeared on [Cher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cher "Cher")'s ABC [television special](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cher..._Special "Cher... Special") and her own joint CBS special with [Carol Burnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_Burnett "Carol Burnett"), *[Dolly & Carol in Nashville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_%26_Carol_in_Nashville "Dolly & Carol in Nashville")*.
Parton served as one of three co-hosts, along with [Roy Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Clark "Roy Clark") and [Glen Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glen_Campbell "Glen Campbell"), on the CBS special *Fifty Years of Country Music*. In 1979, she hosted the [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC "NBC") special *The Seventies: An Explosion of Country Music* at [Ford's Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford%27s_Theatre "Ford's Theatre") in Washington, DC, a performance attended by President [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter "Jimmy Carter"). Her commercial success grew in 1980, with three consecutive No. 1 country hits: "[Starting Over Again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starting_Over_Again_(Dolly_Parton_song) "Starting Over Again (Dolly Parton song)")", "[Old Flames Can't Hold a Candle to You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Flames_Can%27t_Hold_a_Candle_to_You "Old Flames Can't Hold a Candle to You")", and "[9 to 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_to_5_(Dolly_Parton_song) "9 to 5 (Dolly Parton song)")", which topped both the country and the pop charts in early 1981. She had another Top 10 single that year with "Making Plans" from her 1980 [album with Wagoner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porter_%26_Dolly "Porter & Dolly"), released as part of a lawsuit settlement between the pair.

The theme song from the 1980 feature film *[9 to 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_to_5_(film) "9 to 5 (film)")* (1980), in which she starred with [Jane Fonda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Fonda "Jane Fonda") and [Lily Tomlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lily_Tomlin "Lily Tomlin"), reached No. 1 on country, pop, and [adult-contemporary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_contemporary_music "Adult contemporary music") charts in February 1981, giving her a triple No. 1 hit. Parton became one of the few female country singers to have a No. 1 single on the country and pop charts simultaneously. The song received a nomination for an [Academy Award for Best Original Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Song "Academy Award for Best Original Song"). Her singles continued to appear consistently in the country's Top 10.

Between 1981 and 1985, she had twelve Top 10 hits, half of them reaching No. 1. She continued to make inroads on the pop charts. A re-recorded version of "I Will Always Love You", from the feature film *[The Best Little Whorehouse in Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Best_Little_Whorehouse_in_Texas_(film) "The Best Little Whorehouse in Texas (film)")* (1982), reached the lower end of the Top 50 that year, and her duet with [Kenny Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenny_Rogers "Kenny Rogers"), "[Islands in the Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islands_in_the_Stream_(song) "Islands in the Stream (song)")", written by the [Bee Gees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bee_Gees "Bee Gees") and produced by [Barry Gibb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Gibb "Barry Gibb"), spent two weeks at No. 1 in 1983.

In the mid-1980s, her record sales were still relatively strong. "[Save the Last Dance for Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Last_Dance_for_Me "Save the Last Dance for Me")", "[Tennessee Homesick Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_Homesick_Blues "Tennessee Homesick Blues")", "God Won't Get You" (1984), "[Real Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Love_(Dolly_Parton_and_Kenny_Rogers_song) "Real Love (Dolly Parton and Kenny Rogers song)")" (another duet with Kenny Rogers), "[Don't Call It Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don%27t_Call_It_Love_(song)#Dolly_Parton_cover "Don't Call It Love (song)")" (1985), and "[Think About Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Think_About_Love_(song) "Think About Love (song)")" (1986) all reached the country Top 10. "Tennessee Homesick Blues", "Think About Love", and "Real Love" reached No. 1 and became modest crossover hits. Despite this success, RCA Records did not renew her contract in 1986, and she signed with [Columbia Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Records "Columbia Records") in 1987.

### 1987–2005: Country and bluegrass period

With Emmylou Harris and Linda Ronstadt, Parton released *[Trio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trio_(1987_album) "Trio (1987 album)")* (1987) to critical acclaim. The album revitalized her music career, spending five weeks at No. 1 on *Billboard's* Country Albums chart and reaching the Top 10 on the *Billboard* 200. It sold several million copies and produced four Top 10 country hits, including [Phil Spector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Spector "Phil Spector")'s "[To Know Him Is to Love Him](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Know_Him_Is_to_Love_Him "To Know Him Is to Love Him")", which went to No. 1. *Trio* won the [Grammy Award for Best Country Performance by a Duo or Group with Vocal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Country_Performance_by_a_Duo_or_Group_with_Vocal "Grammy Award for Best Country Performance by a Duo or Group with Vocal") and was nominated for [Album of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Album_of_the_Year "Grammy Award for Album of the Year"). A further attempt at pop success with *[Rainbow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_(Dolly_Parton_album) "Rainbow (Dolly Parton album)")* (1987), which included the single "[The River Unbroken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_River_Unbroken "The River Unbroken")", was not a success and its poor commercial performance led Parton to refocus on country material. *[White Limozeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Limozeen "White Limozeen")* (1989) produced two No. 1 hits, "[Why'd You Come in Here Lookin' Like That](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Why%27d_You_Come_in_Here_Lookin%27_Like_That "Why'd You Come in Here Lookin' Like That")" and "[Yellow Roses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Roses_(Dolly_Parton_song) "Yellow Roses (Dolly Parton song)")". Although her career appeared revived, it proved brief before contemporary country music in the early 1990s moved most veteran artists off the charts.

A duet with [Ricky Van Shelton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricky_Van_Shelton "Ricky Van Shelton"), "[Rockin' Years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockin%27_Years "Rockin' Years")" (1991) reached No. 1. Parton's greatest commercial fortune of the decade came when [Whitney Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Houston "Whitney Houston") recorded her [popular rendition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Will_Always_Love_You_(Whitney_Houston_recording) "I Will Always Love You (Whitney Houston recording)") of "I Will Always Love You" for the soundtrack of *[The Bodyguard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bodyguard_(1992_film) "The Bodyguard (1992 film)")* (1992). Both the single and the album were massively successful. Parton's own soundtrack album from the 1992 film *[Straight Talk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight_Talk "Straight Talk")* was less successful. Her 1993 album, *[Slow Dancing with the Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slow_Dancing_with_the_Moon "Slow Dancing with the Moon")*, earned positive reviews from critics and achieved strong chart success, reaching No. 4 on the *Billboard* Top Country Albums chart and No. 16 on the *Billboard* 200. It was later [certified platinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Certified_platinum "Certified platinum").

In 1993, she recorded "The Day I Fall in Love" as a duet with [James Ingram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Ingram "James Ingram") for the feature film *[Beethoven's 2nd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beethoven%27s_2nd_(film) "Beethoven's 2nd (film)")*. Songwriters Ingram, [Carole Bayer Sager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carole_Bayer_Sager "Carole Bayer Sager"), and [Clif Magness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clif_Magness "Clif Magness") were nominated for an Academy Award for Best Original Song, and Parton and Ingram performed it at the telecast. Like her previous collaborative album with Harris and Ronstadt, Parton released *[Honky Tonk Angels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honky_Tonk_Angels "Honky Tonk Angels")* in the fall of 1993 with [Loretta Lynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loretta_Lynn "Loretta Lynn") and [Tammy Wynette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tammy_Wynette "Tammy Wynette"). The album was certified gold by the [Recording Industry Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America "Recording Industry Association of America") and helped to revive both Wynette and Lynn's careers. Also in 1994, Parton contributed "You Gotta Be My Baby" to the AIDS benefit album *[Red Hot + Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Hot_%2B_Country "Red Hot + Country")* produced by the [Red Hot Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Hot_Organization "Red Hot Organization"). A live acoustic album, *[Heartsongs: Live from Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heartsongs:_Live_from_Home "Heartsongs: Live from Home")*, featuring stripped-down versions of her hits and traditional songs, was released later that year.

Parton's recorded music during the mid-to-late 1990s remained steady and eclectic. Her 1995 re-recording of "I Will Always Love You", performed as a duet with [Vince Gill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vince_Gill "Vince Gill") from her album *[Something Special](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Something_Special_(Dolly_Parton_album) "Something Special (Dolly Parton album)")*, won the Country Music Association's [Vocal Event of the Year Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Music_Association_Awards#Awards_by_year "Country Music Association Awards"). In 1996, she released *[Treasures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasures_(Dolly_Parton_album) "Treasures (Dolly Parton album)")*, an album of covers of 1960s and 1970s hits, featuring material by [Mac Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mac_Davis "Mac Davis"), [Pete Seeger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Seeger "Pete Seeger"), [Kris Kristofferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kris_Kristofferson "Kris Kristofferson"), [Cat Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat_Stevens "Cat Stevens"), and [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young"). Her recording of Stevens' "[Peace Train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Train "Peace Train")" was later remixed and released as a dance single, reaching *Billboard's* dance singles chart.

Her 1998 country-rock album *[Hungry Again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungry_Again "Hungry Again")* consisted entirely of her own compositions. Although neither of its singles, "(Why Don't More Women Sing) Honky Tonk Songs" and "[Salt in My Tears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Salt_in_My_Tears_(Dolly_Parton_song) "The Salt in My Tears (Dolly Parton song)")", charted, videos for both songs received significant airplay on [CMT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMT_(American_TV_channel) "CMT (American TV channel)"). A second collaboration with Harris and Ronstadt, *[Trio II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trio_II "Trio II")*, was released in early 1999. Its cover of Neil Young's "[After the Gold Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/After_the_Gold_Rush_(song) "After the Gold Rush (song)")" won a [Grammy Award for Best Country Collaboration with Vocals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Country_Collaboration_with_Vocals "Grammy Award for Best Country Collaboration with Vocals"). Parton was inducted into the [Country Music Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Music_Hall_of_Fame "Country Music Hall of Fame") in 1999.

Parton recorded a series of [bluegrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluegrass_music "Bluegrass music")-inspired albums beginning with *[The Grass Is Blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Grass_Is_Blue "The Grass Is Blue")* (1999), which won the [Grammy Award for Best Bluegrass Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Bluegrass_Album "Grammy Award for Best Bluegrass Album"), and *[Little Sparrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Sparrow "Little Sparrow")* (2001), whose cover of [Collective Soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_Soul "Collective Soul")'s "[Shine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shine_(Collective_Soul_song) "Shine (Collective Soul song)")" won the Grammy Award for Best Female Country Vocal Performance. The third, *[Halos & Horns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halos_%26_Horns "Halos & Horns")* (2002), included a bluegrass version [Led Zeppelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Led_Zeppelin "Led Zeppelin")'s "[Stairway to Heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stairway_to_Heaven "Stairway to Heaven")". In 2005, she released *[Those Were The Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Those_Were_the_Days_(Dolly_Parton_album) "Those Were the Days (Dolly Parton album)")*, consisting of her interpretations of [folk-rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk-rock "Folk-rock") hits from the late 1960s and early 1970s, including "[Imagine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imagine_(John_Lennon_song) "Imagine (John Lennon song)")", "[Where Do the Children Play?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where_Do_the_Children_Play%3F "Where Do the Children Play?")", "[Crimson and Clover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimson_and_Clover "Crimson and Clover")", and "[Where Have All the Flowers Gone?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where_Have_All_the_Flowers_Gone%3F "Where Have All the Flowers Gone?")"

### 2005–2020: Touring and holiday album

Parton earned her second Academy Award nomination for Best Original Song for "[Travelin' Thru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travelin%27_Thru "Travelin' Thru")", which she wrote for the feature film *[Transamerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transamerica_(film) "Transamerica (film)")* (2005). The song and film centered on a [transgender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender "Transgender") woman and Parton received death threats. She returned to No. 1 on the country chart later in 2005 by lending her harmonies to [Brad Paisley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Paisley "Brad Paisley")'s ballad, "[When I Get Where I'm Going](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/When_I_Get_Where_I%27m_Going "When I Get Where I'm Going")". In September 2007, she released her first single on her own label, Dolly Records, "[Better Get to Livin'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Better_Get_to_Livin%27 "Better Get to Livin'")", which peaked at No. 48 on *Billboard*'s [Hot Country Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Country_Songs "Hot Country Songs") chart.

"Better Get to Livin'" was followed by the studio album *[Backwoods Barbie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backwoods_Barbie "Backwoods Barbie")*, released on February 26, 2008, which reached No. 2 on the country chart. Its debut at No. 17 on the all-genre *Billboard* 200 albums chart was the highest in her career. The album produced four additional singles, including the title track, written as part of her score for *[9 to 5: The Musical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_to_5_(musical) "9 to 5 (musical)")*. After the death of [Michael Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jackson "Michael Jackson"), whom Parton knew personally, she released a video expressing her feelings about him and his death.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

On October 27, 2009, Parton released a four-CD box set, *Dolly*, featuring 99 songs spanning most of her career. She released her second live DVD and album, *Live From London*, in October 2009, filmed during her sold-out 2008 concerts at London's [The O2 Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_O2_Arena "The O2 Arena"). On August 10, 2010, Parton's longtime friend [Billy Ray Cyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Ray_Cyrus "Billy Ray Cyrus") released the album *[Brother Clyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brother_Clyde_(album) "Brother Clyde (album)")*. Parton was featured on the track "The Right Time", which she co-wrote with Cyrus and Morris Joseph Tancredi. On January 6, 2011, Parton announced her new album *[Better Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Better_Day_(album) "Better Day (album)")*. In February 2011, she announced the [Better Day World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Better_Day_World_Tour "Better Day World Tour"), beginning July 17, 2011, with shows in northern Europe and the US. The album's lead-off single, "Together You and I", was released on May 23, 2011, and *Better Day* followed on June 28, 2011.

In 2011, Parton voiced Dolly Gnome in the animated film *[Gnomeo & Juliet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnomeo_%26_Juliet "Gnomeo & Juliet")*. On February 11, 2012, after the sudden death of Whitney Houston, Parton stated, "Mine is only one of the millions of hearts broken over the death of Whitney Houston. I will always be grateful and in awe of the wonderful performance she did on my song and I can truly say from the bottom of my heart, 'Whitney, I will always love you. You will be missed.'"

In 2013, Parton joined [Lulu Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lulu_Roman "Lulu Roman") for a re-recording of "I Will Always Love You" for Roman's album, *At Last*. In 2013, Parton and Kenny Rogers reunited for the title song of his album *[You Can't Make Old Friends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_Can%27t_Make_Old_Friends_(album) "You Can't Make Old Friends (album)")*. For their performance, they were nominated at the [2014 Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Grammy_Awards "2014 Grammy Awards") for [Grammy Award for Best Country Duo/Group Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Country_Duo/Group_Performance "Grammy Award for Best Country Duo/Group Performance"). In 2014, Parton embarked on the [Blue Smoke World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Smoke_World_Tour "Blue Smoke World Tour") in support of her 42nd studio album, *[Blue Smoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Smoke_(album) "Blue Smoke (album)")*. The album was first released in Australia and New Zealand on January 31 to coincide with tour dates there in February and reached No. 1 on album charts in both countries.

It was released in the United States on May 13 and debuted at No. 6 on the *Billboard* 200 chart, making it her first Top 10 album and her highest-charting solo album ever; it also reached No. 2 on the US country chart. The album was released in Europe on June 9 and reached No. 2 on the UK album chart. On June 29, 2014, Parton performed for the first time at the UK [Glastonbury Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glastonbury_Festival "Glastonbury Festival"), singing songs such as "Jolene", "9 to 5" and "Coat of Many Colors" to a crowd of more than 180,000. On March 6, 2016, Parton announced that she would be embarking on a tour in support of her new album, *[Pure & Simple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_%26_Simple_(Dolly_Parton_album) "Pure & Simple (Dolly Parton album)")*. The tour was one of Parton's biggest tours within the United States in more than 25 years. 64 dates were planned in the United States and Canada, visiting the most requested markets missed on previous tours.

In the fall of 2016, she released "Jolene" as a single with the *a cappella* group [Pentatonix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentatonix "Pentatonix") and performed on *[The Voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Voice_(U.S._season_11)#Week_4:_Top_10_(November_28_and_29) "The Voice (U.S. season 11)")* with Pentatonix and [Miley Cyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miley_Cyrus "Miley Cyrus") in November 2016. Also in 2016, Parton was one of thirty artists to perform on "[Forever Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forever_Country "Forever Country")": a mashup of "[Take Me Home, Country Roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Take_Me_Home,_Country_Roads "Take Me Home, Country Roads")", "[On the Road Again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Road_Again_(Willie_Nelson_song) "On the Road Again (Willie Nelson song)")" and her own "I Will Always Love You". The song celebrates fifty years of the [CMA Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMA_Awards "CMA Awards"). At the ceremony itself, Parton was honored with the Willie Nelson Lifetime Achievement Award, which was presented by Lily Tomlin and preceded by a tribute featuring [Jennifer Nettles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Nettles "Jennifer Nettles"), Pentatonix, [Reba McEntire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reba_McEntire "Reba McEntire"), [Kacey Musgraves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kacey_Musgraves "Kacey Musgraves"), [Carrie Underwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrie_Underwood "Carrie Underwood") and [Martina McBride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martina_McBride "Martina McBride").

In 2017, Parton appeared on *[Rainbow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_(Kesha_album) "Rainbow (Kesha album)")*, the third studio album by [Kesha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kesha "Kesha") performing a duet of "Old Flames Can't Hold a Candle to You". The track had been co-written by Kesha's mother [Pebe Sebert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pebe_Sebert "Pebe Sebert"). It was previously a hit for Parton and was included on her 1980 album *[Dolly, Dolly, Dolly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly,_Dolly,_Dolly "Dolly, Dolly, Dolly")*. She also co-wrote and provided featuring vocals for "Rainbowland" on *[Younger Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Younger_Now "Younger Now")*, the sixth album by her goddaughter Miley Cyrus.

In July 2019, Parton made an unannounced appearance at the [Newport Folk Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_Folk_Festival "Newport Folk Festival") in [Rhode Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island "Rhode Island") and performed several songs accompanied by [the Highwomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Highwomen "The Highwomen") and [Linda Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Perry "Linda Perry"). In 2019, Parton collaborated with Christian alternative rock duet [For King and Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_King_%26_Country_(band) "For King & Country (band)") and released a version of their hit "[God Only Knows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_Only_Knows_(For_King_%26_Country_song) "God Only Knows (For King & Country song)")". She followed this by recording a duet with Christian music artist [Zach Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zach_Williams_(musician) "Zach Williams (musician)") in "[There Was Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/There_Was_Jesus "There Was Jesus")". In 2020, Parton received worldwide attention after posting four pictures, in which she showed how she would present herself on social media platforms [LinkedIn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LinkedIn "LinkedIn"), [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook"), [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram "Instagram") and [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter"). The original post on Instagram went viral after celebrities posted their own versions of the so-called Dolly Parton challenge on social media.

On April 10, 2020, Parton re-released 93 songs from six of her classic albums: *Little Sparrow, Halos & Horns, For God and Country, Better Day, Those Were The Days* and *Live and Well*. On May 27, 2020, Parton released "[When Life Is Good Again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/When_Life_Is_Good_Again "When Life Is Good Again")" to lift the spirits of those affected by the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"). She also released a music video for the song, which premiered on [*Time* 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100 "Time 100") talks on May 28, 2020.

In October 2020, Parton was featured on the single "Pink" alongside [Monica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monica_(singer) "Monica (singer)"), [Jordin Sparks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordin_Sparks "Jordin Sparks"), [Sara Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Evans "Sara Evans") and [Rita Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rita_Wilson "Rita Wilson"). The single was released in aid of [Breast Cancer Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breast_Cancer_Research "Breast Cancer Research"). Parton released *[A Holly Dolly Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Holly_Dolly_Christmas "A Holly Dolly Christmas")* in October 2020. On December 6, [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS "CBS") aired a Christmas special, *A Holly Dolly Christmas*, where Parton performed songs from her album.

### 2021–2026: *Rockstar* and Las Vegas residency

In 2021, a duet version of "[Does He Love You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Does_He_Love_You "Does He Love You")" with singer Reba McEntire was released. In early 2022, Parton was nominated for induction into the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame "Rock and Roll Hall of Fame"). Parton initially declined the nomination, believing that the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame was "for the people in rock music", but after learning that this was not the case, Parton said she would accept her induction if she were chosen for the honor. In May, her induction was announced and on November 5, 2022, she was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame. In October 2022, Parton stated in an interview that she would no longer tour, but would continue to play live shows occasionally. On December 31, 2022, Parton co-hosted NBC's New Year's special *[Miley's New Year's Eve Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miley%27s_New_Year%27s_Eve_Party "Miley's New Year's Eve Party")*.

On January 17, 2023, Parton announced that she would release her first rock album, titled *[Rockstar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockstar_(Dolly_Parton_album) "Rockstar (Dolly Parton album)")*, later that year, during an interview on *The View*. The lead single "World on Fire" was released on May 11, 2023, and it peaked at No. 1 a week later. The album was released on November 17, 2023, and featured collaborations with [Paul McCartney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_McCartney "Paul McCartney"), [Ringo Starr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringo_Starr "Ringo Starr"), [Sting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sting_(musician) "Sting (musician)"), [Elton John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elton_John "Elton John"), [Stevie Nicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevie_Nicks "Stevie Nicks"), [Sheryl Crow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheryl_Crow "Sheryl Crow"), [Steve Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Perry "Steve Perry"), Miley Cyrus and [Lizzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lizzo "Lizzo"), among others. The album received generally positive reviews from critics and debuted at No. 3 on the *Billboard* 200, becoming Parton's highest-charting solo studio album as well as topping the [Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Country_Albums "Top Country Albums") and [Rock Albums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Albums "Rock Albums") charts.

On January 20, 2023, the soundtrack single "Gonna Be You" from the movie *[80 for Brady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/80_for_Brady "80 for Brady")* was released. The song was written by [Diane Warren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diane_Warren "Diane Warren") and performed by Dolly Parton, [Belinda Carlisle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belinda_Carlisle "Belinda Carlisle"), [Cyndi Lauper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyndi_Lauper "Cyndi Lauper"), [Debbie Harry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debbie_Harry "Debbie Harry") and [Gloria Estefan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Estefan "Gloria Estefan"). On March 29, 2024, Parton was featured on [Beyoncé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyonc%C3%A9 "Beyoncé")'s eighth studio album *[Cowboy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowboy_Carter "Cowboy Carter")*, being involved in the introduction to the reinterpretation of "[Jolene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolene#Beyoncé_version "Jolene")" by her colleague, in which Parton associated the character of "Jolene" with "Becky" from the track "[Sorry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorry_(Beyonc%C3%A9_song) "Sorry (Beyoncé song)")" on Beyoncé's album [*Lemonade*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemonade_(album) "Lemonade (album)"), and as a featured artist on the track "Tyrant". Parton offered to collaborate on the recording of what became her last known duet with [Belles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belles "Belles") on the new music track "Son of Jolene".

On February 14, 2025, Parton featured on [Sabrina Carpenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabrina_Carpenter "Sabrina Carpenter")'s "[Please Please Please](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Please_Please_Please_(Sabrina_Carpenter_song) "Please Please Please (Sabrina Carpenter song)")" on the deluxe edition of her album *[Short n' Sweet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_n%27_Sweet "Short n' Sweet")* and its accompanying music video. On March 7, 2025, Parton released the single "[If You Hadn't Been There](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/If_You_Hadn%27t_Been_There "If You Hadn't Been There")", as a tribute to her husband, who had died a week before. In the United Kingdom, the single peaked at No. 21 on the sales and downloads chart components. In 2025, Dolly Parton recorded a new version of the 1985 single *"Home Sweet Home"* with [Mötley Crüe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B6tley_Cr%C3%BCe "Mötley Crüe"), marking the song's 40th anniversary. The track was released on the band's compilation *From The Beginning*, with a portion of proceeds benefiting Covenant House, a charity supporting homeless youth. On January 16, 2026, in anticipation of her 80th birthday, Parton released a rendition of "Light of a Clear Blue Morning" featuring several celebrity guests: [Queen Latifah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Latifah "Queen Latifah"), [Lainey Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lainey_Wilson "Lainey Wilson"), Miley Cyrus, Reba McEntire, and [David Foster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Foster "David Foster"). On March 13, 2026, Parton made her first major appearance since the health scare to give the keynote address at Dollywood during the 41st anniversary celebration.

Parton made her last public appearance in June 2026, appearing at the grand opening of a truck stop off [Interstate 65](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_65_in_Tennessee "Interstate 65 in Tennessee") in [Cornersville, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornersville,_Tennessee "Cornersville, Tennessee") that was being named in her honor. On August 14, just eleven days before her death, she was set to attend the grand opening of [a new ride](#External_links) at Dollywood. Instead, she appeared in a recorded video from her home in Nashville to explain that because of health issues she could not appear in person.

A new version of "Jolene" is set to be released on October 9, 2026, with parts of the song being sung in Welsh in recognition of her ancestry.

## Acting career

### Breakthrough

In addition to her performing appearances on *[The Porter Wagoner Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Porter_Wagoner_Show "The Porter Wagoner Show")* in the 1960s and into the 1970s, her two self-titled television variety shows in the *[Dolly!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly! "Dolly!")* and *[Dolly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_(1987_TV_series) "Dolly (1987 TV series)")* and on *American Idol* in 2008 and other guest appearances, Parton also had television roles. In 1979, she received an Emmy award nomination as "Outstanding Supporting Actress in a Variety Program" for her guest appearance in a [Cher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cher "Cher") special. During the mid-1970s Parton wanted to expand her audience base. Her first attempt, the television variety show *Dolly!* (1976–1977), had high ratings but lasted only one season. Parton requested to be released from her contract because of the stress it was causing on her [vocal cords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocal_cords "Vocal cords"). She later debuted a second television variety show, also titled *Dolly* (1987–1988). The series was canceled after only one season because of low ratings.

In her first feature film, Parton portrayed a secretary in a leading role with [Jane Fonda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Fonda "Jane Fonda") and [Lily Tomlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lily_Tomlin "Lily Tomlin") in the comedy film *[9 to 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_to_5_(film) "9 to 5 (film)")* (1980). The movie highlighted discrimination against women in the workplace and created awareness of the National Association of Working Women (9–5). She received nominations for a [Golden Globe Award for Best Actress – Musical or Comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Musical_or_Comedy "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy") and a [Golden Globe Award for New Star of the Year – Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_New_Star_of_the_Year_%E2%80%93_Actress "Golden Globe Award for New Star of the Year – Actress"). Parton wrote and recorded the film's title song. It received nominations for an [Academy Award for Best Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Song "Academy Award for Best Song") and a [Golden Globe Award for Best Original Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Original_Song "Golden Globe Award for Best Original Song").

Released as a single, the song won both the [Grammy Award for Best Female Country Vocal Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Female_Country_Vocal_Performance "Grammy Award for Best Female Country Vocal Performance") and the [Grammy Award for Best Country Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Country_Song "Grammy Award for Best Country Song"). It also reached No. 1 on the Hot 100 chart and it was No. 78 on the "[AFI's 100 Years...100 Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFI%27s_100_Years...100_Songs "AFI's 100 Years...100 Songs")" list released by the [American Film Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Film_Institute "American Film Institute") in 2004. *9 to 5* became a major box office success, grossing over $3.9 million its opening weekend and over $103 million worldwide. Parton was named Top Female Box Office Star by the *[Motion Picture Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_Picture_Herald "Motion Picture Herald")* in both 1981 and 1982 due to the film's success.

In late 1981, Parton began work on her second film, the [musical film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_film "Musical film") *The Best Little Whorehouse in Texas* (1982). The film earned her a second nomination for a Golden Globe Award for Best Actress – Motion Picture Musical or Comedy. It was greeted with positive critical reviews and became a commercial success, earning over $69 million worldwide. After a two-year hiatus from films, Parton was teamed with [Sylvester Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone "Sylvester Stallone") for *[Rhinestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinestone_(film) "Rhinestone (film)")* (1984), a comedy film about a country music star's efforts to mold an unknown into a music sensation. The film was a critical and financial failure, making just over $21 million on a $28 million budget.

### Continued roles

In 1989, Parton returned to film acting in *[Steel Magnolias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_Magnolias "Steel Magnolias")*, based on the play [of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_Magnolias_(play) "Steel Magnolias (play)") by [Robert Harling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Harling_(writer) "Robert Harling (writer)"). The film was popular with critics and audiences, grossing over $95 million in the US. Parton starred in the television movies *[A Smoky Mountain Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Smoky_Mountain_Christmas "A Smoky Mountain Christmas")* (1986), *[Wild Texas Wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_Texas_Wind "Wild Texas Wind")* (1991), *[Unlikely Angel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unlikely_Angel "Unlikely Angel")* (1996), portraying an angel sent back to earth after a deadly car crash and *[Blue Valley Songbird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Valley_Songbird "Blue Valley Songbird")* (1999), where her character lives through her music. She starred with [James Woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Woods "James Woods") in *Straight Talk* (1992), which received mixed reviews and grossed a mild $21 million at the box office.

Parton's 1987 variety show, *[Dolly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_(1987_TV_series) "Dolly (1987 TV series)"),* lasted only one season. She made a [cameo appearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance "Cameo appearance") as herself in *[The Beverly Hillbillies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beverly_Hillbillies_(film) "The Beverly Hillbillies (film)")* (1993), an adaptation of the TV sitcom *[The Beverly Hillbillies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beverly_Hillbillies "The Beverly Hillbillies")* (1962–1971). Parton also did voice work for animated television series, playing the character Katrina Eloise "Murph" Murphy (Ms. Frizzle's first cousin) in a 1994 episode of *[The Magic School Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Magic_School_Bus "The Magic School Bus")* ("The Family Holiday Special").

She guest-starred in several sitcoms, including a 1990 episode of *[Designing Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Designing_Women "Designing Women")* ("The First Day of the Last Decade of the Entire Twentieth Century") as herself, the guardian movie star of Charlene's baby. She made a guest appearance on *[Reba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reba_(TV_series) "Reba (TV series)")* ("Reba's Rules of Real Estate") portraying a real-estate agency owner and on *[The Simpsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simpsons "The Simpsons")* ("[Sunday, Cruddy Sunday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunday,_Cruddy_Sunday "Sunday, Cruddy Sunday")" in 1999).

She appeared as herself in 2000 on the Halloween episode of [Bette Midler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bette_Midler "Bette Midler")'s short-lived sitcom *[Bette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bette_(TV_series) "Bette (TV series)")* and on episode 14 of *[Babes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babes_(TV_Series) "Babes (TV Series)")*, produced by Sandollar Productions, Parton and Sandy Gallin's joint production company. She made cameo appearances on the [Disney Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney_Channel "Disney Channel") as "Aunt Dolly", visiting Hannah and her family in fellow Tennessean and real-life [goddaughter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goddaughter "Goddaughter") [Miley Cyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miley_Cyrus "Miley Cyrus")'s series *[Hannah Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannah_Montana "Hannah Montana")* ("Good Golly, Miss Dolly" in 2006, "I Will Always Loathe You" in 2007 and "Kiss It All Goodbye" in 2010).

Parton appeared as an overprotective mother in the comedy *[Frank McKlusky, C.I.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_McKlusky,_C.I. "Frank McKlusky, C.I.")*. (2002) She made a cameo appearance in the comedy film *[Miss Congeniality 2: Armed and Fabulous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_Congeniality_2:_Armed_and_Fabulous "Miss Congeniality 2: Armed and Fabulous")* (2005), starring [Sandra Bullock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_Bullock "Sandra Bullock"). ## Other ventures

In 1998, *Nashville Business* ranked her the wealthiest country music star. As of 2017[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Dolly_Parton&action=edit), her net worth was estimated at $500 million.

### Writing

Parton was a prolific songwriter, writing country music songs with strong elements of [folk music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") and her upbringing in humble mountain surroundings, as well as reflecting her family's Christian background. Her songs "Coat of Many Colors", "I Will Always Love You" and "Jolene", among others, became classics. On November 4, 2003, Parton was honored as a [BMI Icon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_Music,_Inc. "Broadcast Music, Inc.") at the 2003 BMI Country Awards. Parton earned more than 35 BMI Pop and Country Awards. In 2001, she was inducted into the [Songwriters Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songwriters_Hall_of_Fame "Songwriters Hall of Fame"). In a 2009 interview on [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN "CNN")'s *[Larry King Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_King_Live "Larry King Live")*, she said she had written "at least three thousand" songs, writing something – be it a song or an idea – every day since she was seven.

Parton's songwriting was featured prominently in several films. In addition to the title song for *9 to 5*, she also recorded a second version of "I Will Always Love You" for *[The Best Little Whorehouse in Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Best_Little_Whorehouse_in_Texas_(film) "The Best Little Whorehouse in Texas (film)")* (1982). The second version was a No. 1 country hit and also reached No. 53 on the pop charts. "I Will Always Love You" has been covered by many country artists, including Linda Ronstadt on *[Prisoner In Disguise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner_In_Disguise "Prisoner In Disguise")* (1975), Kenny Rogers on *[Vote for Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_for_Love "Vote for Love")* (1996) and [LeAnn Rimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeAnn_Rimes "LeAnn Rimes") on *[Unchained Melody: The Early Years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unchained_Melody:_The_Early_Years "Unchained Melody: The Early Years")* (1997). Whitney Houston's version, which was performed on *The Bodyguard* soundtrack, became a best-selling hit both written and performed by a female vocalist, with worldwide sales of more than twelve million copies. The song has been translated into Italian and performed by the Welsh opera singer [Katherine Jenkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katherine_Jenkins "Katherine Jenkins").

As a songwriter, Parton was twice nominated for an [Academy Award for Best Original Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Song "Academy Award for Best Original Song") for "9 to 5" and "[Travelin' Thru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travelin%27_Thru "Travelin' Thru")" (2005) from the film *[Transamerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transamerica_(film) "Transamerica (film)")*. "Travelin' Thru" won Best Original Song at the 2005 Phoenix Film Critics Society Awards. It was also nominated for both the 2005 [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award "Golden Globe Award") for [Best Original Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Original_Song "Golden Globe Award for Best Original Song") and the 2005 [Broadcast Film Critics Association Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_Film_Critics_Association_Award "Broadcast Film Critics Association Award") (also known as the Critics' Choice Awards) for [Best Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_Film_Critics_Association_Award_for_Best_Song "Broadcast Film Critics Association Award for Best Song"). A cover of "Love Is Like A Butterfly" by [Clare Torry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_Torry "Clare Torry") was used as the theme music for the British TV show *[Butterflies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butterflies_(TV_series) "Butterflies (TV series)")*. Parton collaborated with her youngest sister [Rachel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Parton_George "Rachel Parton George") on the cookbook *Good Lookin' Cookin'*, which featured Parton family recipes and was released in 2024.

### Stage musicals

#### *9 to 5: The Musical*

Parton wrote the score (and Patricia Resnick the book) for *[9 to 5: The Musical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_to_5:_The_Musical "9 to 5: The Musical")*, a [musical-theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_theater "Musical theater") [adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatrical_adaptation "Theatrical adaptation") of Parton's feature film *9 to 5* (1980). The musical ran at the [Ahmanson Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmanson_Theatre "Ahmanson Theatre"), Los Angeles, in late 2008. It opened on Broadway at the [Marquis Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquis_Theatre "Marquis Theatre") in New York on April 30, 2009, to mixed reviews.
The title track of her 2008 album *Backwoods Barbie* was written for the musical's character Doralee. Although her score (as well as the musical debut of actress [Allison Janney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allison_Janney "Allison Janney")) was praised, the show struggled, closing on September 6, 2009, after 24 previews and 148 performances.

Parton received nominations for the [Drama Desk Award for Outstanding Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama_Desk_Award_for_Outstanding_Music "Drama Desk Award for Outstanding Music") and [Drama Desk Award for Outstanding Lyrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama_Desk_Award_for_Outstanding_Lyrics "Drama Desk Award for Outstanding Lyrics") and a nomination for [Tony Award for Best Original Score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Original_Score "Tony Award for Best Original Score"). Developing the musical was not a quick process. According to the [public-radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_broadcasting "Public broadcasting") program *[Studio 360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio_360 "Studio 360")* (October 29, 2005), in October 2005 Parton was in the midst of composing songs for a Broadway musical theater adaptation of the film. In late June 2007, *9 to 5: The Musical* was read for industry presentations. The readings starred [Megan Hilty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megan_Hilty "Megan Hilty"), Allison Janney, [Stephanie J. Block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephanie_J._Block "Stephanie J. Block"), [Bebe Neuwirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bebe_Neuwirth "Bebe Neuwirth") and [Marc Kudisch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Kudisch "Marc Kudisch"). [Ambassador Theatre Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambassador_Theatre_Group "Ambassador Theatre Group") announced a 2012 UK tour for *Dolly Parton's 9 to 5: The Musical*, commencing at [Manchester Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Opera_House "Manchester Opera House"), on October 12, 2012.

#### *Dolly: A True Original Musical*

In June 2024, Parton announced an autobiographical musical about her life and career initially titled *Hello, I'm Dolly* (named after her debut album and also a play on *[Hello, Dolly!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hello,_Dolly!_(musical) "Hello, Dolly! (musical)")*), with a goal of opening on [Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_theatre "Broadway theatre") in 2026 with direction by [Bartlett Sher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartlett_Sher "Bartlett Sher"). The musical, with a co-written [book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_(musical_theater) "Book (musical theater)") by Parton and Maria S. Schlatter, features original songs as well as her more well-known hits. Parton also revealed that she had been working on the musical for the past decade. The title was promoted as *Dolly: An Original Musical* on December 6, coinciding with a nationwide casting call for actresses to play Parton through different stages of her life, using the hashtag #SearchForDolly on social media video posts. The show opened as *[Dolly: A True Original Musical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly:_A_True_Original_Musical "Dolly: A True Original Musical")* at the [Belmont University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belmont_University "Belmont University") Fisher Center for the Performing Arts in Nashville in July 2025.

Parton invested much of her earnings into business ventures in her native East Tennessee, including [Pigeon Forge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigeon_Forge "Pigeon Forge"). She was a co-owner of [The Dollywood Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herschend_Family_Entertainment_Corporation#The_Dollywood_Company "Herschend Family Entertainment Corporation"), which operates the theme park (a former [Silver Dollar City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Dollar_City "Silver Dollar City")), a [dinner theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinner_theater "Dinner theater"), [Dolly Parton's Stampede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_Parton%27s_Stampede "Dolly Parton's Stampede"), the [waterpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterpark "Waterpark") [Dollywood's Splash Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dollywood%27s_Splash_Country "Dollywood's Splash Country") and the Dream More Resort and Spa, all in Pigeon Forge. With 3.1 million visitors in 2024[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Dolly_Parton&action=edit), Dollywood is among the twenty most popular theme parks in the United States, and the fifth most popular theme park that operates seasonally.

The Dolly Parton's Stampede business has venues in [Branson, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branson,_Missouri "Branson, Missouri") and [Myrtle Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrtle_Beach "Myrtle Beach"), South Carolina. In 2011, the Myrtle Beach Stampede location was redesigned under the name Pirates Voyage, and the show later expanded to Tennessee and Florida locations. A former location in [Orlando, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando,_Florida "Orlando, Florida"), closed in January 2008 after the land and building were sold to a developer.

On January 19, 2012, coinciding with Dolly Parton's 66th birthday, [Gaylord Opryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaylord_Opryland "Gaylord Opryland") and Dollywood announced a 50/50 joint venture to develop a 114-acre family entertainment zone in Nashville, featuring a $50 million combination water and snow park as its first phase. In September 2012, Parton officially withdrew her support for the Nashville park because of the restructuring of Gaylord Entertainment Company after its merger with Marriott International. In June 2015, the Dollywood Company purchased the Lumberjack Feud Dinner Show in Pigeon Forge. The show, which opened in June 2011, was owned and operated by Rob Scheer until the close of the 2015 season. The new, renovated show by the Dollywood Company opened in 2016.

## Artistry

### Influences

Parton, though influenced by big-name stars, often credited much of her inspiration to her family and community. In her 2020 book, *Songteller: My Life in Lyrics*, Parton wrote: "So it was just natural for my mom to always be singing. [She] had that old-timey voice and she used to sing all these songs that were brought over from the Old World. They were English, Irish, Welsh, folk songs where people tell stories." Parton calls her mother's voice "haunting". "Lord you would feel it," she wrote.

Her biggest influence was her aunt: "People often ask me who my influences were, they think I'm going to say some big names and there were a few 'stars' I was impressed with. But my hero was my Aunt Dorothy Jo, Mama's baby sister. She was not only an evangelist, she played banjo, she played guitar and she wrote some great songs." Fellow singers also had an impact on Parton; she described [George Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Jones "George Jones") as her "all-time favorite singer", as well as mentioning her love for other artists such as [Kitty Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitty_Wells "Kitty Wells"), [Roy Acuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Acuff "Roy Acuff") and [Rose Maddox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Maddox "Rose Maddox").

### Musicianship

Though unable to read sheet music, Parton was raised in a family that enjoyed making music, including at church. She learned how to write songs and play many instruments, including the [dulcimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulcimer "Dulcimer"), [autoharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoharp "Autoharp"), [banjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo "Banjo"), guitar, [electric guitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_guitar "Electric guitar"), [fiddle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiddle "Fiddle"), [piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano "Piano"), [recorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recorder_(musical_instrument) "Recorder (musical instrument)") and [saxophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxophone "Saxophone"). Reflecting on her abilities Parton said, "I play some of everything. I ain't that good at none of it, but I try to sell it. I really try to lay into it." Parton also used her acrylic fingernails as an instrument, most evident in "[9 to 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_to_5_(Dolly_Parton_song) "9 to 5 (Dolly Parton song)")", where she derived the beat from clacking her nails together while backstage on the set of the film *9 to 5*.

## Philanthropy and advocacy

### Education and literacy

In 1988, Parton founded the non-profit [Dollywood Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dollywood_Foundation "Dollywood Foundation"), originally to improve educational outcomes among children in her home county of [Sevier County, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevier_County,_Tennessee "Sevier County, Tennessee"). Among the foundation's early efforts was the Buddy Program, in which seventh- and eighth-grade students paired with a friend and pledged to support each other in remaining in school and graduating from high school. Students who graduated received $500. The dropout rate among participating classes fell from 35% to 6%, and the program encouraged the community to develop additional initiatives to sustain the improvement.

Parton's literacy program, Dolly Parton's Imagination Library, is also a part of the Dollywood Foundation, and it was founded in 1995 in honor of her father, who never learned to read or write. It mails one book per month to each enrolled child from the time of their birth until they enter kindergarten. More than 1,600 local communities provide the Imagination Library to almost 850,000 children each month across the US, Canada, the UK, Australia and Ireland. In 2006, Parton published a cookbook, *Dolly's Dixie Fixin's: Love, Laughter and Lots of Good Food*, with all proceeds going toward the Imagination Library. On the 30th anniversary of the program, Roxanne Owens provided a summary of the impact of the initiative for *Children and Libraries*, the journal of the [Association for Library Service to Children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Library_Service_to_Children "Association for Library Service to Children").

In February 2018, she donated her 100 millionth free book, a copy of Parton's children's picture book *Coat of Many Colors*, to the [Library of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress "Library of Congress") in Washington, D.C. and was honored by the Library on account of the charity's "sending out its 100 millionth book".

Starting on February 24, 2022, employees of Dollywood who decided to go to college would have their tuition fees, books, and other associated fees, paid for by the theme park. In October 2022, it was revealed that Parton had been paying for the band uniforms for several Tennessee high schools for numerous years.

In February 2025, Indiana governor [Mike Braun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Braun "Mike Braun") declined to continue a state 50% match for Parton's Imagination Library. Dollywood Foundation President Jeff Conyers said, "We are hopeful that Governor Braun and the Indiana Legislature will continue this vital investment by restoring the state's funding match for local Imagination Library programs." Braun would soon afterwards task his wife Maureen with finding ways to keep the Imagination Library's Indiana chapter alive.

For her work in literacy, Parton received awards including the [Association of American Publishers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_American_Publishers "Association of American Publishers") Honors Award (2000), [Good Housekeeping Seal of Approval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Housekeeping_Seal_of_Approval "Good Housekeeping Seal of Approval") (2001), and Parents as Teachers National Center – Child and Family Advocacy Award (2003). The [Chartered Institute of Library and Information Professionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chartered_Institute_of_Library_and_Information_Professionals "Chartered Institute of Library and Information Professionals") awarded Parton an [Honorary Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_Fellowship "Honorary Fellowship") in 2024.

### Healthcare and medical research

The Dollywood Foundation, funded from Parton's profits, has brought jobs and tax revenues to a previously depressed region. Parton also worked to raise money for other causes, including the [American Red Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Red_Cross "American Red Cross") and [HIV/AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS "HIV/AIDS")-related charities.

In December 2006, Parton pledged $500,000 toward a proposed $90 million hospital and cancer center to be constructed in [Sevierville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevierville "Sevierville") in the name of Robert F. Thomas, the physician who delivered her. She announced a benefit concert to raise additional funds for the project. The concert played to about 8,000 people.

Parton was a generous donor to [Vanderbilt University Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_University_Medical_Center "Vanderbilt University Medical Center") (VUMC). Among her gifts was a contribution to the Monroe Carell Jr. Children's Hospital at Vanderbilt Pediatric Cancer Program in honor of a friend, [Naji Abumrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naji_Abumrad "Naji Abumrad") and her niece, Hannah Dennison, who was successfully treated for [leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia "Leukemia") as a child at the Children's Hospital.

Parton also made "generational and transformational" donations of undisclosed amounts to the East Tennessee Children's Hospital, according to the hospital's president and chief executive officer. The hospital was later renamed the [Dolly Parton Children's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_Parton_Children%27s_Hospital "Dolly Parton Children's Hospital").

#### Moderna COVID-19 vaccine

In response to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"), Parton donated $1 million towards research at Vanderbilt University Medical Center and encouraged those who can afford it to make similar donations. She said, "I'm a very proud girl today to know I had anything at all to do with something that's going to help us through this crazy pandemic." Her donation funded the critical early stages of development of the [Moderna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moderna "Moderna") vaccine.

In March 2021, Parton was [vaccinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_vaccine "COVID-19 vaccine") against [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19 "COVID-19") at Vanderbilt University. She strongly encouraged everybody to get vaccinated when eligible, and labeled her social media accounts of the occasion "Dolly gets a dose of her own medicine." Parton celebrated her own vaccination by posting a video clip of herself belting out a few lines of revised lyrics set to the tune of "[Jolene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolene "Jolene")", starting with the word *vaccine*.

### Disaster relief and community aid

In response to the [2016 Great Smoky Mountains wildfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Great_Smoky_Mountains_wildfires "2016 Great Smoky Mountains wildfires"), Parton was one of the country music artists who participated in a telethon to raise money for victims of the fires. This was held in Nashville on December 9. Parton hosted her own telethon for the victims on December 13 and reportedly raised around $9 million. Her fund, the "My People Fund", provided $1,000 a month for six months to more than 900 families affected by the wildfires, culminating with $5,000 to each home in the final month due to increased fundraising, totaling $10,000 per family.

In 2018, the FBI honored Parton for her wildfire aid, awarding her the 2018 Director's Community Leadership Award in a ceremony at [FBI Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI_Headquarters "FBI Headquarters"). The honor was bestowed by Director Christopher Wray and was accepted on Parton's behalf by David Dotson, the CEO of the Dollywood Foundation.

The impact of the fund's financial relief for the 2016 wildfire victims was studied by University of Tennessee College of Social Work professor Stacia West, who examined the impact of cash transfers on poverty alleviation. West surveyed 100 recipients of the emergency relief funds in April 2017 on topics including housing, financial impact, physical and emotional health and sources of support, with a follow-up survey conducted in December 2017. West found that the "My People Fund", in tandem with traditional disaster response, gave families the ability to make decisions that were most beneficial to them and concluded that unconditional cash support may be more beneficial for disaster relief than conditional financial support. The report mentioned the impact of the monthly financial disbursements from the "My People Fund" on residents' emergency savings: "Following the monthly disbursements of unconditional cash assistance, participants were able to return to baseline financial stability reported prior to the wildfire and improve their ability to set aside savings for hypothetical future emergencies."

In the aftermath of 2024's [Hurricane Helene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Helene "Hurricane Helene"), Parton announced a donation of $2 million to relief efforts, $1 million personally and another $1 million through her various businesses and the Dollywood Foundation.

### Animal welfare and conservation

In 2003, her efforts to preserve the [bald eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bald_eagle "Bald eagle") through the [American Eagle Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Eagle_Foundation "American Eagle Foundation")'s sanctuary at Dollywood earned her the Partnership Award from the [US Fish and Wildlife Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Fish_and_Wildlife_Service "US Fish and Wildlife Service"). In 2006, Parton and [Emmylou Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmylou_Harris "Emmylou Harris") allowed use of their music in a [PETA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PETA "PETA") ad campaign that encouraged pet owners to keep their dogs indoors rather than chained outside.

### LGBTQ+ advocacy

Though often politically neutral, Parton was known for her long history of openly supporting the [gay community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay "Gay"), and her example helped pave the way for Reba McEntire, Shania Twain, and younger country artists to do the same. Parton publicly came out in support of [same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage "Same-sex marriage") in 2009. She also voiced her support for the [trans community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender "Transgender") after a series of [anti-trans measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_rights_in_Tennessee#Gender_identity_and_expression "LGBTQ rights in Tennessee") came into law in Tennessee in 2023. The [Tennessee Equality Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_Equality_Project "Tennessee Equality Project") described her as "the outsider who made it big by being herself, and leading with love". They said the LGBTQ community "always felt that she had our back, and it mattered".

### Recognition

In addition to the awards Parton received specifically for her efforts toward promoting literacy, Parton also received recognition for her general philanthropic work. On November 8, 2007, she received the [Woodrow Wilson Award for Public Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson_Awards "Woodrow Wilson Awards") from the [Woodrow Wilson International Center for Scholars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson_International_Center_for_Scholars "Woodrow Wilson International Center for Scholars") of the [Smithsonian Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian_Institution "Smithsonian Institution") at a ceremony in Nashville.

On May 8, 2009, Parton gave the [commencement speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commencement_speech "Commencement speech") at the graduation ceremony for the [University of Tennessee, Knoxville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tennessee,_Knoxville "University of Tennessee, Knoxville")'s College of Arts and Sciences. During the ceremony, she received an honorary [Doctor of Humane Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Humane_Letters "Doctor of Humane Letters") from the university. It was only the second honorary degree given by the university; [Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_(education) "Chancellor (education)") [Jimmy Cheek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Cheek "Jimmy Cheek") said, "Because of her career not just as a musician and entertainer, but for her role as a cultural ambassador, philanthropist and lifelong advocate for education, it is fitting that she be honored with an honorary degree from the flagship educational institution of her home state." Following her death, Parton was the subject of an obituary in the prominent science journal *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_(journal) "Nature (journal)")* celebrating her contributions to fields such as public health and literacy, including a laudatory quotation from [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization "World Health Organization") director [Tedros Ghebreyesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tedros_Ghebreyesus "Tedros Ghebreyesus").

## Public image and appearance

> I patterned my look after the town tramp. Everybody said, "She's trash." And in my little girl mind I thought, "Well, that's what I'm gonna be when I grow up."

— Dolly Parton, 2022

Parton was known for her charisma, strong connections with fans, and characteristic wit. When asked by a teenager what advice she would give American teenagers, she replied, "Act like it's raining every day and wear your rubbers."

Although Parton had previously declined requests to pose nude, she appeared on the cover of the October 1978 issue of *[Playboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playboy "Playboy")*, wearing a classic [bunny costume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playboy_bunny "Playboy bunny"), complete with ears. The issue featured Lawrence Grobel's extensive and candid interview with Parton, representing one of her earliest high-profile interviews with the mainstream press.

The association of [breasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_breast "Human breast") with Parton's public image resulted in the naming of [Dolly the sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_the_sheep "Dolly the sheep") after her in 1996. This sheep was [cloned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloning "Cloning") from a cell taken from an adult ewe's [mammary gland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammary_gland "Mammary gland"), with one of the scientists saying, "we couldn't think of a more impressive pair of glands". In [Mobile, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile,_Alabama "Mobile, Alabama"), the [General W.K. Wilson Jr. Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_W.K._Wilson_Jr._Bridge "General W.K. Wilson Jr. Bridge") is commonly called "the Dolly Parton Bridge" due to its arches resembling her bust. The thickened appearance of the turret frontal armor of the [T-72](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-72 "T-72")A [main battle tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_battle_tank "Main battle tank") led to the unofficial Army nickname "Dolly Parton" and later the T-72BIs got the "Super Dolly Parton" nickname.

Parton was known for having undergone considerable plastic surgery. On a 2003 episode of *[The Oprah Winfrey Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Oprah_Winfrey_Show "The Oprah Winfrey Show")*, Winfrey asked what kind of [cosmetic surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmetic_surgery "Cosmetic surgery") Parton had undergone. Parton replied that cosmetic surgery was imperative in keeping with her famous image. Parton repeatedly joked about her physical image and surgeries saying, "It takes a lot of money to look this cheap." When asked about future plastic surgeries she said, "If I see something sagging, bagging or dragging, I'll get it nipped, tucked or sucked."

Parton's feminine escapism from the harsh rural working conditions of her childhood was acknowledged in her words, "Womanhood was a difficult thing to get a grip on in those hills, unless you were a man." Parton joked in 2012 that she had entered a "Dolly Parton [drag queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drag_queen "Drag queen") look-alike contest" and lost.

## Personal life

### Faith

Parton grew up attending her grandfather's Pentecostal church. She remarked in the 2022 television special *Dolly Parton's Mountain Magic Christmas* that religion had been "crammed [...] down her throat" when she was a child and that she did not want to portray her faith in such a manner. Parton said in 2018 that she believed in God, that he "helps" and "guides" her, and that she "talk[s] to Him every day". She said that her faith is "very important", that she believes "Jesus loves [her]", and that she is "never ashamed of [her] religion". She said she is "not trying to cram it down anyone else's throat", but that she "just think[s] that everybody's got to have something to believe in". In an interview with the *[New Humanist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Humanist "New Humanist")* magazine in 2024, she said that "whether you believe in God or not, whether you call it Mother Nature or Father Time or a higher wisdom. You need to believe in yourself and you need to believe there's a force that can push you and lead you on", and added "I'm not a religious fanatic. I wouldn't even say I'm religious, though I grew up with that background." Separately, Parton also referred to herself as a "person of faith", and "a very spiritual person". She also said that she regularly read the Bible.

In a 2020 interview with the *[Marie Claire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Claire "Marie Claire")* magazine, Parton said: "... Every single day, before I do anything, I wake up and I thank God for the night and ask Him to bless the day and to bring all the right things ... all the wrong people out of my life, and bring all the right stuff in. And just to guide me, lead me. And I always pray that He'll let me uplift Mankind and glorify Him."

### Marriage

Parton arrived in [Nashville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville "Nashville") on a Saturday morning in 1964, the day after graduating from [Sevier County High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevier_County_High_School "Sevier County High School"). After starting her laundry inside the Wishy Washy Laundromat, she got a cold drink and stepped outside. Carl Thomas Dean (1942–2025), a Nashville native, pulled up along the sidewalk where she was standing. He mentioned she might get sunburned because of the style of shirt she was wearing. Following that interaction, they began a conversation. They were married in [Ringgold, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringgold,_Georgia "Ringgold, Georgia"), on May 30, 1966. Although Parton did not use Dean's surname professionally, it was used in her personal life. Her passport read "Dolly Parton Dean" and she sometimes used Dean when signing contracts.

Dean ran an asphalt road and driveway-paving business in Nashville that he inherited from his father. He always shunned publicity and rarely accompanied his wife to public events. According to Parton, he saw her perform only once. She also said in interviews that even though it appeared they spent little time together, it was because nobody saw him publicly. Dean often visited Dollywood unrecognized. Parton commented on Dean's romantic side, saying that he did spontaneous things to surprise her and sometimes wrote poems for her. In 2011 she said they were "... really very proud of our marriage. It's the first for both of us. And the last." The couple renewed their vows in honor of their 50th wedding anniversary in May 2016.

Parton and Dean moved into their long-term home on 75 acres in [Brentwood, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brentwood,_Tennessee "Brentwood, Tennessee"), in 1972. They kept ownership of their prior home in [Antioch, Nashville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioch,_Nashville "Antioch, Nashville"), for several years. Parton's uncle, Dot Watson, and Dean built the home in 1970 and 1971. Her brothers Denver and [Randy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Parton "Randy Parton") also worked on the structure. Several years later, they added a [guest house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guest_house "Guest house") to the property. Dean and Parton lived there together for more than 52 years until Dean's death in Nashville on March 3, 2025.

Although they never had children due to Parton's health issues, Parton and Dean helped raise some of her younger siblings in Nashville. Her nieces and nephews, and later grand‑nieces and grand‑nephews, referred to them as "Uncle Peepaw" and "Aunt Granny". The latter name was later used for one of Parton's Dollywood restaurants. Parton was also the godmother of singer-songwriter [Miley Cyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miley_Cyrus "Miley Cyrus").

### Health issues

Parton was diagnosed with [endometriosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endometriosis "Endometriosis") and had a partial [hysterectomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hysterectomy "Hysterectomy") in her late 30s, which affected her ability to have children and caused some weight gain. She also noted that, throughout her life, she had contended with [kidney stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidney_stones "Kidney stones").

In May 2026, Parton said she was responding well to treatment after her immune and digestive systems, in her words, "got all out of whack". In an interview with *[People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_(magazine) "People (magazine)")* magazine in August shortly before her death, Parton said she had been having "hard times" with her health and had overlooked some issues while caring for her husband.

## Death

Parton was hospitalized at [Vanderbilt-Ingram Cancer Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt-Ingram_Cancer_Center "Vanderbilt-Ingram Cancer Center") in [Nashville, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville,_Tennessee "Nashville, Tennessee"), on August 21, 2026, and died after a "brief battle" with cancer on August 25, at age 80. Her death was announced the same day by her nephew, Bryan Seaver, who had served as her head of security for more than two decades. Parton had asked him several years prior to make the announcement of her death, which he described as "an honor that is mixed with absolute pride and great sadness".

A small, private funeral was held for Parton's immediate family, at her request, on August 28. She was entombed next to her husband in the mausoleum at [Woodlawn Memorial Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodlawn_Memorial_Park_(Nashville,_Tennessee) "Woodlawn Memorial Park (Nashville, Tennessee)") in Nashville.

### Tributes

The [Academy of Country Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Country_Music "Academy of Country Music") (ACM) said that Parton had "a legacy that reaches far beyond the stage". The [Grand Ole Opry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Ole_Opry "Grand Ole Opry") called her "a true country music icon". [SiriusXM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SiriusXM "SiriusXM") dedicated a tribute channel to her on CH 105. US president [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") issued a statement describing her as "one of the greatest country singers" and ordered flags at [half-staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-staff "Half-staff") for a week. Multiple state governors issued similar orders. Former presidents [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden"), [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama"), [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush") and [Bill Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Clinton "Bill Clinton"), as well as numerous politicians, also released tributes to Parton. Figures from the entertainment, film, and music industries paid tribute. Flowers were left at her [Hollywood Walk of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame "Hollywood Walk of Fame") star, her [Music City Walk of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_City_Walk_of_Fame "Music City Walk of Fame") plaque in Nashville, her statue in downtown [Sevierville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevierville "Sevierville"), and at her home in [Brentwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brentwood,_Tennessee "Brentwood, Tennessee"). Books of condolence were opened at the [Country Music Hall of Fame and Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Music_Hall_of_Fame_and_Museum "Country Music Hall of Fame and Museum").

On the evening following the announcement of Parton's death, both the [Empire State Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_State_Building "Empire State Building") in New York City and the [Willis Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willis_Tower "Willis Tower") in Chicago were illuminated in pink colors starting at 9:25 pm ([EDT UTC–4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_time_zone "Eastern time zone")), an allusion to "9 to 5". Before the staging of *[9 to 5: The Musical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_to_5_(musical) "9 to 5 (musical)")* in London on August 25, lead actress Karla Tracey honored Parton in a speech and dedicated that night's performance to her. Other cast members posted tributes on social media. The following day, the [Band of the Coldstream Guards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_of_the_Coldstream_Guards "Band of the Coldstream Guards") played "9 to 5" during the [Changing the Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changing_the_Guard "Changing the Guard") ceremony at [Buckingham Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckingham_Palace "Buckingham Palace"). A petition to posthumously rename [Nashville International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_International_Airport "Nashville International Airport") in her honor surpassed 160,000 signatures after her death. On September 10, it was officially announced that the airport would be renamed.

Following and the day after the announcement of Parton's death, radio stations across the United States paused normal programming to honor her. In [Knoxville, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knoxville,_Tennessee "Knoxville, Tennessee"), [country music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music") station [107.7 WIVK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WIVK-FM "WIVK-FM") rebranded the day after her death as "Dolly 107.7" and dedicated its entire morning show to playing Parton's music. At 8:00 am CDT (9:00 eastern) nearly every major radio station in Tennessee observed a moment of silence and then played "I Will Always Love You". Listeners were encouraged to turn on their headlights and sing along. The Tennessee Association of Broadcasters said the effort was "meant to bring broadcasters and communities together to honor Parton's life, legacy and impact on the state". Across the United States, radio stations of all formats, ranging from [classic hits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_hits "Classic hits") to [contemporary hit radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_hit_radio "Contemporary hit radio") that had largely avoided playing Parton in her later decades, began playing selections from her discography, with "Jolene" and "9 to 5" gaining a particular bump in requests and [spins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airplay "Airplay") and "Jolene" reaching No. 16 on the *Billboard* Hot 100 in the wake of Parton's death. Tennessee lawmaker [Jessie Seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessie_Seal "Jessie Seal") proposed making September 25 (9/25) "9 to 5 Day" to commemorate Parton. The [Grand Ole Opry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Ole_Opry "Grand Ole Opry") performance on the night of August 29 was dedicated to Parton. Performers included [Carly Pearce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carly_Pearce "Carly Pearce"), [Kathy Mattea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathy_Mattea "Kathy Mattea"), and [Dan Tyminski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Tyminski "Dan Tyminski"). Parton's death drew a level of attention and remembrance not seen for a musician in the United States since the [death of Michael Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Michael_Jackson "Death of Michael Jackson") in 2009.

On September 14, it was announced that [Bristol Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Motor_Speedway "Bristol Motor Speedway") would pay tribute to Parton throughout the [2026 Bass Pro Shops Night Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Bass_Pro_Shops_Night_Race "2026 Bass Pro Shops Night Race") weekend. Parton's pink butterfly will be featured on the walls and speedway property, and decal stickers will be given to race teams to place on their cars. A tribute banner will also be displayed for fans to leave messages and signatures, and her music will be incorporated into the atmosphere during race weekend. The [Tennessee Volunteers football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_Volunteers_football "Tennessee Volunteers football") announced that they would honor Parton by wearing a sticker on their helmets during the 2026 season, and the team played her song "[My Tennessee Mountain Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Tennessee_Mountain_Home_(song) "My Tennessee Mountain Home (song)")" at the start of the fourth quarter of their first game of the season.

### Posthumous releases and productions

Prior to her death, Parton worked with a handpicked team for several years to develop a long-term roadmap for projects intended to continue her work for decades. The plans included previously unreleased music, books and consumer products, as well as a biographical film, documentary projects, scripted and animated television series, and several hospitality and entertainment ventures.

On August 31, 2026, Parton's estate announced Dollyfest, a celebration of her life and career to be held over consecutive weekends in Nashville and London in 2027. The festival had originally been conceived by Parton as a multi-artist event in Nashville to mark her 80th birthday in January 2026, at which she planned to host and perform, but was postponed after she stepped back from public appearances because of health concerns. Her final recording will be released on the song "Torn", by Irish band [U2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U2 "U2"), closing their album *[Carnaval de Luz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnaval_de_Luz "Carnaval de Luz")* (2026).

## Awards and honors

Parton is one of the most-honored female country performers in history. The Record Industry Association of America certified 25 of her single or album releases as Gold, Platinum, or Multi-Platinum. She had 26 songs reach No. 1 on the *Billboard* country charts, a record for a female artist. She had 42 career Top 10 country albums, a record for any artist, and 110 career-charted singles over the past forty years. As of 2012, she had written more than 3,000 songs and sold more than a hundred million records, making her one of the best-selling female artists ever. As of 2021, she had appeared on the country music charts in each of seven decades, the most of any artist.

Parton earned 11 Grammy Awards (including her 2011 Lifetime Achievement Grammy) and received 55 Grammy nominations, the third-most nominations of any female artist.

At the [American Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Music_Award "American Music Award"), she won three awards out of eighteen nominations. At the Country Music Association, she won ten awards out of 42 nominations. At the [Academy of Country Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Country_Music "Academy of Country Music"), she won thirteen awards and 44 nominations. She was one of the few female artists to win the Country Music Association's highest honor, Entertainer of the Year (1978). She was also nominated for two [Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards") and a [Tony Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award "Tony Award"). She received an [Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Award "Emmy Award") nomination for her appearance in a 1978 [Cher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cher "Cher") television special.

In 1984, she was awarded a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame "Hollywood Walk of Fame") for her music, located at 6712 Hollywood Boulevard in Hollywood, California, and in 1988, she received a star on the Nashville [StarWalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StarWalk "StarWalk") for Grammy winners. A [bronze statue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Dolly_Parton "Statue of Dolly Parton") of her was unveiled in 1987 in her hometown, the city of Sevierville, Tennessee, being designed by Jim Gray. She called the statue "the greatest honor", because it came from the people who knew her. Parton was inducted into the Grand Ole Opry in 1969 and in 1986 was named one of *[Ms. Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ms._Magazine "Ms. Magazine")*'s Women of the Year. In 1986, she was inducted into the [Nashville Songwriters Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_Songwriters_Hall_of_Fame "Nashville Songwriters Hall of Fame").

In 1999, Parton received country music's highest honor, an induction into the [Country Music Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Music_Hall_of_Fame "Country Music Hall of Fame"). She received an honorary [doctorate degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate_degree "Doctorate degree") from [Carson-Newman College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carson-Newman_College "Carson-Newman College") in 1990. She was inducted into the [National Academy of Popular Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Popular_Music "National Academy of Popular Music")/[Songwriters Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songwriters_Hall_of_Fame "Songwriters Hall of Fame") in 2001. In 2002, she ranked fourth among [CMT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Music_Television "Country Music Television")'s forty Greatest Women of Country Music.

Parton was honored in 2003 with a tribute album called *[Just Because I'm a Woman: Songs of Dolly Parton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_Because_I%27m_a_Woman:_Songs_of_Dolly_Parton "Just Because I'm a Woman: Songs of Dolly Parton")*. The artists who recorded versions of Parton's songs included [Melissa Etheridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melissa_Etheridge "Melissa Etheridge") ("I Will Always Love You"), [Alison Krauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alison_Krauss "Alison Krauss") ("9 to 5"), [Shania Twain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shania_Twain "Shania Twain") ("Coat of Many Colors"), [Meshell Ndegeocello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meshell_Ndegeocello "Meshell Ndegeocello") ("Two Doors Down"), [Norah Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norah_Jones "Norah Jones") ("The Grass is Blue") and [Sinéad O'Connor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sin%C3%A9ad_O%27Connor "Sinéad O'Connor") ("Dagger Through the Heart"). Parton herself contributed a re-recording of the title song, originally the title song for her first RCA album in 1968. Parton was awarded the [Living Legend Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_Legend_Medal "Living Legend Medal") by the US [Library of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress "Library of Congress") on April 14, 2004, for her contributions to the cultural heritage of the United States. She was also the focus of a Library of Congress collection exploring the influences of country music on her life and career. The collection contains images, articles, sheet music and more.

In 2005, she was honored with the [National Medal of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Arts "National Medal of Arts"), the highest honor given by the US government for excellence in the arts. The award is presented by the US president. On December 3, 2006, Parton received the [Kennedy Center Honors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Center_Honors "Kennedy Center Honors") from the [John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy_Center_for_the_Performing_Arts "John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts") for her lifetime of contributions to the arts. During the show, some of country music's biggest names came to show their admiration. [Carrie Underwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrie_Underwood "Carrie Underwood") performed "Islands in the Stream" with Rogers, Parton's original duet partner. Krauss performed "Jolene" and duetted "Coat of Many Colors" with Twain. McEntire and [Reese Witherspoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reese_Witherspoon "Reese Witherspoon") also came to pay tribute. On November 16, 2010, Parton accepted the [Liseberg Applause Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liseberg_Applause_Award "Liseberg Applause Award"), the theme park industry's most prestigious honor, on behalf of Dollywood theme park during a ceremony held at IAAPA Attractions Expo 2010 in [Orlando, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando,_Florida "Orlando, Florida").

In 2015, a newly discovered species of [lichen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichen "Lichen") found growing in the southern Appalachians was named *[Japewiella dollypartoniana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japewiella_dollypartoniana "Japewiella dollypartoniana")* in honor of Parton's music and her efforts to bring national and global attention to that region. In 2018, Parton received a second star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame, inducted alongside [Linda Ronstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Ronstadt "Linda Ronstadt") and [Emmylou Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmylou_Harris "Emmylou Harris") in recognition of their work as a trio. Parton was also recognized in the [Guinness World Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Records "Guinness World Records") 2018 Edition for holding records for the Most Decades with a Top 20 hit on *Billboard*'s Hot Country Songs Chart and Most Hits on *Billboard*'s Hot Country Songs Chart by a Female Artist. In 2020, Parton received a Grammy award for her collaboration with [For King & Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_King_%26_Country_(band) "For King & Country (band)") on their song, "God Only Knows". In 2021, she was included on the [*Time* 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100 "Time 100"), *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")*'s annual list of the hundred most influential people in the world. *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* called her among the three of America's Most Beloved Divas (alongside [Patti LaBelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patti_LaBelle "Patti LaBelle") and [Barbra Streisand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbra_Streisand "Barbra Streisand")).

During Donald Trump's first term, Parton turned down the [Presidential Medal of Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Medal_of_Freedom "Presidential Medal of Freedom") twice due to her husband's illness and the ongoing pandemic. Parton turned down the Presidential Medal of Freedom a third time during the Biden presidency to avoid the appearance of politics. In response to a 2021 proposal by the Tennessee legislature to erect a statue of Parton, she asked the legislature to remove the bill from consideration: "Given all that's going on in the world, I don't think putting me on a pedestal is appropriate at this time."

In late 2022, Parton received a $100-million [Courage and Civility Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courage_and_Civility_Award "Courage and Civility Award") from the founder of Amazon, [Jeff Bezos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Bezos "Jeff Bezos"). According to Bezos, the award was given to Parton because of her charity work focused on improving children's literacy around the world. Asteroid (10731) Dollyparton, the [former](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_designation_in_astronomy "Provisional designation in astronomy") 1998 BL3, was named in her honor in 2022. In 2023, Parton was awarded [American Library Association Honorary Membership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Library_Association_Honorary_Membership "American Library Association Honorary Membership"). She was a [Kentucky Colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_Colonel "Kentucky Colonel"), awarded to her by the [governor of Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Kentucky "Governor of Kentucky") in 2024.

### Dolly Parton Day

Parton was honored with proclamations of "Dolly Parton Day" numerous times. In 1970, her hometown [Sevierville, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevierville,_Tennessee "Sevierville, Tennessee"), declared one day to be Dolly Parton Day. In 2011, the [South Carolina General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_General_Assembly "South Carolina General Assembly") declared June 3, 2011, as Dolly Parton Day, in connection with the grand opening of [Pirates Voyage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_Voyage "Pirates Voyage") in Myrtle Beach. In January 2026, Tennessee declared her 80th birthday to be Dolly Parton Day in the state.

In September 2026, following Parton's death the previous month, California governor [Gavin Newsom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavin_Newsom "Gavin Newsom") signed Senate Bill 931, establishing September 25 as Dolly Parton Day. The date refers to her song "[9 to 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_to_5_(Dolly_Parton_song) "9 to 5 (Dolly Parton song)")", and the law requires the governor to proclaim the day annually. Sevier County, Tennessee, and the municipalities of [Sevierville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevierville,_Tennessee "Sevierville, Tennessee"), [Pigeon Forge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigeon_Forge,_Tennessee "Pigeon Forge, Tennessee"), [Gatlinburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatlinburg,_Tennessee "Gatlinburg, Tennessee") and [Pittman Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittman_Center,_Tennessee "Pittman Center, Tennessee") announced a joint proclamation designating September 25 as an annual Dolly Parton Day.
