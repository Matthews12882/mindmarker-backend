# Jeff Bezos

**Jeffrey Preston Bezos** ([/ˈbeɪzoʊs/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*BAY-zohss*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"); [né](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") **Jorgensen**; born January 12, 1964) is an American businessman, and the founder, executive chairman, and former president and [CEO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_executive_officer "Chief executive officer") of [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_(company) "Amazon (company)"), the world's largest [e-commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-commerce "E-commerce") and [cloud computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_computing "Cloud computing") company. According to the *[Bloomberg Billionaires Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_Billionaires_Index "Bloomberg Billionaires Index")* and *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes "Forbes")*, he was the [world's wealthiest person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World%27s_Billionaires "The World's Billionaires") from 2017 to 2021, and in 2026, his net worth was approximately US$284 billion. He is commonly considered part of the [global elite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elite#Global_elite "Elite").

Bezos was born in [Albuquerque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albuquerque,_New_Mexico "Albuquerque, New Mexico"), and raised in [Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston "Houston") and [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami "Miami"). He graduated from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") in 1986 with a degree in engineering. He worked on [Wall Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street "Wall Street") in a variety of related fields from 1986 to early 1994. He founded Amazon in mid-1994 on a road trip from New York City to [Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle "Seattle"). The company began as an online bookstore and expanded to a variety of other e-commerce products and services, including [video and audio streaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_media "Streaming media"), [cloud computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_computing "Cloud computing"), and [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence"). It is the world's largest online sales company, the [largest Internet company by revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_Internet_companies "List of largest Internet companies"), and the largest provider of [virtual assistants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_assistant "Virtual assistant") and cloud infrastructure services through its [Amazon Web Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Web_Services "Amazon Web Services") branch.

Bezos founded the [aerospace manufacturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_manufacturer "Aerospace manufacturer") and [sub-orbital spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-orbital_spaceflight "Sub-orbital spaceflight") services company [Blue Origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Origin "Blue Origin") in 2000, and he flew into space on [Blue Origin NS-16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Origin_NS-16 "Blue Origin NS-16") in 2021. He purchased the major American newspaper *[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")* in 2013 and manages many other investments through his [venture capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venture_capital "Venture capital") firm, [Bezos Expeditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bezos_Expeditions "Bezos Expeditions").

The first [centibillionaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centibillionaire "Centibillionaire") on the [*Forbes* Real Time Billionaires Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World%27s_Billionaires "The World's Billionaires"), Bezos was named the "richest man in modern history" after his net worth increased to $150 billion in July 2018 (equivalent to $1.9×1011 in 2025). On July 5, 2021, Bezos took over the role of executive chairman and stepped down as the CEO and president of Amazon, succeeded by Amazon Web Services CEO [Andy Jassy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Jassy "Andy Jassy").

## Early life and education

Bezos was born Jeffrey Preston Jorgensen on January 12, 1964, in [Albuquerque, New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albuquerque,_New_Mexico "Albuquerque, New Mexico"), to [Jacklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_Bezos "Jackie Bezos") (*née* Gise; 1946–2025) and [Ted Jorgensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Jorgensen "Ted Jorgensen") (1944–2015). At the time of his birth, his mother was a 17-year-old high-school student and his father was 19. Ted was a [Danish American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Americans "Danish Americans") unicyclist born in Chicago to a family of Baptists. After completing high school despite challenging conditions, Jacklyn attended night school, bringing her baby with her. Jeff attended a [Montessori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montessori_education "Montessori education") school in Albuquerque when he was two.

Ted struggled with alcohol and with his finances. Jacklyn left her husband to live with her parents, filing for divorce in June 1965 when Jeff was 17 months old. After his parents divorced, his mother married [Cuban immigrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_Americans "Cuban Americans") [Miguel "Mike" Bezos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Bezos "Miguel Bezos") in April 1968. Shortly after the wedding, Mike adopted 4-year-old Jeff, whose surname was then legally changed from Jorgensen to Bezos. Jacklyn, her husband, and her son left the area and asked Ted to discontinue contact, to which he agreed.

After Mike received his degree from the [University of New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Mexico "University of New Mexico"), the family moved to [Houston, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston "Houston"), so that he could begin working as an engineer for [Exxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exxon "Exxon"). Jeff attended [River Oaks Elementary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Oaks_Elementary_School_(Houston) "River Oaks Elementary School (Houston)") in Houston from fourth to sixth grade. Jeff's maternal grandfather was Lawrence Preston Gise, a regional director of the [U.S. Atomic Energy Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Atomic_Energy_Commission "United States Atomic Energy Commission") (AEC) in Albuquerque.

Gise retired early to his family's ranch near [Cotulla, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotulla,_Texas "Cotulla, Texas"), where his grandson would spend many summers in his youth and which he would later purchase and expand from 25,000 acres (10,117 ha) to 160,000 acres (64,750 ha). Jeff displayed scientific interests and technological proficiency and once rigged an electric alarm to keep his younger half-siblings out of his room. The family moved to [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami "Miami"), Florida, where Jeff attended [Miami Palmetto High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Palmetto_High_School "Miami Palmetto High School"). In high school, he worked at [McDonald's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonald%27s "McDonald's") as a short-order [line cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chef_de_partie "Chef de partie") during the breakfast shift.

Bezos attended the Student Science Training Program at the [University of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Florida "University of Florida"). He was high school [valedictorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valedictorian "Valedictorian"), a [National Merit Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Merit_Scholarship_Program "National Merit Scholarship Program"), and a [Silver Knight Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Knight_Award "Silver Knight Award") winner in 1982. In his graduation speech, Bezos spoke about the possibility of human [space colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_colonization "Space colonization"). A local newspaper quoted him as saying that he hoped humanity would eventually move heavy industry and large populations into space while preserving Earth as "a huge national park".

After graduating from high school in 1982, Bezos attended [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University"). He initially majored in [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") but later switched to [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering "Electrical engineering") and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science "Computer science"). In 2018, during a talk at [the Economic Club of Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economic_Club_of_Washington,_D.C. "The Economic Club of Washington, D.C."), Bezos revealed that, some thirty years ago, his Princeton classmate [Yasantha Rajakarunanayake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasantha_Rajakarunanayake "Yasantha Rajakarunanayake") had defeated him in solving a mathematical problem, causing him to give up on his dreams of becoming a theoretical physicist.

Bezos was a member of the [Quadrangle Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrangle_Club "Quadrangle Club"), one of [Princeton's 11 eating clubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University_eating_clubs "Princeton University eating clubs"). Additionally, he was the president of the Princeton chapter of the [Students for the Exploration and Development of Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Students_for_the_Exploration_and_Development_of_Space "Students for the Exploration and Development of Space") (SEDS). He had a 4.2 [GPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grading_in_education#United_States "Grading in education") and was elected to [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa "Phi Beta Kappa") and [Tau Beta Pi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tau_Beta_Pi "Tau Beta Pi"). Bezos graduated from Princeton in 1986 with a [Bachelor of Science in Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science_in_Engineering "Bachelor of Science in Engineering") (BSE), *[summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_honors "Latin honors")*.

## Business career

### Early career

After Bezos graduated from college in 1986, he was offered positions at [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel "Intel"), [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs "Bell Labs"), and [Andersen Consulting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andersen_Consulting "Andersen Consulting"), among others. He first worked at Fitel, a [financial technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fintech "Fintech") telecommunications [start-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Startup_company "Startup company"), where he was tasked with building a network for [international trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_trade "International trade"). Bezos was promoted to head of development and director of customer service. He transitioned into the banking industry when he became a [product manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_manager "Product manager") at [Bankers Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bankers_Trust "Bankers Trust") from 1988 to 1990. From 1990 to 1994, he worked at [D. E. Shaw & Co](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._E._Shaw_%26_Co. "D. E. Shaw & Co."), a newly created [hedge fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_fund "Hedge fund") with a strong emphasis on mathematical modeling. Bezos became D. E. Shaw's fourth senior vice president by age 30.

### Amazon

In spring 1994, Bezos read that web usage was growing at a rate of 2,300% a year and eventually decided to establish an [online bookstore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_shopping "Online shopping"). He and his then-wife, [MacKenzie Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacKenzie_Scott "MacKenzie Scott"), left their jobs at D. E. Shaw and founded Amazon in a rented garage in [Bellevue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellevue,_Washington "Bellevue, Washington"), Washington on July 5, 1994, after writing its [business plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_plan "Business plan") on a cross-country drive from New York City to [Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle "Seattle"). Bezos led the company's early development while MacKenzie Scott handled bookkeeping, financial administration, and some of Amazon's early freight negotiations during its startup phase. Before choosing Seattle as Amazon's headquarters, Bezos reportedly explored locating the company on a [Native American reservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_reservation "Indian reservation") near [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco"), partly because of potential tax advantages.

Bezos initially named his new company Cadabra but later changed the name to Amazon after the [Amazon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_River "Amazon River") in South America, in part because the name begins with the letter *A*, which is at the beginning of the alphabet. At the time, website listings were alphabetized, so a name starting with "A" would appear sooner when customers conducted online searches. In addition, he regarded "Amazon," the name of the world's largest river, as fitting for what he hoped would become the world's largest online bookstore. He accepted an estimated $300,000 (equivalent to $700,000 in 2025) from his parents as an investment in Amazon. He warned many early investors that there was a 70% chance that Amazon would fail or go bankrupt. Although Amazon was originally an online bookstore, Bezos had always planned to expand to other products. Three years after Bezos founded Amazon, he took it public with an [initial public offering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_public_offering "Initial public offering") (IPO). In response to critical reports from [Fortune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_(magazine) "Fortune (magazine)") and [Barron's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barron%27s_(newspaper) "Barron's (newspaper)"), Bezos maintained that the growth of the internet would overtake competition from larger book retailers such as [Borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borders_(retailer) "Borders (retailer)") and [Barnes & Noble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnes_%26_Noble "Barnes & Noble").

In 1998, Bezos diversified into the online sale of music and video, and by the end of the year he had expanded the company's product offerings to include a variety of other [consumer goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_good "Final good"). Bezos used funds raised during the company's 1997 [equity offering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equity_(finance) "Equity (finance)") (equivalent to $110,000,000 in 2025) to finance the acquisition of smaller internet companies during the dot-com expansion period. Among these acquisitions were a purchase of a majority stake in [Pets.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pets.com "Pets.com") in 1999 and a purchase of a portion of [Kozmo.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozmo.com "Kozmo.com") for $60 million (equivalent to $120,000,000 in 2025), both of which failed after the [dot-com bubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dot-com_bubble "Dot-com bubble") collapse in 2000. By the end of 2000, Bezos borrowed $2 billion from banks (equivalent to $3.7×109 in 2025), as Amazon's cash balances dipped to only $350 million (equivalent to $650,000,000 in 2025). However, the company continued to expand despite its losses, and in 2002, Bezos led Amazon to launch [Amazon Web Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Web_Services "Amazon Web Services"), which compiled data from weather channels and [website traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_traffic "Web traffic"). Revenues stagnated later that year, and after the company nearly went bankrupt, Bezos closed distribution centers and laid off 14% of the Amazon workforce. In 2003, Amazon rebounded from financial instability and turned a profit of $35 million (equivalent to $61,000,000 in 2025).

In November 2007, Bezos launched the [Amazon Kindle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Kindle "Amazon Kindle"). According to a 2008 *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")* profile, Bezos wished to create a device that allowed a "[flow state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_(psychology) "Flow (psychology)")" in reading similar to the experience of video games. In 2013, Bezos secured a $600-million contract (equivalent to $830,000,000 in 2025) with the [Central Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Intelligence_Agency "Central Intelligence Agency") (CIA) on behalf of Amazon Web Services. In October of that year, Amazon was recognized as the largest online shopping retailer in the world.

In May 2016, Bezos sold slightly more than one million shares of his holdings in the company for $671 million (equivalent to $900,000,000 in 2025), the largest sum he had ever raised from selling some of his Amazon stock. On August 4, 2016, Bezos sold another million of his shares for $756 million (equivalent to $1.0×109 in 2025). A year later, Bezos took on 130,000 new employees when he ramped up hiring at company distribution centers. By January 19, 2018, his Amazon stock holdings had appreciated to slightly over $109 billion (equivalent to $1.40×1011 in 2025); months later he began to sell stock to raise cash for other enterprises, in particular, [Blue Origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Origin "Blue Origin"). On January 29, 2018, he was featured in Amazon's [Super Bowl commercial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_commercials "Super Bowl commercials"). On February 1, 2018, Amazon reported its highest ever profit with quarterly [earnings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earnings "Earnings") of $2 billion (equivalent to $2.6×109 in 2025). Due to the growth of [Alibaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alibaba_Group "Alibaba Group") in China, Bezos has often expressed interest in expanding Amazon into India. On July 27, 2017, Bezos momentarily became the [world's wealthiest person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World%27s_Billionaires "The World's Billionaires") over [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft "Microsoft") co-founder [Bill Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Gates "Bill Gates") when his estimated [net worth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_worth "Net worth") increased to just over $90 billion (equivalent to $1.18×1011 in 2025). His wealth surpassed $100 billion for the first time on November 24, 2017 (equivalent to $1.31×1011 in 2025), and he was formally designated the wealthiest person in the world by *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes "Forbes")* on March 6, 2018, with a net worth of $112 billion (equivalent to $1.44×1011 in 2025).

In March 2018, Bezos dispatched Amit Agarwal, Amazon's global senior vice president, to India with $5.5 billion (equivalent to $7.1×109 in 2025) to localize operations throughout the company's [supply chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_chain "Supply chain") routes. Later in the month, U.S. President Donald Trump accused Amazon and Bezos, specifically, of [sales tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sales_tax "Sales tax") avoidance, misusing [postal routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mail "Mail"), and [anti-competitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-competitive_practices "Anti-competitive practices") business practices. Amazon's share price fell by 9% in response to the President's negative comments; this reduced Bezos's personal wealth by $10.7 billion (equivalent to $1.37×1010 in 2025). Weeks later, Bezos recouped his losses when academic reports out of [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University") indicated that Trump could do little to regulate Amazon in any meaningful way. During July 2018, a number of members of the U.S. Congress called on Bezos to detail the applications of Amazon's [face recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facial_recognition_system "Facial recognition system") software, [Rekognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Rekognition "Amazon Rekognition").

Criticism of Amazon's business practices continued in September 2018 when Senator [Bernie Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Sanders "Bernie Sanders") introduced the Stop Bad Employers by Zeroing Out Subsidies (Stop BEZOS) Act and accused Amazon of receiving [corporate welfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_welfare "Corporate welfare"). This followed revelations by the non-profit group New Food Economy which found that one third of Amazon workers in [Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona "Arizona"), and one tenth of Amazon workers in [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania "Pennsylvania") and [Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio "Ohio"), relied on food stamps. While preparing to introduce the bill, Sanders opined: "Instead of attempting to explore Mars or go to the moon, how about Jeff Bezos pays his workers a living wage?" He later said: "Bezos could play a profound role. If he said today, nobody who is employed at Amazon will receive less than a living wage, it would send a message to every corporation in America." Sanders's efforts elicited a response from Amazon which pointed to the 130,000 jobs it created in 2017 and called the $28,446 figure (equivalent to $37,000 in 2025) for its median salary "misleading" as it included part-time workers. However, Sanders countered that the companies targeted by his proposal have placed an increased focus on part-time workers to escape benefit obligations. On October 2, 2018, Bezos announced a company-wide wage increase, which Sanders applauded. The American workers who were being paid the minimum wage had this increased to $15 per hour (equivalent to $19 in 2025), a decision that was interpreted as support for the [Fight for $15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fight_for_$15 "Fight for $15") movement.

In February 2021, Bezos announced that in the [third quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_quarter_of_a_calendar_year "Third quarter of a calendar year") of 2021 he would step down from his role as CEO of Amazon to become the Executive Chairman of the Amazon Board. He was succeeded as CEO by [Andy Jassy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Jassy "Andy Jassy"). On February 2, 2021, Bezos sent an email to all Amazon employees, telling them the transition would give him "the time and energy [he] need[s] to focus on the Day 1 Fund, the [Bezos Earth Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bezos_Earth_Fund "Bezos Earth Fund"), [Blue Origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Origin "Blue Origin"), [The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post"), and [his] other passions." In February 2024, Bezos sold 24 million shares in Amazon at a total value of $4 billion. Bezos announced that he intended to sell 50 million shares in Amazon over the next year. During an interview at the [DealBook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DealBook "DealBook") Summit in December 2024, Bezos said that he was dedicating 95% of his time to artificial intelligence initiatives at Amazon.

### Blue Origin

In September 2000, Bezos founded Blue Origin, a [human spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_spaceflight "Human spaceflight") [startup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Startup "Startup"). Bezos has long expressed an interest in space travel and the development of human life in the Solar System. His 1982 high school [valedictorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valedictorian "Valedictorian") senior graduation speech was followed up with a *[Miami Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Herald "Miami Herald")* interview in which he expressed an interest to build and develop hotels, amusement parks, and colonies for human beings who were in orbit. The 18-year-old Bezos stated that he wanted to preserve Earth from overuse through [resource depletion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resource_depletion "Resource depletion"). [Rob Meyerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Meyerson "Rob Meyerson") led Blue Origin from 2003 to 2017 and served as its first president.

After its founding, Blue Origin maintained a low profile until 2006 when it purchased a large tract of land in [West Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Texas "West Texas") for a launch and test facility. After the company gained the public's attention during the late 2000s, Bezos additionally indicated his interest in reducing the [cost of space travel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_spaceflight "Human spaceflight") for humans while also increasing the safety of extraterrestrial travel. In September 2011, one of the company's uncrewed prototype vehicles crashed during a short-hop test flight. Although the crash was viewed as a setback, news outlets noted how far the company went from its founding-to-date in advancing [spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceflight "Spaceflight"). After the crash, Bezos has been superstitiously wearing his "lucky" Texas [Cowboy boots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowboy_boots "Cowboy boots") to all rocket launches. In May 2013, Bezos met with [Richard Branson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Branson "Richard Branson"), chairman of [Virgin Galactic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Galactic "Virgin Galactic"), to discuss commercial spaceflight opportunities and strategies. He has been compared to Branson and [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk "Elon Musk") as all three are billionaires who prioritize spaceflight among their business interests.

In 2015, Bezos announced that a new [orbital launch vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_launch_vehicle "Orbital launch vehicle") was under development and would make its first flight in the late-2010s. Later in November, Blue Origin's [New Shepard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Shepard "New Shepard") space vehicle successfully rocketed into space and reached its planned test altitude of 329,839 feet (100.5 kilometers) before executing a [vertical landing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VTVL "VTVL") back at the launch site in West Texas. In 2016, Bezos allowed select journalists to visit, tour, and photograph his facility. He has repeatedly called for increased inter-space energy and industrial manufacturing to decrease the negative costs associated with [business-related pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution "Pollution").

In December 2017, New Shepard successfully flew and landed [dummy passengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crash_test_dummy "Crash test dummy"), amending and pushing its human space travel start date into late 2018. To execute this program, Blue Origin built six of the vehicles to support all phases of testing and operations: no-passenger test flights, flights with test passengers, and commercial-passenger weekly operations. Since 2016, Bezos has spoken more freely about his hopes to [colonize the solar system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_colonization "Space colonization"), and has been selling $1 billion in Amazon stock each year to capitalize Blue Origin in an effort to support this endeavor. In May 2018, Bezos maintained that the primary goal of Blue Origin is to preserve the [natural resources of Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_resource "Natural resource") by making the human species [multi-planetary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplanetary_species "Multiplanetary species"). He announced that *New Shepard* would begin transporting humans into [sub-orbital space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-orbital_spaceflight "Sub-orbital spaceflight") by November 2018. In July 2018, it was announced that Bezos had priced commercial spaceflight tickets from $200,000 to $300,000 per person.

#### Spaceflight

On July 20, 2021, he launched on the [NS-16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NS-16 "NS-16") mission with his half-brother Mark Bezos, [Wally Funk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wally_Funk "Wally Funk"), and [Oliver Daemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Daemen "Oliver Daemen"). He launched nine days after [Richard Branson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Branson "Richard Branson") launched on board the [Virgin Galactic Unity 22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Galactic_Unity_22 "Virgin Galactic Unity 22") mission. Bezos's [suborbital flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-orbital_spaceflight "Sub-orbital spaceflight") lasted over 10 minutes, reaching a peak altitude of 66.5 miles (107.0 km).

### *The Washington Post*

On August 5, 2013, Bezos announced his purchase of *The Washington Post* for $250 million in cash (equivalent to $350,000,000 in 2025), at the suggestion of his friend, [Don Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_E._Graham "Donald E. Graham"). To execute the purchase, he established [limited liability company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_liability_company "Limited liability company") **Nash Holdings** to serve as a [holding company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holding_company "Holding company") through which he would own the newspaper. The sale closed on October 1, 2013, and Nash Holdings took control. In March 2014, Bezos made his first significant change at *The Washington Post* and lifted the online paywall for subscribers of a number of U.S. local newspapers in [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas "Texas"), Hawaii, and [Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota "Minnesota"). In January 2016, Bezos set out to reinvent the newspaper as a media and technology company by rebuilding its digital media, mobile platforms, and analytics software. After a surge in online readership in 2016, the paper was profitable for the first time since Bezos made the purchase in 2013. Bezos' ownership of the *Post* has been subject to scrutiny and [criticism](#Criticism) regarding his treatment of employees as well as his influence on the paper's content, particularly his 2024–25 involvement with the editorial and opinion pages.

Throughout his early years of ownership of *The Washington Post*, Bezos was accused of having a potential [conflict of interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_of_interest "Conflict of interest") with the paper. Bezos and the newspaper's [editorial board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editorial_board "Editorial board") have dismissed accusations that he unfairly controlled the paper's content, and Bezos maintains that the paper is independent. Bezos' treatment of employees at *The Washington Post* has also drawn scrutiny. In 2018, more than 400 *Washington Post* employees wrote an open letter to Bezos criticizing the [wages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_wage "Living wage") and benefits provided to employees. The letter demanded "Fair wages; fair benefits for retirement, family leave and health care; and a fair amount of job security". About 750 employees at *The Washington Post* went on a brief strike in December 2023 in response to Bezos' plans to [lay off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Layoff "Layoff") staff.

In 2024, Bezos [blocked the *Washington Post*'s editorial board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_media_endorsements_in_the_2024_United_States_presidential_election#Suppression_of_Harris_endorsements "News media endorsements in the 2024 United States presidential election") from endorsing [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris "Kamala Harris") in the [presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election "2024 United States presidential election"). The move was criticized by former editor [Marty Baron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marty_Baron "Marty Baron"), who considered it to be an act of "disturbing spinelessness at an institution famed for courage" and said that it would invite intimidation of Bezos by Donald Trump. Editor-at-large [Robert Kagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Kagan "Robert Kagan") and columnist [Michele Norris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michele_Norris "Michele Norris") also resigned in the wake of the decision, and editor [David Maraniss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Maraniss "David Maraniss") said that the paper was "[dying in darkness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_Dies_in_Darkness "Democracy Dies in Darkness")". *Post* opinion columnists jointly authored an article calling the decision to not endorse a "terrible mistake", and it was condemned by the Washington Post Guild, a union unit representing *Post* employees. More than 250,000 people (about ten percent of the *Post*'s subscribers) cancelled their subscriptions, and three members of the editorial board left it.

In February 2025, Bezos announced that the opinion section of the *Post* will give voice to opinions that support "personal liberties" and "free markets". [David Shipley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Shipley "David Shipley"), *The Post*'s opinion editor, resigned after trying to persuade Bezos to reconsider the new direction. Within two days of the announcement, it was reported that over 75,000 digital subscribers had canceled their subscriptions.

Due to his considerable influence on industry, politics, and media, Bezos has been described as an [oligarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oligarchy "Oligarchy").

### Bezos Expeditions

Bezos makes personal investments through his [venture capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venture_capital "Venture capital") vehicle, Bezos Expeditions. He was one of the first [shareholders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investor "Investor") in Google, when he invested $250,000 in 1998 (equivalent to $490,000 in 2025). That $250,000 investment resulted in 3.3 million shares of Google stock, worth about $3.1 billion in 2017 (equivalent to $4.1×109 in 2025). He also invested in [Unity Biotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_Biotechnology "Unity Biotechnology"), a life-extension research firm hoping to slow or stop the process of aging. Bezos is involved in the healthcare sector, which includes investments in Unity Biotechnology, [GRAIL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GRAIL_(company) "GRAIL (company)"), [Juno Therapeutics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juno_Therapeutics "Juno Therapeutics"), and [Zocdoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zocdoc "Zocdoc"). In January 2018, an announcement was made concerning Bezos's role within a new, unnamed healthcare company. This venture, later named [Haven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haven_(healthcare) "Haven (healthcare)"), is expected to be a partnership between Amazon, [JPMorgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JPMorgan_Chase "JPMorgan Chase"), and [Berkshire Hathaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkshire_Hathaway "Berkshire Hathaway").[*[needs update](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")*]

Bezos also supports philanthropic efforts through direct donations and non-profit projects funded by Bezos Expeditions. Bezos used Bezos Expeditions to fund several philanthropic projects, including an Innovation center at the Seattle Museum of History and Industry and the Bezos Center for Neural Circuit Dynamics at [Princeton Neuroscience Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_Neuroscience_Institute "Princeton Neuroscience Institute"). In 2013, Bezos Expeditions funded the recovery of two [Saturn V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn_V "Saturn V") first-stage [Rocketdyne F-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocketdyne_F-1 "Rocketdyne F-1") engines from the floor of the Atlantic Ocean. They were positively identified as belonging to the [Apollo 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_11 "Apollo 11") mission's [S-1C stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-1C "S-1C") from July 1969. The engines are currently on display at the [Seattle Museum of Flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Flight "Museum of Flight").

In September 2021, Bezos invested in [biotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotechnology "Biotechnology") research company [Altos Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altos_Labs "Altos Labs").

### Project Prometheus

In November 2025, Bezos announced that he co-founded [Project Prometheus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Prometheus_(company) "Project Prometheus (company)") with former Google executive [Vik Bajaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vik_Bajaj "Vik Bajaj"). The company will focus on the use of artificial intelligence in engineering and the manufacturing of computers, spacecraft, and automobiles.

## Public image

Journalist [Nellie Bowles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nellie_Bowles "Nellie Bowles") of *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* has described the [public persona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persona "Persona") and personality of Bezos as that of "a brilliant but mysterious and coldblooded corporate titan". During the 1990s, Bezos earned a reputation for relentlessly pushing Amazon forward, often at the expense of [public charity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charitable_organization "Charitable organization") and social welfare. Journalist Mark O'Connell criticized Bezos's relentless customer focus as "very small" in terms of impact on humanity as a whole, a sentiment technologist [Tim O'Reilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_O%27Reilly "Tim O'Reilly") agreed with. His business practices projected a public image of [prudence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prudence "Prudence") and parsimony with his own wealth and that of Amazon. Throughout the early 2000s, he was perceived to be geeky or nerdy.

Bezos was seen by some as needlessly quantitative and data-driven. This perception was detailed by Alan Deutschman, who described him as "talking in lists" and "[enumerating] the criteria, in order of importance, for every decision he has made". Select accounts of his persona have drawn controversy and public attention. Notably, journalist [Brad Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Stone_(journalist) "Brad Stone (journalist)") wrote [a book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Everything_Store "The Everything Store") that described Bezos as a demanding boss as well as [hyper-competitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competition_(companies) "Competition (companies)"), and opined that Bezos perhaps "bet the biggest on the Internet" out of anyone. Bezos has been characterized as a notoriously opportunistic CEO who operates with little concern for obstacles and [externalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Externality "Externality").

During the early 2010s, Bezos solidified his reputation for aggressive business practices, and his public image began to shift. Bezos started to wear [tailored clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tailor "Tailor"); he [weight trained](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weight_training "Weight training"), pursued a regimented diet and began to freely spend his money. His physical transformation has been compared to the transformation of Amazon; he is often referred to as the [metonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metonym "Metonym") of the company. Since 2017, he has been portrayed by [Kyle Mooney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Mooney "Kyle Mooney") and [Steve Carell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Carell "Steve Carell") on *[Saturday Night Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live "Saturday Night Live")*, usually as an undercutting, domineering figure. His physical appearance increased the public's perception of him as a symbolically dominant figure in business and in popular culture, wherein he has been [parodied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parody "Parody") as an enterprising [supervillain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supervillain "Supervillain").

In May 2014, the [International Trade Union Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Trade_Union_Confederation "International Trade Union Confederation") named Bezos the "World's Worst Boss", with its general secretary [Sharan Burrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharan_Burrow "Sharan Burrow") saying: "Jeff Bezos represents the inhumanity of employers who are promoting the North American corporate model", while in 2019, *[Harvard Business Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Business_Review "Harvard Business Review")*, which ranked Bezos the best-performing CEO for 4 years in a row since 2014, did not rank him even in the top 100, citing Amazon's "relatively low ESG ([environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_environmental_responsibility "Corporate environmental responsibility"), [social](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_social_responsibility "Corporate social responsibility"), and [governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_governance "Corporate governance")) scores" that reflect "risks created by [working conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupational_stress "Occupational stress") and employment policies, [data security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_security "Data security"), and [antitrust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antitrust "Antitrust") issues".

During the late 2010s, Bezos reversed his reputation for being reluctant to spend money on non-business-related expenses. His relative lack of philanthropy compared to other billionaires has drawn a negative response from the public since 2016. Bezos has been known to publicly contest claims made in critical articles, as exemplified in 2015 when he sent a memo to employees denouncing a *New York Times* piece.

### Leadership style

"Day 1" Management Philosophy

> Day 1: [start up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Startup_company "Startup company")
> Day 2: [stasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punctuated_equilibrium "Punctuated equilibrium")
> Day 3: [irrelevance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_market_share "Relative market share")
> Day 4: "excruciating, painful decline"
> Day 5: [death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bankruptcy "Bankruptcy")

— Bezos has stated "it is always Day 1" to describe his [growth mindset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindset "Mindset").

Bezos used what he called a "regret-minimization framework" while working at [D. E. Shaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._E._Shaw_%26_Co. "D. E. Shaw & Co.") and again during the early years of Amazon. He described this [life philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebensphilosophie "Lebensphilosophie") by stating: "When I'm 80, am I going to regret leaving Wall Street? No. Will I regret missing the beginning of the Internet? Yes." During the 1990s and early 2000s at Amazon, he was characterized as trying to quantify all aspects of running the company, often listing employees on spreadsheets and basing executive decisions on data. To push Amazon forward, Bezos developed the [mantra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantra "Mantra") "Get Big Fast", establishing the company's need to [scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economies_of_scale "Economies of scale") its operations to produce market dominance. He favored diverting Amazon profits back into the company in lieu of allocating it amongst shareholders in the form of [dividends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dividend "Dividend").

Bezos uses the term "work–life harmony" instead of the more standard "[work–life balance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Work%E2%80%93life_balance "Work–life balance")" because he believes that *balance* implies that you can only have one and not the other. He believes that work and home life are interconnected, informing and calibrating each other. Journalist [Walt Mossberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Mossberg "Walt Mossberg") dubbed the idea that someone who cannot tolerate criticism or critique should not do anything new or interesting "[The Bezos Principle](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Jeff_Bezos "q:Jeff Bezos")". Bezos does not schedule early morning meetings and enforces a two-pizza rule—a preference that meetings are small enough for two [pizzas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pizza "Pizza") to feed everyone in the boardroom. When interviewing candidates for jobs at Amazon, he has stated he considers three inquiries: can he admire the person, can the person raise the [common standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_quo "Status quo"), and under what circumstances could the person become exemplary.

In 2018, it was reported that he met with Amazon investors for just six hours a year. Instead of using [presentation slides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presentation_program "Presentation program"), Bezos requires high-level employees to present information with six-page narratives. Since 1998, Bezos has published an annual letter for Amazon shareholders wherein he frequently refers to five principles: focus on customers, not competitors; take risks for [market leadership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_share "Market share"); facilitate staff morale; build a [company culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organizational_culture "Organizational culture"); and empower people. Bezos maintains the email address jeff@amazon.com as an outlet for customers to reach out to him and the company. Although he does not respond to the emails, he forwards some of them with a question mark in the subject line to executives, who then attempt to address the issues. Bezos has cited [Jeff Immelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Immelt "Jeff Immelt") of [New Enterprise Associates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Enterprise_Associates "New Enterprise Associates"), [Warren Buffett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Buffett "Warren Buffett") of [Berkshire Hathaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkshire_Hathaway "Berkshire Hathaway"), [Jamie Dimon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Dimon "Jamie Dimon") of [JPMorgan Chase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JPMorgan_Chase "JPMorgan Chase"), and [Bob Iger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Iger "Bob Iger") of [The Walt Disney Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company "The Walt Disney Company") as major influences on his leadership style.

Bezos first became a millionaire in 1997 after raising $54 million (equivalent to $110,000,000 in 2025) through Amazon's [initial public offering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_public_offering "Initial public offering") (IPO). He was first included on the *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes "Forbes")* *[World's Billionaires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World%27s_Billionaires "World's Billionaires")* list in 1999 with an estimated [net worth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_worth "Net worth") of $10.1 billion (equivalent to $1.95×1010 in 2025), which placed his on the 19th position in the world and 10th in the USA. His net worth decreased to $6.1 billion a year later (equivalent to $1.14×1010 in 2025), a 40.5% drop. His wealth plummeted even more the following year, dropping 66.6% to $2.0 billion (equivalent to $3.6×109 in 2025). He lost $500 million the following year (equivalent to $900,000,000 in 2025), which brought his net worth down to $1.5 billion (equivalent to $2.7×109 in 2025). The following year, his net worth increased by 66.66% to $2.5 billion (equivalent to $4.4×109 in 2025). From 2005 to 2007, he quadrupled his net worth to $8.7 billion (equivalent to $1.43×1010 in 2025). After the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis "2008 financial crisis") and [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession "Great Recession"), his net worth would decrease to $6.8 billion (equivalent to $1.02×1010 in 2025)—a 17.7% drop. His wealth rose by 85.2% in 2010, leaving him with $12.6 billion (equivalent to $1.86×1010 in 2025). This percentage increase ascended him to the 43rd spot on the ranking from 68th.

After a rumor broke out that Amazon was developing a [smartphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone "Smartphone"), Bezos's net worth rose to $30.5 billion in 2014 (equivalent to $4.15×1010 in 2025). A year later, he entered the top ten when he increased his net worth to a total of $50.3 billion (equivalent to $6.83×1010 in 2025). Bezos rose to become the fifth richest person in the world hours before [market close](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_market "Stock market"); he gained $7 billion in one hour (equivalent to $9.0×109 in 2025). By the time the *Forbes* list was calculated in March 2016, his net worth was registered at $45.2 billion (equivalent to $6.06×1010 in 2025). However, just months later in October 2016, his wealth increased by $16.2 billion (equivalent to $2.17×1010 in 2025) to $66.5 billion (equivalent to $8.92×1010 in 2025), unofficially ranking him the third-richest person in the world, behind [Warren Buffett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Buffett "Warren Buffett"). After sporadic jumps in Amazon's share price, in July 2017 he briefly unseated [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft "Microsoft") co-founder [Bill Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Gates "Bill Gates") as the wealthiest person in the world.

Bezos would continue to sporadically surpass Gates throughout the month of October 2017 after Amazon's share price fluctuated. His net worth surpassed $100 billion for the first time on November 24, 2017 (equivalent to $1.31×1011 in 2025), after Amazon's [share price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Share_price "Share price") increased by more than 2.5%. When the 2017 list was issued, Bezos's net worth was registered at $72.8 billion (equivalent to $9.5620418137×1010 in 2025), adding $27.6 billion from the previous year (equivalent to $3.6×1010 in 2025). His wealth's rapid growth from 2016 to 2017 sparked a variety of assessments about how much money Bezos earned on a controlled, reduced time scale. On October 10, 2017, he made an estimated $6.24 billion (equivalent to $8.2×109 in 2025) in 5 minutes, slightly less than the then annual gross domestic product of [Kyrgyzstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrgyzstan "Kyrgyzstan").

On March 6, 2018, Bezos was designated the [wealthiest person in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World%27s_Billionaires "The World's Billionaires"), with a registered net worth of $112 billion (equivalent to $1.44×1011 in 2025). He unseated Bill Gates ($90 billion, equivalent to $1.15×1011 in 2025), who was $6 billion (equivalent to $7.7×109 in 2025) ahead of Warren Buffett ($84 billion, equivalent to $1.08×1011 in 2025), ranked third. He is considered the first registered [centi-billionaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centibillionaire "Centibillionaire") (not [adjusted for inflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_versus_nominal_value_(economics) "Real versus nominal value (economics)")).

Bezos's net worth increased by $33.6 billion (equivalent to $4.4×1010 in 2025) from January 2017 to January 2018. Following a report by *Quartz* highlighting Bezos' wealth, Amazon workers in [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland "Poland"), Germany, and Spain participated in demonstrations and [labor strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_action "Strike action") draw attention to his growing wealth and the lack of compensation, labor rights, and satisfactory working conditions of select Amazon workers. On July 17, 2018, he was designated the "wealthiest person in modern history" by the [Bloomberg Billionaires Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_Billionaires_Index "Bloomberg Billionaires Index"), *Fortune*, *[MarketWatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MarketWatch "MarketWatch")*, *[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")*, and *Forbes*.

In 2019, Bezos's wealth was reduced by the divorce from his wife [MacKenzie Bezos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacKenzie_Bezos "MacKenzie Bezos"). According to *Forbes*, had the Washington state common law applied to their divorce without a prenuptial agreement, Bezos's wealth could have been equitably divided with his ex-wife; however, she eventually received 25% of Bezos's Amazon shares, then valued at approximately $36 billion (equivalent to $4.5×1010 in 2025), making her the third-richest woman in the world. Bezos retained his interest in *The Washington Post* and Blue Origin, as well as voting control of the shares received by his ex-wife.

In June 2019, Bezos purchased three adjoining apartments overlooking [Madison Square Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Park "Madison Square Park") in [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan "Manhattan"), including a [penthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penthouse_apartment "Penthouse apartment"), for a combined total of $80 million (equivalent to $101,000,000 in 2025), making this one of the most expensive real estate purchases within New York City in 2019. Bezos had also purchased three adjoining apartments at [25 Central Park West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Century_(apartment_building) "The Century (apartment building)") in Manhattan for $7.65 million in 1999 (equivalent to $14,800,000 in 2025); he bought a fourth unit in that building for $5.3 million in 2012 (equivalent to $7,400,000 in 2025).

In February 2020, Bezos purchased the [Warner Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Warner_Estate "Jack Warner Estate") from [David Geffen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Geffen "David Geffen") for $165 million (equivalent to $205,000,000 in 2025), a record price paid for a residence in the Los Angeles area. During the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"), it was reported that Bezos's fortune had grown by $24 billion (equivalent to $2.99×1010 in 2025), citing a surge in demand from households on lockdown shopping on Amazon. He further expanded his residential holdings in February 2022, purchasing a $16.1 million apartment at a 24-story boutique condominium (equivalent to $17,700,000 in 2025), located across from Madison Square Park in the Flatiron neighborhood, where he already owns all the units on the top floor. Bezos is the owner of the *[Y721](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y721 "Y721")*, a luxury superyacht estimated to cost more than $500,000,000 (equivalent to $550,000,000 in 2025); it is the largest yacht in the world. According to *Forbes* Bezos was the second-wealthiest person in America and the third-wealthiest person in the world in 2023.

Bezos added a remote 14-acre estate on La Perouse Bay in [Maui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maui "Maui"), encircled by inactive lava fields, to his real estate holdings for $78 million in 2022 (equivalent to $90,000,000 in 2025).

As of May 2026, the [Bloomberg Billionaires Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_Billionaires_Index "Bloomberg Billionaires Index") ranks Bezos the fourth wealthiest person in the world, with an estimated wealth of $279 billion.

## Personal life

In 1992, while working for [D. E. Shaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._E._Shaw "D. E. Shaw") in Manhattan, Bezos met novelist [MacKenzie Tuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacKenzie_Scott "MacKenzie Scott"), who was a research associate at the firm; the couple married a year later. In 1994, they moved across the country to [Seattle, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle "Seattle"), where Bezos founded Amazon. Bezos and his ex-wife MacKenzie are the parents of four children: three sons, and a daughter adopted from China.

In March 2003, Bezos was a passenger in a helicopter that crashed in [West Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Texas "West Texas") while surveying land to buy for Blue Origin; the other three occupants in the helicopter were pilot Charles "Cheater" Bella, Amazon lawyer Elizabeth Korrell, and local rancher Ty Holland. All survived; Bezos sustained only minor injuries and was discharged from a local hospital the same day.

Bezos portrayed a [Starfleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starfleet "Starfleet") official in the 2016 movie *[Star Trek Beyond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Trek_Beyond "Star Trek Beyond")*, and joined the cast and crew at a [San Diego Comic-Con](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Comic-Con "San Diego Comic-Con") screening. He had lobbied [Paramount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures "Paramount Pictures") for the role apropos of [Alexa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Alexa "Amazon Alexa") and his personal/professional interest in [speech recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_recognition "Speech recognition"). His one line consisted of a response to an alien in distress: "Speak Normally." In his initial discussion of the project which became Alexa with his technical advisor Greg Hart in 2011, Bezos told him that the goal was to create "the *Star Trek* computer." Bezos's family corporation Zefram LLC is named after [Zefram Cochrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zefram_Cochrane "Zefram Cochrane"), a character from *[Star Trek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Trek "Star Trek")*.

In January 2019, Bezos and his wife MacKenzie released a joint statement which revealed that they would be getting divorced after 25 years together. Subsequently, *[National Enquirer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Enquirer "National Enquirer")* revealed that Bezos had an affair with media personality [Lauren Sánchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauren_S%C3%A1nchez "Lauren Sánchez"); the affair with Sánchez had lasted for months. Later, Bezos published an online essay on February 7, 2019, in which he accused [American Media, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Media,_Inc. "American Media, Inc.") owner [David Pecker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Pecker "David Pecker") of "extortion and blackmail" for threatening to publish intimate photos of Bezos and current girlfriend Lauren Sánchez if he did not stop his investigation into how his text messages and other photos had been leaked to the *National Enquirer*. Media reports have accused Sánchez's brother Michael of being the source for the photos obtained by *National Enquirer*; however, Bezos has speculated that it may have been the Saudi Arabian government.

On April 4, 2019, the divorce was finalized, with Bezos keeping 75% of the couple's Amazon stock and MacKenzie getting the remaining 25% ($35.6 billion, equivalent to $4.48×1010 in 2025). However, Bezos would keep all of the couple's voting rights. Sánchez and Bezos became engaged in May 2023. The couple [married in Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedding_of_Jeff_Bezos_and_Lauren_S%C3%A1nchez "Wedding of Jeff Bezos and Lauren Sánchez") on June 27, 2025, with the ceremony attracting mainstream media attention and various celebrities.

Bezos is the Honorary Chair of the [Explorers Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Explorers_Club "The Explorers Club").

Bezos is a member of an investment consortium that purchased a minority stake in [Liverpool F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C. "Liverpool F.C.") in August 2026.

## Politics

According to public [campaign finance records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_finance_in_the_United_States "Campaign finance in the United States"), Bezos supported the electoral campaigns of [Patty Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patty_Murray "Patty Murray") and [Maria Cantwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Cantwell "Maria Cantwell"), two [Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)") U.S. senators from Washington. He has also supported Democrats U.S. representative [John Conyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Conyers "John Conyers"), as well as [Patrick Leahy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Leahy "Patrick Leahy") and Republican [Spencer Abraham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spencer_Abraham "Spencer Abraham"), U.S. senators serving on committees dealing with Internet-related issues. Jeff Bezos and MacKenzie Bezos have supported the legalization of [same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage "Same-sex marriage"), and in 2012 contributed $2.5 million (equivalent to $3,500,000 in 2025) to [Washington United for Marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_United_for_Marriage "Washington United for Marriage"), a group supporting a yes vote on [Washington Referendum 74](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Referendum_74 "Washington Referendum 74"), which affirmed a same-sex marriage law enacted in the state. Bezos donated $100,000 (equivalent to $150,000 in 2025) towards a movement against a Washington [state income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_income_tax "State income tax") in 2010 for "top earners". In 2012, he donated to Amazon's [political action committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_action_committee "Political action committee") (PAC), which has given $56,000 (equivalent to $79,000 in 2025) and $74,500 (equivalent to $104,000 in 2025) to Democrats and Republicans, respectively.

In 2014, Amazon won a bid for a [cloud computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_computing "Cloud computing") contract with the [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Intelligence_Agency "Central Intelligence Agency") valued at $600 million (equivalent to $800,000,000 in 2025). A 2018, $10 billion contract (equivalent to $1.28×1010 in 2025) known as the [Joint Enterprise Defense Infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Enterprise_Defense_Infrastructure "Joint Enterprise Defense Infrastructure") (JEDI) project, this time with the [Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Defense "United States Department of Defense"), was allegedly written up in a way that favors Amazon. Controversy over this was raised when General [James Mattis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Mattis "James Mattis") accepted a headquarters tour invitation from Bezos and coordinated the deal through Sally Donnelly, a lobbyist who previously worked for Amazon. In November 2019, when the contract was awarded to Microsoft instead, Amazon filed a lawsuit with allegations that the bidding process was biased. On July 6, 2021, the Pentagon cancelled the JEDI contract with Microsoft, citing that "due to evolving requirements, increased cloud conversancy, and industry advances, the JEDI Cloud contract no longer meets its needs." Despite Bezos's support for an open borders policy towards immigrants, Amazon has actively marketed facial recognition software to [U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Immigration_and_Customs_Enforcement "U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement") (ICE).

In 2019, a [PAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_action_committee "Political action committee") linked to Bezos spent over $1 million (equivalent to $1,300,000 in 2025) in an unsuccessful attempt to defeat the reelection bid of Seattle city council member and activist [Kshama Sawant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kshama_Sawant "Kshama Sawant"). On November 22, 2021, Jeff Bezos donated $100 million (equivalent to $119,000,000 in 2025) to the [Obama Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obama_Foundation "Obama Foundation") to "help expand the scope of programming that reaches emerging leaders" and requested the [Obama Presidential Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obama_Presidential_Center "Obama Presidential Center")'s plaza to be named after [John Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lewis "John Lewis").

### Donald Trump

After the [2016 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election "2016 United States presidential election"), Bezos was invited to join [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump")'s [Defense Innovation Advisory Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Innovation_Advisory_Board "Defense Innovation Advisory Board"), an advisory council to improve the technology used by the [Defense Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Defense "United States Department of Defense"). Trump has repeatedly criticized Bezos [via Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_on_Twitter "Donald Trump on Twitter"), accused Bezos of avoiding [corporate taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_tax "Corporate tax"), gaining undue political influence, and undermining his presidency by spreading [fake news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fake_news "Fake news"). Nevertheless, Bezos congratulated Trump on his [second election victory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election "2024 United States presidential election"), posting on X, "Big congratulations to our 45th and now 47th President on an extraordinary political comeback and decisive victory. No nation has bigger opportunities. Wishing Donald Trump all success in leading and uniting the America we all love."

Since 2023, Bezos has been a resident of [Indian Creek, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Creek,_Florida "Indian Creek, Florida"). As reported by *[Axios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axios_(website) "Axios (website)")* in February 2025, Bezos held a private phone conversation in July 2024 with then-candidate Trump, planting the seeds of a "Bezos–Trump alliance" months before Bezos [blocked the *Washington Post*'s editorial board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_media_endorsements_in_the_2024_United_States_presidential_election#The_Washington_Post "News media endorsements in the 2024 United States presidential election") from endorsing [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris "Kamala Harris") in the election. After Trump's November victory, Bezos dined with [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk "Elon Musk") and Trump at [Mar-a-Lago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mar-a-Lago "Mar-a-Lago"); Amazon subsequently donated $1 million to [Trump's inauguration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump "Second inauguration of Donald Trump"), at which Bezos was in attendance. Bezos and Trump were reported to have met for dinner again in February 2025, on the same night that Bezos announced [changes to the *Washington Post's* opinion policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post#Suppression_of_views_Bezos_disfavors "The Washington Post") to promote "free markets and personal liberties" and suppress divergent opinions. *Axios* characterized it as "another sign of Trump and Bezos' growing closeness".

According to the *[Financial Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Times "Financial Times")*, Bezos had a contentious relationship with Trump during Trump's first term, but worked to have a positive relationship with Trump in 2024 and during [Trump's second term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_presidency_of_Donald_Trump "Second presidency of Donald Trump"). Bezos reportedly supported Trump to further his business interests, and supports many of Trump's policies. The *Financial Times* also noted that Bezos had made other changes in his life, including stepping down as CEO of Amazon in 2021, focusing on [Blue Origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Origin "Blue Origin"), and being engaged to [Lauren Sánchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauren_S%C3%A1nchez "Lauren Sánchez"), which may have changed his political views.

### Saudi hacking claim

In March 2018, Bezos met in Seattle with [Mohammad bin Salman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_bin_Salman "Mohammad bin Salman"), the crown prince and *de facto* ruler of [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia "Saudi Arabia"), to discuss investment opportunities for [Saudi Vision 2030](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Vision_2030 "Saudi Vision 2030"). In March 2019, Bezos's security consultant accused the Saudi government of [hacking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phone_hacking "Phone hacking") Bezos's phone. According to BBC, Bezos's top security staffer, [Gavin de Becker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavin_de_Becker "Gavin de Becker"), "linked the hack to the *Washington Post*'s coverage of the murder of Saudi writer [Jamal Khashoggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_Khashoggi "Jamal Khashoggi") at the Saudi consulate in Istanbul". Khashoggi, a Saudi journalist and dissident, was employed as a writer at the *Washington Post*, owned by Bezos. Khashoggi was killed in late 2018 in Turkey's Saudi consulate for his critical stance and journalism against the Saudi government and its leader. In January 2020, *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* reported that the hack was initiated before the murder but after Khashoggi wrote critically about the crown prince in the *Washington Post*. Forensic analysis of Bezos's mobile phone conducted by advisory firm [FTI Consulting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FTI_Consulting "FTI Consulting"), concluded it "highly probable" that the hack was achieved using a malicious file hidden in a video sent in a [WhatsApp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhatsApp "WhatsApp") message to Bezos from the personal account of the crown prince on May 1, 2018. Saudi Arabia has denied the claim.

## Philanthropy

Bezos donated to the [Fred Hutchinson Cancer Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Hutchinson_Cancer_Research_Center "Fred Hutchinson Cancer Research Center") several times between 2009 and 2017. In 2013, he pledged $500,000 (equivalent to $700,000 in 2025) to [Worldreader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worldreader "Worldreader"), a non-profit founded by a former Amazon employee. In September 2018, *Business Insider* reported that Bezos was the only one of the top five billionaires in the world who had not signed the [Giving Pledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giving_Pledge "Giving Pledge"), an initiative created by Bill Gates and Warren Buffett that encourages wealthy people to give away a majority of their wealth. That same month, Janet Camarena, director of transparency initiatives at [Foundation Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundation_Center "Foundation Center"), was quoted by CNBC as having questions about Bezos's new Day 1 Fund, including the fund's structure and how exactly it will be funded.

In May 2017, Bezos gave $1 million (equivalent to $1,300,000 in 2025) to the [Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reporters_Committee_for_Freedom_of_the_Press "Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press"), which provides [pro bono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_bono "Pro bono") legal services for American journalists. On June 15, 2017, he posted a message on Twitter asking for ideas for philanthropy: "I'm thinking about a philanthropy strategy that is the opposite of how I mostly spend my time—working on the long term". At the time of the post, Bezos's lifetime spending on charitable causes was estimated to be $100 million (equivalent to $130,000,000 in 2025). Multiple opinion columnists responded by asking Bezos to pay higher wages to Amazon warehouse workers. A year later in June, he tweeted that he would announce two philanthropic foci by the end of summer 2018. Bezos announced in September 2018 that he would commit approximately $2 billion (equivalent to $2.6×109 in 2025) to a fund to deal with [American homelessness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homelessness_in_the_United_States "Homelessness in the United States") and establish a network of non-profit [preschools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preschool "Preschool") for low-income communities. As part of this announcement, he committed to establishing the "Day 1 Families Fund" to finance "[night shelters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeless_shelter "Homeless shelter") and [day care centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_care "Child care") for homeless families" and the "Day 1 Academies Fund" for [early childhood education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_childhood_education "Early childhood education").

In January 2018, Bezos made a $33 million donation (equivalent to $42,000,000 in 2025) to TheDream.US, a college scholarship fund for [undocumented immigrants brought to the United States as minors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undocumented_youth_in_the_United_States "Undocumented youth in the United States"). In June 2018, Bezos donated to [Breakthrough Energy Ventures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Energy_Ventures "Breakthrough Energy Ventures"), a private philanthropic fund founded by [Bill Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Gates "Bill Gates") aimed at promoting [emissions-free energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_energy "Sustainable energy"). In September 2018, Bezos donated $10 million (equivalent to $13,000,000 in 2025) to [With Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/With_Honor "With Honor"), a nonpartisan organization that works to increase the number of veterans in political office.

In February 2020, Bezos pledged $10 billion (equivalent to $1.24×1010 in 2025) to [combat climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_mitigation "Climate change mitigation") through the Bezos Earth Fund. Later that year, in November, Bezos announced $791 million (equivalent to $980,000,000 in 2025) of donations to established, well-known groups, with $100 million (equivalent to $120,000,000 in 2025) each going to [Environmental Defense Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_Defense_Fund "Environmental Defense Fund"), [Natural Resources Defense Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Resources_Defense_Council "Natural Resources Defense Council"), [The Nature Conservancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nature_Conservancy "The Nature Conservancy"), [World Resources Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Resources_Institute "World Resources Institute") and [World Wildlife Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wildlife_Fund "World Wildlife Fund"), and the remainder going to 11 other groups. In April 2020, early in the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"), Bezos donated $100 million (equivalent to $120,000,000 in 2025) to food banks through [Feeding America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feeding_America "Feeding America"). In November 2021, Bezos pledged to donate $2 billion (equivalent to $2.4×109 in 2025) towards restructuring food systems and nature conservation at the [2021 United Nations Climate Change Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_United_Nations_Climate_Change_Conference "2021 United Nations Climate Change Conference"). The Earth Fund supported the [Earth Rover Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_Rover_Program "Earth Rover Program"), which aims to develop high-yield low-environmental-impact farming by novel [seismological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seismological "Seismological") study of soils, with $4m.

In July 2021, Bezos announced the [Courage and Civility Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courage_and_Civility_Award "Courage and Civility Award") and donated $100 million (equivalent to $120,000,000 in 2025) each to lawyer [Van Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Jones "Van Jones") and chef [José Andrés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Andr%C3%A9s "José Andrés"). The next year, he donated $100 million (equivalent to $110,000,000 in 2025) to singer [Dolly Parton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_Parton "Dolly Parton") in recognition of her charity work focused on improving children's literacy around the world. In March 2024, he donated $50 million (equivalent to $55,000,000 in 2025) each to actress [Eva Longoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eva_Longoria "Eva Longoria") and retired admiral [Bill McRaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_H._McRaven "William H. McRaven").

Bezos Academy is a group of tuition-free [preschools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preschool "Preschool") for students from [low-income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-income "Low-income") families, which was created by Bezos, and which operate in a manner similar to the [Montessori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montessori "Montessori") method (but are not accredited as Montessori schools). On November 22, 2022, Bezos awarded $123 million (equivalent to $140,000,000 in 2025) to organizations that are engaged in relocating homeless families to permanent housing. Day 1 Families Fund grants, the amounts of which vary in monetary terms, will be sent to 40 organizations across the country.
