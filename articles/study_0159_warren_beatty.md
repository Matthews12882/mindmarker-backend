# Warren Beatty

**Henry Warren Beatty** (né **Beaty**; born March 30, 1937) is an American actor and filmmaker. His career has spanned over six decades, and he has received an [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award "Academy Award") and three [Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards "Golden Globe Awards"). He also received the [Irving G. Thalberg Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_G._Thalberg_Memorial_Award "Irving G. Thalberg Memorial Award") in 1999, the [BAFTA Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Fellowship "BAFTA Fellowship") in 2002, the [Kennedy Center Honors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Center_Honors "Kennedy Center Honors") in 2004, the [Cecil B. DeMille Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecil_B._DeMille_Award "Cecil B. DeMille Award") in 2007, and the [American Film Institute Life Achievement Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFI_Life_Achievement_Award "AFI Life Achievement Award") in 2008.

Beatty has been nominated for 14 Academy Awards, including four for [Best Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor "Academy Award for Best Actor"), four for [Best Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture"), two for [Best Director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Director "Academy Award for Best Director"), three for [Original Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Screenplay "Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay"), and one for [Adapted Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Adapted_Screenplay "Academy Award for Best Adapted Screenplay") – winning Best Director for *[Reds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reds_(film) "Reds (film)")* (1981). He was nominated for his performances as [Clyde Barrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clyde_Barrow "Clyde Barrow") in the [crime drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_drama "Crime drama") *[Bonnie and Clyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnie_and_Clyde_(film) "Bonnie and Clyde (film)")* (1967), a quarterback mistakenly taken to heaven in the [sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_film "Sports film") [fantasy drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasy_film "Fantasy film") *[Heaven Can Wait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heaven_Can_Wait_(1978_film) "Heaven Can Wait (1978 film)")* (1978), [John Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Reed_(journalist) "John Reed (journalist)") in the [historical epic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_epic "Historical epic") *[Reds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reds_(film) "Reds (film)")* (1981), and [Bugsy Siegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugsy_Siegel "Bugsy Siegel") in the crime drama *[Bugsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugsy "Bugsy")* (1991).

Beatty made his acting debut as a teenager in love in the [Elia Kazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elia_Kazan "Elia Kazan") drama *[Splendor in the Grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Splendor_in_the_Grass "Splendor in the Grass")* (1961). He later acted in [John Frankenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Frankenheimer "John Frankenheimer")'s drama *[All Fall Down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Fall_Down_(1962_film) "All Fall Down (1962 film)")* (1962), [Robert Altman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Altman "Robert Altman")'s [revisionist western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revisionist_western "Revisionist western") *[McCabe & Mrs. Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCabe_%26_Mrs._Miller "McCabe & Mrs. Miller")* (1971), [Alan J. Pakula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_J._Pakula "Alan J. Pakula")'s political thriller *[The Parallax View](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Parallax_View "The Parallax View")* (1974), [Hal Ashby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hal_Ashby "Hal Ashby")'s comedy *[Shampoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shampoo_(film) "Shampoo (film)")* (1975), and [Elaine May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elaine_May "Elaine May")'s [road movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_movie "Road movie") *[Ishtar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishtar_(film) "Ishtar (film)")* (1987). He also directed and starred in the action crime film *[Dick Tracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Tracy_(1990_film) "Dick Tracy (1990 film)")* (1990), the political satire *[Bulworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulworth "Bulworth")* (1998), and the romance *[Rules Don't Apply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rules_Don%27t_Apply "Rules Don't Apply")* (2016), all of which he also produced.

On stage, Beatty made his [Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_(theatre) "Broadway (theatre)") debut in the [William Inge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Inge "William Inge") [kitchen sink drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitchen_sink_drama "Kitchen sink drama") *A Loss of Roses* (1960) for which he was nominated for the [Tony Award for Best Featured Actor in a Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Featured_Actor_in_a_Play "Tony Award for Best Featured Actor in a Play").

## Early life and education

Henry Warren Beaty was born on March 30, 1937, in [Richmond, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_Virginia "Richmond, Virginia"). His mother, Kathlyn Corinne (née MacLean), was a teacher from [Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Scotia "Nova Scotia"). His father, Ira Owens Beaty, studied for a PhD in educational psychology and was a teacher and school administrator, in addition to working in real estate. His grandparents were also teachers. The family was [Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptist "Baptist"). During Warren's childhood, Ira Beaty moved his family from Richmond to [Norfolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk,_Virginia "Norfolk, Virginia") and then to [Arlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_County,_Virginia "Arlington County, Virginia") and [Waverly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waverly,_Virginia "Waverly, Virginia"), then back to Arlington, eventually taking a position at Arlington's Thomas Jefferson Junior High School in 1945. During the 1950s the family resided in the [Dominion Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_Hills_Historic_District "Dominion Hills Historic District") section of Arlington. Beatty's older sister is actress, dancer and writer [Shirley MacLaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirley_MacLaine "Shirley MacLaine") (who altered the phonetic spelling of her mother's maiden surname). His uncle by marriage was Canadian politician [A.A. MacLeod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.A._MacLeod "A.A. MacLeod").

Beatty became interested in movies as a child, often accompanying his sister to theaters. One film that had an important early influence on him was *[The Philadelphia Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Philadelphia_Story_(film) "The Philadelphia Story (film)")* (1940), which he saw when it was re-released in the 1950s. He noticed a strong resemblance between its star, [Katharine Hepburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katharine_Hepburn "Katharine Hepburn"), and his mother, in both appearance and personality, saying that they symbolized "perpetual integrity". Another film that influenced him was *[Love Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Affair_(1939_film) "Love Affair (1939 film)")* (1939), starring one of his favorite actors, [Charles Boyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Boyer "Charles Boyer"). He found it "deeply moving", and recalled that "[t]his is a movie I always wanted to make." He [remade *Love Affair*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Affair_(1994_film) "Love Affair (1994 film)") in 1994, starring alongside his wife [Annette Bening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annette_Bening "Annette Bening") and Katharine Hepburn.

Among his favorite TV shows in the 1950s was the *[Texaco Star Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texaco_Star_Theatre "Texaco Star Theatre")*, and he began to mimic one of its regular host comedians, [Milton Berle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Berle "Milton Berle"). Beatty learned to do a "superb imitation of Berle and his routine", said a friend, and often used Berle-type humor at home. His sister's memories of her brother include seeing him reading books by [Eugene O'Neill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_O%27Neill "Eugene O'Neill") or singing along to [Al Jolson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jolson "Al Jolson") records. In *[Rules Don't Apply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rules_Don%27t_Apply "Rules Don't Apply")* (2016), Beatty plays [Howard Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Hughes "Howard Hughes"), who is shown talking about and singing Jolson songs while flying his plane.

MacLaine noted — on what made her brother want to become a filmmaker, sometimes writing, producing, directing and starring in his films: "That's why he's more comfortable behind the camera ... He's in the total-control aspect. He has to have control over everything." Beatty doesn't deny that need; in speaking about his earliest parts, he said "When I acted in films I used to come with suggestions about the script, the lighting, the wardrobe, and people used to say 'Waddya want, to produce the picture as well?' And I used to say that I supposed I did."

Beatty played [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_football "American football") at [Washington-Lee High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington-Lee_High_School "Washington-Lee High School") in Arlington. Encouraged to act by the success of his sister, who established herself as a Hollywood star, he decided to work as a stagehand at the [National Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Theatre_(Washington,_D.C.) "National Theatre (Washington, D.C.)") in Washington, D.C. during the summer before his senior year. After graduation, he was reportedly offered ten college football scholarships, but turned them down to study liberal arts at [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University "Northwestern University") (1954–55), where he joined the [Sigma Chi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_Chi "Sigma Chi") fraternity. Beatty left college after his first year and moved to New York City to study acting under [Stella Adler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_Adler "Stella Adler") at the [Stella Adler Studio of Acting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_Adler_Studio_of_Acting "Stella Adler Studio of Acting"). He often subsisted on peanut butter and jelly sandwiches, and worked odd jobs, including dishwasher, piano player, bricklayer's assistant, construction worker, and, relatively briefly, a [sandhog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandhog "Sandhog").

## Career

### 1957–1966: Early roles and breakthrough

Beatty started his career making appearances on television shows such as *[Studio One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio_One_(CBS_series) "Studio One (CBS series)")* (1957), *[Kraft Television Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraft_Television_Theatre "Kraft Television Theatre")* (1957), and *[Playhouse 90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playhouse_90 "Playhouse 90")* (1959). He was a semi-regular on *[The Many Loves of Dobie Gillis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Many_Loves_of_Dobie_Gillis "The Many Loves of Dobie Gillis")* during its first season (1959–1960). His performance in [William Inge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Inge "William Inge")'s *A Loss of Roses* on Broadway garnered him a 1960 [Tony Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award "Tony Award") nomination for [Best Featured Actor in a Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Featured_Actor_in_a_Play "Tony Award for Best Featured Actor in a Play") and a 1960 [Theatre World Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_World_Award "Theatre World Award"). It was his sole appearance on Broadway.

In February 1960, Beatty enlisted as an [airman third class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airman_Third_Class "Airman Third Class") in the [California Air National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Air_National_Guard "California Air National Guard") at [Van Nuys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Nuys "Van Nuys") to fulfill his military service obligation. He was discharged the following year due to a [physical disability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_disability "Physical disability") and remained on [inactive duty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_Ready_Reserve "Individual Ready Reserve") after that time.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Beatty made his film debut in [Elia Kazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elia_Kazan "Elia Kazan")'s *[Splendor in the Grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Splendor_in_the_Grass "Splendor in the Grass")* (1961) opposite [Natalie Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalie_Wood "Natalie Wood"). The film was a major critical and box office success; Beatty was nominated for a [Golden Globe Award for Best Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actor_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Drama "Golden Globe Award for Best Actor – Motion Picture Drama") and received the award for [New Star of the Year – Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_New_Star_of_the_Year_%E2%80%93_Actor "Golden Globe Award for New Star of the Year – Actor"). The film was also nominated for two Oscars, winning one.

Author [Peter Biskind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Biskind "Peter Biskind") points out that Kazan "was the first in a string of major directors Beatty sought out, mentors or father figures from whom he wanted to learn." Years later during a [Kennedy Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Center "Kennedy Center") tribute to Kazan, Beatty told the audience that Kazan "had given him the most important break in his career." Biskind adds that they "were wildly dissimilar—mentor vs. protegé, director vs. actor, immigrant outsider vs. native son. Kazan was armed with the confidence born of age and success, while Beatty was virtually aflame with the arrogance of youth." Kazan recalls his impressions of Beatty:

> Warren—it was obvious the first time I saw him—wanted it all and wanted it his way. Why not? He had the energy, a very keen intelligence, and more *chutzpah* than any Jew I've ever known. Even more than me. Bright as they come, intrepid, and with that thing all women secretly respect: complete confidence in his sexual powers, confidence so great that he never had to advertise himself, even by hints.

Beatty followed his initial film with [Tennessee Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_Williams "Tennessee Williams")' *[The Roman Spring of Mrs. Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Roman_Spring_of_Mrs._Stone "The Roman Spring of Mrs. Stone")* (1961), with [Vivien Leigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vivien_Leigh "Vivien Leigh") and [Lotte Lenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotte_Lenya "Lotte Lenya"), directed by [Jose Quintero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_Quintero "Jose Quintero"); *[All Fall Down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Fall_Down_(1962_film) "All Fall Down (1962 film)")* (1962), with [Angela Lansbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Lansbury "Angela Lansbury"), [Karl Malden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Malden "Karl Malden") and [Eva Marie Saint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eva_Marie_Saint "Eva Marie Saint"), directed by [John Frankenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Frankenheimer "John Frankenheimer"); *[Lilith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilith_(film) "Lilith (film)")* (1963), with [Jean Seberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Seberg "Jean Seberg") and [Peter Fonda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Fonda "Peter Fonda"), directed by [Robert Rossen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Rossen "Robert Rossen"); *[Promise Her Anything](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promise_Her_Anything "Promise Her Anything")* (1964), with [Leslie Caron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Caron "Leslie Caron"), [Bob Cummings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Cummings "Bob Cummings") and [Keenan Wynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keenan_Wynn "Keenan Wynn"), directed by [Arthur Hiller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Hiller "Arthur Hiller"); *[Mickey One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickey_One "Mickey One")* (1965), with [Alexandra Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandra_Stewart "Alexandra Stewart") and [Hurd Hatfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurd_Hatfield "Hurd Hatfield"), directed by [Arthur Penn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Penn "Arthur Penn"); and *[Kaleidoscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaleidoscope_(1966_film) "Kaleidoscope (1966 film)")* (1966), with [Susannah York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susannah_York "Susannah York") and [Clive Revill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clive_Revill "Clive Revill"), directed by [Jack Smight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Smight "Jack Smight"). In 1965, he formed a production company, Tatira, which he named for Kathlyn (whose nickname was "Tat") and Ira.

### 1967–1979: Stardom and acclaim

> Mr. Beatty's career has had all the hallmarks of the conventional Hollywood golden boy. Ingratiating good looks, disarming youthfulness, a delight in the social life and no apparently strong feelings about his craft. This image has now been strikingly shattered with his emergence as a vividly individual actor and as a highly imaginative producer in the gangster ballad, *Bonnie and Clyde* ... At 28 [*sic*], the image of Warren Beatty, fun-loving playboy, is dead. Warren Beatty, a man of the cinema, is born.

— Gerald Garrett, syndicated columnist

At age 30, Beatty produced and acted in *[Bonnie and Clyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnie_and_Clyde_(film) "Bonnie and Clyde (film)")*, released in 1967. He assembled a team that included the writers [Robert Benton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Benton "Robert Benton") and [David Newman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Newman_(filmmaker) "David Newman (filmmaker)"), and the director [Arthur Penn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Penn "Arthur Penn"). Beatty selected most of the cast, including [Faye Dunaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faye_Dunaway "Faye Dunaway"), [Gene Hackman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_Hackman "Gene Hackman"), [Estelle Parsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estelle_Parsons "Estelle Parsons"), [Gene Wilder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_Wilder "Gene Wilder") and [Michael J. Pollard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_J._Pollard "Michael J. Pollard"). Beatty also oversaw the script and spearheaded the delivery of the film. Beatty chose Gene Hackman because he had acted with him in *Lilith* in 1964 and felt he was a "great" actor. Upon completion of the film, he credited Hackman with giving the "most authentic performance in the movie, so textured and so moving", recalls Dunaway. Beatty had been so impressed by Gene Wilder after seeing him in a play, that he cast him without an audition for what became Wilder's screen debut. Beatty already knew Pollard: "Michael J. Pollard was one of my oldest friends", Beatty said. "I'd known him forever; I met him the day I got my first television show. We did a play together on Broadway."

*Bonnie and Clyde* became a critical and commercial success, despite the early misgivings by studio head [Jack Warner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_L._Warner "Jack L. Warner") who put up the production money. Before filming began, Warner said, "What does Warren Beatty think he's doing? How did he ever get us into this thing? This gangster stuff went out with [Cagney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cagney "James Cagney")." The film was nominated for ten [Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards"), including [Best Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture") and [Best Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor "Academy Award for Best Actor"), and seven [Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award "Golden Globe Award"), including Best Picture and Best Actor. Beatty was originally entitled to 40% of the film's profits but gave 10% to Penn, and his 30% share earned him more than US$6 million. After *Bonnie and Clyde*, Beatty acted with [Elizabeth Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Taylor "Elizabeth Taylor") in *[The Only Game in Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Only_Game_in_Town_(1970_film) "The Only Game in Town (1970 film)")* (1970), directed by George Stevens; *[McCabe & Mrs. Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCabe_%26_Mrs._Miller "McCabe & Mrs. Miller")* (1971), directed by [Robert Altman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Altman "Robert Altman"); and *[Dollars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/$_(film) "$ (film)")* (1971), directed by Richard Brooks.

In 1972, Beatty produced a series of [benefit concerts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benefit_concert "Benefit concert") to help with publicity and fundraising in the [George McGovern 1972 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_McGovern_1972_presidential_campaign "George McGovern 1972 presidential campaign"). Beatty first put together [Four for McGovern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_for_McGovern "Four for McGovern") at [The Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Forum_(Inglewood,_California) "The Forum (Inglewood, California)") in the Los Angeles area, convincing [Barbra Streisand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbra_Streisand "Barbra Streisand"), [Carole King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carole_King "Carole King") and [James Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Taylor "James Taylor") to perform. Streisand brought [Quincy Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quincy_Jones "Quincy Jones") and his Orchestra, and recorded the album *[Live Concert at the Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_Concert_at_the_Forum "Live Concert at the Forum")*. Two weeks later, Beatty mounted another concert at the [Cleveland Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Arena "Cleveland Arena"), in which [Joni Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joni_Mitchell "Joni Mitchell") and [Paul Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Simon "Paul Simon") joined James Taylor. In June 1972, Beatty produced [Together for McGovern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Together_for_McGovern "Together for McGovern") at [Madison Square Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden "Madison Square Garden"), reuniting [Simon and Garfunkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_and_Garfunkel "Simon and Garfunkel"), [Nichols and May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nichols_and_May "Nichols and May"), and [Peter, Paul and Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter,_Paul_and_Mary "Peter, Paul and Mary"), and featuring [Dionne Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dionne_Warwick "Dionne Warwick"). With these productions, campaign manager [Gary Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Hart "Gary Hart") said that Beatty had "invented the political concert". He had mobilized Hollywood celebrities for a political cause on a scale previously unseen, creating a new power dynamic.

Beatty appeared in the films *[The Parallax View](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Parallax_View "The Parallax View")* (1974), directed by [Alan Pakula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Pakula "Alan Pakula"); and *[The Fortune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fortune "The Fortune")* (1975), directed by [Mike Nichols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Nichols "Mike Nichols"). Taking greater control, Beatty produced, co-wrote and acted in *[Shampoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shampoo_(film) "Shampoo (film)")* (1975), directed by [Hal Ashby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hal_Ashby "Hal Ashby"), which was nominated for four Academy Awards, including [Best Original Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Screenplay "Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay"), as well as five Golden Globe Awards, including [Best Motion Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Musical_or_Comedy "Golden Globe Award for Best Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy") and Best Actor.

In 1978, Beatty directed, produced, wrote and acted in *[Heaven Can Wait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heaven_Can_Wait_(1978_film) "Heaven Can Wait (1978 film)")* (1978) (sharing co-directing credit with [Buck Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buck_Henry "Buck Henry")). The film was nominated for nine Academy Awards, including Best Picture, Director, Actor, and Adapted Screenplay. It also won three Golden Globe Awards, including Best Motion Picture and Best Actor.

### 1980–2001: Directorial debut and expansion

> A film [*Reds*] of this scope and size demands incredible work from the director, and when you consider that Beatty also served as producer, writer and star, it's hard to believe so much work could come from one man. As a film, it's a marvelous view of America in the 1912–19 era, and Beatty brought some superior performances from a large cast.

— Joe Pollack, syndicated columnist

Beatty's next film was *[Reds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reds_(film) "Reds (film)")* (1981), a historical epic about American Communist journalist [John Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Reed_(journalist) "John Reed (journalist)") who observed the Russian [October Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution "October Revolution") –a project Beatty had begun researching and filming for as far back as 1970. It was a critical and commercial success, despite being an American film about an American Communist, made and released at the height of the Cold War. It received 12 Academy Award nominations – including four for Beatty (for Best Picture, Director, Actor, and Original Screenplay), winning three. Beatty won for Best Director, [Maureen Stapleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maureen_Stapleton "Maureen Stapleton") won for Best Supporting Actress (playing anarchist [Emma Goldman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Goldman "Emma Goldman")), and [Vittorio Storaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vittorio_Storaro "Vittorio Storaro") won for Best Cinematography. The film received seven Golden Globe nominations, including Best Motion Picture, Director, Actor and Screenplay. Beatty won the [Golden Globe Award for Best Director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Director "Golden Globe Award for Best Director").

Following *Reds*, Beatty did not appear in a film for five years until 1987's *[Ishtar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishtar_(film) "Ishtar (film)")*, written and directed by [Elaine May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elaine_May "Elaine May"). Following severe criticism in press reviews by the new British studio chief [David Puttnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Puttnam "David Puttnam") just prior to its release, the film received mixed reviews and was unimpressive commercially. Puttnam attacked several other over-budget American films greenlighted by his predecessor and was fired shortly thereafter.

Under his second production company, Mulholland Productions, Beatty produced, directed and played the title role of [comic strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic_strip "Comic strip")-based detective [Dick Tracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Tracy "Dick Tracy") in the 1990 [film of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Tracy_(1990_film) "Dick Tracy (1990 film)"). The film received positive reviews and was one of the highest-grossing films of the year. It received seven [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award "Academy Award") nominations, winning three for [Best Art Direction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Production_Design "Academy Award for Best Production Design"), [Best Makeup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Makeup_and_Hairstyling "Academy Award for Best Makeup and Hairstyling"), and [Best Original Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Song "Academy Award for Best Original Song"). It also received four [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award "Golden Globe Award") nominations, including Best Motion Picture.

In 1991, he produced and starred as the real-life gangster [Bugsy Siegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugsy_Siegel "Bugsy Siegel") in the critically acclaimed and commercially successful film *[Bugsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugsy "Bugsy")*, directed by [Barry Levinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Levinson "Barry Levinson"), which was nominated for ten Academy Awards, including Best Picture and Best Actor; it later won two of the awards for Best Art Direction and [Best Costume Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Costume_Design "Academy Award for Best Costume Design"). The film also received eight Golden Globe Award nominations, including Best Motion Picture and Best Actor, winning for Best Motion Picture. Beatty's next film, *[Love Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Affair_(1994_film) "Love Affair (1994 film)")* (1994), directed by [Glenn Gordon Caron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Gordon_Caron "Glenn Gordon Caron"), received mixed reviews and was a commercial failure.

In 1998, Beatty wrote, produced, directed and starred in the [political satire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_satire "Political satire") *[Bulworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulworth "Bulworth")*, which was critically acclaimed and nominated for the [Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Screenplay "Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay"). The film also received three Golden Globe Award nominations, for Best Motion Picture, Best Actor, and Best Screenplay. Beatty has appeared briefly in numerous documentaries, including *[Madonna: Truth or Dare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna:_Truth_or_Dare "Madonna: Truth or Dare")* (1991) and *[One Bright Shining Moment: The Forgotten Summer of George McGovern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Bright_Shining_Moment:_The_Forgotten_Summer_of_George_McGovern "One Bright Shining Moment: The Forgotten Summer of George McGovern")* (2005).

Following the poor box office performance of *[Town & Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_%26_Country_(film) "Town & Country (film)")* (2001), in which Beatty starred, he did not appear in or direct another film for 15 years.

### Since 2002: Output decline

In May 2005, Beatty sued [Tribune Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribune_Media "Tribune Media"), claiming he still maintained the rights to *Dick Tracy*. On March 25, 2011, U.S. District Judge Dean Pregerson ruled in Beatty's favor.

In 2010, Beatty directed and reprised his role as [Dick Tracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Tracy "Dick Tracy") in the 30-minute television special *Dick Tracy Special*, which premiered on [TCM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turner_Classic_Movies "Turner Classic Movies"). The [metafictional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metafiction "Metafiction") special features an interview with Tracy and film critic and historian [Leonard Maltin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Maltin "Leonard Maltin"), the latter of whom discusses the history and creation of Tracy. Tracy talks about how he admired [Ralph Byrd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Byrd "Ralph Byrd") and [Morgan Conway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Conway "Morgan Conway") who portrayed him in several films, but says he didn't care much for Beatty's portrayal of him or [his film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Tracy_(1990_film) "Dick Tracy (1990 film)"). The production of the special allowed Beatty to retain the rights to the character. At CinemaCon In April 2016, Beatty reiterated that he intends to make a *Dick Tracy* sequel. In 2023, Beatty reprised the role of Tracy and played the character opposite himself in *Dick Tracy Special: Tracy Zooms In*, a follow-up to the *Dick Tracy Special* that also aired on TCM. The 30-minute special, which mostly consists of a [Zoom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoom_(software) "Zoom (software)") interview with [Ben Mankiewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Mankiewicz "Ben Mankiewicz") and a returning Maltin in which Tracy criticizes aspects of the 1990 film adaptation to Beatty's face and suggests that a younger actor should take over the role of Tracy, concludes with Beatty and Tracy meeting in person and suggesting that Dick Tracy will return in the future.

Fools and idiots abound, but demonic, systemic evil does not. Mr. Beatty obviously loves Hollywood, which has been good to him.

In the mid-1970s, Beatty signed a contract with [Warner Bros.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros. "Warner Bros.") to star in, produce, write, and possibly direct a film about [Howard Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Hughes "Howard Hughes"). The project was put on hold when Beatty began *[Heaven Can Wait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heaven_Can_Wait_(1978_film) "Heaven Can Wait (1978 film)")*. Initially, Beatty planned to film the life story of [John Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Reed_(journalist) "John Reed (journalist)") and Hughes back-to-back, but as he was getting deeper into the project, he eventually focused primarily on the Reed film *[Reds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reds_(film) "Reds (film)")*. In June 2011, it was reported that Beatty would produce, write, direct and star in a film about Hughes, focusing on an affair he had with a younger woman in the final years of his life. During this period, Beatty interviewed actors to star in his ensemble cast. He met with [Andrew Garfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Garfield "Andrew Garfield"), [Alec Baldwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alec_Baldwin "Alec Baldwin"), [Owen Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Wilson "Owen Wilson"), [Justin Timberlake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Timberlake "Justin Timberlake"), [Shia LaBeouf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_LaBeouf "Shia LaBeouf"), [Jack Nicholson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Nicholson "Jack Nicholson"), [Evan Rachel Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evan_Rachel_Wood "Evan Rachel Wood"), [Rooney Mara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rooney_Mara "Rooney Mara"), and [Felicity Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felicity_Jones "Felicity Jones"). The film was eventually released under the title *[Rules Don't Apply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rules_Don%27t_Apply "Rules Don't Apply")*, a fictionalized true-life romantic comedy set in 1958 Hollywood and Las Vegas. Beatty wrote, co-produced, directed, and starred alongside [Alden Ehrenreich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alden_Ehrenreich "Alden Ehrenreich") and [Lily Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lily_Collins "Lily Collins"), with supporting cast including Baldwin, [Annette Bening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annette_Bening "Annette Bening"), [Matthew Broderick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Broderick "Matthew Broderick"), [Candice Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candice_Bergen "Candice Bergen"), [Ed Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Harris "Ed Harris"), and [Martin Sheen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Sheen "Martin Sheen"). It was released on November 23, 2016, and was Beatty's first film in 15 years. Rotten Tomatoes' "Top Critics" gave the film a 55% "Rotten" rating. The film was also a commercial disappointment.

In 2017, Beatty reunited with his *Bonnie and Clyde* co-star [Faye Dunaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faye_Dunaway "Faye Dunaway") at the [89th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/89th_Academy_Awards "89th Academy Awards"), in celebration of the film's 50th anniversary. After being introduced by [Jimmy Kimmel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Kimmel "Jimmy Kimmel"), they walked out onto the stage to present the [Best Picture Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture"). They had been given the wrong envelope, leading Dunaway to incorrectly announce *[La La Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_La_Land_(film) "La La Land (film)")* as Best Picture, instead of the actual winner, *[Moonlight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moonlight_(2016_film) "Moonlight (2016 film)")*. This became a social media sensation, trending all over the world. In 2018, Beatty and Dunaway returned to present Best Picture at the [90th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/90th_Academy_Awards "90th Academy Awards"), earning a standing ovation upon their entrance, making jokes about the previous year's flub. Without incident, Beatty announced *[The Shape of Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Shape_of_Water "The Shape of Water")* as the winner.

## Personal life

Beatty has been married to actress [Annette Bening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annette_Bening "Annette Bening") since 1992. They have four children, including actress [Ella Beatty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ella_Beatty "Ella Beatty").

Prior to marrying Bening, Beatty was notorious for his large number of romantic relationships that received generous media coverage, and having been linked to over 100 female celebrities. Early in his career, Beatty was engaged to [Joan Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Collins "Joan Collins"); he later referred to the engagement as "an exaggeration." [Cher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cher "Cher"), who briefly dated him, stated that "Warren has probably been with everybody I know." [Leslie Caron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Caron "Leslie Caron") said "Warren always had girlfriends who resembled his sister [Shirley MacLaine]". Caron thought he was too self-centered, and refused his marriage proposals. [Carly Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carly_Simon "Carly Simon") revealed in 2015 that the second verse of her 1972 song "[You're So Vain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You%27re_So_Vain "You're So Vain")" was about a previous romantic relationship with Beatty.

### Activism

Beatty was a founding board member of the [Center for National Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_National_Policy "Center for National Policy"), a founding member of the [Progressive Majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Majority "Progressive Majority"), a member of the [Council on Foreign Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_on_Foreign_Relations "Council on Foreign Relations"), has served as the Campaign Chair for the Permanent Charities Committee, and has participated in the [World Economic Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Economic_Forum "World Economic Forum") at [Davos, Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davos,_Switzerland "Davos, Switzerland"). He served on the Board of Trustees at the [Scripps Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scripps_Research_Institute "Scripps Research Institute"), and the Board of Directors of the [Motion Picture and Television Fund Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_Picture_and_Television_Fund_Foundation "Motion Picture and Television Fund Foundation"). He was named Honorary Chairman of the [Stella Adler Studio of Acting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_Adler_Studio_of_Acting "Stella Adler Studio of Acting") in 2004. Director and collaborator [Arthur Penn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Penn "Arthur Penn") described Beatty as "the perfect producer", adding, "He makes everyone demand the best of themselves. Warren stays with a picture through editing, mixing, and scoring. He plain works harder than anyone else I have ever seen."

### Political views

Beatty is a longtime supporter of the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)"). In 1972, he was part of the "inner circle" of Senator [George McGovern's presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_McGovern_presidential_campaign,_1972 "George McGovern presidential campaign, 1972"). He traveled extensively and was instrumental in organizing fundraising. Despite differences in politics, Beatty was also a friend of Republican Senator [John McCain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCain "John McCain"), with whom he agreed on the need for campaign finance reform. He was chosen by McCain to be one of the pallbearers at the senator's funeral in 2018.
