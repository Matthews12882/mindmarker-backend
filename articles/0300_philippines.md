# Philippines

The **Philippines**, officially the **Republic of the Philippines**, is an [archipelagic country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelagic_state "Archipelagic state") in [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia"). Located in the western [Pacific Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean "Pacific Ocean"), it consists of about [7,641 islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_the_Philippines "List of islands of the Philippines"), with a total area of about 300,000 square kilometers. The islands are broadly categorized into [three main geographical divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_groups_of_the_Philippines "Island groups of the Philippines"): from north to south, [Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzon "Luzon"), [Visayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visayas "Visayas"), and [Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindanao "Mindanao"). With a population of over 114 million, it is the world's [thirteenth-most-populous country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population "List of countries and dependencies by population").

The Philippines is bounded by the [South China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_Sea "South China Sea") to the west, the [Philippine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Sea "Philippine Sea") to the east, and the [Celebes Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebes_Sea "Celebes Sea") to the south. It shares [maritime borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_border "Maritime border") with [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan") to the north, [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan") to the northeast, [Palau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau "Palau") to southeast, [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia") to the south, [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia") to the southwest, [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam "Vietnam") to the west, and [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") to the northwest. It has [diverse ethnicities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_the_Philippines "Ethnic groups in the Philippines") and [a rich culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_the_Philippines "Culture of the Philippines"). [Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila "Manila") is [the country's capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_of_the_Philippines "Capital of the Philippines"), and [its most populated city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines#Largest_cities "Cities of the Philippines") is [Quezon City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quezon_City "Quezon City"). Both are within [Metro Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Manila "Metro Manila").

[Negritos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negrito "Negrito"), the archipelago's earliest inhabitants, were followed by [waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Models_of_migration_to_the_Philippines "Models of migration to the Philippines") of [Austronesian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples "Austronesian peoples"). The adoption of [animism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animism "Animism"), [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism") with [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist "Buddhist") influence, and [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam") established [island-kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_(900%E2%80%931565) "History of the Philippines (900–1565)"). Extensive overseas trade with neighbors such as the late [Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty "Tang dynasty") or [Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Song "Southern Song") empire brought [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangley "Sangley") people to the archipelago as well, who would also gradually settle in and [intermix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interethnic_marriage "Interethnic marriage") over the centuries. The arrival of the explorer [Ferdinand Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Magellan "Ferdinand Magellan") marked the beginning of [Spanish colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Colonization_in_the_Philippines "Spanish Colonization in the Philippines"). In 1543, Spanish explorer [Ruy López de Villalobos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruy_L%C3%B3pez_de_Villalobos "Ruy López de Villalobos") named the archipelago *las Islas Filipinas* in honor of [King Philip II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain "Philip II of Spain"). [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church "Catholic Church") became the dominant religion, and Manila became the western hub of [trans-Pacific trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_treasure_fleet "Spanish treasure fleet"). [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaniard "Spaniard") immigrants from [Latin America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_Asian "Latin American Asian") and [Iberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberia "Iberia") would also selectively colonize. The [Philippine Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolution "Philippine Revolution") began in 1896, and became entwined with the 1898 [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War "Spanish–American War"). Spain ceded the territory to the United States, and [Filipino revolutionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_Junta "Hong Kong Junta") declared the [First Philippine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Philippine_Republic "First Philippine Republic"). The ensuing [Philippine–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine%E2%80%93American_War "Philippine–American War") ended with the United States controlling the territory until the [Japanese invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_campaign_(1941%E2%80%931942) "Philippines campaign (1941–1942)") of the islands during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). After [the United States retook the Philippines from the Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_campaign_(1944%E2%80%931945) "Philippines campaign (1944–1945)"), the Philippines became independent in 1946. Since then, the country notably experienced a [period of martial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_under_Ferdinand_Marcos "Martial law under Ferdinand Marcos") from 1972 to 1981 under the dictatorship of [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos "Ferdinand Marcos") and his subsequent overthrow by the [People Power Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Power_Revolution "People Power Revolution") in 1986, returning to democracy.

The Philippines is an [emerging market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerging_market "Emerging market") and a [developing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_country "Developing country") and [newly industrialized country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newly_industrialized_country "Newly industrialized country"), [whose economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_Philippines "Economy of the Philippines") is transitioning from being agricultural to service- and manufacturing-centered. It has a variety of natural resources and a globally-significant [level of biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megadiverse_countries "Megadiverse countries"). The country is also part of [multiple international organizations and forums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_organization_membership_of_the_Philippines "International organization membership of the Philippines"), mainly in the [Asia-Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific "Asia-Pacific") region. Despite its fast economic growth, it continues to struggle with [inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_inequality_in_the_Philippines "Income inequality in the Philippines"), widespread [corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_the_Philippines "Corruption in the Philippines"), and vulnerability to natural disasters due to its location both within the Pacific [Ring of Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_Fire "Ring of Fire") and near the [equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator "Equator"), making it prone to [earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_earthquakes_in_the_Philippines "List of earthquakes in the Philippines"), [monsoon rains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon "Monsoon"), and [typhoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoons_in_the_Philippines "Typhoons in the Philippines"), to which the Philippines has built a [resilience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_resilience "Community resilience").

## Etymology

During his 1542 expedition, Spanish explorer [Ruy López de Villalobos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruy_L%C3%B3pez_de_Villalobos "Ruy López de Villalobos") named the islands of [Leyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyte "Leyte") and [Samar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samar "Samar") "*Felipinas*" after the [Prince of Asturias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Asturias "Prince of Asturias"), later [Philip II of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Castile "Philip II of Castile"). Eventually, the name "*Las Islas Filipinas*" would be used for the archipelago's Spanish possessions. Other names, such as "*Islas del Poniente*" (Western Islands), "*Islas del Oriente*" (Eastern Islands), Ferdinand Magellan's name, and "*San Lázaro*" (Islands of St. Lazarus), were used by the Spanish to refer to islands in the region before Spanish rule was established.

During the [Philippine Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolution "Philippine Revolution"), the [Malolos Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malolos_Congress "Malolos Congress") proclaimed it the *República Filipina* ([Philippine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Philippine_Republic "First Philippine Republic")) in 1899. American colonial authorities referred to the country as the Philippine Islands (a translation of the Spanish name). The [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") began changing its nomenclature from "the Philippine Islands" to "the Philippines" in the Philippine Autonomy Act and the [Jones Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jones_Law_(Philippines) "Jones Law (Philippines)"). The official title "Republic of the Philippines" was included in the 1935 constitution as the name of the future independent state, and in all succeeding constitutional revisions.

## History

### Prehistory (pre–900)

There is [evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology_of_the_Philippines "Archaeology of the Philippines") of early [hominins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominins "Hominins") living in what is now the Philippines as early as 709,000 years ago. Bones from [Callao Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callao_Cave "Callao Cave") potentially represent an otherwise unknown species, *[Homo luzonensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_luzonensis "Homo luzonensis")*, who lived 50,000 to 67,000 years ago. The oldest [modern human](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_human "Modern human") remains on the islands are from the [Tabon Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabon_Caves "Tabon Caves") of [Palawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palawan "Palawan"), [U/Th-dated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U/Th-dated "U/Th-dated") to 47,000 ± 11–10,000 years ago. [Tabon Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabon_Man "Tabon Man") is presumably a [Negrito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negrito "Negrito"), among the archipelago's earliest inhabitants descended from the first human migrations out of Africa via the coastal route along [southern Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia") to the now-sunken landmasses of [Sundaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundaland "Sundaland") and [Sahul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahul "Sahul").

The first Austronesians reached the Philippines from Taiwan around 2200 BC, settling the [Batanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batanes "Batanes") Islands (where they built stone fortresses known as *[ijangs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ijang "Ijang")*) and northern [Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzon "Luzon"). Jade artifacts have been dated to 2000 BC, with [lingling-o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingling-o "Lingling-o") jade items made in Luzon with raw materials from Taiwan. By 1000 BC, the inhabitants of the archipelago had developed into four societies: [hunter-gatherer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer "Hunter-gatherer") tribes, warrior societies, highland [plutocracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutocracies "Plutocracies"), and port principalities. The Philippines prior to foreign colonization was believed to be egalitarian.

### Early states (900–1565)

The earliest known surviving written record in the Philippines is the 900 AD [Laguna Copperplate Inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_Copperplate_Inscription "Laguna Copperplate Inscription"), which was written in [Old Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Malay "Old Malay") using the early [Kawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawi_alphabet "Kawi alphabet") script. By the 14th century, several large coastal settlements emerged as trading centers and became the focus of [societal changes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_achievements_of_pre-colonial_Philippines "Cultural achievements of pre-colonial Philippines"). Some [polities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polities "Polities") had exchanges with other states throughout Asia. Trade with China began during the late [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty "Tang dynasty"), and expanded during the [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty "Song dynasty"). Throughout the second millennium AD, some polities were part of the [tributary system of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary_system_of_China "Tributary system of China"). With extensive trade and diplomacy, this brought [Southern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_and_southern_China "Northern and southern China") [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese "Han Chinese") merchants and migrants from [Southern Fujian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Fujian "Southern Fujian"), who would gradually settle and intermix in the Philippines. Indian cultural traits such as linguistic terms and religious practices [began to spread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_influences_in_early_Philippine_polities "Indian influences in early Philippine polities") in the Philippines during the 14th century, via the Indianized Hindu [Majapahit Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majapahit "Majapahit"). By the 15th century, Islam was established in the [Sulu Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Archipelago "Sulu Archipelago") and spread from there.

Polities founded in the Philippines between the 10th and 16th centuries include [Maynila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maynila_(historical_polity) "Maynila (historical polity)"), [Tondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tondo_(historical_polity) "Tondo (historical polity)"), [Namayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namayan "Namayan"), [Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_(historical_polity) "Pangasinan (historical polity)"), [Caboloan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caboloan "Caboloan"), [Cebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebu_(historical_state) "Cebu (historical state)"), [Butuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butuan_(historical_polity) "Butuan (historical polity)"), [Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Maguindanao "Sultanate of Maguindanao"), [Lanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_Lanao "Confederate States of Lanao"), [Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu "Sultanate of Sulu"), and [Ma-i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma-i "Ma-i"). The early polities typically had a three-tier social structure: nobility, freemen, and dependent debtor-bondsmen. Among the nobility were leaders known as [datus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datu "Datu"), who were responsible for ruling autonomous groups ([barangays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precolonial_barangay "Precolonial barangay") or dulohan). When the barangays banded together to form a larger settlement or a geographically looser alliance, their more-esteemed members would be recognized as a "[paramount datu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_rulers_in_early_Philippine_history "Paramount rulers in early Philippine history")", [rajah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajah "Rajah") or [sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan "Sultan"), and would rule the community. Population density is thought to have been low during the 14th to 16th centuries due to the [frequency of typhoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon#Frequency "Typhoon") and the Philippines' location on the Pacific [Ring of Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_Fire "Ring of Fire"). Portuguese explorer [Ferdinand Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Magellan "Ferdinand Magellan") arrived in 1521, claimed the islands for Spain, and was killed by [Lapulapu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapulapu "Lapulapu")'s men in the [Battle of Mactan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mactan "Battle of Mactan").

### Spanish and American colonial rule (1565–1934)

Unification and colonization by the [Crown of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castile "Crown of Castile") began when Spanish explorer [Miguel López de Legazpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_L%C3%B3pez_de_Legazpi "Miguel López de Legazpi") arrived from [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain "New Spain") in 1565. Many [Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipinos "Filipinos") were brought to New Spain [as slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Spanish_slavery_in_the_Philippines "History of Spanish slavery in the Philippines") and forced crew, whereas many [Latin Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_Asian "Latin American Asian") were brought to the Philippines as soldiers and colonists. The Philippines hosts the only Latin American established districts in Asia. [Spanish Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intramuros "Intramuros") became the capital of the [Captaincy General of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captaincy_General_of_the_Philippines "Captaincy General of the Philippines") and the [Spanish East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_East_Indies "Spanish East Indies") in 1571, the Spanish territories in Asia and the Pacific. The Spanish invaded local states using the principle of [divide and conquer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divide_and_rule "Divide and rule"), bringing most of what is the present-day Philippines under one unified administration.

Disparate barangays were deliberately [consolidated into towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reductions "Reductions"), where [Catholic missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friars_in_Spanish_Philippines "Friars in Spanish Philippines") could more easily convert their inhabitants to [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity"), which was initially [syncretist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Syncretism "Religious Syncretism"). [Christianization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization "Christianization") by the Spanish friars occurred mostly across the settled lowlands over the course of time. From 1565 to 1821, the Philippines was governed as a territory of the [Mexico City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico_City "Mexico City")-based Viceroyalty of New Spain; it was then administered from [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid "Madrid") after the [Mexican War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_War_of_Independence "Mexican War of Independence"). Manila became the western hub of [trans-Pacific trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_treasure_fleet "Spanish treasure fleet") by [Manila galleons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_galleon "Manila galleon") built in [Bicol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicol_Region "Bicol Region") and [Cavite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavite "Cavite").

During its rule, Spain spent a significant sum of its treasury quelling [indigenous revolts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_revolts_against_Spain "Philippine revolts against Spain") and defending against external military attacks, including [Moro piracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piracy_in_the_Sulu_and_Celebes_Seas "Piracy in the Sulu and Celebes Seas"), a 17th-century [war against the Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_of_La_Naval_de_Manila "Battles of La Naval de Manila"), 18th-century [British occupation of Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_occupation_of_Manila "British occupation of Manila"), and conflict with Muslims in the south.

Administration of the Philippines was considered a drain on the economy of New Spain, and abandoning it or trading it for other territory was debated. This course of action was opposed because of the islands' economic potential, security, and the desire to continue religious conversion in the region. The colony survived on an annual subsidy from the Spanish crown averaging 250,000 pesos, usually paid as 75 tons of silver bullion from the Americas. [British forces occupied Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_occupation_of_Manila "British occupation of Manila") from 1762 to 1764 during the [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years%27_War "Seven Years' War"). Spanish rule was restored with the [1763 Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1763) "Treaty of Paris (1763)"). The Spanish considered their war with the Muslims in Southeast Asia an extension of the *[Reconquista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconquista "Reconquista")*. The [Spanish–Moro conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93Moro_conflict "Spanish–Moro conflict") lasted for several hundred years. Spain conquered portions of [Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindanao "Mindanao") and [Jolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolo "Jolo") during the last quarter of the 19th century, and the Muslim [Moro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_people "Moro people") in the [Sultanate of Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu "Sultanate of Sulu") acknowledged Spanish sovereignty.

Philippine ports opened to world trade during the 19th century, and Filipino society began to change. Social identity changed, with the term *Filipino* encompassing all residents of the archipelago instead of solely referring to [Spaniards born in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Filipino "Spanish Filipino").

Revolutionary sentiment grew in 1872 after 200 locally recruited [colonial troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_troops "Colonial troops") and laborers alongside [three activist Catholic priests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomburza "Gomburza") were executed on [questionable grounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1872_Cavite_mutiny "1872 Cavite mutiny"). This inspired the [Propaganda Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_Movement "Propaganda Movement"), organized by [Marcelo H. del Pilar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcelo_H._del_Pilar "Marcelo H. del Pilar"), [José Rizal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Rizal "José Rizal"), [Graciano López Jaena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graciano_L%C3%B3pez_Jaena "Graciano López Jaena"), and [Mariano Ponce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariano_Ponce "Mariano Ponce"), which advocated political reform in the Philippines. Rizal was executed on December 30, 1896, for rebellion, and his death radicalized many who had been loyal to Spain. Attempts at reform met with resistance; [Andrés Bonifacio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Bonifacio "Andrés Bonifacio") founded the [Katipunan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katipunan "Katipunan") secret society, which sought independence from Spain through armed revolt, in 1892.

The Katipunan [Cry of Pugad Lawin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cry_of_Pugad_Lawin "Cry of Pugad Lawin") began the [Philippine Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolution "Philippine Revolution") in 1896. Internal disputes led to the [Tejeros Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tejeros_Convention "Tejeros Convention"), at which Bonifacio lost his position and [Emilio Aguinaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilio_Aguinaldo "Emilio Aguinaldo") was elected the new leader of the revolution. The 1897 [Pact of Biak-na-Bato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pact_of_Biak-na-Bato "Pact of Biak-na-Bato") resulted in the [Hong Kong Junta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_Junta "Hong Kong Junta") government in exile. The [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War "Spanish–American War") began the following year, and reached the Philippines; Aguinaldo returned, resumed the revolution, and [declared independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Declaration_of_Independence "Philippine Declaration of Independence") from Spain on June 12, 1898. In December 1898, the islands were [ceded by Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1898) "Treaty of Paris (1898)") to the United States with [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico "Puerto Rico") and [Guam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guam "Guam") after the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War "Spanish–American War"). [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain "Spain") had ruled the Philippines for 333 years.

The [First Philippine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Philippine_Republic "First Philippine Republic") was promulgated on January 21, 1899. Lack of recognition by the United States led to an [outbreak of hostilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Manila_(1899) "Battle of Manila (1899)") that, after refusal by the U.S. on-scene military commander of a cease-fire proposal and a declaration of war by the nascent Republic, escalated into the [Philippine–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine%E2%80%93American_War "Philippine–American War").

The war resulted in the deaths of 250,000 to 1 million civilians, primarily due to famine and disease. Many Filipinos were transported by the Americans to [concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_concentration_and_internment_camps#Philippines "List of concentration and internment camps"), where thousands died. After the fall of the First Philippine Republic in 1902, an [American civilian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insular_Government_of_the_Philippine_Islands "Insular Government of the Philippine Islands") was established with the [Philippine Organic Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Organic_Act_(1902) "Philippine Organic Act (1902)"). American forces continued to secure and extend their control of the islands, suppressing an attempted [extension of the Philippine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_Republic#Sakay "Tagalog Republic"), [securing the Sultanate of Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiram%E2%80%93Bates_Treaty "Kiram–Bates Treaty"), establishing control of interior mountainous areas which had resisted Spanish conquest, and encouraging large-scale resettlement of Christians in once-predominantly-Muslim Mindanao.

Rather than actively resisting or being transformed by American colonial rule, Filipino elites neutralized intended political reforms by "domesticating" US tutelary institutions through their own preexisting cultural schemas, thereby reproducing traditional power dynamics.

### Commonwealth and World War II (1935–1946)

Cultural developments in the Philippines strengthened a national identity, and [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language "Tagalog language") began to take precedence over other local languages. Governmental functions were gradually given to Filipinos by the [Taft Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taft_Commission "Taft Commission"); the 1934 [Tydings–McDuffie Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tydings%E2%80%93McDuffie_Act "Tydings–McDuffie Act") granted a ten-year transition to independence through the creation of the Commonwealth of the Philippines the following year, with [Manuel Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_L._Quezon "Manuel L. Quezon") president and [Sergio Osmeña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Osme%C3%B1a "Sergio Osmeña") vice president. Quezon's priorities were defence, social justice, inequality, economic diversification, and national character. [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language "Filipino language") (a standardized variety of Tagalog) became the national language, [women's suffrage was introduced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1937_Philippine_women%27s_suffrage_plebiscite "1937 Philippine women's suffrage plebiscite"), and [land reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reform_in_the_Philippines "Land reform in the Philippines") was considered. The introduction of women's suffrage signified a progressive step towards [gender equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_equality "Gender equality") in the Philippines. It gave women the right to vote and participate in the country's political processes.

The [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan "Empire of Japan") invaded the Philippines in December 1941 [during World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_the_Philippines_during_World_War_II "Military history of the Philippines during World War II"), and the [Second Philippine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Philippine_Republic "Second Philippine Republic") was established as a [puppet state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_state "Puppet state") governed by [Jose P. Laurel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_P._Laurel "Jose P. Laurel"). Beginning in 1942, the [Japanese occupation of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_the_Philippines "Japanese occupation of the Philippines") was [opposed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_resistance_against_Japan "Philippine resistance against Japan") by large-scale [underground guerrilla activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_the_Philippines#Resistance "Japanese occupation of the Philippines"). [Atrocities and war crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_war_crimes "Japanese war crimes") were committed during the war, including the [Bataan Death March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bataan_Death_March "Bataan Death March") and the [Manila massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_massacre "Manila massacre"). The Philippine resistance and Allied troops [defeated the Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_campaign_(1944%E2%80%931945) "Philippines campaign (1944–1945)") in 1944 and 1945. Over one million Filipinos were estimated to have died by the end of the war. On October 11, 1945, the Philippines became a [founding member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_states_of_the_United_Nations "Member states of the United Nations") of the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations"). On July 4, 1946, during the presidency of [Manuel Roxas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Roxas "Manuel Roxas"), the country's independence was recognized by the United States with the [Treaty of Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Manila_(1946) "Treaty of Manila (1946)").

The Tydings-McDuffie Act was signed by U.S. President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt "Franklin D. Roosevelt") on March 24, 1934 in the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression_in_the_United_States "Great Depression in the United States") period. When the act was signed, [Manuel L. Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_L._Quezon "Manuel L. Quezon") became the 2nd President of the Philippines, which made the Philippines a Commonwealth after [Emilio Aguinaldo y Famy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilio_Aguinaldo "Emilio Aguinaldo"). President [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman "Harry S. Truman") signed Proclamation 2695 and Truman authorized [Paul V. McNutt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_V._McNutt "Paul V. McNutt") to proclaim the independence of the Philippines in the [Treaty of Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Manila_(1946) "Treaty of Manila (1946)") on July 4, 1946 following the [Tydings-McDuffie Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tydings%E2%80%93McDuffie_Act "Tydings–McDuffie Act").

### Independence (1946–present)

Efforts at post-war reconstruction and ending the [Hukbalahap Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hukbalahap_Rebellion "Hukbalahap Rebellion") succeeded during [Ramon Magsaysay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Magsaysay "Ramon Magsaysay")'s presidency, but sporadic communist insurgency continued to flare up long afterward. Under Magsaysay's successor, [Carlos P. Garcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_P._Garcia "Carlos P. Garcia"), the government initiated a [Filipino First policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_First_policy "Filipino First policy") which promoted Filipino-owned businesses. Succeeding Garcia, [Diosdado Macapagal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diosdado_Macapagal "Diosdado Macapagal") moved Independence Day from July 4 to June 12—the date of Emilio Aguinaldo's declaration— and pursued [a claim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Borneo_dispute "North Borneo dispute") on eastern [North Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Borneo "North Borneo").

In 1965, Macapagal [lost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_Philippine_presidential_election "1965 Philippine presidential election") the presidential election to [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos "Ferdinand Marcos"). [Early in his presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_term_of_the_presidency_of_Ferdinand_Marcos "First term of the presidency of Ferdinand Marcos"), Marcos began infrastructure projects funded mostly by foreign loans; this improved the economy, and contributed to his [reelection in 1969](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Philippine_presidential_election "1969 Philippine presidential election"). Near the end of his last constitutionally-permitted term, Marcos [declared martial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_No._1081 "Proclamation No. 1081") on September 21, 1972 using the specter of communism and began to [rule by decree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_by_decree "Rule by decree"); the period was characterized by [political repression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_detainees_under_the_Marcos_dictatorship "Political detainees under the Marcos dictatorship"), [censorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journalism_during_the_Marcos_dictatorship "Journalism during the Marcos dictatorship"), and [human rights violations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_abuses_of_the_Marcos_dictatorship "Human rights abuses of the Marcos dictatorship"). [Monopolies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopolies_in_the_Philippines_(1965%E2%80%931986) "Monopolies in the Philippines (1965–1986)") controlled by [Marcos's cronies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cronies_of_Ferdinand_Marcos "Cronies of Ferdinand Marcos") were established in key industries, including [logging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation_in_the_Philippines#Deforestation_during_the_martial_law_era "Deforestation in the Philippines") and broadcasting; a sugar monopoly led to [a famine on the island of Negros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros_famine "Negros famine"). With his wife, [Imelda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imelda_Marcos "Imelda Marcos"), Marcos was accused of corruption and [embezzling billions of dollars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unexplained_wealth_of_the_Marcos_family "Unexplained wealth of the Marcos family") of public funds. Marcos's heavy borrowing [early in his presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Philippine_balance_of_payments_crisis "1969 Philippine balance of payments crisis") resulted in [economic crashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_history_of_the_Philippines_(1965%E2%80%931986) "Economic history of the Philippines (1965–1986)"), exacerbated by an [early 1980s recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_1980s_recession "Early 1980s recession") where the economy contracted by 7.3 percent annually in 1984 and 1985.

On August 21, 1983, opposition leader [Benigno Aquino Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninoy_Aquino "Ninoy Aquino") was [assassinated on the tarmac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Ninoy_Aquino "Assassination of Ninoy Aquino") at [Manila International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninoy_Aquino_International_Airport "Ninoy Aquino International Airport"). Marcos called a snap [presidential election in 1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Philippine_presidential_election "1986 Philippine presidential election") which proclaimed him the winner, but the results were widely regarded as fraudulent. The resulting protests led to the [People Power Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Power_Revolution "People Power Revolution"), which forced Marcos and his allies to flee to [Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii "Hawaii"). Aquino's widow, [Corazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corazon_Aquino "Corazon Aquino"), was installed as president and a new constitution was promulgated.

The return of democracy and government reforms which began in 1986 were hampered by [national debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_debt_of_the_Philippines "National debt of the Philippines"), government corruption, and [coup attempts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup_attempts_against_Corazon_Aquino "Coup attempts against Corazon Aquino"). A [communist insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_rebellion_in_the_Philippines "Communist rebellion in the Philippines") and military conflict with [Moro separatists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_conflict "Moro conflict") persisted; the administration also faced a series of disasters, including the eruption of [Mount Pinatubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pinatubo "Mount Pinatubo") in June 1991. Aquino was succeeded by [Fidel V. Ramos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidel_V._Ramos "Fidel V. Ramos"), who [liberalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalization "Economic liberalization") the national economy with [privatization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatization "Privatization") and [deregulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deregulation "Deregulation"). Ramos's economic gains were overshadowed by the onset of the [1997 Asian financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Asian_financial_crisis "1997 Asian financial crisis"). His successor, [Joseph Estrada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Estrada "Joseph Estrada"), prioritized public housing but faced corruption allegations which led to his overthrow by the [2001 EDSA Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_EDSA_Revolution "Second EDSA Revolution") and the succession of Vice President [Gloria Macapagal Arroyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Macapagal_Arroyo "Gloria Macapagal Arroyo") on January 20, 2001.

Arroyo's [nine-year administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Gloria_Macapagal_Arroyo "Presidency of Gloria Macapagal Arroyo") was marked by economic growth, but was tainted by corruption and political scandals, including [electoral fraud allegations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hello_Garci_scandal "Hello Garci scandal") during the [2004 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Philippine_presidential_election "2004 Philippine presidential election"). Economic growth continued during [Benigno Aquino III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benigno_Aquino_III "Benigno Aquino III")'s administration, which advocated good governance and transparency. Aquino III signed [a peace agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_Agreement_on_the_Bangsamoro "Comprehensive Agreement on the Bangsamoro") with the [Moro Islamic Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_Islamic_Liberation_Front "Moro Islamic Liberation Front") (MILF) resulting in the [Bangsamoro Organic Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro_Organic_Law "Bangsamoro Organic Law") establishing an autonomous [Bangsamoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro "Bangsamoro") region, but a [shootout with MILF rebels in Mamasapano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamasapano_clash "Mamasapano clash") delayed passage of the law.

Growing public frustration with post-[EDSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Power_Revolution "People Power Revolution") governance led to the [2016 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Philippine_presidential_election "2016 Philippine presidential election") of populist [Rodrigo Duterte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigo_Duterte "Rodrigo Duterte"), whose [presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Rodrigo_Duterte "Presidency of Rodrigo Duterte") saw the decline of [liberalism in the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism_in_the_Philippines "Liberalism in the Philippines") albeit largely retaining liberal economic policies. Among Duterte's priorities was [aggressively increasing infrastructure spending](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Build!_Build!_Build! "Build! Build! Build!") to spur economic growth; the enactment of the Bangsamoro Organic Law; an intensified crackdown on crime and communist insurgencies; and [an anti-drug campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_drug_war "Philippine drug war") that reduced drug proliferation but that has also led to [extrajudicial killings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrajudicial_killing "Extrajudicial killing"). In early 2020, the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_Philippines "COVID-19 pandemic in the Philippines") reached the Philippines, necessitating [nationwide lockdowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_community_quarantines_in_the_Philippines "COVID-19 community quarantines in the Philippines") that caused a brief but severe [economic recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_recession "COVID-19 recession"). Under a promise of continuing Duterte's policies, Marcos's son, [Bongbong Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bongbong_Marcos "Bongbong Marcos"), ran with Duterte's daughter, [Sara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Duterte "Sara Duterte"), and won the [2022 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Philippine_presidential_election "2022 Philippine presidential election"). Marcos's renewal of a pro-US foreign policy, however, has been viewed as a reversal of Duterte's cordiality with China, and [territorial disputes in the South China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_disputes_in_the_South_China_Sea "Territorial disputes in the South China Sea") have since escalated. The Marcos administration also [arrested](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrest_of_Rodrigo_Duterte "Arrest of Rodrigo Duterte") his predecessor Duterte, who was sent to the [International Criminal Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court "International Criminal Court") in the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands "Netherlands") for trial of [crimes against humanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimes_against_humanity "Crimes against humanity"), which led to the collapse of the Marcos-Duterte alliance. Later on, a series of [corruption, mismanagement and irregularities in government-funded flood management projects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flood_control_projects_controversy_in_the_Philippines "Flood control projects controversy in the Philippines") occurred under his administration, resulting in [numerous protests nationwide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_2025_Philippine_protests "September 2025 Philippine protests"). The country suffered an [energy crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_energy_crisis_in_the_Philippines "2026 energy crisis in the Philippines") in 2026 as a result of the [conflict in Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war "2026 Iran war"). The first [parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Bangsamoro_Parliament_election "2026 Bangsamoro Parliament election") in the Bangsamoro region was held on September 14, 2026. The Marcos administration saw the [Philippine peso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_peso "Philippine peso") hit its historic low at ₱63.15 per US dollar, as a result of the energy crisis, seasonal typhoons, governance weakness and widespread corruption.

## Geography

The Philippines is an archipelago of approximately [7,641 islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_the_Philippines "List of islands of the Philippines"), covering a total area (including inland bodies of water) of about 300,000 square kilometers (115,831 sq mi). Stretching 1,850 kilometers (1,150 mi) north to south, from the [South China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_Sea "South China Sea") to the [Celebes Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebes_Sea "Celebes Sea"), the Philippines [is bordered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borders_of_the_Philippines "Borders of the Philippines") by the [Philippine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Sea "Philippine Sea") to the east, and the [Sulu Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Sea "Sulu Sea") to the southwest. The [Luzon Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzon_Strait "Luzon Strait") is the most economical (quickest) traffic node for shipping between the [Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americas "Americas") and [Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia "Asia").
It is also a critical [junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_junction "Electrical junction") of Asia-Pacific-Americas [undersea communication cables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undersea_communication_cable "Undersea communication cable"). The [country's 11 largest islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_the_Philippines "List of islands of the Philippines") are [Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzon "Luzon"), [Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindanao "Mindanao"), [Samar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samar "Samar"), [Negros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros "Negros"), [Palawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palawan_(island) "Palawan (island)"), [Panay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panay "Panay"), [Mindoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindoro "Mindoro"), [Leyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyte "Leyte"), [Cebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebu_(island) "Cebu (island)"), [Bohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohol "Bohol") and [Masbate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masbate_(island) "Masbate (island)"), about 95 percent of its total land area. The Philippines' coastline measures 36,289 kilometers (22,549 mi), the world's [fifth-longest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_length_of_coastline "List of countries by length of coastline"), and the [country's exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone_of_the_Philippines "Exclusive economic zone of the Philippines") covers 2,263,816 km2 (874,064 sq mi).

Its [highest mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mountains_in_the_Philippines "List of mountains in the Philippines") is [Mount Apo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Apo "Mount Apo") on Mindanao, with an altitude of 2,954 meters (9,692 ft) above sea level. The Philippines' [longest river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rivers_of_the_Philippines "List of rivers of the Philippines") is the [Cagayan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_River "Cagayan River") in northern Luzon, which flows for about 520 kilometers (320 mi). [Manila Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Bay "Manila Bay"), on which is the capital city of [Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila "Manila"), is connected to [Laguna de Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_de_Bay "Laguna de Bay") (the country's [largest lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_lakes_of_the_Philippines "List of lakes of the Philippines")) by the [Pasig River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasig_River "Pasig River").

On the western fringes of the Pacific [Ring of Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_Fire "Ring of Fire"), the Philippines has frequent seismic and volcanic activity. The region is [seismically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seismology "Seismology") active, and has been [constructed by plates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subduction_tectonics_of_the_Philippines "Subduction tectonics of the Philippines") converging towards each other from multiple directions. About five earthquakes are recorded daily, although most are too weak to be felt. The [last major earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_earthquakes_in_the_Philippines "List of earthquakes in the Philippines") were in 1976 in the [Moro Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Moro_Gulf_earthquake "1976 Moro Gulf earthquake") and in 1990 on [Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Luzon_earthquake "1990 Luzon earthquake"). The Philippines has [23 active volcanoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_active_volcanoes_in_the_Philippines "List of active volcanoes in the Philippines"); of them, [Mayon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayon "Mayon"), [Taal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taal_Volcano "Taal Volcano"), [Canlaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Canlaon "Mount Canlaon"), and [Bulusan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Bulusan "Mount Bulusan") have the largest number of recorded eruptions.

The country has valuable mineral deposits as a result of its complex geologic structure and high level of seismic activity. It is thought to have the world's second-largest gold deposits (after [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa "South Africa")), large copper deposits, and the world's largest deposits of [palladium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palladium "Palladium"). The country's gold production in 2015 is 21 metric tonnes. Other minerals include [chromium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromium "Chromium"), [nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel "Nickel"), [molybdenum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molybdenum "Molybdenum"), [platinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum "Platinum"), and [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc "Zinc"). However, poor management and law enforcement, opposition from indigenous communities, and past environmental damage have left these resources largely untapped.

### Biodiversity

The Philippines is a [megadiverse country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megadiverse_countries "Megadiverse countries"), with some of the world's highest rates of discovery and [endemism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ecoregions_with_high_endemism "List of ecoregions with high endemism") (67 percent). With an [estimated 13,500 plant species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora_of_the_Philippines "Flora of the Philippines") in the country (3,500 of which are endemic), Philippine rain forests have an array of flora: about 3,500 species of trees, 8,000 [flowering plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flowering_plant "Flowering plant") species, 1,100 [ferns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fern "Fern"), and 998 [orchid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_orchids_of_the_Philippines "List of the orchids of the Philippines") species have been identified. The Philippines has 167 terrestrial [mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal "Mammal") (102 endemic species), 235 [reptiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reptile "Reptile") (160 endemic species), 99 [amphibians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibian "Amphibian") (74 endemic species), 686 [birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_birds_of_the_Philippines "List of birds of the Philippines") (224 endemic species), and over 20,000 [insect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insect "Insect") species.

As an important part of the [Coral Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_Triangle "Coral Triangle") ecoregion, Philippine waters have unique, diverse marine life and the world's greatest diversity of shore-fish species. The country has over 3,200 fish species, 121 endemic. Philippine waters sustain [the cultivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquaculture_in_the_Philippines "Aquaculture in the Philippines") of fish, crustaceans, oysters, and seaweeds.

Eight major types of forests are distributed throughout the Philippines: [dipterocarp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipterocarpaceae "Dipterocarpaceae"), [beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beach "Beach") forest, [pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine "Pine") forest, [molave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitex_parviflora "Vitex parviflora") forest, [lower montane forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montane_ecosystems "Montane ecosystems"), upper montane (or [mossy forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_forest "Cloud forest")), [mangroves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove "Mangrove"), and [ultrabasic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultramafic_rock "Ultramafic rock") forest. According to official estimates, the Philippines had 7,000,000 hectares (27,000 sq mi) of forest cover in 2023. Logging had been systemized during the American colonial period and [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation_in_the_Philippines "Deforestation in the Philippines") continued after independence, accelerating during the [Marcos presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_under_Ferdinand_Marcos "Martial law under Ferdinand Marcos") due to unregulated logging concessions. Forest cover declined from 70 percent of the Philippines' total land area in 1900 to about 18.3 percent in 1999. Rehabilitation efforts have had marginal success.

The Philippines is a [priority hotspot for biodiversity conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_hotspot "Biodiversity hotspot"); it has [more than 200 protected areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_protected_areas_of_the_Philippines "List of protected areas of the Philippines"), which was expanded to 7,790,000 hectares (30,100 sq mi) as of 2023[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Philippines&action=edit). [Three sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_the_Philippines "List of World Heritage Sites in the Philippines") in the Philippines have been included on the UNESCO World Heritage List: the [Tubbataha Reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tubbataha_Reef "Tubbataha Reef") in the Sulu Sea, the [Puerto Princesa Subterranean River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Princesa_Subterranean_River_National_Park "Puerto Princesa Subterranean River National Park"), and the [Mount Hamiguitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hamiguitan "Mount Hamiguitan") Wildlife Sanctuary.

### Climate

The Philippines has a tropical maritime climate which is usually hot and humid. There are three seasons: a hot [dry season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_season "Dry season") from March to May, a [rainy season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_season "Wet season") from June to November, and a cool dry season from December to February. The southwest [monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon "Monsoon") (known as the *habagat*) lasts from May to October. The northeast monsoon (*[amihan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amihan "Amihan")*) lasts from November to April.

The coolest month is January, and the warmest is May. Temperatures at sea level across the Philippines tend to be in the same range, regardless of latitude. The average annual temperature is around 26.6 °C (79.9 °F). It is 18.3 °C (64.9 °F) in [Baguio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguio "Baguio"), 1,500 meters (4,900 ft) above sea level. The country's average [humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humidity "Humidity") is 82 percent. Annual rainfall is as high as 5,000 millimeters (200 in) on the mountainous east coast, but less than 1,000 millimeters (39 in) in some sheltered valleys.

The [Philippine Area of Responsibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Area_of_Responsibility "Philippine Area of Responsibility") has 19 [typhoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoons_in_the_Philippines "Typhoons in the Philippines") in a typical year, usually from July to October. Eight or nine of them make [landfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landfall "Landfall"). The wettest recorded typhoon to hit the Philippines dropped 2,210 millimeters (87 in) in Baguio from July 14 to 18, 1911. The country [is among](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_the_Philippines "Climate change in the Philippines") the world's ten most [vulnerable to climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_vulnerability "Climate change vulnerability").

## Government and politics

The Philippines has a [democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy "Democracy") government, a constitutional [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic "Republic") with a [presidential system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system "Presidential system"). The [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Philippines "President of the Philippines") is [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state "Head of state") and [head of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_government "Head of government"), and is the [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief "Commander-in-chief") of the [armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_the_Philippines "Armed Forces of the Philippines"). The president [is elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_elections_in_the_Philippines "Presidential elections in the Philippines") through [direct election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_election "Direct election") by the [citizens of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_nationality_law "Philippine nationality law") for a six-year term. The [vice president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_Philippines "Vice President of the Philippines"), limited to two consecutive six-year terms, is elected separately from the president. This means the president and vice president may be from different political parties. The president appoints and presides over the [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_Philippines "Cabinet of the Philippines") and officials of various national government agencies and institutions. The [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameralism "Bicameralism") [Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_the_Philippines "Congress of the Philippines") is composed of the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_the_Philippines "Senate of the Philippines") (the [upper house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_house "Upper house"), with members [elected to a six-year term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Senate_elections "Philippine Senate elections")) and the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_of_the_Philippines "House of Representatives of the Philippines") (the [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house "Lower house"), with members elected to a three-year term).

Senators are elected [at-large](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/At-large "At-large"), and representatives are elected from [legislative districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_districts_of_the_Philippines "Legislative districts of the Philippines") and [party lists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party-list_representation_in_the_House_of_Representatives_of_the_Philippines "Party-list representation in the House of Representatives of the Philippines"). Judicial authority is vested in the [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_Philippines "Supreme Court of the Philippines"), composed of a [chief justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_the_Philippines "Chief Justice of the Philippines") and fourteen [associate justices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_Justice_of_the_Supreme_Court_of_the_Philippines "Associate Justice of the Supreme Court of the Philippines"), who are appointed by the president from nominations submitted by the [Judicial and Bar Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_and_Bar_Council "Judicial and Bar Council").

[Attempts to change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_reform_in_the_Philippines "Constitutional reform in the Philippines") the government to a [federal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation "Federation"), [unicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicameralism "Unicameralism"), or [parliamentary government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system "Parliamentary system") have been made since the [Ramos administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Fidel_V._Ramos "Presidency of Fidel V. Ramos"). Philippine politics tends to be dominated by [well-known families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_families_in_the_Philippines "List of political families in the Philippines"), such as [political dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_dynasties_in_the_Philippines "Political dynasties in the Philippines") or [celebrities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebrity_influence_in_politics "Celebrity influence in politics"), and [party switching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_switching "Party switching") is widely practiced. Corruption is significant, attributed by some historians to the Spanish colonial period's [padrino system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padrino_system "Padrino system"). The Roman Catholic church exerts considerable but waning influence in political affairs, although a constitutional provision for the [separation of Church and State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_Church_and_State "Separation of Church and State") exists.

### Foreign relations

A [founding and active member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_and_the_United_Nations "Philippines and the United Nations") of the United Nations, the Philippines has been a non-permanent member of the [Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council "United Nations Security Council"). The country participates in [peacekeeping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacekeeping "Peacekeeping") missions, particularly in [East Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Transitional_Administration_in_East_Timor "United Nations Transitional Administration in East Timor"). The Philippines is a [founding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN_Declaration "ASEAN Declaration") and active member of [ASEAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN "ASEAN") (Association of Southeast Asian Nations) and a member of the [East Asia Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia_Summit "East Asia Summit"), the [Group of 24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_24 "Group of 24"), and the [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement "Non-Aligned Movement"). The country has sought to obtain observer status in the [Organisation of Islamic Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_Islamic_Cooperation "Organisation of Islamic Cooperation") since 2003, and was a member of [SEATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEATO "SEATO"). Over 10 million Filipinos [live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Filipinos "Overseas Filipinos") and [work in 200 countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Filipino_Worker "Overseas Filipino Worker"), giving the Philippines [soft power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_power "Soft power").

During the 1990s, the Philippines began to seek [economic liberalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalization "Economic liberalization") and [free trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_trade "Free trade") to help spur [foreign direct investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment "Foreign direct investment"). It is a member of the [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization "World Trade Organization") and the [Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific_Economic_Cooperation "Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation"). The Philippines entered into the [ASEAN Trade in Goods Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN_Free_Trade_Area "ASEAN Free Trade Area") in 2010 and the [Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Comprehensive_Economic_Partnership "Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership") [free trade agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_trade_agreement "Free trade agreement") (FTA) in 2023. Through ASEAN, the Philippines has signed FTAs with [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN%E2%80%93China_Free_Trade_Area "ASEAN–China Free Trade Area"), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN%E2%80%93India_Free_Trade_Area "ASEAN–India Free Trade Area"), Japan, South Korea, Australia and New Zealand. The country has bilateral FTAs with [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan%E2%80%93Philippines_Economic_Partnership_Agreement "Japan–Philippines Economic Partnership Agreement"), South Korea, and [four European states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Free_Trade_Association "European Free Trade Association"): Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland.

The Philippines has a long [relationship with the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines%E2%80%93United_States_relations "Philippines–United States relations"), involving economics, [security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA_activities_in_the_Philippines "CIA activities in the Philippines"), and interpersonal relations. The Philippines' location [serves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_bases_in_the_Philippines "United States bases in the Philippines") an [important role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_island_chain "First island chain") in the United States' [island chain strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_chain_strategy "Island chain strategy") in the West Pacific; a [Mutual Defense Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_Defense_Treaty_(United_States%E2%80%93Philippines) "Mutual Defense Treaty (United States–Philippines)") between the two countries was signed in 1951, and was supplemented with the [1999 Visiting Forces Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines%E2%80%93United_States_Visiting_Forces_Agreement "Philippines–United States Visiting Forces Agreement") and the 2016 [Enhanced Defense Cooperation Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enhanced_Defense_Cooperation_Agreement "Enhanced Defense Cooperation Agreement"). The country supported American policies during the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War") and participated in the [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_War "Korean War") and [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War") wars. In 2003, the Philippines was designated a major non-NATO ally. [Under President Duterte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Rodrigo_Duterte "Presidency of Rodrigo Duterte"), ties with the United States weakened in favor of improved relations with China and [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines%E2%80%93Russia_relations "Philippines–Russia relations"). The Philippines relies heavily on the United States for its external defense; the U.S. has made regular assurances to defend the Philippines, including the [South China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_Sea "South China Sea").

Since 1975, the Philippines has valued its [relations with China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Philippines_relations "China–Philippines relations")—its top trading partner, and cooperates significantly with the country. Japan is the biggest bilateral contributor of [official development assistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_development_assistance "Official development assistance") to the Philippines; although some tension exists because of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), much animosity has faded. Historical and cultural ties continue to affect [relations with Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines%E2%80%93Spain_relations "Philippines–Spain relations"). Relations with Middle Eastern countries are shaped by the high number of Filipinos working in those countries, and by issues related to the Muslim minority in the Philippines; concerns have been raised about domestic abuse and war affecting the approximately 2.5 million overseas Filipino workers in the region.

The Philippines [has claims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spratly_Islands_dispute "Spratly Islands dispute") in the [Spratly Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spratly_Islands "Spratly Islands") which overlap with claims by China, Malaysia, Taiwan, and Vietnam. The largest of [its controlled islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_and_the_Spratly_Islands "Philippines and the Spratly Islands") is [Thitu Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thitu_Island "Thitu Island"), which contains the [Philippines' smallest town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalayaan,_Palawan "Kalayaan, Palawan"). The 2012 [Scarborough Shoal standoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarborough_Shoal_standoff "Scarborough Shoal standoff"), after China seized the shoal from the Philippines, led to an [international arbitration case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_v._China "Philippines v. China") which the Philippines eventually won; China rejected the result, and other new Philippine maritime laws, and made the shoal a prominent symbol of the broader dispute.

### Military

The [volunteer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volunteer_military "Volunteer military") Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP) consist of three branches: the [Philippine Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Air_Force "Philippine Air Force"), the [Philippine Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Army "Philippine Army"), and the [Philippine Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Navy "Philippine Navy"). Civilian security is handled by the [Philippine National Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_National_Police "Philippine National Police") under the [Department of the Interior and Local Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_the_Interior_and_Local_Government "Department of the Interior and Local Government"). The AFP had a total manpower of around 280,000 as of 2022[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Philippines&action=edit), of which 130,000 were active military personnel, 100,000 were reserves, and 50,000 were [paramilitaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizen_Armed_Force_Geographical_Unit "Citizen Armed Force Geographical Unit").

In 2023, US$477 million, 1.4 percent of GDP, was spent on the Philippine military. Most of the country's defense spending is on the Philippine Army, which leads operations against internal threats such as [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_armed_conflicts_in_the_Philippines "Communist armed conflicts in the Philippines") and [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_conflict "Moro conflict") separatist insurgencies; its preoccupation with internal security contributed to the decline of [Philippine naval capability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_equipment_of_the_Philippine_Navy "List of equipment of the Philippine Navy") which began during the 1970s. [A military modernization program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFP_Modernization_Act "AFP Modernization Act") began in 1995 and expanded in 2012 to build a more capable defense system.

The Philippines has long struggled against [local insurgencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_conflict_in_the_Philippines "Civil conflict in the Philippines"), [separatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separatism_in_the_Philippines "Separatism in the Philippines"), and [terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_in_the_Philippines "Terrorism in the Philippines"). [Bangsamoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro "Bangsamoro")'s largest separatist organizations, the [Moro National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_National_Liberation_Front "Moro National Liberation Front") and the [Moro Islamic Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_Islamic_Liberation_Front "Moro Islamic Liberation Front"), signed final peace agreements with the government in [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Final_Peace_Agreement "1996 Final Peace Agreement") and [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_Agreement_on_the_Bangsamoro "Comprehensive Agreement on the Bangsamoro") respectively. Other, more-militant groups such as [Abu Sayyaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Sayyaf "Abu Sayyaf") and [Bangsamoro Islamic Freedom Fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro_Islamic_Freedom_Fighters "Bangsamoro Islamic Freedom Fighters") have kidnapped foreigners for ransom, particularly in the Sulu Archipelago and [Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao "Maguindanao"), but their presence has been reduced.

The [Communist Party of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_Philippines "Communist Party of the Philippines") (CPP) and its military wing, the [New People's Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_People%27s_Army "New People's Army") (NPA), have been waging [guerrilla warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_People%27s_Army_rebellion "New People's Army rebellion") against the government since the 1970s and have [engaged in ambushes, bombings, and assassinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_communist_rebellion_in_the_Philippines "Timeline of the communist rebellion in the Philippines") of government officials and security forces; although shrinking militarily and politically after the return of democracy in 1986, the CPP-NPA, through the [National Democratic Front of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Front_of_the_Philippines "National Democratic Front of the Philippines"), continues to gather public support in urban areas by setting up [communist fronts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_front "Communist front"), infiltrating sectoral organizations, and rallying public discontent and increased militancy against the government. The Philippines ranked 104th out of 163 countries in the 2024 [Global Peace Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Peace_Index "Global Peace Index").

### Administrative divisions

The Philippines is divided into 18 [regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_the_Philippines "Regions of the Philippines"), 82 [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_the_Philippines "Provinces of the Philippines"), 149 [cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines "Cities of the Philippines"), 1,493 [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_the_Philippines "Municipalities of the Philippines"), and 42,011 [barangays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay "Barangay"). Regions other than [Bangsamoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro "Bangsamoro") are divided for administrative convenience. [Calabarzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabarzon "Calabarzon") was the region with the greatest population as of 2020[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Philippines&action=edit). The [National Capital Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Manila "Metro Manila") (NCR) was the most densely populated.

The Philippines is a [unitary state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state "Unitary state"), with the system being in place ever since the [Spanish colonialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captaincy_General_of_the_Philippines "Captaincy General of the Philippines"), with the exception of the [Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro_Autonomous_Region_in_Muslim_Mindanao "Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao") (BARMM). There have been [steps towards decentralization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism_in_the_Philippines "Federalism in the Philippines"). A 1991 law [devolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution "Devolution") some powers to [local governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_the_Philippines "Local government in the Philippines").

## Economy

According to the [International Monetary Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund "International Monetary Fund") (IMF) in its October 2025 update, the Philippines is the world's 33rd largest economy with a nominal [gross domestic product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_domestic_product "Gross domestic product") (GDP) of $494.16 billion. As a [newly industrialized country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newly_industrialized_country "Newly industrialized country"), the Philippine economy has been transitioning from an agricultural base to one with more emphasis on services and manufacturing. The country's labor force is about 52.09 million with an [unemployment rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states_by_unemployment_rate "List of sovereign states by unemployment rate") of 5.1 percent as of February 2026[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Philippines&action=edit). Gross international reserves totalled US$106.64 billion as of March 2026[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Philippines&action=edit). [Debt-to-GDP ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debt-to-GDP_ratio "Debt-to-GDP ratio") decreased to 60.2 percent at the end of 2023 from a 17-year high 63.7 percent at the end of the third quarter of that year, and indicated resiliency during the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_Philippines "COVID-19 pandemic in the Philippines"). The country's unit of [currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency "Currency") is the [Philippine peso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_peso "Philippine peso") (₱ or PHP).

The Philippines is a net importer, and a [debtor nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_international_investment_position "Net international investment position"). As of 2020[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Philippines&action=edit), the country's main export markets were China, the United States, Japan, Hong Kong, and Singapore; primary exports included [integrated circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuit "Integrated circuit"), office machinery and parts, electrical transformers, insulated wiring, and [semiconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor "Semiconductor"). Its primary import markets that year were China, Japan, South Korea, the United States, and Indonesia. Major export crops include [coconuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut_production_in_the_Philippines "Coconut production in the Philippines"), [bananas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana_industry#Production_and_export "Banana industry"), and [pineapples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_pineapple_production "List of countries by pineapple production"); it is the world's largest producer of [abaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abaca "Abaca"), and was the world's second biggest exporter of [nickel ore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel "Nickel") in 2022, as well as the biggest exporter of gold-clad metals and the biggest importer of [copra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copra "Copra") in 2020.

With an average annual growth rate of six to seven percent since around 2010, the Philippines has emerged as one of the world's fastest-growing economies, driven primarily by its increasing reliance on the service sector. Regional development is uneven, however, with Manila (in particular) gaining most of the new economic growth. [Remittances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remittance "Remittance") from [overseas Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Filipinos "Overseas Filipinos"), mainly [seamen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailor "Sailor") and [nurses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nurses "Nurses"), which the country is the world's largest suppliers of, contribute significantly to the country's economy; they reached a record US$37.20 billion in 2023, accounting for 8.5 percent of GDP. The Philippines is the world's primary [business process outsourcing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_process_outsourcing_in_the_Philippines "Business process outsourcing in the Philippines") (BPO) center. About 1.3 million Filipinos work in the BPO sector, primarily in [customer service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customer_service "Customer service"). The country is a pioneer in digital finance, as the first country to put its entire national budget under the [blockchain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockchain "Blockchain") system.

Like many other governments, the Philippine government engages in [for profit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_profit "For profit") activities through [state-owned enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State-owned_enterprise "State-owned enterprise") known as [Government-Owned and Controlled Corporations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government-owned_and_controlled_corporation "Government-owned and controlled corporation") (GOCCs). With the exception of the [Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangko_Sentral_ng_Pilipinas "Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas"), all GOCCs (including the controversial new [Maharlika Investment Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharlika_Investment_Corporation "Maharlika Investment Corporation")) fall under the supervision of the [Governance Commission for GOCCs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governance_Commission_for_GOCCs "Governance Commission for GOCCs") (GCG).

### Science and technology

The Philippines has one of the largest agricultural-research systems in Asia, despite relatively low spending on agricultural research and development. The country has developed new varieties of crops, including [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_production_in_the_Philippines "Rice production in the Philippines"), coconuts, and bananas. Research organizations include the [Philippine Rice Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Rice_Research_Institute "Philippine Rice Research Institute") and the [International Rice Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Rice_Research_Institute "International Rice Research Institute").

The [Philippine Space Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Space_Agency "Philippine Space Agency") maintains the [country's space program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_space_program "Philippine space program"), and the country bought its first [satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Philippine_satellites "List of Philippine satellites") in 1996. [Diwata-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diwata-1 "Diwata-1"), its first [micro-satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_satellite#Microsatellites "Small satellite"), was launched on the United States' [Cygnus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cygnus_(spacecraft) "Cygnus (spacecraft)") spacecraft in 2016.

The Philippines has a [high concentration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_smartphone_penetration "List of countries by smartphone penetration") of [cellular-phone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phone "Mobile phone") users, and a high level of [mobile commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_commerce "Mobile commerce"). [Text messaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text_messaging "Text messaging") is a popular form of communication, and the nation sent an average of one billion [SMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS "SMS") messages per day in 2007. The [Philippine telecommunications industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_in_the_Philippines "Telecommunications in the Philippines") had been dominated by the [PLDT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLDT "PLDT")-[Globe Telecom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globe_Telecom "Globe Telecom") duopoly for more than two decades, and the 2021 entry of [Dito Telecommunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dito_Telecommunity "Dito Telecommunity") improved the country's telecommunications service. The Philippines was ranked 50th in the [Global Innovation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Innovation_Index "Global Innovation Index") in 2025.

### Tourism

The Philippines is a popular retirement destination for foreigners because of its climate and low cost of living. The country's main tourist attractions are [its numerous beaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_beaches_in_the_Philippines "List of beaches in the Philippines"); the Philippines is also a top destination for diving enthusiasts. [Tourist spots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tourist_attractions_in_the_Philippines "List of tourist attractions in the Philippines") include [Boracay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boracay "Boracay"), called the best island in the world by *[Travel + Leisure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travel_%2B_Leisure "Travel + Leisure")* in 2012; [Coron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coron,_Palawan "Coron, Palawan") and [El Nido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Nido,_Palawan "El Nido, Palawan") in Palawan; [Cebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebu "Cebu"); [Siargao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siargao "Siargao"), and [Bohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohol "Bohol").

Tourism contributed 5.2 percent to the Philippine GDP in 2021 (lower than 12.7 percent in 2019, before the COVID-19 pandemic), and provided 5.7 million jobs in 2019. The Philippines attracted 5.45 million international visitors in 2023, 30 percent lower than the 8.26 million record in pre-pandemic 2019; most tourists came from South Korea (26.4 percent), United States (16.5 percent), Japan (5.6 percent), Australia (4.89 percent), and China (4.84 percent).

## Infrastructure

### Transportation

Transportation in the Philippines is by road, air, rail and water. Roads are the dominant form of transport, carrying 98 percent of people and 58 percent of cargo. In December 2018, there were 210,528 kilometers (130,816 mi) of roads in the country. The backbone of land-based transportation in the country is the [Pan-Philippine Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Philippine_Highway "Pan-Philippine Highway"), which connects the islands of Luzon, Samar, Leyte, and Mindanao. Inter-island transport is by the 919-kilometer (571 mi) [Strong Republic Nautical Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_Republic_Nautical_Highway "Strong Republic Nautical Highway"), an integrated set of highways and [ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferry "Ferry") routes linking 17 cities. [Jeepneys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeepney "Jeepney") are a popular, iconic public utility vehicle; other public land transport includes [buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_bus_companies_of_the_Philippines "List of bus companies of the Philippines"), [UV Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UV_Express "UV Express"), TNVS, Filcab, [taxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxis_of_the_Philippines "Taxis of the Philippines"), and [tricycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorized_tricycle_(Philippines) "Motorized tricycle (Philippines)"). Traffic is a significant issue [in Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_in_Metro_Manila "Traffic in Metro Manila") and on arterial roads to the capital.

Despite wider historical use, [rail transportation in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transportation_in_the_Philippines "Rail transportation in the Philippines") is limited to [transporting passengers within Metro Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transportation_in_the_Greater_Manila_Area "Rail transportation in the Greater Manila Area") and the provinces of [Laguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_(province) "Laguna (province)") and [Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quezon "Quezon"), with a short track in the [Bicol Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicol_Region "Bicol Region"). The country had a railway footprint of only 79 kilometers (49 mi) as of 2019[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Philippines&action=edit), which it planned to expand to 244 kilometers (152 mi). A revival of [freight rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freight_rail "Freight rail") is planned to reduce road congestion.

The Philippines had [90 national government-owned airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_the_Philippines "List of airports in the Philippines") as of 2022[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Philippines&action=edit), of which eight are [international](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_airport "International airport"). [Ninoy Aquino International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninoy_Aquino_International_Airport "Ninoy Aquino International Airport"), formerly known as Manila International Airport, has the [greatest number of passengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_the_Philippines "List of the busiest airports in the Philippines"). The 2017 [air domestic market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airlines_of_the_Philippines "List of airlines of the Philippines") was dominated by [Philippine Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Airlines "Philippine Airlines"), the country's [flag carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_carrier "Flag carrier") and Asia's oldest commercial airline, and [Cebu Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebu_Pacific "Cebu Pacific") (the country's leading [low-cost carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-cost_carrier "Low-cost carrier")).

A variety of boats are used throughout the Philippines; most are [double-outrigger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outrigger_boat "Outrigger boat") vessels known as *banca* or *[bangka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangka_(boat) "Bangka (boat)")*. Modern ships use [plywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plywood "Plywood") instead of logs, and motor engines instead of sails; they are used for fishing and inter-island travel. The Philippines has [over 1,800 seaports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ports_in_the_Philippines "List of ports in the Philippines"); of these, the principal seaports of [Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Manila "Port of Manila") (the country's chief, and busiest, port), [Batangas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batangas_International_Port "Batangas International Port"), [Subic Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Subic_Bay "Port of Subic Bay"), [Cebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Cebu "Port of Cebu"), [Iloilo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Iloilo "Port of Iloilo"), [Davao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Davao "Port of Davao"), [Cagayan de Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Cagayan_de_Oro "Port of Cagayan de Oro"), [General Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_General_Santos "Port of General Santos"), and [Zamboanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Zamboanga "Port of Zamboanga") are part of the ASEAN Transport Network.

### Energy

The Philippines had a total installed power capacity of 26,882 [MW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watt "Watt") in 2021; 43 percent was generated from [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_mining_in_the_Philippines "Coal mining in the Philippines"), 14 percent from [oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil "Oil"), 14 percent [hydropower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydropower "Hydropower"), 12 percent from [natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas "Natural gas"), and seven percent from [geothermal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_power "Geothermal power") sources. It is the world's third-biggest [geothermal-energy producer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_power_in_the_Philippines "Geothermal power in the Philippines"), behind the United States and Indonesia. The country's largest dam is the 1.2-kilometer-long (0.75 mi) [San Roque Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Roque_Dam_(Philippines) "San Roque Dam (Philippines)") on the [Agno River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agno_River "Agno River") in [Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan "Pangasinan"). The [Malampaya gas field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malampaya_gas_field "Malampaya gas field"), discovered in the early 1990s off the coast of Palawan, reduced the Philippines' reliance on imported oil; it provides about 40 percent of Luzon's energy requirements, and 30 percent of the country's energy needs.

The Philippines has three [electrical grids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_grid "Electrical grid"), one each for Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao. The [National Grid Corporation of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Grid_Corporation_of_the_Philippines "National Grid Corporation of the Philippines") manages [the country's power grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_sector_in_the_Philippines "Electricity sector in the Philippines") since 2009 and provides [overhead transmission lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overhead_power_line "Overhead power line") across the country's islands. Electric distribution to consumers [is provided](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_electric_distribution_utilities_in_the_Philippines "List of electric distribution utilities in the Philippines") by privately owned distribution utilities and government-owned [electric cooperatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_cooperative "Electric cooperative"). As of end-2021, the Philippines' household electrification level was about 95.41%.

Plans to harness [nuclear energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_in_the_Philippines "Nuclear power in the Philippines") began during the early 1970s during the [presidency of Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Ferdinand_Marcos "Presidency of Ferdinand Marcos") in response to the [1973 oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis "1973 oil crisis"). The Philippines completed [Southeast Asia's first nuclear power plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bataan_Nuclear_Power_Plant "Bataan Nuclear Power Plant") in [Bataan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bataan "Bataan") in 1984. Political issues following Marcos' ouster and safety concerns after the 1986 [Chernobyl disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_disaster "Chernobyl disaster") prevented the plant from being commissioned, and plans to operate it remain controversial.

### Water supply and sanitation

Water supply and sanitation outside Metro Manila is provided by the government through local [water districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_district "Water district") in cities or towns. Metro Manila is served by [Manila Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Water "Manila Water") and [Maynilad Water Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maynilad_Water_Services "Maynilad Water Services"). Except for shallow wells for domestic use, groundwater users are required to obtain a permit from the [National Water Resources Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Water_Resources_Board "National Water Resources Board"). In 2022, the total water withdrawals increased to 91 billion cubic meters (3.2×10~~^~~12 cu ft) from 89 billion cubic meters (3.1×10~~^~~12 cu ft) in 2021 and the total expenditures on water were amounted to [₱](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_peso "Philippine peso")144.81 billion.

Most sewage in the Philippines flows into septic tanks. In 2015, the [Joint Monitoring Programme for Water Supply and Sanitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Monitoring_Programme_for_Water_Supply_and_Sanitation "Joint Monitoring Programme for Water Supply and Sanitation") noted that 74 percent of the Philippine population had access to [improved sanitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Improved_sanitation "Improved sanitation") and "good progress" had been made between 1990 and 2015. 96 percent of Filipino households had an improved source of drinking water and 92 percent of households had sanitary toilet facilities as of 2016[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Philippines&action=edit); connections of toilet facilities to appropriate sewerage systems remain largely insufficient, however, especially in rural and urban poor communities.

## Demographics

In July 2024, the Philippines had a population of 112,729,484. More than 60 percent of the country's population live in the [coastal zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_zone "Coastal zone"). In 2020, 54 percent lived in [urban areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area "Urban area"). [Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila "Manila"), its capital, and [Quezon City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quezon_City "Quezon City"), the country's most populous city, are in [Metro Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Manila "Metro Manila"). About 13.48 million people, 12 percent of the Philippines' population, live in Metro Manila, the [country's most populous metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_metropolitan_areas_in_the_Philippines "List of metropolitan areas in the Philippines") and the world's [fifth most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_cities "List of largest cities"). Between 1948 and 2010, the population of the Philippines increased almost fivefold, from 19 million to 92 million.

The [country's median age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_median_age "List of countries by median age") is 25.3, and 63.9 percent of its population is between 15 and 64 years old. The Philippines' average annual population growth rate is decreasing, although government attempts to further reduce population growth [have been contentious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reproductive_Health_Bill_(Philippines) "Reproductive Health Bill (Philippines)"). The country [reduced its poverty rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_the_Philippines "Poverty in the Philippines") from 49.2 percent in 1985, to 18.1 percent in 2021. [The Philippines' income inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_inequality_in_the_Philippines "Income inequality in the Philippines") began to decline in 2012.

### Ethnicity

The Philippines has substantial ethnic diversity, due to foreign influence and the archipelago's division by water and topography. In the 2020 census, the Philippines' largest ethnic groups were [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_people "Tagalog people") (26.0 percent), [Visayans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visayans "Visayans") [excluding the [Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_people "Cebuano people"), [Hiligaynon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_people "Hiligaynon people"), and [Waray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waray_people "Waray people")] (14.3 percent), [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_people "Ilocano people") and Cebuano (both eight percent), Hiligaynon (7.9 percent), [Bicolano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicolano_people "Bicolano people") (6.5 percent), and Waray (3.8 percent). The [country's indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Philippines "Indigenous peoples of the Philippines") consisted of 110 enthnolinguistic groups, with a combined population of 15.56 million, in 2020. They include the [Igorot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igorot_people "Igorot people"), [Lumad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumad "Lumad"), [Mangyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangyan "Mangyan"), and the [indigenous peoples of Palawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peoples_of_Palawan "Peoples of Palawan").

[Negritos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negrito "Negrito") are thought to be among the islands' earliest inhabitants. These minority aboriginal settlers are an [Australoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australo-Melanesian "Australo-Melanesian") group, a remnant of the [first human migration from Africa to Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Dispersal "Southern Dispersal") who were probably displaced by later waves of migration. Some Philippine Negritos have a [Denisovan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denisovan "Denisovan") admixture in their [genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genome "Genome"). Ethnic Filipinos generally belong to several Southeast Asian ethnic groups, classified linguistically as [Austronesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples "Austronesian peoples") speaking [Malayo-Polynesian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayo-Polynesian_languages "Malayo-Polynesian languages").

The Austronesian population's origin is uncertain, but relatives of [Taiwanese aborigines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwanese_indigenous_peoples "Taiwanese indigenous peoples") probably brought their language and mixed with the region's existing population. The Lumad and [Sama-Bajau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sama-Bajau "Sama-Bajau") ethnic groups have an ancestral affinity with the [Austroasiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austroasiatic_languages "Austroasiatic languages") and [Mlabri-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mlabri_language "Mlabri language") [Htin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lua_people "Lua people") peoples of mainland Southeast Asia. Westward expansion of [Papuan ancestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papuan_languages "Papuan languages") from [Papua New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papua_New_Guinea "Papua New Guinea") to eastern Indonesia and [Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindanao "Mindanao") has been detected in the [Blaan people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaan_people "Blaan people") and the [Sangir language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangir_language "Sangir language"), while ancient immigration added some [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") ancestry to the precolonial [Indianized kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_(900%E2%80%931565) "History of the Philippines (900–1565)") of the islands.

Immigrants arrived in the Philippines from elsewhere in the Spanish Empire, especially [from the Spanish Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_Asian "Latin American Asian"). A 2016 [National Geographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geno_2.0_Next_Generation "Geno 2.0 Next Generation") project [concluded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_studies_on_Filipinos "Genetic studies on Filipinos") that people living in the Philippine archipelago carried [genetic markers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_marker "Genetic marker") in the following percentages: 53 percent [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia") and [Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania "Oceania"), 36 percent [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia"), 5 percent [Southern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Europe "Southern Europe"), 3 percent [Southern Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia"), and 2 percent Native American (from [Latin America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_America "Latin America")).

Descendants of mixed-race couples are known as [Mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Mestizos "Filipino Mestizos") or *tisoy*, which during the [Spanish colonial times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_(1565%E2%80%931898) "History of the Philippines (1565–1898)"), were mostly composed of [Chinese mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangley "Sangley") (*Mestizos de Sangley*), [Spanish mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Filipino "Spanish Filipino") (*Mestizos de Español*) and the mix thereof (*[tornatrás](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torna_atr%C3%A1s "Torna atrás")*). The modern [Chinese Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipino "Chinese Filipino") are well-integrated into Filipino society. Primarily the descendants of immigrants from [Fujian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujian "Fujian"), the pure ethnic Chinese Filipinos during the [American colonial era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_(1898%E2%80%931946) "History of the Philippines (1898–1946)") (early 1900s) purportedly numbered about 1.35 million; while an estimated 22.8 million (around 20 percent) of Filipinos have half or partial Chinese ancestry from precolonial, colonial, and 20th century [Chinese migrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese "Overseas Chinese"). During the Hispanic era (late 1700s), the tribute-census showed that mixed Spanish Filipinos made up a moderate ratio (around 5 percent) of all citizens. Meanwhile, [a smaller proportion (2.33 percent) of the population were Mexican Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_settlement_in_the_Philippines "Mexican settlement in the Philippines"). Almost 300,000 [American citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans_in_the_Philippines "Americans in the Philippines") live in the country as of 2023[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Philippines&action=edit), this increased to 750,000 by year 2025, (forming 0.75% of the demographics) and up to 250,000 [Amerasians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerasian "Amerasian"), (0.25% of the country), are in the cities of [Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Global_City "Clark Global City"), [Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angeles_City "Angeles City"), Manila, and [Olongapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olongapo "Olongapo") (When combined, [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans "Americans") and Amerasian descent, form 1% of the population). Other significant non-indigenous minorities include [Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Filipino "Indian Filipino") and [Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabs_in_the_Philippines "Arabs in the Philippines"). [Japanese Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_in_the_Philippines "Japanese in the Philippines") include escaped Christians ([Kirishitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirishitan "Kirishitan")) who [fled persecutions by Shogun Tokugawa Ieyasu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyrs_of_Japan "Martyrs of Japan").

### Languages

*[Ethnologue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnologue "Ethnologue")* lists 186 languages for the Philippines, 182 of which are [living languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_language "Living language"). The other four no longer have any known speakers. Most native languages are part of the [Philippine branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_languages "Philippine languages") of the [Malayo-Polynesian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayo-Polynesian_languages "Malayo-Polynesian languages"), which is a branch of the [Austronesian language family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_languages "Austronesian languages"). Spanish-based [creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creole_language "Creole language") varieties, collectively known as [Chavacano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chavacano "Chavacano"), are also spoken. Many [Philippine Negrito languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Negrito_languages#Unique_vocabulary "Philippine Negrito languages") have unique vocabularies which survived Austronesian acculturation.

[Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language "Filipino language") and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_English "Philippine English") are the country's official languages. Filipino, a [standardized version](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_language "Standard language") of [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language "Tagalog language"), is spoken primarily in Metro Manila. Filipino and English are used in government, education, print, broadcast media, and business, often with a third local language. [Code-switching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code-switching "Code-switching") between English and other local languages, notably [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taglish "Taglish"), is common. The Philippine constitution provides for [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Spanish "Philippine Spanish") and Arabic on a voluntary, optional basis. Spanish, a widely used [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca "Lingua franca") during the late nineteenth century, [has declined greatly in use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_language_in_the_Philippines "Spanish language in the Philippines"), although Spanish [loanwords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loanword "Loanword") are still present in Philippine languages. Arabic is primarily taught in [Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindanao "Mindanao") Islamic schools.

In 2020, the top languages generally spoken at home were Tagalog, [Binisaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binisaya "Binisaya"), [Hiligaynon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_language "Hiligaynon language"), [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_language "Ilocano language"), [Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language "Cebuano language"), and [Bikol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bikol_languages "Bikol languages"). ### Religion

Although the Philippines is a [secular state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularism_in_the_Philippines "Secularism in the Philippines") with [freedom of religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_religion_in_the_Philippines "Freedom of religion in the Philippines"), an overwhelming majority of Filipinos [consider religion very important](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Importance_of_religion_by_country "Importance of religion by country") and [irreligion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion_in_the_Philippines "Irreligion in the Philippines") is very low. [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_the_Philippines "Christianity in the Philippines") is the dominant religion followed by about 89 percent of the population. The country had the [world's third-largest Roman Catholic population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_by_country "Catholic Church by country") as of 2013[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Philippines&action=edit), and was Asia's [largest Christian nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Asia "Christianity in Asia").

Census data from [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Philippine_census "2020 Philippine census") found that 78.8 percent of the population professed [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_the_Philippines "Catholic Church in the Philippines"). The Catholic church in the Philippines is unique for being by granted by the [Roman Pontiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Pontiff "Roman Pontiff"), the [Cerulean Indult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerulean_Indult "Cerulean Indult"), the privilege to wear the liturgical color of [Cerulean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerulean "Cerulean") or [Celeste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_blue "Light blue"), in honor of the [Immaculate Conception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immaculate_Conception "Immaculate Conception") of Mary, a privilege, not shared by most other Latin-Rite Catholics, except those under the ambit of the Spanish Empire where it also doubly serves as the color of [Native American Royalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turquoise#Cultural_associations "Turquoise") even though the indult had initially fallen out of use due to wars of independence against Spain, the primary supporter of the Immaculate Conception, but was restored to all former Spanish-territories following the First Provincial Council of Manila as the Philippines earned it through reverting to, or staying loyal towards Catholicism, even though the church was divided on the issue of Philippine independence, amidst the schisms produced by the [Philippine Independent Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Independent_Church "Philippine Independent Church") as well as other break away Protestant and [Masonic influenced churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freemasonry_in_the_Philippines "Freemasonry in the Philippines"), after Spanish Colonialism was replaced by the American Imperialism. Other [Christian denominations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Christian_denominations_in_the_Philippines "List of Christian denominations in the Philippines") include [Iglesia ni Cristo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iglesia_ni_Cristo "Iglesia ni Cristo"), the [Philippine Independent Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Independent_Church "Philippine Independent Church"), and [Seventh-day Adventism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh-day_Adventist_Church "Seventh-day Adventist Church"). [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism_in_the_Philippines "Protestantism in the Philippines") made up about 5% to 7% of the population in 2010. The Philippines sends many [Christian missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_mission "Christian mission") around the world, and is a training center for foreign priests and nuns.

[Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_the_Philippines "Islam in the Philippines") is the country's second-largest religion, with 6.4 percent of the population in the 2020 census. Most Muslims live in Mindanao and nearby islands, and most adhere to the [Shafi'i school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shafi%27i_school "Shafi'i school") of [Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam "Sunni Islam").

About 0.2 percent of the population [follow indigenous religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_Philippine_folk_religions "Indigenous Philippine folk religions"), whose practices and folk beliefs are often [syncretized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_syncretism "Religious syncretism") with Christianity and Islam. [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_the_Philippines "Buddhism in the Philippines") is practiced by about 0.04% of the population, primarily by Filipinos of Chinese descent.

### Health

[Health care in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care_in_the_Philippines "Health care in the Philippines") is provided by the national and local governments, although private payments account for most healthcare spending. Per-capita health expenditure in 2024 was [₱](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_peso "Philippine peso")12,751 and health expenditures were 5.9 percent of the country's GDP. The 2023 budget allocation for healthcare was ₱334.9 billion. The 2019 enactment of the [Universal Health Care Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_health_care "Universal health care") by President Duterte facilitated the automatic enrollment of all Filipinos in the [national health insurance program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Health_Insurance_Corporation "Philippine Health Insurance Corporation"). Since 2018, [Malasakit Centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malasakit_Center "Malasakit Center") (one-stop shops) have been set up in several government-operated hospitals to provide medical and financial assistance to indigent patients.

Average life expectancy in the Philippines as of 2023[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Philippines&action=edit) is 70.48 years (66.97 years for males, and 74.15 years for females). Access to medicine has improved due to increasing Filipino acceptance of [generic drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generic_drug "Generic drug"). The country's leading causes of death in 2021 were [ischaemic heart diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ischaemic_heart_diseases "Ischaemic heart diseases"), [cerebrovascular diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebrovascular_disease "Cerebrovascular disease"), [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19 "COVID-19"), [neoplasms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoplasm "Neoplasm"), and [diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes "Diabetes"). [Communicable diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communicable_disease "Communicable disease") are correlated with natural disasters, primarily floods. One million Filipinos have active [tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculosis "Tuberculosis"), the fourth highest global prevalence rate.

The Philippines has 1,387 [hospitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_hospitals_in_the_Philippines "List of hospitals in the Philippines"), 33 percent of which are government-run; 23,281 barangay health stations, 2,592 rural health units, 2,411 [birthing homes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birthing_center "Birthing center"), and 659 infirmaries provide primary care throughout the country. Since 1967, the Philippines had become the largest global supplier of nurses; seventy percent of [nursing graduates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nursing_in_the_Philippines "Nursing in the Philippines") go overseas to work, causing problems in retaining skilled practitioners.

### Education

Primary and secondary schooling in the Philippines consists of six years of elementary period, four years of junior high school, and two years of senior high school. Public education, provided by the government, is free at the elementary and secondary levels and at [most public higher-education institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Access_to_Quality_Tertiary_Education_Act "Universal Access to Quality Tertiary Education Act"). [Science high schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Science_High_School_System "Philippine Science High School System") for talented students were established in 1963. The government provides technical-vocational training and development through the [Technical Education and Skills Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_Education_and_Skills_Development_Authority "Technical Education and Skills Development Authority"). In 2004, the government began offering [alternative education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_Learning_System_(Philippines) "Alternative Learning System (Philippines)") to out-of-school children, youth, and adults to improve literacy; [madaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrasa "Madrasa") were mainstreamed in 16 regions that year, primarily in Mindanao Muslim areas under the [Department of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Education_(Philippines) "Department of Education (Philippines)"). [Catholic schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Catholic_universities_and_colleges_in_the_Philippines "List of Catholic universities and colleges in the Philippines"), which number more than 1,500, and higher education institutions are an integral part of the educational system.

The Philippines has [1,975 higher education institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_colleges_and_universities_in_the_Philippines "List of colleges and universities in the Philippines") as of 2019[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Philippines&action=edit), of which 246 are public and 1,729 are private. Public universities are non-sectarian, and are primarily classified as [state-administered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_state_schools,_colleges_and_universities_in_the_Philippines "List of state schools, colleges and universities in the Philippines") or [local government-funded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_colleges_and_universities_(Philippines) "Local colleges and universities (Philippines)"). The [national university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_university "National university") is the eight-school [University of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Philippines "University of the Philippines") (UP) system. The country's top-ranked universities are the [University of the Philippines Diliman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Philippines_Diliman "University of the Philippines Diliman"), [Ateneo de Manila University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ateneo_de_Manila_University "Ateneo de Manila University"), [De La Salle University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_La_Salle_University "De La Salle University"), and [University of Santo Tomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Santo_Tomas "University of Santo Tomas").

In 2019[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Philippines&action=edit), the Philippines had a basic [literacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy "Literacy") rate of 93.8 percent of those five years old or older, and a [functional literacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_illiteracy "Functional illiteracy") rate of 91.6 percent of those aged 10 to 64. Education, a significant proportion of the national budget, was allocated ₱900.9 billion from the ₱5.268 trillion 2023 budget. As of 2023[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Philippines&action=edit), the country has 1,640 [public libraries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_libraries "Public libraries") affiliated with the [National Library of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Library_of_the_Philippines "National Library of the Philippines").

## Culture

The Philippines has significant cultural diversity, reinforced by the country's fragmented geography. Spanish and American cultures profoundly influenced Filipino culture as a result of long colonization. The cultures of [Lumads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumad "Lumad") of Mindanao and the [Sulu Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Archipelago "Sulu Archipelago") developed distinctly, since they had limited Spanish influence and more influence from nearby Islamic regions. Indigenous groups such as the [Igorots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igorot "Igorot") have preserved their precolonial customs and traditions by [resisting the Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igorot_resistance_to_Spanish_colonization "Igorot resistance to Spanish colonization"). A [national identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_nationalism "Filipino nationalism") [emerged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_revival "National revival") during the 19th century, however, with [shared national symbols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_symbols_of_the_Philippines "National symbols of the Philippines") and cultural and historical [touchstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touchstone_(metaphor) "Touchstone (metaphor)").

[Hispanic legacies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_influence_on_Filipino_culture "Spanish influence on Filipino culture") includes the dominance of Catholicism, a [creole language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chavacano "Chavacano"), numerous [loanwords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_loanwords_in_the_Tagalog_language#Spanish "List of loanwords in the Tagalog language"), the endangered [Philippine Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Spanish "Philippine Spanish"), arts, architecture, [literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_literature_in_Spanish "Philippine literature in Spanish"), drama, food, music, dance, fashion and the prevalence of [Spanish names and surnames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat%C3%A1logo_alfab%C3%A9tico_de_apellidos "Catálogo alfabético de apellidos"), which resulted from an 1849 edict ordering the systematic distribution of family names and the implementation of [Spanish naming customs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_naming_customs "Spanish naming customs"); the [names of many locations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Philippine_place_names_of_Spanish_origin "List of Philippine place names of Spanish origin") also have Spanish origins. American influence on modern Filipino culture is evident in the use of English, and Filipino consumption of [fast food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_food "Fast food") and American films and music.

[Public holidays in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_the_Philippines "Public holidays in the Philippines") are classified as regular or special. [Festivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_festivals_in_the_Philippines "List of festivals in the Philippines") are primarily religious, and most towns and villages have such a festival (usually to honor a [patron saint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patron_saint "Patron saint")). Better-known festivals include [Ati-Atihan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ati-Atihan_festival "Ati-Atihan festival"), [Dinagyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinagyang "Dinagyang"), [Moriones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moriones_Festival "Moriones Festival"), [Sinulog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinulog "Sinulog"), and [Flores de Mayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flores_de_Mayo "Flores de Mayo")—a month-long devotion to the [Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Mary "Virgin Mary") held in May. The country's [Christmas season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_in_the_Philippines "Christmas in the Philippines") begins as early as September 1, and [Holy Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Week_in_the_Philippines "Holy Week in the Philippines") is a solemn religious observance for its Christian population.

### Values

Filipino [values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_(ethics_and_social_sciences) "Value (ethics and social sciences)") are rooted primarily in personal alliances based in [kinship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_kinship "Philippine kinship"), obligation, friendship, religion (particularly Christianity), and commerce. They center around social harmony through *[pakikisama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakikisama "Pakikisama")*, motivated primarily by the desire for acceptance by a group. Reciprocity through *[utang na loob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utang_na_loob "Utang na loob")* (a debt of gratitude) is a significant Filipino cultural trait, and an internalized debt can never be fully repaid. The main sanction for divergence from these values are the concepts of *hiya* (shame) and loss of *amor propio* ([self-esteem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-esteem "Self-esteem")).

The family is central to Philippine society; norms such as loyalty, maintaining close relationships and care for elderly parents are ingrained in Philippine society. Respect for authority and the elderly is valued, and is shown with gestures such as *mano* and the [honorifics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_styles_and_honorifics "Filipino styles and honorifics") *po* and *opo* and *kuya* (older brother) or *ate* (older sister). Other Filipino values are optimism about the future, pessimism about the present, concern about other people, friendship and friendliness, [hospitality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospitality "Hospitality"), religiosity, respect for oneself and others (particularly [women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_the_Philippines "Women in the Philippines")), and integrity.

### Art and architecture

Philippine art combines indigenous folk art, east-Asian and [Classical traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_art "Classical art"). During the Spanish colonial period, art was used to spread Catholicism mainly through paintings and sculptures. The first recorded sculptor in the Philippines is Juan de los Santos (1590 –1660) known for making retablos. In 1821, [Damian Domingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dami%C3%A1n_Domingo "Damián Domingo"), the father of Filipino paintings, opened the [*Academia de Dibujo*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_de_Dibujo_y_Pintura "Academia de Dibujo y Pintura") art school in Binondo. Other artists during Spanish colonial rule included painters such as Josef Luciano Dans, [Jose Honorato Lozano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Honorato_Lozano "José Honorato Lozano"), Mariano Asuncion and sculptors such as [Isabelo Tampinco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabelo_Tampinco "Isabelo Tampinco") and Crispulo Hocson. Artist whose works drew attention to the Philippines are [Juan Luna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Luna "Juan Luna") and [Félix Resurrección Hidalgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Resurrecci%C3%B3n_Hidalgo "Félix Resurrección Hidalgo"). [Fernando Amorsolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Amorsolo "Fernando Amorsolo") dominated Philippine paintings during the American colonial period, popular for his [pastoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastoral "Pastoral") scenes of Philippine countryside. [Victorio Edades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernism "Modernism") known as the father of Modern Philippine Art, popularized [Modernism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernism "Modernism") in the Philippines in 1920s and 1930s.

Traditional Philippine architecture has two main models: the indigenous *[bahay kubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahay_kubo "Bahay kubo")* and the *[bahay na bato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahay_na_bato "Bahay na bato")*, which developed under Spanish rule. Some regions, such as [Batanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batanes "Batanes"), differ slightly due to climate; limestone was used as a building material, and houses were built to withstand typhoons.

[Spanish architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_architecture "Spanish architecture") left an imprint in town designs around a [central square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poblacion "Poblacion") or *plaza mayor*, but many of its buildings were damaged or destroyed during World War II. [Several](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Baroque_churches_in_the_Philippines "List of Baroque churches in the Philippines") Philippine [churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Catholic_churches_in_the_Philippines "List of Catholic churches in the Philippines") adapted [baroque architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_architecture "Baroque architecture") to withstand earthquakes, leading to the development of [Earthquake Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthquake_Baroque "Earthquake Baroque"); [four baroque churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_Churches_of_the_Philippines "Baroque Churches of the Philippines") have been listed as a collective UNESCO [World Heritage Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Site "World Heritage Site"). [Spanish colonial fortifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_colonial_fortifications_in_the_Philippines "Spanish colonial fortifications in the Philippines") (*fuerzas*) in several parts of the Philippines were primarily designed by missionary architects and built by Filipino stonemasons. [Vigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigan "Vigan"), in [Ilocos Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Sur "Ilocos Sur"), is known for its Hispanic-style houses and buildings.

American rule introduced new architectural styles in the construction of government buildings and [Art Deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Deco "Art Deco") theaters. During the American period, construction of [Gabaldon school buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabaldon_school_buildings "Gabaldon school buildings") began, and some city planning using architectural designs and master plans by [Daniel Burnham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Burnham "Daniel Burnham") was done in portions of [Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnham_Plan_of_Manila "Burnham Plan of Manila") and [Baguio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguio "Baguio"). Part of the Burnham plan was the construction of government buildings reminiscent of [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_architecture "Ancient Greek architecture") or [Neoclassical architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture "Neoclassical architecture"). Buildings from the Spanish and American periods can be seen in [Iloilo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iloilo "Iloilo"), especially in [Calle Real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calle_Real,_Iloilo "Calle Real, Iloilo").

### Music and dance

There are two types of Philippine [folk dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_dance "Folk dance"), stemming from traditional indigenous influences and Spanish influence. Although native dances had become less popular, folk dancing began to revive during the 1920s. The [Cariñosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cari%C3%B1osa "Cariñosa"), a Hispanic Filipino dance, is unofficially considered the country's national dance. Popular indigenous dances include the [Tinikling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinikling "Tinikling") and [Singkil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singkil_(Maranao_dance) "Singkil (Maranao dance)"), which include the rhythmic clapping of bamboo poles. Present-day dances vary from delicate [ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballet "Ballet") to street-oriented [breakdancing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakdancing "Breakdancing").

[Rondalya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rondalla "Rondalla") music, with traditional [mandolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandolin "Mandolin")-type [instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Philippine_musical_instruments "List of Philippine musical instruments"), was popular during the Spanish era. Spanish-influenced musicians are primarily [bandurria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandurria "Bandurria")-based bands with 14-string guitars. [Kundiman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kundiman "Kundiman") developed during the 1920s and 1930s. The American colonial period exposed many Filipinos to [U.S. culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_the_United_States "Culture of the United States") and [popular music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_the_United_States "Music of the United States"). [Rock music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_music "Rock music") was introduced to Filipinos during the 1960s and developed into Filipino rock (or [Pinoy rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinoy_rock "Pinoy rock")), a term encompassing [pop rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_rock "Pop rock"), [alternative rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_rock "Alternative rock"), [heavy metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metal_music "Heavy metal music"), [punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock "Punk rock"), [new wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_wave_music "New wave music"), [ska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ska "Ska"), and [reggae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggae "Reggae"). Martial law in the 1970s produced [Filipino folk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_folk_music "Philippine folk music") bands and artists who [were at the forefront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protest_music_against_the_Marcos_dictatorship "Protest music against the Marcos dictatorship") of political demonstrations. The decade also saw the birth of the [Manila sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_sound "Manila sound") and [Original Pilipino Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_Pilipino_Music "Original Pilipino Music") (OPM). [Filipino hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_hip-hop "Filipino hip-hop"), which originated in 1979, entered the mainstream in 1990. [Karaoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karaoke "Karaoke") is also popular. From 2010 to 2020, [Pinoy pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinoy_pop "Pinoy pop") (P-pop) was influenced by [K-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-pop "K-pop") and [J-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J-pop "J-pop").

Locally produced theatrical drama became established during the late 1870s. Spanish influence around that time introduced *[zarzuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zarzuela "Zarzuela")* plays (with music) and *[comedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comedia_(play) "Comedia (play)")*s, with dance. The plays became popular throughout the country, and were written in a number of local languages. American influence introduced [vaudeville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaudeville "Vaudeville") and ballet. [Realistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realism_(theatre) "Realism (theatre)") theater became dominant during the 20th century, with plays focusing on contemporary political and social issues.

### Literature

Philippine literature consists of [works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipiniana "Filipiniana") usually written in Filipino, [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_literature_in_Spanish "Philippine literature in Spanish"), or [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_literature_in_English "Philippine literature in English"). Some of the earliest well-known works were created from the 17th to the 19th centuries. They include *[Ibong Adarna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibong_Adarna "Ibong Adarna")*, an epic about an eponymous magical bird, and *[Florante at Laura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florante_at_Laura "Florante at Laura")* by Tagalog author [Francisco Balagtas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Balagtas "Francisco Balagtas"). [José Rizal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Rizal "José Rizal") wrote the novels *[Noli Me Tángere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noli_Me_T%C3%A1ngere_(novel) "Noli Me Tángere (novel)")* and *[El filibusterismo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_filibusterismo "El filibusterismo")*, both of which depict the injustices of Spanish colonial rule.

[Folk literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_folk_literature "Philippine folk literature") was relatively unaffected by colonial influence until the 19th century due to Spanish indifference. Most printed literary works during Spanish colonial rule were religious in nature, although [Filipino elites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilustrado "Ilustrado") who later learned Spanish wrote [nationalistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_nationalism "Filipino nationalism") literature. The American arrival [began](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_the_Philippines_during_American_rule "Education in the Philippines during American rule") Filipino literary use of English and influenced the development of the [Philippine comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_comics "Philippine comics") industry that flourished from the 1920s through the 1970s. In the late 1960s, during the presidency of Ferdinand Marcos, Philippine literature was [influenced by political activism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_activism_in_the_Philippines_(1965%E2%80%931972) "Student activism in the Philippines (1965–1972)"); many poets began using Tagalog, in keeping with the country's oral traditions.

[Philippine mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_mythology "Philippine mythology") has been handed down primarily through oral tradition; popular [figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Philippine_mythological_figures "List of Philippine mythological figures") are [Maria Makiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Makiling "Maria Makiling"), [Lam-ang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biag_ni_Lam-Ang "Biag ni Lam-Ang"), and the [Sarimanok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarimanok "Sarimanok"). The country has a number of [folk epics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_epic_poetry "Philippine epic poetry"). Wealthy families could preserve transcriptions of the epics as family heirlooms, particularly in Mindanao; the [Maranao-language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maranao_language "Maranao language") *[Darangen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darangen "Darangen")* is an example.

### Media

Philippine media primarily uses Filipino and English, although [broadcasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcasting "Broadcasting") has shifted to Filipino. [Television shows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Philippine_television_shows "List of Philippine television shows"), commercials, and [films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lists_of_Philippine_films "Lists of Philippine films") are regulated by the [Movie and Television Review and Classification Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movie_and_Television_Review_and_Classification_Board "Movie and Television Review and Classification Board"). Most Filipinos obtain news and information from television, the Internet, and [social media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_media_use_in_the_Philippines "Social media use in the Philippines"). The country's flagship state-owned broadcast-television network is the [People's Television Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Television_Network "People's Television Network") (PTV). [ABS-CBN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABS-CBN "ABS-CBN") and [GMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GMA_Network "GMA Network"), both [free-to-air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-to-air "Free-to-air"), were the dominant TV networks; before the May 2020 [Philippine government denial of ABS-CBN's franchise renewal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABS-CBN_franchise_renewal_controversy "ABS-CBN franchise renewal controversy"), it was the country's largest network. [Philippine television dramas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_television_drama "Philippine television drama"), known as *teleserye*s and mainly produced by [ABS-CBN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ABS-CBN_original_drama_series "List of ABS-CBN original drama series") and [GMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_GMA_Network_original_drama_series "List of GMA Network original drama series"), are also seen in several other countries.

Local film-making [began in 1919](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Philippine_films_before_1940 "List of Philippine films before 1940") with the release of the first Filipino-produced [feature film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feature_film "Feature film"): *[Dalagang Bukid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalagang_Bukid "Dalagang Bukid")* (*A Girl from the Country*), directed by [Jose Nepomuceno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Nepomuceno "José Nepomuceno"). [Production companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Philippine_film_studios "List of Philippine film studios") remained small during the [silent film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_film "Silent film") era, but sound films and larger productions emerged in 1933. The postwar 1940s to the early 1960s are considered a high point for Philippine cinema. The 1962–1971 decade saw a decline in quality films, although the commercial film industry expanded until the 1980s. Critically acclaimed Philippine films include *[Himala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himala "Himala")* (*Miracle*) and *[Oro, Plata, Mata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oro,_Plata,_Mata "Oro, Plata, Mata")* (*Gold, Silver, Death*), both released in 1982. Since the turn of the 21st century, the country's film industry has struggled to compete with larger-budget foreign films (particularly [Hollywood films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_the_United_States "Cinema of the United States")). [Art films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_film "Art film") have thrived, however, and several [indie films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_New_Wave "Philippine New Wave") have been successful domestically and abroad.

The Philippines has a large number of [radio stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_radio_stations_in_the_Philippines "List of radio stations in the Philippines") and [newspapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_newspapers_in_the_Philippines "List of newspapers in the Philippines"). English [broadsheets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadsheet "Broadsheet") are popular among executives, professionals and students. Less-expensive Tagalog [tabloids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabloid_(newspaper_format) "Tabloid (newspaper format)"), which grew during the 1990s, are popular (particularly in Manila); however, overall newspaper readership is declining in favor of [online news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_media_as_a_news_source "Social media as a news source"). The top three newspapers, by nationwide readership and credibility, are the *[Philippine Daily Inquirer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Daily_Inquirer "Philippine Daily Inquirer")*, *[Manila Bulletin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Bulletin "Manila Bulletin")*, and *[The Philippine Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Philippine_Star "The Philippine Star")*. Although [freedom of the press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_the_press "Freedom of the press") is protected by the constitution, the country was listed as the seventh-most-dangerous country for journalists in 2022 by the [Committee to Protect Journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_to_Protect_Journalists "Committee to Protect Journalists") due to 13 unsolved murders of journalists.

The Philippine population are the world's top Internet users. In early 2021, 67 percent of Filipinos (73.91 million) had Internet access; the overwhelming majority used [smartphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone "Smartphone").

### Cuisine

From its Malayo-Polynesian origins, traditional Philippine cuisine has evolved since the 16th century. It was primarily influenced by Hispanic, [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Chinese_cuisine "Filipino Chinese cuisine"), and [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_cuisine "American cuisine") cuisines, which were adapted to the Filipino palate. Filipinos tend to prefer robust flavors, centered on sweet, salty, and sour combinations. Regional variations exist throughout the country; rice is the general staple [starch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starch#Food "Starch") but [cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava-based_dishes "Cassava-based dishes") is more common in parts of Mindanao. [Adobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_adobo "Philippine adobo") is the unofficial national dish. Other [popular dishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Philippine_dishes "List of Philippine dishes") include *[lechón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lech%C3%B3n "Lechón")*, *[kare-kare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kare-kare "Kare-kare")*, *[sinigang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinigang "Sinigang")*, *[pancit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancit "Pancit")*, *[lumpia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumpia "Lumpia")*, and *[arroz caldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arroz_caldo "Arroz caldo")*. [Traditional desserts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Philippine_desserts "List of Philippine desserts") are *kakanin* ([rice cakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_cake "Rice cake")), which include *[puto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puto_(food) "Puto (food)")*, *[suman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suman_(food) "Suman (food)")*, and *[bibingka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibingka "Bibingka")*. Ingredients such as [calamansi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calamansi "Calamansi"), *[ube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ube_halaya "Ube halaya")*, and *[pili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canarium_ovatum "Canarium ovatum")* are used in Filipino desserts. The generous use of [condiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_condiments "Philippine condiments") such as *[patis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patis_(sauce) "Patis (sauce)")*, *[bagoong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagoong "Bagoong")*, and *[toyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soy_sauce#Filipino "Soy sauce")* impart a distinctive Philippine flavor.There are vegan filipino food and they include laing, lumpiang gulay, ensaladang talong, turon, ginataang bilo-bilo, biko, espasol, banana cue, camote cue, taho, suman, carioca, and lumpiang sariwa.

Unlike other East or Southeast Asian countries, most Filipinos do not eat with [chopsticks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chopsticks "Chopsticks"); they use spoons and forks. Traditional eating with the hands or fingers (known as *[kamayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamayan "Kamayan")*) had been used in less urbanized areas, but has been popularized with the introduction of Filipino food to foreigners and city residents.

### Sports and recreation

[Basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball_in_the_Philippines "Basketball in the Philippines"), played at the amateur and professional levels, is considered the country's most popular sport. Other popular sports include [boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_boxing_in_the_Philippines "History of boxing in the Philippines") and billiards, boosted by the achievements of [Manny Pacquiao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manny_Pacquiao "Manny Pacquiao") and [Efren Reyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efren_Reyes "Efren Reyes"). The national [martial art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_martial_arts "Filipino martial arts") is [Arnis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnis "Arnis"). *Sabong* ([cockfighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockfight "Cockfight")) is popular entertainment, especially among Filipino men, and was documented by the [Magellan expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magellan_expedition "Magellan expedition"). [Video gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_games_in_the_Philippines "Video games in the Philippines") and [esports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esports_in_the_Philippines "Esports in the Philippines") are emerging pastimes, with the popularity of [indigenous games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_games_in_the_Philippines "Traditional games in the Philippines") such as *[patintero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patintero "Patintero")*, *[tumbang preso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumbang_preso "Tumbang preso")*, *[luksong tinik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luksong_tinik "Luksong tinik")*, and *[piko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piko_(Filipino_game) "Piko (Filipino game)")* declining among young people.

The [men's national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_national_football_team "Philippines national football team") has participated in one [Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup "2019 AFC Asian Cup"). The [women's national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_women%27s_national_football_team "Philippines women's national football team") qualified for the [2023 FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2023 FIFA Women's World Cup"), their first [World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "FIFA Women's World Cup"), in January 2022. The Philippines has participated in every [Summer Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_Olympic_Games "Summer Olympic Games") since [1924](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_at_the_1924_Summer_Olympics "Philippines at the 1924 Summer Olympics"), except when they supported the [American-led boycott of the 1980 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Summer_Olympics_boycott "1980 Summer Olympics boycott"). It was the first [tropical nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_nations_at_the_Winter_Olympics "Tropical nations at the Winter Olympics") to compete at the [Winter Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_Olympic_Games "Winter Olympic Games"), [debuting in 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_at_the_1972_Winter_Olympics "Philippines at the 1972 Winter Olympics"). In 2021, the Philippines received its first-ever Olympic gold medal with [weightlifter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_weightlifting "Olympic weightlifting") [Hidilyn Diaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidilyn_Diaz "Hidilyn Diaz")'s victory in [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics "2020 Summer Olympics").
