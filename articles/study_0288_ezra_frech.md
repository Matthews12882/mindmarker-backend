# Ezra Frech

**Ezra Frech** (born May 11, 2005) is an American [track and field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Track_and_field "Track and field") athlete who competes in [high jump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_jump "High jump"), [long jump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_jump "Long jump") and [sprinting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sprint_(running) "Sprint (running)") events. He is a two-time [Paralympian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paralympian "Paralympian"), having competed at the [2020 Summer Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Paralympics "2020 Summer Paralympics") and won two gold medals at the [2024 Summer Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Paralympics "2024 Summer Paralympics"). He also won two silver medals at the [2019 Parapan American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Parapan_American_Games "2019 Parapan American Games"). Frech is a [motivational speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motivational_speaker "Motivational speaker") and [disability rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disability_rights "Disability rights") advocate.

## Early life

Frech was born in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles"), [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California "California"), to [Bahar Soomekh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahar_Soomekh "Bahar Soomekh"), an actress born in [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran") and raised in Los Angeles, and Clayton Frech, an American of German heritage. He has two younger brothers, Gabriel and Elijah.

Frech was born without most of his left leg (missing a knee and [fibula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibula "Fibula")) and missing fingers on his left hand. He received his first [prosthetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosthetic "Prosthetic") leg when he was 11 months old. When he was 2 years old, Frech had surgery to remove his lower left leg and transplanted a toe from his amputated foot onto his left hand at [Boston Children's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Children%27s_Hospital "Boston Children's Hospital").

Frech is a 2023 graduate of the [Brentwood School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brentwood_School_(Los_Angeles) "Brentwood School (Los Angeles)"). In February 2024, he became the first above-the-knee amputee to commit to an [NCAA Division I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_I "NCAA Division I") track and field team at the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California "University of Southern California"). As of 2025[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ezra_Frech&action=edit), he was studying for a [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor%27s_degree "Bachelor's degree") in cinematic arts.

## Career

As a child, Frech played [basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball "Basketball"), [baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball "Baseball"), [soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer "Soccer"), and [karate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karate "Karate") before focusing on [track and field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Track_and_field "Track and field") when he was 8 years old. His first track meet was in 2013 at the [Endeavor Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Endeavor_Games&action=edit&redlink=1 "Endeavor Games (page does not exist)") in [Edmond, Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmond,_Oklahoma "Edmond, Oklahoma"), where he broke several national youth records and was inspired to continue in track and field.

Frech was a 2014 finalist for *[Sports Illustrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_Illustrated "Sports Illustrated")* Sportkid of the Year.

Frech's first international meet was at the [2019 World Para Athletics Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Para_Athletics_Junior_Championships "World Para Athletics Junior Championships"), where he won one gold and two bronze medals. He won two silver medals at the [2019 Parapan American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Parapan_American_Games "2019 Parapan American Games").

Frech was the youngest athlete to compete at the [2019 World Para Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_World_Para_Athletics_Championships "2019 World Para Athletics Championships") in [Dubai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai "Dubai"). He made the finals in all three of his events, but did not medal.

In 2021, at the delayed [2020 Summer Paralympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Paralympics "2020 Summer Paralympics") in [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo "Tokyo"), Frech placed 5th in the [high jump T63 event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2020_Summer_Paralympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_high_jump "Athletics at the 2020 Summer Paralympics – Men's high jump") with a personal best of 1.80 m (5 ft 10+3⁄4 in) (missing the podium by 3 cm) and 8th in the [long jump T63 event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2020_Summer_Paralympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_long_jump "Athletics at the 2020 Summer Paralympics – Men's long jump") with 5.85 m (19 ft 2+1⁄4 in).

At the [2023 World Para Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Para_Athletics_Championships "2023 World Para Athletics Championships") in [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris"), Frech won the gold medal in high jump T63 and set a new world record at 1.95 m (6 ft 4+3⁄4 in). He also competed in the [men's long jump T63](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Para_Athletics_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_long_jump "2023 World Para Athletics Championships – Men's long jump") event.

At the [2024 World Para Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_World_Para_Athletics_Championships "2024 World Para Athletics Championships") in [Kobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe "Kobe"), he placed 2nd in the high jump T63 and 4th in the long jump T63.

Frech broke his own world record in high jump T63 at the 2024 USA Paralympic Trials with a jump of 1.97 m (6 ft 5+1⁄2 in). He also placed first in the long jump T63, breaking the U.S. record with a jump of 7.05 m (23 ft 1+1⁄2 in).

Frech competed at the [2024 Summer Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Paralympics "2024 Summer Paralympics") in Paris, winning gold in the 100 meters T63 with a time of 12.06 seconds, and gold in the men's high jump T63 on his first jump at a height of 1.94 m (6 ft 4+1⁄4 in), which also set a Paralympic Games record.

## Philanthropy

In 2006, Frech's family founded Team Ezra to provide financial resources to organizations that serve people with physical disabilities. Frech started speaking to schools at the age of 4 to raise awareness for people with impairments as part of his organization's project.

In 2013, Frech founded Angel City Sports to provide free, year-round access to sport training, equipment, and competitive opportunities for kids and adults with physical disabilities. Angel City Sports hosts one of the largest adaptive sporting events in the country, the Angel City Games, as well as events for veterans, first responders, and youth.
