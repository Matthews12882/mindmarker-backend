# John Ternus

**John Patrick Ternus** ([/ˈtɜːrnəs/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"); born May 1975) is an American engineer and business executive who has been the [chief executive officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_executive_officer "Chief executive officer") (CEO) of [Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Inc. "Apple Inc.") since September 1, 2026. He is also a member of the company's board of directors. Ternus joined Apple's product design team in 2001 and was the company's senior vice president of hardware engineering from 2021 to 2026.

Apple has credited Ternus with work on the [iPad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPad "IPad") and [AirPods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AirPods "AirPods") product lines, and he oversaw hardware engineering during the company's [transition from Intel processors to Apple silicon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mac_transition_to_Apple_silicon "Mac transition to Apple silicon") in [Mac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mac_(computer) "Mac (computer)") computers. Upon inheriting [Dan Riccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Riccio "Dan Riccio")'s position as senior vice president of hardware engineering, it has been widely reported by tech analysts that he has radically changed Apple's hardware and design philosophy, pivoting Apple's hardware engineering into prioritizing function over form, marking a major departure from [Jony Ive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jony_Ive "Jony Ive")'s form over function focus for Apple products.

## Early life and education

John Patrick Ternus was born in May 1975 in [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California "California"), United States. He received a [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") in [mechanical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering "Mechanical engineering") from the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania "University of Pennsylvania") in 1997. While at Penn, Ternus competed on the men's swimming team there. For his senior project, he developed a [mechanical feeding arm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistive_eating_devices "Assistive eating devices") operable by individuals with [quadriplegia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadriplegia "Quadriplegia") using head movements.

## Career

Ternus began his career as a mechanical engineer designing [virtual reality headsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_reality_headset "Virtual reality headset") at Virtual Research Systems. He joined [Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Inc. "Apple Inc.") in 2001 as a member of the product design team, working first on the [Apple Cinema Display](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Cinema_Display "Apple Cinema Display"). He arrived during a pivotal era for the company shortly after [Steve Jobs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Jobs "Steve Jobs") returned and the [iMac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMac "IMac") kept revitalising the brand. In 2013, he was appointed vice president of hardware engineering under [Dan Riccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Riccio "Dan Riccio"), overseeing the development of the [AirPods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AirPods "AirPods"), [Mac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mac_(computer) "Mac (computer)") and [iPad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPad "IPad") product lines.

In 2020, he was also put in charge of [iPhone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone "IPhone") hardware, previously overseen directly by Riccio. He was also in charge of overseeing the [transition from Intel processors to Apple silicon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mac_transition_to_Apple_silicon "Mac transition to Apple silicon") in Mac computers that started the same year. He was promoted to senior vice president of hardware engineering in 2021, replacing Riccio, and was put in charge of [Apple Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Watch "Apple Watch") hardware in late 2022. *[Bloomberg News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_News "Bloomberg News")* reported that Ternus was then the "youngest member of Apple's executive team", and described him as "charismatic and well-liked".

In January 2026, Ternus was given oversight of Apple's industrial design team, a change that [Bloomberg News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_News "Bloomberg News") described as a further indication of his candidacy for the chief executive role.

During his tenure as senior vice president, Ternus was widely reported to be a potential successor to [Tim Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Cook "Tim Cook") as the company's CEO. His appointment was announced on April 20, 2026, and took effect on September 1, when he also joined Apple's board and Cook became executive chair.

## Personal life

Ternus is a [cycling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling "Cycling") and [car racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car_racing "Car racing") enthusiast, and has taken colleagues to [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_(state) "Washington (state)") state for off-road rally car racing. His hobbies include racing his [Porsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche "Porsche") at tracks such as California's [Laguna Seca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_Seca "Laguna Seca") raceway.
He is married, with children.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]
