# Toxic (2026 film)

***Toxic: A Fairy Tale for Grown-Ups*** is a 2026 Indian [psychological thriller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological_thriller "Psychological thriller") [gangster film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangster_film "Gangster film") directed by [Geetu Mohandas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geetu_Mohandas "Geetu Mohandas") and jointly produced by Venkat K. Narayana and [Yash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yash_(actor) "Yash (actor)") through [KVN Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KVN_Productions "KVN Productions") and Monster Mind Creations respectively. It stars [Yash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yash_(actor) "Yash (actor)"), [Kiara Advani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiara_Advani "Kiara Advani"), [Nayanthara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nayanthara "Nayanthara"), [Huma Qureshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huma_Qureshi "Huma Qureshi"), [Tara Sutaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tara_Sutaria "Tara Sutaria"), and [Rukmini Vasanth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rukmini_Vasanth "Rukmini Vasanth").

The film was officially announced in December 2023 under the tentative title *Yash 19*, as it is the 19th film of Yash as a leading actor, with the official title revealed a few days later. The film is reportedly budgeted at ₹500–1000 crore, making it one of the [most expensive Indian films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_expensive_Indian_films "List of most expensive Indian films") ever made. [Principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography "Principal photography") took place from August 2024 to October 2025 across [Bengaluru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengaluru "Bengaluru"), [Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai "Mumbai"), [Goa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goa "Goa"), [Thoothukudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoothukudi "Thoothukudi") and [Jaipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaipur "Jaipur") simultaneously shot in Kannada and English.

*Toxic* was released worldwide on 26 August 2026. The film received mostly negative reviews from critics, who criticised its screenplay, pacing, themes, substance abuse, sexual content and excessive violence, though the performances (particularly of Yash and Kiara) and action sequences received some praise.

Despite the film becoming the [sixth highest-grossing Indian film of 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_films_of_2026#Box_office_collection "List of Indian films of 2026") and [fourth highest-grossing Kannada film of all-time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_Kannada_films "List of highest-grossing Kannada films"), the film was a massive [box-office bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box-office_bomb "Box-office bomb"), grossing [₹](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_rupee "Indian rupee")320 [crore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crore "Crore") (US$33 million)–[₹](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_rupee "Indian rupee")341.39 [crore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crore "Crore") (US$35 million) against an estimated production budget of ₹500 crore (US$52 million) to ₹1,000 crore (US$100 million).

## Plot

The film follows Raya, a gangster in pre-independence [Goa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goa "Goa"), under [Portuguese rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_India "Portuguese India"). In the 1920s, his mother kills his father, leaving his elder sister, Ganga, to raise him. In 1947, Raya and Ganga avenge their father's death by killing gangster Fernandez Almeida, then begin working for his brother, Salvador Almeida. Raya eventually becomes a notorious and ruthless underworld figure and enters a relationship with Salvador's daughter, Rebecca. His growing power, however, provokes the jealousy of Salvador's mistress, Elizabeth, and her brother, Tony. During a visit to a circus, Raya is attacked by assassins disguised as clowns and meets its popular performer, Nadia. After killing the attackers, he investigates the assassination attempt and, with Nadia's help, discovers Tony's involvement. Tony takes responsibility to protect Elizabeth, who is forced to kill him as a test of loyalty in front of Raya, Ganga and Salvador.

Raya falls in love with Nadia, beginning a relationship that strains his bond with Rebecca. Salvador and Ganga urge him to marry Rebecca and abandon Nadia, and he reluctantly agrees. On his wedding night, however, Raya learns that Nadia is pregnant with his child and that Salvador intends to kill them. Raya kills Salvador, but Rebecca shoots him during the ensuing gunfight, prompting Ganga to kill Rebecca. Raya escapes with the help of his friend Karmadi, while Ganga sacrifices herself to secure his escape. Seeking revenge, Elizabeth forces Nadia to kill Ganga, destroying Nadia and Raya's relationship. Raya kills Elizabeth but, devastated by Ganga's death, leaves Goa, abandoning Nadia and their son, Ticket. Nadia subsequently spirals into despair.

Years later, following the Portuguese withdrawal from Goa in the 1960s, Raya returns from expanding his criminal operations overseas, including in [Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macau "Macau"). He visits Nadia's circus but refuses to see her, prompting her to commit suicide. Raya then takes Ticket for a drive but, while drunk, accidentally drives off a cliff into the sea. Although he saves his son, his distressed behaviour frightens Ticket away.

Raya continues to expand his criminal empire while deteriorating emotionally. He eventually meets Melissa and marries her. On the day she gives birth to their son, Adam, Raya is attacked by assassins, one of whom appears to be Ticket, seeking revenge for his father's past actions. As an adult, Ticket begins systematically destroying Raya's businesses and turns his father's ally, Xian Wei King, against him by killing King's son. On Christmas Eve, Ticket breaks into Raya's house, prompting Melissa to leave with Adam. Raya attempts to stop them, but Ticket captures both father and son and tries to kill them. Raya overpowers Ticket but spares his life. Later, the two argue bitterly, with Ticket confronting Raya over his mistakes and the suffering he has inflicted on those around him.

Overcome with anger and guilt, Raya urges Ticket to kill him, but Ticket refuses. The following morning, Melissa arrives with the police to retrieve Adam. Raya and Ticket briefly unite to fight the police, and Raya attempts to escape with Adam. When Ticket tries to reconcile with his father, Raya tells him to leave and asks his name. Enraged, Ticket shoots Raya in the head. It is then revealed that Raya and Ticket are the same person: Ticket is a [split personality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split_personality "Split personality") created by Raya, while the real Ticket died in the car accident years earlier after Raya failed to save him.

Realising the truth, Raya sends Adam away with Melissa before experiencing [hallucinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallucinations "Hallucinations") of his deceased loved ones. He joins a battle between his men and Xian Wei King's forces, seeking revenge for King's son, whom he killed while acting as Ticket. Karmadi and Raya's remaining men are killed. Still hallucinating, Raya fights alone, shoots down King's plane, killing him, and triggers an [avalanche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avalanche "Avalanche") that sweeps away the remaining enemy forces. As the avalanche approaches, Raya sees a hallucination of young Ticket and kneels to embrace him, leaving his ultimate fate unknown.

## Production

### Development

In April 2023, [Yash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yash_(actor) "Yash (actor)") was reported to be preparing his next film after the [*KGF* film franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGF_(film_series) "KGF (film series)") (2018–present) with [Geetu Mohandas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geetu_Mohandas "Geetu Mohandas"). The project was being referred to as *Yash 19*, meaning Yash's 19th film as a lead actor. Reports said Geetu was developing it as her next directorial project and the two had committed to the collaboration. Some reports at the time claimed filming could begin in June 2023, but that schedule did not materialise. Geethu described her next film as a [gangster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangster_film "Gangster film") [drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama_(film_and_television) "Drama (film and television)") film with a female perspective and explained that, for the first time, she was working with a writing room rather than writing alone. In late September 2023, [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_the_United_States "Cinema of the United States") action choreographer [J. J. Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._J._Perry "J. J. Perry") posted a photograph on [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram "Instagram") with himself and Yash, hinting at a possible collaboration. The project was officially unveiled on 8 December 2023. [KVN Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KVN_Productions "KVN Productions") and Yash's Monster Mind Creations were announced as production partners, with Geethu Mohandas confirmed as director. The title announcement revealed the original release date, 10 April 2025.

In January 2024, cinematographer [Rajeev Ravi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajeev_Ravi "Rajeev Ravi") was announced as part of the film's technical crew. In January 2025, Geetu stated that Yash was also part of the film's writing team. *Toxic* is reportedly budgeted at ₹500–1,000 crore, making it one of the [most expensive Indian films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_expensive_Indian_films "List of most expensive Indian films") ever made.

[Huma Qureshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huma_Qureshi "Huma Qureshi") joined the cast in June 2024 for a key negative role. In January 2025, Oberoi confirmed that [Nayanthara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nayanthara "Nayanthara") joined the cast for a key role. [Sudev Nair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudev_Nair "Sudev Nair") joined the cast in April 2025 while [Rukmini Vasanth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rukmini_Vasanth "Rukmini Vasanth") joined the cast in August 2025 following the completion of *[Kantara: Chapter 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kantara:_Chapter_1 "Kantara: Chapter 1")*. Various foreign actors like [Darrell D'Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darrell_D%27Silva "Darrell D'Silva"), Kyle Paul, and Beatriz Taufenbach featured in the film.

### Filming

[Principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography "Principal photography") commenced in August 2024 in [Bengaluru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengaluru "Bengaluru") along with the pooja ceremony. As part of the production process, the film utilised [previsualisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Previsualisation "Previsualisation") to plan stunt sequences and ensure safety. A major portion of the shoot began in September 2024 at a large-scale 20-acre set near [Bengaluru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengaluru "Bengaluru"), recreating the period from the 1940s to the 1970s. The production involved an extensive team of over 1,000 crew members and 450 actors, including foreign extras. In October 2024, some sequences were filmed in the forest areas of [Peenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peenya "Peenya") in Bengaluru. Later, an [FIR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Information_Report "First Information Report") was filed against the producer for allegedly cutting trees in the forest area without permission. In November 2024, sequences featuring Advani were shot in [Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai "Mumbai"). A dance sequence featuring Yash and Advani was choreographed by [Ganesh Acharya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganesh_Acharya "Ganesh Acharya") in January 2025 in [Goa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goa "Goa"). In February 2025, Yash alongside Advani, Nayanthara, Qureshi and Sutaria shot several sequences in Bengaluru. In June 2025, the film's remaining shoot was shifted to Mumbai from Bengaluru so as to facilitate Advani's pregnancy. In September 2025, a massive 45 day shoot featuring many action sequences choreographed by [J. J. Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._J._Perry "J. J. Perry") was completed in Mumbai. In October 2025, the final schedule was filmed in Bengaluru.

Additionally shooting also took place in [Thoothukudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoothukudi "Thoothukudi"), where an action sequence was filmed, while a special song sequence was also shot in [Jaipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaipur "Jaipur").

## Release

### Theatrical

*Toxic* was released worldwide on 26 August 2026, coinciding with [Onam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onam "Onam"); in the standard, [IMAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAX "IMAX"), [Dolby Cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolby_Cinema "Dolby Cinema"), [4DX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4DX "4DX") and [EPIQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EPIQ_(format) "EPIQ (format)") formats. Apart from the film's original [Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada "Kannada") and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language "English language") languages, it was also released in the [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi "Hindi"), [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language "Tamil language"), [Telugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_language "Telugu language") and [Malayalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam "Malayalam") languages. The film's English version was released in India on 4 September 2026 with a shorter runtime of 176 minutes.

Originally, it was scheduled for release on 10 April 2025 but was eventually postponed due to delayed shooting and production issues. Then, it was announced for release on 19 March 2026; however, it was again postponed due to the ongoing [crisis in Middle East Countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war "2026 Iran war"), as well as to avoid a clash with the Hindi film *[Dhurandhar: The Revenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhurandhar:_The_Revenge "Dhurandhar: The Revenge")*. Later, it was announced for release on 4 June 2026, but was postponed again due to alignments for a better global distribution. The film was reportedly released in 12,000 screens worldwide.

The distribution rights across [Tamil Nadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_Nadu "Tamil Nadu") were acquired by Think Studios, S Picture, [Trident Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trident_Arts "Trident Arts") and Five Star Senthil for ₹63 crore (US$6.5 million). ## Reception

### Box office

*Toxic: A Fairy Tale for Grown-Ups* grossed ₹340 crores in 18 days. It grossed ₹337.80 crore (US$35 million) worldwide. It grossed ₹294.09 crore (US$31 million) in India and ₹43.71 crore (US$4.5 million) in the [overseas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaspora "Diaspora") market.

The film earned ₹239.30 crore (US$25 million) net in India, of which ₹130.29 crore (US$14 million) net was from Hindi version.

On its opening day, the film collected a total India net of ₹101.10 crore (US$10 million), including ₹50.31 crore (US$5.2 million)–₹55 crore (US$5.7 million) from Hindi version with total India gross collections at ₹120.75 crore (US$13 million). Worldwide, it grossed ₹140.75 crore (US$15 million) on its opening day.

### Critical response

On the [review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator "Review aggregator") website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes"), 21% of 28 critics' reviews are positive, with an average rating of 4.60/10.

Latha Srinivasan of *[NDTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDTV "NDTV")* gave the film 3 stars out of 5, concluding, "*Toxic* is not the gangster Hollywood-style epic one expected (it has some shades of *[KGF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGF_(film_series) "KGF (film series)")*) but it's an ambitious effort undertaken by Yash and Geetu Mohandas to give the Indian audience a unique experiment and it delivers on that for the most part." Sridevi S. of *[The Times of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times_of_India "The Times of India")* also gave the film 3 stars out of 5. She appreciated the visual presentation, cinematography, [world-building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worldbuilding "Worldbuilding"), ambition behind the project, acting performances (especially that of Kiara Advani and Yash), screen presence of Yash, set design and action sequences. However, she criticised the screenplay, editing, music and pointed out the lack of cohesion in the plot and lack of depth in the characters. She critiqued, "Yet Toxic often mistakes visual grandeur for storytelling depth. The film soon begins to feel like a series of [Instagram reels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reels_(Meta) "Reels (Meta)") stitched together: each segment looks like something and feels like something, but ultimately adds up to very little. Beneath the violence, sex, swagger and scale, the screenplay needed considerably more substance." A critic at *[Zoom TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoom_(Indian_TV_channel) "Zoom (Indian TV channel)")* gave the film 3 stars out of 5 as well and observed, "Despite its ambitious scope and a high-profile ensemble cast, the film presents a mixed experience that balances visual spectacle with narrative inconsistencies."

Zinia Bandyopadhyay of *[Firstpost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firstpost "Firstpost")* gave the film 2.5 stars out of 5, praising the acting performances of Yash and the ensemble cast despite the 'limited screen time' of latter but criticising the 'sluggish pacing.' She wrote, "My biggest complaint, however, remains the editing. Everything seems to run in slow motion. Dialogues are slowed down to give them weight, scenes are stretched for impact and the pace before the interval feels deliberately held back. At times, it feels as though you are watching the movie in 0.75x speed. At 3 hours and 15 minutes, *Toxic* could have benefited from some serious trimming." Siddhant Adlakha of *[IGN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IGN "IGN")* gave the film a 5 out of 10 review score, concluding, "Led by megastar Yash in a dual role, the decades-spanning gangster saga Toxic: A Fairy Tale for Grown-Ups is oppressively overbearing and cut within an inch of its life in the vein of a modern movie trailer. While it eventually coheres in its final minutes with a reveal that — although over-explained — brings the themes to a grand crescendo, it's hard to say the 3-hour buildup is worth the wait."

[Kaveree Bamzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaveree_Bamzai "Kaveree Bamzai") of *[Open Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_(Indian_magazine) "Open (Indian magazine)")* gave the film 2 stars out of 5 and concluded in her review, "So many women, so little time. What you get is beautiful gowns for gorgeous women, some ghosts from the past plaguing Raya, and set pieces costing the [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_domestic_product "Gross domestic product") of a minor state, and then, like a circus trick, poof, it's all gone. But not before an interminable running time tests one's patience." [Sukanya Verma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukanya_Verma "Sukanya Verma") of *[Rediff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rediff.com "Rediff.com")* also gave the film 2 stars out of 5, noting "Purely relying on Yash's aura to the point of draining him dry, Toxic doesn't have an smidgen of storytelling in its body rolling in sex, shock and sounds of bullets fired at anyone and everyone." A critic at *[Bollywood Hungama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bollywood_Hungama "Bollywood Hungama")* gave the film 2 stars out of 5 as well, concluding, "On the whole, TOXIC relies far too heavily on style, swagger and the familiar KGF-esque aura of Yash, while falling short on a compelling story and emotional connect. The performances, scale, action and technical finesse provide occasional highs, but the uneven screenplay, sluggish portions and a climax twist that is unlikely to resonate with the mass audience prove to be major deterrents." Arfa Javaid of *[ABP Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABP_News "ABP News")*, too, gave the film 2 stars out of 5, noting "Toxic is an over-the-top action spectacle rescued by Yash, but weighed down by a screenplay that struggles to give all that violence, sex and visual excess a compelling purpose." Likewise, Sanjay Ponnappa of *[India Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_Today "India Today")* gave the film 2 stars out of 5, remarking, "The biggest problem is the film's lack of clarity about what it wants to be. Is it a gangster drama? An emotional drama? A [psychological thriller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological_thriller "Psychological thriller")? It tries to dabble in all of these, but never quite commits to any one of them. Being a [jack of all trades and master of none](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_of_all_trades "Jack of all trades") can still work if the film manages to keep you invested. But what happens when the card you're dealt is a dud? That is, unfortunately, what *Toxic* feels like."

[Shubhra Gupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shubhra_Gupta "Shubhra Gupta") of *[The Indian Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Indian_Express "The Indian Express")* gave the film 1.5 stars out of 5, writing "It may be Geetu Mohandas at the helm, but it is finally, and irrevocably, a Rocking Star Yash film, with eye-glazing, endless litany of violence." Pranati A. S. of *[Deccan Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Herald "Deccan Herald")* also gave the film 1.5 stars out of 5, summing up in her review, "Perhaps it is time to say this again: [Godard's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Luc_Godard "Jean-Luc Godard") famous line, "All you need to make a movie is a girl and a gun", was never meant to be taken this literally."

Debanjan Dhar of *[Outlook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlook_(Indian_magazine) "Outlook (Indian magazine)")* gave the film 1 star out of 5, noting "Director Geetu Mohandas probes how far men with vain, violently maniacal impulses can go, but her design is almost cartoonish and her voice is dulled by studio artillery, despite DP Rajeev Ravi's extravagant mounting." Neeshita Nyayapati of *[Hindustan Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustan_Times "Hindustan Times")* also gave the film 1 star out of 5, summing up in her review, "Toxic tries to be the antithesis of glorifying problematic characters but ends up pandering to them." Mayank Shekhar of *[Mid-Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Day "Mid-Day")* gave the film 1 star out of 5 as well and was critical towards the storytelling and unclear character motivations.

Sruthi Ganapathy Raman of *[The Hollywood Reporter India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter_India "The Hollywood Reporter India")* wrote in her review, "The star's charisma gets him and his character out of a lot of sticky situations in Geetu Mohandas's 'Toxic,' but is still not enough to save the film's inconsistent and lavishly hollow drama." Nandini Ramnath of *[Scroll.in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scroll.in "Scroll.in")* juxtaposed, "Despite the opportunity to populate an overtly male genre with female characters, *Toxic* peddles tiresome stereotypes about damaging mothers and damaged single mothers. There are sharper insights into [toxic masculinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toxic_masculinity "Toxic masculinity") from [Sandeep Reddy Vanga's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandeep_Reddy_Vanga "Sandeep Reddy Vanga") *[Animal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_(2023_Indian_film) "Animal (2023 Indian film)")*, which, while valourising its hero, also provides a glimpse into the roots of his derangement."

Vivek M.V. of *[The Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hindu "The Hindu")* highlighted the blurred lines of distinction between *Toxic* and *KGF* writing, "With *Toxic*, Yash makes it clear that he is, above all, an image-conscious star. The dialogues of his character, which he is now understood to have written, feel like they belong to the *KGF* universe. Even his dialogue delivery seems like an extension of his performance in *KGF*. None of it fits the world of *Toxic*." Sowmya Rajendran of *[The News Minute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_News_Minute "The News Minute")* appreciated Kiara Advani particularly, writing, "The real show-stopper, however, is Kiara Advani as Nadia. Her luminescent beauty lends a fairytale quality to the scenes, mingling the princess archetype of innocence and purity with a luscious sensuality that makes her ['Other Woman'](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/other_woman "wikt:other woman") role irresistible." She also had praise for Yash's portrayal as Ticket and wrote, "Yash also plays Ticket, Raya's son, who appears to be part *Animal*, part *[Joker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joker_(character) "Joker (character)")*. Here, too, there is manic energy and deadpan comedy, but the actor also brings a certain shattered vulnerability to the role. This is crucial because beneath all the glitz and glamour, *Toxic* aspires to be a [character study](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/character_study "wikt:character study")." However, she criticised the lack of subversion in the film, as a counterweight against toxicity, writing, "When the toxicity offers so many concrete pay-offs, the subversion also has to do that to dilute its impact. Instead, the unravelling, set in a snowy fairyland with a montage of abstract visuals, does not have the same power over the audience."

BVS Prakash of *[Deccan Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Chronicle "Deccan Chronicle")* critiqued, "The problem is that the fictional world created by director Geetu Mohandas never manages to draw the audience in. Instead of building a compelling narrative around its period setting and gangster backdrop, the film relies heavily on violence, gore, intimacy and glamour. The result is an over-the-top action adventure with very little substance." He juxtaposed, "Unlike KGF, where the core story had a strong emotional and relatable foundation that made Yash's larger-than-life heroics work, Toxic feels contrived from the outset. The film's premise of a [power struggle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_struggle "Power struggle") surrounding [Portugal-ruled Goa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_India "Portuguese India"), opium and casinos is difficult to buy, and the narrative repeatedly sacrifices logic for style."

### Thematic analysis

Sreeju Sudhakaran of *[Rediff.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rediff.com "Rediff.com")* pointed out, "It is deeply ironic that a film helmed by a female director, featuring five major actresses, utterly fails the [Bechdel Test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bechdel_Test "Bechdel Test")."

Before the film's release, director Geetu Mohandas described it as a gangster film with a "female gaze". Yash also said that Mohandas brought a different perspective to the depiction of desire, romance and sensuality in the film. After its release, however, some critics argued that the female characters remained largely defined by their relationships with the male protagonist, raising questions about how fully the film realised its stated perspective.

## Controversies

Following the release of the glimpse video for one of Yash's characters in the film, the Women's wing of [Aam Aadmi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aam_Aadmi_Party "Aam Aadmi Party") submitted a complaint to the [Karnataka State Commission for Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnataka_State_Commission_for_Women "Karnataka State Commission for Women") as they had objections with the obscene visuals featured in the teaser. In February 2026, another formal complaint was lodged to the [CBFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Board_of_Film_Certification "Central Board of Film Certification") by the National Christian Federation where they alleged that the film's teaser depicts obscene scenes and also hurts religious sentiments by including a statue of [Archangel Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_(archangel) "Michael (archangel)") in the fight sequence inside the [cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cemetery "Cemetery").

Following the film's Hyderabad pre-release event in [Malla Reddy University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malla_Reddy_University "Malla Reddy University") that was held on 22 August, a case was registered against the event organisers and management after a scaffolding-mounted soundbox collapsed, which injured five people.
