# Elizabeth II

**Elizabeth II** (Elizabeth Alexandra Mary; 21 April 1926 – 8 September 2022) was [Queen of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_of_the_United_Kingdom "Queen of the United Kingdom") and other [Commonwealth realms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_realm "Commonwealth realm") from 6 February 1952 until [her death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_state_funeral_of_Elizabeth_II "Death and state funeral of Elizabeth II") in 2022. She was [queen regnant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_regnant "Queen regnant") of [32 sovereign states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states_headed_by_Elizabeth_II "List of sovereign states headed by Elizabeth II") during her lifetime and was the monarch of 15 realms at her death. Her reign of 70 years and 214 days is the [longest of any British monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_monarchs_in_Britain_by_length_of_reign "List of monarchs in Britain by length of reign"), the [second-longest of any sovereign state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_longest-reigning_monarchs "List of longest-reigning monarchs"), and the [longest of any queen regnant in history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_female_monarchs "List of female monarchs").

Elizabeth was born in [Mayfair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayfair "Mayfair"), London, during the reign of her paternal grandfather, [King George V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_George_V "King George V"). She was the first child of the Duke and Duchess of York (later [King George VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_George_VI "King George VI") and [Queen Elizabeth the Queen Mother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_the_Queen_Mother "Queen Elizabeth the Queen Mother")). Her father acceded to the throne in 1936 upon [the abdication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdication_of_Edward_VIII "Abdication of Edward VIII") of his brother [Edward VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_VIII "Edward VIII"), making the ten-year-old Princess Elizabeth the [heir presumptive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heir_presumptive "Heir presumptive"). She was educated privately at home and began to undertake public duties during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War "Second World War"), serving in the [Auxiliary Territorial Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auxiliary_Territorial_Service "Auxiliary Territorial Service"). In November 1947, [she married](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedding_of_Princess_Elizabeth_and_Philip_Mountbatten "Wedding of Princess Elizabeth and Philip Mountbatten") a former [prince of Greece and Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Greece_and_Denmark "Prince of Greece and Denmark"), [Philip Mountbatten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Mountbatten "Philip Mountbatten"). They had four children: [Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_III "Charles III"), [Anne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne,_Princess_Royal "Anne, Princess Royal"), [Andrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Mountbatten-Windsor "Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor"), and [Edward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Edward,_Duke_of_Edinburgh "Prince Edward, Duke of Edinburgh").

When [her father died](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_state_funeral_of_George_VI "Death and state funeral of George VI") in February 1952, Elizabeth, then 25 years old, became queen of seven independent Commonwealth countries and [head of the Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_the_Commonwealth "Head of the Commonwealth"). She reigned as a [constitutional monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarch "Constitutional monarch") through times of significant political change such as [the Troubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Troubles "The Troubles") in Northern Ireland, [devolution in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution_in_the_United_Kingdom "Devolution in the United Kingdom"), the [decolonisation of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation_of_Africa "Decolonisation of Africa"), and the [United Kingdom's accession to the European Communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom%27s_accession_to_the_European_Communities "United Kingdom's accession to the European Communities") as well as its [subsequent withdrawal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brexit "Brexit"). The number of her realms varied over time as territories gained independence and as most realms [became republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republics_in_the_Commonwealth_of_Nations "Republics in the Commonwealth of Nations"). As queen, Elizabeth was advised by [more than 170 prime ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_prime_ministers_of_Elizabeth_II "List of prime ministers of Elizabeth II") across her realms. Her many historic visits and meetings included [state visits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_state_visits_made_by_Elizabeth_II "List of state visits made by Elizabeth II") [to China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_visit_by_Elizabeth_II_to_China "State visit by Elizabeth II to China") in 1986, [to Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_visit_by_Elizabeth_II_to_Russia "State visit by Elizabeth II to Russia") in 1994, and [to the Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_visit_by_Elizabeth_II_to_the_Republic_of_Ireland "State visit by Elizabeth II to the Republic of Ireland") in 2011; she met five popes and fourteen US presidents.

Significant events of Elizabeth's reign included [her coronation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation_of_Elizabeth_II "Coronation of Elizabeth II") in 1953 and the celebrations of her [Silver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Jubilee_of_Elizabeth_II "Silver Jubilee of Elizabeth II"), [Golden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Jubilee_of_Elizabeth_II "Golden Jubilee of Elizabeth II"), [Diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond_Jubilee_of_Elizabeth_II "Diamond Jubilee of Elizabeth II"), and [Platinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum_Jubilee_of_Elizabeth_II "Platinum Jubilee of Elizabeth II") [jubilees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jubilee "Jubilee"). Although there was occasional [republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republicanism_in_the_United_Kingdom "Republicanism in the United Kingdom") sentiment and media criticism of her family – particularly after the breakdowns of her children's marriages, her *[annus horribilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annus_horribilis#Elizabeth_II "Annus horribilis")* in 1992, and [the death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Diana,_Princess_of_Wales "Death of Diana, Princess of Wales") of her former daughter-in-law [Diana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diana,_Princess_of_Wales "Diana, Princess of Wales") in 1997 – support for the monarchy and her popularity in the United Kingdom remained consistently high. Elizabeth died aged 96 at [Balmoral Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balmoral_Castle "Balmoral Castle") in Scotland, and was succeeded by her eldest son, Charles III.

## Early life

Elizabeth was born at 2:40 am on 21 April 1926 by [Caesarean section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesarean_section "Caesarean section") at her maternal grandfather's London home, [17 Bruton Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17_Bruton_Street "17 Bruton Street") in [Mayfair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayfair "Mayfair"), the first child of [Prince Albert, Duke of York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Albert,_Duke_of_York "Prince Albert, Duke of York") (later King George VI), and his wife, [Elizabeth, Duchess of York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth,_Duchess_of_York "Elizabeth, Duchess of York") (later Queen Elizabeth the Queen Mother). Her father was the second son of [King George V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_George_V "King George V") and [Queen Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_of_Teck "Mary of Teck"), and her mother was the youngest daughter of Scottish aristocrat [Claude Bowes-Lyon, 14th Earl of Strathmore and Kinghorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Bowes-Lyon,_14th_Earl_of_Strathmore_and_Kinghorne "Claude Bowes-Lyon, 14th Earl of Strathmore and Kinghorne"), and his wife [Cecilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecilia_Bowes-Lyon,_Countess_of_Strathmore_and_Kinghorne "Cecilia Bowes-Lyon, Countess of Strathmore and Kinghorne") ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") Cavendish-Bentinck). She was [baptised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptised "Baptised") in the private chapel at [Buckingham Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckingham_Palace "Buckingham Palace") on 29 May by the [Archbishop of York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_York "Archbishop of York"), [Cosmo Gordon Lang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmo_Gordon_Lang "Cosmo Gordon Lang"), and was named Elizabeth after her mother; Alexandra after [her paternal great-grandmother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandra_of_Denmark "Alexandra of Denmark"), who had [died five months earlier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_funeral_of_Alexandra_of_Denmark "Death and funeral of Alexandra of Denmark"); and Mary after her paternal grandmother. Her close family affectionately called her "Lilibet", based on what she called herself at first. She was cherished by her grandfather George V, whom she affectionately called "Grandpa England", and her regular visits during his serious illness in 1929 were credited in the popular press and by later biographers with raising his spirits and aiding his recovery.

Elizabeth's sole sibling, [Princess Margaret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Margaret "Princess Margaret"), was born in 1930. They were cared for by their nanny, [Clara Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clara_Knight_(nanny) "Clara Knight (nanny)"), and educated at home under the supervision of their mother and their [governess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governess "Governess"), [Marion Crawford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marion_Crawford "Marion Crawford"). Lessons concentrated on history, language, literature, and music. Crawford published a biography of Elizabeth and Margaret's childhood years titled *[The Little Princesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Little_Princesses "The Little Princesses")* in 1950, much to the dismay of the [royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_royal_family "British royal family"). The book describes Elizabeth's love of horses and dogs, her orderliness, and her attitude of responsibility. Others echoed such observations: [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill "Winston Churchill") described Elizabeth when she was two as "a character. She has an air of authority and reflectiveness astonishing in an infant." Her cousin [Margaret Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Rhodes "Margaret Rhodes") described her as "a jolly little girl, but fundamentally sensible and well-behaved". Elizabeth's early life was spent primarily at the Yorks' residences at [145 Piccadilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/145_Piccadilly "145 Piccadilly") (their [town house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_house "Town house") in London) and [Royal Lodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Lodge "Royal Lodge") in Windsor.

## Heir presumptive

During her grandfather's reign, Elizabeth was third in the [line of succession to the British throne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_of_succession_to_the_British_throne "Line of succession to the British throne"), behind her uncle [Edward, Prince of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_VIII "Edward VIII"), and her father. Although her birth generated public interest, she was not expected to become queen, as Edward was still young and expected to marry and have children of his own, who would precede Elizabeth in the line of succession. When [her grandfather died](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_state_funeral_of_George_V "Death and state funeral of George V") in 1936 and her uncle succeeded as Edward VIII, she became second in line to the throne, after her father. Later that year, [Edward abdicated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdication_of_Edward_VIII "Abdication of Edward VIII"), after his proposed marriage to divorced American socialite [Wallis Simpson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallis_Simpson "Wallis Simpson") provoked a [constitutional crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_crisis "Constitutional crisis"). Consequently, Elizabeth's father became king, taking the [regnal name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regnal_name "Regnal name") George VI. Since Elizabeth had no brothers, she became [heir presumptive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heir_presumptive "Heir presumptive"). If her parents had subsequently had a son, he would have been [heir apparent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heir_apparent "Heir apparent") and before her in the line of succession, which was determined by the [male-preference primogeniture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Male-preference_primogeniture "Male-preference primogeniture") in effect at the time.

Elizabeth received private tuition in [constitutional history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_history "Constitutional history") from [Henry Marten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Marten_(educator) "Henry Marten (educator)"), [Vice-Provost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_provosts_of_Eton_College "List of provosts of Eton College") of [Eton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eton_College "Eton College"), and learned French from a succession of native-speaking governesses. A [Girl Guides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girl_Guides "Girl Guides") company, the [1st Buckingham Palace Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Buckingham_Palace_Company "1st Buckingham Palace Company"), was formed specifically so she could socialise with girls her age. Later, she was enrolled as a [Sea Ranger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranger_(Girl_Guide) "Ranger (Girl Guide)").

In 1939, Elizabeth's parents [toured Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1939_royal_tour_of_Canada "1939 royal tour of Canada") and the United States. As in 1927, when they had [toured Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_tours_of_Australia "Royal tours of Australia") and [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_visits_to_New_Zealand#1951–2000 "Royal visits to New Zealand"), Elizabeth remained in Britain since her father thought she was too young to undertake public tours. She "looked tearful" as her parents departed. They corresponded regularly, and she and her parents made the first royal [transatlantic telephone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transatlantic_telephone "Transatlantic telephone") call on 18 May.

### Second World War

In September 1939, [Britain entered the Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_declaration_of_war_on_Germany_(1939) "United Kingdom declaration of war on Germany (1939)"). [Lord Hailsham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Hogg,_1st_Viscount_Hailsham "Douglas Hogg, 1st Viscount Hailsham") suggested that Elizabeth and Margaret should be [evacuated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evacuations_of_civilians_in_Britain_during_World_War_II "Evacuations of civilians in Britain during World War II") to Canada to avoid the frequent [aerial bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_bombing_of_cities "Aerial bombing of cities") of London by the *[Luftwaffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luftwaffe "Luftwaffe")*. This was rejected by their mother, who declared, "The children won't go without me. I won't leave without the King. And the King will never leave." The princesses stayed at [Balmoral Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balmoral_Castle "Balmoral Castle"), Scotland, until Christmas 1939, when they moved to [Sandringham House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandringham_House "Sandringham House"), Norfolk. From February to May 1940, they lived at Royal Lodge, Windsor, until moving to [Windsor Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windsor_Castle "Windsor Castle"), where they lived for most of the next five years. At Windsor, [the princesses staged pantomimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princesses_Elizabeth_and_Margaret%27s_Christmas_pantomimes "Princesses Elizabeth and Margaret's Christmas pantomimes") at Christmas in aid of the Queen's Wool Fund, which bought [yarn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarn "Yarn") to knit into military garments. In 1940, the 14-year-old Elizabeth made her first radio broadcast during the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC")'s *[Children's Hour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children%27s_Hour "Children's Hour")*, addressing other children who had been evacuated from the cities. She stated: "We are trying to do all we can to help our gallant sailors, soldiers, and airmen, and we are trying, too, to bear our own share of the danger and sadness of war. We know, every one of us, that in the end all will be well."

In 1943, Elizabeth undertook her first solo public appearance on a visit to the [Grenadier Guards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenadier_Guards "Grenadier Guards"), of which she had been appointed [colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel "Colonel") the previous year. As she approached her 18th birthday, Parliament changed the law so that she could act as one of five [counsellors of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counsellors_of_state "Counsellors of state") in the event of her father's incapacity or absence abroad, such as his visit to Italy in July 1944. In February 1945, she was appointed an honorary [second subaltern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auxiliary_Territorial_Service#Ranks "Auxiliary Territorial Service") in the Auxiliary Territorial Service with the [service number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_number "Service number") 230873. She trained as a driver and mechanic and was given the rank of honorary junior commander (female equivalent of [captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_(British_Army_and_Royal_Marines) "Captain (British Army and Royal Marines)") at the time) five months later.

At the end of the war in Europe, on [Victory in Europe Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_in_Europe_Day "Victory in Europe Day"), Elizabeth and Margaret mingled incognito with the celebrating crowds in the streets of London. In 1985, Elizabeth recalled in a rare interview, "... we asked my parents if we could go out and see for ourselves. I remember we were terrified of being recognised ... I remember lines of unknown people linking arms and walking down [Whitehall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitehall "Whitehall"), all of us just swept along on a tide of happiness and relief."

During the war, plans were drawn to quell [Welsh nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_nationalism "Welsh nationalism") by affiliating Elizabeth more closely with Wales. Proposals, such as appointing her Constable of [Caernarfon Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caernarfon_Castle "Caernarfon Castle") or a patron of [Urdd Gobaith Cymru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdd_Gobaith_Cymru "Urdd Gobaith Cymru") (the Welsh League of Youth), were abandoned for several reasons, including fear of associating Elizabeth with [conscientious objectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscientious_objector "Conscientious objector") in the Urdd at a time when Britain was at war. Welsh politicians suggested she be made [Princess of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_of_Wales "Princess of Wales") on her 18th birthday. Home Secretary [Herbert Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Morrison "Herbert Morrison") supported the idea, but the King rejected it because he felt such a title belonged solely to the wife of a [prince of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Wales "Prince of Wales") and the prince of Wales had always been the heir apparent. In 1946, she was inducted into [the Gorsedd of Bards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gorsedd_of_Bards "The Gorsedd of Bards") at the [National Eisteddfod of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Eisteddfod_of_Wales "National Eisteddfod of Wales").

Elizabeth went on her first overseas tour in 1947, accompanying her parents through southern Africa. During the tour, in [a broadcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_birthday_speech_of_Princess_Elizabeth "21st birthday speech of Princess Elizabeth") to the [British Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Commonwealth "British Commonwealth") on her 21st birthday, she made the following pledge:

> I declare before you all that my whole life, whether it be long or short, shall be devoted to your service and the service of our great imperial family to which we all belong. But I shall not have strength to carry out this resolution alone unless you join in it with me, as I now invite you to do: I know that your support will be unfailingly given. God help me to make good my vow, and God bless all of you who are willing to share in it.

### Marriage

Elizabeth met her future husband, [Prince Philip of Greece and Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Philip_of_Greece_and_Denmark "Prince Philip of Greece and Denmark"), in 1934 and again in 1937. They were [second cousins once removed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cousin#Cousin_chart "Cousin") through [King Christian IX of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Christian_IX_of_Denmark "King Christian IX of Denmark") and third cousins through [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria "Queen Victoria"). After meeting for the third time at the [Royal Naval College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britannia_Royal_Naval_College "Britannia Royal Naval College") in [Dartmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth,_Devon "Dartmouth, Devon") in July 1939, Elizabeth – though only 13 years old – said she fell in love with Philip, who was 18, and they began to exchange letters. She was 21 when their engagement was officially announced on 9 July 1947.

The engagement attracted some controversy. Philip had no financial standing, was foreign-born (though a [British subject](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_subject "British subject") who had served in the [Royal Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Navy "Royal Navy") throughout the Second World War), and his sisters had married German noblemen with [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi "Nazi") links. Marion Crawford wrote, "Some of the King's advisors did not think him good enough for her. He was a prince without a home or kingdom. Some of the papers played long and loud tunes on the string of Philip's foreign origin." Later biographies reported that Elizabeth's mother had reservations about the union initially and teased Philip as "[the Hun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hun_(pejorative) "Hun (pejorative)")". In later life, however, she told the biographer [Tim Heald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Heald "Tim Heald") that Philip was "an English gentleman".

Before the marriage, Philip renounced his Greek and Danish titles, and adopted the style *Lieutenant Philip Mountbatten*, taking [the surname of his mother's British family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountbatten_family "Mountbatten family"). Shortly before the wedding, he was created [Duke of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_of_Edinburgh "Duke of Edinburgh") and granted the style *His Royal Highness*. Elizabeth and Philip were married on 20 November 1947 at [Westminster Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_Abbey "Westminster Abbey"). They received 2,500 wedding gifts from around the world. Elizabeth required [ration coupons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rationing_in_the_United_Kingdom "Rationing in the United Kingdom") to buy the material for [her gown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedding_dress_of_Princess_Elizabeth_of_the_United_Kingdom "Wedding dress of Princess Elizabeth of the United Kingdom") (which was designed by [Norman Hartnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Hartnell "Norman Hartnell")) because Britain had not yet completely recovered from the devastation of the war. In [post-war Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-war_Britain_(1945%E2%80%931979) "Post-war Britain (1945–1979)"), it was not acceptable for Philip's German relations, including his three surviving sisters, to be invited to the wedding. Neither was an invitation extended to the Duke of Windsor, formerly King Edward VIII.

Elizabeth gave birth to her first child, [Prince Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Charles "Prince Charles"), in November 1948. One month earlier, the King had issued [letters patent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letters_patent_(United_Kingdom) "Letters patent (United Kingdom)") allowing her children to use the style and title of a royal prince or princess, to which they otherwise would not have been entitled, as their father was no longer a royal prince. A second child, [Princess Anne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Anne "Princess Anne"), was born in August 1950.

Following their wedding, the couple leased [Windlesham Moor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windlesham_Moor "Windlesham Moor"), near Windsor Castle, until July 1949, when they took up residence at [Clarence House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarence_House "Clarence House") in London. At various times between 1949 and 1951, Philip was stationed in the British [Crown Colony of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Colony_of_Malta "Crown Colony of Malta") as a serving Royal Navy officer. He and Elizabeth lived intermittently in Malta for several months at a time in the [hamlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamlet_(place) "Hamlet (place)") of [Gwardamanġa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwardaman%C4%A1a "Gwardamanġa"), at [Villa Guardamangia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Guardamangia "Villa Guardamangia"), the rented home of Philip's uncle [Lord Mountbatten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Mountbatten "Lord Mountbatten"). Their two children remained in Britain.

## Reign

### Accession and coronation

As George VI's health declined during 1951, Elizabeth frequently stood in for him at public events. When she visited Canada and [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman "Harry S. Truman") in Washington, DC, in October 1951, her private secretary [Martin Charteris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Charteris,_Baron_Charteris_of_Amisfield "Martin Charteris, Baron Charteris of Amisfield") carried a draft accession declaration in case the King died while she was on tour. In early 1952, Elizabeth and Philip set out for a tour of Australia and New Zealand by way of the British colony of [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Colony "Kenya Colony"). On 6 February, they had just returned to their Kenyan home, [Sagana Lodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagana_Lodge "Sagana Lodge"), after a night spent at [Treetops Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treetops_Hotel "Treetops Hotel"), when word arrived of [the death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_state_funeral_of_George_VI "Death and state funeral of George VI") of Elizabeth's father. Philip broke the news to the new queen. She chose to retain Elizabeth as her regnal name, and was therefore called Elizabeth II. The numeral offended some Scots, as she was the first Elizabeth to rule in Scotland. She was [proclaimed queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_accession_of_Elizabeth_II "Proclamation of accession of Elizabeth II") throughout her realms, and the royal party hastily returned to the United Kingdom. Elizabeth and Philip moved into Buckingham Palace.

With Elizabeth's accession, it seemed possible that the [royal house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_house "Royal house") would take her husband's name, in line with the custom for married women of the time. Lord Mountbatten advocated for *House of [Mountbatten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountbatten "Mountbatten")*, and Philip suggested *House of Edinburgh*, after his ducal title. The British prime minister, Winston Churchill, and Elizabeth's grandmother Queen Mary favoured the retention of the [House of Windsor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Windsor "House of Windsor"). Elizabeth issued a declaration on 9 April 1952 that the royal house would continue to be *Windsor*. Philip complained, "I am the only man in the country not allowed to give his name to his own children." In 1960, the surname *[Mountbatten-Windsor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountbatten-Windsor "Mountbatten-Windsor")* was adopted for Philip and Elizabeth's male-line descendants who do not carry royal titles.

Amid preparations for the coronation, Margaret told her sister she wished to marry [Peter Townsend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Townsend_(RAF_officer) "Peter Townsend (RAF officer)"), a divorcé 16 years Margaret's senior with two sons from his previous marriage. Elizabeth asked them to wait for a year; in the words of her [private secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_Secretary_to_the_Sovereign "Private Secretary to the Sovereign"), "the Queen was naturally sympathetic towards the Princess, but I think she thought – she hoped – given time, the affair would peter out." Senior politicians were against the match and the [Church of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_England "Church of England") did not permit [remarriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remarriage "Remarriage") after divorce. If Margaret had contracted a [civil marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_marriage "Civil marriage"), she would have been expected to renounce her [right of succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_of_succession "Right of succession"). Margaret decided to abandon her plans with Townsend. In 1960, she married [Antony Armstrong-Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antony_Armstrong-Jones "Antony Armstrong-Jones"), who was created [Earl of Snowdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_of_Snowdon "Earl of Snowdon") the following year. They divorced in 1978; Margaret did not remarry.

Despite [Queen Mary's death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_funeral_of_Mary_of_Teck "Death and funeral of Mary of Teck") on 24 March 1953, the coronation went ahead on 2 June, as originally scheduled, as Mary had requested. The coronation ceremony in Westminster Abbey was televised for the first time, with the exception of the [anointing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anointing "Anointing") and [communion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucharist "Eucharist"). On Elizabeth's instruction, [her coronation gown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation_gown_of_Elizabeth_II "Coronation gown of Elizabeth II") was embroidered with the [floral emblems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floral_emblem "Floral emblem") of Commonwealth countries.

### Early reign

From Elizabeth's birth onwards, the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire") continued its transformation into the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations "Commonwealth of Nations"). By the time of her accession in 1952, the monarch's role as head of multiple independent states was already established. In 1953, Elizabeth and Philip embarked on a seven-month round-the-world tour, visiting 13 countries and covering more than 40,000 miles (64,000 km) by land, sea and air. She became the first reigning [monarch of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarch_of_Australia "Monarch of Australia") and [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_New_Zealand "Monarchy of New Zealand") to visit those nations. During the tour, crowds were immense; three-quarters of the population of Australia were estimated to have seen her. Throughout her reign, she made hundreds of [state visits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_state_visits_made_by_Elizabeth_II "List of state visits made by Elizabeth II") to other countries and [tours of the Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Commonwealth_visits_made_by_Elizabeth_II "List of Commonwealth visits made by Elizabeth II"); she was the most widely travelled [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state "Head of state").

During the run-up to the [Suez Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Crisis "Suez Crisis") in 1956, the British and French prime ministers, [Sir Anthony Eden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Anthony_Eden "Sir Anthony Eden") and [Guy Mollet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Mollet "Guy Mollet"), secretly discussed the possibility of France joining the Commonwealth. But Mollet's proposal was not accepted, and the following year, France signed the [Treaty of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Rome "Treaty of Rome"), which established the [European Economic Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Economic_Community "European Economic Community"), the precursor to the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union"). In November 1956, Britain and France invaded Egypt in an ultimately unsuccessful attempt to capture the [Suez Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Canal "Suez Canal"). Lord Mountbatten said that Elizabeth was opposed to the invasion, though Eden denied it. Eden resigned two months later.

The governing [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK) "Conservative Party (UK)") had no formal mechanism for choosing a leader, meaning that it fell to Elizabeth to decide whom to [commission to form a government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kissing_hands "Kissing hands") following Eden's resignation. Eden recommended she consult [Lord Salisbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Gascoyne-Cecil,_5th_Marquess_of_Salisbury "Robert Gascoyne-Cecil, 5th Marquess of Salisbury"), the [lord president of the council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_president_of_the_council "Lord president of the council"). Lord Salisbury and [Lord Kilmuir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Kilmuir "Lord Kilmuir"), the [lord chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_chancellor "Lord chancellor"), consulted the [British Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Cabinet "British Cabinet"), Churchill, and the chairman of the backbench [1922 Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_Committee "1922 Committee"), resulting in Elizabeth appointing their recommended candidate: [Harold Macmillan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Macmillan "Harold Macmillan").

The Suez crisis and the choice of Eden's successor led, in 1957, to the first major personal criticism of Elizabeth. In a magazine, which he owned and edited, [Lord Altrincham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Grigg "John Grigg") accused her of being "out of touch". Altrincham was denounced by public figures and slapped by a member of the public appalled by his comments. Six years later, in 1963, Macmillan resigned and advised Elizabeth to appoint [Alec Douglas-Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alec_Douglas-Home "Alec Douglas-Home") as the prime minister, advice she followed. Elizabeth again came under criticism for appointing the prime minister on the advice of a small number of ministers or a single minister. In 1965, the Conservatives adopted a formal mechanism for electing a leader, thus relieving Elizabeth of her involvement.

In 1957, Elizabeth made a state visit to the United States, where she addressed the [United Nations General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly "United Nations General Assembly") on behalf of the Commonwealth. On the same tour, she opened the [23rd Canadian Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23rd_Canadian_Parliament "23rd Canadian Parliament"), becoming the first [monarch of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarch_of_Canada "Monarch of Canada") to open a parliamentary session. Two years later, solely in her capacity as Queen of Canada, she revisited the United States and toured Canada. In 1961, she toured Cyprus, India, Pakistan, [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Nepal "Kingdom of Nepal"), and [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahlavi_Iran "Pahlavi Iran"). On a visit to Ghana the same year, she dismissed fears for her safety, even though her host, President [Kwame Nkrumah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwame_Nkrumah "Kwame Nkrumah"), who had replaced her as head of state, was a target for assassins. Harold Macmillan wrote, "The Queen has been absolutely determined all through ... She is impatient of the attitude towards her to treat her as ... a film star ... She has indeed '[the heart and stomach of a man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_to_the_Troops_at_Tilbury "Speech to the Troops at Tilbury")' ... She loves her duty and means to be a Queen." Before her tour through parts of [Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec "Quebec") in 1964, the press reported that extremists within the [Quebec separatist movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_separatist_movement "Quebec separatist movement") were plotting Elizabeth's assassination. No assassination attempt was made, but a riot did break out while she was in [Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal "Montreal"); her "calmness and courage in the face of the violence" was noted.

Elizabeth gave birth to her third child, [Andrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Mountbatten-Windsor "Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor"), in February 1960; this was the first birth to a reigning British monarch since 1857. Her fourth child, [Prince Edward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Edward,_Duke_of_Edinburgh "Prince Edward, Duke of Edinburgh"), was born in March 1964. According to biographer [Gyles Brandreth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyles_Brandreth "Gyles Brandreth"), Elizabeth suffered a miscarriage at some point during her reign.

### Political reforms and crises

The 1960s and 1970s saw an acceleration in the [decolonisation of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation_of_Africa "Decolonisation of Africa") and the Caribbean. More than 20 countries gained independence from Britain as part of a planned transition to self-government. In 1965, however, the Rhodesian prime minister, [Ian Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Smith "Ian Smith"), in opposition to moves towards [majority rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority_rule "Majority rule"), [unilaterally declared independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodesia%27s_Unilateral_Declaration_of_Independence "Rhodesia's Unilateral Declaration of Independence") with Elizabeth as "[Queen of Rhodesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_of_Rhodesia "Queen of Rhodesia")". Although Elizabeth formally dismissed him, and the international community applied sanctions against Rhodesia, his regime survived for over a decade. As Britain's ties to its former empire weakened, the British government sought entry to the [European Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Community "European Community"), a goal it [achieved in 1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_the_United_Kingdom_to_the_European_Communities "Accession of the United Kingdom to the European Communities").

In 1966, Elizabeth was criticised for waiting eight days before visiting the village of [Aberfan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberfan "Aberfan"), where [a mining disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberfan_disaster "Aberfan disaster") killed 116 children and 28 adults. [Martin Charteris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Charteris "Martin Charteris") said that the delay, made on his advice, was a mistake that she later regretted. Elizabeth toured [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia "Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia") in October 1972, becoming the first British monarch to visit a [communist country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_country "Communist country"). She was received at the airport by President [Josip Broz Tito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josip_Broz_Tito "Josip Broz Tito"), and a crowd of thousands greeted her in [Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade "Belgrade").

In February 1974, British prime minister [Edward Heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Heath "Edward Heath") advised Elizabeth to call [a general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_1974_United_Kingdom_general_election "February 1974 United Kingdom general election") in the middle of her tour of the [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesia "Austronesia") [Pacific Rim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Rim "Pacific Rim"), requiring her to fly back to Britain. The election resulted in a [hung parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hung_parliament "Hung parliament"); Heath's Conservatives were not the largest party but could stay in office if they formed a coalition with the [Liberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_(UK) "Liberal Party (UK)"). When discussions on forming a coalition foundered, Heath resigned, and Elizabeth asked the [Leader of the Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_(United_Kingdom) "Leader of the Opposition (United Kingdom)"), [Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_(UK) "Labour Party (UK)")'s [Harold Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Wilson "Harold Wilson"), to form a government.

A year later, at the height of the [1975 Australian constitutional crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Australian_constitutional_crisis "1975 Australian constitutional crisis"), the Australian prime minister, [Gough Whitlam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gough_Whitlam "Gough Whitlam"), was dismissed from his post by Governor-General [Sir John Kerr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_John_Kerr "Sir John Kerr"), after the Opposition-controlled [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Senate "Australian Senate") rejected Whitlam's budget proposals. As Whitlam had a majority in the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_House_of_Representatives "Australian House of Representatives"), Speaker [Gordon Scholes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Scholes "Gordon Scholes") appealed to Elizabeth to reverse Kerr's decision. She declined, saying she would not interfere in decisions reserved by the [Constitution of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Australia "Constitution of Australia") for the [governor-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_Australia "Governor-General of Australia"). The crisis fuelled [Australian republicanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_republicanism "Australian republicanism").

In 1977, Elizabeth marked the [Silver Jubilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Jubilee_of_Elizabeth_II "Silver Jubilee of Elizabeth II") of her accession. Parties and events took place throughout the Commonwealth, many coinciding with [her associated national and Commonwealth tours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_events_during_the_Silver_Jubilee_of_Elizabeth_II "List of events during the Silver Jubilee of Elizabeth II"). The celebrations re-affirmed Elizabeth's popularity, despite virtually coincident negative press coverage of Margaret's separation from her husband, Lord Snowdon. In 1978, Elizabeth endured a state visit to the United Kingdom by [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Romania "Socialist Republic of Romania")'s communist leader, [Nicolae Ceaușescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Ceau%C8%99escu "Nicolae Ceaușescu"), and his wife, [Elena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elena_Ceau%C8%99escu "Elena Ceaușescu"), though privately she thought they had "blood on their hands". The following year brought two blows: the unmasking of [Anthony Blunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Blunt "Anthony Blunt"), former [Surveyor of the Queen's Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveyor_of_the_Queen%27s_Pictures "Surveyor of the Queen's Pictures"), as a communist spy and the [assassination of Lord Mountbatten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Lord_Mountbatten "Assassination of Lord Mountbatten") by the [Provisional Irish Republican Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Irish_Republican_Army "Provisional Irish Republican Army").

According to [Paul Martin Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Martin_Sr. "Paul Martin Sr."), by the end of the 1970s, Elizabeth was worried [the Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crown "The Crown") "had little meaning for" [Pierre Trudeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Trudeau "Pierre Trudeau"), the Canadian prime minister. [Tony Benn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Benn "Tony Benn") said Elizabeth found Trudeau "rather disappointing". Trudeau's supposed [republicanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republicanism_in_Canada "Republicanism in Canada") seemed to be confirmed by his antics, such as sliding down banisters at Buckingham Palace and pirouetting behind Elizabeth's back in 1977, and the removal of various [Canadian royal symbols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_royal_symbols "Canadian royal symbols") during his term of office. In 1980, Canadian politicians sent to London to discuss the [patriation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriation "Patriation") of the [Canadian constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_constitution "Canadian constitution") found Elizabeth "better informed ... than any of the British politicians or bureaucrats". She was particularly interested after the failure of Bill C-60, which would have affected her role as head of state.

### Perils and dissent

During the 1981 [Trooping the Colour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trooping_the_Colour "Trooping the Colour") ceremony, six weeks before the [wedding of Prince Charles and Lady Diana Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedding_of_Prince_Charles_and_Lady_Diana_Spencer "Wedding of Prince Charles and Lady Diana Spencer"), six shots were fired at Elizabeth from close range as she rode down [The Mall, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mall,_London "The Mall, London"), on her horse, [Burmese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_(horse) "Burmese (horse)"). Police later discovered the shots were blanks. The 17-year-old assailant, [Marcus Sarjeant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Sarjeant "Marcus Sarjeant"), was sentenced to five years in prison and released after three. Elizabeth's composure and skill in controlling her mount were widely praised. That October, Elizabeth was the subject of another attack while on a visit to [Dunedin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunedin "Dunedin"), New Zealand. [Christopher John Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_John_Lewis "Christopher John Lewis"), who was 17 years old, fired a shot with a [.22 rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.22_rifle ".22 rifle") from the fifth floor of a building overlooking the parade but missed. Lewis was arrested, but instead of being charged with [attempted murder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attempted_murder "Attempted murder") or [treason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treason "Treason") was sentenced to three years in jail for unlawful possession and discharge of a firearm. Two years into his sentence, he attempted to escape a [psychiatric hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychiatric_hospital "Psychiatric hospital") with the intention of assassinating Charles, who was visiting the country with [Diana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diana,_Princess_of_Wales "Diana, Princess of Wales") and their son [Prince William](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_William "Prince William").

From April to September 1982, Elizabeth's son Andrew served with British forces in the [Falklands War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_War "Falklands War"), for which she reportedly felt anxiety and pride. On 9 July, she awoke in her bedroom at Buckingham Palace to find an intruder, [Michael Fagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Fagan "Michael Fagan"), in the room with her. In a serious lapse of security, assistance only arrived after two calls to the Palace police switchboard. After hosting US president [Ronald Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan "Ronald Reagan") at Windsor Castle in 1982 and visiting [his California ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_del_Cielo "Rancho del Cielo") in 1983, Elizabeth was angered when [his administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Ronald_Reagan "Presidency of Ronald Reagan") ordered the [invasion of Grenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Grenada "Invasion of Grenada"), one of her Caribbean realms, without informing her.

Intense media interest in the opinions and private lives of the royal family during the 1980s led to a series of sensational stories in the press, pioneered by *[The Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sun_(United_Kingdom) "The Sun (United Kingdom)")* tabloid. As [Kelvin MacKenzie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelvin_MacKenzie "Kelvin MacKenzie"), editor of *The Sun*, told his staff: "Give me a Sunday for Monday splash on the Royals. Don't worry if it's not true—so long as there's not too much of a fuss about it afterwards." Newspaper editor [Donald Trelford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trelford "Donald Trelford") wrote in *[The Observer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Observer "The Observer")* of 21 September 1986: "The royal soap opera has now reached such a pitch of public interest that the boundary between fact and fiction has been lost sight of ... it is not just that some papers don't check their facts or accept denials: they don't care if the stories are true or not." It was reported, most notably in *[The Sunday Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sunday_Times "The Sunday Times")* of 20 July 1986, that Elizabeth was worried that [Margaret Thatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Thatcher "Margaret Thatcher")'s [economic policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thatcherism#economicposition "Thatcherism") fostered social divisions and was alarmed by high unemployment, [a series of riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_England_riots "1981 England riots"), the violence of [a miners' strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984%E2%80%931985_United_Kingdom_miners%27_strike "1984–1985 United Kingdom miners' strike"), and Thatcher's refusal to apply sanctions against the [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid "Apartheid") regime in South Africa. The sources of the rumours included royal aide [Michael Shea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Shea_(diplomat) "Michael Shea (diplomat)") and Commonwealth secretary-general [Shridath Ramphal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shridath_Ramphal "Shridath Ramphal"), but Shea claimed his remarks were taken [out of context](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out_of_context "Out of context") and embellished by speculation. Thatcher reputedly said Elizabeth would vote for the [Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_(UK) "Social Democratic Party (UK)") – Thatcher's political opponents. Thatcher's biographer [John Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Campbell_(biographer) "John Campbell (biographer)") claimed "the report was a piece of journalistic mischief-making". Reports of acrimony between them were exaggerated, and Elizabeth gave two honours in her personal gift – membership in the [Order of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Merit "Order of Merit") and the [Order of the Garter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Garter "Order of the Garter") – to Thatcher after her replacement as prime minister by [John Major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Major "John Major"). [Brian Mulroney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Mulroney "Brian Mulroney"), Canadian prime minister between 1984 and 1993, said Elizabeth was a "behind the scenes force" in ending apartheid.

In 1986, Elizabeth paid a six-day [state visit to the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_visit_by_Elizabeth_II_to_China "State visit by Elizabeth II to China"), becoming the first British monarch to visit the country. The tour included the [Forbidden City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbidden_City "Forbidden City"), the [Great Wall of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Wall_of_China "Great Wall of China"), and the [Terracotta Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terracotta_Warriors "Terracotta Warriors"). At a [state banquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_banquet "State banquet"), Elizabeth joked about the first British emissary to China being lost at sea with [Queen Elizabeth I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_I "Queen Elizabeth I")'s letter to the [Wanli Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanli_Emperor "Wanli Emperor"), and remarked, "fortunately postal services have improved since 1602". Elizabeth's visit also signified the acceptance of both countries that [sovereignty over Hong Kong would be transferred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handover_of_Hong_Kong "Handover of Hong Kong") from the United Kingdom to China in 1997.

By the end of the 1980s, Elizabeth had become the target of satire. The involvement of younger members of the royal family in the charity game show *[It's a Royal Knockout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%27s_a_Royal_Knockout "It's a Royal Knockout")* in 1987 was ridiculed. In Canada, Elizabeth publicly supported politically divisive [constitutional amendments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meech_Lake_Accord "Meech Lake Accord"), prompting criticism from opponents of the proposed changes, including Pierre Trudeau. The same year, the elected Fijian government was deposed in [a military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Fijian_coups_d%27%C3%A9tat "1987 Fijian coups d'état"). As [monarch of Fiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarch_of_Fiji "Monarch of Fiji"), Elizabeth supported the attempts of Governor-General [Ratu Sir Penaia Ganilau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratu_Sir_Penaia_Ganilau "Ratu Sir Penaia Ganilau") to assert executive power and negotiate a settlement. Coup leader [Sitiveni Rabuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitiveni_Rabuka "Sitiveni Rabuka") deposed Ganilau and declared Fiji a republic.

### Turbulent years

In the wake of coalition victory in the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War "Gulf War"), Elizabeth became the first British monarch to address a [joint meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_session_of_the_United_States_Congress "Joint session of the United States Congress") of the [United States Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Congress "United States Congress") in May 1991. In November 1992, in a speech to mark the [Ruby Jubilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruby_Jubilee_of_Elizabeth_II "Ruby Jubilee of Elizabeth II") of her accession, Elizabeth called 1992 her *[annus horribilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annus_horribilis "Annus horribilis")* (a Latin phrase, meaning 'horrible year'). [Republican feeling in Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_feeling_in_Britain "Republican feeling in Britain") had risen because of press estimates of Elizabeth's private wealth – contradicted by the Palace – and reports of affairs and strained marriages among her extended family. In March, her second son, Andrew, separated from his wife, [Sarah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Ferguson "Sarah Ferguson"); her daughter, Anne, divorced Captain [Mark Phillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Phillips "Mark Phillips") in April; angry demonstrators in [Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresden "Dresden") threw eggs at Elizabeth during a state visit to Germany in October; and [a large fire broke out at Windsor Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Windsor_Castle_fire "1992 Windsor Castle fire"), one of her [official residences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_residence "Official residence"), in November. The monarchy came under increased criticism and public scrutiny. In an unusually personal speech, Elizabeth said that any institution must expect criticism, but suggested it might be done with "a touch of humour, gentleness and understanding". Two days later, John Major announced plans to reform the royal finances, drawn up the previous year, including Elizabeth paying [income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_tax "Income tax") from 1993 onwards, and a reduction in the [civil list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_list "Civil list"). In December, Charles and his wife, Diana, formally separated. At the end of the year, Elizabeth sued *The Sun* newspaper for breach of copyright when it published the text of her [annual Christmas message](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Christmas_Message "Royal Christmas Message") two days before it was broadcast. The newspaper was forced to pay her legal fees and donated £200,000 to charity. Elizabeth's solicitors had taken successful action against *The Sun* five years earlier for breach of copyright after it published a photograph of her daughter-in-law the Duchess of York and her granddaughter [Princess Beatrice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Beatrice "Princess Beatrice").

In January 1994, Elizabeth broke her left wrist when a horse she was riding at Sandringham tripped and fell. In October 1994, she became the first reigning British monarch to set foot on Russian soil. In October 1995, she was tricked into a [hoax call](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoax_call "Hoax call") by Montreal radio host [Pierre Brassard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Brassard "Pierre Brassard") impersonating Canadian prime minister [Jean Chrétien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Chr%C3%A9tien "Jean Chrétien"). Elizabeth, who believed that she was speaking to Chrétien, said she supported Canadian unity and would try to influence [Quebec's referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Quebec_referendum "1995 Quebec referendum") on proposals to break away from Canada.

In the year that followed, public revelations on the state of Charles and Diana's marriage continued. In consultation with her husband and John Major, as well as the [Archbishop of Canterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury "Archbishop of Canterbury") ([George Carey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Carey "George Carey")) and her private secretary ([Robert Fellowes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Fellowes,_Baron_Fellowes "Robert Fellowes, Baron Fellowes")), Elizabeth wrote to Charles and Diana at the end of December 1995, suggesting that a divorce would be advisable.

In August 1997, a year after the divorce, Diana [was killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Diana,_Princess_of_Wales "Death of Diana, Princess of Wales") in a car crash in Paris. Elizabeth was on holiday with her extended family at Balmoral. Diana's two sons, William and [Harry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Harry,_Duke_of_Sussex "Prince Harry, Duke of Sussex"), wanted to attend church, so Elizabeth and Philip took them that morning. Afterwards, for five days, the royal couple shielded their grandsons from the intense press interest by keeping them at Balmoral where they could grieve in private, but the royal family's silence and seclusion, and the failure to fly a flag at [half-mast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-mast "Half-mast") over Buckingham Palace, caused public dismay. Pressured by the hostile reaction, Elizabeth agreed to return to London and address the nation in a [live television broadcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addresses_to_the_nation_by_Elizabeth_II "Addresses to the nation by Elizabeth II") on 5 September, the day before [Diana's funeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diana%27s_funeral "Diana's funeral"). In the broadcast, she expressed admiration for Diana and her feelings "as a grandmother" for the two princes. As a result, much of the public hostility evaporated.

In October 1997, Elizabeth and Philip made a state visit to India, which included a controversial visit to the site of the [Jallianwala Bagh massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jallianwala_Bagh_massacre "Jallianwala Bagh massacre") to pay her respects. Protesters chanted "Killer Queen, go back", and there were demands for her to apologise for the action of British troops 78 years earlier. At the memorial in the park, she and Philip laid a [wreath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wreath "Wreath") and stood for a 30‑second [moment of silence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_of_silence "Moment of silence"). As a result, much of the fury among the public softened, and the protests were called off. Back in London that November, the royal couple held a reception at [Banqueting House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banqueting_House "Banqueting House") to mark their golden wedding anniversary. Elizabeth made a speech and praised Philip for his role as consort, referring to him as "my strength and stay".

In 1999, as part of the process of [devolution in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution_in_the_United_Kingdom "Devolution in the United Kingdom"), Elizabeth formally opened newly established legislatures for Wales and Scotland: the [National Assembly for Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_for_Wales "National Assembly for Wales") at [Cardiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff "Cardiff") in May, and the [Scottish Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Parliament "Scottish Parliament") at [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh "Edinburgh") in July.

### Dawn of the new millennium

On the eve of the new millennium, Elizabeth and Philip boarded a vessel from [Southwark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwark "Southwark"), bound for the [Millennium Dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Dome "Millennium Dome"). Before passing under [Tower Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_Bridge "Tower Bridge"), she lit the National Millennium Beacon in the [Pool of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pool_of_London "Pool of London") using a laser torch. Shortly before midnight, she officially opened the Dome. During the singing of *[Auld Lang Syne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auld_Lang_Syne "Auld Lang Syne")*, Elizabeth held hands with Philip and British prime minister [Tony Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Blair "Tony Blair"). Following the [9/11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9/11_attacks "9/11 attacks") in the United States, Elizabeth, breaking with tradition, ordered the [American national anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_national_anthem "American national anthem") to be played during the [changing of the guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changing_of_the_guard_(United_Kingdom) "Changing of the guard (United Kingdom)") at Buckingham Palace to express her solidarity with the country.

In 2002, Elizabeth marked her [Golden Jubilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Jubilee_of_Elizabeth_II "Golden Jubilee of Elizabeth II"), the 50th anniversary of her accession. Her sister died in February and [her mother in March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_funeral_of_Queen_Elizabeth_the_Queen_Mother "Death and funeral of Queen Elizabeth the Queen Mother"), and the media speculated on whether the Jubilee would be a success or a failure. Margaret's death shook Elizabeth; her funeral was one of the rare occasions where Elizabeth openly cried. Elizabeth again undertook an extensive tour of her realms, beginning in Jamaica in February, where she called the farewell banquet "memorable" after a [power cut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_cut "Power cut") plunged [King's House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King%27s_House,_Jamaica "King's House, Jamaica"), the official residence of the [governor-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_Jamaica "Governor-General of Jamaica"), into darkness. As in 1977, there were street parties and commemorative events, and monuments were named to honour the occasion. One million people attended each day of the three-day main Jubilee celebration in London, and the enthusiasm shown for Elizabeth by the public was greater than many journalists had anticipated.

In 2003, Elizabeth sued the *[Daily Mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Mirror "Daily Mirror")* for [breach of confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breach_of_confidence_in_English_law "Breach of confidence in English law") and obtained an [injunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injunction "Injunction") which prevented the outlet from publishing information gathered by a reporter who posed as a [footman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Footman "Footman") at Buckingham Palace. The newspaper also paid £25,000 towards her legal costs. Though generally healthy throughout her life, in 2003 she had [keyhole surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keyhole_surgery "Keyhole surgery") on both knees. In October 2006, she missed the opening of the new [Emirates Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirates_Stadium "Emirates Stadium") because of a strained back muscle that had been troubling her since the summer.

In May 2007, citing unnamed sources, *[The Daily Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph "The Daily Telegraph")* reported that Elizabeth was "exasperated and frustrated" by the policies of Tony Blair, that she was concerned the [British Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Armed_Forces "British Armed Forces") were overstretched in Iraq and [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Afghanistan "Islamic Republic of Afghanistan"), and that she had raised concerns over rural and countryside issues with Blair. She was, however, said to admire Blair's efforts to achieve [peace in Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_in_Northern_Ireland "Peace in Northern Ireland"). She became the first British monarch to celebrate a diamond wedding anniversary in November 2007. On 20 March 2008, at the [Church of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Ireland "Church of Ireland") [St Patrick's Cathedral, Armagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Patrick%27s_Cathedral,_Armagh_(Church_of_Ireland) "St Patrick's Cathedral, Armagh (Church of Ireland)"), Elizabeth attended the first [Maundy service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maundy_service "Maundy service") held outside England and Wales.

Elizabeth addressed the UN General Assembly for a second time in 2010, again in her capacity as Queen of all Commonwealth realms and [Head of the Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_the_Commonwealth "Head of the Commonwealth"). The [UN secretary-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_secretary-general "UN secretary-general"), [Ban Ki-moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Ki-moon "Ban Ki-moon"), introduced her as "an anchor for our age". During her visit to New York, which followed a tour of Canada, she officially opened a memorial garden for British [victims of the 9/11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victims_of_the_9/11_attacks "Victims of the 9/11 attacks"). Elizabeth's 11-day visit to Australia in October 2011 was her 16th visit to the country since 1954. By invitation of the [Irish president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_president "Irish president"), [Mary McAleese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_McAleese "Mary McAleese"), she made the first [state visit to the Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_visit_by_Elizabeth_II_to_the_Republic_of_Ireland "State visit by Elizabeth II to the Republic of Ireland") by a British monarch in May 2011.

### Diamond Jubilee and milestones

The [2012 Diamond Jubilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Diamond_Jubilee "2012 Diamond Jubilee") marked 60 years since Elizabeth's accession, and celebrations were held throughout her realms, the wider Commonwealth, and beyond. She and Philip undertook an extensive tour of the United Kingdom, while their children and grandchildren embarked on royal tours of other Commonwealth states on her behalf. On 4 June, Jubilee beacons were lit around the world. On 18 December, Elizabeth became the first British [sovereign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign "Sovereign") to attend a peacetime [Cabinet meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_United_Kingdom "Cabinet of the United Kingdom") since [George III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_III "George III") in 1781.

Elizabeth, who opened the [Montreal Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Summer_Olympics "Montreal Summer Olympics") in 1976, also opened the [2012 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics "2012 Summer Olympics") and [Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Summer_Paralympics "2012 Summer Paralympics") in London, making her the first [head of state to open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_people_who_have_opened_the_Olympic_Games "List of people who have opened the Olympic Games") two [Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Games "Olympic Games") in two countries. For the London Olympics, she portrayed herself in [a short film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happy_%26_Glorious "Happy & Glorious") as part of [the opening ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony "2012 Summer Olympics opening ceremony"), alongside [Daniel Craig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Craig "Daniel Craig") as [James Bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bond "James Bond"). On 4 April 2013, she received an honorary [BAFTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA "BAFTA") award for her [patronage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patronage "Patronage") of the film industry and was called "the most memorable [Bond girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_girl "Bond girl") yet" at a special presentation at Windsor Castle.

In March 2013, Elizabeth stayed overnight at [King Edward VII's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Edward_VII%27s_Hospital "King Edward VII's Hospital") as a precaution after developing symptoms of [gastroenteritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastroenteritis "Gastroenteritis"). A week later, she signed the new [Charter of the Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_of_the_Commonwealth "Charter of the Commonwealth"). That year, because of her age and the need for her to limit travelling, she chose not to attend the biennial [Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Heads_of_Government_Meeting "Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting") for the first time in 40 years. She was represented at the [summit in Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Commonwealth_Heads_of_Government_Meeting "2013 Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting") by Charles. On 20 April 2018, the Commonwealth heads of government announced that Charles would succeed her as Head of the Commonwealth, which the Queen stated as her "sincere wish". She underwent [cataract surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cataract_surgery "Cataract surgery") in May 2018. In March 2019, she gave up driving on public roads, largely as a consequence of a car accident involving her husband two months earlier.

On 21 December 2007, Elizabeth surpassed her great-great-grandmother, Queen Victoria, to become the longest-lived British monarch, and she became the [longest-reigning British monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_monarchs_in_Britain_by_length_of_reign "List of monarchs in Britain by length of reign") and [longest-reigning queen regnant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_longest-reigning_monarchs "List of longest-reigning monarchs") and female head of state in the world on 9 September 2015. She became the oldest living monarch after the death of [King Abdullah of Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Abdullah_of_Saudi_Arabia "King Abdullah of Saudi Arabia") on 23 January 2015. She later became the longest-reigning current monarch and the [longest-serving current head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_current_state_leaders_by_date_of_assumption_of_office "List of current state leaders by date of assumption of office") following the [death of King Bhumibol Adulyadej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_King_Bhumibol_Adulyadej "Death of King Bhumibol Adulyadej") of Thailand on 13 October 2016, and the [oldest current head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_state_leaders_by_age "List of state leaders by age") on the [resignation of Robert Mugabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resignation_of_Robert_Mugabe "Resignation of Robert Mugabe") of Zimbabwe on 21 November 2017. On 6 February 2017, she became the first British monarch to commemorate [a sapphire jubilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapphire_Jubilee_of_Elizabeth_II "Sapphire Jubilee of Elizabeth II"), and on 20 November that year, she was the first British monarch to celebrate a platinum wedding anniversary. Philip had retired from his official duties as Elizabeth's consort in August 2017.

### Pandemic and widowhood

On 19 March 2020, as the [COVID-19 pandemic hit the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_United_Kingdom "COVID-19 pandemic in the United Kingdom"), Elizabeth moved to Windsor Castle and sequestered there as a precaution. Public engagements were cancelled and Windsor Castle followed a strict sanitary protocol nicknamed "HMS Bubble". On 5 April, in a televised broadcast watched by an estimated 24 million viewers in the United Kingdom, Elizabeth asked people to "take comfort that while we may have more still to endure, better days will return: we will be with our friends again; we will be with our families again; we will meet again." On 8 May, the 75th anniversary of [VE Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VE_Day "VE Day"), in a television broadcast at 9 pm—the exact time at which her father had broadcast to the nation on the same day in 1945—she asked people to "never give up, never despair". In 2021, she received her first and second [COVID-19 vaccinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_vaccinations "COVID-19 vaccinations") in January and April respectively.

[Philip died](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_funeral_of_Prince_Philip,_Duke_of_Edinburgh "Death and funeral of Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh") on 9 April 2021, after 73 years of marriage, making Elizabeth the first British monarch to reign as a [widow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Widow "Widow") or widower since Queen Victoria. She was reportedly at her husband's bedside when he died, and remarked in private that his death had "left a huge void". Due to the COVID-19 restrictions in place in England at the time, Elizabeth sat alone at Philip's funeral service, which evoked sympathy from people around the world. It was later reported in the press that Elizabeth had rejected a government offer to relax the rules. In her Christmas broadcast that year, which was ultimately her last, she paid a personal tribute to her "beloved Philip", saying, "That mischievous, inquiring twinkle was as bright at the end as when I first set eyes on him."

Despite the pandemic, Elizabeth attended the [2021 State Opening of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_State_Opening_of_Parliament "2021 State Opening of Parliament") in May, the [47th G7 summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/47th_G7_summit "47th G7 summit") in June, and hosted US president [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden") at Windsor Castle. Biden was the 14th US president that she had met. In October 2021, Elizabeth cancelled a planned trip to Northern Ireland and stayed overnight at King Edward VII's Hospital for "preliminary investigations". On Christmas Day 2021, while she was staying at Windsor Castle, 19-year-old Jaswant Singh Chail broke into the gardens using a rope ladder and carrying a [crossbow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossbow "Crossbow") with the aim of assassinating Elizabeth in revenge for the [Amritsar massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amritsar_massacre "Amritsar massacre"). Before he could enter any buildings, he was arrested and detained under the [Mental Health Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_Health_Act_2007 "Mental Health Act 2007"). In February 2023, Chail pleaded guilty to [attempting to injure or alarm the sovereign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treason_Act_1842#Section_2 "Treason Act 1842"), and was sentenced in October to a nine-year custodial sentence plus an additional five years on extended licence. The sentencing judge also placed Chail under a hybrid order under section 45A of the [Mental Health Act 1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_Health_Act_1983 "Mental Health Act 1983"), ordering that he remain at [Broadmoor Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadmoor_Hospital "Broadmoor Hospital") to be transferred into custody only after receiving psychiatric treatment.

### Platinum Jubilee and beyond

[Elizabeth's Platinum Jubilee celebrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth%27s_Platinum_Jubilee_celebrations "Elizabeth's Platinum Jubilee celebrations") began on 6 February 2022, marking 70 years since her accession. In her [accession day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_day "Accession day") message, she renewed her commitment to a lifetime of public service, which she had originally made in 1947.

Later that month, Elizabeth fell ill with COVID-19 along with several family members, but she only exhibited "mild cold-like symptoms" and recovered by the end of the month. She was present at [the service of thanksgiving for her husband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Service_of_Thanksgiving_for_the_life_of_The_Duke_of_Edinburgh "A Service of Thanksgiving for the life of The Duke of Edinburgh") at Westminster Abbey on 29 March, but was unable to attend both the annual [Commonwealth Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Day "Commonwealth Day") service that month and the [Royal Maundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Maundy "Royal Maundy") service in April, because of "episodic mobility problems". In May, she missed the [State Opening of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Opening_of_Parliament "State Opening of Parliament") for the first time in 59 years. (She did not attend the state openings in 1959 and 1963 as she was pregnant with Andrew and Edward, respectively.) Later that month she made a surprise visit to [Paddington Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddington_Station "Paddington Station") and officially opened the [Elizabeth line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_line "Elizabeth line"), named in her honour.

Elizabeth was largely confined to balcony appearances during the public jubilee celebrations, and she missed the [National Service of Thanksgiving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum_Jubilee_National_Service_of_Thanksgiving "Platinum Jubilee National Service of Thanksgiving") on 3 June. On 13 June, she became the second-longest reigning monarch in history (among those whose exact dates of reign are known), with 70 years and 127 days on the throne – surpassing [King Bhumibol Adulyadej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Bhumibol_Adulyadej "King Bhumibol Adulyadej") of Thailand. On 6 September, she appointed her 15th British prime minister, [Liz Truss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liz_Truss "Liz Truss"), at Balmoral Castle in Scotland. This was the only occasion on which Elizabeth received a new prime minister at a location other than Buckingham Palace. No other British monarch appointed as many prime ministers. Elizabeth's last public message was issued on 7 September, in which she expressed her sympathy for those affected by the [Saskatchewan stabbings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatchewan_stabbings "Saskatchewan stabbings").

Elizabeth did not plan to [abdicate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdicate "Abdicate"), though she took on fewer public engagements in her later years and Charles performed more of her duties. She told Canadian governor-general [Adrienne Clarkson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrienne_Clarkson "Adrienne Clarkson") in a meeting in 2002 that she would never abdicate, saying, "It is not our tradition. Although, I suppose if I became completely gaga, one would have to do something." In June 2022, Elizabeth met the Archbishop of Canterbury, [Justin Welby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Welby "Justin Welby"), who "came away thinking there is someone who has no fear of death, has hope in the future, knows the rock on which she stands and that gives her strength."

## Death

On 8 September 2022, Buckingham Palace stated, "Following further evaluation this morning, the Queen's doctors are concerned for Her Majesty's health and have recommended she remain under medical supervision. The Queen remains comfortable and at Balmoral." Her [immediate family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immediate_family "Immediate family") rushed to Balmoral. Elizabeth died peacefully at 3:10 pm, aged 96. Her death was announced to the public at 6:30 pm, setting in motion [Operation London Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_London_Bridge "Operation London Bridge") and, because she died in Scotland, [Operation Unicorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_London_Bridge#Operation_Unicorn "Operation London Bridge"). Elizabeth was the first monarch to die in Scotland since [James V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_V "James V") in 1542. Her [death certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_certificate "Death certificate") recorded her cause of death as "[old age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_age "Old age")". According to former prime minister [Boris Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Johnson "Boris Johnson") and the biographer [Gyles Brandreth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyles_Brandreth "Gyles Brandreth"), she was suffering from a form of [bone marrow cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone_marrow_cancer "Bone marrow cancer"), which Brandreth wrote was [multiple myeloma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_myeloma "Multiple myeloma").

On 12 September, Elizabeth's coffin was carried up the [Royal Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Mile "Royal Mile") in a procession to [St Giles' Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Giles%27_Cathedral "St Giles' Cathedral"), where the [Crown of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Scotland "Crown of Scotland") was placed on it. Her coffin lay at rest at the cathedral for 24 hours, guarded by the [Royal Company of Archers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Company_of_Archers "Royal Company of Archers"), during which around 33,000 people filed past it. On 13 September, the coffin was flown to [RAF Northolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Northolt "RAF Northolt") in west London, before continuing its journey by road to Buckingham Palace. On 14 September, her coffin was taken in a military procession to [Westminster Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_Hall "Westminster Hall"), where Elizabeth's body [lay in state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lay_in_state "Lay in state") for four days. The coffin was guarded by members of both the [Sovereign's Bodyguard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign%27s_Bodyguard "Sovereign's Bodyguard") and the [Household Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Household_Division "Household Division"). An estimated 250,000 members of the public [filed past the coffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queue_for_the_lying-in-state_of_Elizabeth_II "Queue for the lying-in-state of Elizabeth II"), as did politicians and other public figures. On 16 September, Elizabeth's children held [a vigil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigil_of_the_Princes#Queen_Elizabeth_II "Vigil of the Princes") around her coffin, and the next day her eight grandchildren did the same.

Elizabeth's [state funeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_funerals_in_the_United_Kingdom "State funerals in the United Kingdom") was held at Westminster Abbey on 19 September, marking the first time a monarch's funeral service had been held there since that of [George II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_II_of_Great_Britain "George II of Great Britain") in 1760. More than a million people lined the streets of [central London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_London "Central London"), and the day was declared a holiday in several Commonwealth countries. In Windsor, a final procession involving 1,000 military personnel took place and was witnessed by 97,000 people. Elizabeth's [fell pony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fell_pony "Fell pony") and two [royal corgis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_corgis "Royal corgis") stood at the side of the procession. After a committal service at [St George's Chapel, Windsor Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_George%27s_Chapel,_Windsor_Castle "St George's Chapel, Windsor Castle"), Elizabeth's body was interred with her husband Philip's in the [King George VI Memorial Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_George_VI_Memorial_Chapel "King George VI Memorial Chapel") later the same day, in a private ceremony attended by her closest family members.

## Public image

### Beliefs, activities, and interests

Elizabeth had a deep sense of religious and [civic duty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_duty "Civic duty"), and took her [Coronation Oath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation_Oath "Coronation Oath") seriously. Aside from her [official religious role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_the_United_Kingdom#Religious_role "Monarchy of the United Kingdom") as [supreme governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_governor "Supreme governor") of the [established](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_religion "State religion") Church of England, she worshipped with that church and with the national [Church of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Scotland "Church of Scotland"). She demonstrated support for [inter-faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-faith "Inter-faith") relations and met with leaders of other churches and religions, including five popes: [Pius XII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pius_XII "Pius XII"), [John XXIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_XXIII "John XXIII"), [John Paul II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Paul_II "John Paul II"), [Benedict XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedict_XVI "Benedict XVI") and [Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Francis "Pope Francis"). A personal note about her faith often featured in her annual [Christmas Message](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_Message "Christmas Message") broadcast to the Commonwealth. In 2000, she said:

> To many of us, our beliefs are of fundamental importance. For me the teachings of Christ and my own personal accountability before God provide a framework in which I try to lead my life. I, like so many of you, have drawn great comfort in difficult times from Christ's words and example.

Elizabeth rarely gave interviews, and little was known of her political opinions, which she did not express explicitly in public. By convention, the monarch’s political views are neither asked about nor publicly disclosed. When *Times* journalist [Paul Routledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Routledge "Paul Routledge") asked her about the [miners' strike of 1984–85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miners%27_strike_of_1984%E2%80%9385 "Miners' strike of 1984–85") during a royal tour of the newspaper's offices, she replied that it was "all about one man" (a reference to [Arthur Scargill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Scargill "Arthur Scargill")), with which Routledge disagreed. Routledge was widely criticised in the media for asking the question and claimed that he was unaware of the protocols. After the [2014 Scottish independence referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Scottish_independence_referendum "2014 Scottish independence referendum"), Prime Minister [David Cameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Cameron "David Cameron") was overheard telling [Mayor of New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_New_York_City "Mayor of New York City") [Michael Bloomberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Bloomberg "Michael Bloomberg") that Elizabeth was pleased with the outcome, an incident for which Cameron later apologised. She had arguably issued a public coded statement about the referendum by telling one woman outside Balmoral Kirk that she hoped people would think "very carefully" about the outcome. It emerged later that Cameron had specifically requested that she register her concern.

Elizabeth was [patron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patron "Patron") of more than 600 organisations and charities. The [Charities Aid Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charities_Aid_Foundation "Charities Aid Foundation") estimated that Elizabeth helped raise over £1.4 billion for her patronages during her reign. Her main leisure interests included [equestrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horses_of_Elizabeth_II "Horses of Elizabeth II") and dogs, especially her [Pembroke Welsh Corgis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pembroke_Welsh_Corgi "Pembroke Welsh Corgi"). Her lifelong love of corgis began in 1933 with [Dookie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dookie_(dog) "Dookie (dog)"), the first of many royal corgis. Scenes of a relaxed, informal home life were occasionally witnessed; she and her family, from time to time, prepared a meal together and washed the dishes afterwards.

### Media depiction and public opinion

In the 1950s, as a young woman at the start of her reign, Elizabeth was depicted as a glamorous "fairytale Queen". After the trauma of the Second World War, it was a time of hope, a period of progress and achievement heralding a "new [Elizabethan age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabethan_age "Elizabethan age")". Lord Altrincham's accusation in 1957 that her speeches sounded like those of a "[priggish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prig "Prig") schoolgirl" was an extremely rare criticism. In the late 1960s, attempts to portray a more modern image of the monarchy were made in the television documentary *[Royal Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Family_(film) "Royal Family (film)")* and by televising [Prince Charles's investiture as Prince of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Charles%27s_investiture_as_Prince_of_Wales "Prince Charles's investiture as Prince of Wales"). Elizabeth also instituted other new practices; her first royal walkabout, meeting ordinary members of the public, took place during a tour of Australia and New Zealand in 1970. Her wardrobe developed a recognisable, signature style driven more by function than fashion. In public, she took to wearing mostly solid-colour [overcoats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overcoat "Overcoat") and decorative hats, allowing her to be seen easily in a crowd. By the end of her reign, nearly one third of Britons had seen or met Elizabeth in person.

At Elizabeth's Silver Jubilee in 1977, the crowds and celebrations were genuinely enthusiastic; but, in the 1980s, public criticism of the royal family increased, as the personal and working lives of Elizabeth's children came under media scrutiny. Her popularity sank to a low point in the 1990s. Under pressure from public opinion, she began to pay income tax for the first time, and Buckingham Palace was opened to the public. Although support for [republicanism in Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republicanism_in_Britain "Republicanism in Britain") seemed higher than at any time in living memory, republican ideology was still a minority viewpoint, and Elizabeth herself had high approval ratings. Criticism was focused on the institution of the monarchy itself, and the conduct of Elizabeth's wider family, rather than her own behaviour and actions. Discontent with the monarchy reached its peak on the death of Diana, Princess of Wales, although Elizabeth's personal popularity – as well as general support for the monarchy – rebounded after her live television broadcast to the world five days after Diana's death.

In November 1999, [a referendum in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Australian_republic_referendum "1999 Australian republic referendum") on the future of the [Australian monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_monarchy "Australian monarchy") favoured its retention in preference to an indirectly elected head of state. Many republicans credited Elizabeth's personal popularity with the survival of the monarchy in Australia. In 2010, Prime Minister [Julia Gillard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Gillard "Julia Gillard") noted that there was a "deep affection" for Elizabeth in Australia and that another referendum on the monarchy should wait until after her reign. Gillard's successor, [Malcolm Turnbull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Turnbull "Malcolm Turnbull"), who led the republican campaign in 1999, similarly believed that Australians would not vote to become a republic in her lifetime. "She's been an extraordinary head of state", Turnbull said in 2021, "and I think frankly, in Australia, there are more Elizabethans than there are monarchists." Similarly, referendums in both [Tuvalu in 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Tuvaluan_constitutional_referendum "2008 Tuvaluan constitutional referendum") and [Saint Vincent and the Grenadines in 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Vincentian_constitutional_referendum "2009 Vincentian constitutional referendum") saw voters reject proposals to become republics.

Polls in Britain in 2006 and 2007 revealed strong support for the monarchy, and in 2012, Elizabeth's Diamond Jubilee year, her approval ratings hit 90 per cent. Her family came under scrutiny again in the last few years of her life due to her son Andrew's association with convicted sex offenders [Jeffrey Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein "Jeffrey Epstein") and [Ghislaine Maxwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghislaine_Maxwell "Ghislaine Maxwell"), [his lawsuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuffre_v._Prince_Andrew "Giuffre v. Prince Andrew") with [Virginia Giuffre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Giuffre "Virginia Giuffre") amidst accusations of sexual impropriety, and her grandson Harry and his wife [Meghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meghan,_Duchess_of_Sussex "Meghan, Duchess of Sussex")'s [exit from the working royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megxit "Megxit") and subsequent move to the United States. Polling in Great Britain during the Platinum Jubilee, however, showed support for maintaining the monarchy and Elizabeth's personal popularity remained strong. As of 2021 she remained the third [most admired woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most_admired_woman "Most admired woman") in the world according to the annual [Gallup poll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallup_poll "Gallup poll"), her 52 appearances on the list meaning she had been in the top ten more than any other woman in the poll's history.

Elizabeth was portrayed in a variety of media by many notable artists, including painters [Pietro Annigoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Annigoni "Pietro Annigoni"), [Peter Blake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Blake_(artist) "Peter Blake (artist)"), [Chinwe Chukwuogo-Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinwe_Chukwuogo-Roy "Chinwe Chukwuogo-Roy"), [Terence Cuneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terence_Cuneo "Terence Cuneo"), [Lucian Freud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucian_Freud "Lucian Freud"), [Rolf Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolf_Harris "Rolf Harris"), [Damien Hirst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damien_Hirst "Damien Hirst"), [Juliet Pannett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juliet_Pannett "Juliet Pannett") and [Tai-Shan Schierenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai-Shan_Schierenberg "Tai-Shan Schierenberg"). Notable photographers of Elizabeth included [Cecil Beaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecil_Beaton "Cecil Beaton"), [Yousuf Karsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yousuf_Karsh "Yousuf Karsh"), [Anwar Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anwar_Hussein_(photographer) "Anwar Hussein (photographer)"), [Annie Leibovitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_Leibovitz "Annie Leibovitz"), [Lord Lichfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Anson,_5th_Earl_of_Lichfield "Patrick Anson, 5th Earl of Lichfield"), [Terry O'Neill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_O%27Neill_(photographer) "Terry O'Neill (photographer)"), [John Swannell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Swannell_(photographer) "John Swannell (photographer)") and [Dorothy Wilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothy_Wilding "Dorothy Wilding"). The first official [portrait photograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portrait_photograph "Portrait photograph") of Elizabeth was taken by [Marcus Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Adams_(photographer) "Marcus Adams (photographer)") in 1926.

## Titles, styles, honours, and arms

### Titles and styles

Elizabeth held many titles and honorary military positions throughout [the Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Commonwealth "The Commonwealth"), was sovereign of many orders in her own countries and received honours and awards from around the world. In each of her realms, she had a distinct title that follows a similar formula: *Queen of Saint Lucia and of Her other Realms and Territories* in [Saint Lucia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_Saint_Lucia "Monarchy of Saint Lucia"), *Queen of Australia and Her other Realms and Territories* in Australia, etc. She was also styled *[Defender of the Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defender_of_the_Faith "Defender of the Faith")*.

### Arms

From 21 April 1944 until her accession, Elizabeth's arms consisted of a [lozenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lozenge_(heraldry) "Lozenge (heraldry)") bearing the [royal coat of arms of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_coat_of_arms_of_the_United_Kingdom "Royal coat of arms of the United Kingdom") differenced with a [label](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Label_(heraldry) "Label (heraldry)") of three points [argent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argent "Argent"), the centre point bearing a [Tudor rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudor_rose "Tudor rose") and the first and third a [cross of Saint George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_of_Saint_George "Cross of Saint George"). Upon her accession, she inherited the various arms her father held as sovereign, with a subsequently modified representation of the crown. Elizabeth also possessed [royal standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heraldic_banner "Heraldic banner") and personal flags for use [in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Standard_of_the_United_Kingdom "Royal Standard of the United Kingdom"), [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_standards_of_Canada "Royal standards of Canada"), [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen%27s_Personal_Australian_Flag "Queen's Personal Australian Flag"), [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen%27s_Personal_New_Zealand_Flag "Queen's Personal New Zealand Flag"), [Jamaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen%27s_Personal_Jamaican_Flag "Queen's Personal Jamaican Flag"), and elsewhere. Elizabeth approved her modified British arms on 26 May 1954.
