# Charles Spencer, 9th Earl Spencer

**Charles Edward Maurice Spencer, 9th Earl Spencer** (born 20 May 1964), styled **Viscount Althorp** between 1975 and 1992, is a British [peer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peerage_of_the_United_Kingdom "Peerage of the United Kingdom"), historian, author, journalist and broadcaster. He is the younger brother of [Diana, Princess of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diana,_Princess_of_Wales "Diana, Princess of Wales"), and the maternal uncle of [William, Prince of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William,_Prince_of_Wales "William, Prince of Wales"), and [Prince Harry, Duke of Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Harry,_Duke_of_Sussex "Prince Harry, Duke of Sussex").

## Early life and education

Charles Edward Maurice Spencer was born on 20 May 1964 in [Marylebone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marylebone "Marylebone"), London, the youngest of five children of [John Spencer, 8th Earl Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Spencer,_8th_Earl_Spencer "John Spencer, 8th Earl Spencer"), and [Frances Roche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Shand_Kydd "Frances Shand Kydd") (later Shand Kydd), daughter of [Maurice Roche, 4th Baron Fermoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Roche,_4th_Baron_Fermoy "Maurice Roche, 4th Baron Fermoy"). His parents married in 1954 at [Westminster Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_Abbey "Westminster Abbey"), with the [royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_royal_family "British royal family") in attendance. Spencer grew up with three elder sisters, [Sarah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Sarah_McCorquodale "Lady Sarah McCorquodale"), [Jane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Jane_Fellowes "Lady Jane Fellowes") and [Diana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diana,_Princess_of_Wales "Diana, Princess of Wales"). An elder brother, John, had been born four years before him, but died 10 hours after birth, leaving Charles as the eventual heir to the earldom. Within the family, he was also known informally as "Carlos" and "Buzz". He lived at [Park House, Sandringham Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_House,_Sandringham "Park House, Sandringham"), until the death of his paternal grandfather, after which the family moved to [Althorp House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Althorp "Althorp"), the Spencers' estate.

Both sides of Spencer's family had long‑standing connections to the royal household, and he was baptised at Westminster Abbey, with [Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II "Queen Elizabeth II") serving as one of his godmothers. His father was an equerry to [King George VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_VI "George VI") and Queen Elizabeth ll, and both his grandmothers, [Lady Cynthia Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cynthia_Spencer,_Countess_Spencer "Cynthia Spencer, Countess Spencer") and [Ruth Roche, Baroness Fermoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Roche,_Baroness_Fermoy "Ruth Roche, Baroness Fermoy"), were ladies-in-waiting to [Queen Elizabeth the Queen Mother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_the_Queen_Mother "Queen Elizabeth the Queen Mother"). His brother-in-law, [Robert Fellowes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Fellowes,_Baron_Fellowes "Robert Fellowes, Baron Fellowes"), was the private secretary of Queen Elizabeth ll from 1986 to 1999.

Spencer was three years old when his parents' troubled marriage ended in divorce following his mother's affair with [Peter Shand Kydd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Shand_Kydd "Peter Shand Kydd"). In 1975, Spencer became styled Viscount Althorp when his father succeeded as [Earl Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_Spencer_(peerage) "Earl Spencer (peerage)") on the death of [his paternal grandfather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Spencer,_7th_Earl_Spencer "Albert Spencer, 7th Earl Spencer"). He began his formal education at Silfield Private School in [King's Lynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King%27s_Lynn "King's Lynn"), Norfolk, and from the age of eight attended the boys' [private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_schools_in_the_United_Kingdom "Private schools in the United Kingdom") [boarding school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boarding_school "Boarding school") [Maidwell Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maidwell_Hall "Maidwell Hall") in [Northamptonshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northamptonshire "Northamptonshire"). He was later educated at [Eton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eton_College "Eton College") and completed a degree in [Modern History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_history "Modern history") at [Magdalen College, Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalen_College,_Oxford "Magdalen College, Oxford").

In his memoir *[A Very Private School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Very_Private_School "A Very Private School")*, published in March 2024, Spencer described the violent beatings, sexual abuse, "culture of cruelty", "hopelessness and abandonment", and the absence of affection he experienced at Maidwell Hall, one of England's most prestigious boarding schools.

## Career

Spencer worked as an on-air correspondent with [NBC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_News "NBC News") from 1986 to 1995, primarily for the network's morning programme, *[Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Today_(NBC_program) "Today (NBC program)")* and *[NBC Nightly News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_Nightly_News "NBC Nightly News").* He wrote and presented the 12-part documentary series *Great Houses of the World* (1994–1995) for [NBC Super Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_Europe "NBC Europe"). He also worked as a reporter for [Granada Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_Granada "ITV Granada") from 1991 to 1993.

Spencer has written several book reviews for *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* and *[The Independent on Sunday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent_on_Sunday "The Independent on Sunday"),* as well as feature stories for *The Guardian*, *[The Daily Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph "The Daily Telegraph")*, *[The Sunday Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sunday_Telegraph "The Sunday Telegraph")* and American publications such as *[Vanity Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanity_Fair_(magazine) "Vanity Fair (magazine)")*, *Verandah* and *Nest*.

Upon his father's death on 29 March 1992, Spencer succeeded as 9th [Earl Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_Spencer_(peerage) "Earl Spencer (peerage)"). He also inherited [Althorp House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Althorp "Althorp"), the family's ancestral seat in [Northamptonshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northamptonshire "Northamptonshire"). Since 2009, he has restored Althorp, re-roofing it and restoring its entire exterior for the first time since the 1780s. He has also helped establish Althorp Living History, a handmade fine-furniture line reproducing pieces from the collection at Althorp. The Spencer family's wealth derived from their profitable sheep farming in the [Tudor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Tudor "House of Tudor") era.

Spencer was a member of the [House of Lords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Lords "House of Lords") from 29 March 1992, the day his father died and he inherited the peerage, until the [House of Lords Act 1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Lords_Act_1999 "House of Lords Act 1999") excluded most [hereditary peers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hereditary_peers "Hereditary peers") on 11 November 1999.

On several occasions, Spencer has been accused of refusing to allow his sister Diana to live in a cottage on the Althorp estate, despite her request at the height of her emotional difficulties. The claim has however been disputed, by accounts that state Spencer offered Diana alternative properties. In 2021, *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times "The Times")* issued an apology acknowledging that "having considered his sister's safety, and in line with police advice, Spencer offered the Princess of Wales a number of properties included [Wormleighton Manor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wormleighton_Manor "Wormleighton Manor") (in [Warwickshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwickshire "Warwickshire")), the Spencer family's original ancestral home". In the correspondence which came to light during the 2002 trial of Diana's former butler [Paul Burrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Burrell "Paul Burrell"), Spencer told the princess that their relationship was the weakest he had with any of his sisters. He called her a "fickle friend" who was deceitful and manipulative. He wrote: "I know how manipulation and deceit are parts of the illness... I pray that you are getting appropriate and sympathetic treatment for your mental problems".

On 31 August 1997, Diana [died after a car crash in Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Diana,_Princess_of_Wales "Death of Diana, Princess of Wales") and Spencer delivered the eulogy at [her funeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funeral_of_Diana,_Princess_of_Wales "Funeral of Diana, Princess of Wales") service held at Westminster Abbey six days later. In his eulogy, he rebuked both [Britain's royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britain%27s_royal_family "Britain's royal family") and the press for their treatment of his sister. Spencer ruled out [conspiracy theories concerning his sister's death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_theories_about_the_death_of_Diana,_Princess_of_Wales "Conspiracy theories about the death of Diana, Princess of Wales") and called the alleged letter she wrote 10 months before her death, in which she discussed her fears of a planned accident, "just a bizarre coincidence rather than tied in with reality." Spencer received an apology from [Tim Davie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Davie "Tim Davie"), the BBC's director general, in late 2020 for the unethical practices used by [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") staff to gain his sister's consent to, “[An Interview with HRH The Princess of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Interview_with_HRH_The_Princess_of_Wales "An Interview with HRH The Princess of Wales")" in November 1995 for the corporation's *[Panorama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panorama_(British_TV_programme) "Panorama (British TV programme)")* television programme. Former BBC chairman [Michael Grade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Grade "Michael Grade") said a full inquiry should be conducted, which Davie has said would happen. Spencer said that he would not have introduced [Martin Bashir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Bashir "Martin Bashir") to his sister without the falsified documents that Bashir used to make false and [defamatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defamation "Defamation") claims about senior members of the royal family.

Diana was buried on an island in a lake on Spencer's ancestral estate, Althorp, where he built a garden temple memorial and a museum to her memory, displaying her wedding dress and other personal items. The museum was opened to the public in 1998, with all profits going to the [Diana, Princess of Wales Memorial Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diana,_Princess_of_Wales_Memorial_Fund "Diana, Princess of Wales Memorial Fund"); the museum has since closed. At this stage, Spencer began writing a series of books dealing with the estate itself and with his family history, beginning with an account of his ancestral home, *Althorp: the Story of an English House*, published in 1998.

In 2003, Spencer founded the Althorp Literary Festival. Speakers at the annual event have included the authors [Bill Bryson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Bryson "Bill Bryson"), [Helen Fielding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Fielding "Helen Fielding"), [Antonia Fraser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonia_Fraser "Antonia Fraser") and [Boris Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Johnson "Boris Johnson"). In 2004, he presented two documentaries for the [History Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_Channel "History Channel") on *Blenheim: Battle for Europe*.

Spencer was appointed a [Deputy Lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Lieutenant "Deputy Lieutenant") of [Northamptonshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northamptonshire "Northamptonshire") in November 2005; the Spencer family have had a long association with the county, the home of the family seat. Spencer is also a patron of the [Northamptonshire County Cricket Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northamptonshire_County_Cricket_Club "Northamptonshire County Cricket Club"). In 2021, Spencer authored an audiovisual walking tour for [St. James's Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_James%27s_Park "St James's Park") about the execution of [Charles I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_I_of_England "Charles I of England"), entitled *Death of a King: The Path to Execution,* on the BARD*EUM* mobile app.

In 2023, he began presenting the podcast *The Rabbit Hole Detectives* with [Richard Coles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Coles "Richard Coles") and Cat Jarman, in which each of them is given an obscure topic and they then discuss their findings. In May 2025, Spencer joined the panel of ITV's *Loose Men*, a male version of the channel's *[Loose Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loose_Women "Loose Women")* programme.

Spencer's memoir of Diana, *[Swan Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_Song_(memoir) "Swan Song (memoir)")*, was published on 22 September 2026. In extracts released ahead of publication, Spencer alleged that the then [Prince Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_III "Charles III") (later King Charles III) said "we'll forget her soon enough" in a phone call with him after her death, describing his tone as "giddily elated" and as if he were a "lottery winner". The allegations prompted [Buckingham Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckingham_Palace "Buckingham Palace") to issue a statement, saying that the King "is mindful that the pain of fraternal grief can cloud reason, affect judgment and colour memory in ways others do not recognise, even many years after such a loss." Journalist [Piers Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piers_Morgan "Piers Morgan") said he would sue Spencer, disputing allegations in the memoir and denying Spencer's allegation that he was responsible for publishing photographs of Diana taken in a gym. He noted that the images appeared in the *[Sunday Mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunday_Mirror "Sunday Mirror")* in 1993, "nearly two years before I became editor of the *[Daily Mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Mirror "Daily Mirror")*", and described the memoir as containing "fabricated inaccuracies". In a statement, Spencer acknowledged the error and apologised to Morgan.

## Personal life

On 16 September 1989, Spencer, then known by the [courtesy title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtesy_title "Courtesy title") of Viscount Althorp, married [Victoria Lockwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Aitken "Victoria Aitken"). They have four children: [Kitty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Kitty_Spencer "Lady Kitty Spencer") (born 1990), [Eliza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Eliza_Spencer "Lady Eliza Spencer") and [Amelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Amelia_Spencer "Lady Amelia Spencer") (born 1992, twins), and [Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Spencer,_Viscount_Althorp "Louis Spencer, Viscount Althorp") (born 1994). It was reported that Spencer had an extra-marital affair with a journalist early in the marriage. In the spring of 1994, after the birth of their last child, he met a married South African socialite with whom he began an affair that lasted two years and five months, ending in December 1996. Spencer and Lockwood were divorced on 3 December 1997, with Diana's death occurring while the case was in progress. After the divorce, Spencer returned from [Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town "Cape Town"), South Africa, where he and Lockwood had relocated their family in 1995 to avoid media attention. Spencer did not attend the weddings of his daughters Kitty and Amelia in 2021 and 2023, respectively, amid reports that his relationship with his elder children had "cooled".

On 15 December 2001, Spencer married [Caroline Freud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Hutton "Caroline Hutton") (née Hutton), former wife of businessman [Matthew Freud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Freud "Matthew Freud"). They have two children: Ned (born 2003) and Lara (born 2006). They separated in 2007 and later divorced. [Mr Justice Munby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Munby "James Munby") was the presiding judge. Spencer's barrister [Nicholas Mostyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Mostyn "Nicholas Mostyn") advised his client that the case could be heard in private, which Munby rejected. Spencer was upset at the final settlement and unsuccessfully sued Mostyn.

On 18 June 2011 at Althorp, Spencer married [Karen Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Gordon "Karen Gordon") (née [Villeneuve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villeneuve_(surname) "Villeneuve (surname)")), a Canadian philanthropist, the founder and chief executive of [Whole Child International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whole_Child_International "Whole Child International"), a charity based in Los Angeles. They have a daughter: Charlotte (born 2012). In June 2024, it was announced that Spencer and Gordon had separated in April 2024 and planned to divorce. Spencer was represented by [Baroness Shackleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroness_Shackleton "Baroness Shackleton"), who represented the then-Prince of Wales in his divorce from Spencer's sister Diana. Their divorce was finalised in December 2025.

It was reported in 2024 that Spencer was in a relationship with archaeologist [Cat Jarman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat_Jarman "Cat Jarman"), with whom he was co-hosting the podcast *The Rabbit Hole Detectives*. The two met in 2021 when Spencer was asked to review Jarman's book *River Kings*, the same year they worked together on an archaeological project at the Spencer family estate. Spencer has noted that their differing backgrounds, particularly Jarman's lack of connection to British social circles, has contributed positively to the relationship. In October 2024, Jarman brought a claim against Gordon for alleged misuse of private information relating to her [MS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_sclerosis "Multiple sclerosis") diagnosis. The claim was settled in December 2025 via a [Part 36 provision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settlement_offer "Settlement offer"), with Spencer ordered to indemnify some of the costs as part of the Spencers' divorce arbitration. On 15 May 2026, Spencer married Jarman in [Sedona, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedona,_Arizona "Sedona, Arizona"), United States.

Spencer has stated that he suffers from [rejection sensitive dysphoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rejection_sensitive_dysphoria "Rejection sensitive dysphoria"). He has also spoken openly about his struggles with alcohol and marked six months without alcohol in October 2025.
