# Trinity Rodman

**Trinity Rain Moyer-Rodman** (born May 20, 2002) is an American professional [soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football "Association football") player who plays as a [forward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_(association_football) "Forward (association football)") for the [Washington Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Spirit "Washington Spirit") of the [National Women's Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Women%27s_Soccer_League "National Women's Soccer League") (NWSL) and the [United States national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_women%27s_national_soccer_team "United States women's national soccer team").

Rodman was the second overall pick by the Spirit in the [2021 NWSL Draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_NWSL_Draft "2021 NWSL Draft"), becoming the NWSL's youngest draftee at the time at age 18. She helped the club win the [NWSL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_NWSL_Championship "2021 NWSL Championship") and was named the [NWSL Rookie of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NWSL_Rookie_of_the_Year "NWSL Rookie of the Year") and [NWSL Best XI First Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NWSL_Best_XI_First_Team "NWSL Best XI First Team") in her debut season. She has been named to the NWSL Best XI three times with the Spirit.

Rodman was named the [U.S. Soccer Young Female Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Soccer_Player_of_the_Year "U.S. Soccer Player of the Year") in 2021, making her senior debut for the United States the following year. She scored three goals at the [2024 Paris Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_tournament "Football at the 2024 Summer Olympics – Women's tournament"), where the United States won gold.

## Early life

Trinity Rain Moyer-Rodman was born on May 20, 2002, in [Newport Beach, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_Beach,_California "Newport Beach, California"), to Michelle Moyer and former professional basketball player [Dennis Rodman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Rodman "Dennis Rodman"). She and her brother, [DJ Rodman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJ_Rodman "DJ Rodman"), were raised primarily by their mother in southern California. Rodman has said she went through long stretches without speaking to her father, and she briefly lived with her mother in their car. She also has a half-sister.

Rodman began playing soccer at age four and said it "felt like home" to her as young as seven or eight. She was encouraged by her mother and sister to pursue her dream of becoming a professional soccer player.

Rodman began playing club soccer with SoCal Blues at the age of ten. Her team won four national championships in the [Elite Clubs National League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elite_Clubs_National_League "Elite Clubs National League") (ECNL) and maintained a five-year undefeated streak. Rodman attended and played for [Corona del Mar High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corona_del_Mar_High_School "Corona del Mar High School") for one year as a freshman before transferring to [JSerra Catholic High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JSerra_Catholic_High_School "JSerra Catholic High School") in nearby [San Juan Capistrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_Capistrano,_California "San Juan Capistrano, California") but did not play for the school.

Rodman initially committed to play collegiate soccer for the [UCLA Bruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA_Bruins_women%27s_soccer "UCLA Bruins women's soccer") before deciding to follow her older brother to the [Washington State Cougars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_Cougars_women%27s_soccer "Washington State Cougars women's soccer") beginning in the 2020 season. However, she never played a match in college as her freshman season was canceled due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic") and she decided to turn professional.

## Club career

### Washington Spirit

#### 2021

At age 18, Rodman became the youngest player to be drafted in NWSL history when the [Washington Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Spirit "Washington Spirit") selected her as their first draft pick (second overall) at the [2021 NWSL College Draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_NWSL_College_Draft "2021 NWSL College Draft"). Before being drafted by the Spirit, she had never been to Washington, D.C. Rodman made her professional debut on April 10, 2021, scoring a goal within five minutes of being subbed on against the [North Carolina Courage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Courage "North Carolina Courage") in the [2021 NWSL Challenge Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_NWSL_Challenge_Cup "2021 NWSL Challenge Cup") group stage. On April 15, she assisted [Ashley Sanchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashley_Sanchez "Ashley Sanchez")'s stoppage-time winner in a 1–0 victory over [Racing Louisville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racing_Louisville "Racing Louisville"). She appeared in all four of the Spirit's matches during the Challenge Cup, where they finished in fourth place in the East Division with a 1–2–1 record.

Rodman was the Spirit's second-leading scorer with 6 goals (behind [Ashley Hatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashley_Hatch "Ashley Hatch")) and added 5 assists in 22 games in her rookie regular season. The team finished in third place during the regular season with a 11–7–6 record and advanced to the [playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NWSL_Playoffs "NWSL Playoffs"). After the Spirit defeated [North Carolina Courage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Courage "North Carolina Courage") 1–0, Rodman scored in the Spirit's 2–1 win against [OL Reign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OL_Reign "OL Reign") to advance to [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_NWSL_Championship "2021 NWSL Championship"), where they faced the [Chicago Red Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Red_Stars "Chicago Red Stars"). Rodman helped lift the Spirit to their first league championship with an assist [Kelley O'Hara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelley_O%27Hara "Kelley O'Hara")'s winning goal. At age 19, Rodman became the youngest player in league history to record an assist in the playoffs.

On November 17, 2021, Rodman was named [NWSL Rookie of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NWSL_Rookie_of_the_Year "NWSL Rookie of the Year") and to the [NWSL Best XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NWSL_Best_XI#2021 "NWSL Best XI"). She earned [U.S. Soccer Young Female Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Soccer_Player_of_the_Year "U.S. Soccer Player of the Year") honors the following month. On February 2, 2022, Rodman signed a contract extension with the Washington Spirit running until after the 2024 season, with an option for 2025. It was reported that the new contract was worth $1.1 million, making Rodman the highest paid player in NWSL history.

#### 2022

On April 3, 2022, Rodman scored once and assisted twice in a 4–1 win against the [Orlando Pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Pride "Orlando Pride") in the [2022 NWSL Challenge Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_NWSL_Challenge_Cup "2022 NWSL Challenge Cup") group stage. In the following game, she scored a [brace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brace_(association_football) "Brace (association football)") as the Spirit came back to defeat [NJ/NY Gotham FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NJ/NY_Gotham_FC "NJ/NY Gotham FC") 3–1 on April 17. She recorded four goals and three assists in eight games in the Challenge Cup as Washington reached [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_NWSL_Challenge_Cup_Championship "2022 NWSL Challenge Cup Championship"), a 2–1 losing effort against the [North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina "North Carolina") despite Rodman's game-tying assist to [Ashley Hatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashley_Hatch "Ashley Hatch").

On August 13, 2022, Rodman was nominated for the [Ballon d'Or Féminin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballon_d%27Or_F%C3%A9minin "Ballon d'Or Féminin"). She was a nominee for the [Best Breakthrough Athlete ESPY Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Breakthrough_Athlete_ESPY_Award "Best Breakthrough Athlete ESPY Award"). She finished the 2022 regular season with 4 goals and 2 assists in 18 games as the defending champions crashed to 11th out of 12 teams in the league.

#### 2023

Rodman opened the 2023 season with two goals and an assist in the first three games and helped the Spirit earn points from their first seven games. On May 6, 2023, she scored and assisted [Ashley Sanchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashley_Sanchez "Ashley Sanchez") in a 3–1 win against the [San Diego Wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Wave "San Diego Wave"). She scored a third-minute goal against Racing Louisville on June 3. She finished the 2023 season with 5 goals and 2 assists in 19 games, with Washington finishing in eighth place and missing the playoffs. Rodman was named in the [NWSL Best XI Second Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NWSL_Best_XI_Second_Team "NWSL Best XI Second Team").

#### 2024

Rodman was the Spirit's joint-leading scorer in the 2024 regular season with 8 goals (tied with [Ouleymata Sarr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouleymata_Sarr "Ouleymata Sarr")) and 6 assists in 23 games. Two of her goals won [NWSL Goal of the Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NWSL_Goal_of_the_Week "NWSL Goal of the Week"). On September 20, 2024, she left the game against the [Kansas City Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_Current "Kansas City Current") in a wheelchair due to back spasms. She returned to action three games later and helped the team finish second in the regular-season standings. Despite still dealing with the back injury, she played all 330 minutes of the playoffs as Washington beat [Bay FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_FC "Bay FC") in overtime, overcame NJ/NY Gotham FC on penalties, and fell one goal short to the [Orlando Pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Pride "Orlando Pride") in the [championship game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NWSL_Championship "2024 NWSL Championship"). Rodman was named in the [NWSL Best XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NWSL_Best_XI "NWSL Best XI") and was one of five nominees for [NWSL Most Valuable Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NWSL_Most_Valuable_Player "NWSL Most Valuable Player"), losing to [Temwa Chawinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temwa_Chawinga "Temwa Chawinga").

#### 2025

Rodman began the season as an unused substitute in the [2025 NWSL Challenge Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NWSL_Challenge_Cup "2025 NWSL Challenge Cup") before playing off the bench in the season-opening win over the [Houston Dash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Dash "Houston Dash"). However, she continued to suffer from her back injury and took time off to rehab with a doctor in London. On August 3, after four months away, she returned to the NWSL and scored an emotional game-winner in a 2–1 victory over the [Portland Thorns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Thorns "Portland Thorns"). She was named the [NWSL Player of the Month](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NWSL_Player_of_the_Month "NWSL Player of the Month") for September after scoring three goals with two assists in four starts. On October 15, she suffered a Grade 1 [medial collateral ligament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medial_collateral_ligament "Medial collateral ligament") (MCL) sprain in her right knee during a [CONCACAF W Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_CONCACAF_W_Champions_Cup "2025–26 CONCACAF W Champions Cup") match against [Monterrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.F._Monterrey_(women) "C.F. Monterrey (women)"). She missed the rest of the regular season and finished with 5 goals in 15 league games as the Spirit placed second for a second consecutive year. She returned in the playoff semifinals on November 15, as a late substitute in a 2–0 win over the Portland Thorns. She then played in a second consecutive [NWSL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NWSL_Championship "2025 NWSL Championship") but said she "definitely underperformed" as she recorded no shots in 33 minutes in the 1–0 loss to Gotham FC. Her impending free agency created much discussion, including questioning of the league's [salary cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salary_cap "Salary cap"), and NWSL commissioner [Jessica Berman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Berman "Jessica Berman") said: "We want Trinity in our league and we will fight for her".

#### 2026

On January 22, 2026, it was reported that the Washington Spirit had re-signed Rodman on a three-year contract, with an annual salary of over $2 million, reportedly making her "the highest-paid player in league history and the highest-paid female player in the world".

## International career

Rodman has represented the United States on the [senior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_women%27s_national_soccer_team "United States women's national soccer team"), [under-20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_U-20_women%27s_national_soccer_team "United States U-20 women's national soccer team"), and [under-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_U-17_women%27s_national_soccer_team "United States U-17 women's national soccer team") national teams. She competed at the [2018 FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_U-17_Women%27s_World_Cup "2018 FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup") in Uruguay where she played 165 minutes and notched one assist. In 2020, she scored nine goals (including four against Honduras and two against Mexico in the final) and helped the United States win the [2020 CONCACAF Women's U-20 Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_CONCACAF_Women%27s_U-20_Championship "2020 CONCACAF Women's U-20 Championship"). She was nominated for the [U.S. Soccer Young Female Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Soccer_Player_of_the_Year "U.S. Soccer Player of the Year") award the same year, but did not win. She won the award in 2021.

In January 2022, Rodman was named to the senior national team's camp for the first time ahead of the [2022 SheBelieves Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_SheBelieves_Cup "2022 SheBelieves Cup"). She made her national team debut on February 17, 2022, in a 0–0 draw with [Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Republic_women%27s_national_football_team "Czech Republic women's national football team") at the tournament, and scored her first goal on April 12, 2022, in a friendly against Uzbekistan, in her third national team appearance. In June 2022, Rodman was named to the U.S. roster for the [2022 CONCACAF W Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_CONCACAF_W_Championship "2022 CONCACAF W Championship") team.

In June 2023, Rodman was named to the U.S. squad for the [2023 FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2023 FIFA Women's World Cup") in Australia and New Zealand. During a World Cup send-off international friendly against [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_women%27s_national_football_team "Wales women's national football team") on July 9, she scored a [brace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brace_(sports) "Brace (sports)") leading the U.S. to a 2–0 win after subbing in for [Alex Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Morgan "Alex Morgan") during the 46th minute. Named Woman of the Match, Rodman is the youngest player to score a brace in team history. Head coach [Vlatko Andonovski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlatko_Andonovski "Vlatko Andonovski") described her second goal as "world class".

Rodman made her World Cup debut during the team's first group stage match against [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_women%27s_national_football_team "Vietnam women's national football team"): a 3–0 win.

On June 26, 2024, Rodman was one of the 18 players named by [Emma Hayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Hayes "Emma Hayes") to represent the United States at the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_tournament "Football at the 2024 Summer Olympics – Women's tournament"). After scoring two goals in the group stage, she scored in extra time in the first knockout game against [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_women%27s_national_football_team "Japan women's national football team") on August 3 to advance to the semifinals. She started in the gold medal game against [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_women%27s_national_soccer_team "Brazil women's national soccer team"), which the United States won 1–0 on a goal from [Mallory Swanson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallory_Swanson "Mallory Swanson").

In December 2024 she was voted by *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* in 5th place among the top 100 women footballers in the world.

## Personal life

In July 2023, Rodman was in a relationship with Chris Kuzemka.

In May 2024, Rodman began dating former [NFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League "National Football League") wide receiver [Trinity Benson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Benson "Trinity Benson").

In December 2024, Rodman appeared on the *[Call Her Daddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_Her_Daddy "Call Her Daddy")* podcast and spoke about her relationship with her father, Dennis, saying, "He's not a dad. Maybe by blood, but nothing else." She described him as a controlling parent and said that, as a child, she gave up on living with him because of his party lifestyle. Rodman said she "lost hope in ever getting him back" after he attended one of her Washington Spirit matches unannounced in 2021 and then did not speak to her again until 2023.

In early 2025, Rodman began dating tennis player [Ben Shelton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Shelton "Ben Shelton").

## Other work

Rodman has endorsement deals with [Red Bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bull "Red Bull"), [Oakley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakley,_Inc. "Oakley, Inc."), and [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas "Adidas"). She is the author of a children's book, entitled *Wake Up and Kick It*. In June 2022, Rodman was featured in ESPN SportsCenter's "Trinity Rodman" as part of ESPN's Fifty/50 month-long programming in honor of the 50th anniversary of [Title IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_IX "Title IX") in the United States. She has starred in television commercials for [Verizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verizon "Verizon"), [Rexona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rexona "Rexona"), and [Fox Soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Soccer "Fox Soccer").
