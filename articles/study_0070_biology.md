# Biology

**Biology** is the [scientific study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_study "Scientific study") of [life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life "Life") and living [organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organism "Organism"). It is a broad [natural science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_science "Natural science") that encompasses a wide range of fields and unifying principles that explain the structure, function, growth, [origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_life "History of life"), [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution "Evolution"), and distribution of life. Central to biology are five fundamental themes: the [cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_(biology) "Cell (biology)") as the basic unit of life, [genes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene "Gene") and [heredity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heredity "Heredity") as the basis of inheritance, evolution as the driver of [biological diversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_diversity "Biological diversity"), [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy "Energy") transformation for sustaining life processes, and [homeostasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeostasis "Homeostasis"), the maintenance of internal stability.

Biology examines life across multiple [levels of organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_organisation "Biological organisation"), from [molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecule "Molecule") and cells to organisms, [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_(biology) "Population (biology)"), and [ecosystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystem "Ecosystem"). Subdisciplines include [molecular biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_biology "Molecular biology"), [physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology "Physiology"), [ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecology "Ecology"), [evolutionary biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_biology "Evolutionary biology"), [developmental biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developmental_biology "Developmental biology"), and [systematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systematics "Systematics"), among others. Each of these fields applies a range of methods to investigate biological phenomena, including [observation, experimentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_method "Scientific method"), and [mathematical modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_modeling "Mathematical modeling"). Modern biology is grounded in the theory of evolution by [natural selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_selection "Natural selection"), first articulated by [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin "Charles Darwin"), and in the molecular understanding of genes encoded in [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA"). The discovery of the [structure of DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_of_DNA "Structure of DNA") and advances in [molecular genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_genetics "Molecular genetics") have transformed many areas of biology, leading to applications in [medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine "Medicine"), [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture "Agriculture"), [biotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotechnology "Biotechnology"), and [environmental science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_science "Environmental science").

Biologists classify [organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organism "Organism")—from single-celled [archaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaea "Archaea") and [bacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteria "Bacteria") to multicellular [plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant "Plant"), [fungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fungi "Fungi"), and [animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal "Animal")—based on shared characteristics and evolutionary relationships, using [taxonomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxonomy_(biology) "Taxonomy (biology)") and [phylogenetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylogenetic "Phylogenetic").

## Etymology

From [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language "Greek language") [βίος](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%CE%B2%CE%AF%CE%BF%CF%82 "wikt:βίος") (*bíos*) 'life', (from [Proto-Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European_language "Proto-Indo-European language") root \*gwei-, to live) and [λογία](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%CE%BB%CE%BF%CE%B3%CE%AF%CE%B1 "wikt:λογία") (*logia*) 'study of'. The compound appears in the title of Volume 3 of [Michael Christoph Hanow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Christoph_Hanow "Michael Christoph Hanow")'s *Philosophiae naturalis sive physicae dogmaticae: Geologia, biologia, phytologia generalis et dendrologia*, published in 1766. The term *biology* in its modern sense appears to have been introduced independently by [Thomas Beddoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Beddoes "Thomas Beddoes") (in 1799), [Karl Friedrich Burdach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Friedrich_Burdach "Karl Friedrich Burdach") (in 1800), [Gottfried Reinhold Treviranus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Reinhold_Treviranus "Gottfried Reinhold Treviranus") (*Biologie oder Philosophie der lebenden Natur*, 1802) and [Jean-Baptiste Lamarck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Baptiste_Lamarck "Jean-Baptiste Lamarck") (*Hydrogéologie*, 1802).

## History

The earliest of roots of science, which included medicine, can be traced to [ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt "Ancient Egypt") and [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia "Mesopotamia") in around 3000 to 1200 [BCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Era "Common Era"). Their contributions shaped ancient Greek [natural philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_philosophy "Natural philosophy"). [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece "Ancient Greece") philosophers such as [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle "Aristotle") (384–322 BCE) contributed extensively to the development of biological knowledge. He explored biological causation and the diversity of life. His successor, [Theophrastus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theophrastus "Theophrastus"), began the scientific study of plants. Scholars of the [medieval Islamic world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_in_the_medieval_Islamic_world "Science in the medieval Islamic world") who wrote on biology included [al-Jahiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Jahiz "Al-Jahiz") (781–869), [Al-Dīnawarī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-D%C4%ABnawar%C4%AB "Al-Dīnawarī") (828–896), who wrote on botany.

Biology began to develop quickly with [Anton van Leeuwenhoek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_van_Leeuwenhoek "Anton van Leeuwenhoek")'s dramatic improvement of the [microscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microscope "Microscope"). It was then that scholars discovered [spermatozoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spermatozoa "Spermatozoa"), bacteria, [infusoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infusoria "Infusoria") and the diversity of microscopic life. Investigations by [Jan Swammerdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Swammerdam "Jan Swammerdam") led to new interest in [entomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entomology "Entomology") and helped to develop techniques of microscopic [dissection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissection "Dissection") and [staining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staining "Staining"). Advances in microscopy had a profound impact on biological thinking. In the early 19th century, biologists pointed to the central importance of the [cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_(biology) "Cell (biology)"). In 1838, [Schleiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Jakob_Schleiden "Matthias Jakob Schleiden") and [Schwann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Schwann "Theodor Schwann") began promoting the now universal ideas that (1) the basic unit of organisms is the cell and (2) that individual cells have all the characteristics of life, although they opposed the idea that (3) all cells come from the division of other cells, continuing to support [spontaneous generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spontaneous_generation "Spontaneous generation"). However, [Robert Remak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Remak "Robert Remak") and [Rudolf Virchow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Virchow "Rudolf Virchow") were able to reify the third tenet, and by the 1860s most biologists accepted all three tenets which consolidated into [cell theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_theory "Cell theory").

Meanwhile, taxonomy and classification became the focus of natural historians. [Carl Linnaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Linnaeus "Carl Linnaeus") published a basic [taxonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxonomy_(biology) "Taxonomy (biology)") for the natural world in 1735, and in the 1750s introduced [scientific names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binomial_nomenclature "Binomial nomenclature") for all his species. [Georges-Louis Leclerc, Comte de Buffon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges-Louis_Leclerc,_Comte_de_Buffon "Georges-Louis Leclerc, Comte de Buffon"), treated species as artificial categories and living forms as malleable—even suggesting the possibility of [common descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_descent "Common descent").

Serious evolutionary thinking originated with the works of [Jean-Baptiste Lamarck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Baptiste_Lamarck "Jean-Baptiste Lamarck"), who presented a coherent theory of evolution. The British [naturalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_history "Natural history") [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin "Charles Darwin"), combining the biogeographical approach of [Humboldt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Humboldt "Alexander von Humboldt"), the uniformitarian geology of [Lyell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Lyell "Charles Lyell"), [Malthus's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Malthus "Thomas Malthus") writings on population growth, and his own morphological expertise and extensive natural observations, forged a more successful evolutionary theory based on [natural selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_selection "Natural selection"); similar reasoning and evidence led [Alfred Russel Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Russel_Wallace "Alfred Russel Wallace") to independently reach the same conclusions.

The basis for modern genetics began with the work of [Gregor Mendel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregor_Mendel "Gregor Mendel") in 1865. This outlined the principles of biological inheritance. However, the significance of his work was not realized until the early 20th century when evolution became a unified theory as the [modern synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_synthesis_(20th_century) "Modern synthesis (20th century)") reconciled Darwinian evolution with [classical genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_genetics "Classical genetics"). In the 1940s and early 1950s, a [series of experiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hershey%E2%80%93Chase_experiment "Hershey–Chase experiment") by [Alfred Hershey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Hershey "Alfred Hershey") and [Martha Chase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Chase "Martha Chase") pointed to [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA") as the component of [chromosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromosome "Chromosome") that held the trait-carrying units that had become known as [genes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene "Gene"). A focus on new kinds of model organisms such as [viruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virus "Virus") and bacteria, along with the discovery of the double-helical structure of DNA by [James Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Watson "James Watson") and [Francis Crick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Crick "Francis Crick") in 1953, marked the transition to the era of [molecular genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_genetics "Molecular genetics"). From the 1950s onwards, biology has been vastly extended in the [molecular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_Biology "Molecular Biology") domain. The [genetic code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_code "Genetic code") was cracked by [Har Gobind Khorana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Har_Gobind_Khorana "Har Gobind Khorana"), [Robert W. Holley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_W._Holley "Robert W. Holley") and [Marshall Warren Nirenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Warren_Nirenberg "Marshall Warren Nirenberg") after DNA was understood to contain [codons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codon "Codon"). The [Human Genome Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Genome_Project "Human Genome Project") was launched in 1990 to map the human [genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genome "Genome").

## Fields

### Biochemistry

[Biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry "Biochemistry") is the study of [chemical processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_process "Chemical process") within and relating to living [organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organism "Organism").

### Molecular biology

[Molecular biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_biology "Molecular biology") is the branch of biology that seeks to understand the molecular basis of biological activity in and between cells. It is centered largely on the study of [nucleic acids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleic_acid "Nucleic acid") (such as [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA") and [RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA "RNA")) and [proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein"). It examines the structure, function, and interactions of these [macromolecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macromolecule "Macromolecule") as they orchestrate processes such as [replication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_replication "DNA replication"), [transcription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcription_(biology) "Transcription (biology)"), [translation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translation_(biology) "Translation (biology)"), [protein synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_biosynthesis "Protein biosynthesis"), and complex [biomolecular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomolecule "Biomolecule") interactions.

In 1953, the [Miller–Urey experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller%E2%80%93Urey_experiment "Miller–Urey experiment") showed that organic compounds could be synthesized abiotically within a closed system mimicking the conditions of [early Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Earth "Early Earth"), thus suggesting that complex organic molecules could have arisen spontaneously in early Earth in the process of [abiogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abiogenesis "Abiogenesis").

### Cell biology

[Cell biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_biology "Cell biology") is the branch of biology that studies the structure, function, and behaviour of [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_(biology) "Cell (biology)").

[Bioenergetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioenergetics "Bioenergetics") is a field in [biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry "Biochemistry") and cell biology that concerns [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy "Energy") flow through living systems. This is an active area of biological research that includes the study of the transformation of energy in living organisms and the study of thousands of different [cellular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_(biology) "Cell (biology)") processes such as [cellular respiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_respiration "Cellular respiration") and other [metabolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolism "Metabolism") and [enzymatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzyme "Enzyme") processes that enable the use of energy.

### Genetics

[Genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics "Genetics") is the scientific study of inheritance. [Classical genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_genetics "Classical genetics"), specifically, is the study of how genes and traits are passed on from parents to offspring; its principles are called [Mendelian inheritance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendelian_inheritance "Mendelian inheritance"). A [Punnett square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punnett_square "Punnett square") can be used to predict the results of a test cross. The [chromosome theory of inheritance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boveri%E2%80%93Sutton_chromosome_theory "Boveri–Sutton chromosome theory"), which states that genes are found on chromosomes, was supported by [Thomas Morgans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Hunt_Morgan "Thomas Hunt Morgan")'s experiments with [fruit flies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drosophila_melanogaster "Drosophila melanogaster"), which established the [sex linkage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_linkage "Sex linkage") between eye color and sex in these insects.

### Evolutionary developmental biology

Evolutionary developmental biology compares the [developmental processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developmental_biology "Developmental biology") of different [organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organism "Organism") to infer how developmental processes evolved. The field grew from 19th-century beginnings, where [embryology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryology "Embryology") faced a mystery: [zoologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoology "Zoology") did not know how [embryonic development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryogenesis "Embryogenesis") was controlled at the molecular level. [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin "Charles Darwin") noted that having similar embryos implied common ancestry, but little progress was made until the 1970s. Then, [recombinant DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recombinant_DNA "Recombinant DNA") technology at last brought embryology together with [molecular genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_genetics "Molecular genetics"). A key early discovery was that of [homeotic genes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeotic_gene "Homeotic gene") that regulate development in a wide range of [eukaryotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eukaryote "Eukaryote"). The field explores [deep homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_homology "Deep homology"), the finding that dissimilar organs such as the eyes of [insects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insect "Insect"), [vertebrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebrate "Vertebrate") and [cephalopod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cephalopod "Cephalopod") molluscs, long thought to have evolved separately, are controlled by similar genes from the [evo-devo gene toolkit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evo-devo_gene_toolkit "Evo-devo gene toolkit").

### Evolutionary biology

[Evolutionary biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_biology "Evolutionary biology") is a subfield of biology that analyzes the mechanisms of [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution "Evolution"). Evolution accounts for the unity and [diversity of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diversity_of_life "Diversity of life") on Earth. [Theodosius Dobzhansky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodosius_Dobzhansky "Theodosius Dobzhansky") famously said "nothing in biology makes sense except in the light of evolution". [Population genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_genetics "Population genetics") for example studies how genetic variation develops, how it is inherited, and how the evolutionary mechanisms shape a population's genetic composition. Research in evolutionary biology covers many topics and incorporates ideas from diverse areas, such as [molecular genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_genetics "Molecular genetics") and [mathematical and theoretical biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_and_theoretical_biology "Mathematical and theoretical biology"). Some fields of evolutionary research try to explain phenomena that were poorly accounted for in the modern evolutionary synthesis. These include [speciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speciation "Speciation"), the [evolution of sexual reproduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_of_sexual_reproduction "Evolution of sexual reproduction"), the evolution of [cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-operation_(evolution) "Co-operation (evolution)"), the [evolution of ageing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_of_ageing "Evolution of ageing"), and [evolvability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolvability "Evolvability").

### Ecology

Ecology is the study of the distribution and abundance of life, the interaction between organisms and their [environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_environment "Natural environment").

### Systematics, phylogenetics, and taxonomy

[Systematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systematics "Systematics") is the study of the diversification of living forms, both past and present, and the relationships among living things through time. Relationships are visualized as [evolutionary trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylogenetic_tree "Phylogenetic tree"), studied using [phylogenetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylogenetics "Phylogenetics"), and creating a unified [taxonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxonomy_(biology) "Taxonomy (biology)") of life.

### Conservation biology

Conservation biology is the study of the conservation of Earth's [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity "Biodiversity") with the aim of protecting [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species "Species"), their [habitats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitats "Habitats"), and [ecosystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystems "Ecosystems") from excessive rates of [extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinction "Extinction") and the erosion of biotic interactions. It is concerned with factors that influence the maintenance, loss, and restoration of biodiversity and the science of sustaining evolutionary processes that engender [genetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics "Genetics"), population, species, and ecosystem diversity. The concern stems from estimates suggesting that up to 50% of all species on the planet will disappear within the next 50 years, which has contributed to poverty, starvation, and will reset the course of evolution on this planet. Conservation biologists research the trends of [biodiversity loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_loss "Biodiversity loss"), species extinctions, and the negative effect these are having on our capabilities to [sustain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainability "Sustainability") the well-being of human society.
