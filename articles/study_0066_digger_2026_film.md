# Digger (2026 film)

***Digger*** is a 2026 American [satirical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satire_(film_and_television) "Satire (film and television)") [black comedy film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_comedy_film "Black comedy film") co-written, directed, and co-produced by [Alejandro González Iñárritu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Gonz%C3%A1lez_I%C3%B1%C3%A1rritu "Alejandro González Iñárritu"). [Tom Cruise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cruise "Tom Cruise"), who also produced the film, stars as Digger Rockwell, an aging [oil magnate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_magnate "Business magnate") whose drilling sets off an ecological disaster. The supporting cast includes [Riz Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riz_Ahmed "Riz Ahmed"), [John Goodman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Goodman "John Goodman"), [Sandra Hüller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_H%C3%BCller "Sandra Hüller"), [Michael Stuhlbarg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Stuhlbarg "Michael Stuhlbarg"), and [Jesse Plemons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Plemons "Jesse Plemons").

Iñárritu conceived the idea for *Digger* after working on *[The Revenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Revenant_(2015_film) "The Revenant (2015 film)")* (2015). He wrote the screenplay with [Sabina Berman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabina_Berman "Sabina Berman"), [Alexander Dinelaris Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dinelaris_Jr. "Alexander Dinelaris Jr."), and [Nicolás Giacobone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Giacobone "Nicolás Giacobone"), from a story by Iñárritu, Berman, and [Jez Butterworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jez_Butterworth "Jez Butterworth"). Filming took place from November 2024 to May 2025 in the United Kingdom.

*Digger* premiered in [Leicester Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicester_Square "Leicester Square"), London on September 22, 2026, and [Warner Bros. Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures "Warner Bros. Pictures") released it theatrically in the United States on October 2, 2026. This made it the final film release by Warner Bros. before the [acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery by Paramount Skydance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acquisition_of_Warner_Bros._Discovery_by_Paramount_Skydance "Acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery by Paramount Skydance") on October 6, 2026. The film polarized critics, and has grossed $40.8 million on a production budget of $125–180 million.

## Plot

Digger Rockwell is an aging oil magnate who colludes with U.S. President Compson to allow Digger's company, Rockwell United, to extract oil in [Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska "Alaska") and [Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland "Greenland"). Despite repeated warnings about [methane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methane "Methane") beneath the [Greenland field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category:Petroleum_in_Greenland "Category:Petroleum in Greenland"), Digger proceeds, later learning that a pocket has exploded which dislodged a massive [ice shelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_shelf "Ice shelf") and sent it toward Europe. Environmental Safety Chief Dr. Nirav Ganesh warns that its collision with [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway "Norway") in three days will trigger devastating tidal waves across the [North Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea "North Sea"). Rather than inform the public, Compson and Digger establish an information firewall, gambling that the shelf might drift into the Atlantic Ocean and melt. They also discuss Compson's deteriorating health due to [rectal cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectal_cancer "Rectal cancer").

A prop failure reveals that all the above is actually a [play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Play_(theatre) "Play (theatre)") and Digger is actually Curtis Markson, the lead actor in *Fossil Fools*, a satirical stage play about the tycoon. Everything so far has occurred during the play's [dress rehearsal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dress_rehearsal "Dress rehearsal") before its premiere. Between scenes, Curtis learns that a plane carrying his daughter has disappeared after encountering an [extreme hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_weather "Extreme weather"). Producer Kenny offers to locate her through his private intelligence network.

Continuing the play, the ice shelf strikes Europe, flooding major [Northern European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Europe "Northern Europe") cities. The United States and Rockwell United are summoned before the [European Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Parliament "European Parliament"). Vice President Eddie represents Compson, who is undergoing cancer surgery, and promises American assistance. Digger accepts responsibility for the disaster but argues that neither he nor his company can be blamed for society's dependence on oil, since he has merely fulfilled global demand. His speech provokes the attendees into fighting among themselves rather than confronting him. Later, a drunken Ganesh warns Digger that an even larger ice shelf has broken away from a Russian glacier and is heading toward Alaska.

Digger and Ganesh inform the sickly Compson, who remains heavily drugged and deluded. Compson proposes launching nuclear weapons at China as a distraction, but [Military Chief of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_staff "Chief of staff") Fitzgerald recommends targeting the ice shelf instead. Ganesh warns that the strike will fragment the shelf without slowing it down. Nevertheless, Compson authorizes the attack, and Ganesh's prediction proves correct.

Meanwhile, Curtis grows increasingly troubled by the play's excesses and eventually breaks down on stage. He accuses the production of distorting real events and trivializing the suffering of generations harmed by those responsible. Kenny reveals that he is the real Digger's nephew and has purchased the rights to *Fossil Fools* in the hopes the play will help him overcome his bitterness toward his uncle while giving the public a constructive way to process its collective grief. Understanding the significance of his performance, Curtis completes the rehearsal.

Following the ice shelf disaster, Digger and his family are [exiled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exile "Exile") to a converted [offshore platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offshore_platform "Offshore platform"). Five years later, Eddie has succeeded Compson as president after his death, withdrawing all funding and military protection from the platform, which prompts most of Digger's employees to abandon him and steal his food, gold, and boats. Increasingly delusional, Digger attempts to bully God into providing food for the remaining occupants. When this fails, he scatters the ashes of his cat, Maggie, shares a brief moment with Kenny (named Kyle in the play), and commits suicide by jumping into the polluted ocean.

The audience watching the play cheers Digger's death and Curtis's performance. Curtis's wife arrives to congratulate him and reveals that their daughter has been found safe, thanks to Kenny. Theater management then announces a "Level 4" condition outside. Audience members put on manufactured breathing masks and leave the theater, emerging into a flooded, devastated city patrolled by soldiers, robots, and drones.

## Production

### Development

Iñárritu first conceived *Digger* after working on *[The Revenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Revenant_(2015_film) "The Revenant (2015 film)")* (2015):

> I had an idea. Not a script, not a film, just a relentless, recurring obsession that has endured through all these years. I knew who this character was. I knew how he spoke, how he survived, how he seduced reality into agreeing with him. But it took me 10 years to do this film, because I wasn't looking for a story. I was looking for the right way of saying it.

Iñárritu developed *Digger* at [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix")—then under the title *Blah, Blah, Blah*—with [Leonardo DiCaprio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_DiCaprio "Leonardo DiCaprio") in talks to star, but Netflix scrapped the project because it was too similar to another of its films, *[Don't Look Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don%27t_Look_Up "Don't Look Up")*.

In 2019, he began discussing the project with [Tom Cruise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cruise "Tom Cruise") while filming *[Top Gun: Maverick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Gun:_Maverick "Top Gun: Maverick")* (2022). Cruise was a longtime fan, having seen Iñárritu's directorial debut *[Amores perros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amores_perros "Amores perros")* (2000) in its initial release. After turning his attention to his passion project, *[Bardo, False Chronicle of a Handful of Truths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bardo,_False_Chronicle_of_a_Handful_of_Truths "Bardo, False Chronicle of a Handful of Truths")* (2022), Iñárritu returned to developing *Digger*.

Iñárritu, [Sabina Berman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabina_Berman "Sabina Berman"), and *[Birdman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birdman_(film) "Birdman (film)")* (2014) writers [Nicolas Giacobone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Giacobone "Nicolas Giacobone") and [Alexander Dinelaris Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dinelaris_Jr. "Alexander Dinelaris Jr.") co-wrote *Digger* and finished its screenplay in 2023. Iñárritu called Cruise's 10-minute monologue the film's "spine", telling *[Mid-Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Day "Mid-Day")* there were "maybe 70 different versions of the monologue as we wrote the script over nine years". In writing *Digger*, Iñárritu said he was "not interested in pointing fingers or reducing the film to a political commentary or a right or left thing" while singling out Cruise's monologue, adding "we are all part of this madness. That there are no good people or bad people. The system has trapped all of us, and we have to find a way out together."

### Casting

The project was announced and Cruise officially cast in the lead role in February 2024, after he attended a meeting with Iñárritu, following his strategic partnership with [Warner Bros. Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures "Warner Bros. Pictures"), with [Legendary Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legendary_Pictures "Legendary Pictures") to produce the film. In August 2024, [Sandra Hüller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_H%C3%BCller "Sandra Hüller"), [John Goodman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Goodman "John Goodman"), [Michael Stuhlbarg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Stuhlbarg "Michael Stuhlbarg"), [Jesse Plemons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Plemons "Jesse Plemons") and [Sophie Wilde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophie_Wilde "Sophie Wilde") were added to the cast, with [Riz Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riz_Ahmed "Riz Ahmed") in negotiations to also join. In early 2025, [Emma D'Arcy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_D%27Arcy "Emma D'Arcy"), [Robert John Burke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_John_Burke "Robert John Burke") and [Burn Gorman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burn_Gorman "Burn Gorman") were revealed to have joined the cast. Indian actor [Fahadh Faasil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahadh_Faasil "Fahadh Faasil") was originally considered for the role of Ganesh, but backed out and was replaced by Ahmed due to [per diem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_diem "Per diem") disagreements.

### Filming

[Principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography "Principal photography") began in the United Kingdom on November 7, 2024, taking place at [Pinewood Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinewood_Studios "Pinewood Studios"), with [Emmanuel Lubezki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Lubezki "Emmanuel Lubezki") serving as the cinematographer, and was planned to be concluded by late April 2025. Filming was halted for two days in March 2025 while Goodman underwent hip surgery, during which he was hospitalized. The film was shot on [35 mm film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/35_mm_movie_film "35 mm movie film") using [VistaVision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VistaVision "VistaVision") equipped with Leitz Cine ELSIE lenses.

The original process of applying prosthetics to Cruise to play the character of Digger Rockwell took six hours, which Cruise protested, claiming it would not work with his schedule. Cruise brought a chalkboard and physically timed how long it took each prosthetic to be applied to deduce the fastest time possible, recounting that "I had a guy there with a stopwatch and I'm like, 'OK, you see that time, six hours? By the time we're done, we're going to get it under an hour". The application process was eventually pared down to under an hour.

Production wrapped on May 3, 2025, after six months of filming under the [working title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_title "Working title") *Judy*. Later that month, Iñárritu confirmed in an interview that the film would be a comedy.

[Puck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puck_(media_company) "Puck (media company)") reported that the film had been greenlit with a $125 million production budget, as a part of Warner Bros. chiefs [Michael De Luca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_De_Luca "Michael De Luca") and [Pamela Abdy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamela_Abdy "Pamela Abdy")'s strategy supporting [auteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auteur "Auteur") driven productions. Industry insiders claimed that, due to production delays, the initial budget ballooned the final cost to $160 million, with some sources listing the figure as high as $180 million. [*Variety*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)") and *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")* reported that studio representatives denied these larger figures. According to Warner Bros. executives, it kept to its $125 million budget.

### Post-production

In December 2025, the title was officially revealed as *Digger* alongside a 50-second teaser with the song "[O Green World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_Green_World "O Green World")" by [Gorillaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorillaz "Gorillaz"). [Industrial Light & Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Light_%26_Magic "Industrial Light & Magic") and [Legacy Effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legacy_Effects "Legacy Effects") provided the visual effects.

## Release

*Digger* had a [Royal Film Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Film_Performance "Royal Film Performance") screening at London's [Leicester Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicester_Square "Leicester Square") on September 22, 2026. It received an international theatrical release on September 30, 2026. The Mexican release occurred on October 1, 2026. It was released in the United States and the United Kingdom on October 2, 2026.

*Digger* was released in [Dolby Cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolby_Cinema "Dolby Cinema"), [IMAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAX "IMAX"), and other premium large formats (PLFs). The film's IMAX release concluded the initial run of *[The Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Odyssey_(2026_film) "The Odyssey (2026 film)")* in the [IMAX 70 mm film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/70_mm_film "70 mm film") format after 78 days. In addition, the film received VistaVision screenings. The film is released in a variable aspect ratio expanding horizontally to [1.85:1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1.85:1 "1.85:1") Flat for standard or non-IMAX PLFs or expanding vertically in either 1.43:1 for [IMAX GT with Laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAX_with_Laser "IMAX with Laser") or [1.5:1 (3:2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3:2_aspect_ratio "3:2 aspect ratio") in native VistaVision presentations. It is the final film release from Warner Bros. Pictures under [Warner Bros. Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Discovery "Warner Bros. Discovery") ownership before becoming part of the [Skydance Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydance_Corporation "Skydance Corporation") as part of their [merger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acquisition_of_Warner_Bros._Discovery_by_Paramount_Skydance "Acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery by Paramount Skydance") on October 6.

### Marketing

On April 14, 2026, Cruise and Iñárritu presented new footage during the Warner Bros. Pictures panel at [CinemaCon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_United "Cinema United") 2026. *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")* called the footage among the best shown at that year's CinemaCon. Thirty seconds of footage from the film, set to [Dire Straits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dire_Straits "Dire Straits")'s "[Money for Nothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money_for_Nothing "Money for Nothing")", were shown after a retrospective of Cruise's film career over the years before screenings of the re-releases of Cruise's films *[Top Gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Gun "Top Gun")* (1986) and *[Top Gun: Maverick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Gun:_Maverick "Top Gun: Maverick")* (2022) starting on May 13; the trailer was eventually released online a month later on June 23, followed by three teaser posters, with a full trailer, set to [Talking Heads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talking_Heads "Talking Heads")'s "[Burning Down the House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burning_Down_the_House "Burning Down the House")", released on July 13.

Iñárritu said he planned for there to be a misdirection in the film's marketing since its inception, stating "I want to make sure people are surprised when they see the film ... [which is] why our trailers were like, 'What is this about?' Well, we cannot say anything more."

After *Digger* received mixed reviews before its theatrical launch, marketing shifted to addressing negative reception and encouraging audiences to decide for themselves, with clips featuring review quotes from critics accompanied by a snippet of Cruise's character from the film proclaiming "You know what? Fuck those people!" The tactic was compared to the marketing strategy employed by [Francis Ford Coppola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Ford_Coppola "Francis Ford Coppola")'s 2024 film *[Megalopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalopolis_(film) "Megalopolis (film)")*, which had similarly mixed reception.

## Reception

### Box office

As of October 14, 2026[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Digger_(2026_film)&action=edit), *Digger* has grossed $16 million in the United States and Canada, and $24 million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $40 million. Due to its large production and marketing budget, *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* reported the film would need to gross $350 million in order to [break even](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Break_even "Break even"), although a Warner Bros. spokesman put the break-even figure at $300 million. Following the film's $20 million worldwide opening, it was estimated that the film would lose $150–250 million on its theatrical run, and even after taking into account projected future income from digital sales and streaming, profitability would take longer due to Cruise's [first-dollar gross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-dollar_gross "First-dollar gross") deal.

In the United States and Canada, the film earned $3.4 million on its first day, including $1.2 million from Thursday night previews. It went on to take $8 million over its opening weekend, finishing in fifth at the box office. The low opening weekend gross was partially blamed on the film's trailers, which *[Deadline Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadline_Hollywood "Deadline Hollywood")* said confused audiences and made them unsure of the film's intended tone and plot. In its second weekend the film made $3.2 million (a drop of 60%), falling to eighth place.

The film received polarized reviews from critics, which some news outlets also described as split down the middle. On the [review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator "Review aggregator") website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes"), 53% of 328 critics' reviews are positive, with an average rating of 6.1/10. The website's consensus reads: "A stylized satire whose audacious design proves too mannered for some of the laughs to smoothly land, *Digger* is a bold swing of the shovel by Tom Cruise and Alejandro González Iñárritu that misses as often as it strikes gold." [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), which uses a [weighted average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_arithmetic_mean "Weighted arithmetic mean"), assigned the film a score of 46 out of 100, based on 60 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews. Audiences polled by [CinemaScore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CinemaScore "CinemaScore") gave the film an average grade of "C+" on an A+ to F scale, while 47% of those surveyed by [PostTrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PostTrak "PostTrak") said they would definitely recommend the film. *[Ars Technica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ars_Technica "Ars Technica")* stated that "some appreciat[ed] Iñárritu's bold, creative swings and others [found] the film practically unwatchable".

[Manohla Dargis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manohla_Dargis "Manohla Dargis") for *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* panned the film, calling it a "lumbering white elephant of a movie that almost makes you feel sorry for everyone involved" while offering tepid criticism of Cruise's performance, writing that "all of Cruise's strenuous energy helps weigh down an already ponderous movie". Robert Daniels of [RogerEbert.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RogerEbert.com "RogerEbert.com") gave the film two out of four stars, stating that "it's excruciatingly painful that this languid, self-satisfied film doesn't work". [Bilge Ebiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilge_Ebiri "Bilge Ebiri") of *[Vulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulture_(website) "Vulture (website)")* described *Digger* as "a torturous experience, sometimes purposefully so, often unintentionally so". [Peter Bradshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bradshaw "Peter Bradshaw") of *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* rated the film two out of five stars, declaring it "an exasperatingly shallow, laborious, self-congratulatory satire with a trick up its sleeve, featuring very good actors giving not very good performances".

*[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")*'s [Owen Gleiberman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Gleiberman "Owen Gleiberman") wrote that, despite agreeing with the message of *Digger*, "[it] is practically guilt-tripping its audience", adding "I'm not sure that *Digger* is going to connect, because it's so full of itself—so top-heavy with importance." [David Ehrlich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ehrlich "David Ehrlich") of *[IndieWire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndieWire "IndieWire")* gave the film a C+ rating, writing that the film is "so detached from human truth that its most believable character is a one-eyed animatronic cat named Maggie". Meanwhile, *IndieWire* reviewer [Jim Hemphill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Hemphill "Jim Hemphill") called it "by a country mile, the best movie of the year, an exuberant, maximalist comedy with more visual invention and conceptual ambition than any studio film since *[One Battle After Another](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Battle_After_Another "One Battle After Another")*." Reviewing for the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")*, [Amy Nicholson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Nicholson "Amy Nicholson") considered Digger Rockwell to be Cruise's "riskiest role in eons" and wrote, "while I don't entirely love this strange comedy, I'm giddy that it's lurched into existence."

For the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC"), Nicholas Barber praised *Digger* and Cruise's performance and commended Iñárritu's direction, but suggested Cruise's inclusion inadvertently acts as a hindrance in attracting audiences: "If this art-house extravaganza had featured an actor known for left-field indie films, it would have been lauded as a daring experiment. But as it features the actor at the very top of [Hollywood's A-list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_A-list "Hollywood A-list"), many viewers will wonder what he was thinking." [Robbie Collin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Collin "Robbie Collin") of *[The Daily Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph "The Daily Telegraph")* rated the film four out of five stars, billing it as "a serpentine, swaggering satire", "a *[Dr. Strangelove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Strangelove "Dr. Strangelove")* for the climate crisis". [Stephanie Zacharek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephanie_Zacharek "Stephanie Zacharek") of *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")* called *Digger* an "aggressively unfunny, boneheaded farce". In a four-out-of-four review, Randy Myers of *[The Mercury News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mercury_News "The Mercury News")* wrote, "What should be talked about is Cruise's spectacle of a performance. He's better here than even in his great turn in [Paul Thomas Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Thomas_Anderson "Paul Thomas Anderson")'s *[Magnolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnolia_(film) "Magnolia (film)")*. That is saying a lot." Author [Jayson Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayson_Greene "Jayson Greene") wrote that "*Digger* may be the best climate crisis movie ever made, cleverly disguised as the worst", calling it "such an accurate portrayal of the necessity of bad art that it's very easy to mistake it for bad art".

Tim Grierson of *[Screen International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_International "Screen International")* called *Digger* an "ambitious misfire", "consistently sabotaged by Iñárritu's gratingly unsubtle comedic approach", adding, "despite the endless actorly fireworks, there's a terminal hollowness at [Digger Rockwell's] centre". Placing himself in the camp of those "who most definitely did not dig" the film, David Rooney of *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")* wrote that it "epitomizes the difference between big swing and hubristic folly more than any film since [Francis Ford Coppola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Ford_Coppola "Francis Ford Coppola")'s *[Megalopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalopolis_(film) "Megalopolis (film)")*".
