# Game

A **game** is an activity defined by a challenge and a set of rules, typically done for enjoyment, competition, or development. Games may be played informally or in professional competitive settings before audiences. Popular formats include [board games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_game "Board game"), [card games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Card_game "Card game"), [video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game "Video game"), and [sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport "Sport"), distinguished from unstructured play by having rules. Scholars have long debated the essence of games, with no single definition achieving universal acceptance.

Games are a universal feature of human culture. The [Royal Game of Ur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Game_of_Ur "Royal Game of Ur") and [Senet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senet "Senet") are among the oldest known games, dating back to at least 2600 BCE. Throughout history, games have served as tools for entertainment, teaching, ritual, and socialization. In the modern era, the global video game industry has grown to surpass both film and music in annual revenue, reflecting how central games have become to contemporary life.

Philosophically, games have attracted attention as a test case for the nature of rules and meaning. Psychologists link play to cognitive development in children and to the intense, satisfying concentration called [flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_(psychology) "Flow (psychology)") in adults. Game theory uses the structure of games to model decision making in fields such as [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics "Economics") and [evolutionary biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_biology "Evolutionary biology").

## Definitions

### Ludwig Wittgenstein

[Ludwig Wittgenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Wittgenstein "Ludwig Wittgenstein") is well known in the history of philosophy for having addressed the definition of the word *game*. In his *[Philosophical Investigations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical_Investigations "Philosophical Investigations")*, Wittgenstein argued that the elements of games, such as [play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Play_(activity) "Play (activity)"), rules, and competition, all fail to adequately define what games are. From this, Wittgenstein concluded that people apply the term *game* to a range of disparate human activities that bear to one another only what one might call [family resemblances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_resemblance "Family resemblance").

### Bernard Suits

[Bernard Suits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Suits "Bernard Suits") offers this definition of games in *Grasshopper: Games, Life and Utopia* (1978). "To play a game is to engage in activity directed towards bringing about a specific state of affairs, using only means permitted by rules, where the rules prohibit more efficient in favour of less efficient means, and where such rules are accepted just because they make possible such activity." He calls players' state of mind [lusory attitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusory_attitude "Lusory attitude"), accepting unnecessary obstacles in order to play.

### Roger Caillois

French sociologist [Roger Caillois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Caillois "Roger Caillois"), in his book *Les jeux et les hommes (Games and Men)*(1961), defined a game as an activity that must have the following characteristics:

* *fun*: the activity is chosen for its light-hearted character
* *separate*: it is circumscribed in time and place
* *uncertain*: the outcome of the activity is unforeseeable
* *non-productive*: participation does not accomplish anything useful
* *governed by rules*: the activity has rules that are different from everyday life
* *fictitious*: it is accompanied by the awareness of a different reality

### Other definitions

* "A game is a form of art in which participants, termed *players*, make decisions in order to manage resources through game tokens in the pursuit of a goal." ([Greg Costikyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Costikyan "Greg Costikyan")) According to this definition, some "games" that do not involve choices, such as [Chutes and Ladders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chutes_and_Ladders "Chutes and Ladders"), [Candy Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candy_Land "Candy Land"), and [War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_(card_game) "War (card game)") are not technically games any more than a slot machine is.
* "A game is a form of play with goals and structure." (Kevin J. Maroney)
* "A game is a system in which players engage in an artificial conflict, defined by rules, that results in a quantifiable outcome." ([Katie Salen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Salen "Katie Salen") and [Eric Zimmerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Zimmerman "Eric Zimmerman"))
* "A game is an *activity* among two or more independent *decision-makers* seeking to achieve their *objectives* in some *limiting context.*" ([Clark C. Abt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_C._Abt "Clark C. Abt"))
* "At its most elementary level then we can define game as an exercise of voluntary control systems in which there is an opposition between forces, confined by a procedure and rules in order to produce a disequilibrial outcome." (Elliot Avedon and [Brian Sutton-Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Sutton-Smith "Brian Sutton-Smith"))
* "When you strip away the genre differences and the technological complexities, all games share four defining traits: a goal, rules, a feedback system, and voluntary participation." ([Jane McGonigal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_McGonigal "Jane McGonigal"))

## History

The history of games goes back millennia. The [Royal Game of Ur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Game_of_Ur "Royal Game of Ur"), from ancient [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia "Mesopotamia") in roughly 2600 BCE, is among the oldest known [board games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_game "Board game"). A [cuneiform tablet](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cuneiform_tablet&action=edit&redlink=1 "Cuneiform tablet (page does not exist)") from the 2nd century BCE contained its rules, representing the earliest recorded game design. [Senet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senet "Senet"), a board game played in [ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt "Ancient Egypt"), was important enough to be portrayed in tomb artwork with fragments of the board found from approximately 3100 BCE. A game board inscribed for [Amenhotep III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amenhotep_III "Amenhotep III") (c. 1390 – c. 1353 BCE) survives intact. Both were race games and precursors to [backgammon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backgammon "Backgammon"). Senet had religious significance with the board representing the pathway through the [netherworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duat "Duat").

In East Asia, the board game [Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_(game) "Go (game)") has written accounts as early as the [Spring and Autumn period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_and_Autumn_period "Spring and Autumn period"). Go's strategic complexity resisted computation until deep learning advances in [AlphaGo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AlphaGo "AlphaGo"). Other ancient games include [mancala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mancala "Mancala"), a count-and-capture game with Neolithic versions found in Jordan from approximately 6900 BCE.

Chess traces back to the [Gupta Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire "Gupta Empire") of India around the 6th century CE with [chaturanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaturanga "Chaturanga") a likely ancestor. It spread through Persia as [chatrang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shatranj "Shatranj"), and entered Europe via the [Moorish Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus "Al-Andalus"), approaching its modern form by the 15th century.

[Playing cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playing_card "Playing card") may date to [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty "Tang dynasty") China (618–907 CE), reaching Europe via the [Mamluk Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Sultanate "Mamluk Sultanate") in the 14th century. With mass production, standardized regional decks eventually evolved.

[Pachisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pachisi "Pachisi"), played in the [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire "Mughal Empire") court, served as the basis for [Parcheesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parcheesi "Parcheesi") and [Ludo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludo "Ludo"). The [Aztecs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztecs "Aztecs") in Central America played a race game called [patolli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patolli "Patolli").

In the 20th century, developments included the rise of mass-market [tabletop games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabletop_game "Tabletop game") such as *[Monopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopoly_(game) "Monopoly (game)")*, *[Scrabble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrabble "Scrabble")*, *[Clue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluedo "Cluedo")*, and *[Trivial Pursuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_Pursuit "Trivial Pursuit")* and the emergence of [video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game "Video game"). *[Pong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pong "Pong")*, the first commercially successful arcade video game appeared in 1972. In the 21st century, the global video game industry had grown to surpass film and music in annual revenue.

## Gameplay elements and classification

Games can be characterized by "what the player does". This is often referred to as [gameplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gameplay "Gameplay"). Major key elements identified in this context are tools and rules that define the overall context of game.

### Tools

Games are often [classified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_classification "Game classification") by the components required to play them. Common categories include tabletop games (using boards, cards, tokens, dice, or other items), card games (using a [standard deck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_52-card_deck "Standard 52-card deck") or game-specific cards), dice games, video games (using electronic hardware), and games with no equipment like [hide-and-seek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hide-and-seek "Hide-and-seek").

Game tokens are the means used to manage resources and achieve goals. Pieces in a board game, cards in a card game, and characters in a role-playing game are tokens.

The ball has been a popular game piece for millennia, resulting in a worldwide popularity of ball games such as [rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_football "Rugby football"), [basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball "Basketball"), [soccer (football)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer_(football) "Soccer (football)"), [cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket "Cricket"), [tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis "Tennis"), and [volleyball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volleyball "Volleyball"). Other tools are more idiosyncratic to a certain region. Playing cards in Europe had decks with suits that varied from country to country before standardization. Other games, such as [chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess "Chess"), may be traced primarily through the development and evolution of their game pieces.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Rules and aims

Game structures include boundaries, rules, goals, and scoring systems.

Game rules specify how actions are allowed to work. The rules of a game may be distinguished from its aims. For most competitive games, the *ultimate* aim is winning: in this sense, checkmate is the aim of chess. Common win conditions are being the first to amass a certain quota of points or tokens (as in [Settlers of Catan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settlers_of_Catan "Settlers of Catan")), having the greatest number of tokens at the end of the game (as in [Monopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopoly_(game) "Monopoly (game)")), or some relationship of one's game tokens to those of one's opponent (as in chess's [checkmate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checkmate "Checkmate")). Intermediate goals can provide motivation when long-term goals may be far off.

An aim identifies a [sufficient condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufficient_condition "Sufficient condition") for successful action, whereas the rule identifies a [necessary condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necessary_condition "Necessary condition") for permissible action. For example, the aim of chess is to checkmate, but although it is expected that players will *try* to checkmate each other, it is not a rule of chess that a player *must* checkmate the other player whenever possible. Some games include rules about changing rules. In [Nomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomic "Nomic"), invented by philosopher [Peter Suber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Suber "Peter Suber"), players' turns include proposing rule changes and voting upon them.

### Skill, strategy, and chance

Games are commonly classified along the chance/skill spectrum. Winning in chess is based entirely on skill, while in [Candy Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candy_Land "Candy Land") it is based entirely on luck. Most games are in between. [Backgammon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backgammon "Backgammon") combines strategy with the chance of dice roll. Settlers of Catan offers several paths to victory, and players may switch strategies, increasing their perceived autonomy.

## Types

Games can take a variety of forms, from competitive [sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport "Sport") to [board games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_game "Board game") and video games.

### Sports

Sports are competitive games involving physical skill with accepted rules. Many sports require special equipment and dedicated playing fields, leading to the involvement of a community much larger than the group of players. A city or town may set aside such resources for the organization of sports leagues. [Association football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football "Association football") (soccer) is the world's most popular sport by participation, with 250 million players in over 200 countries.

[Spectator sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectator_sport "Spectator sport") have been part of human culture for thousands of years. Local and national sports teams are arenas for group identity, promoting civic pride in the [home team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_(sports) "Home (sports)") in municipal rivalries.

[Lawn games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawn_game "Lawn game") spread in the late 19th century as outdoor recreation. [Croquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croquet "Croquet"), [bocce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bocce "Bocce"), and [boules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boules "Boules") provide leisurely competition and social interaction.

### Tabletop games

Tabletop games are designed to be played on a table, including board games, card games, and dice games.

#### Board games

Board games use as a central tool a board on which location, interactions, and choices are tracked. [Abstract strategy games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_strategy_game "Abstract strategy game"), such as [chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess "Chess") and [Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_(game) "Go (game)"), are entirely determined by player moves not chance. [Race games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_game "Race game") such as Parcheesi and backgammon incorporate dice rolls, while Settlers of Catan involves [resource management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_mechanics#Resource_management "Game mechanics"), trading, and negotiating with other players.

Board games have grown more dynamic in the 21st century, with [Eurogames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurogame "Eurogame") emphasizing efficient resource management over direct conflict. Tens of thousands of tabletop games have launched on [crowdfunding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowdfunding "Crowdfunding") platform [Kickstarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickstarter "Kickstarter"), with projects raising funds to pay for a game's production.

#### Card games

Card games use a deck of cards as their central tool. [Standard 52-card decks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_52-card_deck "Standard 52-card deck") support many varieties of games including [trick-taking games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trick-taking_game "Trick-taking game") such as [bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contract_bridge "Contract bridge") and [whist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whist "Whist"). [Uno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uno_(card_game) "Uno (card game)") was originally played with standard decks and has since been commercialized with customized decks. Variations of the standard deck exist across the world such as "French-suited" 32-card decks, tarot-derived 78-card decks, and Italian 40-card decks.

[Collectible card games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectible_card_game "Collectible card game") were pioneered by [Magic: The Gathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering "Magic: The Gathering") in 1993, where players form decks and compete against others. The market has grown to $1.5 billion in 2021. Collectible card game booster packs are random, while [living card game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expandable_card_game "Expandable card game") pack contents are known.

#### Dice games

Dice games use [randomized dice rolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_number_generation "Random number generation") as their central element. Examples include [Yahtzee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahtzee "Yahtzee"), [Farkle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farkle "Farkle"), and [craps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craps "Craps"). [Dominoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominoes "Dominoes"), while [tile-based](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tile-based_game "Tile-based game") rather than using dice, also uses draw-and-play mechanics.

### Video games

Video games use electronic devices. Commercial video games were first available in the 1970s, though earlier games included [Tennis for Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_for_Two "Tennis for Two") and [Spacewar!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacewar! "Spacewar!"). Video games are interactive and may have a game engine simulating complex physics and environments. [Genres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_genre "Video game genre") include [action/shooter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooter_game "Shooter game"), [fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighting_game "Fighting game"), and [platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platformer "Platformer") games.

[Massively multiplayer online role-playing games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massively_multiplayer_online_role-playing_game "Massively multiplayer online role-playing game") can support thousands of players simultaneously within a virtual world. As of 2013[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Game&action=edit), the MMORPG *[World of Warcraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_of_Warcraft "World of Warcraft")* had 36% market share. [Esports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esports "Esports") give away millions of dollars in prize money while millions of viewers watch across the world.

### Role-playing games

In [role-playing games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Role-playing_game "Role-playing game") (RPGs), players enact fictional characters and collaboratively shape a narrative. [Gary Gygax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Gygax "Gary Gygax") and [Dave Arneson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Arneson "Dave Arneson") created the first RPG [Dungeons & Dragons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungeons_%26_Dragons "Dungeons & Dragons") in 1974. Players make choices for their characters, with a dungeon master adjudicating things the rules don't cover.

[Video game RPGs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Role-playing_video_game "Role-playing video game") tend to run characters through a predetermined story line. Most [Final Fantasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_Fantasy "Final Fantasy") games present a fantasy/science fiction narrative with [hero's journey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero%27s_journey "Hero's journey"), while [Dark Souls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Souls "Dark Souls") is known for difficult action sequences.

## Games and society

### Psychology of play

Psychologists have long recognized play as a fundamental developmental activity. [Jean Piaget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Piaget "Jean Piaget") described play as a way to experiment with different cognitive schemas and assimilate experience. [Lev Vygotsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Vygotsky "Lev Vygotsky") emphasized play's effectiveness in communicative, narrative, and representational competence.

In adults, games serve a range of psychological functions. [Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihaly_Csikszentmihalyi "Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi") called [flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_(psychology) "Flow (psychology)") a state of intense concentration and total immersion which boosts life satisfaction. To help players achieve flow, games offer clear goals, immediate feedback, and balance challenge with skill.

Research into problematic gaming has grown alongside the video game industry. In 2019, the [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization "World Health Organization") designated [gaming disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_addiction "Video game addiction") as a mental disorder, with effects such as health problems, [sleep loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleep_deprivation "Sleep deprivation"), and [social isolation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_isolation "Social isolation").

### Games as education

[Serious games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serious_game "Serious game") are designed for training, education, or health care rather than entertainment. [Flight simulators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_simulator "Flight simulator") are used for pilot training, [mission rehearsal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wargame "Wargame") for military planners, and [laparoscopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laparoscopy "Laparoscopy") equipment simulators for surgery.

In formal education, [game-based learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_game#Game-based_learning "Educational game") has received extensive research. Well-designed educational games can improve knowledge retention and deepen understanding.

### Game theory

[John Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Forbes_Nash "John Forbes Nash") proved that games with several players have a stable solution provided that coalitions between players are disallowed. Nash won the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") for economics for this important result which extended [von Neumann's theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimax "Minimax") of [zero-sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-sum "Zero-sum") games. Nash's stable solution is known as the [Nash equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nash_equilibrium "Nash equilibrium").

If cooperation between players is allowed, then the game becomes more complex; many concepts have been developed to analyze such games. [Cooperative game theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative_game_theory "Cooperative game theory") uses coalition formation, joint actions, and payoffs for analysis.

In [quantum game theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_game_theory "Quantum game theory"), it has been found that the introduction of [quantum information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_information "Quantum information") into multiplayer games allows a new type of equilibrium strategy not found in traditional games. The [entanglement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_entanglement "Quantum entanglement") of player's choices can have the effect of a contract by preventing players from profiting from what is known as *betrayal*. Games with many independent players are difficult to analyze formally using [game theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_theory "Game theory") as the players may form and switch [coalitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition "Coalition").
