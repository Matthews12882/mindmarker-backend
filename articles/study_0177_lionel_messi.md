# Lionel Messi

**Lionel Andrés** "**Leo**" **Messi Cuccittini** (born 24 June 1987) is an Argentine professional [footballer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football "Association football") who plays as a [forward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_(association_football) "Forward (association football)") for and [captains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_(association_football) "Captain (association football)") the [Major League Soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Soccer "Major League Soccer") club [Inter Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Miami_CF "Inter Miami CF"). Widely regarded as one of the greatest players in history, he played for the [Argentina national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team "Argentina national football team") for 21 years, retiring in 2026. He has set numerous records for [individual accolades won](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_career_achievements_by_Lionel_Messi#Individual_honours "List of career achievements by Lionel Messi") throughout [his professional footballing career](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Career_of_Lionel_Messi "Career of Lionel Messi"), including eight [Ballons d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballons_d%27Or "Ballons d'Or"), six [European Golden Shoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Golden_Shoe "European Golden Shoe"), and being named the [world's best player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Best_FIFA_Men%27s_Player#FIFA_Player_of_the_Year_(including_predecessors) "The Best FIFA Men's Player") by [FIFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA "FIFA") eight times. He was named the [Laureus World Sportsman of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laureus_World_Sports_Award_for_Sportsman_of_the_Year "Laureus World Sports Award for Sportsman of the Year") in 2020 and 2023, and was named the [All Time Men's World Best Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFFHS_World%27s_Best_Player#Men's_Historical_All-time_World_Best_Players "IFFHS World's Best Player") by the [IFFHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFFHS "IFFHS") in 2025. He was among *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")*'s [100 most influential people in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100 "Time 100") in 2011, 2012, and 2023.

Messi is the most decorated player in the history of professional football, having won [47 team trophies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_career_achievements_by_Lionel_Messi#Collective_awards "List of career achievements by Lionel Messi") across [more than 1,200 career appearances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_men%27s_footballers_with_1,000_or_more_official_appearances "List of men's footballers with 1,000 or more official appearances"). He has scored [over 930 senior career goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_footballers_with_500_or_more_goals "List of footballers with 500 or more goals") and provided [over 420 assists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assist_(association_football)#Players_with_most_assists_in_all_competitions_(all-time) "Assist (association football)") for club and country, making him the second-highest goalscorer and the highest assist provider in the history of football. He was ranked as the [world's highest-paid athlete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_list_of_the_world%27s_highest-paid_athletes "Forbes list of the world's highest-paid athletes") by *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes "Forbes")* in 2019 and 2022. In 2020, he became the second footballer to surpass $1 billion in career earnings.

In 2004, Messi made his competitive debut for the [La Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Liga "La Liga") club [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Barcelona "FC Barcelona") at age 17. He was awarded the [FIFPRO Young Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFPRO#FIFPRO_Young_Player_of_the_Year_(2005–2008) "FIFPRO") in 2006, 2007 and 2008. During the [2008–09 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_FC_Barcelona_season "2008–09 FC Barcelona season"), he helped Barcelona achieve the first [treble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treble_(association_football) "Treble (association football)") in Spanish football, earning him the [first](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Ballon_d%27Or "2009 Ballon d'Or") of four consecutive Ballons d'Or. By the [2011–12 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_FC_Barcelona_season "2011–12 FC Barcelona season") he had set the European record for most goals in a season and was [Barcelona's all-time top scorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_FC_Barcelona_records_and_statistics#Top_goalscorers "List of FC Barcelona records and statistics"). During the [2014–15 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_FC_Barcelona_season "2014–15 FC Barcelona season"), Messi formed the successful attacking trio *[MSN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSN_(association_football) "MSN (association football)")* with [Luis Suárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Su%C3%A1rez_(Uruguayan_footballer) "Luis Suárez (Uruguayan footballer)") and [Neymar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neymar "Neymar"), helping Barcelona achieve a historic second treble and subsequently earning his [fifth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Ballon_d%27Or "2015 Ballon d'Or") Ballon d'Or.

Messi was named Barcelona's captain in 2018. While playing for the club, he won a [club-record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_FC_Barcelona_records_and_statistics#Other_individual_records_for_the_club "List of FC Barcelona records and statistics") 34 trophies, including 10 La Liga titles and four [UEFA Champions Leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League "UEFA Champions League"). With Barcelona, he set records for most goals for a single club (672) and most goals in La Liga (474). Financial difficulties at Barcelona forced Messi to depart in 2021 and sign with [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1 "Ligue 1") club [Paris Saint-Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain "Paris Saint-Germain"), where he won the Ligue 1 title twice. He joined the MLS club Inter Miami in 2023 and led them to their first [MLS Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS_Cup "MLS Cup") victory in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS_Cup_2025 "MLS Cup 2025"), while also winning the [league MVP award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landon_Donovan_MVP_Award "Landon Donovan MVP Award") in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Major_League_Soccer_season "2024 Major League Soccer season") and [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Major_League_Soccer_season "2025 Major League Soccer season"). Following his arrival and impact on football in the United States, Messi was named *Time*'s [Athlete of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Person_of_the_Year#Athlete_of_the_Year "Time Person of the Year") in 2023 and was awarded the [Presidential Medal of Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Medal_of_Freedom "Presidential Medal of Freedom") by US president [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden") in 2025.

Messi is the [all-time leading goalscorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team#Top_goalscorers "Argentina national football team") ([126](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_international_goals_scored_by_Lionel_Messi "List of international goals scored by Lionel Messi")) and [most-capped player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team#Most_appearances "Argentina national football team") (208) of the Argentina national team. Several years after his senior debut in 2005, he won the [gold medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_medal "Olympic medal") at the [2008 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_tournament "Football at the 2008 Summer Olympics – Men's tournament"). Assuming captaincy in 2011, he led Argentina to the finals of the [2014 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_World_Cup "2014 World Cup"), the [2015 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica "2015 Copa América"), and the 2016 [Copa América Centenario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_Centenario "Copa América Centenario"), all of which they lost. He subsequently announced his retirement from international football, but returned soon after. Messi led Argentina to victory at the [2021 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica "2021 Copa América"), the [2022 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup "2022 FIFA World Cup"), (where he became the first player to win the [World Cup Golden Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_Golden_Ball "FIFA World Cup Golden Ball") twice) and the [2024 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica "2024 Copa América"). He led Argentina to their second consecutive [World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_final "2026 FIFA World Cup final") at the [2026 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup "2026 FIFA World Cup"), where he won the [Silver Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_awards#Official_winners_(1982–present) "FIFA World Cup awards") and his second consecutive [Silver Boot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_awards#Golden_Boot "FIFA World Cup awards"). Messi retired from international football on 6 October 2026.

## Early life

Lionel Andrés Messi Cuccittini was born on 24 June 1987 in [Rosario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosario "Rosario"), Santa Fe Province, Argentina. His father, [Jorge Messi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Messi "Jorge Messi") (1958–2026), was the head of department at an [Acindar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acindar "Acindar") pressed steel manufacturing plant in [Villa Constitución](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Constituci%C3%B3n "Villa Constitución"). His mother, Celia Cuccittini, worked in a magnet manufacturing workshop. Messi's parents met as youngsters in the Las Heras district of Rosario, where Messi would later grow up. Jorge and Celia married in 1978, after Jorge completed military service in the [Argentine Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_the_Argentine_Republic "Armed Forces of the Argentine Republic"). On his father's side, Messi is of Italian and Spanish descent, the great-grandson of immigrants from the [Marche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marche "Marche") region of Italy (with his surname coming from [Porto Recanati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto_Recanati "Porto Recanati")), and on his mother's side he has primarily Italian ancestry. Growing up in a tight-knit, football-loving family, "Leo" developed a passion for the sport from an early age, playing constantly with his older brothers, Rodrigo and Matías, and his cousins, [Maximiliano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxi_Biancucchi "Maxi Biancucchi") and [Emanuel Biancucchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emanuel_Biancucchi "Emanuel Biancucchi"), both of whom later became professional footballers. Messi also has a younger sister, María Sol.

At the age of four, Messi joined the local [football club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_club_(association_football) "Football club (association football)") Abanderado Grandoli where he was coached by his father. His maternal grandmother, Celia, accompanied him to training and matches. He was greatly affected by her death, shortly before his eleventh birthday. Since then, as a devout [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church "Catholic Church"), he has celebrated his goals by looking up and pointing to the sky in tribute to his grandmother. Messi has a general understanding of English but cannot speak it fluently, and has expressed regret for refusing to learn the language during his childhood.

## Youth career

### Newell's Old Boys

> "When you saw him you would think: this kid can't play ball. He's a dwarf, he's too fragile, too small. But immediately you'd realise that he was born different, that he was a phenomenon."

— [Newell's Old Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newell%27s_Old_Boys "Newell's Old Boys") youth coach [Adrián Coria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adri%C3%A1n_Coria_(football_manager) "Adrián Coria (football manager)"), sharing his first impression of the 12-year-old Messi

Messi started playing for the [Rosario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosario "Rosario")-based football club [Newell's Old Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newell%27s_Old_Boys "Newell's Old Boys") when he was seven years old; he would become a lifelong supporter of the club. During the six years he played for Newell's, he scored almost 500 goals, and was a member of "The Machine of '87", a nearly unbeatable youth team named for the year of their birth. Messi regularly entertained crowds by performing ball tricks during half-time, when the Newell's [first team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_association_football_terms#First_team "Glossary of association football terms") played home games. His goalscoring idol growing up was Brazilian striker [Ronaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronaldo_(Brazilian_footballer) "Ronaldo (Brazilian footballer)"), who Messi called "the best forward I've ever seen".

Messi's future as a professional player was threatened when, at the age of 10, he was diagnosed with a [growth hormone deficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growth_hormone_deficiency "Growth hormone deficiency"). He began [growth hormone therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growth_hormone_therapy "Growth hormone therapy") at age 11; however, his father's health insurance covered only two years of the therapy, which cost at least 1,000 pesos per month. Newell's agreed to contribute, but later reneged on their promise. Messi was scouted by the Buenos Aires club [River Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Atl%C3%A9tico_River_Plate "Club Atlético River Plate"), whose [playmaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playmaker "Playmaker") [Pablo Aimar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Aimar "Pablo Aimar") he idolised. It was speculated for a time that Messi failed to sign with River Plate due to his ill health; however, in a 2019 interview, he revealed that River Plate had wanted to sign him after he scored four goals at a trial, and even offered to pay for his medical treatment, but Newell's refused to release his player card, preventing the transfer.

### Barcelona

As the Messi family had relatives in Catalonia, they sought to arrange a trial with [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Barcelona "FC Barcelona") in September 2000, when Messi was 13. First-team director [Carles Rexach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carles_Rexach "Carles Rexach") immediately wanted to sign him, but the board of directors hesitated; at the time it was highly unusual for European clubs to sign foreign players of such a young age. On 14 December, an ultimatum was issued for Barcelona to prove their commitment, and Rexach, with no other paper at hand, wrote a contract on a paper napkin. In February 2001, Messi's family relocated to Barcelona, where they moved into an apartment near the club's stadium, [Camp Nou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Nou "Camp Nou").

During his first year in Spain, Messi rarely played with the *Infantiles* due to a transfer conflict with Newell's; as a foreigner, he could only be fielded in friendlies and the Catalan league. Without football, he struggled to integrate into the team. He was reserved by nature, and was so quiet that some of his teammates initially believed he was mute. At home, he suffered from homesickness after his mother moved back to Rosario with his brothers and younger sister, María Sol, while he stayed in Barcelona with his father.

After a year at Barcelona's youth academy, [La Masia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Masia "La Masia"), Messi was enrolled in the [Royal Spanish Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Spanish_Football_Federation "Royal Spanish Football Federation") (RFEF) in February 2002. He could now play in all competitions, and he quickly befriended his teammates, including [Cesc Fàbregas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesc_F%C3%A0bregas "Cesc Fàbregas") and [Gerard Piqué](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_Piqu%C3%A9 "Gerard Piqué"). After completing his growth hormone therapy at age 14, Messi became an integral part of the "Baby Dream Team", which has been lauded as Barcelona's greatest-ever youth team. During his first full season (2002–03), he was the top scorer with 36 goals in 30 games for the *Cadetes* *A*, who won an unprecedented treble of the league, the Spanish cup, and the Copa Catalunya. In the Copa Catalunya final, their 4–1 victory over [Espanyol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCD_Espanyol "RCD Espanyol") came to be referred to in club lore as the *partido de la máscara,* the "final of the mask". A week after suffering a broken cheekbone during a league match, Messi was allowed to play on the condition that he wear a protective plastic mask. When the mask became a hindrance, Messi took it off and scored two goals within 10 minutes. At the end of the season, he received an offer to join the English football club [Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_F.C. "Arsenal F.C."), but he chose to remain in Barcelona.

Messi continued to progress through his club's ranks at a rapid pace, debuting for four youth teams during the 2003–04 season. After being named player of the tournament in four separate international pre-season competitions with the *Juvenil B*, he played only one official match with the team before being promoted to the *[Juvenil A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Barcelona_(youth)#Juvenil_A "FC Barcelona (youth)")*. In the *Juvenil A* he scored 18 goals in 11 league games. During the international break, Messi was one of several youth players called up to strengthen the depleted first team. [Ludovic Giuly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludovic_Giuly "Ludovic Giuly"), one of Barcelona's [wingers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winger_(soccer) "Winger (soccer)"), described Messi's performance during a training session: "He destroyed us all ... He would dribble past four players and score a goal. Even the team's starting centre-backs were nervous ... He was an alien."

At 16 years old, Messi made his first-team debut when he came on in the 75th minute during a [friendly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exhibition_game "Exhibition game") against [FC Porto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Porto "FC Porto") on 16 November 2003. His performance impressed the technical staff, and he subsequently began training daily with the club's reserve team, [Barcelona B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_B "Barcelona B"), as well as weekly with the first team. After Messi's first training session with the senior squad, Barcelona's new star player [Ronaldinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronaldinho "Ronaldinho") told his teammates that he believed the 16-year-old would become an even better player than himself. Ronaldinho called Messi "little brother", and his friendship greatly eased Messi's transition onto the first team.

To gain further match experience, Messi joined his club's third team, [Barcelona C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Barcelona_C "FC Barcelona C"), in addition to the *Juvenil A*. He helped save the third team from the [relegation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_association_football_terms#Relegation "Glossary of association football terms") zone of the [Tercera División](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tercera_Divisi%C3%B3n "Tercera División"), scoring five goals in ten games, including a [hat-trick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hat-trick "Hat-trick") in eight minutes during a [Copa del Rey Juvenil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Copa_del_Rey_Juvenil "2004 Copa del Rey Juvenil") match. His progress was reflected in his first professional contract, signed on 4 February 2004, which lasted until 2012 and contained an initial [buyout clause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buyout_clause "Buyout clause") of €30 million. A month later, he made his debut for Barcelona B in the [Segunda División B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segunda_Divisi%C3%B3n_B "Segunda División B"), and his buyout clause automatically increased to €80 million. He played five games with the B team that season but did not score. He was physically weaker than his opponents, who were often much older and taller, and he worked on increasing his muscle mass and overall strength. Towards the end of the season, he returned to both youth teams, helping the *Juvenil B* win the league. He finished the campaign having scored goals for four of his five teams, with a total of 36 goals in all official competitions.

## Club career

### Barcelona

#### 2004–2008: Rise to the first team

Messi began the [2004–05 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_FC_Barcelona_season "2004–05 FC Barcelona season") as a guaranteed starter for the [Barcelona B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Barcelona_B "FC Barcelona B") team, but after some lobbying by the senior players, he was promoted to the first team by manager [Frank Rijkaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Rijkaard "Frank Rijkaard"). He made his [La Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Liga "La Liga") debut for Barcelona on 16 October 2004, at 17 years old. He scored his first senior goal on 1 May 2005, becoming the youngest-ever scorer for the club at the time. He was at that time the youngest player to represent Barcelona in an official competition, and the club won the [league title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_La_Liga "2004–05 La Liga") during that season.

On his 18th birthday, Messi signed his first contract as a senior team player, which kept him with Barcelona through 2010, and had a release clause of €150 million. Three months later, as his performance continued to make waves, his contract was updated to double his salary and keep him with the club until 2014. By the end of the [2005–06 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_FC_Barcelona_season "2005–06 FC Barcelona season"), Barcelona had won [La Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_La_Liga "2005–06 La Liga") again as well as the [UEFA Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_UEFA_Champions_League "2005–06 UEFA Champions League").

During the [2006–07 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_FC_Barcelona_season "2006–07 FC Barcelona season"), Messi scored his first [hat-trick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hat-trick "Hat-trick") during a game against [Real Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Madrid_CF "Real Madrid CF"), becoming the first player to score a hat-trick in a *[Clásico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Cl%C3%A1sico "El Clásico")* in 12 years. Two goals scored by Messi against [Getafe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getafe_CF "Getafe CF") and Espanyol during the season drew notice for their similarities to two famous goals scored by fellow Argentine [Diego Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Maradona "Diego Maradona") in the [1986 World Cup match against England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_v_England_(1986_FIFA_World_Cup) "Argentina v England (1986 FIFA World Cup)"), leading to [comparisons between Messi and Maradona](#Comparisons_with_Diego_Maradona) that would endure throughout Messi's career. Barcelona finished the 2006–07 season with only one trophy – the [2006 Supercopa de España](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a "2006 Supercopa de España") – and the [2007–08 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_FC_Barcelona_season "2007–08 FC Barcelona season") without a single trophy, leading to Rijkaard's departure.

#### 2008–2012: Success under Pep Guardiola

At the beginning of the [2008–09 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_FC_Barcelona_season "2008–09 FC Barcelona season") – his first under Barcelona's new manager, former captain [Pep Guardiola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pep_Guardiola "Pep Guardiola") – Messi was given the [number 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squad_number_(association_football) "Squad number (association football)") shirt. Over time, he effectively became the tactical focal point of Guardiola's strategy, increasing his goalscoring rate as a result. During that season, Messi scored 38 goals, and alongside [Samuel Eto'o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Eto%27o "Samuel Eto'o") and [Thierry Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thierry_Henry "Thierry Henry"), contributed to a total of 100 goals in all competitions, a record at the time for the club. Messi played in his first [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Copa_del_Rey_Final "2009 Copa del Rey Final") as Barcelona won the [Copa del Rey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_Copa_del_Rey "2008–09 Copa del Rey"). Barcelona also won the [La Liga title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_La_Liga "2008–09 La Liga") and the [Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_UEFA_Champions_League "2008–09 UEFA Champions League"), thus achieving the first [treble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treble_(association_football) "Treble (association football)") in the history of Spanish football.

During the first half of the [2009–10 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_FC_Barcelona_season "2009–10 FC Barcelona season"), Barcelona won the [Supercopa de España](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a "2009 Supercopa de España"), the [UEFA Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_UEFA_Super_Cup "2009 UEFA Super Cup") and the [FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_FIFA_Club_World_Cup "2009 FIFA Club World Cup"), becoming the first club to achieve the sextuple. Messi finished as the [Champions League top scorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_European_Cup_and_UEFA_Champions_League_top_scorers#Top_scorers_by_season "List of European Cup and UEFA Champions League top scorers"), the youngest in the tournament's history. For his efforts in 2009, Messi won the [Ballon d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Ballon_d%27Or "2009 Ballon d'Or"), the [FIFA World Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_FIFA_World_Player_of_the_Year "2009 FIFA World Player of the Year") award and his first [European Golden Shoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Golden_Shoe "European Golden Shoe"). He scored a total of 47 goals in all competitions, equalling [Ronaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronaldo_(Brazilian_footballer) "Ronaldo (Brazilian footballer)")'s club record from the [1996–97 campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996%E2%80%9397_FC_Barcelona_season "1996–97 FC Barcelona season"). He signed a new 7-year contract with Barcelona through 2016.

During the [2010–11 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_FC_Barcelona_season "2010–11 FC Barcelona season"), Barcelona won the [Supercopa de España](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a "2010 Supercopa de España"), the [Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_UEFA_Champions_League "2010–11 UEFA Champions League"), and a third consecutive [La Liga title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_La_Liga "2010–11 La Liga"). Messi's club performances in 2010 earned him his second consecutive [Ballon d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_Ballon_d%27Or#Winners_and_nominees "2010 FIFA Ballon d'Or"). He was the top scorer in the Champions League for the third consecutive year, and the league's top scorer and assist provider. He became Barcelona's all-time single-season top scorer with 53 goals. During the [2011–12 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_FC_Barcelona_season "2011–12 FC Barcelona season"), Barcelona won both the [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a "2011 Supercopa de España") and [European Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_UEFA_Super_Cup "2011 UEFA Super Cup") trophies, as well as the [FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_FIFA_Club_World_Cup "2011 FIFA Club World Cup"). Messi won the [Golden Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_FIFA_Club_World_Cup_awards#Golden_Ball "List of FIFA Club World Cup awards") for the second time and the [Ballon d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_FIFA_Ballon_d%27Or#Winners_and_nominees "2011 FIFA Ballon d'Or") for the third time, as well as the inaugural [UEFA Best Player in Europe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Best_Player_in_Europe_Award "UEFA Best Player in Europe Award"). During 2012, Messi became the second player to be top scorer in four Champions League campaigns. He became the top goalscorer in Barcelona's history at age 24, overtaking [César Rodríguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9sar_Rodr%C3%ADguez_(footballer,_born_1920) "César Rodríguez (footballer, born 1920)")'s 57-year record of 232 goals. He finished [the season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_FC_Barcelona_season "2011–12 FC Barcelona season") as the league top scorer in Spain for the second time with 50 goals, a La Liga record. His [73 goals in all competitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_association_football_club_records#Most_goals_in_a_season "European association football club records") made him the single-season top scorer in the history of European club football excluding regional and local competitions. Barcelona won the [Copa del Rey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Copa_del_Rey_final "2012 Copa del Rey final") that season, their 14th trophy under Guardiola, who resigned after a four-year cycle of success.

#### 2012–2014: Record-breaking year and *Messidependencia*

For the start of the [2012–13 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_FC_Barcelona_season "2012–13 FC Barcelona season"), Barcelona had virtually secured their La Liga title by the start of 2013. A double scored against [Real Betis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Betis "Real Betis") saw Messi becoming Barcelona's all-time top scorer in La Liga, and surpassed [Gerd Müller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerd_M%C3%BCller "Gerd Müller")'s record of most goals scored in a calendar year; Messi scored a record 91 goals in all competitions for Barcelona and Argentina throughout 2012. Messi again won the [FIFA Ballon d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_FIFA_Ballon_d%27Or#Winners_and_nominees "2012 FIFA Ballon d'Or"), becoming the first player in history to win the Ballon d'Or four times. He signed a new contract committing himself to the club through 2018, and wore the captain's armband for the first time in a league match against [Rayo Vallecano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayo_Vallecano "Rayo Vallecano"). The team won [La Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_La_Liga "2012–13 La Liga") again that year, Messi's sixth, equalling Real Madrid's 100-point record of the previous season. With 60 goals in all competitions, including 46 goals in La Liga, he finished the campaign as league top scorer in Spain and Europe for the second consecutive year, becoming the first player in history to win the [European Golden Shoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Golden_Shoe "European Golden Shoe") three times.

Messi's overall input into the team's attack had increased significantly. Whereas he contributed to 24% of the team's goals in their treble-winning campaign in 2008–09, this number rose to more than 40% by the end of the 2012–13 season. These statistics, as well as lopsided losses in the Champions League where Messi was unfit, gave credence to the notion of *Messidependencia*, Barcelona's perceived tactical and psychological dependence on their star player.

To offset the load on Messi, Barcelona signed Brazilian forward [Neymar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neymar "Neymar") from [Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santos_FC "Santos FC") before the [2013–14 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_FC_Barcelona_season "2013–14 FC Barcelona season"). The team won the [Supercopa de España](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a "2013 Supercopa de España") at the beginning of the season. Messi finished the campaign with his worst output in five seasons, though he still managed to score 41 goals in all competitions. For the first time in five years, Barcelona ended the season without a major trophy.

#### 2014–2017: Arrival of Luis Enrique and birth of *MSN*

Barcelona hired coach [Luis Enrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Enrique "Luis Enrique") before the [2014–15 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_FC_Barcelona_season "2014–15 FC Barcelona season"), and continued to aid Messi in the attack by signing Uruguayan forward [Luis Suárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Su%C3%A1rez_(Uruguayan_footballer) "Luis Suárez (Uruguayan footballer)"), who had won the European Golden Shoe with [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C. "Liverpool F.C.") the previous year. Luis Enrique's system featured quick transitions from defence to attack, led by the forwards Messi, Suárez and Neymar. The attacking trio, which colloquially became known as *[MSN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSN_(association_football) "MSN (association football)")*, broke goalscoring records. With Messi in the right wing position, Suárez in the middle, and Neymar in the left wing position, the trio were able to create a "symbiotic" relationship in which Suárez was a relentless runner, Messi was at the centre of play orchestration, and Neymar was an explosive and creative threat.

A hat-trick by Messi against [Sevilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevilla_FC "Sevilla FC") in November 2014 made him the [all-time top scorer in La Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Liga#Most_goals "La Liga"), as he surpassed the 59-year record of 251 league goals held by [Telmo Zarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telmo_Zarra "Telmo Zarra"). After securing the [La Liga title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_La_Liga "2014–15 La Liga"), the [Copa del Rey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Copa_del_Rey_Final "2015 Copa del Rey Final"), and the [Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_UEFA_Champions_League_final "2015 UEFA Champions League final") that year, Messi helped Barcelona become the first club to win the continental treble twice. Messi recorded 58 goals during the season, while the *MSN* trio scored a total of 122 goals in all competitions, a record in Spanish football.

Messi opened the [2015–16 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_FC_Barcelona_season "2015–16 FC Barcelona season") by helping Barcelona defeat Sevilla in the [UEFA Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_UEFA_Super_Cup "2015 UEFA Super Cup"). He finished 2015 by winning the [FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIFA_Club_World_Cup "2015 FIFA Club World Cup"), defeating [River Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Atl%C3%A9tico_River_Plate "Club Atlético River Plate") in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIFA_Club_World_Cup_final "2015 FIFA Club World Cup final") and collecting his fifth club trophy of the calendar year. In January 2016, he won the [FIFA Ballon d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIFA_Ballon_d%27Or "2015 FIFA Ballon d'Or") for a record fifth time in his career, and he finished the season by winning both [La Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_La_Liga "2015–16 La Liga") and the [Copa del Rey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Copa_del_Rey_final "2016 Copa del Rey final") again. Messi scored 41 goals during the season, while the *MSN* trio scored a total of 131 goals, breaking the record they had set the previous season.

The [2016–17 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_FC_Barcelona_season "2016–17 FC Barcelona season") ended with Barcelona winning the [Supercopa de España](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a "2016 Supercopa de España") and the [Copa del Rey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Copa_del_Rey_final "2017 Copa del Rey final"). Messi finished the season with 54 goals, while his 37 goals in La Liga saw him claim both the [Pichichi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pichichi_Trophy "Pichichi Trophy") and European Golden Shoe Awards for the fourth time in his career. *MSN* came to be regarded as one of the greatest attacking trios in the game, and ended with Neymar's August 2017 transfer to [Paris Saint-Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain "Paris Saint-Germain").

#### 2017–2021: Final years at Barcelona

Messi signed a new deal with Barcelona on 25 November 2017, keeping him with the club through 2021. The [2017–18 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_FC_Barcelona_season "2017–18 FC Barcelona season") saw Barcelona winning [La Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_La_Liga "2017–18 La Liga") and the [Copa del Rey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Copa_del_Rey_final "2018 Copa del Rey final") once again. Messi again finished the season as the top scorer in La Liga, with 34 goals, and won his fifth European Golden Shoe award. With the departure of captain [Andrés Iniesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Iniesta "Andrés Iniesta") in May 2018, Messi was named the team's new captain for the [2018–19 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_FC_Barcelona_season "2018–19 FC Barcelona season"). He lifted his first trophy as Barcelona's captain, the [Supercopa de España](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a "2018 Supercopa de España"), following a 2–1 victory over Sevilla. He also helped Barcelona clinch the [La Liga title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_La_Liga "2018–19 La Liga"). With 36 goals in 34 appearances that season, Messi won his sixth La Liga Golden Shoe trophy. He also captured his sixth [European Golden Shoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Golden_Shoe "European Golden Shoe") award, setting a record for winning the award three seasons in a row.

Messi won his sixth [Ballon d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Ballon_d%27Or "2019 Ballon d'Or"), but the subsequent [2019–20 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_FC_Barcelona_season "2019–20 FC Barcelona season") saw Barcelona go trophyless for the first time since 2007–08. Following a disappointing season, Messi expressed to the club his desire to leave, but ultimately decided to fulfill the final year of his contract. In 2021, he led Barcelona to victory in the [Copa del Rey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Copa_del_Rey_final "2021 Copa del Rey final"). His last two seasons with Barcelona saw him lead La Liga in goal scoring, giving him a record-breaking total of eight Pichichi trophies. With 30 goals in the [2020–21 La Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_La_Liga "2020–21 La Liga") campaign and a triumph with Argentina at the [2021 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica "2021 Copa América"), where he was jointly named [Best Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica#Awards "2021 Copa América") and won the Golden Boot, Messi was awarded the [2021 Ballon d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Ballon_d%27Or "2021 Ballon d'Or").

Although Messi became a free agent after his contract expired, he expressed his intention to stay with Barcelona, even though the club was facing significant financial challenges due in part to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"). Following initial progress in contract negotiations, Messi intended to sign a new contract with a 50% salary cut, but Barcelona announced that they could not re-sign him, citing financial constraints and structural obstacles posed by La Liga regulations. On 8 August 2021, Messi held a tearful press conference at [Camp Nou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Nou "Camp Nou"), confirming that he would leave the club.

### Paris Saint-Germain

#### 2021–2023: 7th Ballon d'Or and consecutive Ligue 1 titles

On 10 August 2021, two days after his farewell remarks to Barcelona, Messi joined the [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1 "Ligue 1") club [Paris Saint-Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_FC "Paris Saint-Germain FC") (PSG) on a two-year contract with an option for an extra year. He chose 30 as his squad number, which is the number he wore when he made his senior debut for Barcelona. He scored his first goal for the club in a Champions League group stage win over former manager Pep Guardiola's [Manchester City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_City_F.C. "Manchester City F.C."). Messi won his first [Ligue 1 title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Ligue_1 "2021–22 Ligue 1"), which was a recording-tying 10th league title for PSG. Overall, he had an underwhelming season, scoring 6 goals in the league.

Near the start of the [2022–23 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season "2022–23 Paris Saint-Germain FC season"), Messi won his second trophy with PSG in the [Trophée des Champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Troph%C3%A9e_des_Champions "2022 Trophée des Champions"). A goal against [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OGC_Nice "OGC Nice") saw him surpass [Cristiano Ronaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristiano_Ronaldo "Cristiano Ronaldo") as the all-time highest goalscorer in European club football with 702 goals; during the match, he also achieved 1,000 career goal contributions (goals scored and assists served) at the club level. By the end of the Ligue 1 season, he had scored 16 goals and had the highest number of assists with 16, which helped PSG clinch their 11th [Ligue 1 title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Ligue_1 "2022–23 Ligue 1") and Messi's second in a row. Messi was awarded the Ligue 1 [Best Foreign Player of the Season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Ligue_1#Annual "2022–23 Ligue 1") and was recognised as the season's best player in Europe, according to [Opta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opta_Sports "Opta Sports") data. At the end of the season, he departed PSG.

### Inter Miami

#### 2023–present: 8th Ballon d'Or, all-time assists record and MLS champion

On 15 July 2023, the [Major League Soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Soccer "Major League Soccer") (MLS) club [Inter Miami CF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Miami_CF "Inter Miami CF") announced the signing of Messi on a two-and-a-half-year contract. Messi's pay set an MLS record, with his earnings from salary, signing bonus, and an equity stake in the club reportedly surpassing $50 million. According to [*Goal*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal_(website) "Goal (website)"), Messi's arrival in the United States helped to raise the profile of MLS within the US and abroad. Inter Miami co-president Xavier Asensi said that for MLS "there is a before and after Messi. He has changed everything."

After Messi joined Inter Miami, the club's games began selling out. The frenzy over his arrival was dubbed "Messimania", and Inter Miami's No. 10 Messi jersey became the best-selling jersey in the league, and nearly the best-selling in the world. Messi was made the team captain of his new club, and began the [2023 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Inter_Miami_CF_season "2023 Inter Miami CF season") by scoring nine goals in his first six games. He led Inter Miami to their first-ever trophy as they defeated [Nashville SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_SC "Nashville SC") in the [Leagues Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Leagues_Cup_final "2023 Leagues Cup final"). Miami missed the MLS playoffs, however, finishing second to last in the [Eastern Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Conference_(MLS) "Eastern Conference (MLS)"). On 30 October 2023, following his World Cup win with Argentina and Ligue 1 trophy with Paris Saint-Germain, Messi was awarded a record [eighth Ballon d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Ballon_d%27Or "2023 Ballon d'Or"). He was also named [*Time* Athlete of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Person_of_the_Year#Athlete_of_the_Year "Time Person of the Year"), the first footballer ever to win the award.

During the [2024 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Inter_Miami_CF_season "2024 Inter Miami CF season"), Messi broke the record for the most assists in a single MLS game with five assists. On 2 October, he scored two goals in a 3–2 win over the [Columbus Crew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus_Crew "Columbus Crew"), clinching the [Supporters' Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supporters%27_Shield "Supporters' Shield") award for Miami. In the final game of the season, Messi scored his first hat-trick for the club in a 6–2 victory. Miami finished the regular season with 74 points, an MLS league record, while Messi finished with 20 goals and 16 assists in 19 matches, becoming the [all-time top goalscorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Inter_Miami_CF_records_and_statistics "List of Inter Miami CF records and statistics") for the young team. Miami made their first postseason appearance in the [2024 MLS Cup playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_MLS_Cup_playoffs "2024 MLS Cup playoffs") but was eliminated in the first round. Messi was named the [MLS Most Valuable Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landon_Donovan_MVP_Award "Landon Donovan MVP Award") for the season.

During the [2025 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Inter_Miami_CF_season "2025 Inter Miami CF season"), Messi became the fastest player in MLS history to reach 40 league goals. He ended the season by winning the [MLS Golden Boot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS_Golden_Boot "MLS Golden Boot") as the league's top scorer, with 29 goals and 19 assists in 28 games. On 23 October, he signed a contract extension with the club through 2028, by which time he would be 41 years old. During the Eastern Conference final against [New York City FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_FC "New York City FC") in the [2025 MLS Cup playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_MLS_Cup_playoffs "2025 MLS Cup playoffs"), an assist from Messi put his career assists across all competitions at 405, surpassing [Ferenc Puskás](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferenc_Pusk%C3%A1s "Ferenc Puskás") for the most [career assists of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assist_(association_football)#Players_with_most_assists_in_all_competitions_(all-time) "Assist (association football)"). Messi led Miami to [MLS Cup 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS_Cup_2025 "MLS Cup 2025"), where they won 3–1 over the [Vancouver Whitecaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Whitecaps "Vancouver Whitecaps") to win the team's first league championship. Providing two assists during the match, Messi was named [MLS Cup MVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS_Cup_MVP "MLS Cup MVP"). At the end of the season, Messi was again named MLS MVP, becoming the first player in league history to win the award in back-to-back years.

In March 2026 it was reported that Messi was earning between $70 and $80 million per year from Inter Miami from his compensation and equity rights. On 18 March 2026, Messi scored his 900th career goal during Inter Miami's 1–1 draw with Nashville in the [CONCACAF Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_CONCACAF_Champions_Cup "2026 CONCACAF Champions Cup"). He became the second player to reach the mark in top-level men's football after Cristiano Ronaldo.

## International career

As a citizen of both Argentina and Spain, Messi was eligible to play for both national teams. He began his international career in 2004 with [Argentina's U20 team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_national_under-20_football_team "Argentina national under-20 football team"), and was subsequently included in the squad for the [2005 South American U-20 Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_South_American_U-20_Championship "2005 South American U-20 Championship"), in which they finished third. Messi then [led the team to victory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship_final "2005 FIFA World Youth Championship final") in the [2005 FIFA World Youth Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship "2005 FIFA World Youth Championship"). He finished the tournament with six goals and two assists, and won the [Golden Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_U-20_World_Cup#Awards "FIFA U-20 World Cup").

Messi made his debut with the [senior national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team "Argentina national football team") in 2005, at age 18, and scored his first international goal in a 2006 [friendly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friendly_(association_football) "Friendly (association football)") against [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team "Croatia national football team"). At the [2006 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup "2006 FIFA World Cup"), he became the youngest player to represent and score for Argentina at the World Cup, where he scored in his debut match. Messi was named the best young player of the [2007 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica "2007 Copa América"), during which he scored two goals and provided one assist. At the [2008 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_tournament "Football at the 2008 Summer Olympics – Men's tournament"), Messi led [Argentina's U23 team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_national_under-23_football_team "Argentina national under-23 football team") to claim the [Olympic gold medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_medal "Olympic medal") over [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_under-23_football_team "Nigeria national under-23 football team"); he was singled out by FIFA as the stand-out player from the tournament's best team.

With the international retirement of [Juan Román Riquelme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Rom%C3%A1n_Riquelme "Juan Román Riquelme") in 2009, Messi was given Argentina's number 10 shirt. Argentina built their team around Messi ahead of the [2010 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup "2010 FIFA World Cup") and [2011 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica "2011 Copa América"); Messi scored no goals during either tournament, as the team suffered quarter-final exits on both occasions. At 24 years old, Messi became captain of the national team, but over the next several years was unable to lead Argentina to a trophy. During the [2014 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup "2014 FIFA World Cup"), Argentina lost to Germany in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_Final "2014 FIFA World Cup Final"), though Messi was awarded the [Golden Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_awards#Golden_Ball "FIFA World Cup awards"). The following year, Argentina lost to [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_national_football_team "Chile national football team") in the [2015 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica "2015 Copa América") [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_final "2015 Copa América final"); Messi was reportedly selected to receive the [Golden Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica#Awards "2015 Copa América"), but he rejected the honour. During a semi-final match against the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_men%27s_national_soccer_team "United States men's national soccer team") during the 2016 [Copa América Centenario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_Centenario "Copa América Centenario"), Messi became [Argentina's all-time leading goalscorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team#Top_goalscorers "Argentina national football team") in international matches. Argentina won the semi-final but lost again to Chile in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_Centenario_final "Copa América Centenario final").

Losing three finals in three consecutive years caused Messi to retire from international football, but a nationwide campaign in Argentina helped convince him to return to the national team to lead them to the [2018 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup "2018 FIFA World Cup"). Argentina were in jeopardy of missing the tournament on the last game of the [qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_(CONMEBOL) "2018 FIFA World Cup qualification (CONMEBOL)") against [Ecuador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuador_national_football_team "Ecuador national football team"), but a [hat-trick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hat-trick "Hat-trick") from Messi secured their entry. They went on to lose to [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_national_football_team "France national football team") in the round of 16 during the World Cup, with Messi scoring one goal and making two assists in the tournament. The next year, Messi contributed one goal and one assist at the [2019 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica "2019 Copa América"). Argentina finished the tournament in third place after defeating Chile, a victory which marked the start of a 36-game winning streak for the team that lasted for more than three years.

Messi led his team to the end of a 28-year trophy drought in the 2021 Copa América, during which he surpassed [Javier Mascherano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_Mascherano "Javier Mascherano") for most international appearances for Argentina. Messi's team defeated Brazil in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_final "2021 Copa América final"), and he was named the player of the tournament, having either scored or assisted nine of Argentina's 12 goals. He led Argentina to another victory over [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_national_football_team "Italy national football team") at the [2022 Finalissima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Finalissima "2022 Finalissima"), where he was named player of the match, providing two assists in the 3–0 win. At the [2022 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup "2022 FIFA World Cup"), Messi led Argentina to their first World Cup victory in 36 years, scoring twice in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_final "2022 FIFA World Cup final") to defeat France. Having scored seven goals and assisted three during the tournament, Messi again won the World Cup's Golden Ball, becoming the first player to win it twice.

In 2023, Messi became the third male player to reach 100 international goals. Later that year, he became the all-time top goalscorer in [CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL "CONMEBOL") World Cup qualifiers. In 2024, Argentina won the [Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica "2024 Copa América") for a second consecutive time, with Messi setting a new record for most appearances at Copa América tournaments, with 39. Later that year, he became the all-time [top assist-provider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assist_(association_football)#Players_with_most_assists_in_international_football_(all-time) "Assist (association football)") in men's international football. At the [2026 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup "2026 FIFA World Cup"), he registered eight goals and four assists, overtaking [Miroslav Klose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miroslav_Klose "Miroslav Klose") and [Marta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marta_(footballer) "Marta (footballer)") to become the [all-time leading World Cup goalscorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_top_goalscorers "FIFA World Cup top goalscorers") of any gender, before his record was surpassed by [Kylian Mbappé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kylian_Mbapp%C3%A9 "Kylian Mbappé") later in the tournament. He also set a new record for the most assists in World Cup history (12) since records began in 1966. Messi led Argentina to a second consecutive World Cup [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_final "2026 FIFA World Cup final"), where they lost 1–0 to [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_national_football_team "Spain national football team") in extra time. He was awarded the [Silver Boot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup#Awards "2026 FIFA World Cup") as the second-highest goalscorer of the tournament, and won the Silver Ball as the second-best player.

On 31 August 2026, Messi announced his retirement from international football. However, he was called up to the national team again to play one [final match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_v_Benin "Argentina v Benin") against [Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin_national_football_team "Benin national football team") at [Estadio Monumental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Monumental_(Buenos_Aires) "Estadio Monumental (Buenos Aires)") in [Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires "Buenos Aires") on 6 October. Messi contributed to every goal in the 3–0 victory, assisting the first two before scoring his final international goal on a penalty.

## Player profile

### Style of play

Due to his short stature, Messi has a lower centre of gravity than taller players, which gives him greater agility and allows him to change direction more quickly and evade opposing tackles; the Spanish media has dubbed him ***La Pulga Atómica*** ("The Atomic Flea"). Despite being physically unimposing, Messi possesses significant upper-body strength, which, when combined with his low centre of gravity and resulting balance, aids him in withstanding physical challenges from opponents; he has consequently been noted for his lack of [diving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diving_(association_football) "Diving (association football)") in a sport rife with playacting. Messi's short, strong legs allow him short bursts of acceleration, while his quick feet enable him to retain control of the ball when [dribbling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dribbling#Association_football "Dribbling") at speed. His former Barcelona manager [Pep Guardiola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pep_Guardiola "Pep Guardiola") once stated, "Messi is the only player that runs faster with the ball than he does without it." Messi is predominantly a left-footed player, although he has improved his ability with his weaker right foot since his mid-20s. He usually begins dribbling runs with the outside of his left foot, while using the inside of that foot to score and provide passes and assists.

A prolific goalscorer, Messi is known for his positioning, quick reactions, and ability to make attacking runs to beat the defensive line. He also functions in a [playmaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playmaker "Playmaker") role, courtesy of his vision and range of passing. He has often been described as a magician, conjuring goals and opportunities where none seemingly exist. He is known for the accuracy of his [free kicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_free_kick "Direct free kick"), and as of September 2026 he ranks second in career goals scored from direct free kicks with 76. He also has a penchant for scoring from [chips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chip_(association_football) "Chip (association football)").

Messi's pace and technical ability enable him to undertake individual dribbling runs towards the goal, in particular during counterattacks, usually starting from the halfway line or the right side of the pitch. He is widely considered to be one of the greatest dribblers of all time. Former Argentina manager [Diego Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Maradona "Diego Maradona") said of him, "The ball stays glued to his foot ... I've never seen anyone with Messi's ball control." Beyond Messi's individual qualities, he is also a well-rounded, hard-working team player, known for his creative combinations, in particular with former Barcelona midfielders [Xavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xavi_(footballer,_born_1980) "Xavi (footballer, born 1980)") and [Andrés Iniesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Iniesta "Andrés Iniesta").

As his career advanced and his athleticism and tendency to dribble diminished with age, Messi began to dictate play in [deeper areas of the pitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midfielder#Deep-lying_playmaker_(Strolling_10) "Midfielder") and developed into one of the best passers and playmakers in football history. His work-rate off the ball and defensive responsibilities have decreased as his career has progressed; by covering less ground on the pitch, and instead conserving his energy for short bursts of speed, he has been able to improve his efficiency, movement, and positional play, and has been able to avoid muscular injuries. While he was injury-prone in his early career, he was able to decrease his injury count by running less off the ball, and by adopting a stricter diet, training regime, and sleep schedule.

### Tactical positioning

Tactically, Messi plays in a free attacking role; a versatile player, he is capable of attacking on either wing or through the centre of the pitch. His favoured position in childhood was the playmaker behind two strikers, known as the *enganche* in Argentine football, but he began his career in Spain as a [left-winger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football_positions#Winger "Association football positions") or [left-sided forward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_(association_football)#Second_striker "Forward (association football)"). Upon his first-team debut, he was moved onto the right wing by manager [Frank Rijkaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Rijkaard "Frank Rijkaard"); from this position, he could more easily cut through the defence into the middle of the pitch and [curl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curl_(football) "Curl (football)") shots on goal with his left foot, rather than predominantly [cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_(football) "Cross (football)") balls for teammates. Under Guardiola and subsequent managers, he most often played in a [false nine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_(association_football)#False_9 "Forward (association football)") role; positioned as a [centre-forward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_(association_football)#Centre-forward "Forward (association football)") or lone [striker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_(association_football)#Striker "Forward (association football)"), he roamed the centre, often moving deep into midfield and drawing defenders with him, in order to create and exploit spaces for passes, other teammates' attacking runs off the ball, his own dribbling runs, or combinations with Xavi and Iniesta.

Under the stewardship of Barcelona manager [Luis Enrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Enrique "Luis Enrique"), Messi returned to playing in the right-sided position that characterised much of his early career. He played in the manager's 4–3–3 formation, and was increasingly deployed in a deeper, free playmaking role in later seasons. Under [Ernesto Valverde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernesto_Valverde "Ernesto Valverde"), Messi played in a variety of roles. While he was still occasionally deployed in a deeper role—from which he could make runs into the box, on the right wing or as a false nine—he was also used in a more offensive, central role in a 4–2–3–1. He was also used as a second striker in a 4–4–2 formation, where he was once again given the licence to drop deep, link up with midfielders, orchestrate his team's attacking plays, and create chances for his attacking partner [Luis Suárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Su%C3%A1rez_(Uruguayan_footballer) "Luis Suárez (Uruguayan footballer)"). With the Argentina national team, Messi has similarly played anywhere along the frontline. Under various managers, he has been employed on the right wing, as a false nine, as an out-and-out striker, in a supporting role alongside another forward, or in a deeper, free creative role as a classic number 10 playmaker or [attacking midfielder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midfielder#Attacking_midfielder "Midfielder") behind the strikers.

### Reception

Messi is widely regarded as one of the two best players of his generation, alongside Portuguese forward [Cristiano Ronaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristiano_Ronaldo "Cristiano Ronaldo"). He is also considered one of the greatest footballers in the history of the sport. In 2025, he was named the [All Time Men's World Best Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFFHS#Top_10_players_of_all-time_(2025) "IFFHS") by the [IFFHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFFHS "IFFHS").

A prodigious talent as a teenager, Messi established himself among the world's best players before age 20. After winning the [2005 Ballon d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Ballon_d%27Or "2005 Ballon d'Or"), Messi's teammate [Ronaldinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronaldinho "Ronaldinho") remarked, "I'm not even the best at Barça" in reference to the 18-year-old Messi. Four years later, after Messi won his first [Ballon d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballon_d%27Or "Ballon d'Or") by a record margin, the public debate regarding his qualities as a player moved beyond his status in contemporary football to the possibility that he was one of the greatest players in history. An early proponent was his then-[Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Barcelona "FC Barcelona") manager [Pep Guardiola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pep_Guardiola "Pep Guardiola"), who, as early as August 2009, described Messi as the best player he had ever seen and would ever see. In the following years, this opinion gained greater acceptance among pundits, managers, former and current players. By the end of Barcelona's second treble-winning season, the perception of Messi as one of the greatest footballers of all time had become the apparent view among many fans and pundits in continental Europe.

Many of Messi's teammates at Barcelona and Argentina have publicly marveled over his talent, including [Thierry Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thierry_Henry "Thierry Henry"), [Zlatan Ibrahimović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zlatan_Ibrahimovi%C4%87 "Zlatan Ibrahimović"), [Neymar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neymar "Neymar"), [Luis Suárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Su%C3%A1rez_(Uruguayan_footballer) "Luis Suárez (Uruguayan footballer)"), [Xavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xavi_(footballer,_born_1980) "Xavi (footballer, born 1980)"), [Ángel Di María](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81ngel_Di_Mar%C3%ADa "Ángel Di María") and [Javier Mascherano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_Mascherano "Javier Mascherano"). Superstar peers [Thomas Müller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_M%C3%BCller "Thomas Müller"), [Eden Hazard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden_Hazard "Eden Hazard"), [Wayne Rooney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Rooney "Wayne Rooney"), [David Beckham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Beckham "David Beckham") and [Didier Drogba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Drogba "Didier Drogba") have lauded Messi as the greatest player of all time, or the best of his generation. Longtime [Real Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Madrid "Real Madrid") rivals [Luka Modrić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luka_Modri%C4%87 "Luka Modrić") and [Sergio Ramos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Ramos "Sergio Ramos") have both proclaimed Messi the best player in history. Messi was a childhood idol for [Jamal Musiala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_Musiala "Jamal Musiala"), [Lamine Yamal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamine_Yamal "Lamine Yamal"), [Cole Palmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cole_Palmer "Cole Palmer"), [Estêvão Willian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%AAv%C3%A3o_Willian "Estêvão Willian"), [Julián Alvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juli%C3%A1n_Alvarez "Julián Alvarez") and [Enzo Fernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzo_Fern%C3%A1ndez "Enzo Fernández").

Messi received dismissals by critics throughout his career because he had not won an international tournament at the senior level with Argentina. However, his subsequent victories in the [2021 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica "2021 Copa América") and the [2022 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup "2022 FIFA World Cup") resulted in him finally winning every top tier trophy at both the club and country levels, an achievement that many pundits felt cemented Messi's legacy.

### Rivalry with Cristiano Ronaldo

Among his contemporary peers, Messi is most often compared and contrasted with [Cristiano Ronaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristiano_Ronaldo "Cristiano Ronaldo"), who many consider his [career rival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messi%E2%80%93Ronaldo_rivalry "Messi–Ronaldo rivalry"). Both achieved numerous individual accolades, won dozens of trophies for both club and country, and are the two [leading goal scorers in history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_footballers_with_500_or_more_goals "List of footballers with 500 or more goals"). Although Messi has at times denied any rivalry, they are widely believed to push one another in their aim to be the best player in the world. Pundits have compared the ongoing rivalry to [past sports rivalries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sports_rivalries "List of sports rivalries") like the [Muhammad Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali "Muhammad Ali")–[Joe Frazier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Frazier "Joe Frazier") rivalry in boxing, the [Prost–Senna rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prost%E2%80%93Senna_rivalry "Prost–Senna rivalry") in motorsport, and the tennis rivalries between [Federer–Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federer%E2%80%93Nadal_rivalry "Federer–Nadal rivalry") and [Borg–McEnroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borg%E2%80%93McEnroe_rivalry "Borg–McEnroe rivalry").

Fans and pundits alike regularly argue the individual merits of both players. Messi is lauded for his combination of dribbling, playmaking, passing and goalscoring, while Ronaldo has received praise for his exceptional speed and athleticism, goalscoring skills, and performance under pressure. Beyond their playing styles, the debate also revolves around their differing physiques – Ronaldo is 1.87 m (6 ft 1+1⁄2 in) with a muscular build, while Messi is much shorter at 1.70 m (5 ft 7 in) – and their contrasting public personalities, with Ronaldo's self-confidence and theatrics differing from Messi's humility. Regarding individual achievements, Messi has won eight Ballons d'Or to Ronaldo's five, eight [FIFA World's Best Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Best_FIFA_Men%27s_Player#FIFA_Player_of_the_Year_(including_predecessors) "The Best FIFA Men's Player") awards to Ronaldo's five, and six [European Golden Shoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Golden_Shoe "European Golden Shoe") to Ronaldo's four. Off the pitch, Ronaldo is his direct competitor in terms of salary, sponsorships, and social media fanbase.

Messi's [head-to-head record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messi-Ronaldo_rivalry#Head-to-head_summary "Messi-Ronaldo rivalry") against teams that feature Ronaldo consists of 15 wins, 9 draws, and 10 losses in competitive club matches. The first competitive matchup between the two occurred in 2008, when Ronaldo's [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United "Manchester United") played Messi's Barcelona in the [2007–08 UEFA Champions League semi-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_UEFA_Champions_League_knockout_stage#Semi-finals "2007–08 UEFA Champions League knockout stage"); [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United "Manchester United") advanced and eventually won the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_UEFA_Champions_League_final "2008 UEFA Champions League final"). The two players and their club teams met again in the next year's [Champions League final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_UEFA_Champions_League_final "2009 UEFA Champions League final"), with Messi and Barcelona achieving a 2–0 victory. After this, Ronaldo transferred to [Real Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Madrid "Real Madrid"), the main rivals of Barcelona, which resulted in Messi facing Ronaldo many times in *[El Clásico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Cl%C3%A1sico "El Clásico")*, which ranks among the world's most viewed annual sports events. A number of football critics, commentators, and players have said that Messi's triumph with Argentina in the 2022 World Cup has settled the contest between the two players.

### Comparisons with Diego Maradona

Throughout his career, Messi has been compared with [Diego Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Maradona "Diego Maradona"), who was also considered the best player of his generation and one of the greatest in the history of the sport. The comparisons stem broadly from both players' short statures, their similar playing styles as left-footed [playmakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playmaker "Playmaker"), and their shared nationality as Argentines. Initially, Messi was merely one of many young Argentine players to receive the "[New Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Maradona "New Maradona")" moniker, but as his career progressed, he proved his similarity beyond all previous contenders, establishing himself as the best player Argentina had produced since Maradona. When Messi was 18 years old, Maradona called him the best player in the world and hailed him as his successor. From 2008 through the [2010 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup "2010 FIFA World Cup"), Messi played for Argentina's [national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team "Argentina national football team") with Maradona as his manager, and it was during this period that Messi was given the number 10 shirt that Maradona famously wore. Maradona gave Messi his blessing, telling him: "the No 10 is yours. There's nobody better than you to wear it."

During the early and middle parts of Messi's career, he was generally held in lesser esteem than Maradona in Argentine society. Part of this had to do with Messi's lack of international tournament success and perceived uneven output with the national team; Maradona had famously led Argentina to victory in the [1986 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_FIFA_World_Cup "1986 FIFA World Cup"), which set an expectation for Messi to do the same. Argentines also identified more with the fiery, extroverted and controversial Maradona who came from the slums, as opposed to the reserved, introverted and unassuming Messi who had a comparably unremarkable upbringing in Rosario. Maradona, along with several pundits and footballing figures, questioned whether Messi's temperament made him a capable leader for the national team. Furthermore, Messi's lack of outward passion for the Argentina shirt, his early tendency not to sing the national anthem, and his relative lack of emotional displays have led to a perception that he feels more Catalan than Argentine. However, despite having lived in Barcelona since age 13, Messi rejected the option of representing Spain internationally, saying: "Argentina is my country, my family, my way of expressing myself. I would change all my records to make the people in my country happy."

> "I have seen the player who will inherit my place in Argentinian football and his name is Messi."

— [Diego Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Maradona "Diego Maradona") hailing the 18-year-old Messi as his successor in February 2006

Football journalist [Tim Vickery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Vickery "Tim Vickery") said the perception of Messi among Argentines changed in 2019, when Messi made a conscious effort to become "more one of the group, more Argentine". Other pundits noted that Messi had grown more assertive as a leader during the [2019 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica "2019 Copa América") by becoming more vocal with his teammates both on and off the pitch, finally singing the national anthem with the team before matches, and speaking with journalists at length after matches. Following Argentina's 2022 World Cup win, Vickery felt that Messi would now be held in the same esteem by his compatriots as Maradona. Journalist and former footballer [Jorge Valdano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Valdano "Jorge Valdano") said he saw a "Maradonian" edge to Messi's performances during the tournament, while his compatriot [Osvaldo Ardiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osvaldo_Ardiles "Osvaldo Ardiles") said that Messi's provocative actions towards the Netherlands team during the [quarter-final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lusail "Battle of Lusail") resembled Maradona's behavior, which further endeared Messi to his fellow Argentines. Messi himself later remarked that the World Cup victory "won over all the people of Argentina. Today 95% or 100% of Argentines love me and that's a beautiful feeling."

## Cultural impact

### Popularity

Messi was among the [*Time* 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100 "Time 100"), an annual list of the world's most influential people, in 2011, 2012 and 2023. According to a 2014 survey in 15 international markets, Messi was familiar to 87% of respondents around the world, of whom 78% perceived him favourably, making him the second-most recognised player globally, behind [Cristiano Ronaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristiano_Ronaldo "Cristiano Ronaldo"), and the most likeable of all contemporary players. [World Press Photo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Press_Photo "World Press Photo") selected "The Final Game", a photo of Messi facing the [World Cup trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_Trophy "FIFA World Cup Trophy") after Argentina's final defeat to Germany, as the best sports image of 2014.

Messi's fanbase on [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook") is among the largest of public figures: by July 2023, he had [more than 114 million followers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most-followed_Facebook_pages "List of most-followed Facebook pages"), the second highest for an athlete after Cristiano Ronaldo. Messi has over 500 million [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram "Instagram") followers, the [second highest for an individual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most-followed_Instagram_accounts "List of most-followed Instagram accounts") and athlete after Ronaldo. Messi's World Cup celebration post from 18 December 2022 is the [most liked post on Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most-liked_Instagram_posts "List of most-liked Instagram posts") with more than 75 million likes. In April 2023, Messi was featured in the [Thrissur Pooram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrissur_Pooram "Thrissur Pooram") festival in [Kerala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala "Kerala"), India. In December 2023, a set of shirts Messi had worn during the 2022 World Cup was sold at auction for $7.8 million.

During an [exhibition match on 4 February 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_v_Inter_Miami "Hong Kong v Inter Miami") between Inter Miami and [Hong Kong League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_League "Hong Kong League") players at [Hong Kong Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_Stadium "Hong Kong Stadium"), Messi remained on the bench the entire game. His absence from the pitch caused massive backlash in Hong Kong and China, as thousands of spectators had hoped to see him play. One Messi commercial was taken off the air in those regions, while other commercials continued to air despite pressure from Chinese social-media users. The [Chinese Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Football_Association "Chinese Football Association") temporarily halted its partnership with the [Argentine Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Football_Association "Argentine Football Association") over the incident.

### Wealth and sponsorships

In 2026, [Bloomberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_News "Bloomberg News") reported that Messi's net worth had surpassed $1 billion. Messi was the world's highest-paid footballer for five out of six years between 2009 and 2014. His salary broke records in 2013 (€41 million) and 2014 (€65 million). *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes "Forbes")* listed him as the world's second highest-paid athlete in 2016, and as the [world's highest-paid athlete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes%27_list_of_the_world%27s_highest-paid_athletes "Forbes' list of the world's highest-paid athletes") in 2019. In 2018, he was the first player to exceed the €100 million benchmark for a calendar year, with earnings of €126 million in combined income from salaries, bonuses and endorsements. In 2020, Messi became the second footballer to surpass $1 billion in career earnings. In 2026, he appeared on *[The World's Billionaires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World%27s_Billionaires "The World's Billionaires")* list for the first time with a $1.1 billion net worth.

In addition to salary and bonuses, much of Messi's income comes from endorsements. His brand was at first based exclusively on his talents and achievements as a player, in contrast to arguably more glamorous players like Ronaldo and [David Beckham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Beckham "David Beckham"). At the start of his career, Messi mainly held sponsorship contracts with companies that employed sports-oriented marketing, such as [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas "Adidas") and Pepsi. Since 2006, Adidas has been his largest sponsor, and he eventually became the company's leading brand endorser. Starting in 2010, Messi's marketing appeal broadened as he reached higher achievements as a player. He signed long-term endorsement deals with luxury brands [Dolce & Gabbana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolce_%26_Gabbana "Dolce & Gabbana") and [Audemars Piguet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audemars_Piguet "Audemars Piguet"), and became a [brand ambassador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_ambassador "Brand ambassador") for [Gillette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillette_(brand) "Gillette (brand)"), [Turkish Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Airlines "Turkish Airlines"), [Ooredoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ooredoo "Ooredoo"), and [Tata Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Motors "Tata Motors"), among other companies. Messi has also been the face of two video game series: [Konami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konami "Konami")'s *[Pro Evolution Soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_Evolution_Soccer "Pro Evolution Soccer")* and [EA Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EA_Sports "EA Sports")' *[FIFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_(video_game_series) "FIFA (video game series)")*.

*[SportsPro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SportsPro "SportsPro")* cited Messi as one of the world's most marketable athletes every year between 2010 and 2015. He became the first footballer to receive his own sub-brand of Adidas boots, the Adidas Messi, in 2015. The same year, a Barcelona jersey with Messi's name and number was the best-selling replica jersey worldwide. During the 2022 World Cup, Adidas sold out of Messi's No. 10 Argentina jersey worldwide.

In 2013, a Turkish Airlines advertisement starring Messi and [Kobe Bryant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe_Bryant "Kobe Bryant") became the most-watched advertisement on [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube"), with 137 million views. It was voted by users as the best advertisement of the decade 2005–2015. In June 2021, Messi signed a five-year deal to become a brand ambassador for [Hard Rock Cafe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_Rock_Cafe "Hard Rock Cafe"), and the following year he became a tourism ambassador for [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia "Saudi Arabia"). He was criticised for partnering with Saudi Arabia because of the country's poor record on human rights.

### Public art

[Street art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_art "Street art") and public [murals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mural "Mural") depicting Messi have been painted around the world. One prominent work of art is the *[Sistine Chapel of Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sistine_Chapel_of_Football "Sistine Chapel of Football")*, a parody of [Michelangelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelangelo "Michelangelo")'s *[The Creation of Adam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Creation_of_Adam "The Creation of Adam")* that features Messi, [Diego Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Maradona "Diego Maradona") and several other Argentine footballers. The work is exhibited at the Sportivo Pereyra club in [Barracas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barracas,_Buenos_Aires "Barracas, Buenos Aires"), [Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires "Buenos Aires").

[Madame Tussauds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madame_Tussauds "Madame Tussauds") unveiled a wax sculpture of Messi at [Wembley Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley_Stadium "Wembley Stadium") in 2012. Several days after Messi announced his retirement from [Argentina's national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team "Argentina national football team") in June 2016, a bronze statue of him was erected in [Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires "Buenos Aires") in an attempt to convince him to return. Following Argentina's 2022 World Cup victory, a life-sized statue of Messi holding the World Cup trophy was unveiled outside the [CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL "CONMEBOL") headquarters in [Luque, Paraguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luque,_Paraguay "Luque, Paraguay") in March 2023. The statue of Messi stands alongside statues of [Pelé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pel%C3%A9 "Pelé") and Maradona. In December 2025, during Messi's [tour of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Messi%27s_2025_India_Tour "Lionel Messi's 2025 India Tour"), a 70-foot (21 m) statue of him holding the World Cup trophy was unveiled at the Sree Bhumi Sporting Club in [Lake Town, Kolkata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Town,_Kolkata "Lake Town, Kolkata"). The statue was later removed due to instability. In June 2026, an 85-foot (26 m) [statue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutral_Co_Messi_Statue "Cutral Co Messi Statue") of Messi was erected in [Cutral Có](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutral_C%C3%B3 "Cutral Có"), Argentina.

### Business ventures

On 4 June 2024, Messi announced the release of [Más+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1s%2B "Más+"), an American brand of [sports drinks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_drink "Sports drink") and [energy drinks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_drink "Energy drink"). Messi created the drinks because he could not find a flavourful and healthy hydration option that suited his needs. On 19 September, Messi launched the production company 525 Rosario, which produces films, sporting events coverage and branded commercials for athletes worldwide. It is a joint venture with Smuggler Entertainment, which co-produced *Messi's World Cup: The Rise of a Legend* earlier in 2024. In 2025, Messi was invited by his [Inter Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Miami_CF "Inter Miami CF") teammate [Luis Suárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Su%C3%A1rez_(Uruguayan_footballer) "Luis Suárez (Uruguayan footballer)") to play a role in [Deportivo LSM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportivo_LSM "Deportivo LSM"), the Uruguayan professional football club that Suárez founded. On 16 April 2026, Messi acquired full ownership of the Spanish football club [UE Cornellà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UE_Cornell%C3%A0 "UE Cornellà").

## Philanthropy

Throughout his career, Messi has been involved in charitable efforts aimed at children, a commitment that stems in part from the medical difficulties he faced in his own childhood. Since 2004, he has contributed his time and finances to the [United Nations Children's Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNICEF "UNICEF") (UNICEF), an organisation that has also collaborated with [FC Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Barcelona "FC Barcelona"). Messi has served as a [UNICEF Goodwill Ambassador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNICEF_Goodwill_Ambassador "UNICEF Goodwill Ambassador") since 2010; he completed his first field mission for the organisation that year as he travelled to Haiti to bring awareness to the plight of the country's children following the [2010 Haiti earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Haiti_earthquake "2010 Haiti earthquake"). He has since participated in UNICEF campaigns related to [HIV prevention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prevention_of_HIV/AIDS "Prevention of HIV/AIDS"), education, and the social inclusion of disabled children. In November 2013, Messi and his one-year-old son Thiago were part of a publicity campaign to raise awareness of mortality rates among disadvantaged children.

In 2007, Messi launched the Leo Messi Foundation, which supports access to health care, education, and sport for children. It was established following Messi's visit to a hospital in Boston for terminally ill children, an experience that resonated with him to the point that he decided to reinvest part of his earnings into society. Through his foundation, Messi has awarded research grants, financed medical training, and invested in the development of medical centres and projects in Argentina, Spain, and elsewhere in the world. In addition to receiving funds from Messi's own fundraising activities, the foundation receives financial support from various companies which have endorsement agreements with Messi, with Adidas as the main sponsor. A gold replica of Messi's left foot, weighing 25 kg (55 lb) and valued at $5.3 million, went on sale in Japan in 2013 to raise funds for victims of the [2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_T%C5%8Dhoku_earthquake_and_tsunami "2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami").

Messi has invested in youth football in Argentina. He supports Sarmiento, a football club based in the Rosario neighbourhood where he was born, and in 2013 he committed to the refurbishment of the club's facilities and the installation of all-weather pitches. Messi also financially supports the management of several youth players at [Newell's Old Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newell%27s_Old_Boys "Newell's Old Boys") (his boyhood club) and rival club [Rosario Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosario_Central "Rosario Central"), as well as at [River Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Atl%C3%A9tico_River_Plate "Club Atlético River Plate") and [Boca Juniors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boca_Juniors "Boca Juniors") in Buenos Aires. In 2012, Messi funded the construction of a new gymnasium and a dormitory inside Newell's [stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Marcelo_Bielsa "Estadio Marcelo Bielsa") for their youth academy. His youth coach at Newell's, Ernesto Vecchio, is employed by the Leo Messi Foundation as a talent scout for young players. On 7 June 2016, Messi won a libel case against [*La Razón* newspaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Raz%C3%B3n_(Madrid) "La Razón (Madrid)") and was awarded €65,000 in damages, which he donated to the charity [Médecins Sans Frontières](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9decins_Sans_Fronti%C3%A8res "Médecins Sans Frontières"). During the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"), Messi and his FC Barcelona teammates announced they would take a 70% salary cut to offset the club's financial losses, and said they would make further contributions to ensure club employees received full salaries while unable to work. Messi also donated €1 million to fight the spread of COVID-19.

In November 2016, while the [Argentine Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Football_Association "Argentine Football Association") was being run by a FIFA committee due to an economic crisis, three of the national team's security staff told Messi that they had not received their salaries for six months. He paid the salaries of the three employees. In February 2021, Messi made a donation to the [Museu Nacional d'Art de Catalunya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museu_Nacional_d%27Art_de_Catalunya "Museu Nacional d'Art de Catalunya"): the Adidas shoes he wore when he scored his 644th goal for Barcelona and broke [Pelé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pel%C3%A9 "Pelé")'s record for most goals scored for a single club. The museum later auctioned off the shoes for £125,000 to support a charity focused on children with cancer.

Ahead of the [2021 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica "2021 Copa América"), Messi donated three signed sweatshirts to the Chinese pharmaceutical firm [Sinovac Biotech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinovac_Biotech "Sinovac Biotech") – whose directors spoke of their admiration for Messi – in order to secure 50,000 doses of Sinovac's [COVID-19 vaccine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_vaccine "COVID-19 vaccine"), [CoronaVac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CoronaVac "CoronaVac"), in the hope of vaccinating all South American football players. The plan, which was brokered by Uruguay's president [Luis Lacalle Pou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Lacalle_Pou "Luis Lacalle Pou"), caused some controversy for its focus on football players, given widespread vaccine scarcity on the continent. In January 2025, Messi was awarded the [Presidential Medal of Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Medal_of_Freedom "Presidential Medal of Freedom"), the highest civilian award of the United States, for his contributions to healthcare and education programs for children around the world.

On 4 August 2026, Messi donated €80,000 to support reconstruction efforts in the [Sierra Oeste](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sierra_Oeste&action=edit&redlink=1 "Sierra Oeste (page does not exist)") [[es](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Oeste_de_Madrid "es:Sierra Oeste de Madrid")] region of the [Community of Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_of_Madrid "Community of Madrid") following the [wildfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Spain_wildfires "2026 Spain wildfires"). [Isabel Díaz Ayuso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabel_D%C3%ADaz_Ayuso "Isabel Díaz Ayuso"), President of the Community of Madrid, publicly thanked him for the donation.

## Personal life

### Family and relationships

Since 2008, Messi has been in a relationship with [Antonela Roccuzzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonela_Roccuzzo "Antonela Roccuzzo"), whom he married on 30 June 2017 in their home city of [Rosario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosario,_Santa_Fe "Rosario, Santa Fe"). Roccuzzo is the cousin of Messi's childhood best friend, [Lucas Scaglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_Scaglia "Lucas Scaglia"), and Messi has known her since he was five years old. After keeping their relationship private for a year, Messi confirmed their romance in an interview in January 2009. Messi and Roccuzzo have three sons, born in 2012, 2015, and 2018. To celebrate Roccuzzo's first pregnancy, Messi placed the ball under his shirt after scoring in Argentina's 4–0 win against Ecuador on 2 June 2012, before confirming the pregnancy in an interview two weeks later. Messi and his family are [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic "Catholic").

Messi has a close relationship with his immediate family members, particularly his mother, Celia, whose face is tattooed on his left shoulder. His professional affairs are largely run as a family business. His father [Jorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Messi "Jorge Messi") was his agent and manager from 2000 until 2026, when he died at the age of 68. His oldest brother, Rodrigo, handles his daily schedule and publicity. His mother and other brother, Matías, manage his charitable organisation, the Leo Messi Foundation, and take care of personal and professional matters in Rosario. His younger sister, María Sol, had a leadership role at Messi's fashion brand The Messi Store before joining his production company 525 Rosario.

Since leaving for Spain at age 13, Messi has maintained close ties to Rosario, and has preserved his [Rosarino accent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rioplatense_Spanish "Rioplatense Spanish"). He has retained ownership of his family's old house, although it has long stood empty, and he maintains a penthouse apartment in an exclusive residential building for his mother, as well as a family compound just outside the city. Messi keeps in frequent contact with a small group of confidants in Rosario, most of whom were fellow members of "The Machine of '87" at [Newell's Old Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newell%27s_Old_Boys "Newell's Old Boys"). He was on bad terms with the club after his transfer to Barcelona, but by 2012 their public feud had ended, with Newell's embracing their ties with Messi and issuing a club membership card to his newborn son. Messi has long planned to end his playing career at Newell's. Messi has citizenship in Argentina, Italy, and Spain.

### Tax fraud

Messi came under investigation in 2013 for suspected [tax evasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_evasion "Tax evasion") in Spain. Offshore companies in [tax havens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_haven "Tax haven") such as [Uruguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruguay "Uruguay"), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland "Switzerland") and [Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize "Belize") were used to evade €4.1 million in taxes related to his sponsorship earnings between 2007 and 2009. Messi, who pleaded ignorance to the scheme, voluntarily paid arrears of €5.1 million in August 2013. On 6 July 2016, Messi and his father were both found guilty of tax fraud and were given [suspended](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suspended_sentence "Suspended sentence") 21-month prison sentences and ordered to pay €1.7 million and €1.4 million in fines, respectively. In court, Messi told the judge, "I just played football. I signed the contracts because I trusted my dad and the lawyers and we had decided that they would take charge of those things." An unrelated [shell company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_company "Shell company") in Panama set up in 2012 was subsequently identified as belonging to the Messis in the [Panama Papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_Papers "Panama Papers") data leak.
