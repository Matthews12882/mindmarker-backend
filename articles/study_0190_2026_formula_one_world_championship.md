The **2026 FIA Formula One World Championship** is a motor racing championship for [Formula One cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_One_cars "Formula One cars") and the 77th running of the [Formula One World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_One_World_Championship "Formula One World Championship"). It is recognised by the [Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9d%C3%A9ration_Internationale_de_l%27Automobile "Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile") (FIA), the governing body of international [motorsport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorsport "Motorsport"), as the highest class of competition for [open-wheel racing cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-wheel_racing_car "Open-wheel racing car"). The championship is contested over twenty-three Grands Prix held around the world. It began in March and is scheduled to end in December. Drivers and teams compete for the titles of [World Drivers' Champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Drivers%27_Champion "World Drivers' Champion") and [World Constructors' Champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Constructors%27_Champion "World Constructors' Champion"), respectively. [Lando Norris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lando_Norris "Lando Norris") is the reigning World Drivers' Champion, while [McLaren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McLaren "McLaren")-[Mercedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes_AMG_High_Performance_Powertrains "Mercedes AMG High Performance Powertrains") are the reigning World Constructors' Champions.

The 2026 season features a major set of regulation changes with a revised power unit configuration and new active aerodynamics along with the addition of an eleventh team competing for the first time since [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Formula_One_World_Championship "2016 Formula One World Championship"). [Audi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audi_in_Formula_One "Audi in Formula One"), who acquired [Sauber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauber_Motorsport "Sauber Motorsport") in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Formula_One_World_Championship "2024 Formula One World Championship"), enter as a [works team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factory-backed "Factory-backed") with its own power unit, while [Cadillac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadillac_in_Formula_One "Cadillac in Formula One") are making their debut in the championship using [Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuderia_Ferrari "Scuderia Ferrari") power units. [Honda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_in_Formula_One "Honda in Formula One"), through its [Honda Racing Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Racing_Corporation "Honda Racing Corporation") subsidiary, entered into an exclusive works team agreement with [Aston Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aston_Martin_in_Formula_One "Aston Martin in Formula One"), and are supplying them with their own power unit after ending its relationship with [Red Bull Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bull_Racing "Red Bull Racing"). [Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_in_Formula_One "Ford in Formula One") returned to the sport for the first time since [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Formula_One_World_Championship "2004 Formula One World Championship"), supporting [Red Bull Powertrains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bull_Powertrains "Red Bull Powertrains") in supplying power units to Red Bull Racing and [Racing Bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racing_Bulls "Racing Bulls"). [Renault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_in_Formula_One "Renault in Formula One") is no longer an engine supplier as [Alpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_F1_Team "Alpine F1 Team") switched to Mercedes power units.

The [2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war "2026 Iran war") affected the championship's schedule. The [Saudi Arabian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Saudi_Arabian_Grand_Prix "2026 Saudi Arabian Grand Prix") was cancelled, and the [Bahrain Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_Grand_Prix "Bahrain Grand Prix") was postponed and ultimately [hosted in Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Bahrain_Grand_Prix_in_Malaysia "2026 Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia"), with the return of Formula One to [Sepang International Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepang_International_Circuit "Sepang International Circuit").

## Entries

Each team is required to enter at least two drivers, one for each of the two mandatory cars. All teams compete with tyres supplied by [Pirelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirelli "Pirelli").

### Team changes

[Cadillac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadillac_in_Formula_One "Cadillac in Formula One") became the eleventh team, marking Cadillac's first appearance in the series and the first new team on the grid since [Haas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haas_F1_Team "Haas F1 Team") in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Formula_One_World_Championship "2016 Formula One World Championship"). The team, initially using [Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuderia_Ferrari "Scuderia Ferrari") power units and gearboxes, would transition to a power unit developed in-house by its parent company, [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors "General Motors"), in collaboration with [TWG Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TWG_Motorsports "TWG Motorsports"). This power unit programme has received approval from the FIA and is scheduled to be introduced from the 2029 season. Cadillac had previously tried to enter Formula One in conjunction with [Andretti Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andretti_Global "Andretti Global").

Three new engine manufacturers entered Formula One, coinciding with the engine regulation changes. [Audi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audi_in_Formula_One "Audi in Formula One") entered the sport for the first time, having purchased the existing [Sauber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauber_Motorsport "Sauber Motorsport") team in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Formula_One_World_Championship "2024 Formula One World Championship"). Audi's predecessor company [Auto Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auto_Union "Auto Union") did [compete in Grand Prix racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auto_Union_racing_cars "Auto Union racing cars") prior to [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") and the inception of the World Championship in [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_Formula_One_season "1950 Formula One season"). The team raced in 2024 and [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Formula_One_World_Championship "2025 Formula One World Championship") as [Kick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kick_(service) "Kick (service)") Sauber, using Ferrari engines, before it became the Audi factory team in 2026. Thus, Ferrari is supplying Haas and Cadillac as customer teams.

[Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_in_Formula_One "Ford in Formula One") returned to Formula One as an [engine supplier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Formula_One_engine_manufacturers "List of Formula One engine manufacturers") for the first time since it provided engines for its former customer team [Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Grand_Prix "Jordan Grand Prix") in [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Formula_One_World_Championship "2004 Formula One World Championship"). It formed a partnership with [Red Bull Powertrains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bull_Powertrains "Red Bull Powertrains"), which supplies [Red Bull Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bull_Racing "Red Bull Racing") and their second team [Racing Bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racing_Bulls "Racing Bulls"). [Honda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_in_Formula_One "Honda in Formula One"), which partially withdrew from Formula One in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Formula_One_World_Championship "2021 Formula One World Championship") whilst remaining partners of Red Bull Powertrains, split from the two Red Bull-owned teams and relaunched a fully-independent engine programme supplying [Aston Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aston_Martin_in_Formula_One "Aston Martin in Formula One") under the [Honda Racing Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Racing_Corporation "Honda Racing Corporation") subsidiary. Aston Martin had used [Mercedes power units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes_AMG_High_Performance_Powertrains "Mercedes AMG High Performance Powertrains") since they re-joined the sport, who had supplied engines to Aston Martin's predecessors since [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Formula_One_World_Championship "2009 Formula One World Championship"). Honda had previously collaborated with Aston Martin's predecessor Jordan from [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Formula_One_World_Championship "1998 Formula One World Championship") to [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Formula_One_World_Championship "2002 Formula One World Championship").

[Renault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_in_Formula_One "Renault in Formula One") ceased to provide engines for [Alpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_F1_Team "Alpine F1 Team") from 2026 after achieving poor results since the beginning of the hybrid power unit regulations in [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Formula_One_World_Championship "2014 Formula One World Championship"). 2026 is thus the first season without Renault as engine supplier since the [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Formula_One_World_Championship "2000 Formula One World Championship") championship. Alpine became a customer team, as opposed to a full works outfit, as had been the case since Renault reacquired [Team Enstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_Enstone "Team Enstone") ahead of the [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Formula_One_World_Championship "2016 Formula One World Championship") season. Alpine became a customer team of [Mercedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_in_Formula_One "Mercedes-Benz in Formula One"), utilising their engines and gearboxes from this season onwards; up until this point, [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Formula_One_World_Championship "2015 Formula One World Championship") marked the last time a rendition of Team Enstone used customer Mercedes engines, competing as [Lotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotus_F1 "Lotus F1").

### Driver changes

[Sergio Pérez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_P%C3%A9rez "Sergio Pérez") and [Valtteri Bottas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valtteri_Bottas "Valtteri Bottas") returned to full time seats with [Cadillac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadillac_in_Formula_One "Cadillac in Formula One"). Pérez had previously signed a two-year contract with [Red Bull Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bull_Racing "Red Bull Racing") until 2026, but this was terminated by mutual agreement at the end of the [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Formula_One_World_Championship "2024 Formula One World Championship") season. Bottas last competed with [Sauber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauber_Motorsport "Sauber Motorsport") in 2024 and was one of [Mercedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_in_Formula_One "Mercedes-Benz in Formula One")'s reserve drivers for [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Formula_One_World_Championship "2025 Formula One World Championship"). [Isack Hadjar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isack_Hadjar "Isack Hadjar") moved from [Racing Bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racing_Bulls "Racing Bulls") to Red Bull Racing, replacing [Yuki Tsunoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuki_Tsunoda "Yuki Tsunoda"), who became Red Bull's test and reserve driver. Hadjar's replacement was [Arvid Lindblad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvid_Lindblad "Arvid Lindblad"), who was promoted from [Formula 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA_Formula_2_Championship "FIA Formula 2 Championship").

#### Mid-season changes

Prior to the [Dutch Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Dutch_Grand_Prix "2026 Dutch Grand Prix"), [Isack Hadjar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isack_Hadjar "Isack Hadjar") sustained a wrist injury. Racing Bulls driver [Liam Lawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liam_Lawson "Liam Lawson") substituted for him, while Lawson was replaced at Racing Bulls by reserve driver [Yuki Tsunoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuki_Tsunoda "Yuki Tsunoda"). ### Postponed and cancelled Grands Prix

The [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Bahrain_Grand_Prix "2026 Bahrain Grand Prix") and [Saudi Arabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Saudi_Arabian_Grand_Prix "2026 Saudi Arabian Grand Prix") Grands Prix were included on the original calendar published by the [World Motor Sport Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Motor_Sport_Council "World Motor Sport Council") and were contracted for the 2026 season, but were postponed following the outbreak of the [Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war "2026 Iran war"). Concerns revolving around safety following the breakout of the war were taken into consideration, with the FIA monitoring the situation as it unfolded. Ultimately, it was announced that neither race would take place on its originally scheduled April date. As the Grands Prix were to be held early in the season, replacement races could not be organised in short notice. The Saudi Arabian Grand Prix was not reinstated, while the Bahrain Grand Prix was rescheduled and relocated to the [Sepang International Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepang_International_Circuit "Sepang International Circuit") in [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia"), rather than being held at its originally planned venue, the [Bahrain International Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_International_Circuit "Bahrain International Circuit"). Despite the venue change, the event retained its original name because the Bahraini government remained the primary financial promoter, rather than this event becoming an instance of the [Malaysian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Grand_Prix "Malaysian Grand Prix").

## Regulation changes

Financial, power unit, aerodynamic, geometric, and safety regulations were altered for the 2026 season.

### Financial regulation

For 2026, the Formula One cost cap for team operational expenditures was increased from $135 million to $215 million. This was primarily due to inflation adjustments and the inclusion of costs that were previously exempted from the cap. Key expenses remaining outside the cost cap include driver salaries, the salaries of the three highest-paid team members, marketing, travel and hospitality costs, as well as infrastructure investments. A separate cost cap for power unit manufacturers, which was introduced in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Formula_One_World_Championship "2023 Formula One World Championship"), was also increased from $95 million to $130 million from 2026 onwards to support the development of the new hybrid engines.

### Technical regulations

#### Power units

New power unit regulations have been introduced for the 2026 season. The new power units still produce over 1,000 [bhp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horsepower#Brake_horsepower "Horsepower") (750 [kW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilowatt "Kilowatt")), although the power comes from different sources. The engine regulations saw the turbocharged 1.6-litre V6 [internal combustion engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_combustion_engine "Internal combustion engine") configuration used since [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Formula_One_World_Championship "2014 Formula One World Championship") retained. However, the [MGU-H](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MGU-H "MGU-H") (Motor Generator Unit – Heat), which has also been in use since 2014, has been removed, while the [MGU-K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MGU-K "MGU-K") (Motor Generator Unit – Kinetic) output increased to 470 bhp (350 kW) from 160 bhp (120 kW). The power output of the internal combustion part of the power unit decreased to 540 bhp (400 kW) from 850 bhp (630 kW). Fuel flow rates are measured and limited based on energy, rather than mass of the fuel itself. The power units use a fully [sustainable fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_fuel "Sustainable fuel") being developed by Formula One. The power units are expected to recover twice as much electrical energy as before.

During the Commission meeting in early 2025, it was agreed to explore for catch-up options for power unit development for manufacturers who may have fallen behind their competitors in the initial development phase. The Commission also suggested that a relevant advisory committee look at whether the energy deployment of new power units should be reduced in race trim from the planned 470 bhp (350 kW) after some teams became concerned about the possibility of their cars running out of deployable energy in a race.

The [Japanese Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Japanese_Grand_Prix "2026 Japanese Grand Prix") saw [Oliver Bearman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Bearman "Oliver Bearman") crash at a high speed due to a speed differential that was caused by him using more electrical energy than [Franco Colapinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Colapinto "Franco Colapinto"), who opted to harvest his energy. The power unit changes that mandated the increased usage of the battery and electric power deployment modes came under scrutiny from drivers. The FIA held a review with power unit developers on 9 April regarding potential refinements to the regulations. One of the solutions was to reduce the deployment rate of electrical energy while increasing the amount that can be harvested while undergoing "super clipping", a phenomenon that occurs when the battery is being charged on full throttle against the engine. Three further meetings were held on 15, 16 and 20 April, where further options would be evaluated and agreed on by team representatives, the FIA, Formula One Management, and power unit manufacturers.

Going into the [Miami Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Miami_Grand_Prix "2026 Miami Grand Prix"), changes were announced to be made to the power unit. Those include an increase to the "super clipping" limit from 340 bhp (250 kW) to 470 bhp (350 kW). It intends to reduce its duration to 2–4 seconds per lap. Though the cars will still lose top speed on the straights, this reduces the need to apply lift and coast as the speed difference is considered much more natural. For qualifying, the harvesting limit was reduced from 8 MJ (2.2 [kWh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilowatt-hour "Kilowatt-hour")) to 7 MJ (1.9 [kWh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilowatt-hour "Kilowatt-hour")), with the potential to reduce it further for some races.

#### Car size and aerodynamics

On 6 June 2024, the 2026 car concept was revealed. The concept featured new active aerodynamics in both the front and rear wings. The concept saw the elimination of the [drag reduction system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drag_reduction_system "Drag reduction system") (DRS), being replaced by a new overtake mode, initially referred to as manual override mode. The wheelbase was reduced from 360 cm (140 in) to 340 cm (130 in), the width was reduced from 200 cm (79 in) to 190 cm (75 in), and the minimum mass was reduced by 30 kg (66 lb). The tyres' widths were also reduced by 2.5 cm (0.98 in) on the front pair and by 3.0 cm (1.2 in) on the rears. The floor reduced [ground effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_effect_(cars) "Ground effect (cars)") to ease the issues cars have suffered with [porpoising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porpoising "Porpoising").

In October 2024, FIA announced that the downforce reduction of the 2026 cars compared to the [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Formula_One_World_Championship "2022 Formula One World Championship")–[2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Formula_One_World_Championship "2025 Formula One World Championship") generation of cars would be less than initially proposed for performance and safety reasons. Later in the month, the FIA confirmed that the reduction in downforce from the 2026 generation of cars would be around 15%, a significantly smaller reduction than the originally drafted regulations which the FIA claimed had given the 2026 cars downforce reduction of over 40% compared to their predecessors. This would make the 2026 cars approximately two seconds slower a lap than the 2022–2025 generation of cars rather than the four seconds slower initially envisaged. On 17 December 2025, Formula One announced the official terms describing features of the 2026 cars. The term "boost mode" is used to describe the normal deployment of the energy recovery system (ERS) to defend or attack; "active aerodynamics" (or simply "active aero") to describe the moveable front and rear wing elements – in both higher and lower drag/downforce configuration (called "corner mode" and "straightline mode" respectively); "recharge" to describe when a car is harvesting electrical energy; and "overtake mode" to describe a mode where extra energy stored is used in order to help one driver overtake another, but only if the driver behind is within one second of the one ahead (similar to pre-2026 DRS deployment rules).

Due to [the track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_de_Monaco "Circuit de Monaco")'s tight, twisty nature, the only Grand Prix where active aero was disabled was the [Monaco Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Monaco_Grand_Prix "2026 Monaco Grand Prix"), which historically had one DRS zone.

#### Safety features

The regulations for the front impact structure (FIS) were updated with the intent to enhance [safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safety_in_Formula_One "Safety in Formula One") during crashes. A two-stage FIS design has been introduced to address previous issues where the structure detached near the survival cell after a primary collision, leaving the vehicle vulnerable to further impacts. Side intrusion protection, particularly around the cockpit and fuel cell was also improved. These upgrades aim to shield critical areas of the car during side collisions, while maintaining the vehicle's weight. The roll hoop's strength was improved, withstanding loads increased from 16 *g* to 20 *g*, aligning with safety standards of other [single-seater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-seater "Single-seater") series. The load testing requirements were raised from 141 kN to 167 kN. To further safeguard drivers and [track marshals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Track_marshals "Track marshals"), lateral safety lights were mandated. These lights display the [energy recovery system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_recovery_system_(Formula_One) "Energy recovery system (Formula One)") (ERS) status of a car when it stops on track, providing visual indicators of potential electrical risks, especially in emergency situations.

### Sporting regulations

* The rules surrounding the permanent driver numbering system introduced in [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Formula_One_World_Championship "2014 Formula One World Championship") were modified. Drivers are permitted to change their race number during their careers pending permission from the FIA and any past driver that may have used a requested number.
* In order to discourage teams leaving excessive amounts of exposed black carbon on their cars in order to save weight, the FIA mandated that a minimum of 55% of the surface area when viewed from the side and above must be covered by painted or stickered liveries.
* Driver cooling vests are mandated when the FIA declares a heat hazard during a race weekend. Prior to 2026, the cooling vest was optional. Additionally, the vest underwent a redesign.
* For 2026, stewards have the option to initiate a review based on new evidence. Under the previous version of the ISC, a competitor had to submit a right of review request for any decision to be reviewed. The option for competitors to submit a right of review remains in place. Additionally, an "out of competition" stewards panel was introduced for time sensitive decisions that cannot wait until the next event.
* In 2026, the requirement to make a minimum two-stop strategy at the [Monaco Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monaco_Grand_Prix "Monaco Grand Prix") was dropped. This was implemented in the [2025 race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Monaco_Grand_Prix "2025 Monaco Grand Prix") in an effort to promote better racing, but teams and drivers considered that it did not improve the racing quality and overtake opportunities.

## Season summary

### Pre-season

Three pre-season tests were held, a significant expansion over previous years' testing programs to account for the new chassis and engine regulations. The first was a private test which took place at the [Circuit de Barcelona-Catalunya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_de_Barcelona-Catalunya "Circuit de Barcelona-Catalunya") from 26 to 30 January, with teams allowed to run in a maximum of three of the five days. All teams ran in the private test except [Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_Racing "Williams Racing"). [Lewis Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Hamilton "Lewis Hamilton") ([Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuderia_Ferrari "Scuderia Ferrari")) set the fastest time in the five-day test. The second test was held at the [Bahrain International Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_International_Circuit "Bahrain International Circuit") from 11 to 13 February, with [Kimi Antonelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimi_Antonelli "Kimi Antonelli") ([Mercedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_in_Formula_One "Mercedes-Benz in Formula One")) setting the fastest time in the three-day test. The third test was also held at the same venue from 18 to 20 February, with [Charles Leclerc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Leclerc "Charles Leclerc") (Ferrari) setting the fastest time across the three-day test.

### Opening rounds

[Mercedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_in_Formula_One "Mercedes-Benz in Formula One")' [George Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Russell_(racing_driver) "George Russell (racing driver)") took pole position for the season opening [Australian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Australian_Grand_Prix "2026 Australian Grand Prix"), ahead of teammate [Kimi Antonelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimi_Antonelli "Kimi Antonelli"). [Isack Hadjar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isack_Hadjar "Isack Hadjar") qualified third on his debut for [Red Bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bull_Racing "Red Bull Racing"). [Charles Leclerc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Leclerc "Charles Leclerc") jumped from fourth on the grid to take the lead at the start, and proceeded to battle Russell in the early laps, with both drivers taking stints in the lead. After a virtual safety car was called for Hadjar stopping on track with an engine issue, Mercedes brought both their cars in for a pit stop, whereas Ferrari stayed out hoping to take advantage of having newer tyres later on in the race. Mercedes' strategy came up trumps and Russell went on to secure victory, with Antonelli finishing second to complete a Mercedes one–two. Leclerc finished third, holding off a late charge from Hamilton. Reigning World Champion [Lando Norris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lando_Norris "Lando Norris") finished fifth for [McLaren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McLaren "McLaren"), having been the team's only starter after [Oscar Piastri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Piastri "Oscar Piastri") crashed while driving to the grid before the race. [Max Verstappen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Verstappen "Max Verstappen") recovered from 20th on the grid, due to a crash in qualifying, to finish sixth, ahead of [Oliver Bearman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Bearman "Oliver Bearman") and rookie [Arvid Lindblad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvid_Lindblad "Arvid Lindblad"). [Gabriel Bortoleto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Bortoleto "Gabriel Bortoleto") and [Pierre Gasly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Gasly "Pierre Gasly") completed the top ten and the points-scoring positions. Five drivers retired from the race. In the [Drivers' Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Drivers%27_Champion "World Drivers' Champion"), Russell took the lead for the first time in his career with 25 points, ahead of his teammate Antonelli with 18 points and Leclerc with 15 points.

Russell took his maiden sprint pole position for the [Chinese Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Chinese_Grand_Prix "2026 Chinese Grand Prix"), ahead of Antonelli and Norris. The opening laps saw Hamilton take the lead, as he exchanged positions several times with Russell. Hamilton and Leclerc fought for podium places with the latter gaining the advantage. Antonelli dropped down the order in the opening laps, only to have his recovery to second place be thwarted by a penalty from causing a collision with Hadjar on the opening lap, ultimately finishing fifth. Thus, the podium was rounded out by Russell, Leclerc and Hamilton. Antonelli later became the youngest ever Formula One pole-sitter after taking pole position for the main race, while teammate Russell recovered from a mechanical issue to qualify second. Piastri, Norris, Bortoleto, and Albon all did not start the race due to separate car issues. The opening lap saw Hamilton reach the lead again within the first few turns, this time ahead of Antonelli, while Leclerc overtook Russell to take third. Antonelli regained the lead by overtaking Hamilton on lap 2. Following Stroll's battery issue resulting in being stranded at turn 1 and bringing out the safety car, most of the grid initially on the medium compound moved to a one stop strategy. From there, Antonelli held onto the lead to claim his maiden victory ahead of Russell who complete another Mercedes one–two, while Hamilton took third after a lengthy battle with Leclerc, thus achieving his first podium for [Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuderia_Ferrari "Scuderia Ferrari") and first since the [2024 Las Vegas Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Las_Vegas_Grand_Prix "2024 Las Vegas Grand Prix"). Colapinto also scored his first points for Alpine, and his first since the [2024 United States Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_Grand_Prix "2024 United States Grand Prix") at Williams.

Antonelli took a second consecutive pole position for the [Japanese Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Japanese_Grand_Prix "2026 Japanese Grand Prix") ahead of Russell and Piastri. After dropping down to as low as sixth, Antonelli made use of a well-timed safety car, brought out due to [Oliver Bearman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Bearman "Oliver Bearman")'s heavy [Haas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haas_F1_Team "Haas F1 Team") crash, to retain the lead as he switched to fresher tyres, while teammate Russell finished fourth after briefly dropping down the order to both Ferraris (he managed to repass only Hamilton). Antonelli's victory meant he took his first consecutive victories of the season, and overtook Russell to become the [youngest World Drivers' Championship leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Formula_One_driver_records#Championships,_other "List of Formula One driver records"); also becoming Italy's first Drivers' Championship leader since [Alberto Ascari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Ascari "Alberto Ascari") led, and won, the [1953](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1953_Formula_One_season "1953 Formula One season") championship. Behind him, Piastri, recording his first race start of the season, and Leclerc rounded out the podium.

Several changes to the power unit came into effect at the [Miami Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Miami_Grand_Prix "2026 Miami Grand Prix"), having been drafted during the extended break after the [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Bahrain_Grand_Prix "2026 Bahrain Grand Prix") and [Saudi Arabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Saudi_Arabian_Grand_Prix "2026 Saudi Arabian Grand Prix") Grands Prix did not take place on their originally scheduled April dates (see [§ Power units](#Power_units)). [Lando Norris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lando_Norris "Lando Norris") took pole in the sprint and proceeded to lead it lights-to-flag, ahead of Piastri and Leclerc. Antonelli, who finished sixth in the sprint after receiving a penalty for track limits, recovered to take pole for the race. Norris passed both Antonelli and Leclerc to lead early on, but lost out to Antonelli during the pit sequence and was unable to repass the Italian. Antonelli went on to win again, and became the first driver to convert his first three consecutive pole positions into wins at the same events. The McLarens of Norris and Piastri completed the podium. The race was eventful; Verstappen and Leclerc spun at the start and tail end of the race, respectively, with Leclerc sustaining damage to his front-left suspension as a result. Meanwhile, [Liam Lawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liam_Lawson "Liam Lawson"), whose car had an issue, hit Gasly, who flipped into a wall, while [Isack Hadjar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isack_Hadjar "Isack Hadjar"), crashed at the turn fourteen chicane on the same lap. [Franco Colapinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Colapinto "Franco Colapinto") finished a career-best seventh, bettering his previous result of tenth despite contact with Hamilton on the opening lap, leaving the latter with damage that affected his car's performance.

### Mid-season rounds

The [Canadian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Canadian_Grand_Prix "2026 Canadian Grand Prix") saw Russell take pole position for the sprint event. During the sprint, he squabbled with teammate Antonelli, who went off-track numerous times trying to take the lead. Russell ultimately won the event ahead of Norris and Antonelli, the latter having dropped down to third after attempting another move on Russell. Russell then took pole position for the race itself; once again, he battled against Antonelli for the lead, though Russell was forced out of the race and contention for the win after a power unit failure, with Antonelli taking his fourth consecutive victory. Behind him, Hamilton bettered his highest finish at Ferrari with a second-place finish, with [Max Verstappen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Verstappen "Max Verstappen") achieving his and Red Bull's first podium of the season just behind Hamilton. Six drivers retired, including Norris, whose gearbox experienced a failure, and [Sergio Pérez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_P%C3%A9rez "Sergio Pérez"), recording [Cadillac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadillac_in_Formula_One "Cadillac in Formula One")'s first retirement thanks to a spontaneous suspension failure. Colapinto bettered his Miami result by finishing sixth, and [Oscar Piastri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Piastri "Oscar Piastri") finished outside the points: he struck [Alexander Albon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Albon "Alexander Albon"), forcing Albon to retire on the spot and Piastri to pit for repairs, and received a ten-second penalty for the incident.

Active aerodynamics were disabled for the [Monaco Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Monaco_Grand_Prix "2026 Monaco Grand Prix") due to [the track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_de_Monaco "Circuit de Monaco")'s narrow layout and lack of straights (see [#Car size and aerodynamics](#Car_size_and_aerodynamics)). During the Grand Prix, Verstappen, Bottas, Bearman and Norris retired all due to reliability issues, and numerous penalties were applied. During the late stages of the race, [Lance Stroll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Stroll "Lance Stroll") crashed his car in a manner that led to the safety car being deployed; at the resulting restart Leclerc crashed at the exact same corner, bringing about a lengthy red flag while officials inspected the track surface. Furthermore, [Carlos Sainz Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Sainz_Jr. "Carlos Sainz Jr.")'s streak of points at Monaco came to an end due to two separate hits to his [Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_Racing "Williams Racing") by [Nico Hülkenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nico_H%C3%BClkenberg "Nico Hülkenberg") and Colapinto, with Hülkenberg receiving a ten-second time penalty which dropped him out of the points as well. Antonelli converted his pole position into a win, becoming the youngest winner of the [Monaco Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monaco_Grand_Prix "Monaco Grand Prix") and the youngest to achieve a *grand chelem*. [Isack Hadjar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isack_Hadjar "Isack Hadjar") stood on the podium after [Pierre Gasly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Gasly "Pierre Gasly") received two five-second penalties for pit-lane speeding during the race, which dropped Gasly from third to seventh in the classification. Hadjar was temporarily demoted to fourth when [Alpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_F1_Team "Alpine F1 Team") successfully appealed the penalties, reinstating Gasly to third. This decision was ultimately reversed in September by the FIA after McLaren and Red Bull filed an appeal against the decision. [Fernando Alonso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Alonso "Fernando Alonso") recorded Aston Martin's first point of the season following a deluge of penalties being applied, which initially saw Pérez provisionally finish in tenth. Repeating his result in Canada, Hamilton finished in second for Ferrari, while his former Mercedes teammate Russell finished outside the points following a drive-through penalty.

Russell topped two out of three practice sessions and the qualification for the maiden [Barcelona-Catalunya Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Barcelona-Catalunya_Grand_Prix "2026 Barcelona-Catalunya Grand Prix") and led the first half of the race, before [Fernando Alonso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Alonso "Fernando Alonso") retired with a battery failure. This brought out the virtual safety car and allowed [Lewis Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Hamilton "Lewis Hamilton"), who was running on an alternate strategy, to switch to new hard tyres and rejoin ahead of Russell in the lead. Late in the race, Russell was hounded by Antonelli, who passed him for second only to retire a lap later with a power unit failure. This retirement allowed Russell to take points off of Antonelli. Hamilton took his maiden win for Ferrari, and his first victory since the [2024 Belgian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Belgian_Grand_Prix "2024 Belgian Grand Prix"). Russell finished second, with [Lando Norris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lando_Norris "Lando Norris") third, producing the first all-British Formula One podium since the [1968 United States Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_United_States_Grand_Prix "1968 United States Grand Prix").

Russell took his second consecutive pole in [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Austrian_Grand_Prix "2026 Austrian Grand Prix") despite having to lift on his final lap due to a yellow flag following an incident involving [Max Verstappen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Verstappen "Max Verstappen"). He converted the pole to a win (and retook second in the Drivers' Championship from Hamilton), fending off pressure from Verstappen who recovered from the qualifying incident to take second. Antonelli rounded out the podium, finishing 1.9 seconds behind Russell and three tenths behind Verstappen. During the race, multiple duels between both Ferraris was observed, Sainz retired on the pit straight after an electrical problem, and the Cadillacs retired early following overheating brake issues.

At the [British Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_British_Grand_Prix "2026 British Grand Prix"), Hamilton took sprint pole position, though Antonelli passed him for the win. Antonelli built on this momentum into Grand Prix qualifying and took pole position, however he received wheelspin at the start and dropped behind the [Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuderia_Ferrari "Scuderia Ferrari") cars of Leclerc and Hamilton. Leclerc led a majority of the race; during the closing stages, Antonelli, who was closing in on Leclerc, damaged the front-left wheel shield after running over a kerb. This hampered the car's ability to turn and he had to pit twice to fix the issue, before then attaining a five-second penalty for repeated track limits infringements. The race ended under safety car after Verstappen, who was running third, had to retire after a rear wing failure resulted in him spinning off into the gravel trap at Stowe. Leclerc won the race, his first British Grand Prix victory and first victory since the [2024 United States Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_Grand_Prix "2024 United States Grand Prix"). Russell and Hamilton completed the podium at their home race with both drivers recovering from earlier setbacks; Russell from a slow puncture and Hamilton from a five-second penalty for a false start. Antonelli finished outside the points in fifteenth after the track limits penalty was added on to his race time. In addition to Verstappen, [Alex Albon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Albon "Alex Albon") and [Nico Hülkenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nico_H%C3%BClkenberg "Nico Hülkenberg") also retired.

Antonelli started the [Belgian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Belgian_Grand_Prix "2026 Belgian Grand Prix") from pole position, although Verstappen briefly overtook him on the opening lap before the Mercedes driver reclaimed the lead along the Kemmel Straight. Russell retired following an opening-lap collision with Hamilton, who was deemed responsible and received a five-second penalty. A virtual safety car brought out for gravel on the racing line allowed Leclerc to jump both Antonelli and Verstappen through the pit stops and take the lead, but Antonelli closed the gap and passed Leclerc on lap 34. He held off the Ferrari driver to claim his sixth victory of the season, with Verstappen completing the podium. Hamilton recovered from his penalty to finish fourth ahead of Piastri, while [Isack Hadjar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isack_Hadjar "Isack Hadjar") progressed from twenty-first on the grid to sixth. The result extended Antonelli's championship lead to 45 points over Hamilton and 50 over Russell.

The [Hungarian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Hungarian_Grand_Prix "2026 Hungarian Grand Prix") saw [Aston Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aston_Martin_in_Formula_One "Aston Martin in Formula One") bring no less than 16 updates to their car, with [Fernando Alonso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Alonso "Fernando Alonso") giving the team their first Q2 appearance of the season as a result. Norris edged out Hamilton by 0.012 seconds to take the seventeenth pole of his career, and become the first non-[Mercedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_in_Formula_One "Mercedes-Benz in Formula One") driver to take pole in 2026. Both Hamilton and Antonelli would receive three-place grid penalties post-qualifying; Hamilton for impeding [Oscar Piastri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Piastri "Oscar Piastri"), and Antonelli for failing to slow sufficiently for yellow flags. Piastri moved into the lead on the opening lap after Norris ran wide at turn 2, and proceeded to lead the first half of the race. After Piastri's second stop, he was slowed by minor contact with the lapped car of [Carlos Sainz Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Sainz_Jr. "Carlos Sainz Jr.") and further delayed by other lapped cars, allowing Norris to extend his second stint and emerge ahead of Piastri after pitting on lap 40. Norris controlled the remainder of the race to claim his and [McLaren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McLaren "McLaren")'s first victory of the season, while Piastri retired from second place with a gearbox failure, triggering a virtual safety car with 14 laps remaining. Ferrari pitted Hamilton under the virtual safety car, dropping him behind Verstappen and Antonelli, before a five-second penalty for speeding in the pit lane demoted him to fifth behind Leclerc. Verstappen and Antonelli completed the podium, with Hadjar finishing sixth and Russell recovering from stalling at the start to take seventh. [Nico Hülkenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nico_H%C3%BClkenberg "Nico Hülkenberg") finished ninth, scoring his first points of the season and first as an [Audi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audi_in_Formula_One "Audi in Formula One") driver. Antonelli extended his championship lead to 50 points over Hamilton and 59 over Russell, with Leclerc and Norris 81 and 91 points behind respectively heading into the summer break.

Following the summer break, the season resumed with the [Dutch Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Dutch_Grand_Prix "2026 Dutch Grand Prix"); it was the last to be hosted at [Circuit Zandvoort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_Zandvoort "Circuit Zandvoort"), and the first and only to be a sprint event. [Red Bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bull_Racing "Red Bull Racing") driver [Isack Hadjar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isack_Hadjar "Isack Hadjar") missed the event after sustaining a wrist injury during the summer break; [Liam Lawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liam_Lawson "Liam Lawson") was promoted from [Racing Bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racing_Bulls "Racing Bulls") to replace him, and reserve driver [Yuki Tsunoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuki_Tsunoda "Yuki Tsunoda") was called up to drive for Racing Bulls. Russell won the sprint with a lights-to-flag drive from pole, ahead of Leclerc and Norris. Norris responded in qualifying for the Grand Prix, taking pole position by a tenth over Russell. The Grand Prix was interrupted by a lengthy red flag following a heavy crash for Verstappen at turn 14 on the opening lap, the Dutchman emerged unharmed but had to retire from his home race. Antonelli took the lead at the ensuing [standing start](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_start "Standing start") when the race resumed; he led for a majority of the race, closely shadowed by Norris. On lap 54, Norris took advantage of Antonelli getting held up by Hamilton who was on an alternate strategy to pass him into turn 1, he then passed Hamilton later on the same lap and moved into the lead. Norris survived two further virtual safety car periods to take his second win in a row, with Antonelli finishing second and extending his championship lead to 59 points. Russell took the final spot on the podium after holding off the Ferraris of Hamilton and Leclerc, who finished fourth and fifth respectively.

[Alpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_F1_Team "Alpine F1 Team") driver [Pierre Gasly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Gasly "Pierre Gasly") claimed his first career pole position in qualifying for the [Italian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Italian_Grand_Prix "2026 Italian Grand Prix") ahead of Russell and [Oscar Piastri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Piastri "Oscar Piastri"), although Piastri received a three-place grid penalty for impeding [Liam Lawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liam_Lawson "Liam Lawson"). Leclerc crashed heavily at the Parabolica on the second lap of the Grand Prix, bringing out the red flag; he emerged unharmed but his car was completely destroyed. On the subsequent restart, Russell, who had taken the lead from Gasly before the red flag, led away but was soon hounded by Verstappen. Antonelli, who started down the order after taking a grid penalty for exceeding his quota of power unit elements, worked his way through the field and caught up to the leading pair. During a virtual safety car period caused by [Lance Stroll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Stroll "Lance Stroll") stopping on track with a hydraulic failure, Russell chose to stay out while Antonelli and Verstappen pitted for fresh tyres. Antonelli, making use of the substantial tyre offset, caught and passed his teammate in the closing stages of the race to win from nineteenth on the grid. Antonelli became the first driver since [John Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Watson_(racing_driver) "John Watson (racing driver)") at the [1983 United States Grand Prix West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_United_States_Grand_Prix_West "1983 United States Grand Prix West") to win from a starting position this low, and the first [Italian driver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_One_drivers_from_Italy "Formula One drivers from Italy") to win the Italian Grand Prix since [Ludovico Scarfiotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludovico_Scarfiotti "Ludovico Scarfiotti") [sixty years prior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_Italian_Grand_Prix "1966 Italian Grand Prix"). Verstappen completed the podium ahead of the [McLaren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McLaren "McLaren") duo of Norris and Piastri.

Norris claimed pole position for the inaugural running of the [Spanish Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Spanish_Grand_Prix "2026 Spanish Grand Prix") at the new [Madring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madring "Madring") circuit, edging out Antonelli by 0.011 seconds. He comfortably controlled the initial stages of the race, but lost out to both Antonelli and Verstappen after a poorly-timed virtual safety car and a slow pit stop. [Charles Leclerc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Leclerc "Charles Leclerc") led for a majority of the race by electing to stay out during the virtual safety car and doing a long stint on hard tyres, before pitting with 10 laps left to cover Russell. Antonelli took over the lead and won the race ahead of Verstappen, who held off Norris.

Russell claimed pole by 0.837 seconds for the [Azerbaijan Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Azerbaijan_Grand_Prix "2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix") and ended up leading the whole race to claim his first career [Grand Slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_(Formula_One) "Grand Slam (Formula One)"), albeit it came down to the wire between him Verstappen, finishing with a margin of 0.196 seconds, the closest margin between first and second since the [2002 United States Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_United_States_Grand_Prix "2002 United States Grand Prix"). From the moment the race started the Red Bull showed evident pace, as Verstappen and Hadjar quickly jumped from eighth and fourth to fourth and third, respectively. Hadjar would cede position to Verstappen, believing that he would have better pace to overtake Piastri for second. This would not occur until the first safety car restart, being caused by Albon crashing into the barriers. The safety car would quickly be redeployed after an incident involving Colapinto, Gasly, and Norris at turn 1, which would take all three out of the race. After the second restart, Verstappen and Russell would quickly accelerate away from the rest of the field. Piastri would lock up into turn 1 on lap 40, dropping back to fourteenth and conceding the final podium position to Hadjar. While there would be drama involving Bottas on the last lap, the podium would remain as Russell from Verstappen from Hadjar. This was Red Bull's first double podium since the [2024 Chinese Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Chinese_Grand_Prix "2024 Chinese Grand Prix"). Antonelli, having qualified sixteenth after an incident, would maintain a 66 point championship lead after finishing fifth.

### Closing rounds

Verstappen secured his and [Red Bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bull_Racing "Red Bull Racing")'s first pole position of the season at the [Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Bahrain_Grand_Prix_in_Malaysia "2026 Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia"), ahead of Hamilton and teammate Hadjar. It was the season's first pole position for a car not powered by a [Mercedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes_AMG_High_Performance_Powertrains "Mercedes AMG High Performance Powertrains") engine. Hadjar's five-place grid penalty for taking a new internal combustion engine dropped him to eighth, promoting championship leader Antonelli to third alongside Leclerc on the second row. Following heavy rain and two delays to the start, Verstappen recovered from losing the lead at the start to take his first victory of the season, ahead of Antonelli and Hamilton. The victory was the first for a [Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_in_Formula_One "Ford in Formula One")-powered car since the [2003 Brazilian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Brazilian_Grand_Prix "2003 Brazilian Grand Prix"). Hamilton fell to 18th after starting on slick tyres on the wet circuit, but recovered to secure third by passing Hadjar after the final safety-car restart; Leclerc also passed Hadjar to finish fourth. Russell retired while running third during the late safety-car period with a power-unit problem, allowing Antonelli to extend his championship lead over his teammate to 84 points with seven rounds remaining.

Verstappen dominated the sprint race at the [Singapore Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Singapore_Grand_Prix "2026 Singapore Grand Prix") from pole position. The sprint was delayed once again due to inclement weather. After taking the lead, Russell crashed out of the sprint, which was held in damp track conditions, with both Ferraris of Hamilton and Leclerc completing the podium. The McLarens spun while attacking each other on the final lap, which led to Norris and Piastri both spinning; a compromised Norris, who had been fighting for fourth, continued on while Piastri met the wall and retired on the spot. Verstappen dominated the Grand Prix to secure his first victory at the [Marina Bay Street Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina_Bay_Street_Circuit "Marina Bay Street Circuit"), and his second consecutive victory of the season.

### Scoring system

Points are awarded to the top ten classified drivers in the race and to the top eight finishers in the [sprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_One_racing#Sprint "Formula One racing"). In the case of a tie on points, a countback system is used where the driver with the most Grand Prix wins is ranked higher. If the number of wins is identical, the number of second places is considered, and so on. Points are awarded using the following system:

| Position | 1st | 2nd | 3rd | 4th | 5th | 6th | 7th | 8th | 9th | 10th |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Race | 25 | 18 | 15 | 12 | 10 | 8 | 6 | 4 | 2 | 1 |
| Sprint | 8 | 7 | 6 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 |  | |
