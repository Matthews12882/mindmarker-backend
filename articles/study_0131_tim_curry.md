# Tim Curry

**Timothy James Curry** (19 April 1946 – 25 August 2026) was an English actor and singer who amassed [more than 240 credits on screen and stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Tim_Curry_performances "List of Tim Curry performances") in a career that spanned nearly 60 years. Curry made his acting debut in the [West End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_End_theatre "West End theatre") production of *[Hair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hair_(musical) "Hair (musical)")* (1968–1969) and gained wide recognition for portraying [Dr. Frank-N-Furter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Frank-N-Furter "Dr. Frank-N-Furter") in *[The Rocky Horror Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rocky_Horror_Show "The Rocky Horror Show")* (1973–1975) and its 1975 film adaptation *[The Rocky Horror Picture Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rocky_Horror_Picture_Show "The Rocky Horror Picture Show")*. He received three [Tony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award "Tony Award") nominations for his [Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_(theatre) "Broadway (theatre)") performances in *[Amadeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amadeus_(play) "Amadeus (play)")* (1980–1981) as [Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart "Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart"), *[My Favorite Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Favorite_Year_(musical) "My Favorite Year (musical)")* (1992–1993) as Alan Swann, and *[Spamalot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spamalot "Spamalot")* (2005) as [King Arthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Arthur "King Arthur"); and two [Olivier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurence_Olivier_Awards "Laurence Olivier Awards") nominations for West End productions of *[The Pirates of Penzance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pirates_of_Penzance "The Pirates of Penzance")* (1982) and *Spamalot* (2006–2007).

In film, Curry starred as Rooster Hannigan in *[Annie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_(1982_film) "Annie (1982 film)")* (1982), the Lord of Darkness in *[Legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legend_(1985_film) "Legend (1985 film)")* (1985), Wadsworth in *[Clue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clue_(film) "Clue (film)")* (1985), the Concierge in *[Home Alone 2: Lost in New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Alone_2:_Lost_in_New_York "Home Alone 2: Lost in New York")* (1992), [Cardinal Richelieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_Richelieu "Cardinal Richelieu") in *[The Three Musketeers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Three_Musketeers_(1993_film) "The Three Musketeers (1993 film)")* (1993), and [Long John Silver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_John_Silver "Long John Silver") in *[Muppet Treasure Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muppet_Treasure_Island "Muppet Treasure Island")* (1996). Other film credits include *[The Worst Witch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Worst_Witch_(film) "The Worst Witch (film)")* (1986), *[The Hunt for Red October](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunt_for_Red_October_(film) "The Hunt for Red October (film)")* (1990), *[Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_(film) "Congo (film)")* (1995), *[Charlie's Angels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie%27s_Angels_(2000_film) "Charlie's Angels (2000 film)")* (2000), *[Scary Movie 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scary_Movie_2 "Scary Movie 2")* (2001), and *[Kinsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinsey_(film) "Kinsey (film)")* (2004). On television, he played [Pennywise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennywise "Pennywise") in the miniseries *[It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It_(miniseries) "It (miniseries)")* (1990) and earned a [Primetime Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Awards "Primetime Emmy Awards") nomination for his role as a family of hillbillies in the [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO "HBO") [anthology series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthology_series "Anthology series") *[Tales from the Crypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tales_from_the_Crypt_(TV_series) "Tales from the Crypt (TV series)")* (1993).

A prolific voice-over performer, Curry won a [Daytime Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daytime_Emmy_Awards "Daytime Emmy Awards") for voicing [Captain Hook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Hook "Captain Hook") on *[Peter Pan & the Pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Pan_%26_the_Pirates "Peter Pan & the Pirates")* (1990–1991) and was [Grammy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Spoken_Word_Album_for_Children "Grammy Award for Best Spoken Word Album for Children")-nominated for his [audiobook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audiobook "Audiobook") reading of *[A Series of Unfortunate Events: The Bad Beginning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bad_Beginning "The Bad Beginning")* (2001). He also voiced Hexxus in *[FernGully: The Last Rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FernGully:_The_Last_Rainforest "FernGully: The Last Rainforest")* (1992), Sir Nigel Thornberry in *[The Wild Thornberrys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wild_Thornberrys "The Wild Thornberrys")* (1998–2004), Doviculus in *[Brütal Legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%BCtal_Legend "Brütal Legend")* (2009), and [Chancellor Palpatine / Darth Sidious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palpatine "Palpatine") in *[Star Wars: The Clone Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars:_The_Clone_Wars "Star Wars: The Clone Wars")* (2012–2014). Outside of acting, he released three studio albums: *[Read My Lips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Read_My_Lips_(Tim_Curry_album) "Read My Lips (Tim Curry album)")* (1978), *[Fearless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fearless_(Tim_Curry_album) "Fearless (Tim Curry album)")* (1979), and *[Simplicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplicity_(Tim_Curry_album) "Simplicity (Tim Curry album)")* (1981), with the latter two charting on the US [*Billboard* 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200"). His memoir *Vagabond* was published in 2025, a year before his death.

## Early life and education

Timothy James Curry was born on 19 April 1946 in [Grappenhall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grappenhall "Grappenhall"), Cheshire, the son of school secretary Patricia (née Langmead) and [Royal Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Navy "Royal Navy") chaplain James Curry. He had an elder sister, Judith. Within a year of Curry's birth, the family moved to [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong "Hong Kong"). During his early childhood, the family moved to a different British seaside town every 18 months to follow his father's career, eventually settling in [Plymouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plymouth "Plymouth") when Curry was 11.

Curry's father suffered a stroke in 1958 and died several weeks later in the hospital from complications of [pneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumonia "Pneumonia"), aged 49. Curry later attended [Kingswood School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswood_School "Kingswood School") in [Bath, Somerset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bath,_Somerset "Bath, Somerset"). In church choirs, he developed into a [boy soprano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boy_soprano "Boy soprano"). Deciding to concentrate on acting, he graduated from the [University of Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Birmingham "University of Birmingham") with a combined [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") degree in English and drama in 1968.

## Career

### 1968–1975: Breakthrough with *Hair* and *The Rocky Horror Picture Show*

Curry's first full-time role was as part of the original London cast of the musical *[Hair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hair_(musical) "Hair (musical)")* in 1968, where he met [Richard O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_O%27Brien "Richard O'Brien"), who went on to write Curry's role of [Dr Frank-N-Furter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr_Frank-N-Furter "Dr Frank-N-Furter") in *[The Rocky Horror Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rocky_Horror_Show "The Rocky Horror Show")* (1973). After *Hair*, Curry continued to perform in theatre productions at venues such as the [Royal Court Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Court_Theatre "Royal Court Theatre") in London and the [Citizens Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens_Theatre "Citizens Theatre") in [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow "Glasgow").

Curry recalled his first encounter with *The Rocky Horror Show*:

> I'd heard about the play because I lived on [Paddington Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddington_Street "Paddington Street"), off [Baker Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baker_Street "Baker Street"), and there was an old gym a few doors away. I saw Richard O'Brien in the street, and he said he'd just been to the gym to see if he could find a muscleman who could sing. I said, "Why do you need him to sing?" [laughs] And he told me that his musical was going to be done, and I should talk to [Jim Sharman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Sharman "Jim Sharman"). He gave me the script, and I thought, "Boy, if this works, it's going to be a smash."

Originally, Curry rehearsed the character with a German [accent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accent_(sociolinguistics) "Accent (sociolinguistics)") and [peroxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peroxide "Peroxide") blond hair, and later, with an American accent. In March 2005, in an interview with [Terry Gross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Gross "Terry Gross") of [NPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR "NPR")'s *[Fresh Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresh_Air "Fresh Air")*, he explained that he decided to play Dr Frank-N-Furter with an English accent after listening to an English woman say, "Do you have a house in town or a house in the country?," and decided, "Yes, [Frank-N-Furter] should sound like the Queen."

He originally thought the character was merely a laboratory doctor dressed in a white lab coat. However, at the suggestion of director Sharman, the character evolved into the diabolical [mad scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mad_scientist "Mad scientist") and [transvestite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transvestite "Transvestite") with an upper-class [Belgravia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgravia "Belgravia") accent. An immediate hit, a reviewer at the premiere in London in June 1973 wrote that Curry gives a "garishly [Bowiesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowiesque "Bowiesque") performance as the ambisextrous doctor". This change carried over to the 1975 film adaptation, *[The Rocky Horror Picture Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rocky_Horror_Picture_Show "The Rocky Horror Picture Show")*, which made Curry a household name and gave him a [cult following](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult_following "Cult following"). Curry continued to play the character in London, Los Angeles, and New York City until 1975.

In an interview with *[Fresh Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresh_Air "Fresh Air")*, Curry called *Rocky Horror* a "[rite of passage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rite_of_passage "Rite of passage")", and added that the film is "a guaranteed weekend party to which you can go with or without a date and probably find one if you don't have one, and it's also a chance for people to try on a few roles for size, you know? Figure out, help them maybe figure out their own [sexuality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexuality "Sexuality")."

During the original run of *Rocky Horror*, Curry's television career also began to flourish. He played Eugene in *[Napoleon and Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_and_Love "Napoleon and Love")* (1974), and had guest roles in *[Armchair Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armchair_Theatre "Armchair Theatre")* and the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC")'s *[Play for Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Play_for_Today "Play for Today")*, the latter being a production of [Dennis Potter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Potter "Dennis Potter")'s teleplay *Schmoedipus* (1974).

### 1975–1989: Further acting and studio albums

Curry's first role after *Rocky Horror*'s run on Broadway was in [Stephen Frears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Frears "Stephen Frears")'s [1975 adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Men_in_a_Boat_(1975_film) "Three Men in a Boat (1975 film)") of *[Three Men in a Boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Men_in_a_Boat "Three Men in a Boat")*, scripted by [Tom Stoppard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Stoppard "Tom Stoppard"). This collaboration led to Curry's return to the stage with Stoppard's *[Travesties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travesties "Travesties")*, which ran in London and New York from 1975 to 1976. *Travesties* was a Broadway hit. It won two [Tony Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award "Tony Award") ([Best Performance by an Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Play "Tony Award for Best Actor in a Play") for [John Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wood_(English_actor) "John Wood (English actor)") and [Best Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Play "Tony Award for Best Play")), as well as the [New York Drama Critics Circle Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Drama_Critics_Circle_Award "New York Drama Critics Circle Award") (Best Play), and Curry's performance as the famous [dadaist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadaist "Dadaist") [Tristan Tzara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tristan_Tzara "Tristan Tzara") received good reviews.

Around this time, Curry launched his solo musical artist career. Curry received classical vocal training as a boy. He mentioned that his musical influences included jazz vocalists such as [Billie Holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billie_Holiday "Billie Holiday") and [Louis Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Armstrong "Louis Armstrong"), and he idolised [The Beatles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beatles "The Beatles") and [The Rolling Stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rolling_Stones "The Rolling Stones") as a teenager. He first recorded a nine-track album for [Ode Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ode_Records "Ode Records") in 1976. However, the album remained unreleased in its entirety until February 2010, when it was made available as a legal download entitled *...From the Vaults*. The album, produced by [Lou Adler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Adler "Lou Adler"), included Curry's rendition of [the Supremes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Supremes "The Supremes")' hit "[Baby Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby_Love "Baby Love")". In 1978, [A&M Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%26M_Records "A&M Records") released Curry's official debut solo album *[Read My Lips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Read_My_Lips_(Tim_Curry_album) "Read My Lips (Tim Curry album)")*. The album featured an eclectic range of songs (mostly [covers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cover_version "Cover version")) performed in diverse genres. Highlights of the album are a [reggae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggae "Reggae") version of the Beatles' song "[I Will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Will "I Will")," a rendition of "Wake Nicodemus" featuring the Pipes and Drums of the [48th Highlanders of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/48th_Highlanders_of_Canada "48th Highlanders of Canada"), and a barroom ballad, "Alan", composed by Canadian singer-songwriter Tony Kosinec. The following year, Curry released his second and most successful album *[Fearless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fearless_(Tim_Curry_album) "Fearless (Tim Curry album)")*. The [LP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gramophone_record "Gramophone record") was more rock-oriented than *Read My Lips* and mostly featured original songs rather than cover versions. The album included Curry's only US [*Billboard* Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100 "Billboard Hot 100") charting song: "I Do the Rock". Curry toured North America and some European countries with his band between 1978 and 1980. Curry's third album, *[Simplicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplicity_(Tim_Curry_album) "Simplicity (Tim Curry album)")*, was released in 1981, again by A&M Records. This record, which did not sell as well as the previous offerings, combined both original songs and cover versions. Still, it was the only Curry recording to hit the charts in Canada, reaching number 45 on the album chart.

In 1980, Curry formed part of the original cast in [Peter Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Hall_(director) "Peter Hall (director)")'s Broadway production of [Peter Shaffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Shaffer "Peter Shaffer")'s *[Amadeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amadeus_(play) "Amadeus (play)")*, playing the title character, [Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart "Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart"). Curry was nominated for his first [Tony Award (Best Performance by a Leading Actor in a Play)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Play "Tony Award for Best Actor in a Play") for this role but lost out to his co-star [Ian McKellen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_McKellen "Ian McKellen"), who played [Antonio Salieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Salieri "Antonio Salieri"). In 1982, Curry returned to the London stage to play the Pirate King in the [Drury Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_Royal,_Drury_Lane "Theatre Royal, Drury Lane") production of [Joe Papp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Papp "Joe Papp")'s version of [Gilbert and Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_and_Sullivan "Gilbert and Sullivan")'s *[The Pirates of Penzance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pirates_of_Penzance "The Pirates of Penzance")* opposite [George Cole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Cole_(actor) "George Cole (actor)") and [Pamela Stephenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamela_Stephenson "Pamela Stephenson"), earning enthusiastic reviews. In 1983, Curry performed in *[The Rivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rivals "The Rivals")* at the [Royal National Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_National_Theatre "Royal National Theatre") of Great Britain, alongside [Michael Hordern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Hordern "Michael Hordern"), [Geraldine McEwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geraldine_McEwan "Geraldine McEwan"), [Anne Louise Lambert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Louise_Lambert "Anne Louise Lambert"), and [Fiona Shaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiona_Shaw "Fiona Shaw"). From 1985 to 1986 he performed at the National as [Macheath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macheath "Macheath") in *[The Threepenny Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Threepenny_Opera "The Threepenny Opera")*, in Stoppard's *[Dalliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalliance_(play) "Dalliance (play)")*, and *[Love For Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_For_Love "Love For Love")*. All four National productions were directed by his *Travesties* director [Peter Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Wood_(director) "Peter Wood (director)").

Curry had further roles in film and television throughout the 1970s and 1980s, including [William Shakespeare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Shakespeare "William Shakespeare") in the [John Mortimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Mortimer "John Mortimer")-scripted mini-series *[Will Shakespeare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Shakespeare_(TV_series) "Will Shakespeare (TV series)")* (1978), [Robert Graves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Graves "Robert Graves") in the [Jerzy Skolimowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerzy_Skolimowski "Jerzy Skolimowski") [horror film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horror_film "Horror film") *[The Shout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Shout "The Shout")* (1978), Leonard Brazil in [Stephen Poliakoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Poliakoff "Stephen Poliakoff")'s *[City Sugar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Sugar "City Sugar")* (1978), Johnny LaGuardia in *[Times Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square_(1980_film) "Times Square (1980 film)")* (1980), Daniel Francis "Rooster" Hannigan in [John Huston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Huston "John Huston")'s 1982 film version of *[Annie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_(1982_film) "Annie (1982 film)")*, [Bill Sikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Sikes "Bill Sikes") in *[Oliver Twist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Twist_(1982_TV_film) "Oliver Twist (1982 TV film)")* (1982), Jeremy Hancock in [Richard Eyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Eyre "Richard Eyre")'s political film *[The Ploughman's Lunch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ploughman%27s_Lunch "The Ploughman's Lunch")* (1983) written by [Ian McEwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_McEwan "Ian McEwan"), aspiring actor-singer Larry Gormley in *[Blue Money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Money_(1985_film) "Blue Money (1985 film)")* (1985), and as the Grand Wizard in the children's [Halloween](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halloween "Halloween") film *[The Worst Witch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Worst_Witch_(film) "The Worst Witch (film)")* (1986). In 1985, Curry starred in the [fantasy film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasy_film "Fantasy film") *[Legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legend_(1985_film) "Legend (1985 film)")* as The Lord of Darkness. Director [Ridley Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridley_Scott "Ridley Scott") cast Curry in the film after watching him in *Rocky Horror*, thinking he was ideal to play the role of Darkness. It took five and a half hours to apply the makeup needed for Darkness onto Curry, and at the end of the day, he would spend an hour in a bath to liquefy the soluble spirit gum. The same year, Curry appeared in the [comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comedy_film "Comedy film") [mystery film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mystery_film "Mystery film") *[Clue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clue_(film) "Clue (film)")* as Wadsworth the butler.

From 1987 to 1988, he did the American National Tour of *[Me and My Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Me_and_My_Girl "Me and My Girl")* in the lead role of Bill Snibson, a role originated on Broadway by [Robert Lindsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Lindsay_(actor) "Robert Lindsay (actor)"). In 1989, Curry returned once again to the New York stage in the [Off-Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-Broadway "Off-Broadway") production of *[The Art of Success](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Art_of_Success "The Art of Success")*.

In 1989, [A&M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%26M_Records "A&M Records") released *The Best of Tim Curry* on CD and cassette, featuring songs from his albums (including a live version of "Alan") and a previously unreleased song, a live cover version of [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan "Bob Dylan")'s "[Simple Twist of Fate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_Twist_of_Fate "Simple Twist of Fate")".

### 1989–2003: Hollywood career

Curry moved permanently to the United States in the late 1980s and began to appear more frequently in [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood,_Los_Angeles "Hollywood, Los Angeles") film productions, including comedic roles such as Dr Thornton Poole in [John Landis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Landis "John Landis")'s *[Oscar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_(1991_film) "Oscar (1991 film)")* (1991), the suspicious [Plaza Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Hotel "Plaza Hotel") [concierge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concierge "Concierge") in *[Home Alone 2: Lost in New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Alone_2:_Lost_in_New_York "Home Alone 2: Lost in New York")* (1992), Jigsaw in *[Loaded Weapon 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loaded_Weapon_1 "Loaded Weapon 1")* (1993), and [Long John Silver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_John_Silver "Long John Silver") in *[Muppet Treasure Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muppet_Treasure_Island "Muppet Treasure Island")* (1996). Curry also appeared in action films, such as the [John McTiernan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McTiernan "John McTiernan")-directed [thriller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thriller_film "Thriller film") *[The Hunt for Red October](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunt_for_Red_October_(film) "The Hunt for Red October (film)")* (1990) as Dr Yevgeni Petrov, the [1993 adaptation of *The Three Musketeers*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Three_Musketeers_(1993_film) "The Three Musketeers (1993 film)") as [Cardinal Richelieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_Richelieu "Cardinal Richelieu"), the [superhero film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superhero_film "Superhero film") *[The Shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Shadow_(1994_film) "The Shadow (1994 film)")* (1994) as Farley Claymore, and the adventure film *[Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_(film) "Congo (film)")* (1995) as Romanian philanthropist Herkermer Homolka. He also starred in the 1998 direct-to-video film *[Addams Family Reunion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addams_Family_Reunion "Addams Family Reunion")* playing [Gomez Addams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomez_Addams "Gomez Addams").

Focusing on film, Curry's stage performances decreased, but he did perform as the Prosecutor in [Roger Waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Waters "Roger Waters")'s 1990 production of *[The Wall – Live in Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_%E2%80%93_Live_in_Berlin "The Wall – Live in Berlin")*, and in 1992 he played Alan Swann in the Broadway version of *[My Favorite Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Favorite_Year_(musical) "My Favorite Year (musical)")*, earning him his second Tony Award nomination, this time for [Best Performance by a Leading Actor in a Musical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Musical "Tony Award for Best Actor in a Musical"). In 2001, Curry returned to the theatre, appearing as [Scrooge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebenezer_Scrooge "Ebenezer Scrooge") in a [musical version](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Christmas_Carol_(musical) "A Christmas Carol (musical)") of [Charles Dickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens "Charles Dickens")'s *[A Christmas Carol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Christmas_Carol "A Christmas Carol")* which played at [Madison Square Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden "Madison Square Garden").

On American television during this time, he appeared in the "Dead Dog Records" storyline of the television series [crime drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_drama "Crime drama") *[Wiseguy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiseguy_(TV_series) "Wiseguy (TV series)")* (1989) as Winston Newquay, and was nominated for a [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Guest Actor in a Drama Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Guest_Actor_in_a_Drama_Series "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Guest Actor in a Drama Series") for portraying all three members of a deranged family in *[Tales from the Crypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tales_from_the_Crypt_(TV_series) "Tales from the Crypt (TV series)")* (1993). He also had recurring roles on the short-lived [science fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction "Science fiction") television series *[Earth 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_2_(TV_series) "Earth 2 (TV series)")* (1994), and the sitcom *[Rude Awakening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rude_Awakening_(TV_series) "Rude Awakening (TV series)")* (1999–2000). Although Curry appeared in numerous television series throughout his career, he only had lead roles in two live-action series: *[Over the Top](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over_the_Top_(TV_series) "Over the Top (TV series)")* (1997), a [sitcom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitcom "Sitcom") that he also produced, and the revival series of *[Family Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Affair_(2002_TV_series) "Family Affair (2002 TV series)")* (2002–2003). Both were cancelled after one season. One of Curry's best-known television roles is as [Pennywise the Clown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennywise "Pennywise") in the [horror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horror_fiction "Horror fiction") miniseries *[Stephen King's It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King%27s_It "Stephen King's It")* (1990). Aside from one *[Fangoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fangoria "Fangoria")* interview in 1990, Curry never publicly acknowledged his involvement in *It* until an interview with [Moviefone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moviefone "Moviefone") in 2015, where he called the role of Pennywise "a wonderful part".

When he moved to America, Curry started performing [voice overs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_over "Voice over") for many animated productions, video games and audiobooks, starting with the performance of the [Serpent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serpents_in_the_Bible#Eden "Serpents in the Bible") in *[The Greatest Adventure: Stories from the Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Greatest_Adventure:_Stories_from_the_Bible "The Greatest Adventure: Stories from the Bible")* (1988). Curry won a [Daytime Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daytime_Emmy_Award "Daytime Emmy Award") for his performance as [Captain Hook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Hook "Captain Hook") in the [Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company "Fox Broadcasting Company") animated series *[Peter Pan and the Pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Pan_and_the_Pirates "Peter Pan and the Pirates")* (1990–1991) and received a second Daytime Emmy nomination for voicing Skullmaster in *[Mighty Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mighty_Max_(TV_series) "Mighty Max (TV series)")* (1993–1994). His longest-running animated role was as Nigel Thornberry in *[The Wild Thornberrys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wild_Thornberrys "The Wild Thornberrys")* (1998–2004), which ran for five seasons on [Nickelodeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon "Nickelodeon"). Other voice roles include MAL in *[Captain Planet and the Planeteers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Planet_and_the_Planeteers "Captain Planet and the Planeteers")* (1991–1996), the titular character in the video games *[Gabriel Knight: Sins of the Fathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Knight:_Sins_of_the_Fathers "Gabriel Knight: Sins of the Fathers")* (1993) and *[Gabriel Knight 3: Blood of the Sacred, Blood of the Damned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Knight_3:_Blood_of_the_Sacred,_Blood_of_the_Damned "Gabriel Knight 3: Blood of the Sacred, Blood of the Damned")* (1999), Zimbo in *[Aaahh!!! Real Monsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaahh!!!_Real_Monsters "Aaahh!!! Real Monsters")* (1994–1997), King Chicken in *[Duckman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duckman "Duckman")* (1994–1997), Dr Anton Sevarius in *[Gargoyles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gargoyles_(TV_series) "Gargoyles (TV series)")* (1995–1996), Slagar the Cruel in *[Redwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redwall_(TV_series) "Redwall (TV series)")* (2000–2001), and Professor Finbarr Calamitous in *[The Adventures of Jimmy Neutron: Boy Genius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Adventures_of_Jimmy_Neutron:_Boy_Genius "The Adventures of Jimmy Neutron: Boy Genius")* (2003–2005). Curry also did voice acting in a number of animated films, such as *[FernGully: The Last Rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FernGully:_The_Last_Rainforest "FernGully: The Last Rainforest")* (1992); [Don Bluth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Bluth "Don Bluth")'s *[The Pebble and the Penguin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pebble_and_the_Penguin "The Pebble and the Penguin")* (1995); *[Beauty and the Beast: The Enchanted Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beauty_and_the_Beast:_The_Enchanted_Christmas "Beauty and the Beast: The Enchanted Christmas")* (1997), for which he received an [Annie Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_Award "Annie Award") nomination; *[Scooby-Doo! and the Witch's Ghost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scooby-Doo!_and_the_Witch%27s_Ghost "Scooby-Doo! and the Witch's Ghost")* (1999); the first [Barbie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbie "Barbie") film, *[Barbie in the Nutcracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbie_in_the_Nutcracker "Barbie in the Nutcracker")* (2001); and reprising his role of Nigel Thornberry in *[The Wild Thornberrys Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wild_Thornberrys_Movie "The Wild Thornberrys Movie")* (2002) and *[Rugrats Go Wild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugrats_Go_Wild "Rugrats Go Wild")* (2003). Curry's audiobook work includes the [Richard Jury mystery series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Jury "Richard Jury"), [Anne Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Rice "Anne Rice")'s *[Cry to Heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cry_to_Heaven "Cry to Heaven")*, the *[Prelude to Dune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prelude_to_Dune "Prelude to Dune")* prequel trilogy, the original *[The Old Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Old_Kingdom "The Old Kingdom")* trilogy, and his [Grammy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award "Grammy Award")-nominated narration of [Lemony Snicket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemony_Snicket "Lemony Snicket")'s *[A Series of Unfortunate Events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Series_of_Unfortunate_Events "A Series of Unfortunate Events")*.

In the early 2000s, Curry's big screen appearances lessened, but he did portray Roger Corwin in the [film adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie%27s_Angels_(2000_film) "Charlie's Angels (2000 film)") of *[Charlie's Angels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie%27s_Angels "Charlie's Angels")* (2000), Professor Oldman in the [parody film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parody_film "Parody film") *[Scary Movie 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scary_Movie_2 "Scary Movie 2")* (2001), and Thurman Rice in the [biographical film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biographical_film "Biographical film") *[Kinsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinsey_(film) "Kinsey (film)")* (2004).

### 2004–2012: *Spamalot* and later roles

In 2004, Curry began his role of [King Arthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Arthur "King Arthur") in *[Spamalot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spamalot "Spamalot")* in Chicago. Directed by [Mike Nichols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Nichols "Mike Nichols"), written by [Monty Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monty_Python "Monty Python") member [Eric Idle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Idle "Eric Idle") and based on *[Monty Python and the Holy Grail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monty_Python_and_the_Holy_Grail "Monty Python and the Holy Grail")*, the show successfully moved to Broadway in February 2005. It sold more than $1 million worth of tickets in its first 24 hours. His performance brought him a third Tony nomination, again for Best Performance by a Leading Actor in a Musical. Curry reprised this role at the [Palace Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_Theatre,_London "Palace Theatre, London") in the [West End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_End_theatre "West End theatre"), where *Spamalot* opened on 16 October 2006. His final performance came on 6 January 2007. Curry was nominated for a [Laurence Olivier Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurence_Olivier_Award "Laurence Olivier Award") for his work in *Spamalot*, and he also won the [Theatregoers' Choice Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatregoers%27_Choice_Award "Theatregoers' Choice Award") (receiving 39% of the votes cast by over 12,000 theatregoers) as Best Actor in a Musical.

After *Spamalot*, he returned to working on British television, with the miniseries adaptation of *[Terry Pratchett's The Colour of Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Pratchett%27s_The_Colour_of_Magic "Terry Pratchett's The Colour of Magic")* (2008), a guest appearance on the long-running *[Agatha Christie's Poirot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agatha_Christie%27s_Poirot "Agatha Christie's Poirot")* (2008), and the miniseries *[Return to Cranford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_to_Cranford "Return to Cranford")* (2009). Curry also made a [single-episode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Duos "American Duos") appearance on the comedy-detective series *[Psych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psych "Psych")* (2007), directed by his *Oscar* director John Landis. His final episodic television role was in 2010 on *[Criminal Minds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_Minds "Criminal Minds")*, portraying unsub Billy Flynn in two episodes. Curry's voice work also continued, with the English dub of [Studio Ghibli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio_Ghibli "Studio Ghibli")'s *[The Cat Returns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cat_Returns "The Cat Returns")* (2005); *[Garfield: A Tail of Two Kitties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garfield:_A_Tail_of_Two_Kitties "Garfield: A Tail of Two Kitties")* (2006); [Tim Schafer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Schafer "Tim Schafer")'s video game *[Brütal Legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%BCtal_Legend "Brütal Legend")* (2009); the first game in the *[Dragon Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Age "Dragon Age")* series, *[Dragon Age: Origins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Age:_Origins "Dragon Age: Origins")* (2009); [G. Gordon Godfrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._Gordon_Godfrey "G. Gordon Godfrey") in *[Young Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Justice_(TV_series) "Young Justice (TV series)")* (2012–2013); and the Sorcerer in *[Randy Cunningham: 9th Grade Ninja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Cunningham:_9th_Grade_Ninja "Randy Cunningham: 9th Grade Ninja")* (2012–2014). The latter two roles were recast after Curry had his stroke. He also became the voice of [Chancellor Palpatine/Darth Sidious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palpatine "Palpatine") in *[Star Wars: The Clone Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars:_The_Clone_Wars "Star Wars: The Clone Wars")* upon the death of [Ian Abercrombie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Abercrombie "Ian Abercrombie"). Curry's performance as Premier Anatoly Cherdenko in the [live-action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live-action "Live-action") [cut scenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cut_scenes "Cut scenes") in *[Command & Conquer: Red Alert 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_%26_Conquer:_Red_Alert_3 "Command & Conquer: Red Alert 3")* (2008) went [viral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_phenomenon "Viral phenomenon") as a [meme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meme "Meme").

His last major role in a feature film was as [Alexander Monro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Monro_(tertius) "Alexander Monro (tertius)") in the British [black comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_comedy "Black comedy") *[Burke & Hare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burke_%26_Hare_(2010_film) "Burke & Hare (2010 film)")* (2010), reuniting him once again with director John Landis. From May to August 2011, Curry was scheduled to portray the Player in a [Trevor Nunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trevor_Nunn "Trevor Nunn") stage production of Tom Stoppard's *[Rosencrantz and Guildenstern Are Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosencrantz_and_Guildenstern_Are_Dead "Rosencrantz and Guildenstern Are Dead")* at the [Chichester Festival Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chichester_Festival_Theatre "Chichester Festival Theatre") and then in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London"). This would be his fourth time performing a Stoppard piece. Curry withdrew from the production on 27 May, citing ill health but later admitting that he was fired for being unable to memorise his lines. From 26 to 29 April 2012, he appeared in Eric Idle's play *[What About Dick?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_About_Dick%3F "What About Dick?")* at the [Orpheum Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orpheum_Theatre_(Los_Angeles) "Orpheum Theatre (Los Angeles)") in Los Angeles. Curry had originally appeared at a script reading for the play back in 2007 when it was still a work in progress.

### 2013–2026: Final years

Following his 2012 stroke, Curry continued to work in voice-over, portraying Auntie Whispers on the [Primetime Creative Arts Emmy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Arts_Emmy_Awards "Creative Arts Emmy Awards")-winning miniseries *[Over the Garden Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over_the_Garden_Wall "Over the Garden Wall")* (2014) and narrating an advertisement for [Paramount Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Network "Paramount Network"). He also occasionally performed as a singer alongside [Jamie Donnelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Donnelly "Jamie Donnelly") at the Rockwell Table & Stage cabaret venue in Los Angeles, singing Broadway tunes and original music. Curry's career in theatre was honoured on 7 June 2015 at the [Actors Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actors_Fund "Actors Fund")'s 19th annual Tony Awards Viewing Party, where he was awarded an Artistic Achievement Award.

In 2016, Curry played the [Criminologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminologist "Criminologist") in the [television film remake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rocky_Horror_Picture_Show:_Let%27s_Do_the_Time_Warp_Again "The Rocky Horror Picture Show: Let's Do the Time Warp Again") of *The Rocky Horror Picture Show*. This was his last onscreen performance. Four years later, Curry reprised his role as Dr Frank-N-Furter in *The Rocky Horror Show Livestream*, a read-through of the show in support of the [Democratic Party of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_of_Wisconsin "Democratic Party of Wisconsin") to raise funds for [Joe Biden's presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden_2020_presidential_campaign "Joe Biden 2020 presidential campaign"). In 2025, Curry was an invited guest speaker at the [Academy Museum of Motion Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Museum_of_Motion_Pictures "Academy Museum of Motion Pictures") to celebrate the 50th anniversary of *The Rocky Horror Picture Show*.

During 2024, Curry made a voice cameo appearance in the mid-credits of the horror film *[Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stream_(film) "Stream (film)")*, portraying the masked character "Lockwood". Before dying in 2026, Curry completed work on *Stream 2: Sudden Death* and also a [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") animated special reimagining of *[Barbie in the Nutcracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbie_in_the_Nutcracker "Barbie in the Nutcracker")* (2026).

Curry's memoir *Vagabond* was released by [Grand Central Publishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Central_Publishing "Grand Central Publishing") on 14 October 2025, for which he also read the audiobook. Asked by a fan about the origin of the title, Curry said: "because in the 17th century in the criminal code, actors were lumped together with thieves and vagabonds".

## Personal life

Curry lived in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles") from 1988 until his death in 2026. He considered his love life private, and never married or had children.

As a keen [horticulturalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horticulturalist "Horticulturalist"), Curry developed and restored gardens across many of his past and other residences in Los Angeles. On where his interest in gardening came from, he commented: "I think if you're English, a [trowel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trowel "Trowel") appears in your hand when you're 30 like a prosthetic device".

A heavy [cigarette smoker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_smoking "Tobacco smoking"), Curry stated that he had used the drugs [hashish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashish "Hashish") and [cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine "Cocaine"). He also spent time in [alcohol rehabilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_rehabilitation "Drug rehabilitation"), but did not consider himself an [alcoholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholism "Alcoholism").

## Health and death

On 13 July 2012, while receiving a massage, Curry suffered a major stroke which required brain surgery. He used a wheelchair afterwards and continued to experience speech and mobility issues, particularly in his left arm and leg. It also affected his short-term memory. In September 2025, Curry said he was still unable to walk, but his facial control had visibly improved.

In an interview with *[CBS Sunday Morning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Sunday_Morning "CBS Sunday Morning")* in October 2025, Curry said "I don't fear death. I try to avoid it, as I think we all do. But, I suspect that in the end, I will welcome it." He died on 25 August 2026 at his home in [Toluca Lake, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toluca_Lake,_Los_Angeles "Toluca Lake, Los Angeles"), at the age of 80. His cause of death was later disclosed as [coronary artery disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronary_artery_disease "Coronary artery disease"), with his stroke and [kidney cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidney_cancer "Kidney cancer") being contributing factors.

## Reputation

Following his death, *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* praised Curry's "gift for garish eccentricity" while also noting that he was a "favorite in roles calling for expressive villainy or general cartoonishness". [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN "CNN") called Curry a "massively popular cult figure" for his roles across a variety of genres. *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")* also called Curry a "genuine pop-culture icon" who had "assured a place in cinema-history books".
