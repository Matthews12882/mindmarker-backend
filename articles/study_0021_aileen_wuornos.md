# Aileen Wuornos

**Aileen Carol Wuornos** ([/ˈwɔːrnoʊs/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"); [née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") **Pittman**; February 29, 1956 – October 9, 2002) was an American [serial killer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serial_killer "Serial killer"). Between 1989 and 1990, while engaging in [street prostitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_prostitution "Street prostitution") along highways in [Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida "Florida"), Wuornos shot, killed, and robbed seven of her male clients. She initially claimed that her victims had either [raped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rape "Rape") or attempted to rape her, and that the homicides were committed in [self-defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-defense "Self-defense"), but later abandoned this defense. Wuornos was [sentenced to death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_Florida "Capital punishment in Florida") for six of the murders and was executed in 2002 after spending more than ten years on Florida's [death row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_row "Death row").

## Early life

Aileen Carol Pittman was born on February 29, 1956, in [Rochester, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_Michigan "Rochester, Michigan"), a suburb of [Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit "Detroit"). Her mother, Diane Wuornos, was 14 years old when she married Aileen's father, 18-year-old Leo Pittman, on June 3, 1954. On March 14, 1955, Diane gave birth to Aileen's older brother Keith. After less than two years of marriage, and two months before Aileen was born, Diane filed for [divorce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divorce "Divorce"). She gave birth to Aileen at the age of 16.

In January 1960, when Wuornos was almost four years old, Diane abandoned her children, leaving them with their maternal grandparents, Lauri and Britta Wuornos, who legally adopted Keith and Aileen on March 18, 1960. Both grandparents had [alcoholism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholism "Alcoholism"). Wuornos never met her father. In 1967, Leo Pittman was sentenced to [life imprisonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_imprisonment "Life imprisonment") for kidnapping and raping a seven-year-old girl. Pittman, diagnosed with [schizophrenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schizophrenia "Schizophrenia"), died by [suicide by hanging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_by_hanging "Suicide by hanging") in prison on January 30, 1969.

Wuornos' childhood friend stated that she personally witnessed Wuornos' grandfather beating her with a belt for five minutes. Wuornos also said that her grandfather had sexually assaulted her and forced her to undress before beating her. By age 11, Wuornos began engaging in sexual activity at school in exchange for cigarettes, drugs, and food. She had also engaged in sexual activity [with her brother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incest "Incest"). In 1970, at age 14, she became pregnant after being [raped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rape "Rape") by a friend of her grandfather. Wuornos' friends have claimed that the rapist who impregnated her was a local pedophile in his 60s.

Wuornos gave birth to a boy at a [home for unwed mothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_for_unwed_mothers "Home for unwed mothers") on March 23, 1971, and her grandfather forced her to give up the child for [adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adoption "Adoption"). A few months after her son was born, she dropped out of school around the same time that her grandmother died of [liver failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liver_failure "Liver failure"). When Wuornos was aged 15, her grandfather threw her out of the house. Living in the woods near her old home, Wuornos supported herself through [prostitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostitution "Prostitution").

## Early criminal activity

On May 27, 1974, at age 18, Wuornos was arrested in [Jefferson County, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_County,_Colorado "Jefferson County, Colorado"), for [driving under the influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driving_under_the_influence "Driving under the influence") (DUI), [disorderly conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disorderly_conduct "Disorderly conduct"), and firing a [.22-caliber pistol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pistol "Pistol") from a moving vehicle. She was later charged with [failure to appear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Failure_to_appear "Failure to appear").

In 1976, Wuornos hitchhiked to [Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida "Florida"), where she met 69-year-old yacht club president Lewis Gratz Fell. They married quickly, and the announcement of their nuptials was printed in the local newspaper's society pages. Wuornos continually involved herself in confrontations at their local bar and went to jail briefly for [assault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assault "Assault"). She also hit Fell with his own cane, leading him to gain a [restraining order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restraining_order "Restraining order") against her within weeks of the marriage. She returned to Michigan where, on July 14, 1976, she was arrested at Bernie's Club in [Mancelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mancelona,_Michigan "Mancelona, Michigan"), [Antrim County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antrim_County,_Michigan "Antrim County, Michigan") and charged with assault and [disturbing the peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breach_of_the_peace "Breach of the peace") for throwing a [cue ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cue_ball "Cue ball") at a bartender's head.

On July 17, her brother Keith died of [esophageal cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esophageal_cancer "Esophageal cancer") and Wuornos received $10,000 (equivalent to $57,000 in 2025) from his [life insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_insurance "Life insurance"). Wuornos and Fell annulled their marriage on July 21 after only nine weeks. In August 1976, Wuornos was given a $105 fine for drunk driving. She used the money inherited from her brother to pay the fine, and bought a car, which she wrecked shortly afterwards. She spent the rest of the money within 2 months.

In 1978, at age 22, she attempted suicide by shooting herself in the stomach. Between ages 14 and 22, she attempted suicide six times. On May 20, 1981, Wuornos was arrested in [Edgewater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgewater,_Volusia_County,_Florida "Edgewater, Volusia County, Florida"), Florida, for the [armed robbery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_robbery "Armed robbery") of a convenience store, where she stole $35 and two packs of cigarettes. She was sentenced to prison on May 4, 1982, and released on June 30, 1983. On May 1, 1984, Wuornos was arrested for attempting to pass forged checks at a bank in [Key West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Key_West,_Florida "Key West, Florida"). On November 30, 1985, she was named as a suspect in the theft of a [revolver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolver "Revolver") and ammunition in [Pasco County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasco_County,_Florida "Pasco County, Florida").

On January 4, 1986, Wuornos was arrested in [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami "Miami") and charged with [car theft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car_theft "Car theft"), [resisting arrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resisting_arrest "Resisting arrest"), and [obstruction of justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obstruction_of_justice "Obstruction of justice") for providing identification bearing her aunt's name. Miami police officers found a [.38 caliber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.38_caliber ".38 caliber") revolver and a box of ammunition in the stolen car. On June 2, 1986, [Volusia County deputy sheriffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volusia_Sheriff%27s_Office "Volusia Sheriff's Office") detained Wuornos for questioning after a male companion accused her of pulling a gun in his car and demanding $200. Wuornos was found to be carrying spare ammunition, and police discovered a .22-caliber pistol under the passenger seat she had occupied.

In 1986, 30-year-old Wuornos met 24-year-old Tyria Moore, a motel maid, at a [Daytona Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daytona_Beach,_Florida "Daytona Beach, Florida") gay bar called Zodiac. They moved in together, and Wuornos supported them with her earnings as a prostitute. On July 4, 1987, Daytona Beach police detained Wuornos and Moore at a bar for questioning regarding an incident in which they were accused of assault and battery with a beer bottle.

On March 12, 1988, Wuornos accused a Daytona Beach bus driver of assault. She claimed that he pushed her off the bus following a confrontation. Moore was listed as a witness to the incident. Later, at her trial, Wuornos stated, "It was love beyond imaginable. Earthly words cannot describe how I felt about Tyria." Before her execution, Wuornos claimed to still be in love with Moore.

## Series of murders

Wuornos murdered seven men within 12 months. All the men were motorists between ages 40 and 65.

* **Richard Charles Mallory**, age 51, electronics store owner in [Clearwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clearwater,_Florida "Clearwater, Florida") (date of murder: November 30, 1989). Wuornos claimed that Mallory beat, [raped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rape "Rape"), and [sodomized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anal_sex "Anal sex") her after he drove her to an abandoned area for sexual services. Mallory was Wuornos' first victim and she claimed to have killed him in [self-defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-defense "Self-defense"). Later, it became known that Mallory had previously been convicted of attempted rape in [Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland "Maryland"). Wuornos made no mention of this until she stood trial, and Moore never claimed she mentioned it to her. Two days after the murder, a Volusia County deputy sheriff found Mallory's abandoned vehicle. On December 13, his body was found several miles away in a wooded area; he had been shot several times, and two bullets to the left lung were found to have been the cause of death.
* **David Andrew Spears**, age 47, construction worker in [Winter Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_Garden,_Florida "Winter Garden, Florida"). He was declared missing as of May 19, 1990. On June 1, 1990, his naked body was found along [US 19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_19_in_Florida "U.S. Route 19 in Florida") in [Citrus County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrus_County,_Florida "Citrus County, Florida"). He had been shot six times with a .22 pistol.
* **Charles Edmund Carskaddon**, age 40, part-time [rodeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodeo "Rodeo") worker (date of murder: May 31, 1990). On June 6, 1990, his naked body was found in [Pasco County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasco_County,_Florida "Pasco County, Florida"). He had been shot nine times with a .22 caliber weapon. The body had been wrapped in an electric blanket and was badly decomposed. Witnesses saw Wuornos in possession of Carskaddon's car, and Wuornos had pawned a gun identified as belonging to Carskaddon.
* **Peter Abraham Siems**, age 65, retired merchant seaman. In June 1990, Siems left [Jupiter, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter,_Florida "Jupiter, Florida"), for [Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas "Arkansas"). On July 4, 1990, his car was found in [Orange Springs, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Springs,_Florida "Orange Springs, Florida"). Moore and Wuornos were seen abandoning the car, and Wuornos' handprint was found on the interior door handle. His body was never found.
* **Troy Eugene Burress**, age 50, sausage salesman from [Ocala, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocala,_Florida "Ocala, Florida"). On July 31, 1990, he was reported missing. On August 4, 1990, his body was found in a wooded area along State Road 19 in [Marion County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marion_County,_Florida "Marion County, Florida"). He had been shot twice.
* **Charles Richard "Dick" Humphreys**, age 56, retired [United States Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force "United States Air Force") [major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_(United_States) "Major (United States)"), former state [child abuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_abuse "Child abuse") investigator, and former [chief of police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_police "Chief of police") (date of murder: September 11, 1990). On September 12, 1990, his body was found in [Marion County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marion_County,_Florida "Marion County, Florida"). He was fully clothed and had been shot seven times in the head and torso. His car was found in [Suwannee County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwannee_County,_Florida "Suwannee County, Florida").
* **Walter Gino Antonio**, age 61, trucker, security guard, and reserve police officer. On November 19, 1990, Antonio's nearly naked body was found near a remote logging road in [Dixie County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dixie_County,_Florida "Dixie County, Florida"). He had been shot four times. Five days later, his car was found in [Brevard County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brevard_County,_Florida "Brevard County, Florida").

## Arrest and trial

On July 4, 1990, Wuornos and Moore abandoned victim Peter Siems' car after they were involved in a crash. Rhonda Bailey, who witnessed the crash, provided police with a description of two women, which later led to a media campaign to locate them. Police also found some of the victims' belongings in [pawnshops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pawnshop "Pawnshop"). Wuornos' fingerprint that was found on a receipt at one of the pawnshops matched the print that was left in Siems' car. Wuornos had a criminal record in Florida, and samples of her prints were in a database.

On January 9, 1991, Wuornos was arrested at The Last Resort [biker bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biker_bar "Biker bar") in [Volusia County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volusia_County,_Florida "Volusia County, Florida"). According to *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")*, the arrest was made on the basis of an [outstanding warrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outstanding_warrant "Outstanding warrant") for [carrying a concealed weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrying_a_concealed_weapon "Carrying a concealed weapon"), although by this point, authorities already came to regard her as a suspect in the murders, based on witnesses' testimony, and fingerprints found on an item found in a pawn shop that had belonged to one of the murdered men. The warrant had been issued in the name of Lori Grody. Police located Moore the next day in [Pittston, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittston,_Pennsylvania "Pittston, Pennsylvania"). She agreed to elicit a confession from Wuornos in exchange for [immunity from prosecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_immunity "Legal immunity"). Moore returned with the police to Florida, where she was put up in a motel. Under police guidance, she made numerous telephone calls to Wuornos, pleading for help in clearing her name. Three days later, on January 16, 1991, Wuornos confessed to the murders. She claimed the men had tried to rape her and she killed them in self-defense.

On January 14, 1992, Wuornos went to trial for the murder of Richard Charles Mallory. Although previous convictions are normally inadmissible in criminal trials, under Florida's [Williams Rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_Rule "Williams Rule"), the prosecution was allowed to introduce evidence related to her other crimes to show a pattern of illegal activity. On January 27, 1992, Wuornos was convicted of Mallory's murder with help from Moore's testimony. At her sentencing, [psychiatrists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychiatrist "Psychiatrist") for the defense testified that Wuornos was mentally unstable and diagnosed her with [borderline personality disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borderline_personality_disorder "Borderline personality disorder") and [antisocial personality disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisocial_personality_disorder "Antisocial personality disorder"). Four days later, she was [sentenced to death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_penalty "Death penalty").

Wuornos' defense made efforts during the trial to introduce evidence that Mallory was previously convicted for attempted rape in [Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland "Maryland") and served a sentence in a maximum-security correctional facility providing remediation to [sexual offenders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_offender "Sexual offender"). Records obtained from the correctional institution showed that from 1958 to 1962, Mallory was committed for treatment and observation resulting from a criminal charge of assault with intent to rape. These records also reflect eight years of overall treatment from the facility. In 1961, "it was observed of Mr. Mallory that he possessed strong [sociopathic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociopathy "Sociopathy") trends". However, the judge refused to allow the records to be admitted in court as evidence, and denied Wuornos' request for a retrial.

On March 31, 1992, Wuornos pleaded [no contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_contest "No contest") to the murders of Charles Richard Humphreys, Troy Eugene Burress, and David Andrew Spears, saying she wanted to "get right with God". In her statement to the court, she said, in part, "I wanted to confess to you that Richard Mallory did violently rape me as I've told you; but these others did not. [They] only began to start to". On May 15, 1992, Wuornos was given three more death sentences. In June 1992, Wuornos pleaded guilty to the murder of Charles Edmund Carskaddon. In November 1992, she received her fifth death sentence. In February 1993, Wuornos pleaded guilty to the murder of Walter Gino Antonio and was sentenced to death again. No charges were brought against her for the murder of Peter Abraham Siems, as his body was never found. In all, Wuornos received six death sentences.

Wuornos told several inconsistent stories about the killings. She claimed initially that all seven men had either raped or attempted to rape her while she was working as a prostitute but later recanted the claim of self-defense, citing [robbery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbery "Robbery") and a desire to leave no witnesses as the reason for murder. During an interview with documentarian [Nick Broomfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Broomfield "Nick Broomfield"), when Wuornos thought the cameras were off, she told him that it was, in fact, self-defense, but she could not stand being on [death row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_row "Death row")—where she had been for ten years at that point—and wanted to die.

## Death row and execution

### Death row

Wuornos was incarcerated at the [Broward Correctional Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broward_Correctional_Institution "Broward Correctional Institution") (BCI) [death row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_row "Death row") for women, then transferred to the [Florida State Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_Prison "Florida State Prison") for execution. Her appeal to the [U.S. Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Supreme_Court "U.S. Supreme Court") was denied in 1996. In a 2001 petition to the [Florida Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Supreme_Court "Florida Supreme Court"), she stated her intention to dismiss her legal counsel and terminate all pending appeals. "I killed those men", she wrote, "robbed them as cold as ice. And I'd do it again, too. There's no chance in keeping me alive or anything, because I'd kill again. I have hate crawling through my system ... I am so sick of hearing this 'she's crazy' stuff. I've been evaluated so many times. I'm competent, sane, and I'm trying to tell the truth. I'm one who seriously hates human life and would kill again." While her attorneys argued that she was not mentally competent to make such a request, Wuornos insisted that she knew what she was doing, and a court-appointed panel of psychiatrists agreed.

In 2002, Wuornos began accusing prison matrons of tainting her food with dirt, [saliva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saliva "Saliva"), and [urine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urine "Urine"). She said she had overheard conversations among prison personnel "trying to get me so pushed over the brink by them I'd wind up committing suicide before the execution" and "wishing to rape me before execution". She also complained of strip searches, tight handcuffing, door kicking, frequent window checks, low water pressure, mildew on her mattress, and "cat calling ... in distaste and a pure hatred towards me". Wuornos threatened to boycott showers and food trays when certain officers were on duty. "In the meantime, my stomach's growling away and I'm taking showers through the sink of my cell." Her attorney stated that "Ms. Wuornos really just wants to have proper treatment, humane treatment until the day she's executed." He added, "She believes what she's written".

In the weeks before her execution, Wuornos gave a series of interviews to documentarian [Nick Broomfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Broomfield "Nick Broomfield") and talked about "being taken away to meet God and Jesus and the angels and whatever is beyond the beyond". In her final interview, she once again charged that her mind was "tortured" at BCI, and her head crushed by "sonic pressure". Food poisonings and other abuses worsened, she said, each time she complained, to make her appear insane, or to drive her insane. She also turned on her interviewer: "You sabotaged my ass! Society, and the cops, and the system! A raped woman got executed, and was used for books and movies and shit!" She also went on to call the mainstream media “an inhumane bunch of fucking living bastards and bitches” and also mentioned that she believed “a rock would nuke them” in 2019. Her final on-camera words were "Thanks a lot, society, for railroading my ass". Dawn Botkins, a childhood friend of Wuornos, later told Broomfield that her verbal abuse was directed at society and the media in general, not at him specifically.

### Execution and death

Wuornos' execution by a [lethal injection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lethal_injection "Lethal injection") cocktail of [sodium thiopental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_thiopental "Sodium thiopental"), [pancuronium bromide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancuronium_bromide "Pancuronium bromide"), and [potassium chloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium_chloride "Potassium chloride") took place on October 9, 2002. She declined her [last meal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_meal "Last meal") which could have been anything under $20 and instead received a cup of coffee. Her last words were, "Yes, I would just like to say I'm sailing with the rock, and I'll be back, like *[Independence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_(1996_film) "Independence Day (1996 film)")*, with Jesus. June 6, like the movie. Big mother ship and all, I'll be back, I'll be back". She died at 9:47 a.m. She was the second woman in Florida and the tenth in the United States to be executed since the [1976 United States Supreme Court decision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_v._Georgia "Gregg v. Georgia") restoring capital punishment.

After her death, Wuornos' body was [cremated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremation "Cremation"). Wuornos' ashes were scattered beneath a tree in her native Michigan by Wuornos' childhood friend Dawn Botkins. At Wuornos' request, [Natalie Merchant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalie_Merchant "Natalie Merchant")'s song "[Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival_(Natalie_Merchant_song) "Carnival (Natalie Merchant song)")" from Merchant's album *[Tigerlily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigerlily "Tigerlily")* (1995) was played at Wuornos' funeral. Wuornos spent many hours listening to this album on death row. When Merchant found out about this, she gave permission to use the song in the closing credits of Broomfield's documentary *[Aileen: Life and Death of a Serial Killer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aileen:_Life_and_Death_of_a_Serial_Killer "Aileen: Life and Death of a Serial Killer")* (2003):

> When director Nick Broomfield sent a working edit of the film, I was so disturbed by the subject matter that I couldn't even watch it. Aileen Wuornos led a tortured, torturing life that is beyond my worst nightmares. It wasn't until I was told that Aileen spent many hours listening to my album *Tigerlily* while on death row and requested "Carnival" be played at her funeral that I gave permission for the use of the song. It's very odd to think of the places my music can go once it leaves my hands. If it gave her some solace, I have to be grateful.

Broomfield later speculated on Wuornos' state of mind and motives:

> I think this anger developed inside her. And she was working as a prostitute. I think she had a lot of awful encounters on the roads. And I think this anger just spilled out from inside her. And finally exploded. Into incredible violence. That was her way of surviving. I think Aileen really believed that she had killed in self-defense. I think someone who's deeply psychotic can't really tell the difference between something that is life threatening and something that is a minor disagreement, that you could say something that she didn't agree with. She would get into a screaming black temper about it. And I think that's what had caused these things to happen. And at the same time, when she wasn't in those extreme moods, there was an incredible humanity to her.

## Psychopathological profile

According to some specialists, Wuornos' crimes have been related to her [psychopathic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychopathy "Psychopathy") personality and her [traumatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological_trauma "Psychological trauma") past. Assessed using the [Psychopathy Checklist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychopathy_Checklist "Psychopathy Checklist"), Wuornos scored 32/40 with the cutoff score of 30 for determining psychopathy.

According to Brice Arrigo, Wuornos' childhood sexual abuse and being forced into sexual labor irrevocably damaged her, and it could be seen that traumatic experiences throughout most of her young life could play a part in Wuornos' psychological state, including her biological mother's departure as well as her grandmother ignoring the abuse she endured from her grandfather, thus leading to the lack of development of a "mother–daughter" bond for Wuornos as a young girl. The damage was then made worse because both Wuornos and her brother believed that their grandparents were their biological parents, but at the age of 11 they learned that this was not the case.

Wuornos was also known to have early behavioral problems such as having an explosive temper which limited her ability to make friends, as well as making it increasingly difficult for her to maintain relationships. Her traumatic upbringing, including her physical and sexual abuse, have been partially linked to the development of her borderline personality disorder. Such severe trauma can also disrupt the [structuralization of the mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structuralism_(psychology) "Structuralism (psychology)") at various developmental points and result in "primitive, dissociative, and splitting defenses to ward off the intensity of emotional and sexual stimulation that cannot be integrated as a child."

[FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Investigation "Federal Bureau of Investigation") profiler [Robert Ressler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Ressler "Robert Ressler") briefly mentions Wuornos in his autobiographical history of his 20 years with the FBI. Writing in 1992, he said he often does not discuss female serial killers because they tend to kill in sprees instead of in a sequential fashion. He noted Wuornos as the sole exception.

### Documentaries

Filmmaker [Nick Broomfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Broomfield "Nick Broomfield") directed two documentaries about Wuornos:

* *[Aileen Wuornos: The Selling of a Serial Killer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aileen_Wuornos:_The_Selling_of_a_Serial_Killer "Aileen Wuornos: The Selling of a Serial Killer")* (1992)
* *[Aileen: Life and Death of a Serial Killer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aileen:_Life_and_Death_of_a_Serial_Killer "Aileen: Life and Death of a Serial Killer")* (2003)

Wuornos was the subject of episodes of the documentary TV series *[American Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Justice "American Justice")*, *[Biography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biography_(TV_program) "Biography (TV program)")* and *[Deadly Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadly_Women "Deadly Women")*. She was also featured in an episode of the TV series *[The New Detectives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Detectives "The New Detectives")* (season 3, episode 1: "Fatal Compulsion").

An episode of *Murder Made Me Famous* on the [Reelz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reelz "Reelz") television network, airing December 1, 2018, chronicled the case. In February 2020, the series *Very Scary People* was shown on the [Crime & Investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%26E_Networks#Crime_&_Investigation "A&E Networks"); episodes 3 and 4 describes how the investigation into Wuornos was conducted. A 2021 episode of *[Catching Killers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catching_Killers "Catching Killers")* from [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") is centered around Wuornos: the 40-minute episode is titled, "Manhunter: Aileen Wuornos."

The Netflix documentary film *[Aileen: Queen of the Serial Killers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aileen:_Queen_of_the_Serial_Killers "Aileen: Queen of the Serial Killers")* (2025) was directed by Emily Turner.

### Films

The biographical drama film *[Monster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monster_(2003_film) "Monster (2003 film)")* (2003), stars [Charlize Theron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlize_Theron "Charlize Theron") as Wuornos and [Christina Ricci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christina_Ricci "Christina Ricci") as Tyria Moore (Selby Wall in the film). The film centers on Wuornos' series of murders and her relationship with Moore. Theron won the [Academy Award for Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress "Academy Award for Best Actress") for her performance in the film.

The horror thriller film *[Aileen Wuornos: American Boogeywoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aileen_Wuornos:_American_Boogeywoman "Aileen Wuornos: American Boogeywoman")* (2021) stars [Peyton List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peyton_List_(actress,_born_1998) "Peyton List (actress, born 1998)") as Wuornos. It shows a fictional version of Wuornos' marriage to Lewis Gratz Fell in 1976. The film was released via [video-on-demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video-on-demand "Video-on-demand") and on DVD.

### Music

An operatic adaptation of Wuornos' life premiered at [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco")'s [Yerba Buena Center for the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerba_Buena_Center_for_the_Arts "Yerba Buena Center for the Arts") on June 22, 2001. Titled *Wuornos*, the opera was written by [composer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composer "Composer") and [librettist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libretto "Libretto") [Carla Lucero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carla_Lucero "Carla Lucero"), conducted by Mary Chun, and produced by the [Jon Sims Center for the Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Sims_Center_for_the_Performing_Arts "Jon Sims Center for the Performing Arts").

Several musicians have written songs about Wuornos, including [Jewel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewel_(singer) "Jewel (singer)") ("Nicotine Love"), the New York-based [metalcore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metalcore "Metalcore") band [It Dies Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It_Dies_Today "It Dies Today") ("Sixth of June"), and [Pablo Hasél](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Has%C3%A9l "Pablo Hasél") ("Inéditas por culpa de Aileen Wuornos").

The singer [Diamanda Galás](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamanda_Gal%C3%A1s "Diamanda Galás") recorded a live cover of the [Phil Ochs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Ochs "Phil Ochs") song "Iron Lady", which she would often perform as a tribute to Wuornos, for her performance album *[Malediction and Prayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malediction_and_Prayer "Malediction and Prayer")* (1998).

Japanese [doom metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_metal "Doom metal") band [Church of Misery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Misery "Church of Misery") released the song "Filth Bitch Boogie (Aileen Wuornos)" on their 2004 studio album *[The Second Coming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Second_Coming_(Church_of_Misery_album) "The Second Coming (Church of Misery album)")*.

The song "The Damsel of Death" by the Puerto Rican all-female metal band Matriarch from their 2007 album *Revered Unto the Ages* is written from the point of view of Wuornos.

Samples of interviews with Wuornos feature prominently throughout [Dragged into Sunlight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragged_into_Sunlight "Dragged into Sunlight")'s 2009 album *Hatred for Mankind*, and [Lingua Ignota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_Ignota_(musician) "Lingua Ignota (musician)")'s 2017 albums *All Bitches Die* and *Let The Evil of His Own Lips Cover Him* through-out the songs "Disease of Men", "For I Am the Light (and Mine is the Only Way)", and "Holy is the Name (Of My Ruthless Axe)". Lingua Ignota's song "If the Poison Won't Take You My Dogs Will" of her 2019 album *[Caligula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caligula_(Lingua_Ignota_album) "Caligula (Lingua Ignota album)")* is also about Wuornos.

The song "Poor Aileen" by [Superheaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superheaven "Superheaven"), which is the final track from the 2015 album *[Ours Is Chrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ours_Is_Chrome "Ours Is Chrome")*, is written about Aileen Wuornos.

A parody cover version of [Dolly Parton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_Parton "Dolly Parton")'s song "[Jolene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolene "Jolene")" called "Aileen", dedicated to Wuornos, is featured on [Willam Belli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willam_Belli "Willam Belli")'s [third album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Now_That%27s_What_I_Call_Drag_Music,_Vol._1 "Now That's What I Call Drag Music, Vol. 1"). The music video, featuring [Gigi Gorgeous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigi_Gorgeous "Gigi Gorgeous") portraying Wuornos, was released on November 1, 2018.

In 2019, rapper [Cardi B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardi_B "Cardi B") recreated Wuornos' famous mugshot for her single "[Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Press_(Cardi_B_song) "Press (Cardi B song)")". In 2020, rapper [Sadistik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadistik "Sadistik") released the song "Aileen Wuornos", dedicated to the serial killer, on his *Delirium* EP.

The band [SKYND](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skynd_(band) "Skynd (band)") released their song "Aileen Wuornos" in March 2025.

## Legacy

Wuornos' case is seen in some feminist circles as an example of [battered woman syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battered_woman_syndrome "Battered woman syndrome") deserving of some sympathy.

In an interview on [*Dateline*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dateline_NBC "Dateline NBC"), interviewer Michele Gillen asks Wuornos:

> …the seventh men that you shot, did you say to yourself: “I’m out of control, I’m killing people! I need help-”

to which Wuornos notably responded:

> No, no. No I thought to myself: those men are out of control. I’m sick and tired of those men out there thinking they can control us and do whatever they damn well please with our bodies! And then that they can get away with it: ‘cause it’s a male dominated society and “we’re gonna treat you the way we want to, abuse you and destroy you, it doesn’t matter to us ‘cause… we can get away with doing that.

In response to the [Cornell University rape allegations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Cornell_University_rape_allegations "2024 Cornell University rape allegations"), using the [hashtag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashtag "Hashtag") #WhatWouldAileenDo, some women alluded to Wuornos' actions as a response to sexual violence by men.
