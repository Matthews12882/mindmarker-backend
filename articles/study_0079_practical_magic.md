# Practical Magic

***Practical Magic*** is a 1998 American [fantasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasy_film "Fantasy film") [romantic comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romantic_comedy "Romantic comedy") [drama film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama_film "Drama film") based on [the 1995 novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Practical_Magic_(novel) "Practical Magic (novel)") by [Alice Hoffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Hoffman "Alice Hoffman"). The film was directed by [Griffin Dunne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffin_Dunne "Griffin Dunne") and stars [Sandra Bullock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_Bullock "Sandra Bullock"), [Nicole Kidman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Kidman "Nicole Kidman"), [Dianne Wiest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dianne_Wiest "Dianne Wiest"), [Stockard Channing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockard_Channing "Stockard Channing"), and [Aidan Quinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aidan_Quinn "Aidan Quinn"). Bullock and Kidman play sisters Sally and Gillian Owens, descended from a long line of witches. Raised by their aunts after their parents' death from a family curse, the sisters are taught the uses of practical magic as they grow up. Years later, they must use their magic to destroy the evil spirit of Gillian's abusive boyfriend before it kills them.

*Practical Magic* was released by [Warner Bros.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures "Warner Bros. Pictures") on October 16, 1998, and grossed $94.4 million worldwide against a production budget of $60–75 million. The film received poor reviews from critics and has since gained a [cult following](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult_film "Cult film"). A sequel, *[Practical Magic 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Practical_Magic_2 "Practical Magic 2")*, was released on September 10, 2026.

## Plot

In [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts "Massachusetts"), the Owens family have been persecuted for over three centuries because their ancestor Maria Owens survived an attempted execution for witchcraft. Heartbroken when the father of her unborn child left her banished on an offshore island, Maria cast a spell to prevent herself from ever falling in love again. The spell developed into a curse upon Maria's descendants, dooming any man an Owens woman loves.

In the present, Sally and Gillian Owens are taken in by their aunts Frances and Jet after their parents succumb to the Owens curse. As children, Sally and Gillian are frequently ridiculed by the town's schoolchildren. Upon witnessing their aunts cast a love spell for a woman obsessed with her beloved, Sally casts a spell on herself to ensure she will only fall in love with a man who possesses certain seemingly impossible traits, with the intention of never falling in love. Witnessing the same incident, Gillian cannot wait to fall in love. As teenagers, Gillian [elopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elopement "Elopement") with her boyfriend and leaves for Los Angeles. Before she departs, she and Sally use a blood spell to swear to always be faithful to one another, grow old together, and die at the same time.

Gillian spends the next decade moving from relationship to relationship across the country, while back in Massachusetts, Sally meets and marries a local named Michael. They have two daughters, Kylie and Antonia. After a truck fatally hits Michael, Sally and her girls move in with the aunts. Learning that they secretly cast a love spell on her so that she could marry and be happy, Sally vows that the aunts will never teach her daughters magic.

Gillian tells Sally her involvement with a man named Jimmy Angelov, a powerful presence whom she often sedates with small amounts of [belladonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atropa_bella-donna "Atropa bella-donna"). Gillian eventually phones Sally and asks her to come get her. Soon after Sally arrives to rescue her now abused sister, Angelov takes them hostage in his car. Sally puts belladonna into Jimmy's tequila to knock him unconscious, but inadvertently kills him. The sisters take Jimmy's body back to the aunts' house, where they resurrect him with a forbidden spell, but he begins to choke Gillian again and Sally kills him for the second time. The sisters bury Angelov's remains in the garden. Sally, Gillian, and the aunts have a midnight drinking session in which Jimmy's tequila seems to be influencing them to turn against each other; the aunts leave home the following morning, leaving a message to the sisters to "clean up their own mess".

State investigator Gary Hallett arrives from [Tucson, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucson,_Arizona "Tucson, Arizona"), in search of Jimmy, who is revealed to be a serial killer. Gillian attempts to separate them with a potion, but Kylie and Antonia realize he is the man from Sally's childhood spell and they dispose of the pancake syrup containing the potion. After Gillian and Sally fight, a distraught Sally confesses to Gary, only to discover he is the impossible man from her spell. Unable to deny their attraction, the two kiss, which makes Sally ultimately stop and flee, and then runs towards her home knowing that Gillian is in trouble.

Arriving, Sally realizes that Jimmy's spirit has entered Gillian's body. Gary arrives and witnesses Jimmy's spirit materialize before him and, after Jimmy attacks him, uses his silver badge to thwart Angelov's spirit from possessing him. Sally tells Gary he is there because of her spell, the feelings they have for each other are not real, and the family curse will kill him if they pursue a relationship. Gary replies that curses only work if one believes in them and that he wished for her too, and returns to Tucson.

Jimmy uses Gillian again in an attempt to kill Sally before Frances and Jet return. Realizing she must embrace magic to save her sister, Sally asks the local townswomen to form a coven and [exorcise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exorcism "Exorcism") Jimmy's spirit. They exorcise Angelov's spirit and permanently exile him, and hope that this might also mean the end of the Owens curse.

Gary clears the sisters of any suspicion in Jimmy's case and returns to Massachusetts to be with Sally. Sally chooses love with Gary despite the risks of an uncertain future. The townsfolk finally welcome the Owens women into the community, as witches, and witness the family's annual Halloween tradition of flying down from the roof.

## Production

*Practical Magic* was filmed in part on an artificial set in [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California "California"). Because the film's producers decided the house was a big part of the depiction of the Owens culture, a house to accurately represent that vision was built on [San Juan Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_Island "San Juan Island") in the state of Washington. While much of the set from California was brought to that location and placed inside the house, it took nearly a year to perfect the image of the house and the interior. The house, actually only a shell with nothing inside, was built only for this filming and was torn down after filming was completed. The small town scenes were filmed in downtown [Coupeville, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupeville,_Washington "Coupeville, Washington"), a Victorian-era seaside port town located on the south side of [Penn Cove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penn_Cove "Penn Cove") on [Whidbey Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whidbey_Island "Whidbey Island"). [Nicole Kidman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Kidman "Nicole Kidman")'s work on *Practical Magic* and her ex-husband [Tom Cruise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cruise "Tom Cruise")'s work on *[Mission: Impossible 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission:_Impossible_2 "Mission: Impossible 2")* were respectively delayed due to production of *[Eyes Wide Shut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyes_Wide_Shut "Eyes Wide Shut")* taking much longer than expected.

The film's production design was led by Robin Standefer and Stephen Alesch of the design studio Roman and Williams. They created the film’s richly detailed sets, including the Owens family home, and designed many of the props by hand. According to Standefer and Alesch, they also designed the film’s distinctive spellbook prop, which features original hand-drawn illustrations by Alesch. Standefer has stated, “Stephen and I devoted ourselves to that. We made every little thing in the film, down to the tincture jars and pressed flowers,” adding that Alesch “did incredible etchings of plants that you can see all around the dining room.”

Director Griffin Dunne said he originally had a darker vision for the film. The scene with Sally and Gilly inserting needles into Jimmy was supposed to be much more disturbing and the domestic violence plot line was more intense, but the studio cut the darker material. He expressed interest in a [director's cut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director%27s_cut "Director's cut").

According to Sandra Bullock in the DVD commentary, while filming the scene where the Owens women are drunk and slinging insults, the actresses actually got drunk on very bad [tequila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tequila "Tequila") brought by Kidman.

The cast further stated in the film's commentary that they felt supernatural elements of the house started to affect them. Both the cast and crew claimed they heard supernatural noises while filming the coven scene at the end of the film. For the final scene with all of the townspeople at the Owens home, the entire population of the town where filming took place was invited to show up in costume and appear as townsfolk.

## Music

Composer [Michael Nyman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Nyman "Michael Nyman")'s score for the film was abruptly replaced with music by [Alan Silvestri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Silvestri "Alan Silvestri") for the theatrical release. This last-minute change resulted in the release of two soundtracks, although as primarily a [compilation album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compilation_album "Compilation album") only the two tracks of newly created material were changed. A 50-track demo (the last two tracks being "Convening the Coven" and "Maria Owens") of Nyman's score has been circulating among fans as a [bootleg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bootleg_recording "Bootleg recording"). The complete Nyman score runs 62:30 and contains music that would later appear, in altered form, in *[Ravenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravenous_(1999_film) "Ravenous (1999 film)")* and *[The Actors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Actors "The Actors")*, as well as a bit of his stepwise chord progression theme from *[Out of the Ruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out_of_the_Ruins "Out of the Ruins")*/*[String Quartet No. 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_Quartets_1%E2%80%933#String_Quartet_No._3 "String Quartets 1–3")*/*[Carrington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrington_(film)#Music "Carrington (film)")*/*[The End of the Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_End_of_the_Affair_(1999_film)#Music "The End of the Affair (1999 film)")*/*[The Claim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Claim_(2000_film) "The Claim (2000 film)")*. "Convening the Coven", though not "Maria Owens", was subsequently reissued on *[The Very Best of Michael Nyman: Film Music 1980–2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Very_Best_of_Michael_Nyman:_Film_Music_1980%E2%80%932001 "The Very Best of Michael Nyman: Film Music 1980–2001")*, and music that uses material related to this piece has not been used elsewhere. "Convening the Coven" became "City of Turin" on *[The Glare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Glare "The Glare")*.

Singer [Stevie Nicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevie_Nicks "Stevie Nicks") headlined the soundtrack's published advertisements. The lead single was "If You Ever Did Believe", a song Nicks had originally written in the 1970s for [Fleetwood Mac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleetwood_Mac "Fleetwood Mac"). After the song did not make the track list for [*Rumours*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumours_(album) "Rumours (album)"), Nicks allowed [Louise Goffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louise_Goffin "Louise Goffin") to record it for her 1981 album. The *Practical Magic* soundtrack marked the first time Nicks released her own version of the song. Nicks also recorded a new version of her song "[Crystal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_(Fleetwood_Mac_song) "Crystal (Fleetwood Mac song)")". Both songs feature [Sheryl Crow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheryl_Crow "Sheryl Crow") on back-up vocals.

## Reception

### Box office

*Practical Magic* opened at #1 with $13.1 million in ticket sales, beating *[Antz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antz "Antz")*, *[Bride of Chucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bride_of_Chucky "Bride of Chucky")*, and *[Beloved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beloved_(1998_film) "Beloved (1998 film)")*. In Australia, the film debuted at the top of the box office ahead of *[Ronin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronin_(film) "Ronin (film)")* and *[The Waterboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Waterboy "The Waterboy")*, making $1.6 million in its first weekend. The film went on to gross $94.4 million worldwide, on a production budget of $60–75 million.

### Critical response

*Practical Magic* received negative reviews from critics upon release, who panned its inconsistent tone and script. On the [review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator "Review aggregator") website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes"), the film holds an approval rating of 31% based on 111 reviews, with an average rating of 5.1/10. The website's critics consensus reads, "*Practical Magic*'s jarring tonal shifts sink what little potential its offbeat story may have – though Nicole Kidman and Sandra Bullock's chemistry makes a strong argument for future collaborations." On [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), which assigns a [weighted average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_average "Weighted average") score out of 100 to reviews from mainstream critics, the film received an average score of 46 based on 22 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews. Audiences polled by CinemaScore gave the film an average grade of B− on an A+ to F scale.

[Owen Gleiberman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Gleiberman "Owen Gleiberman") of *[Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")* gave *Practical Magic* a negative review, calling it "a witch comedy so slapdash, plodding, and muddled it seems to have had a hex put on it." [Roger Ebert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ebert "Roger Ebert") of the *[Chicago Sun-Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Sun-Times "Chicago Sun-Times")* spoke of the film's unsure tone, "veering uncertainly from horror to laughs to romance", and opined that the film "is too scary for children and too childish for adults". Angie Errigo of *[Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_(magazine) "Empire (magazine)")* stated that "it tootles along being cute and fluffy like a twentysomethings' version of *[Sabrina the Teenage Witch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabrina_the_Teenage_Witch "Sabrina the Teenage Witch")*, but to further its notions of sisterhood and the power of women, it also takes a spin through *[Thelma & Louise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thelma_%26_Louise "Thelma & Louise")* territory, then revisits *[The Exorcist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Exorcist "The Exorcist")* to up the supernatural content. It's enough to make your head spin".

Garth Stahl of the *[Hartford Courant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartford_Courant "Hartford Courant")* was more positive, noting that women in [dark comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_comedy "Dark comedy") and depictions of sisterhood are rare in film. He wrote *Practical Magic* "has its flaws. Some scenes are weak, occasionally lines are a little muddled, and there is some plot underdevelopment. Yet it is daring and fun — a frolic for two charming actresses and a dapper film premise". He concluded "The main source of attraction is undeniably the enchanting and witty witchcraft. It is simultaneously what makes the Owen sisters outcasts and what makes them special. With a few very sharp scenes, including a jazzy dance sequence, 'Practical Magic' is worth [it]. It is a step toward allowing women to assert themselves in the genre of dark comedy. Now isn't that practical."

## Release

*Practical Magic* was released theatrically in the United States on October 16, 1998, by Warner Bros. Pictures.

The film was released on [VHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VHS "VHS") and [DVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD "DVD") in the United States on February 16, 1999, by Warner Home Video. A 4K Ultra HD Blu-ray was released on August 25, 2026, from Warner Bros. Home Entertainment.

## Legacy

The film has acquired a cult following over the years. Writing about *Practical Magic*'s legacy in 2018, David Sims of *[The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic "The Atlantic")* discussed how the film centers relationships between women through the Owens bloodline as well as in the final scene involving the town's women uniting to free Gillian from Jimmy's control. He described the film as a mainstream [studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_film_studios "Major film studios") feature "that wove dark themes about gender and power into an ostensible crowd-pleasing comedy", qualities that made it unusual for its time and have contributed to its staying power.

When first streamed on [Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_(streaming_service) "Max (streaming service)") in October 2024, *Practical Magic* secured the #5 spot for top films on the streaming platform.

### In other media

Warner Bros. and [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS "CBS") produced the 2004 television pilot *Sudbury*, written by Becky Hartman Edwards and directed by [Bryan Spicer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Spicer "Bryan Spicer"), starring [Kim Delaney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Delaney "Kim Delaney") in the Bullock role and [Jeri Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeri_Ryan "Jeri Ryan") in the Kidman role. Named for the [Sudbury, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudbury,_Massachusetts "Sudbury, Massachusetts") location of the novel and film, the series was not picked up.

Warner Bros. and [ABC Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeform_(TV_channel) "Freeform (TV channel)") attempted to develop a prequel television series in 2010.

[HBO Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO_Max "HBO Max") announced development of a prequel television series in 2019, based on [Alice Hoffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Hoffman "Alice Hoffman")'s 2017 prequel novel *The Rules of Magic*, with *[Jessica Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Jones_(TV_series) "Jessica Jones (TV series)")* creator [Melissa Rosenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melissa_Rosenberg "Melissa Rosenberg") writing and executive producing. The project never came to fruition.

### Sequel

Development of a sequel was announced in June 2024, with Bullock and Kidman returning to produce the film with Di Novi. Later that same month, Nicole Kidman confirmed that she and Sandra Bullock would reprise their roles in the sequel. Akiva Goldsman, who co-wrote the screenplay for the original film, would return to write the script. Griffin Dunne hinted that a woman would direct the sequel, while he served as an executive producer.

The sequel is based on Alice Hoffman's 2021 novel *The Book of Magic*, the fourth installment in her *Practical Magic* series. [Susanne Bier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susanne_Bier "Susanne Bier") was ultimately confirmed to direct the sequel, [Joey King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_King "Joey King") and [Maisie Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maisie_Williams "Maisie Williams") were cast as Sally's daughters, and in July 2025, [Stockard Channing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockard_Channing "Stockard Channing") and [Dianne Wiest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dianne_Wiest "Dianne Wiest") were confirmed to reprise their roles as Frances and Jet. The film was released in September 2026.

### Musical

On February 9, 2026, it was confirmed that a Broadway musical based on the film was in development, to be written by series author [Alice Hoffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Hoffman "Alice Hoffman") and [Peter Duchan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Duchan "Peter Duchan"), with an original score by [Norah Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norah_Jones "Norah Jones") and [Gregg Wattenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Wattenberg "Gregg Wattenberg"), and [Maria Friedman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Friedman "Maria Friedman") directing.
