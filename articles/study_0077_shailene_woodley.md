# Shailene Woodley

**Shailene Diann Woodley** (born November 15, 1991) is an American actress. Known for her work on screen and stage, [her accolades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Shailene_Woodley "List of awards and nominations received by Shailene Woodley") include a [Primetime Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award "Primetime Emmy Award") and a [Trophée Chopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troph%C3%A9e_Chopard "Trophée Chopard"), in addition to nominations for two [Actor Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actor_Awards "Actor Awards"), a [British Academy Film Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Award "British Academy Film Award"), and two [Golden Globes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globes "Golden Globes").

Woodley gained acclaim for playing a rebellious daughter in the [Alexander Payne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Payne "Alexander Payne") dramedy *[The Descendants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Descendants "The Descendants")* (2011), which earned her a [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture "Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actress – Motion Picture") nomination. She achieved wider recognition for her roles in the teen dramas *[The Spectacular Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Spectacular_Now "The Spectacular Now")* (2013) and [*The Fault in Our Stars*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fault_in_Our_Stars_(film) "The Fault in Our Stars (film)") (2014) and for her portrayal of [Beatrice Prior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatrice_Prior "Beatrice Prior") in the science-fiction trilogy *[The Divergent Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Divergent_Series "The Divergent Series")* (2014–2016). She has since acted in the films *[Snowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowden_(film) "Snowden (film)")* (2016), *[Adrift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrift_(2018_film) "Adrift (2018 film)")* (2018), *[The Mauritanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mauritanian "The Mauritanian")* (2021), *[Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrari_(2023_film) "Ferrari (2023 film)")* (2023), and *[To Catch a Killer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Catch_a_Killer_(2023_film) "To Catch a Killer (2023 film)")* (2023), the latter of which she also produced.

On television, she had her breakthrough playing [Amy Juergens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Juergens "Amy Juergens") in the [ABC Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeform_(TV_channel) "Freeform (TV channel)") teen drama series *[The Secret Life of the American Teenager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Secret_Life_of_the_American_Teenager "The Secret Life of the American Teenager")* (2008–2013). She played a former med student and survivor in the [Hulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulu "Hulu") apocalyptic thriller series *[Paradise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise_(2025_TV_series) "Paradise (2025 TV series)")* (2026) earning the [Primetime Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Guest_Actress_in_a_Drama_Series "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Guest Actress in a Drama Series"). She was previously Emmy-nominated for her role as a sexual assault survivor in the [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO "HBO") drama series *[Big Little Lies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Little_Lies_(TV_series) "Big Little Lies (TV series)")* (2017–2019).

Woodley made her [Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_(theater) "Broadway (theater)") debut in [Leslye Headland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslye_Headland "Leslye Headland")'s family [dramedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dramedy "Dramedy") play *[Cult of Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult_of_Love "Cult of Love")* (2024), for which she was honored with a [Theater World Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_World_Award "Theater World Award"). She is also an [environmental activist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentalism "Environmentalism") and a [Greenpeace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenpeace "Greenpeace") Oceans Ambassador, and serves on a number of boards dedicated to environmental causes. She is co-founder of the [nonprofit organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonprofit_organization "Nonprofit organization") All it Takes, which focuses on [youth development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_development "Youth development").

## Early life

Woodley was born in [San Bernardino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bernardino "San Bernardino"), California. Her mother, Lori, is a [middle-school counselor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_counselor "School counselor") and her father, Lonnie, is a former [school principal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_teacher "Head teacher") and a [family therapist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_therapy "Family therapy"). Shailene has a younger brother, Tanner. Her parents separated when she was 14. Woodley has a diverse ethnic background, with British ancestry on her father's side and a maternal heritage that includes African-American and [Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creole_peoples "Creole peoples") roots, as well as French, Spanish, Swiss, and German ancestry.

Woodley was discovered by an agent while she was taking a local theater class, which was part of a $700 program she begged her parents to enroll her in after accompanying her cousin to a theater class in her hometown. At the age of five, she began working in commercials, including advertisements for [Leapfrog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeapFrog_Enterprises "LeapFrog Enterprises"), [Hertz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hertz_Corporation "The Hertz Corporation"), and a [Honda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda "Honda") minivan, appearing in more than sixty [TV spots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_spots "TV spots") before she turned eleven years old. She told *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")* that her parents only agreed to allow her to work professionally if she promised to adhere to three rules: "I had to stay the person they knew I was; have fun; and do good in school". Despite an [ADHD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADHD "ADHD") diagnosis, Woodley was a [4.0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_grading_in_the_United_States "Academic grading in the United States") student, who took [AP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Placement "Advanced Placement") classes and graduated from [Simi Valley High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Valley_High_School "Simi Valley High School").

She considered studying [Interior Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interior_design_education "Interior design education") at [New York University (NYU)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University "New York University") but her acting career became more demanding as she signed on to star in *[The Secret Life of the American Teenager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Secret_Life_of_the_American_Teenager "The Secret Life of the American Teenager")*. During a hiatus from her TV show, Woodley took a job at [American Apparel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Apparel "American Apparel") in New York City. Two days into the new job, she got a call to meet with director [Alexander Payne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Payne "Alexander Payne") and after two months, she had to quit the job after joining the film *[The Descendants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Descendants "The Descendants")*. She also took acting classes from [Anthony Meindl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Meindl "Anthony Meindl").

At fifteen, she was diagnosed with [scoliosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scoliosis "Scoliosis") and was put in a chest-to-hips plastic brace for two years. Woodley told *[Us Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Us_Weekly "Us Weekly")* that "It's like wearing a tacky, disgusting, plastic [corset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corset "Corset") for 18 hours a day. In the beginning, it was hard to eat or breathe. And I had to give up [cross-country running](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_country_running "Cross country running"). But I needed to have it to realign my spine". Her condition did not affect her work on set as she "would [just] take the brace off during filming and put it back on during breaks".

## Career

### 1999–2010: Acting beginnings

Woodley began her acting career in 1999 with a minor role in the television film *[Replacing Dad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Replacing_Dad "Replacing Dad")*. She went on to feature in minor television roles in *[The District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_District "The District")* and *[Crossing Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossing_Jordan "Crossing Jordan")* (in the latter, she portrayed the 10-year-old version of [Jill Hennessy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jill_Hennessy "Jill Hennessy")'s title character). Next she had a leading role in the television film *A Place Called Home* (2004) as California Ford, which earned her a nomination for a [Young Artist Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Artist_Award "Young Artist Award") for [Best Leading Young Actress in a TV Movie, Miniseries or Special](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/26th_Young_Artist_Awards "26th Young Artist Awards"). She also originally played the young [Kaitlin Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaitlin_Cooper "Kaitlin Cooper") in *[The O.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_O.C. "The O.C.")* She appeared as the titular character [Felicity Merriman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felicity_Merriman "Felicity Merriman") in the television film *[Felicity: An American Girl Adventure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felicity:_An_American_Girl_Adventure "Felicity: An American Girl Adventure")* (2005). Her performance received another Young Artist Award nomination, this time for Best Performance in a TV Movie, Miniseries, or Special (Comedy or Drama). Following this, Woodley appeared in numerous guest roles in other television series, including *[Everybody Loves Raymond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everybody_Loves_Raymond "Everybody Loves Raymond")*, *[My Name Is Earl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Name_Is_Earl "My Name Is Earl")*, *[CSI: NY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSI:_NY "CSI: NY")*, *[Close to Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_to_Home_(2005_TV_series) "Close to Home (2005 TV series)")*, and *[Cold Case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_Case_(TV_series) "Cold Case (TV series)")*.

Woodley was cast as the main character, [Amy Juergens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Juergens "Amy Juergens"), in the ABC Family series *[The Secret Life of the American Teenager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Secret_Life_of_the_American_Teenager "The Secret Life of the American Teenager")* (2008–2013), about a 15-year-old girl who learns she is pregnant. The show explores the effects of her pregnancy on her family, friends and herself, as well as life at [Grant High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grant_High_School_(Los_Angeles) "Grant High School (Los Angeles)"). [Ken Tucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Tucker "Ken Tucker") of *[Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")* praised her performance, stating, "[It] lifts a well-meaning, rather brave, but ramshackle show a notch." Popular among viewers, the show became one of [ABC Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_Family_Worldwide "ABC Family Worldwide")'s most-watched telecasts throughout its five-season run, spanning over 121 episodes.

### 2011–2014: Film debut and breakthrough

In 2011, Woodley made her feature film debut in [Alexander Payne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Payne "Alexander Payne")'s *[The Descendants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Descendants "The Descendants")*, where she played Alex, the troubled elder daughter of Matt King (played by [George Clooney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Clooney "George Clooney")). Her performance received positive reviews from critics. [A. O. Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._O._Scott "A. O. Scott") from *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* said, "Ms. Woodley [gives] one of the toughest, smartest, most credible adolescent performances in recent memory." Peter Debruge from *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* said that her performance is a "revelation" and that "in the role of Alex, [she is] displaying both the edge and depth the role demands." Following accolades for her performance, Woodley received a [Golden Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards "Golden Globe Awards") nomination for [Best Supporting Actress – Motion Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture "Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actress – Motion Picture"), and won the [Independent Spirit Award for Best Supporting Female](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Spirit_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Female "Independent Spirit Award for Best Supporting Female"). Consequently, she was awarded the [Trophée Chopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troph%C3%A9e_Chopard "Trophée Chopard") at the 2012 [Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes_Film_Festival "Cannes Film Festival") and the 2012 [Santa Barbara International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Barbara_International_Film_Festival "Santa Barbara International Film Festival") Virtuoso Award. *[People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_(magazine) "People (magazine)")* named her one of 2012's "Most Beautiful at Every Age." Woodley was also considered one of the 55 faces of the future by *[Nylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nylon_Magazine "Nylon Magazine")*'s "Young Hollywood Issue". She also received the Emerging Star Spotlight Award at *[Elle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elle_(magazine) "Elle (magazine)")*'s 20th Annual "Women In Hollywood".

Woodley starred in the [film adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Spectacular_Now "The Spectacular Now") of Tim Tharp's novel, *[The Spectacular Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Spectacular_Now "The Spectacular Now"),* as Aimee Finecky, an innocent, bookish teenager who begins dating the charming, freewheeling high-school senior Sutter Keely ([Miles Teller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Teller "Miles Teller")). The film premiered at [Sundance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundance_Festival "Sundance Festival") on January 18, 2013. Her portrayal of Aimee garnered much praise; *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")'* critic Betsy Sharkey said that Woodley and Teller "bring such an authentic face of confidence and questioning, indifference and need, pain and denial, friendship and first love", while another critic from *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* said that they gave "remarkably strong performances" that "display a depth of feeling that's breathtaking in its simplicity and honest[y]." Additionally, Woodley won the [Special Jury Award for Acting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Sundance_Film_Festival_award_winners#2013 "List of Sundance Film Festival award winners"), alongside Teller, at the [2013 Sundance Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Sundance_Film_Festival "2013 Sundance Film Festival") and received a nomination for the [Independent Spirit Award for Best Female Lead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Spirit_Award_for_Best_Female_Lead "Independent Spirit Award for Best Female Lead").

In October 2012, Woodley was offered the role of [Mary Jane Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Jane_Watson "Mary Jane Watson") in *[The Amazing Spider-Man 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Amazing_Spider-Man_2 "The Amazing Spider-Man 2").* On June 19, 2013, she was cut from the film. Director [Marc Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Webb "Marc Webb") told *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")* that the cut was "a creative decision to streamline the story and focus on [Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man "Spider-Man") and [Gwen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwen_Stacy "Gwen Stacy") and their relationship," and that everyone loved working with Woodley. She had also signed on to star in *[White Bird in a Blizzard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Bird_in_a_Blizzard "White Bird in a Blizzard"),* directed by [Gregg Araki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Araki "Gregg Araki"). Although filming took place in October 2012, the film was not released until January 20, 2014, at the [Sundance Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Sundance_Film_Festival "2014 Sundance Film Festival") and then on October 24, 2014, to wider audiences, where it received mixed reviews. In the film, she plays teenager Katrina "Kat" Connors, whose life is thrown into chaos when her mother disappears. Critic Moira MacDonald commended her by saying, "Woodley's depiction of Kat is low-key, natural, and utterly unaffected; as she has in every role, she makes the character her own, with her scratchy little voice and level gaze."

In 2014, Woodley starred as [Beatrice "Tris" Prior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tris_Prior "Tris Prior") in the film *[Divergent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divergent_(film) "Divergent (film)"),* an adaptation of [Veronica Roth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veronica_Roth "Veronica Roth")'s best-selling [young-adult novel of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divergent_(novel) "Divergent (novel)"), and the first installment in *The Divergent Series.* Woodley was the first and only actress considered for the role of Tris. To prepare for the role, Woodley trained three to five days a week for four weeks in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago") before shooting for the film began. She had sessions of fight training, gun training, and knife training with stunt coordinator Garrett Warren. Set in a [dystopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dystopian "Dystopian") and [postapocalyptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postapocalyptic "Postapocalyptic") [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago"), the film received mixed reviews, but Woodley's performance as Tris received a positive reception; Sam Allard from *[Orlando Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Weekly "Orlando Weekly")* said, "with her performance as Tris Prior in *Divergent,* Woodley rescues and then raises up a film that could have been an utter disaster." *Divergent* reached the number-one spot at the box office during its opening weekend, and was a financial success. Woodley received the 2014 [CinemaCon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CinemaCon "CinemaCon")'s Female Star of Tomorrow Award.

Also in 2014, Woodley starred as [Hazel Grace Lancaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazel_Grace_Lancaster "Hazel Grace Lancaster") in *[The Fault in Our Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fault_in_Our_Stars_(film) "The Fault in Our Stars (film)"),* the film adaptation of [John Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Green "John Green")'s [novel of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fault_in_Our_Stars "The Fault in Our Stars"). She portrayed a 16-year-old cancer patient who meets and falls in love with Augustus Waters ([Ansel Elgort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansel_Elgort "Ansel Elgort"), who also played her brother in the *Divergent* series), a similarly affected teen from her cancer support group. Green said via [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter") about Woodley; "There were so many amazing auditions for the role of Hazel, but Shailene's love for the book and her understanding of Hazel blew me away." The film was a blockbuster success, grossing over $307 million worldwide. Woodley's performance received critical acclaim; [Peter Travers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Travers "Peter Travers") in *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")* called her a "sublime actress with a résumé that pretty much proves she's incapable of making a false move on camera", and [Richard Roeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Roeper "Richard Roeper") of *[Chicago Sun-Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Sun-Times "Chicago Sun-Times")* called her performance as Hazel Oscar-worthy, adding, "she's that memorable". On November 14, 2014, she received [The Hollywood Film Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18th_Hollywood_Film_Awards "18th Hollywood Film Awards") for Hollywood Breakout Performance – Actress for her depiction of Hazel.

Given her career breakthrough and continuing success since her film debut, she earned a nomination for the [BAFTA Rising Star Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Rising_Star_Award "BAFTA Rising Star Award") in 2015.

### 2015–2019: Hiatus and mature roles

In 2015, Woodley reprised her role as Tris in *[The Divergent Series: Insurgent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Divergent_Series:_Insurgent "The Divergent Series: Insurgent"),* the second installment in *The Divergent Series.* Her performance once again received critical acclaim, with Daniel M. Kimmel of *New England Movies Weekly* writing, "Woodley does solid work here as she's done elsewhere, and continues to be someone to watch." Despite a more negative critical reception than the previous film, *Insurgent* was commercially successful, making nearly $100 million in its worldwide debut and grossed $295.2 million worldwide. She reprised her role again in the final film of the series *[Allegiant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Divergent_Series:_Allegiant "The Divergent Series: Allegiant")* (2016). The film, however, was poorly reviewed by critics and a [box-office bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box-office_bomb "Box-office bomb"). Lionsgate had planned for the final film in the series, named *Ascendant,* to be made for television, but Woodley announced that she would not be a part of it. In an interview with *[MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV "MTV")*, Woodley clarified that she had every intention of doing the final *Divergent* installment but she wanted to see its rightful end, and to her, that meant finishing what she, the cast and crew set out to do from the beginning and that is making *Ascendant a* full-length feature film. She added, "I want to do justice to everybody who believes in the character of Tris as much as I believe in the character of Tris".

She next starred opposite [Joseph Gordon-Levitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Gordon-Levitt "Joseph Gordon-Levitt") in [Oliver Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Stone "Oliver Stone")'s biographical thriller *[Snowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowden_(film) "Snowden (film)")* (2016), in which Gordon-Levitt portrayed [Edward Snowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Snowden "Edward Snowden") and Woodley portrayed [Lindsay Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindsay_Mills "Lindsay Mills"), Snowden's girlfriend. The film made its debut at the [Toronto International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_International_Film_Festival "Toronto International Film Festival"). [Owen Gleiberman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Gleiberman "Owen Gleiberman")'s review said that Woodley "gives a performance of breathtaking dimension: As the movie goes on, she makes Lindsay supportive and selfish, loving and stricken."

Starting in 2015, Woodley took a break from filming for nearly a year and considered quitting as an actor, stating in a later interview that she "had hit a wall with acting" and "felt it was time to do something different". But she eventually "fell in love" with acting again through *[Big Little Lies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Little_Lies_(TV_series) "Big Little Lies (TV series)")*.

In 2017, Woodley starred as a sexual assault survivor, alongside [Nicole Kidman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Kidman "Nicole Kidman") and [Reese Witherspoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reese_Witherspoon "Reese Witherspoon"), in the [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO "HBO") critically acclaimed drama series *[Big Little Lies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Little_Lies_(TV_series) "Big Little Lies (TV series)")* directed by [Jean-Marc Vallée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Marc_Vall%C3%A9e "Jean-Marc Vallée"). Sarah Rense of *[Esquire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esquire_(magazine) "Esquire (magazine)")* called Woodley's performance "underrated" and "brought a quiet complexity", adding that "she was the most realistic character" and her "cool demeanor interrupted by sudden, short outbursts – just seemed so real to anyone who thinks more than they talk". She was nominated for an [Emmy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Awards "Emmy Awards") and [Golden Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards "Golden Globe Awards") Award for Best Supporting Actress in a Limited Series or Movie and Best Performance by an Actress in a Supporting Role in a Series, Limited Series or Motion Picture Made for Television respectively for her role. She reprised her role as Jane Chapman for the second season in 2019.

She also starred in and produced the biographical film *[Adrift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrift_(2018_film) "Adrift (2018 film)")* (2018), with [Baltasar Kormákur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltasar_Korm%C3%A1kur "Baltasar Kormákur") as director. Woodley learned [sailing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailing "Sailing") and did her own stunts on the film, ninety percent of which was shot on the open ocean off [Fiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiji "Fiji"). Daniel Feingold from *[WSVN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WSVN "WSVN")* called her performance as [Tami Oldham Ashcraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tami_Oldham_Ashcraft "Tami Oldham Ashcraft"), a real-life sailor who was stranded at sea after a storm, "Oscar-worthy". [Owen Gleiberman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Gleiberman "Owen Gleiberman") of *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* called her "a sensual actress", stating that she "has the gift of making sensuality dramatic; there's a beautiful severity to her features that allows you to feel the things she's showing you. That's a talent, but it's also an instinct". Woodley was awarded the Rising Star Award during the 2018 [Deauville Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deauville_Film_Festival "Deauville Film Festival").

In 2019, Woodley starred in the romantic drama *[Endings, Beginnings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endings,_Beginnings "Endings, Beginnings")* alongside [Sebastian Stan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Stan "Sebastian Stan"), [Jamie Dornan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Dornan "Jamie Dornan"), and [Matthew Gray Gubler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Gray_Gubler "Matthew Gray Gubler"). The film premiered at the [Toronto International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_International_Film_Festival "Toronto International Film Festival") and was introduced as semi-improvised, relying on an 80-page outline. Woodley played Daphne, a contemporary character attempting to get her life on track through self-realization. The film was poorly received with a critical consensus that reads, "*Endings, Beginnings* smothers its talented ensemble cast's committed work in a carelessly constructed, aimlessly dawdling story". [Peter Travers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Travers "Peter Travers") of [*Rolling* *Stone*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone") said that "it's the performances that carry the day with Woodley raising the bar in every scene".

### 2021–present: Further film and television work

In 2021, Woodley had a supporting role in [Kevin Macdonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Macdonald_(director) "Kevin Macdonald (director)")'s real-life [Guantanamo Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guantanamo_Bay_detention_camp "Guantanamo Bay detention camp") drama film *[The Mauritanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mauritanian "The Mauritanian")* alongside [Jodie Foster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jodie_Foster "Jodie Foster"), [Tahar Rahim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahar_Rahim "Tahar Rahim") and [Benedict Cumberbatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedict_Cumberbatch "Benedict Cumberbatch"). ## Artistry

Early in her career, Woodley admitted to being uneducated about the film and television industry. She said she is drawn to "human scripts" which "are raw, and real, and risky" with her playing "vulnerable scary" characters. She stated, "I'm such a happy, optimistic person in real life for some reason, I have a great time taking out my alter-ego, dark side on screen".

Acting since the age of five, Woodley is not a [method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_acting "Method acting") nor a [classically trained](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_acting "Classical acting") actor. She insisted, "I'm not a good actor, just a professional listener". Over the years, Woodley has altered her acting style by incorporating the method of whoever she's working with.

## Activism

### Activism and protests

Woodley is an avid [environmental activist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentalism "Environmentalism") and climate advocate. In 2010, she and her mother co-founded the [nonprofit organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonprofit_organization "Nonprofit organization") All it Takes, a youth leadership program that aims to educate young people to practice empathy, compassion, responsibility, and purpose in hopes to foster sustainable, positive change for themselves, others and the environment.

Woodley organized the *Up to Us* movement, a cross country caravan to the [Democratic National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_National_Convention "Democratic National Convention"). Its goal is to unite Americans, in an act of solidarity, to raise awareness on the political, social and environmental issues political candidates need to address leading up to the elections.

In 2016, Woodley [protested against the Dakota Access Pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_Access_Pipeline_protests "Dakota Access Pipeline protests"), an underground petroleum transport pipeline. She was arrested for criminal trespassing in [Saint Anthony, North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Anthony,_North_Dakota "Saint Anthony, North Dakota"). The arrest was broadcast as it happened, as Woodley was about to end her two-hour-long [Facebook livestream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook_live "Facebook live") of the protest. Her mugshot circulated widely on [social media platforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_media "Social media") along with the [hashtags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashtag_activism "Hashtag activism") #FreeShailene, #NoDAPL, and #IStandWithStandingRock. Consequently, Woodley gained support from fellow celebrities which created further DAPL discourse.

She was released after she reportedly posted [bail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bail "Bail") for herself and 26 other protestors who had been arrested. Ten days after her arrest, she authored a piece for [*Time*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)"), titled "The Truth About My Arrest", detailing her experience and raising awareness for the environmental and social issues surrounding the cause. She pleaded guilty and was sentenced to a year of probation.

### Boards and honors

Woodley received the Philanthropy Award at *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")*'s fourth Annual Power of Youth event for her work with All it Takes.

In mid-2016, Woodley joined the board of [Our Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Revolution "Our Revolution"), a political organization aimed to educate voters about issues, get people involved in the political process, support progressive policies such as [Medicare for All](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicare_for_All "Medicare for All"), and work to organize and elect [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism "Progressivism") leaders. Woodley was one of the speakers at a national [town hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_hall_meeting "Town hall meeting") event in 2018, titled "Solving Our Climate Crisis", hosted by [Our Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Revolution "Our Revolution") founder [Bernie Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Sanders "Bernie Sanders").

On September 29, 2016, Woodley was honored at the 20th Anniversary Global Green Environmental Awards, receiving the Entertainment Industry Environmental Leadership Award for co-founding All it Takes. In October 2016, she was given the Female EMA Futures Award during the 26th Annual [Environmental Media Association (EMA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_Media_Association "Environmental Media Association") Awards. She also received the Advocate Award at the 2016 *[InStyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InStyle "InStyle")* Awards. In 2017, the [Sierra Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Club "Sierra Club") honored Woodley at its 125th Anniversary Trail Blazers Ball, for her [grassroots activism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grassroots "Grassroots").

### Positions

Woodley supported and campaigned for [Bernie Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Sanders "Bernie Sanders") for president in 2016 and 2020.

Woodley supports [renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy "Renewable energy"). In 2017, she wrote an [opinion piece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_piece "Opinion piece") for *[The Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hill_(newspaper) "The Hill (newspaper)"),* titled "US Should Run on Renewable Energy by 2050". It ultimately gave her the opportunity to introduce the *100 by '50 Act*, a [bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_(law) "Bill (law)") that calls for the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") to aggressively reduce carbon pollution and achieve 100% clean and renewable energy by 2050, sponsored by [Senator Jeff Merkley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Merkley "Jeff Merkley").

In 2019, she supported *Indigenized Energy*, a local [nonprofit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonprofit_organization "Nonprofit organization") that brings [solar energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_energy "Solar energy") and jobs to [Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States "Native Americans in the United States"). In 2018, Woodley took [Suquamish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suquamish_people "Suquamish people") activist and musician Calina Lawrence to the [75th Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/75th_Golden_Globe_Awards "75th Golden Globe Awards") as her guest. They had met at [Standing Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_Rock "Standing Rock") while protesting the Dakota Access Pipeline.

### Campaigns

Woodley has lent her voice to several environmental campaigns such as the [Conservation International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_International "Conservation International")'s *[Nature Is Speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Is_Speaking "Nature Is Speaking")* series, focusing on its goal to reframe why conservation is important and personify different aspects of nature. She is a member of Conservation International's Leadership Council. She partnered with [American Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Express "American Express") and [Parley for the Oceans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parley_for_the_Oceans "Parley for the Oceans") on the *#BackourOceans* initiative to combat marine [plastic pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_pollution "Plastic pollution"). She also joined *Ocean Unite's* *30x30* mission to protect at least 30% of the Earth's oceans by 2030 and the WILDOCEANS's *Ocean Impact*, promoting the conservation of [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa "South Africa")'s oceans.

In July 2019, Woodley became an Oceans Ambassador for [Greenpeace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenpeace "Greenpeace") and embarked on a three-week-long expedition to the [Sargasso Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sargasso_Sea "Sargasso Sea") to study the impact of plastics and [microplastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microplastic "Microplastic") on marine life, and to document the importance of this unique ecosystem for protection under a new global ocean [treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty "Treaty") that is being negotiated at the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations"). She wrote a piece for [*Time*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)"), titled "How I'm Changing My Life to Help Save the Seas", chronicling the time she spent on the [Greenpeace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenpeace "Greenpeace") ship and the actions to take to save the marine life. The expedition reached its desired outcome in March 2023 as the [UN Ocean Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Seas_Treaty "High Seas Treaty") had finally been agreed at the United Nations. The agreement keeps the 30x30 target –protecting 30% of the world's oceans by 2030 – alive.

In 2022, Woodley became inaugural member of [GoodLeap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GoodLeap "GoodLeap")'s Advisory Council, which seeks to help the company continue its exponential growth and mission to connect a world where everyone can live more [sustainably](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainability "Sustainability"). In 2023, Woodley joined [Greenpeace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenpeace "Greenpeace") in [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris"), demanding the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations") create a strong and ambitious Global Treaty that would effectively end plastic pollution. This coincided with the [UNEP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Environment_Programme "United Nations Environment Programme")'s second round of Intergovernmental Negotiating Committee meeting, in Paris, which aims to secure a legally binding [Global Plastics Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_plastic_pollution_treaty "Global plastic pollution treaty").

Woodley is a signatory to the [Artists4Ceasefire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artists4Ceasefire "Artists4Ceasefire") letter. In 2023, she also appeared in the Italian docufilm [Materia Viva](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materia_Viva "it:Materia Viva") focused on [Waste from Electrical and Electronic Equipment (WEEE)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste_Electrical_and_Electronic_Equipment_Directive "Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment Directive") alongside [Susan Sarandon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Sarandon "Susan Sarandon") to talk about their care and activism for the environment.

### Products

In 2020, Woodley partnered with *Karün*, a certified [B Corp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B_Corporation_(certification) "B Corporation (certification)") that creates high-quality eyewear using recycled waste like fishing nets, ropes, and metals collected by local rural entrepreneurs. Their collaboration created the product line *Karün by Shailene Woodley*, building its identity around the connection of product design with nature protection.
