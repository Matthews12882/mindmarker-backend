# Cindy Crawford

**Cynthia Ann Crawford** (born February 20, 1966) is an American model, actress, and television personality. One of the most popular [supermodels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermodels "Supermodels") of the 1980s and 1990s, she achieved a ubiquitous presence on magazine covers, runways, and fashion campaigns. She subsequently expanded into acting and business ventures.

## Early life

Crawford was born in [DeKalb, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeKalb,_Illinois "DeKalb, Illinois"), on February 20, 1966, the daughter of Daniel Kenneth Crawford and Jennifer Sue Crawford-Moluf (née Walker). She has two sisters, Chris and Danielle, and a brother, Jeffery, who died of [childhood leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Childhood_leukemia "Childhood leukemia") at age 3.

On social media, she stated that her family had been in the United States for generations and that her ancestry is mostly German, English and French. She is a descendant of English [Puritan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puritan "Puritan") settler Thomas Trowbridge, who helped establish the [Congregational Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregational_Church "Congregational Church") in [New Haven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Haven "New Haven"). She was raised in the [Congregationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregationalist "Congregationalist") faith and found it "incredible" that religious values "trickled down" to her family. According to official census records, Crawford's paternal great-grandfather David Crawford was of Scottish ancestry from [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland "Northern Ireland"), who in 1904 settled in Wisconsin. Appearing in an episode of *[Who Do You Think You Are?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who_Do_You_Think_You_Are%3F_(U.S._TV_series) "Who Do You Think You Are? (U.S. TV series)")* in 2013, she discovered that her ancestors included European [nobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobility "Nobility") and that she is descended from [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne "Charlemagne").

In her sophomore year at high school, she received a call from a local clothing store regarding modeling work, only to discover it was a prank by two of her classmates. However, the following year, another store hired a number of high school girls, including Crawford, to work for them (including a fashion shoot). In her junior year, local photographer Roger Legel, whose duties included photographing a different college girl to be that week's coed in the *DeKalb Nite Weekly*, asked to take her picture for the publication; the result was Crawford's first cover. The photo and positive feedback she received were enough to convince her to take up modeling. Initially, she worked with a small agency, which was sold to [Elite Model Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elite_Model_Management "Elite Model Management") shortly after she signed. In 1983, she entered Elite's Look of the Year contest at 17 and made the national finals.

Crawford graduated from [DeKalb High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeKalb_High_School_(Illinois) "DeKalb High School (Illinois)") in 1984 as [valedictorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valedictorian "Valedictorian"). She earned an academic [scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholarship "Scholarship") to study [chemical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_engineering "Chemical engineering") at [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University "Northwestern University"), which she attended for only one quarter before dropping out to pursue a full-time modeling career. After working for photographer [Victor Skrebneski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Skrebneski "Victor Skrebneski") in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago"), she moved to [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") in 1986 and signed with the Elite New York modeling agency.

## Career

In 1987, Crawford appeared in the opening credits of the [Michael J. Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_J._Fox "Michael J. Fox") film *[The Secret of My Success](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Secret_of_My_Success_(1987_film) "The Secret of My Success (1987 film)")*. Three years later, she was featured alongside top models [Christy Turlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christy_Turlington "Christy Turlington"), [Linda Evangelista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Evangelista "Linda Evangelista"), [Tatjana Patitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatjana_Patitz "Tatjana Patitz") and [Naomi Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Campbell "Naomi Campbell") on the cover of the January 1990 edition of *[British Vogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Vogue "British Vogue")*. Crawford and the other four models subsequently appeared in the video for [George Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Michael "George Michael")'s hit "[Freedom! '90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom!_%2790#Music_video "Freedom! '90")" later that year. Subsequently, Crawford played the lost love of [Jon Bon Jovi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Bon_Jovi "Jon Bon Jovi") in the 1994 video for his version of "[Please Come Home For Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Please_Come_Home_For_Christmas "Please Come Home For Christmas")", "[John Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Taylor_(bass_guitarist) "John Taylor (bass guitarist)")" in the 2011 video for [Duran Duran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duran_Duran "Duran Duran")'s "Girl Panic" (featuring supermodels as the band, including Naomi Campbell as [Simon Le Bon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Le_Bon "Simon Le Bon")), and Headmistress in the 2015 video for [Taylor Swift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift "Taylor Swift")'s "[Bad Blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Blood_(Taylor_Swift_song) "Bad Blood (Taylor Swift song)")" as part of a cast including [Jessica Alba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Alba "Jessica Alba"), [Selena Gomez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selena_Gomez "Selena Gomez"), and fellow models [Lily Aldridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lily_Aldridge "Lily Aldridge"), [Cara Delevingne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cara_Delevingne "Cara Delevingne"), [Gigi Hadid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigi_Hadid "Gigi Hadid"), [Martha Hunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Hunt "Martha Hunt") and [Karlie Kloss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlie_Kloss "Karlie Kloss").

She was frequently featured on the cover of multiple fashion and lifestyle magazines, including *[Vogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vogue_(magazine) "Vogue (magazine)")*, *[W](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W_(magazine) "W (magazine)")*, *[People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_(magazine) "People (magazine)")*, *[Harper's Bazaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harper%27s_Bazaar "Harper's Bazaar")*, *[Elle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elle_(magazine) "Elle (magazine)")*, *[Cosmopolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmopolitan_(magazine) "Cosmopolitan (magazine)")*, and *[Allure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allure_(magazine) "Allure (magazine)")*. A partial count in 1998 totalled over 500 appearances. Crawford has walked the runway shows for [Chanel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanel "Chanel"), [Versace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versace "Versace"), [Dolce & Gabbana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolce_%26_Gabbana "Dolce & Gabbana"), [Christian Dior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Dior "Christian Dior"), [Calvin Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Klein "Calvin Klein"), [Ralph Lauren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Lauren "Ralph Lauren"), [Roberto Cavalli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Cavalli "Roberto Cavalli"), [Michael Kors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Kors "Michael Kors"), [Thierry Mugler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thierry_Mugler "Thierry Mugler"), [Todd Oldham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Oldham "Todd Oldham"), [DKNY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DKNY "DKNY") and [Valentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentino_SpA "Valentino SpA"). Crawford also appeared in many fashion campaigns during her career, including those for [Versace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versace "Versace"), [Calvin Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Klein "Calvin Klein"), [Escada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escada "Escada"), [David Yurman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Yurman "David Yurman"), [Oscar De La Renta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_De_La_Renta "Oscar De La Renta"), [Balmain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balmain_(fashion_house) "Balmain (fashion house)"), [Hermes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermes "Hermes"), [Ellen Tracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellen_Tracy "Ellen Tracy"), [Valentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentino_SpA "Valentino SpA"), [Bally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bally_Shoe "Bally Shoe"), [Liz Claiborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liz_Claiborne "Liz Claiborne"), [Hervé Leger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herv%C3%A9_Leger "Hervé Leger"), [Halston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halston "Halston"), [Anne Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Klein_(fashion_designer) "Anne Klein (fashion designer)"), [Isaac Mizrahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Mizrahi "Isaac Mizrahi"), [Blumarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blumarine "Blumarine"), [Guess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guess "Guess"), [Ink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ink "Ink"), [Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gap_Inc. "Gap Inc.") and [Revlon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revlon "Revlon"). She has also worked for [Omega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omega_SA "Omega SA"), [Maybelline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maybelline "Maybelline"), [Clairol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clairol "Clairol"), [Pepsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepsi "Pepsi"), and Chilean retail stores [Ripley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripley_S.A. "Ripley S.A.") (partner of [Macy's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macy%27s "Macy's")).

In July 1988, she posed nude for *[Playboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playboy "Playboy")* magazine in a shoot by photographer [Herb Ritts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herb_Ritts "Herb Ritts"). In October 1998, Crawford returned to the pages of *Playboy* for a second nude pictorial, again taken by Ritts. She was ranked number 5 on *Playboy'*s list of the 100 sexiest stars of the 20th century. A 1997 *[Shape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shape_(magazine) "Shape (magazine)")* magazine survey of 4,000 picked her as the second (after [Demi Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demi_Moore "Demi Moore")) most beautiful woman in the world. In 2002, Crawford was named one of the 50 most beautiful people by *[People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_(magazine) "People (magazine)")* magazine. In her forties, she claimed No. 26 in the 2006 Hot 100 issue of *[Maxim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxim_(magazine) "Maxim (magazine)")* magazine. She was named No. 3 on [VH1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VH1 "VH1")'s *40 Hottest Hotties of the 90s* and was named one of the "100 Hottest Women of All-Time" by *[Men's Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men%27s_Health_(magazine) "Men's Health (magazine)")*.

The [red Versace dress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Versace_dress_of_Cindy_Crawford "Red Versace dress of Cindy Crawford") she wore to the [63rd Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/63rd_Academy_Awards "63rd Academy Awards") in 1991 had a major influence on fashion, and many copies and fakes of the dress were produced. In 1992, Crawford—through [GoodTimes Home Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GoodTimes_Home_Video "GoodTimes Home Video") and her company Crawdaddy Productions—made an exercise video with Radu Teodorescu named *Cindy Crawford: Shape Your Body*; although criticised by some[*[who?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Words_to_watch#Unsupported_attributions "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Words to watch")*] for being unsafe,[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] it was hugely successful and led to two equally lucrative followups, *Cindy Crawford: The Next Challenge* in 1993 (again with Radu) and *Cindy Crawford: A New Dimension* in 2000; the latter, made with fitness expert [Kathy Kaehler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathy_Kaehler "Kathy Kaehler") and produced not long after Crawford gave birth to her first child, was aimed at new mothers getting back into shape. In 2001, Crawford also made a shorter fitness video aimed at children, *Mini-Muscles with Cindy Crawford and the Fit-wits*, an animated production featuring the voices of Crawford (who also appears at the beginning in live action), Radu and [Kobe Bryant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe_Bryant "Kobe Bryant").

The inaugural issue of *[George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_(magazine) "George (magazine)")*, a short lived political magazine in the 1990s, featured Crawford dressed like [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington "George Washington") on the cover. In 2005, the [American Society of Magazine Editors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_of_Magazine_Editors "American Society of Magazine Editors") listed it as the 22nd best magazine cover of the last 40 years.

Crawford has also been on TV and in films. From 1989 to 1995, Crawford was host and executive producer of [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV "MTV")'s *[House of Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Style "House of Style")*. In the early 1990s, Crawford starred in the [Pepsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepsi "Pepsi") and [Pepsi Stuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepsi_Stuff "Pepsi Stuff") advertising. In 1992, she appeared in Pepsi's [Super Bowl commercial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_commercial "Super Bowl commercial"), aired during [Super Bowl XXVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXVI "Super Bowl XXVI"). In 2016, Pepsi released a remake of the commercial, also featuring Crawford. In 1995, Crawford broke into movies as the female lead in the movie *[Fair Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_Game_(1995_film) "Fair Game (1995 film)")*. Her performance was panned by [critics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critic "Critic")—[Leonard Maltin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Maltin "Leonard Maltin") commented "In her acting debut, supermodel Crawford makes a good jogger." The film was also a financial failure, with expenses of $50 million and $11 million takings at the [box office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box_office "Box office"). In 2001, she costarred as part of an ensemble cast in *[The Simian Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simian_Line "The Simian Line")*. Again the film was not successful or critically acclaimed, but Crawford's acting was not criticized. She has had many lesser roles guest starring on TV and as supporting roles, often playing herself. For example, in 2000, she was one of the celebrities (along with [Victoria Silvstedt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Silvstedt "Victoria Silvstedt"), [Anna Falchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Falchi "Anna Falchi") and [Megan Gale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megan_Gale "Megan Gale")) playing themselves in the Italian comedy *[Body Guards – Guardie del corpo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_Guards_%E2%80%93_Guardie_del_corpo "Body Guards – Guardie del corpo")*. In the 1990s, Carol Shaw, her make-up artist, named a lipstick color after Crawford as a part of the Lorac Cosmetics lip-wear line.

In the late 1990s, she made a number of appearances in magician [David Copperfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Copperfield_(illusionist) "David Copperfield (illusionist)")'s stage shows, standing in for her fellow supermodel [Claudia Schiffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudia_Schiffer "Claudia Schiffer") as Copperfield's guest assistant. During these appearances, she took part in a number of different illusions including being levitated, [guillotined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillotine_(magic_trick) "Guillotine (magic trick)"), and [sawn in half](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawing_a_woman_in_half "Sawing a woman in half") in Copperfield's [Clearly Impossible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clearly_Impossible "Clearly Impossible") illusion. On a number of occasions, she joined Copperfield and Schiffer in a performance of the [Double Sawing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawing_a_woman_in_half#Double_sawing "Sawing a woman in half") illusion in which both women were sawn in half and then reassembled with their lower halves swapped.

### After modeling

Crawford quit full-time modeling in 2000 and now appears only occasionally in fashion [magazines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magazine "Magazine"). In 2005, Crawford created a line of beauty products with [Jean-Louis Sebagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Louis_Sebagh "Jean-Louis Sebagh") called Meaningful Beauty for [Guthy-Renker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guthy-Renker "Guthy-Renker"). Crawford has stated that she regularly receives certain cosmetic procedures, including [Botox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botox "Botox") and vitamin injections. She first saw a [plastic surgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_surgeon "Plastic surgeon") at the age of 29.

In 2005, Crawford launched a new line of furniture under the "Cindy Crawford Home Collection" name. The collection is manufactured by HM Richards Inc. She assisted in the creation of the line by consulting on the features, colors, or styles that fit the needs of families or reflected her own tastes. She also has a furniture line with [Rooms to Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rooms_to_Go "Rooms to Go"), [Raymour & Flanigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymour_%26_Flanigan "Raymour & Flanigan") and launched a home goods line with [J. C. Penney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._C._Penney "J. C. Penney") in late 2009.

In 2009, Crawford was one of many celebrities to be photographed by [Deborah Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_Anderson "Deborah Anderson") for the coffee table book *Room 23*, produced by philanthropist [Diana Jenkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diana_Jenkins "Diana Jenkins"). In addition to appearing in the book, Crawford was the cover model and wrote the dedication. *Becoming*, a book about Crawford's life and career co-written by Crawford and Katherine O'Leary, was published in September 2015.

Crawford appeared on the American version of *[Lip Sync Battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lip_Sync_Battle "Lip Sync Battle")* reenacting her performance in the bathtub from the [George Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Michael "George Michael") music for "[Freedom! '90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom!_%2790 "Freedom! '90")", performed by actor [Zachary Quinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zachary_Quinto "Zachary Quinto").

Crawford and her daughter Kaia appeared together on the cover of the April 2016 issue of *Vogue* Paris. In 2016, she became a spokesperson for [Acqua Minerale San Benedetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acqua_Minerale_San_Benedetto "Acqua Minerale San Benedetto") in Italy. In September 2017, Crawford, along with models [Claudia Schiffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudia_Schiffer "Claudia Schiffer"), [Naomi Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Campbell "Naomi Campbell"), [Helena Christensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helena_Christensen "Helena Christensen") and [Carla Bruni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carla_Bruni "Carla Bruni"), closed the [Versace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versace "Versace") spring/summer 2018 fashion show, which was an homage to the late [Gianni Versace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gianni_Versace "Gianni Versace").

In January 2019, Crawford, along with her husband [Rande Gerber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rande_Gerber "Rande Gerber"), [Michael Meldman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Meldman "Michael Meldman"), [Jeff Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Shell "Jeff Shell") and [Jay Sures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Sures "Jay Sures") announced they would purchase the Hollywood deli [Nate 'n Al](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nate_%27n_Al_of_Beverly_Hills "Nate 'n Al of Beverly Hills") to keep its doors open after three generations of family ownership.

Crawford was featured in a four-part [Apple TV+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_TV%2B "Apple TV+") [docuseries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_documentary "Television documentary") titled *The Super Models*, which premiered on September 20, 2023. The series also featured Naomi Campbell, Christy Turlington and Linda Evangelista and was directed by [Roger Ross Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ross_Williams "Roger Ross Williams") and Larissa Bills.

## Personal life

Crawford was married to actor [Richard Gere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Gere "Richard Gere") from 1991 until their divorce in 1995. She married businessman and former model [Rande Gerber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rande_Gerber "Rande Gerber") on May 29, 1998. They had two children, [Presley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presley_Gerber "Presley Gerber") (1999–2026) and [Kaia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaia_Gerber "Kaia Gerber") (born 2001).

In August 2021, the couple sold their home in [Trousdale Estates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trousdale_Estates "Trousdale Estates"), [Beverly Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beverly_Hills "Beverly Hills"), for a reported $13.5 million.

### Style and appearance

Crawford is 5 feet 9+1⁄2 inches (1.77 m) tall with brown hair and eyes. Her measurements are 34–25.5–36. Her trademark is a [mole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mole_(skin_marking) "Mole (skin marking)") (or "[beauty mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beauty_mark "Beauty mark")") above her upper lip. She is so closely associated with this physical feature that she appeared in an Australian advertising campaign for flavoured milk featuring a TV commercial wherein she "licked off" her own mole. In her early years of modelling, her resemblance to model [Gia Carangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gia_Carangi "Gia Carangi") led to her being called "Baby Gia".

## Advocacy and charity work

When Crawford was 10 years old, her three-year-old brother Jeff—whom she continues to praise as "the fourth most influential person in [her] life"—died of [leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia "Leukemia"). Since becoming a model, Crawford has made [childhood leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Childhood_leukemia "Childhood leukemia") a focal point of her charity work, donating proceeds of her calendars to [medical research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_research "Medical research"). Crawford has been a long-time supporter of the [pediatric oncology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pediatric_oncology "Pediatric oncology") program at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin%E2%80%93Madison "University of Wisconsin–Madison"), where Jeff was treated, stating that she believes he received the best care possible. She is also an honorary board member of the [Multiple Myeloma Research Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_Myeloma_Research_Foundation "Multiple Myeloma Research Foundation").

In 2007, she became an official supporter of the [Ronald McDonald House Charities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_McDonald_House_Charities "Ronald McDonald House Charities") and is a member of their celebrity board, called the Friends of RMHC. Crawford is on the honorary committee of the California Wildlife Center.

In 2021, Crawford recreated her famous Pepsi ad with photographer David Yarrow to help raise funds for the [American family Children's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Family_Children%27s_Hospital "American Family Children's Hospital") in [Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison,_Wisconsin "Madison, Wisconsin"), Wisconsin where her brother, Jeff, was treated for leukemia.

### Political endorsements

In [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election "2008 United States presidential election"), Crawford endorsed [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") for [President of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States "President of the United States"). Despite reports, Crawford denied endorsing [Mitt Romney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitt_Romney "Mitt Romney") for the 2012 election.
