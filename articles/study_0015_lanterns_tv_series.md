# Lanterns (TV series)

***Lanterns*** is an American [superhero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superhero_fiction "Superhero fiction") television series created by Chris Mundy, [Damon Lindelof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damon_Lindelof "Damon Lindelof"), and [Tom King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_King_(writer) "Tom King (writer)") for [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO "HBO"), based on the [DC Comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Comics "DC Comics") [Green Lantern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Lantern "Green Lantern") characters [Hal Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hal_Jordan "Hal Jordan") and [John Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stewart_(character) "John Stewart (character)"). It is the third television series in the [DC Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Universe_(franchise) "DC Universe (franchise)") (DCU). It features experienced Lantern Hal Jordan and new recruit John Stewart as they team up with local sheriff Kerry Kane to investigate murders in [Rushville, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rushville,_Nebraska "Rushville, Nebraska") that leads to a wider mystery. The series was produced by [DC Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Studios "DC Studios") and [Warner Bros. Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Television "Warner Bros. Television") with Mundy serving as showrunner.

[Kyle Chandler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Chandler "Kyle Chandler"), [Aaron Pierre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Pierre_(actor) "Aaron Pierre (actor)") and [Kelly Macdonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_Macdonald "Kelly Macdonald") respectively star as Jordan, Stewart and Kerry. [Garret Dillahunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garret_Dillahunt "Garret Dillahunt"), [Poorna Jagannathan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poorna_Jagannathan "Poorna Jagannathan") and [Ulrich Thomsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulrich_Thomsen "Ulrich Thomsen") also star. [Greg Berlanti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Berlanti "Greg Berlanti") began developing a Green Lantern television series by October 2019. After [James Gunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gunn "James Gunn") and [Peter Safran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Safran "Peter Safran") became co-CEOs of DC Studios in October 2022, the series was redeveloped. It was announced in January 2023, along with its [detective story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detective_fiction "Detective fiction") style, which was inspired by the series *[True Detective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Detective "True Detective")* (2014–present) and *[Slow Horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slow_Horses "Slow Horses")* (2022–present). The involvement of Mundy, Lindelof, and King was confirmed in mid-2024 when HBO ordered the series. *Slow Horses*'s [James Hawes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hawes "James Hawes") was hired to direct the first two episodes in October, when Chandler and Pierre were cast. Filming took place in Los Angeles from February to July 2025, with [Stephen Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Williams_(director) "Stephen Williams (director)"), [Geeta Vasant Patel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geeta_Vasant_Patel "Geeta Vasant Patel"), and [Alik Sakharov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alik_Sakharov "Alik Sakharov") also directing.

*Lanterns* premiered on August 16, 2026, and ran for eight episodes through October 4. It is part of the DCU's [Chapter One: Gods and Monsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapter_One:_Gods_and_Monsters "Chapter One: Gods and Monsters"). The series received positive reviews and had strong viewership.

## Premise

[Green Lanterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Lantern_(DC_Universe) "Green Lantern (DC Universe)") are interstellar peacekeepers who wear [rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_ring_(DC_Universe) "Power ring (DC Universe)") that give them extraordinary powers. The series follows experienced Lantern [Hal Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hal_Jordan_(DC_Universe) "Hal Jordan (DC Universe)") and new recruit [John Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stewart_(DC_Universe) "John Stewart (DC Universe)") as they investigate a murder in [Rushville, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rushville,_Nebraska "Rushville, Nebraska"), which Hal believes is an extraterrestrial incident and leads them to darker mysteries and reckonings.

## Cast and characters

### Main

* [Kyle Chandler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Chandler "Kyle Chandler") as [Hal Jordan / Green Lantern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hal_Jordan_(DC_Universe) "Hal Jordan (DC Universe)"):
  A [USAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force "United States Air Force") veteran, former test pilot, and legendary member of the [Green Lantern Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Lantern_Corps_(DC_Universe) "Green Lantern Corps (DC Universe)"), who is responsible for protecting Sector 2814. In the series, Hal is approaching retirement, and is filling in to train John Stewart. The [Guardians of the Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardians_of_the_Universe_(DC_Universe) "Guardians of the Universe (DC Universe)"), who founded and lead the Corps, are concerned about what Hal may or may not have inherited from his former mentor Thaal Sinestro. The writers took inspiration for the character from [Sam Shepard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Shepard "Sam Shepard")'s portrayal of [Chuck Yeager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Yeager "Chuck Yeager") in the film *[The Right Stuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Right_Stuff_(film) "The Right Stuff (film)")* (1983). [Showrunner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showrunner "Showrunner") Chris Mundy felt Chandler had the same qualities as well as a "dry wit" that they felt was important for Hal. Henry Samiri portrays a young Hal in flashback scenes.
* [Aaron Pierre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Pierre_(actor) "Aaron Pierre (actor)") as [John Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stewart_(DC_Universe) "John Stewart (DC Universe)"):
  A new [Green Lantern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Lantern_(DC_Universe) "Green Lantern (DC Universe)") recruit whom Hal is training to replace himself. John is considered an anomaly because he is the first Green Lantern in the Corps' history to have been recruited by the Guardians of the Universe instead of being chosen by a [power ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_ring_(DC_Universe) "Power ring (DC Universe)"), as is customary. Mundy said the character was both a [Marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marine_Corps "United States Marine Corps") and an artistic architect, and felt Pierre could portray both of these aspects. He said Pierre was a "serious theater actor, yet he also looks like he was built in a lab to be an action star". Director [James Hawes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hawes "James Hawes") said Pierre had "a magnificent presence. He feels so forceful, so cool, so understated." Jayce Eiland and Cairo Cash Lee portray John as a child and a teenager, respectively, in flashback scenes.
* [Kelly Macdonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_Macdonald "Kelly Macdonald") as Kerry Kane: The sheriff of [Rushville, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rushville,_Nebraska "Rushville, Nebraska"), who originally hailed from [Gotham City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotham_City_(DC_Universe) "Gotham City (DC Universe)"). Kerry is deeply devoted to her family and close-knit town, while also territorial around Hal and John upon their arrival at Rushville. Kerry is a love interest for Hal in the series.
* [Garret Dillahunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garret_Dillahunt "Garret Dillahunt") as William "Will" Macon: Kerry's father-in-law and a powerful rancher whose avowed aim is to protect his community from alien threats
* [Poorna Jagannathan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poorna_Jagannathan "Poorna Jagannathan") as Zoe Macon: A [Manhunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhunters_(DC_Comics) "Manhunters (DC Comics)") posing as William's human wife who seduces John to gain his trust
* [Ulrich Thomsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulrich_Thomsen "Ulrich Thomsen") as [Thaal Sinestro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinestro_(DC_Universe) "Sinestro (DC Universe)"): A "ruthless yet undeniably charming" former Green Lantern, and Hal's mentor and father figure, who went rogue and was expelled from the Green Lantern Corps after a failed [coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup "Coup"), during which he killed one of the twelve Guardians of the Universe. Sinestro appears imprisoned in space as of 2016, though he had escaped by 2026.

## Production

### Background

[Greg Berlanti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Berlanti "Greg Berlanti"), the producer of multiple [DC Comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Comics "DC Comics")–based television series, announced that he was developing a series based on the [Green Lantern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Lantern "Green Lantern") characters for the streaming service [HBO Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO_Max "HBO Max") in October 2019. Berlanti had previously co-written the film *[Green Lantern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Lantern_(film) "Green Lantern (film)")* (2011). In January 2020, he said the series would span several decades and tell two stories about Green Lanterns on Earth, as well as a story about the villainous character [Sinestro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinestro "Sinestro") in space. The series was officially picked up for a 10-episode season by HBO Max that October. [Marc Guggenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Guggenheim "Marc Guggenheim"), who also co-wrote the *Green Lantern* film, and [Seth Grahame-Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Grahame-Smith "Seth Grahame-Smith") were revealed to be writing the series, with Grahame-Smith serving as [showrunner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showrunner "Showrunner"). The series could not make use of the main Green Lanterns from the comic books, [Hal Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hal_Jordan "Hal Jordan") and [John Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stewart_(character) "John Stewart (character)"), because they were being reserved for [DC Extended Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Extended_Universe "DC Extended Universe") (DCEU) films. Instead, the series was set to explore other Green Lanterns from the comics, including [Guy Gardner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Gardner_(character) "Guy Gardner (character)"), [Jessica Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Cruz "Jessica Cruz"), [Simon Baz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Baz "Simon Baz"), [Alan Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Scott "Alan Scott"), [Kilowog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilowog "Kilowog"), and new Green Lanterns created for the series.

In April 2021, the series was revealed to primarily focus on Scott, a [secretly gay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closeted "Closeted") [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI "FBI") agent in 1941 who becomes Earth's first Green Lantern, and Gardner, an "embodiment of 1980s hyper-patriotism", alongside the half-alien Bree Jarta in 1984. [Finn Wittrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finn_Wittrock "Finn Wittrock") was cast as Gardner after Berlanti received permission from producer [Ryan Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Murphy_(producer) "Ryan Murphy (producer)") for the actor to prioritize *Green Lantern* over the planned second season of Murphy's [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") series *[Ratched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratched_(TV_series) "Ratched (TV series)")* (2020). An actor was in negotiations to portray Scott. The series was said to be the most expensive in Berlanti's career and was expected to begin filming later in 2021. Berlanti had been writing with Grahame-Smith and Guggenheim, who were all serving as executive producers alongside [Geoff Johns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoff_Johns "Geoff Johns"), [Sarah Schechter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Schechter_(producer) "Sarah Schechter (producer)"), [David Madden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Madden_(executive) "David Madden (executive)"), and David Katzenberg. [Pornsak Pichetshote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pornsak_Pichetshote "Pornsak Pichetshote"), who previously served as an editor for DC's [Vertigo Comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertigo_Comics "Vertigo Comics") imprint and was an executive at [DC Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Entertainment "DC Entertainment") overseeing its television content, was later revealed to be part of the [writers' room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writers%27_room "Writers' room") as well. [Jeremy Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Irvine "Jeremy Irvine") was revealed to be in talks to portray Scott in May, and was officially cast soon after. [Lee Toland Krieger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Toland_Krieger "Lee Toland Krieger") was hired to direct the first two episodes at the end of the month. In August 2021, Wittrock said filming would begin at the end of the year or in early 2022.

In April 2022, [Discovery, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery,_Inc. "Discovery, Inc.") and Warner Bros.' parent company [WarnerMedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WarnerMedia "WarnerMedia") merged to become [Warner Bros. Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Discovery "Warner Bros. Discovery") (WBD), led by president and CEO [David Zaslav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Zaslav "David Zaslav"). The new company was expected to restructure DC Entertainment while Zaslav began searching for an equivalent to [Marvel Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Studios "Marvel Studios") president [Kevin Feige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Feige "Kevin Feige") to lead the new subsidiary. In June, Irvine said there was no scheduled start date for filming the series and the production was working on "getting all the stars to align". A month later, the series was reaffirmed to be in development despite the cancellation of other HBO Max and DC projects by WBD. In October 2022, Grahame-Smith was revealed to have left the series, which was being redeveloped to focus on Stewart. [James Gunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gunn "James Gunn") and [Peter Safran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Safran "Peter Safran") were announced as the co-chairs and co-CEOs of the newly formed [DC Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Studios "DC Studios") at the end of that month. A week after starting their new roles, the pair had begun developing an eight-to-ten-year plan for a new [DC Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Universe_(franchise) "DC Universe (franchise)") (DCU) that would be a "[soft reboot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_reboot_(fiction) "Soft reboot (fiction)")" of the DCEU.

### Development

Gunn said in December 2022 that [Green Lantern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Lantern_(DC_Universe) "Green Lantern (DC Universe)") characters would be an important part of the new DCU. On January 31, 2023, he and Safran unveiled the first projects from their DCU slate, which begins with [Chapter One: Gods and Monsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapter_One:_Gods_and_Monsters "Chapter One: Gods and Monsters"). The third television series in the slate was *Lanterns*, a new iteration of the long-in-development Green Lantern series. This version features the two best-known Green Lanterns, [Hal Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hal_Jordan_(DC_Universe) "Hal Jordan (DC Universe)") and [John Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stewart_(DC_Universe) "John Stewart (DC Universe)"), and Safran said it would be an Earth-based [detective story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detective_fiction "Detective fiction") rather than the [space opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_opera "Space opera") that Berlanti had envisioned. He said the series would be an "HBO-quality event" in the style of [crime drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_fiction "Crime fiction") series *[True Detective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Detective "True Detective")* (2014–present), and [spy thriller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spy_thriller "Spy thriller") series *[Slow Horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slow_Horses "Slow Horses")* (2022–present). In January 2023, Safran said the mystery that Jordan and Stewart investigate leads into the main storyline for the DCU, so the series was an important project for Gunn and Safran.

[Damon Lindelof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damon_Lindelof "Damon Lindelof") was consulting on the series as a producer by January 2024, when it was said to be a priority project for HBO Max (at that time briefly renamed to simply "[Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_(streaming_service) "Max (streaming service)")"). The following month, Chris Mundy was reported to be serving as [showrunner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showrunner "Showrunner") while [Tom King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_King_(writer) "Tom King (writer)"), a member of the DC Studios writers' room, was also attached as a producer. Gunn confirmed the involvement of Mundy, King, and Lindelof in May 2024. King had originally [pitched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitch_(filmmaking) "Pitch (filmmaking)") the concept for the series, and Mundy was hired by Gunn and Safran based on his work on the crime drama *[Ozark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozark_(TV_series) "Ozark (TV series)")* (2017–2022) as well as the [fourth season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Detective_season_4 "True Detective season 4") of *True Detective* (2024). Mundy and King began developing the series together and enlisted Lindelof, who created the DC Comics–based limited series *[Watchmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watchmen_(TV_series) "Watchmen (TV series)")* (2019). Lindelof agreed to join because of his love for *Ozark* and King's comic books. Mundy, Lindelof, and King are credited as the series' creators.

The series received an eight-episode straight-to-series order from Max's corporate sibling channel [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO "HBO") in June 2024, when Mundy was confirmed as showrunner and executive producer. The move to HBO was made after WBD decided to shift many of its planned big-budget Max series based on their own intellectual property to be HBO originals instead; *Lanterns* was expected to still stream on Max in addition to airing on HBO. Each episode was expected to have an approximately hour-long runtime. DC Studios was meeting with possible directors for the series' pilot by September, including [Stephen Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Williams_(director) "Stephen Williams (director)"), who worked with Lindelof on *Watchmen*. [James Hawes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hawes "James Hawes"), who directed the first season of *Slow Horses*, was hired to direct the first two episodes of *Lanterns* and serve as an executive producer the next month. In February 2025, Williams was confirmed to be directing for the series, alongside [Geeta Vasant Patel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geeta_Vasant_Patel "Geeta Vasant Patel") and [Alik Sakharov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alik_Sakharov "Alik Sakharov"), while Ron Schmidt joined as an executive producer the following month.

Also in February 2025, Safran stated that the series could continue beyond its first season. Mundy added that *Lanterns* would stand alone from other DC Studios projects and was designed to tell a complete story, but he hoped that it could expand to have multiple seasons. *Lanterns* was expected to be a multi-season series by May 2026, when television and comic book writer [Christopher Cantwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Cantwell_(writer) "Christopher Cantwell (writer)") was hired to work on a potential second season as a writer, executive producer, and co-showrunner alongside Mundy. Lindelof and King did not return as writers because of their commitments to the HBO series *[The Chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Chain_(TV_series) "The Chain (TV series)")* and the DCU animated series *[Mister Miracle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mister_Miracle_(TV_series) "Mister Miracle (TV series)")*, respectively, though King remained involved in some capacity. A writers' room had been working on the season for more than a month, around six weeks, by that August, but HBO was waiting to greenlight a second season until after the series premiered later that month to gauge its critical and audience reception and viewership ratings. On the potential demand for a second season, Mundy stated, "We feel good that we told the story in season one well. The question is whether [season two] is a story people are interested in. If that's the case, then we'll get to do another one. We'll tell it in a slightly different way, so that it feels fresh, with characters you know and care about." He added that they were "just in the breaking-story stages" and that they would discuss it with HBO and DC Studios executives "really soon" once they had mapped out a whole season.

### Writing

Mundy, King, and Lindelof had written the pilot script and [series bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Series_bible "Series bible") for *Lanterns* by the end of May 2024, when they were assembling a full writers' room. Justin Britt-Gibson, Breannah Gibson, and [Vanessa Baden Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanessa_Baden "Vanessa Baden") are also writers on the series. They were present on set to answer questions regarding comic book history and instructed the actors to have a holistic understanding of Jordan and Stewart's characters rather than reading comics from a specific time period. King also took inspiration from his experience as a [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA "CIA") [counterterrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterterrorism "Counterterrorism") officer. Britt-Gibson previously worked with Lindelof on the script for [an untitled *Star Wars* film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Star_Wars_films#Untitled_Sharmeen_Obaid-Chinoy_film_(TBA) "List of Star Wars films"). Writing for the series was completed by the start of filming in February 2025.

*Lanterns* unfolds across two storylines: the first, set in 2016, involves a murder in [Rushville, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rushville,_Nebraska "Rushville, Nebraska"), which Jordan believes to be an extraterrestrial incident; and the second, involving Jordan's own murder, takes place in 2026. Mundy likened the story's structure to the "emotional mysteries" in the [first season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Detective_season_1 "True Detective season 1") of *True Detective* (2014), which he described as less of a traditional [whodunit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whodunit "Whodunit") and more of an exploration of why the mystery takes place, bound by the relationship between Jordan and Stewart in *Lanterns* that Mundy compared to the characters [Rust Cohle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rust_Cohle "Rust Cohle") and [Marty Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marty_Hart "Marty Hart") from *True Detective*. The series primarily occurs in [Sheridan County, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheridan_County,_Nebraska "Sheridan County, Nebraska"), with some scenes taking place in [Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa "Iowa"). It does not adapt any specific storylines from the comics, though Mundy said it is "steeped" in the Green Lantern comic book lore, as the main characters possess [power rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_ring_(DC_Universe) "Power ring (DC Universe)"), the primary weapons of the [Green Lantern Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Lantern_Corps_(DC_Universe) "Green Lantern Corps (DC Universe)") and the source of their powers, while other aspects of the lore are either seen or referenced in the series. However, the series is more grounded than the comics. Mundy said he was excited to create "something really grounded inside this big, amazing mythology. From the beginning, all we talked about was, how can we take all the things we loved about the source material and turn it into a layered, human HBO drama?" He wanted the series to be accessible to audiences unfamiliar with the comics' history, yet satisfying to those who were. Mundy emphasized the series spanning across various timeframes and the presence of the [buddy cop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddy_cop "Buddy cop") elements, noting that Jordan and Stewart would act as "opposing forces" in season one. Lindelof, Mundy and King decided early in development to kill Hal as a [plot twist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plot_twist "Plot twist") to provide greater emotional stakes and deepen his relationship with John. They initially considered Sheriff Kerry Kane as the victim but rejected the idea to avoid using the "[fridging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_refrigerators "Women in refrigerators")" trope. Hal's hologram references Hal's multiple deaths in the source material. The creators also changed Hal's [origin story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_story "Origin story"), revealing through the Halogram that his father, [Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Jordan "Martin Jordan"), died by suicide rather than in an accident. This change allowed them to explore Hal's fear while creating their own interpretation of his origins.

Gunn was excited for the series to have a different tone from the first DCU film *[Superman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superman_(2025_film) "Superman (2025 film)")* (2025), despite that project also featuring a Green Lantern in [Guy Gardner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Gardner_(DC_Universe) "Guy Gardner (DC Universe)"), whom Mundy described as "fabulously obnoxious" and said would appear at "a few different times" in the series; the events of *Superman* occur between the two time periods of the series. Gardner appears as part of the 2026 storyline, with references to the events of *Superman*. Mundy explained the creative team had full creative freedom over the story and were not required to make it adhere to any other DCU projects. He further elaborated that the story did not have any required "specific landing point," feeling particularly satisfied with Stewart's characterization. Gunn also teased that *Lanterns* would be "really important in setting up things" in the wider context of the DCU.

### Design

Cynthia Summers and John Paino were, respectively, the costume and production designers. The production team created 20 designs for the Lantern before finalizing it. Jordan and Stewart both wear civilian clothing throughout the series, with Jordan's dusty leather jacket and salt-and-pepper hair resembling his appearance in the [1990s *Green Lantern* comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Lantern_(comic_book)#Volume_3_(1990–2004) "Green Lantern (comic book)") drawn by [Pat Broderick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Broderick "Pat Broderick") that featured an older Jordan with white streaks of hair. Jordan's Green Lantern suit in the series is a muted, faded green jacket, contrasting the more colorful and poppy green-and-black suits that the characters are typically depicted wearing. The suit's design is reminiscent of the ones in Gabriel Hardman and Corinna Bechko's *[Green Lantern: Earth One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Lantern:_Earth_One "Green Lantern: Earth One")* (2018–2020) graphic novels, which also feature a more grounded approach. Summers stated they did not want Jordan's suit to resemble a futuristic sci-fi aesthetic.

### Filming

[Principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography "Principal photography") began during the week of February 17, 2025, in Los Angeles, California, as well as at [Warner Bros. Studios Burbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Studios_Burbank "Warner Bros. Studios Burbank") in [Burbank, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burbank,_California "Burbank, California"), under the [working title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_title "Working title") *Latitude*. Armando Salas and [Florian Hoffmeister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florian_Hoffmeister "Florian Hoffmeister") served as cinematographers. The Stewart family home was created on Stage 19 of the Burbank lot. Filming was previously expected to occur in [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta "Atlanta"), Georgia, from January to June. Gunn and Safran worked with WBD to get the tax credits and studio deals necessary to move the production from Atlanta, where Gunn filmed *Superman*, to Los Angeles. Safran said they were thrilled to be filming in the city and supporting locals following the [January 2025 Southern California wildfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2025_Southern_California_wildfires "January 2025 Southern California wildfires"). Scenes set in Rushville, Nebraska were filmed in [Piru, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piru,_California "Piru, California"), with its main street location doubling for downtown Rushville. A diner was also built in the town while the wardrobe department was located in the town's Corazon de Piru business. Hawes said comparisons to *True Detective* were valid due to Mundy's writing and the buddy cop structure, but *Lanterns* had more humor than that series. He compared it to films with "[Americana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americana "Americana") heart" such as *[Fargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fargo_(1996_film) "Fargo (1996 film)")* (1996) and *[No Country for Old Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Country_for_Old_Men "No Country for Old Men")* (2007). Mundy said Hawes's direction for the first two episodes captured the scope he was hoping for.

Williams, Patel, and Sakharov directed additional episodes for the series. Four episodes had been filmed by early May, when Hawes [wrapped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrap_(filmmaking) "Wrap (filmmaking)") filming his episodes. At that time, filming was about to begin on the third and fourth episodes. Ritter said that the story was "jumping all around". Filming was more than halfway finished by mid-June, and Thomsen said filming for his scenes concluded by the end of July. Pierre confirmed that production had wrapped by at least that October. He also said that the filmmakers extensively discussed the characters' flaws on set, and noted that the characters "do their best to redeem themselves actively".

## Marketing

A first-look promotional image of Pierre's John Stewart and Chandler's Hal Jordan was released in February 2025. Armando Tinoco of *[Deadline Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadline_Hollywood "Deadline Hollywood")* described the pair as "looking ready for action", while Rachel Leishman at *[The Mary Sue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mary_Sue "The Mary Sue")* admired their appearances, dubbing the image "hot". She questioned whether the extent of Jordan's role would be limited to a single season because the image only featured one Green Lantern power ring. Germain Lussier at *[Gizmodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gizmodo "Gizmodo")* was surprised by the grounded tone and the characters' civilian clothing, describing it as depicting "Two dudes, standing on the street, looking like hell. And almost no green at all." He felt the image aligned with the series's Earth-based setting and was what DC Studios was ready to show, considering that filming had only recently started. He expected later promotional material to depict some more cosmic elements. Other commentators also addressed the notable absence of the characters' traditional Green Lantern suits and how the image's gritty and grounded tone contrasted the superhero and [space opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_opera "Space opera") aspects that many would have expected from a Green Lantern adaptation.

A teaser trailer was shown at HBO Max's [upfront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upfront_(advertising) "Upfront (advertising)") presentation in [São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo "São Paulo"), Brazil, on November 25, 2025. Matthew Aguilar at *[ComicBook.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ComicBook.com "ComicBook.com")* felt the footage offered exciting elements and "plenty of room for rich dynamics" between Jordan and Stewart, as well as how the series incorporates the characters' cosmic aspects while maintaining focus on the grounded plot. The first footage was released on December 12 during an HBO Max sizzle reel for their 2026 projects, with Marco Vito Oddo of *ComicBook.com* opining that the footage conveyed Jordan and Stewart's "friction-heavy" relationship while establishing the stakes as being "life and death at the very beginning". The teaser trailer was released online on March 4, 2026, a day earlier than intended after it had leaked online. Discussion of the teaser trailer focused on the grounded *True Detective* buddy cop nature of the series and its correlation to the Green Lantern mythos. While Jennifer Ouellette at *[Ars Technica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ars_Technica "Ars Technica")* felt the teaser was light on revealing plot details, she described it as "tonally unique for the DCU" and one of her most-anticipated series for the year. Joe George at *[Den of Geek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Den_of_Geek "Den of Geek")* commended that Chandler "fully looks the part" of the older Jordan from the 1990s *Green Lantern* comics. He contrasted Jordan's calm yet cocky demeanour to [Mel Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mel_Gibson "Mel Gibson")'s [Martin Riggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Riggs "Martin Riggs") from the *Lethal Weapon* films and [Andy Samberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Samberg "Andy Samberg")'s [Jake Peralta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Peralta "Jake Peralta") from the television series *[Brooklyn Nine-Nine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Nine-Nine "Brooklyn Nine-Nine")* (2013–2021). Some commentators questioned the absence of the more fantastical elements typically associated with the Green Lantern characters, comparing *Lanterns* more to the *[X-Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-Men_(film_series) "X-Men (film series)")* film *[Logan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_(film) "Logan (film)")* (2017) as well as the television series *[Yellowstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellowstone_(TV_series) "Yellowstone (TV series)")* (2018–2024) and *[Reacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reacher_(TV_series) "Reacher (TV series)")* (2022–present). The teaser featured the song "[State Trooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Trooper_(song) "State Trooper (song)")" by [Bruce Springsteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Springsteen "Bruce Springsteen"), but was temporarily taken down the following month due to an expired music license, before being re-uploaded with a different musical score. Nick Romano at *[Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")* explained that this was a "fairly common practice when it comes to trailer and teaser assets that contain licensed tracks", and refuted speculation that its removal stemmed from comments Lindelof made joking about the lack of the color green in marketing up to that point.

A second teaser trailer was released on May 18, 2026. The trailer was noted by commentators for the appearances of Linney and Thomsen's Sinestro; the discussion of fear, relating to the [Yellow Lantern Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Lantern_Corps "Yellow Lantern Corps"); and for showcasing Jordan wearing his Green Lantern suit and using his power ring to create green constructs, both of which were not present in previous marketing material. George further praised Chandler's performance and noted that "many of the promos and interviews leading into the show have been strangely reluctant to embrace the franchise's sci-fi roots, [...] even stripping the color green from the series' title and marketing". Tom Power at *[TechRadar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TechRadar "TechRadar")* believed the inclusion of such elements in this trailer was a direct response to criticsm and concerns about the lack of comics-accurate aspects in the first trailer, while Scott Davis at *[SciFiNow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SciFiNow "SciFiNow")* felt the trailer was an improvement over the first, calling it "much more impressive". The official trailer was revealed at [San Diego Comic-Con](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Comic-Con "San Diego Comic-Con") on July 24, 2026, during a panel at Hall H; the panel was joined by the series's co-creators Mundy, Lindelof, and King, alongside cast members Pierre, Chandler, Dillahunt, Jagannathan, and Fillion. At the panel, Mundy revealed Linney's role.

## Release

*Lanterns* premiered on August 16, 2026, airing on HBO and streaming on HBO Max, and consists of eight episodes, which were released weekly until October 4. It is part of the DCU's Chapter One: Gods and Monsters. In February 2025, Gunn and Safran stated that they could "safely" say the series would premiere in early 2026. In November 2025, the series was reaffirmed for an expected early 2026 debut in Warner Bros. Discovery's third-quarter shareholder letter. However, at the end of the month, HBO and HBO Max chairman and CEO Casey Bloys announced that *Lanterns* would premiere at the "end of summer" 2026. In March 2026, the series was revealed to premiere that August.

## Reception

### Viewership

The premiere attracted 9.3 million viewers worldwide during its first three days on HBO and HBO Max, including 6.6 million in the United States. Its U.S. premiere audience surpassed those of several recent HBO series, such as *[It: Welcome to Derry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It_%E2%80%93_Welcome_to_Derry "It – Welcome to Derry")* (2025–present) and *[The Penguin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Penguin_(TV_series) "The Penguin (TV series)")* (2024), and became the biggest premiere for a DC Studios series on HBO Max. The third episode reached a series high of 10 million viewers worldwide and 7 million in the U.S. during its first three days. The series continued to grow week-to-week, with its pilot episode surpassing 20 million viewers worldwide. It also ranked among the five biggest series launches in HBO Max history. The penultimate episode was watched by 7 million viewers in the United States during its first three days, combining Nielsen's linear television measurements with HBO Max streams. The figure made it the series' most-watched episode in the U.S. at the time, while the series averaged more than 12 million U.S. viewers and nearly 19 million worldwide per episode. The season finale drew 10.5 million global viewers in its first three days, a 13% increase over the series premiere. The series averaged 19.3 million global viewers across the season, while its premiere was approaching over 30 million.

### Critical response

On review aggregator [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes"), 91% of 159 critics gave the season a positive review, with an average rating of 7.7/10. The website's critics' consensus reads, "*Lanterns* lights the way to a controlled and fantastical escape, in which Kyle Chandler and Aaron Pierre bring audiences into their world of wonder through fearless performances, grounded writing, and charismatic chemistry." [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), which uses a weighted average, assigned a score of 72 out of 100 based on 38 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.

Daniel Fienberg of *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")* praised the series for its "prickly chemistry" between Pierre and Chandler and its strong supporting cast, while noting that its comic-book elements can feel underwhelming and that some episodes are duller and less thematically developed. Alison Herman of *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* praised the character study of Jordan and Stewart and the exploration of John's experiences as a Black superhero, while criticizing the series for relying heavily on influences from other prestige dramas and for struggling to reconcile its grounded approach with its superhero elements. William Bibbiani of *[TheWrap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TheWrap "TheWrap")* highlighted the series' increasingly complex characters, relationships, and comic-book references, particularly Stewart's backstory and the "prickliest of interactions" between him and Jordan, as well as the action sequences, but criticized the series for featuring "too much talking, scene repetition and narrative padding".

Jesen Schedeen of *[IGN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IGN "IGN")* described the first episode as a successful blend of superhero drama and grounded murder mystery, highlighting the chemistry between Chandler and Pierre, but noted its limited focus on the cosmic side of the Green Lantern mythos. Ben Travers of *[IndieWire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndieWire "IndieWire")* gave the series an A-, praising its blend of superhero science fiction and police mystery, its exploration of racial discrimination, and the performances of the two leads. Amon Warmann of *[Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_(magazine) "Empire (magazine)")* gave the series 4 out of 5 stars, praising its grounded mystery, the strong performances, and the "entertainingly easy friend-foe chemistry" between Chandler and Pierre, as well as its exploration of racial themes, while noting its slow pacing and limited traditional superhero elements.
