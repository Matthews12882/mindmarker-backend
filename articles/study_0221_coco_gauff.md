# Coco Gauff

**Cori Dionne** "**Coco**" **Gauff** ([/ˈɡɔːf/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*GAWF*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"); born March 13, 2004) is an American professional [tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis "Tennis") player. She has a career-high ranking of world No. 2 in singles and of [world No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_WTA_number_1_ranked_doubles_tennis_players "List of WTA number 1 ranked doubles tennis players") in doubles by the [WTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_Tennis_Association "Women's Tennis Association"). Gauff has won twelve career singles titles, including two [majors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis)#Tournaments "Grand Slam (tennis)") at the [2023 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 US Open – Women's singles") and [2025 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2025 French Open – Women's singles"), as well as the [2024 WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2024 WTA Finals – Singles") and four [WTA 1000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_1000 "WTA 1000") events. She has also won ten doubles titles, including the [2024 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2024 French Open – Women's doubles"), partnering with [Kateřina Siniaková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate%C5%99ina_Siniakov%C3%A1 "Kateřina Siniaková").

A [teen sensation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_prodigy "Child prodigy"), Gauff made her [WTA Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_Tour "WTA Tour") debut in 2019 at age 15. At [that year's Wimbledon Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2019 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles"), she became the youngest player in the tournament's history to qualify for the main draw, where she defeated [Venus Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus_Williams "Venus Williams") and reached the fourth round. Gauff won her first WTA Tour singles title at the [2019 Linz Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Upper_Austria_Ladies_Linz_%E2%80%93_Singles "2019 Upper Austria Ladies Linz – Singles"). She reached her first major singles final at the [2022 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 French Open – Women's singles"). In 2023, Gauff won her first major singles title at the US Open, followed by the WTA Finals title the following year. In 2025, she won a second major singles title at the French Open.

In doubles, Gauff has reached three major finals, finishing runner-up with [Caty McNally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caty_McNally "Caty McNally") at the [2021 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2021 US Open – Women's doubles") and [Jessica Pegula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pegula "Jessica Pegula") at the [2022 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2022 French Open – Women's doubles") and prevailing with Siniaková at the 2024 French Open. She reached the No. 1 ranking in 2022 in partnership with Pegula.

## Early life

Gauff was born at West Boca Medical Center in [Boca Raton, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boca_Raton,_Florida "Boca Raton, Florida"), on March 13, 2004, to Candi ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") Odom) and Corey Gauff, both from [Delray Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delray_Beach,_Florida "Delray Beach, Florida"). She has two younger brothers. Her father played college basketball at [Georgia State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_State_University "Georgia State University") and later worked as a health care executive. Her mother was a track-and-field athlete at [Florida State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_University "Florida State University") and worked as an educator. Gauff lived her early years in Atlanta. She began playing tennis at age six. When she was seven, her family moved back to Delray Beach to have better training opportunities. She worked with Gerard Loglo at the New Generation Tennis Academy starting from the age of eight.

Gauff recalled, "I wasn't much of a team person. I loved tennis. I was so-so about it in the beginning because when I was younger I didn't want to practice at all. I just wanted to play with my friends. When I turned eight, that was when I played '[Little Mo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maureen_Connolly "Maureen Connolly")' [tournament] and after that I decided to do that for the rest of my life."

Gauff's parents gave up their careers to focus on training their daughter. Her father later became her primary coach, while her mother oversaw her [homeschooling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeschooling "Homeschooling"). Her father had limited experience playing tennis growing up. At the age of 10, Gauff began to train at the [Mouratoglou Tennis Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mouratoglou_Tennis_Academy "Mouratoglou Tennis Academy") in France run by [Patrick Mouratoglou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Mouratoglou "Patrick Mouratoglou"), longtime coach of [Serena Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serena_Williams "Serena Williams"). Mouratoglou commented, "I'll always remember the first time I saw Coco. She came over to the Mouratoglou Academy in 2014 to try out and she impressed me with her determination, athleticism and fighting spirit.... When she looks at you and tells you she will be number one, you can only believe it." He helped sponsor Gauff through his Champ'Seed foundation, which he created to provide funding for talented juniors who did not have the financial resources to afford high-level training.

Gauff won the USTA Clay Court National 12-and-under title at the age of 10 years and three months to become the youngest champion in the tournament's history.

## Juniors

### French Open singles and US Open doubles champion

Gauff is a former world No. 1 junior. She entered the prestigious [Les Petits As](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Petits_As "Les Petits As") 14-and-under tournament in 2016 at age 12 and made it to the semifinals. Gauff began playing on the [ITF Junior Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITF_Junior_Circuit "ITF Junior Circuit") at the age of 13, skipping directly to the highest-level Grade A and Grade 1 tournaments. She finished runner-up to [Jaimee Fourlis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaimee_Fourlis "Jaimee Fourlis") in her third career event, the Grade 1 Prince George's County Junior Tennis Championships in Maryland. At her next event, Gauff made her junior [Grand Slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis) "Grand Slam (tennis)") tournament debut at the [2017 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_US_Open_(tennis) "2017 US Open (tennis)") and finished runner-up to [Amanda Anisimova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Anisimova "Amanda Anisimova"). Gauff did not drop a set before the final in either tournament. She became the youngest girls' singles finalist in US Open history at 13 years, 181 days old.

After beginning 2018 with a semifinal at the Grade 1 Traralgon Junior International in Australia, Gauff lost her opening round match at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Australian_Open "2018 Australian Open"). She did not enter another tournament in singles until the French, where she won her first career junior Grand Slam tournament title at the [2018 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Girls%27_singles "2018 French Open – Girls' singles"). She did not drop a set until the final, where she came from behind to defeat [Caty McNally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caty_McNally "Caty McNally") in three sets. With the title, Gauff became the fifth youngest girls' singles champion in French Open history at 14 years, 89 days old. A month later, following another final win against McNally at the Grade 1 Junior International Roehampton, she became the No. 1 junior in the world.

Gauff reached the quarterfinals in singles at the final two majors of the year. She fared better in doubles at both tournaments, reaching the semifinals at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Wimbledon "2018 Wimbledon") with partner [María Lourdes Carlé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mar%C3%ADa_Lourdes_Carl%C3%A9 "María Lourdes Carlé") and winning her first junior Grand Slam tournament doubles title at the [2018 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Girls%27_doubles "2018 US Open – Girls' doubles") with McNally. Gauff and McNally defeated compatriots [Hailey Baptiste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hailey_Baptiste "Hailey Baptiste") and [Dalayna Hewitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalayna_Hewitt "Dalayna Hewitt") in the final, in straight sets. In September 2018, Gauff represented the United States at the [Junior Fed Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_Davis_Cup_and_Junior_Fed_Cup "Junior Davis Cup and Junior Fed Cup") with [Alexa Noel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexa_Noel "Alexa Noel") and Connie Ma. The team reached the final against Ukraine. After Gauff won her singles rubber and Noel lost hers, Gauff and Noel won the Junior Fed Cup by defeating Lyubov Kostenko and Dasha Lopatetskaya 11–9 in a match tiebreak. Gauff finished the year with another Grade A title in singles at the [Orange Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Bowl_(tennis) "Orange Bowl (tennis)"). She ended the season ranked world No. 2 behind [Clara Burel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clara_Burel "Clara Burel").

## Professional

### 2018–19: Wimbledon fourth round, WTA 250 title

In May 2018, at the age of 14, Gauff made her debut on the [ITF Women's Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITF_Women%27s_Circuit "ITF Women's Circuit") in the 25k event at [Osprey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osprey,_Florida "Osprey, Florida") as a qualifier. She won her first professional match. Gauff received a wildcard into qualifying at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles_qualifying "2018 US Open – Women's singles qualifying"), but lost her opening match.

In her first 2019 tournament, Gauff finished runner-up in doubles at the 100k [Midland Tennis Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Dow_Tennis_Classic_%E2%80%93_Doubles "2019 Dow Tennis Classic – Doubles") alongside [Ann Li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Li_(tennis) "Ann Li (tennis)"). Two weeks later, Gauff played her next event at the $25k level in [Surprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surprise,_Arizona "Surprise, Arizona"). She finished runner-up in singles and won her first WTA Tour title in doubles alongside [Paige Hourigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paige_Hourigan "Paige Hourigan"). Gauff received a wildcard into the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2019 Miami Open – Women's singles"). In her [WTA Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_Tour "WTA Tour") main draw debut, she came from a set down to defeat wildcard entrant [Caty McNally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caty_McNally "Caty McNally"). She lost in the second round to No. 22 [Daria Kasatkina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daria_Kasatkina "Daria Kasatkina"). Gauff received a wildcard into qualifying at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles_qualifying "2019 French Open – Women's singles qualifying"), but lost in the second round.

Ranked No. 313, Gauff received a wildcard into qualifying at Wimbledon. She won all three qualifying matches. At the age of 15 years and three months, she became the youngest player to reach the main draw at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2019 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles") by qualifying in the Open Era. In her Grand Slam main-draw debut, Gauff upset five-time Wimbledon champion [Venus Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus_Williams "Venus Williams") in straight sets. She became the youngest player to win a match at Wimbledon since [Jennifer Capriati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Capriati "Jennifer Capriati") in 1991. In the second round, she defeated [Magdaléna Rybáriková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdal%C3%A9na_Ryb%C3%A1rikov%C3%A1 "Magdaléna Rybáriková"). In the third round, she defeated [Polona Hercog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polona_Hercog "Polona Hercog"), saving two match points in the second set. The hype surrounding her first-round match win led to her third-rounder being moved to [Centre Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_Court "Centre Court"). In the fourth round, she was eliminated by No. 7 and eventual champion [Simona Halep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simona_Halep "Simona Halep"). All four of Gauff's matches were most-watched matches on [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN "ESPN") on their respective days during the first week of coverage. Her ranking rose to No. 141.

Entering the [Washington Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Citi_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2019 Citi Open – Women's singles") after qualifying, Gauff lost in the first round to [Zarina Diyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zarina_Diyas "Zarina Diyas"). In [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Citi_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2019 Citi Open – Women's doubles"), however, she and Caty McNally won their first career WTA doubles title, defeating [Maria Sanchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Sanchez_(tennis) "Maria Sanchez (tennis)") and [Fanny Stollár](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanny_Stoll%C3%A1r "Fanny Stollár") in the final. Gauff received wildcards into singles and doubles main draw at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2019 US Open – Women's singles"). She defeated qualifier [Anastasia Potapova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasia_Potapova "Anastasia Potapova") and [Tímea Babos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%ADmea_Babos "Tímea Babos"), both in three sets on [Louis Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Armstrong_Stadium "Louis Armstrong Stadium"), but lost in the third round to world No. 1 and defending champion [Naomi Osaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Osaka "Naomi Osaka"). Osaka graciously allowed Gauff an on-court interview after the match, usually only afforded to the winner. In [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2019 US Open – Women's doubles"), Gauff and McNally won two matches, including an upset over ninth seeds [Nicole Melichar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Melichar "Nicole Melichar") and [Květa Peschke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kv%C4%9Bta_Peschke "Květa Peschke"). They lost in the third round to eventual runners-up [Victoria Azarenka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Azarenka "Victoria Azarenka") and [Ashleigh Barty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashleigh_Barty "Ashleigh Barty").

Although Gauff lost in qualifying at the [Linz Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Upper_Austria_Ladies_Linz_%E2%80%93_Singles "2019 Upper Austria Ladies Linz – Singles"), she entered the main draw as a [lucky loser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucky_loser "Lucky loser"). She upset No. 8 [Kiki Bertens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiki_Bertens "Kiki Bertens") in the quarterfinals in straight sets for her first top-10 victory. She defeated [Jeļena Ostapenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Je%C4%BCena_Ostapenko "Jeļena Ostapenko") in the final in three sets. At 15 years and seven months old, Gauff became the youngest WTA player to win a singles title since [Nicole Vaidišová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Vaidi%C5%A1ov%C3%A1 "Nicole Vaidišová") in 2004. With this title, she made her top-75 debut in WTA singles rankings. With a semifinal appearance in [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Upper_Austria_Ladies_Linz_%E2%80%93_Doubles "2019 Upper Austria Ladies Linz – Doubles") with McNally, she also made her top-100 debut in WTA doubles rankings. Gauff and McNally ended their year with a second WTA doubles title at the [Luxembourg Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_BGL_Luxembourg_Open_%E2%80%93_Doubles "2019 BGL Luxembourg Open – Doubles") over [Kaitlyn Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaitlyn_Christian "Kaitlyn Christian") and [Alexa Guarachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexa_Guarachi "Alexa Guarachi").

### 2020: Australian Open fourth round, top 50

Gauff started 2020 in [Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_ASB_Classic_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2020 ASB Classic – Women's singles"). She defeated [Viktória Kužmová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikt%C3%B3ria_Ku%C5%BEmov%C3%A1 "Viktória Kužmová") before losing to [Laura Siegemund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Siegemund "Laura Siegemund"). At the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2020 Australian Open – Women's singles"), Gauff beat Venus Williams again in the first round in straight sets. In the second round, she defeated [Sorana Cîrstea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorana_C%C3%AErstea "Sorana Cîrstea"). In the third round, she upset No. 4 and defending champion Naomi Osaka in straight sets. She became the youngest player to reach the last 16 of two major tournaments since [Martina Hingis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martina_Hingis "Martina Hingis") in 1996. She lost in the last 16 to No. 15 and eventual champion [Sofia Kenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia_Kenin "Sofia Kenin") in three sets. Following the tournament, Gauff broke into top 50, becoming the youngest woman to be ranked in the top 50 since [Sesil Karatancheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesil_Karatantcheva "Sesil Karatantcheva") in 2005. In [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2020 Australian Open – Women's doubles"), Gauff and McNally recorded their best result in a major championship to date, reaching the quarterfinals before falling to second seeds and eventual champions, Tímea Babos and [Kristina Mladenovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristina_Mladenovic "Kristina Mladenovic").

After the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic") shut down the WTA Tour, Gauff entered the [Top Seed Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Top_Seed_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2020 Top Seed Open – Singles") in August. She upset No. 11 [Aryna Sabalenka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryna_Sabalenka "Aryna Sabalenka") in the second round in three sets. She lost in the semifinals to eventual champion [Jennifer Brady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Brady "Jennifer Brady") in straight sets. At the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Western_%26_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2020 Western & Southern Open – Women's singles"), she lost in the first round to No. 21 [Maria Sakkari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Sakkari "Maria Sakkari"). At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2020 US Open – Women's singles"), she was defeated in the first round by [Anastasija Sevastova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasija_Sevastova "Anastasija Sevastova").

At the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2020 Italian Open – Women's singles"), Gauff beat [Ons Jabeur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ons_Jabeur "Ons Jabeur") before losing to No. 17 [Garbiñe Muguruza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garbi%C3%B1e_Muguruza "Garbiñe Muguruza"). At the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2020 French Open – Women's singles"), she defeated No. 13 [Johanna Konta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johanna_Konta "Johanna Konta") before losing to qualifier [Martina Trevisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martina_Trevisan "Martina Trevisan").

Gauff entered the [Ostrava Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_J%26T_Banka_Ostrava_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2020 J&T Banka Ostrava Open – Singles") after qualifying. She defeated wildcard entrant [Kateřina Siniaková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate%C5%99ina_Siniakov%C3%A1 "Kateřina Siniaková") before losing to No. 12 Aryna Sabalenka in three sets.

### 2021: French Open quarterfinal, top 20

Gauff started the year at the [Abu Dhabi Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Abu_Dhabi_Women%27s_Tennis_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 Abu Dhabi Women's Tennis Open – Singles"). She beat [Ulrikke Eikeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulrikke_Eikeri "Ulrikke Eikeri") before falling to No. 22 Maria Sakkari. At the [Gippsland Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Gippsland_Trophy_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 Gippsland Trophy – Singles"), she beat [Jil Teichmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jil_Teichmann "Jil Teichmann") before losing to [Katie Boulter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Boulter "Katie Boulter"). At the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2021 Australian Open – Women's singles"), she beat Jil Teichmann again before falling to No. 5 [Elina Svitolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elina_Svitolina "Elina Svitolina"). In [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2021 Australian Open – Women's doubles"), she and McNally reached the quarterfinals, where they fell to [Nicole Melichar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Melichar-Martinez "Nicole Melichar-Martinez") and [Demi Schuurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demi_Schuurs "Demi Schuurs"). Entering the [Adelaide International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Adelaide_International_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 Adelaide International – Singles") after qualifying, Gauff reached the semifinals following three-set wins over qualifier [Jasmine Paolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasmine_Paolini "Jasmine Paolini"), No. 21 [Petra Martić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petra_Marti%C4%87 "Petra Martić"), and [Shelby Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shelby_Rogers "Shelby Rogers"). She became the youngest player to reach the semifinals of a WTA 500 level tournament or higher since Nicole Vaidišová in 2006. She lost in the semifinals to No. 12 [Belinda Bencic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belinda_Bencic "Belinda Bencic") in three sets. As a result, Gauff moved to a career-high ranking of No. 38.

At the [Dubai Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Dubai_Tennis_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2021 Dubai Tennis Championships – Women's singles"), Gauff defeated [Ekaterina Alexandrova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekaterina_Alexandrova "Ekaterina Alexandrova"), No. 20 [Markéta Vondroušová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark%C3%A9ta_Vondrou%C5%A1ov%C3%A1 "Markéta Vondroušová"), and qualifier [Tereza Martincova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tereza_Martincov%C3%A1 "Tereza Martincová") to reach her first WTA 1000 quarterfinal. She lost in the quarterfinals to Jil Teichmann in straight sets.

At the [Charleston Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Volvo_Car_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 Volvo Car Open – Singles"), Gauff defeated [Tsvetana Pironkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsvetana_Pironkova "Tsvetana Pironkova"), [Liudmila Samsonova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liudmila_Samsonova "Liudmila Samsonova"), and [Lauren Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauren_Davis "Lauren Davis") to reach her first quarterfinal on clay. She lost in the quarterfinals to No. 12 Ons Jabeur in straight sets.

At the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2021 Italian Open – Women's singles"), Gauff defeated [Yulia Putintseva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yulia_Putintseva "Yulia Putintseva"), No. 19 Maria Sakkari, and No. 4 Aryna Sabalenka to reach the quarterfinals. She reached her first career WTA 1000 semifinal after world No.1 [Ashleigh Barty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashleigh_Barty "Ashleigh Barty") retired. She lost in the semifinals to No. 15 and eventual champion [Iga Świątek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iga_%C5%9Awi%C4%85tek "Iga Świątek") in straight sets. As a result, Gauff rose to top 30 for the first time.

At the [Emilia-Romagna Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Emilia-Romagna_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2021 Emilia-Romagna Open – Women's singles") in Parma, Gauff defeated [Kaia Kanepi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaia_Kanepi "Kaia Kanepi"), [Camila Giorgi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camila_Giorgi "Camila Giorgi"), [Amanda Anisimova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Anisimova "Amanda Anisimova") and Kateřina Siniaková to reach her second career final and first on clay. She defeated [Wang Qiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Qiang_(tennis) "Wang Qiang (tennis)") in straight sets to win her second singles title. Partnering with McNally, Gauff also won the [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Emilia-Romagna_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2021 Emilia-Romagna Open – Women's singles") title, defeating [Darija Jurak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darija_Jurak "Darija Jurak") and [Andreja Klepac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreja_Klepac "Andreja Klepac") in the final. She became the youngest player to win both the singles and doubles titles at an event since [Maria Sharapova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Sharapova "Maria Sharapova") in 2004.

At the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2021 French Open – Women's singles"), Gauff beat qualifier [Aleksandra Krunić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandra_Kruni%C4%87 "Aleksandra Krunić") and Wang Qiang to reach the third round. She reached the fourth round after No. 14 Jennifer Brady retired while trailing by a set. She beat No. 25 Ons Jabeur in straight sets to reach her first major quarterfinal, becoming the youngest player to reach a major quarterfinal since Nicole Vaidišová in 2006. She lost in the quarterfinals to eventual champion [Barbora Krejčíková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbora_Krej%C4%8D%C3%ADkov%C3%A1 "Barbora Krejčíková") in straight sets.

At the [Wimbledon Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2021 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles"), Gauff defeated wildcard entrant [Francesca Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesca_Jones_(tennis) "Francesca Jones (tennis)"), [Elena Vesnina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elena_Vesnina "Elena Vesnina"), and [Kaja Juvan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaja_Juvan "Kaja Juvan") to reach the fourth round, where she lost to No. 28 [Angelique Kerber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelique_Kerber "Angelique Kerber") in straight sets.

Gauff qualified for [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics "United States at the 2020 Summer Olympics") tennis team for the [2020 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics "2020 Summer Olympics"), but was forced to withdraw after testing positive for COVID-19.

At the [Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2021 National Bank Open – Women's singles"), Gauff defeated Anastasija Sevastova in the first round. She reached the third round after qualifier Anastasia Potapova retired, and reached the quarterfinals after Johanna Konta withdrew. She lost in the quarterfinals to eventual champion Camila Giorgi in straight sets. At the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Western_%26_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2021 Western & Southern Open – Women's singles"), she defeated qualifier [Hsieh Su-wei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hsieh_Su-wei "Hsieh Su-wei") before losing to world No. 2 Naomi Osaka. At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2021 US Open – Women's singles"), she beat [Magda Linette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magda_Linette "Magda Linette") before falling to [Sloane Stephens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloane_Stephens "Sloane Stephens"). Despite the loss, Gauff broke into the top 20 after the US Open. In [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2021 US Open – Women's doubles"), Gauff and McNally reached their first major final, losing to [Sam Stosur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samantha_Stosur "Samantha Stosur") and [Zhang Shuai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Shuai "Zhang Shuai").

### 2022: French Open final, singles top 5, doubles No. 1

Gauff started the season at the [Adelaide International 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Adelaide_International_1_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 Adelaide International 1 – Women's singles"). She defeated qualifier Ulrikke Eikeri before losing to world No. 1 and eventual champion Ashleigh Barty. At the [Adelaide International 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Adelaide_International_2_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 Adelaide International 2 – Women's singles"), Gauff defeated Kateřina Siniaková, [Marta Kostyuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marta_Kostyuk "Marta Kostyuk"), and [Ana Konjuh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ana_Konjuh "Ana Konjuh") to reach the semifinals, where she lost to eventual champion [Madison Keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Keys "Madison Keys") in three sets. At the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 Australian Open – Women's singles"), she lost in the first round against Wang Qiang.

At the [Qatar Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Qatar_Total_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 Qatar Total Open – Singles"), Gauff defeated Shelby Rogers, wildcard entrant [Caroline Garcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Garcia "Caroline Garcia") and No. 4 [Paula Badosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paula_Badosa "Paula Badosa") to reach the quarterfinals. She lost in the quarterfinals to No. 6 Maria Sakkari in straight sets. In [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Qatar_Total_Open_%E2%80%93_Doubles "2022 Qatar Total Open – Doubles"), Gauff paired with [Jessica Pegula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pegula "Jessica Pegula") to win her first WTA 1000 doubles title, beating third-seeded pair of [Veronika Kudermetova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veronika_Kudermetova "Veronika Kudermetova") and [Elise Mertens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elise_Mertens "Elise Mertens") in the final. With the win, Gauff climbed to a career-high No. 10 in the doubles rankings.

At the [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 BNP Paribas Open – Women's singles"), Gauff defeated wildcard entrant [Claire Liu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claire_Liu "Claire Liu") before losing to No. 26 Simona Halep. In [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 Miami Open – Women's singles"), she defeated qualifier Wang Qiang and Zhang Shuai before losing to world No. 2 and eventual champion Iga Świątek in straight sets.

At the [Stuttgart Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 Porsche Tennis Grand Prix – Singles"), Gauff was knocked out in the first round by Daria Kasatkina. In [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix_%E2%80%93_Doubles "2022 Porsche Tennis Grand Prix – Doubles"), however, Gauff reached the final with Zhang Shuai, where they lost to [Desirae Krawczyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desirae_Krawczyk "Desirae Krawczyk") and Demi Schuurs.

At the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 French Open – Women's singles"), Gauff defeated qualifier [Rebecca Marino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_Marino "Rebecca Marino"), [Alison Van Uytvanck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alison_Van_Uytvanck "Alison Van Uytvanck"), Kaia Kanepi, and Elise Mertens to reach the French Open quarterfinals for a second consecutive year, without dropping a set. She defeated Sloane Stephens in straight sets to reach her first major semifinal. She defeated Martina Trevisan in straight sets to reach her first major final, becoming the youngest player to reach the French Open final since [Kim Clijsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Clijsters "Kim Clijsters") in 2001. In the final, she lost again to world No. 1 Iga Świątek in straight sets. Partnering with Pegula, Gauff also reached the final in [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2022 French Open – Women's doubles"), where they were defeated by Caroline Garcia and Kristina Mladenovic. As a result, Gauff reached a new career-high doubles ranking of top 5.

At the [Berlin Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_WTA_German_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 WTA German Open – Singles"), Gauff defeated [Ann Li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Li_(tennis) "Ann Li (tennis)"), qualifier [Wang Xinyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Xinyu "Wang Xinyu"), and No. 7 [Karolína Plíšková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol%C3%ADna_Pl%C3%AD%C5%A1kov%C3%A1 "Karolína Plíšková") to reach the semifinals, without dropping a set. She lost in the semifinals to No. 4 and eventual champion Ons Jabeur in straight sets. At [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles"), she defeated [Elena-Gabriela Ruse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elena-Gabriela_Ruse "Elena-Gabriela Ruse") and [Mihaela Buzărnescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihaela_Buz%C4%83rnescu "Mihaela Buzărnescu") before losing to No. 25 Amanda Anisimova in three sets.

At the [Silicon Valley Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Silicon_Valley_Classic_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 Silicon Valley Classic – Singles"), Gauff defeated [Anhelina Kalinina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anhelina_Kalinina "Anhelina Kalinina") and Naomi Osaka to reach the quarterfinals, where she lost to No. 4 Paula Badosa in straight sets.

At the [Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 National Bank Open – Women's singles"), Gauff defeated qualifier [Madison Brengle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Brengle "Madison Brengle") in the first round. She then beat [Elena Rybakina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elena_Rybakina "Elena Rybakina") and No. 6 Aryna Sabalenka, winning both matches in a third-set tiebreak. In the quarterfinals, she lost to No. 7 and eventual champion Simona Halep in straight sets. In [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2022 National Bank Open – Women's doubles"), Gauff won her second WTA 1000 doubles title together with Pegula, defeating Nicole Melichar and [Ellen Perez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellen_Perez "Ellen Perez") in the final. As a result, Gauff became the [No. 1 doubles player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_WTA_number_1_ranked_doubles_tennis_players "List of WTA number 1 ranked doubles tennis players") in the world. She was the second-youngest player in history to earn the doubles No.1 ranking after Martina Hingis in 1998.

At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 US Open – Women's singles"), Gauff defeated qualifier [Leolia Jeanjean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9olia_Jeanjean "Léolia Jeanjean"), Elena-Gabriela Ruse, No. 20 Madison Keys and Zhang Shuai, without dropping a set, to advance to her first US Open quarterfinal. She became the youngest American woman to reach the US Open quarterfinals since [Melanie Oudin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanie_Oudin "Melanie Oudin") in 2009. She lost in the quarterfinals to No. 17 Caroline Garcia in straight sets. As a result, Gauff made her top 10 debut in the singles rankings, at world No. 8.

At the [San Diego Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_San_Diego_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 San Diego Open – Women's singles"), Gauff defeated qualifier [Robin Montgomery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Montgomery "Robin Montgomery") and [Bianca Andreescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bianca_Andreescu "Bianca Andreescu") to reach the quarterfinals. In the quarterfinals, she lost again to world No. 1 and eventual champion Iga Świątek in straight sets. She won the [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_San_Diego_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2022 San Diego Open – Women's doubles") event with Pegula, defeating [Gabriela Dabrowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriela_Dabrowski "Gabriela Dabrowski") and [Giuliana Olmos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuliana_Olmos "Giuliana Olmos") in the final. At the [Guadalajara Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Guadalajara_Open_Akron_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 Guadalajara Open Akron – Singles"), Gauff defeated qualifier [Elisabetta Cocciaretto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisabetta_Cocciaretto "Elisabetta Cocciaretto") and No. 28 Martina Trevisan to reach the quarterfinals, where she lost to [Victoria Azarenka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Azarenka "Victoria Azarenka") in three sets. Nevertheless, her ranking climbed to No. 4.

Gauff qualified for the [WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 WTA Finals – Singles") for the first time, becoming the youngest player to compete at the WTA Finals since Maria Sharapova in 2005. She and Pegula also qualified in [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Doubles "2022 WTA Finals – Doubles"), becoming the first Americans to compete at the WTA Finals in singles and doubles since [Serena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serena_Williams "Serena Williams") and Venus Williams in 2009. Gauff lost all her three matches against Caroline Garcia, Daria Kasatkina, and Iga Świątek in the round-robin stage, in straight sets.

### 2023: US Open champion, No. 3

Gauff started her 2023 season in [Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_ASB_Classic_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 ASB Classic – Women's singles"). She defeated [Tatjana Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatjana_Maria "Tatjana Maria"), wildcard entrant [Sofia Kenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia_Kenin "Sofia Kenin"), [Zhu Lin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhu_Lin_(tennis) "Zhu Lin (tennis)"), [Danka Kovinić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danka_Kovini%C4%87 "Danka Kovinić"), and qualifier [Rebeka Masarova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebeka_Masarova "Rebeka Masarova") to win third WTA Tour title, without dropping a set en route. At the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 Australian Open – Women's singles"), she defeated Kateřina Siniaková, [Emma Raducanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Raducanu "Emma Raducanu"), and [Bernarda Pera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernarda_Pera "Bernarda Pera") to advance to the fourth round, where she lost to No. 17 [Jeļena Ostapenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Je%C4%BCena_Ostapenko "Jeļena Ostapenko") in straight sets.

At the [Qatar Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Qatar_Total_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 Qatar Total Open – Singles"), Gauff defeated No. 13 Petra Kvitová to reach the quarterfinals, where she lost to No. 11 Veronika Kudermetova in three sets. At the same tournament in [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Qatar_Total_Open_%E2%80%93_Doubles "2023 Qatar Total Open – Doubles"), Gauff and Pegula defended their title, defeating [Lyudmyla Kichenok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyudmyla_Kichenok "Lyudmyla Kichenok") and Jeļena Ostapenko in the final. At the [Dubai Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Dubai_Tennis_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 Dubai Tennis Championships – Women's singles"), Gauff defeated [Aliaksandra Sasnovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliaksandra_Sasnovich "Aliaksandra Sasnovich") to reach the third round. She reached the quarterfinals when No. 10 Elena Rybakina withdrew. She defeated Madison Keys to reach the semifinals, where she lost to world No. 1 Iga Świątek in straight sets.

At the [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 BNP Paribas Open – Women's singles"), Gauff defeated qualifier [Cristina Bucșa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristina_Buc%C8%99a "Cristina Bucșa"), [Linda Nosková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Noskov%C3%A1 "Linda Nosková"), and qualifier [Rebecca Peterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_Peterson "Rebecca Peterson") to reach the quarterfinals, where she lost to world No. 2 Aryna Sabalenka in straight sets. In [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 Miami Open – Women's singles"), she defeated Rebecca Marino before losing to No. 26 Anastasia Potapova in three sets. However, she won her third WTA 1000 [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2023 Miami Open – Women's doubles") title with Pegula, defeating Leylah Fernandez and [Taylor Townsend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Townsend "Taylor Townsend") in the final.

In [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 Mutua Madrid Open – Women's singles"), Gauff defeated qualifier [Irene Burillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irene_Burillo "Irene Burillo") before losing to Paula Badosa in straight sets. At the same tournament in [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2023 Mutua Madrid Open – Women's doubles"), she and Pegula reached the final, losing to Victoria Azarenka and [Beatriz Haddad Maia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatriz_Haddad_Maia "Beatriz Haddad Maia"). In [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 Italian Open – Women's singles"), Gauff defeated Yulia Putintseva before losing to [Marie Bouzková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Bouzkov%C3%A1 "Marie Bouzková") in three sets. At the same tournament in [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2023 Italian Open – Women's doubles"), she and Pegula again reached the final, losing to [Storm Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storm_Hunter "Storm Hunter") and Elise Mertens.

At the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_French_Open_-_Women%27s_singles "2023 French Open - Women's singles"), Gauff defeated Rebeka Masarova, [Julia Grabher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Grabher "Julia Grabher"), qualifier [Mirra Andreeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirra_Andreeva "Mirra Andreeva"), and [Anna Karolina Schmiedlova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Karolina_Schmiedlova "Anna Karolina Schmiedlova") to reach her third straight French Open quarterfinal. In the quarterfinals, she again lost to world No. 1 Iga Świątek in straight sets.

At the [Eastbourne International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Eastbourne_International_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 Eastbourne International – Women's singles"), Gauff defeated Bernarda Pera, lucky loser [Jodie Burrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jodie_Burrage "Jodie Burrage"), and No. 3 Jessica Pegula, without dropping a set, to reach the semifinals but lost to eventual champion Madison Keys in straight sets. At [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles"), she lost in the first round to qualifier Sofia Kenin.

At the [Washington Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Mubadala_Citi_DC_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 Mubadala Citi DC Open – Women's singles"), Gauff defeated qualifier [Hailey Baptiste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hailey_Baptiste "Hailey Baptiste"), No. 15 Belinda Bencic, No. 18 Liudmila Samsonova, and No. 9 Maria Sakkari to win her first WTA 500 title, without dropping a set en route. She became the youngest female champion of the tournament.

At the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Western_%26_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 Western & Southern Open – Women's singles"), Gauff defeated [Mayar Sherif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayar_Sherif "Mayar Sherif") and qualifiers Linda Nosková and Jasmine Paolini, without dropping a set, to reach the semifinals. She stunned world No. 1 Iga Świątek for the first time after losing their previous seven meetings to reach her first WTA 1000 final. She defeated [Karolína Muchová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol%C3%ADna_Muchov%C3%A1 "Karolína Muchová") in straight sets to win her first WTA 1000 title.

At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 US Open – Women's singles"), Gauff came from a set down to defeat qualifier Laura Siegemund in the first round. In the second round, she defeated Mirra Andreeva. In the third round, she came from a set down to defeat Elise Mertens. She defeated wildcard entrant [Caroline Wozniacki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Wozniacki "Caroline Wozniacki") in three sets to reach her second straight US Open quarterfinal. She defeated No. 21 Jeļena Ostapenko in straight sets to reach her first US Open semifinal, and defeated No. 10 Karolína Muchová in straight sets to reach her first US Open final. She came from a set down to defeat world No. 2 Aryna Sabalenka and win her first major title. She became the youngest American player to reach and win the US Open final since Serena Williams in 1999. As a result, she reached a new career-high ranking of No. 3. Following Barbora Krejčíková and Kateřina Siniaková's second-round exit, Gauff and Pegula also jointly rose to No.1 in the [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2023 US Open – Women's doubles") rankings.

At the [China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_China_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 China Open – Women's singles"), Gauff defeated Ekaterina Alexandrova, Petra Martić, No. 16 Veronika Kudermetova, and No. 6 Maria Sakkari to reach the semifinals, where she lost to world No. 2 and eventual champion Iga Świątek in straight sets.

Gauff qualified for the [WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 WTA Finals – Singles") for the second year in a row. She and Pegula also qualified in [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Doubles "2023 WTA Finals – Doubles") for the second year in a row. Gauff defeated Ons Jabeur and Markéta Vondroušová to advance to the semifinals. She lost her group match against Iga Świątek and lost in the semifinals to Jessica Pegula, in straight sets.

### 2024: WTA Finals champion, No. 2

Gauff started her clay season at the [Stuttgart Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix_%E2%80%93_Singles "2024 Porsche Tennis Grand Prix – Singles"). She defeated qualifier [Sachia Vickery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sachia_Vickery "Sachia Vickery") to reach the quarterfinals, where she lost to Marta Kostyuk in three sets.

At the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 Italian Open – Women's singles"), Gauff defeated [Magdalena Fręch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalena_Fr%C4%99ch "Magdalena Fręch"), lucky loser [Jaqueline Cristian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaqueline_Cristian "Jaqueline Cristian"), and Paula Badosa to reach the quarterfinals. She defeated No. 7 [Zheng Qinwen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zheng_Qinwen "Zheng Qinwen") in straight sets to reach the semifinals. In the semifinals, she lost to world No. 1 and eventual champion Iga Świątek in straight sets. At the same tournament in [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2024 Italian Open – Women's doubles"), Gauff partnered [Erin Routliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erin_Routliffe "Erin Routliffe") to reach the final, losing to [Sara Errani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Errani "Sara Errani") and Jasmine Paolini.

At the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 French Open – Women's singles"), Gauff defeated qualifiers [Julia Avdeeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Avdeeva "Julia Avdeeva") and [Tamara Zidanšek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamara_Zidan%C5%A1ek "Tamara Zidanšek"), [Dayana Yastremska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayana_Yastremska "Dayana Yastremska"), and Elisabetta Cocciaretto to reach her fourth straight French Open quarterfinal, without dropping a set en route. She came from a set down to defeat No. 9 Ons Jabeur and reach her second French Open semifinal. In the semifinals, she lost again to world No. 1 Iga Świątek in straight sets. Despite the loss, Gauff reached a new career-high ranking of world No. 2. In [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "2024 French Open – Women's doubles"), Gauff partnered with Katerina Siniaková to win her first major doubles title, defeating Sara Errani and Jasmine Paolini in the final.

Gauff started her grass season at the [Berlin Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Berlin_Ladies_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2024 Berlin Ladies Open – Singles"). She defeated Ekaterina Alexandrova to reach the quarterfinals, and reached the semifinals as No. 10 Ons Jabeur retired after losing first set. In the semifinals, she lost to No. 5 and eventual champion Jessica Pegula in straight sets. At the [Wimbledon Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles"), she defeated [Caroline Dolehide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Dolehide "Caroline Dolehide") and qualifiers [Anca Todoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anca_Todoni "Anca Todoni") and [Sonay Kartal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonay_Kartal "Sonay Kartal") to reach the fourth round, where she lost to No. 17 [Emma Navarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Navarro "Emma Navarro") in straight sets.

Gauff was chosen by her Olympic teammates to be the female flag-bearer for the United States at the opening ceremony of the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics "2024 Summer Olympics") in Paris, alongside [LeBron James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeBron_James "LeBron James"). She became the youngest athlete to be so honored. In [singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "Tennis at the 2024 Summer Olympics – Women's singles"), she lost in the third round to No. 21 [Donna Vekić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donna_Veki%C4%87 "Donna Vekić"). In [women's doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles "Tennis at the 2024 Summer Olympics – Women's doubles"), she and Jessica Pegula lost in the second round to the Czech duo of Karolína Muchová and Linda Nosková.

In October, Gauff won the 2024 [China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Open_(tennis) "China Open (tennis)") title, beating Karolína Muchová in the final. It was Gauff's second WTA 1000 singles title. She became the first player to win each of her seven WTA hardcourt finals in the [Open era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_era "Open era") and the second American to win the championship, following [Serena Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serena_Williams "Serena Williams")' title runs in 2004 and 2013.

On November 9, Gauff became the [2024 WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2024 WTA Finals – Singles") singles champion, becoming the youngest player to win the year-end championships since Maria Sharapova in 2004 and the first American to win the finals since Serena Williams in 2014. She beat Iga Świątek, the No. 2 seed and defending champion of the WTA Finals in [Riyadh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riyadh "Riyadh"). She then defeated Sabalenka in the semifinals and Zheng Qinwen in the finals to capture her first year-end singles championship title.

## Rivalries

### Iga Świątek

Gauff and [Iga Świątek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iga_%C5%9Awi%C4%85tek "Iga Świątek") have met 16 times since 2021, with Świątek controlling the head-to-head 11–5. Gauff lost to Świątek in her first [major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis) "Grand Slam (tennis)") final at the [2022 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2022 French Open – Women's singles") championships. They met again in the [2023 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 French Open – Women's singles") quarterfinal, and the [2024 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 French Open – Women's singles") semifinal, with Świątek winning each time. Gauff has won their last four meetings, including the [2024 WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2024 WTA Finals – Singles") and most recently at the [2026 United Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_Cup "2026 United Cup").

### Aryna Sabalenka

Gauff and [Aryna Sabalenka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryna_Sabalenka "Aryna Sabalenka") have developed a noteworthy rivalry, competing in multiple major finals. Their contrasting demeanors and styles of play shine throughout in their matches. The two have met 13 times since 2020, with Sabalenka having the edge in their head-to-head at 7–6. Gauff won her first major title by defeating Sabalenka in the [2023 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2023 US Open – Women's singles") final. Sabalenka would then defeat Gauff on her way to defending her title in the [2024 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2024 Australian Open – Women's singles") semifinal. Gauff defeated Sabalenka in the [2024 WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2024 WTA Finals – Singles") semifinal on her way to winning the tournament. Gauff would then win her second [major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis) "Grand Slam (tennis)") title at the [2025 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2025 French Open – Women's singles") by defeating Sabalenka in three sets. After a close first set, Sabalenka avenged her French Open loss by knocking Gauff out of the [2025 WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 WTA Finals – Singles") where Gauff was the defending champion in straight sets. Gauff and Sabalenka would face off in the [2026 Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles "2026 Miami Open – Women's singles") final, where Sabalenka defeated Gauff in three sets.

## Playing style and coaching

While hard court is Gauff's favorite surface, she has yielded her best results on clay. Although many analysts would describe her as an "opportunistic counterpuncher",[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] she describes herself as having a highly aggressive playing style and a powerful serve, while on the defensive side she uses her athleticism and speed to remain in points.

From July 2023 to September 2024 her coach was [Brad Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Gilbert "Brad Gilbert"). Gilbert encouraged Gauff to put high, heavy topspin on her ball and to chase down balls in every corner of the court. In September 2024 Gauff hired coach Matt Daly, who has focused on improving her serve and forehand with a grip on her racket that is closer to the [eastern backhand style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grip_(tennis) "Grip (tennis)") rather than the more frequently used [continental style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grip_(tennis) "Grip (tennis)"). In August 2025, Gauff replaced Daly with Gavin MacMillan, a biomechanics specialist who helped rescue Aryna Sabalenka's serve.

## Personal life

Gauff is a [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity"). Since she was eight years old, she has prayed with her father before every match that she and her opponent would be safe. After winning the Cincinnati Open in August 2023, she said: "... I'd like to thank my Lord and Savior, Jesus Christ. I spent a lot of nights alone, crying trying to figure it out. I still have a lot to figure out, but I thank Him for covering me." After winning her first major title in September 2023, she said: "... I don't pray for results, I just ask that I get the strength to give it my all and whatever happens happens. I'm so blessed in this life."

Gauff's tennis idols are Serena and Venus Williams. "Serena Williams has always been my idol...and Venus," she has said. "They are the reason why I wanted to pick up a tennis racquet." Gauff first met Serena when she won the Little Mo' national tournament at the age of eight, and later met her again to film a commercial for [Delta Air Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Air_Lines "Delta Air Lines") and at the Mouratoglou Academy. After defeating Venus Williams at Wimbledon in 2019, Gauff expressed her respect when they shook hands at the net. "I was just telling her thank you for everything she's done for the sport," Gauff said. "She's been an inspiration for many people. I was just really telling her thank you."

Gauff stated in 2020 that she had experienced [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_(mood) "Depression (mood)") and stress related to her sporting career, though her parents clarified that she was not diagnosed with depression in the clinical sense, and had not sought medical attention relating to her psychological well-being.

Gauff is a fan of [anime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anime "Anime"), including *[My Hero Academia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Hero_Academia "My Hero Academia")*. She runs an "editing account" on TikTok, mainly of anime dramas.

Gauff is a fan of popular YouTubers and streamers [Ludwig Ahgren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Ahgren "Ludwig Ahgren"), [Valkyrae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valkyrae "Valkyrae"), and [Kai Cenat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Cenat "Kai Cenat").

In July 2023, Gauff and actress [Storm Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storm_Reid "Storm Reid") helped fund a new playground and purple-painted tennis courts in East Atlanta's [Brownwood Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownwood_Park "Brownwood Park").
