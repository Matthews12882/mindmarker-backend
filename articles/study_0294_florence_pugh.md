# Florence Pugh

**Florence Pugh** ([/pjuː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*PEW*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"); born 3 January 1996) is an English actress. [Her accolades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Florence_Pugh "List of awards and nominations received by Florence Pugh") include a [British Independent Film Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Independent_Film_Award "British Independent Film Award"), in addition to nominations for an [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award "Academy Award") and three [BAFTA Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Awards "BAFTA Awards").

After making her acting debut in the drama film *[The Falling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Falling_(2014_film) "The Falling (2014 film)")* (2014), Pugh gained praise for starring in the independent drama *[Lady Macbeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Macbeth_(film) "Lady Macbeth (film)")* (2016) and the miniseries *[The Little Drummer Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Little_Drummer_Girl_(TV_series) "The Little Drummer Girl (TV series)")* (2018). Her international breakthrough came in 2019 with her portrayals of professional wrestler [Paige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraya_Bevis "Saraya Bevis") in the sports film *[Fighting with My Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighting_with_My_Family "Fighting with My Family")*, Dani Ardor in the horror film *[Midsommar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midsommar "Midsommar")*, and [Amy March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_March "Amy March") in the period drama *[Little Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Women_(2019_film) "Little Women (2019 film)")*. For the last of these, she was nominated for the [Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress "Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress").

Pugh has played [Yelena Belova / Black Widow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yelena_Belova_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Yelena Belova (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") in the [Marvel Cinematic Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe "Marvel Cinematic Universe"), starring in the films *[Black Widow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Widow_(2021_film) "Black Widow (2021 film)")* (2021), *[Thunderbolts\*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunderbolts* "Thunderbolts*")* (2025), and *[Spider-Man: Brand New Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man:_Brand_New_Day "Spider-Man: Brand New Day")* (2026), as well as the [Disney+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney%2B "Disney+") miniseries [*Hawkeye*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawkeye_(miniseries) "Hawkeye (miniseries)") (2021). In her highest-grossing releases, she voiced [Goldilocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldilocks "Goldilocks") in *[Puss in Boots: The Last Wish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puss_in_Boots:_The_Last_Wish "Puss in Boots: The Last Wish")* (2022), and portrayed [Jean Tatlock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Tatlock "Jean Tatlock") in *[Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppenheimer_(film) "Oppenheimer (film)")* (2023) and [Princess Irulan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Irulan "Princess Irulan") in *[Dune: Part Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dune:_Part_Two "Dune: Part Two")* (2024); she also continued to gain praise for her performances in dramas such as *[We Live in Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Live_in_Time "We Live in Time")* (2024).

## Early life

Florence Pugh was born on 3 January 1996 in [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford "Oxford") to dancer Deborah and restaurateur Clinton Pugh. She has three siblings: actor and musician [Toby Sebastian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toby_Sebastian "Toby Sebastian"), actress Arabella Gibbins, and Rafaela "Raffie" Pugh. As a child she suffered from [tracheomalacia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracheomalacia "Tracheomalacia"), which caused breathing problems and led to frequent hospitalisations. The family moved to [Manilva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manilva "Manilva") in Spain when Pugh was three, hoping the warmer climate would improve her health. They lived there until she was six, when they returned to Oxford.

She was educated privately at [Wychwood School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wychwood_School "Wychwood School") and [St Edward's School, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Edward%27s_School,_Oxford "St Edward's School, Oxford"), but disliked the lack of support for her acting ambitions.

## Career

### Early roles (2014–2018)

While still studying in [sixth form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_form "Sixth form"), Pugh made her professional acting debut in the 2014 drama, *[The Falling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Falling_(2014_film) "The Falling (2014 film)")*, playing a precocious teenager opposite [Maisie Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maisie_Williams "Maisie Williams"). Tara Brady of *[The Irish Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Irish_Times "The Irish Times")* deemed Pugh "remarkable", while [IndieWire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndieWire "IndieWire")'s Oliver Lyttelton called her "striking". In the same year, Pugh was nominated for Best British Newcomer at the [BFI London Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BFI_London_Film_Festival "BFI London Film Festival") as well as for Young British / Irish Performer of the Year by the [London Film Critics' Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Film_Critics%27_Circle "London Film Critics' Circle"). The next year, she was cast as a singer-songwriter in the [dramedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dramedy "Dramedy") pilot *Studio City*, co-starring [Eric McCormack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_McCormack "Eric McCormack") as the character's father. The pilot was not picked up to series. Pugh later characterised her experience on *Studio City* negatively due to pressures to change her appearance.

In 2016, Pugh starred in the independent drama *[Lady Macbeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Macbeth_(film) "Lady Macbeth (film)")*, a film based on [Nikolai Leskov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Leskov "Nikolai Leskov")'s novella *[Lady Macbeth of the Mtsensk District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Macbeth_of_the_Mtsensk_District_(novella) "Lady Macbeth of the Mtsensk District (novella)")*, and appeared in the first series of the [ITV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_(TV_network) "ITV (TV network)") detective series *[Marcella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcella_(TV_series) "Marcella (TV series)")*. In the former, she played Katherine, an unhappily married bride who grows violent. Pugh attributed her attraction to the part to her partiality to characters with "confusing or at least interesting" motivations. The role earned her acclaim. She also credited the production with reviving her interest in cinema after being dispirited by *Studio City*. Reviewing the film for *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")*, Guy Lodge commended her portrayal of the character's "complex, under-the-skin transformation". She won the [BIFA for Best Performance by an Actress in a British Independent Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BIFA_for_Best_Performance_by_an_Actress_in_a_British_Independent_Film "BIFA for Best Performance by an Actress in a British Independent Film") for the role.

In 2018, Pugh was nominated for the [BAFTA Rising Star Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Rising_Star_Award "BAFTA Rising Star Award") at the [71st British Academy Film Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/71st_British_Academy_Film_Awards "71st British Academy Film Awards"). She then played [Cordelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordelia_(King_Lear) "Cordelia (King Lear)") to [Anthony Hopkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Hopkins "Anthony Hopkins")'s titular [King Lear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Lear "King Lear") in [Richard Eyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Eyre "Richard Eyre")'s television film *[King Lear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Lear_(2018_film) "King Lear (2018 film)")* and appeared in the short film *[Leading Lady Parts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leading_Lady_Parts "Leading Lady Parts")* in support of the [Time's Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time%27s_Up_(organization) "Time's Up (organization)") initiative. Later that year, Pugh portrayed [Elizabeth de Burgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_de_Burgh "Elizabeth de Burgh") in the [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") historical film *[Outlaw King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlaw_King "Outlaw King")*, co-starring [Chris Pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Pine "Chris Pine") as [Robert the Bruce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_the_Bruce "Robert the Bruce"). Charles Bramesco of *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* found her to be "excellent despite her thankless role". She next starred in a [six-part miniseries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Little_Drummer_Girl_(miniseries) "The Little Drummer Girl (miniseries)") adaptation of [John le Carré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_le_Carr%C3%A9 "John le Carré")'s spy novel *[The Little Drummer Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Little_Drummer_Girl_(novel) "The Little Drummer Girl (novel)")*, in which she played an actress who becomes embroiled in an espionage plot. Her performance was met with praise. While divided on the series overall, [Richard Lawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Lawson_(writer) "Richard Lawson (writer)") of *[Vanity Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanity_Fair_(magazine) "Vanity Fair (magazine)")* called Pugh "terrific throughout" and added that she "smartly mixes earthiness with sophistication, wisdom with naïveté."

### Breakthrough and critical recognition (2019–present)

Pugh starred in three major films in 2019, during which she was recognised as having experienced an international breakthrough. She first played professional wrestler [Paige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paige_(wrestler) "Paige (wrestler)") in *[Fighting with My Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighting_with_My_Family "Fighting with My Family")*, a comedy-drama about Paige's career. The film premiered at the [2019 Sundance Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Sundance_Film_Festival "2019 Sundance Film Festival") to positive reviews. Geoffrey Macnab of *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent "The Independent")* called Pugh "completely convincing as the wrestler", adding that she showed "the same defiance, scruffy glamour and self-deprecating humour as the real life ... Paige". Pugh next headlined [Ari Aster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ari_Aster "Ari Aster")'s horror film *[Midsommar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midsommar "Midsommar")*, which chronicles an American couple, played by her and [Jack Reynor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Reynor "Jack Reynor"), who travel to Sweden and encounter a cult. Critics complimented Pugh's portrayal of the desolate Dani Ardor, with [David Edelstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Edelstein "David Edelstein") of *[Vulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulture_(magazine) "Vulture (magazine)")* calling it "amazingly vivid".

In her final film release of the year, Pugh starred in *[Little Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Women_(2019_film) "Little Women (2019 film)")*, a [period drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Period_drama "Period drama") film adaptation of [Louisa May Alcott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisa_May_Alcott "Louisa May Alcott")'s [novel of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Women "Little Women") directed by [Greta Gerwig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greta_Gerwig "Greta Gerwig"). She portrayed [Amy March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_March "Amy March"), a fickle artist, from age 12 into adulthood, and has said that the character is in a "sweet spot of not knowing how to deal with her emotions". The film received critical acclaim and grossed $209 million. In his review, David Rooney of *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")* praised the "disarming grace, humor and a willful streak that grows almost imperceptibly into wisdom" with which Pugh managed the part's "tricky contradictions". Pugh earned nominations for the [BAFTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Supporting_Role "BAFTA Award for Best Actress in a Supporting Role") and [Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress "Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress") for her performance.

Pugh portrayed [Yelena Belova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yelena_Belova_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Yelena Belova (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"), a spy, in the [Marvel Cinematic Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe "Marvel Cinematic Universe") superhero film *[Black Widow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Widow_(2021_film) "Black Widow (2021 film)")*. She said the film was about "girls who are stolen from around the world". Released in 2021, it garnered positive reviews from critics, who highlighted Pugh's performance. [Caryn James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caryn_James "Caryn James") of [BBC Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Culture "BBC Culture") credited Pugh for making Belova "the most vibrant person in the film, more lived-in than most action-movie characters". She reprised the role in the [Disney+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney%2B "Disney+") series *[Hawkeye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawkeye_(miniseries) "Hawkeye (miniseries)")* later in the year.

In 2022, Pugh starred in the thriller *[Don't Worry Darling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don%27t_Worry_Darling "Don't Worry Darling")*, directed by [Olivia Wilde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_Wilde "Olivia Wilde"), and the drama *[The Wonder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wonder_(film) "The Wonder (film)")*, an adaptation of [Emma Donoghue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Donoghue "Emma Donoghue")'s [namesake novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wonder_(Donoghue_novel) "The Wonder (Donoghue novel)"). While filming the former, she allegedly clashed with Wilde, causing her to limit the amount of promotion she did for the film. *Don't Worry Darling* premiered at the [79th Venice International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/79th_Venice_International_Film_Festival "79th Venice International Film Festival"), where critics deemed Pugh's performance superior to the film. In *The Wonder*, she played a nurse in 1862 who is sent to investigate an alleged supernatural miracle. Kevin Maher of *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times "The Times")* found Pugh's "impossibly vivid and convincing" performance to be the film's prime asset. In her final release of the year, she voiced [Goldilocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldilocks "Goldilocks") in the [DreamWorks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DreamWorks_Animation "DreamWorks Animation") animated film *[Puss in Boots: The Last Wish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puss_in_Boots:_The_Last_Wish "Puss in Boots: The Last Wish")*, which earned over $480 million worldwide.

[Zach Braff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zach_Braff "Zach Braff")'s drama film *[A Good Person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Good_Person "A Good Person")* (2023), in which Pugh starred as a car crash survivor, marked her first producing venture. Instead of opting for a wig, Pugh cut off her own hair for the part. She also wrote and sang two songs, "The Best Part" and "I Hate Myself", for the film's soundtrack. In [Christopher Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan "Christopher Nolan")'s biographical film *[Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppenheimer_(film) "Oppenheimer (film)")*, starring [Cillian Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cillian_Murphy "Cillian Murphy") in the [title role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Robert_Oppenheimer "J. Robert Oppenheimer"), Pugh played [Communist Party USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_USA "Communist Party USA") member [Jean Tatlock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Tatlock "Jean Tatlock"). *[Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_(magazine) "Empire (magazine)")*'s Dan Jolin wrote that she "elegantly dominate[s] her few scenes". With a worldwide gross of over $967 million, *Oppenheimer* is Pugh's highest-grossing release.

Pugh next played [Princess Irulan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Irulan "Princess Irulan") in *[Dune: Part Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dune:_Part_Two "Dune: Part Two")*, a sequel to the [2021 science fiction film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dune_(2021_film) "Dune (2021 film)"). Released in 2024, the film garnered positive reviews and grossed over $711 million worldwide. [Andrew Garfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Garfield "Andrew Garfield") and Pugh starred as a couple navigating cancer in [John Crowley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Crowley_(director) "John Crowley (director)")'s romantic drama *[We Live in Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Live_in_Time "We Live in Time")*. She shaved her head for her role in the film. Glenn Whipp of the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")* deemed her performance to be the production's prime asset.

In 2025, Pugh reprised the role of Yelena Belova in *[Thunderbolts\*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunderbolts* "Thunderbolts*")*. She insisted on filming the scene where Belova jumps from the top of [Merdeka 118](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merdeka_118 "Merdeka 118") without using stunt doubles. [Marvel Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Studios "Marvel Studios") was initially reluctant to allow Pugh to perform the stunt due to insurance concerns, but after her persistent efforts, including discussions with [Kevin Feige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Feige "Kevin Feige"), she was permitted to perform the jump. Pugh stated her reasoning being: "It's our duty as actors to protect and defend your characters and to put in the life. There's so much that's on the page, but it's really what you add to it in the last 20% that actually makes every character that any actor plays. There's so many -isms that I was very welcomed into putting into the movie." The film and Pugh's performance received positive reviews from critics.

Pugh played the lead role of [Cathy Ames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathy_Ames "Cathy Ames") in the 2026 [limited series adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_of_Eden_(2026_miniseries) "East of Eden (2026 miniseries)") of [John Steinbeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Steinbeck "John Steinbeck")'s novel *[East of Eden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_of_Eden_(novel) "East of Eden (novel)")* and will reprise her roles as Yelena Belova in *[Avengers: Doomsday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers:_Doomsday "Avengers: Doomsday")* and Princess Irulan in *[Dune: Part Three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dune:_Part_Three "Dune: Part Three")*.

## Public image

Pugh is known for her fashion sense, with publications such as *[Harper's Bazaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harper%27s_Bazaar "Harper's Bazaar")* and *[British Vogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Vogue "British Vogue")* calling her fashion choices "bold", "daring" and "unique". At a [Valentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentino_(fashion_house) "Valentino (fashion house)") show in 2022, she wore a sheer pink gown, which led to some backlash as it showed her nipples. Commenting on the reception, she defended her choice and her body on Instagram. A year later, she wore another transparent gown to a Valentino event.

Journalists have often noted Pugh's outspokenness when using social media to address issues such as [body shaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_shaming "Body shaming"), [cyberbullying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberbullying "Cyberbullying"), and [beauty standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beauty_standards "Beauty standards"). *British Vogue*'s Raven Smith reported that her sincerity, interest in others, and humility distinguish her from more guarded actors, whereas Andrea Cuttler of *Harper's Bazaar* said that her open off-screen persona has come to be expected due to establishing "a career playing women who refuse to be silenced". Annie Lourd of *The Independent* credits Pugh with "carving out a niche playing difficult characters you know you should hate, but, for whatever reason, end up rooting for". Pugh has said that she gravitates towards roles that terrify or challenge her, and observed that "all of my movies have that element of women being forced into a corner, forced into an opinion, forced into a way of life ... And then finally, something cracks".

In a 2022 readers' poll by *Empire* magazine, she was voted one of the 50 greatest actors of all time. Terming her "one of the very best of her generation", the magazine attributed her success to bringing "a grounded empathy to her characters". Pugh was included on the entertainment category of *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes "Forbes")* magazine's annual [30 Under 30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30_Under_30 "30 Under 30") list, which recognises the 30 most influential people in Europe under the age of 30, in 2019. *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")* magazine placed her on the artists category of its [100 Next](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100_Next "100 Next") list, which highlights rising stars and emerging leaders in their fields, in 2021. In 2023, the magazine featured her in their [Next Generation Leaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Generation_Leaders "Next Generation Leaders") list.

## Personal life and other work

From 2019 to 2022, Pugh was in a relationship with American actor and filmmaker [Zach Braff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zach_Braff "Zach Braff"). They met while working on the short film *In the Time It Takes to Get There*, which Braff directed, and lived together in Los Angeles. She defended the 21-year age-gap between them after public scrutiny. She dated photographer Charlie Gooch in 2023. She has been in a relationship with British actor [Finn Cole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finn_Cole "Finn Cole") since 2024.

From 2013 to 2016, she performed [cover songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cover_songs "Cover songs") on YouTube under the name Flossie Rose. She was featured on her brother's song "Midnight", released on 15 May 2021. In 2020, she took part in the series *[Acting for a Cause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acting_for_a_Cause "Acting for a Cause")* for a live reading of [Kenneth Lonergan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Lonergan "Kenneth Lonergan")'s play *[This Is Our Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Is_Our_Youth "This Is Our Youth")* to raise funds for the [Entertainment Industry Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Industry_Foundation "Entertainment Industry Foundation") during the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic").

In 2024, Pugh revealed that she has [polycystic ovary syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycystic_ovary_syndrome "Polycystic ovary syndrome") and [endometriosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endometriosis "Endometriosis"), two conditions that can make conceiving children difficult later in life, and had frozen her eggs as a result.

Speaking at the [Together for Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Together_for_Palestine "Together for Palestine") fundraising event in London in September 2025, she said, "Silence in the face of such suffering is not neutrality; it is complicity in a crime." She urged attendees to call on governments to respond to the situation in [Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_war "Gaza war"). Pugh has also signed a petition calling on the British government to end its "complicity in the horrors in Gaza".

## Accolades

Pugh has been nominated for an [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award "Academy Award") and two [British Academy Film Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Awards "British Academy Film Awards"). She received an Academy Award nomination for Best Supporting Actress and a BAFTA Award nomination for Best Actress in a Supporting Role, both for her work in *Little Women*, as well as a BAFTA Rising Star Award nomination. Her performances in *Lady Macbeth* and *The Wonder* respectively earned her a [British Independent Film Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Independent_Film_Award "British Independent Film Award") win and another nomination. At the [2019 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Cannes_Film_Festival "2019 Cannes Film Festival"), Pugh was awarded the [Trophée Chopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troph%C3%A9e_Chopard "Trophée Chopard"). As part of the ensemble of [Christopher Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan "Christopher Nolan")'s *[Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppenheimer_(film) "Oppenheimer (film)")*, Pugh won the [Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Cast in a Motion Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Cast_in_a_Motion_Picture "Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Cast in a Motion Picture").
