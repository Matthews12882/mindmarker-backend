# Tupac Shakur

**Tupac Amaru Shakur** ([/ˈtuːpɑːk ʃəˈkʊər/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:En-us-Tupac_Shakur.wav "File:En-us-Tupac Shakur.wav") [*TOO-pahk shə-KOOR*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"); [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andean_Spanish "Andean Spanish"): *Túpac*; June 16, 1971 – September 13, 1996), also known by his stage names **2Pac** and **Makaveli**, was an American rapper and actor. He is regarded as one of the most influential musical artists of the 20th century and as a prominent political activist for [Black America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_America "Black America"). His record sales exceed [75 million](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_best-selling_music_artists "List of best-selling music artists") worldwide. Some of Shakur's music addressed social injustice, political issues, and the marginalization of [African Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Americans "African Americans"). While some of his other songs were gangsta rap.

Shakur was born in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") to parents who were [Black Panther Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Panther_Party "Black Panther Party") members. Raised by his mother, [Afeni Shakur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afeni_Shakur "Afeni Shakur"), he relocated to the [San Francisco Bay Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay_Area "San Francisco Bay Area") in 1988. His debut album *[2Pacalypse Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2Pacalypse_Now "2Pacalypse Now")* (1991) cemented him as a central figure in [West Coast hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_hip-hop "West Coast hip-hop") for his [political rap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_rap "Political rap") lyrics. Shakur achieved further critical and commercial success with his subsequent albums *[Strictly 4 My N.I.G.G.A.Z...](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strictly_4_My_N.I.G.G.A.Z... "Strictly 4 My N.I.G.G.A.Z...")* (1993) and *[Me Against the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Me_Against_the_World "Me Against the World")* (1995). His [Diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIAA_certification "RIAA certification")-certified album *[All Eyez on Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Eyez_on_Me "All Eyez on Me")* (1996), the first hip-hop [double album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_album "Double album"), abandoned introspective lyrics for volatile gangsta rap. It yielded two [*Billboard* Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100 "Billboard Hot 100")-number one singles, "[California Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Love "California Love")" and "[How Do U Want It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_Do_U_Want_It "How Do U Want It")". Alongside his solo career, Shakur formed the group [Thug Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thug_Life_(group) "Thug Life (group)") and collaborated with artists like [Snoop Dogg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snoop_Dogg "Snoop Dogg"), [Dr. Dre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Dre "Dr. Dre"), and the [Outlawz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlawz "Outlawz"). As an actor, Shakur starred in the films *[Juice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juice_(1992_film) "Juice (1992 film)")* (1992), *[Poetic Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poetic_Justice_(film) "Poetic Justice (film)")* (1993), *[Above the Rim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_the_Rim "Above the Rim")* (1994), *[Bullet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullet_(1996_film) "Bullet (1996 film)")* (1996), *[Gridlock'd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gridlock%27d "Gridlock'd")* (1997), and *[Gang Related](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gang_Related "Gang Related")* (1997).

In November 1994, Shakur was shot five times in the lobby of a New York recording studio and experienced legal troubles, including [incarceration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incarceration "Incarceration"). He served eight months in prison on [sexual abuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_abuse "Sexual abuse") charges, but was released pending appeal in 1995. Following his release, he signed to [Marion "Suge" Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suge_Knight "Suge Knight")'s label [Death Row Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_Row_Records "Death Row Records") and became embroiled in the [East Coast–West Coast hip-hop rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast%E2%80%93West_Coast_hip-hop_rivalry "East Coast–West Coast hip-hop rivalry"), which included a high-profile feud with his former friend [the Notorious B.I.G.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Notorious_B.I.G. "The Notorious B.I.G.") On September 7, 1996, [Shakur was murdered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_Tupac_Shakur "Murder of Tupac Shakur") by an unidentified assailant in a [drive-by shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drive-by_shooting "Drive-by shooting") in [Paradise, Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise,_Nevada "Paradise, Nevada"); he died six days later. On August 31, 2026, [South Side Compton Crips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Side_Compton_Crips "South Side Compton Crips") member Duane "Keffe D" Davis was convicted of orchestrating Shakur's murder, but the shooter (suspected to be [Baby Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby_Lane "Baby Lane")) has not been conclusively identified. On October 13, 2026, Davis was sentenced to 40 years to life in prison for Shakur's murder.

Shakur's double-length posthumous album [*Greatest Hits*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greatest_Hits_(Tupac_Shakur_album) "Greatest Hits (Tupac Shakur album)") (1998) is one of his two releases—and one of only nine hip-hop albums—to have been certified Diamond in the United States. Five more albums have been released since Shakur's death, including the acclaimed *[The Don Killuminati: The 7 Day Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Don_Killuminati:_The_7_Day_Theory "The Don Killuminati: The 7 Day Theory")* (1996) under the stage name Makaveli, all of which have been certified multi-platinum in the United States. In 2002, Shakur was inducted into the [Hip-Hop Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip_Hop_Hall_of_Fame "Hip Hop Hall of Fame"). In 2017, he was inducted into the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame "Rock and Roll Hall of Fame") in his first year of eligibility. *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")* ranked Shakur among the [100 Greatest Artists of All Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone%27s_100_Greatest_Artists_of_All_Time "Rolling Stone's 100 Greatest Artists of All Time"). In 2023, he was awarded a posthumous star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame "Hollywood Walk of Fame"). His influence in music, activism, songwriting, and other areas of culture has been the subject of academic studies.

## Early life

### Birth and ancestry

Tupac Amaru Shakur was born on June 16, 1971, in [East Harlem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Harlem "East Harlem"), Manhattan, New York City, to [Afeni Shakur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afeni_Shakur "Afeni Shakur") (January 10, 1947 – May 2, 2016) and [Billy Garland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Garland_(activist) "Billy Garland (activist)") (born March 14, 1949). While his name was listed on his birth certificate (and in public mentions during his infancy) as **Parish Lesane Crooks**, he was later rechristened in honor of [Túpac Amaru II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BApac_Amaru_II "Túpac Amaru II"); a descendant of the last [Inca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_Empire "Inca Empire") ruler, who was executed in Peru in 1781 after his [revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebellion_of_T%C3%BApac_Amaru_II "Rebellion of Túpac Amaru II") against [Spanish rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire "Spanish Empire").

His mother explained, "I wanted him to have the name of revolutionary, indigenous people in the world. I wanted him to know he was part of a world culture and not just from a neighborhood." Tupac's surname came from Lumumba Shakur, whom his mother married in November 1968. Their marriage fell apart when it was discovered that Lumumba was not Tupac's biological father.

Shakur's parents, stepfather [Mutulu Shakur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutulu_Shakur "Mutulu Shakur"), godmother, [Assata Shakur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assata_Shakur "Assata Shakur") and godfather [Geronimo Pratt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geronimo_Pratt "Geronimo Pratt") had all been active members of the [Black Panther Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Panther_Party "Black Panther Party") in New York in the late 1960s and early 1970s. A month before Shakur's birth, his mother was tried in New York City as part of the [Panther 21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panther_21 "Panther 21") criminal trial. She was acquitted of over 150 charges. To not have her son associated with the party, his mother originally listed the surname of her friend, one-time girlfriend, and ex-cellmate Carol Crooks on his birth certificate.

### 1971–1988: Early life

Tupac attended P.S. 028 Wright Brothers elementary school in [Harlem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlem "Harlem"), New York around age 12 before moving to [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore "Baltimore").

In the 1980s, Shakur's mother found it difficult to find work and struggled with drug addiction. In 1984, his family moved from New York City to [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore "Baltimore"), Maryland. Beginning in 1984, when Shakur was 13, he lived in the [Pen Lucy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pen_Lucy,_Baltimore "Pen Lucy, Baltimore") neighborhood with his mother and younger sister at 3955 Greenmount Ave. The home was a two-story [rowhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowhouse "Rowhouse") that had been subdivided into two separate rental units; the Shakur family lived on the first floor. After his death, the block was renamed Tupac Shakur Way.

While living in Baltimore, Shakur attended eighth grade at Roland Park Middle School, then ninth grade at [Paul Laurence Dunbar High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Laurence_Dunbar_High_School_(Maryland) "Paul Laurence Dunbar High School (Maryland)"). He transferred to the [Baltimore School for the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_School_for_the_Arts "Baltimore School for the Arts") in the tenth grade, where he studied acting, poetry, [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz"), and ballet. He performed in [Shakespearean plays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakespeare%27s_plays "Shakespeare's plays")—the themes of which he identified in patterns of gang warfare—and as the Mouse King in *[The Nutcracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nutcracker "The Nutcracker")* ballet. At the Baltimore School for the Arts, Shakur befriended actress [Jada Pinkett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jada_Pinkett_Smith "Jada Pinkett Smith"), who became the subject of some of his poems ("Jada" and "The Tears in Cupid's Eyes"). With his friend Dana "Mouse" Smith as a [beatbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatboxing "Beatboxing"), he won competitions for the school's best rapper. Known for his humor, he was popular with all crowds of students. He listened to a diverse range of music that included [Kate Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Bush "Kate Bush"), [Culture Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_Club "Culture Club"), [Sinéad O'Connor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sin%C3%A9ad_O%27Connor "Sinéad O'Connor"), and [U2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U2 "U2").

Shakur dated Mary Baldridge, who was the daughter of the director of the local chapter of the [Communist Party USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_USA "Communist Party USA"). In 1988, Shakur moved to [Marin City, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marin_City,_California "Marin City, California"), an impoverished community in the San Francisco Bay Area. In nearby [Mill Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mill_Valley,_California "Mill Valley, California"), he attended [Tamalpais High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamalpais_High_School "Tamalpais High School"), where he performed in several theater productions. Shakur did not graduate from high school, but later earned his [GED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Educational_Development "General Educational Development").

## Career

### 1988–1991: Early career

Shakur began recording under the stage name "MC New York" in 1989. That year, he began attending the poetry classes of [Leila Steinberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leila_Steinberg "Leila Steinberg"), and she soon became his manager. Steinberg organized a concert for Shakur and his rap group Strictly Dope. Steinberg managed to get Shakur signed by Atron Gregory, manager of the rap group [Digital Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Underground "Digital Underground"). In 1990, Gregory placed him with the Underground as a [roadie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roadie "Roadie") and [backup dancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backup_dancer "Backup dancer"). At the request of Steinberg, Digital Underground co-founder Jimi "Chopmaster J" Dright worked with Shakur, [Ray Luv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Luv "Ray Luv") and Dize, a DJ, on their earliest studio recordings. Dright recalls that Shakur did not work well as part of a group, and added, "this guy was on a mission. From day one. Maybe he knew he wasn't going to be around seven years later." From 1988 to 1991, Dright and Digital Underground produced Shakur's earliest work with his crew at the time, Strictly Dope. The recordings were rediscovered in 2000 and released as *The Lost Tapes: Circa 1989.* Afeni Shakur sued to stop the sale of the recordings but the suit was settled in June 2001 and rereleased as *[Beginnings: The Lost Tapes 1988–1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beginnings:_The_Lost_Tapes_1988%E2%80%931991 "Beginnings: The Lost Tapes 1988–1991").*

Shakur debuted under the stage name 2Pac on [Digital Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Underground "Digital Underground"), under a new record label, [Interscope Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interscope_Records "Interscope Records"), on the group's January 1991 single "[Same Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same_Song_(Digital_Underground_song) "Same Song (Digital Underground song)")". The song was featured on the soundtrack of the 1991 film *[Nothing but Trouble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nothing_but_Trouble_(1991_film) "Nothing but Trouble (1991 film)")*, starring [Dan Aykroyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Aykroyd "Dan Aykroyd"), [John Candy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Candy "John Candy"), [Chevy Chase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevy_Chase "Chevy Chase"), and [Demi Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demi_Moore "Demi Moore"). He also appeared with the group in the film, marking Shakur's first film appearance credited as a [cameo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance "Cameo appearance") by the Digital Underground. "Same Song" opened the group's January 1991 EP titled *[This Is an EP Release](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Is_an_EP_Release "This Is an EP Release")*, while Shakur appeared in the music video. Shakur's early days with [Digital Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Underground "Digital Underground") made him acquainted with [Randy "Stretch" Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stretch_(rapper) "Stretch (rapper)"), who along with his brother, dubbed Majesty, and a friend debuted with an EP as a rap group and production team, [Live Squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_Squad "Live Squad"), in [Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens "Queens"), New York. Stretch was featured on a track of the Digital Underground's 1991 album *[Sons of the P](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sons_of_the_P "Sons of the P")*. Becoming fast friends, Shakur and Stretch recorded and performed together often.

### 1991–1993: *2Pacalypse Now*

On November 12, 1991, Shakur released his debut album, *[2Pacalypse Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2Pacalypse_Now "2Pacalypse Now")* thru [Interscope Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interscope_Records "Interscope Records"). Compared to Digital Underground's works, the album explored contemporary social issues in American society at the time, including [racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism_in_the_United_States "Racism in the United States"), [police brutality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_brutality_in_the_United_States "Police brutality in the United States"), [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_the_United_States "Poverty in the United States"), [gang violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_and_crime_in_the_United_States "Race and crime in the United States"), [teenage pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teenage_pregnancy_in_the_United_States "Teenage pregnancy in the United States") and [drug abuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substance_abuse "Substance abuse"). The album generated controversy when [Ronald Ray Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Ray_Howard "Ronald Ray Howard") murdered a [Texas Highway Patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Highway_Patrol "Texas Highway Patrol") trooper and his defense attorney claimed he was influenced by *2Pacalypse Now* and its strong theme of [police brutality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_brutality "Police brutality"). U.S. vice president [Dan Quayle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Quayle "Dan Quayle") criticized the album, making the statement, "There's no reason for a record like this to be released. It has no place in our society." Tupac, finding himself misunderstood, explained, in part:

> I just wanted to rap about things that affected young black males. When I said that, I didn't know that I was gonna tie myself down to just take all the blunts and hits for all the young black males, to be the media's kicking post for young black males.

Despite controversy, *2Pacalypse Now* was [certified Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Certified_Gold "Certified Gold"), and peaked at No. 64 on the [*Billboard* 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200") and No. 13 on the [Top R&B/Hip-Hop Albums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_R%26B/Hip-Hop_Albums "Top R&B/Hip-Hop Albums"). Three singles were released: "[Trapped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapped_(Tupac_Shakur_song) "Trapped (Tupac Shakur song)")", "[Brenda's Got a Baby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenda%27s_Got_a_Baby "Brenda's Got a Baby")" (No. 23 on [Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_R%26B/Hip-Hop_Songs "Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Songs") and No. 3 on [Hot Rap Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Rap_Songs "Hot Rap Songs")), and "[If My Homie Calls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/If_My_Homie_Calls "If My Homie Calls")" (No. 3 on Hot Rap Songs). Some prominent rappers—like [Nas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nas "Nas"), [Eminem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eminem "Eminem"), [the Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Game_(rapper) "The Game (rapper)"), and [Talib Kweli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talib_Kweli "Talib Kweli")—cite it as an inspiration. Aside from "If My Homie Calls", the singles "[Trapped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapped_(2Pac_song) "Trapped (2Pac song)")" and "Brenda's Got a Baby" poetically depict individual struggles under socioeconomic disadvantage.

In 1992, Shakur made his feature film debut in *[Juice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juice_(1992_film) "Juice (1992 film)")*, in which he plays the film's [antagonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antagonist "Antagonist"), Roland Bishop. [*Rolling Stone*'s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone") [Peter Travers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Travers "Peter Travers") calls him "the film's most magnetic figure". Multiple actors had auditioned for the role. Shakur accompanied [Money-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money-B "Money-B") to the audition and asked producer [Neal H. Moritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neal_H._Moritz "Neal H. Moritz") to read. He was given 15 minutes to rehearse before the audition and secured the role.

### 1993–1994: *Strictly 4 My N.I.G.G.A.Z...*, film career, and The Notorious B.I.G.

Shakur's second album, *[Strictly 4 My N.I.G.G.A.Z...](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strictly_4_My_N.I.G.G.A.Z... "Strictly 4 My N.I.G.G.A.Z...")*, was released in February 1993. A critical and commercial success, it debuted at No. 24 on the [*Billboard* 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200"). Emphasizing Tupac's sociopolitical views, the song "Last Wordz" features [Ice Cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_Cube "Ice Cube") and [Ice-T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice-T "Ice-T"). The album's single "[I Get Around](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Get_Around_(Tupac_Shakur_song) "I Get Around (Tupac Shakur song)")" is a party anthem featuring Digital Underground's [Shock G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_G "Shock G") and [Money-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money-B "Money-B"), reached No. 11 on the [*Billboard* Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100 "Billboard Hot 100"). The next single, "[Keep Ya Head Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keep_Ya_Head_Up "Keep Ya Head Up")", an anthem for [women's empowerment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_empowerment "Women's empowerment"), peaked at No. 12 on the *Billboard Hot 100*. The fourth and final single, "[Papa'z Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papa%27z_Song "Papa'z Song")", peaked at No. 87 on the *Billboard* Hot 100. The album was certified [Platinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum_certification "Platinum certification") in April 1995.

Shakur starred with [Janet Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Jackson "Janet Jackson") in [John Singleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Singleton "John Singleton")'s 1993 romance film, *[Poetic Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poetic_Justice_(film) "Poetic Justice (film)"),* and portrayed the character, Birdie in the 1994 film, *[Above the Rim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_the_Rim "Above the Rim")*. Director [Allen Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_Hughes_(director) "Allen Hughes (director)") had originally cast Shakur as the character, Sharif for the 1993 film, *[Menace II Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menace_II_Society "Menace II Society"),* however he was replaced after Shakur assaulted the director on set due to a discrepancy with the script. In 2013, Hughes stated that Shakur would have outshone the other actors "because he was bigger than the movie". Singleton initially cast Shakur in his 1995 film *[Higher Learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_Learning "Higher Learning")*, but was fired because the studio would not finance the film following his arrest. Singleton also stated that he had Shakur in mind in the early stages of what became the 2001 film *[Baby Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby_Boy_(film) "Baby Boy (film)")*, [Tyrese Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrese_Gibson "Tyrese Gibson") ultimately landed the role. The [set design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_design "Set design") includes a Shakur mural in the protagonist's bedroom, and the film's score includes Shakur's song "[Hail Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hail_Mary_(2Pac_song) "Hail Mary (2Pac song)")".

In 1993, while visiting Los Angeles, [the Notorious B.I.G.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Notorious_B.I.G. "The Notorious B.I.G.") asked a local drug dealer to introduce him to Shakur and they quickly became friends. The pair would socialize when Shakur went to New York or B.I.G. to Los Angeles. During this period, at his own live shows, Shakur would call B.I.G. onto stage to rap with him and Stretch. Reportedly, B.I.G. asked Shakur to manage him, whereupon Shakur advised him that [Sean Combs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Combs "Sean Combs") would make him a star. Yet in the meantime, Shakur's lifestyle was comparatively lavish to B.I.G. who had not yet established himself. Shakur had a falling out with B.I.G. after Shakur was shot at Quad Studios in 1994.

### 1995: *Me Against the World*

Shakur's third album, *[Me Against the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Me_Against_the_World "Me Against the World")*, was released while he was incarcerated in March 1995. It is now hailed as his [magnum opus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masterpiece "Masterpiece"), and commonly ranks among the greatest, most influential rap albums. The album debuted at No. 1 on the [*Billboard* 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200") and sold 240,000 copies in its first week, setting a then record for highest first-week sales for a solo male rapper.

The lead single, "[Dear Mama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dear_Mama "Dear Mama")", was released in February 1995 with "Old School" as the [B-side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-side_and_B-side "A-side and B-side"). It is the album's most successful single, topping the [Hot Rap Singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Rap_Songs "Hot Rap Songs") chart, and peaking at No. 9 on the *Billboard* Hot 100. In July, it was certified Platinum. It ranked No. 51 on the [year-end charts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Year-End_Hot_100_singles_of_1995 "Billboard Year-End Hot 100 singles of 1995"). The second single, "[So Many Tears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/So_Many_Tears "So Many Tears")", was released in June 1995, reaching No. 6 on the Hot Rap Singles chart and No. 44 on Hot 100. The final single, "[Temptations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temptations_(song) "Temptations (song)")", was released in August 1995. It reached No. 68 on the Hot 100, No. 35 on the [Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Singles & Tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_R%26B/Hip-Hop_Songs "Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Songs"), and No. 13 on the Hot Rap Singles. Several celebrities showed their support for Shakur by appearing in the music video for "Temptations".

Shakur won best rap album at the [1996 Soul Train Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Soul_Train_Music_Awards "1996 Soul Train Music Awards"). In 2001, it ranked 4th among his total albums in sales, with about 3 million copies sold in the U.S.

### 1995–1996: *All Eyez on Me* and *Tha Don Killuminati: The 7 Day Theory*

While Shakur was imprisoned in 1995, his mother was about to lose her house. Shakur had his wife Keisha Morris contact [Death Row Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_Row_Records "Death Row Records") founder [Suge Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suge_Knight "Suge Knight") in Los Angeles. Reportedly, Shakur's mother promptly received $15,000. After an August visit to [Clinton Correctional Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_Correctional_Facility "Clinton Correctional Facility") in northern New York state, Knight traveled southward to New York City to attend the [2nd Annual Source Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Source_Awards "1995 Source Awards") ceremony. Meanwhile, an [East Coast–West Coast hip-hop rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast%E2%80%93West_Coast_hip-hop_rivalry "East Coast–West Coast hip-hop rivalry") was brewing between Death Row and [Bad Boy Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Boy_Records "Bad Boy Records"). In October 1995, Knight visited Shakur in prison again and posted $1.4 million bond. Shakur returned to Los Angeles and joined Death Row with the appeal of his December 1994 conviction pending.

Shakur's fourth album, *All Eyez on Me*, arrived on February 13, 1996. It was rap's first double album—meeting two of the three albums due in Shakur's contract with Death Row—and bore five singles. The album shows Shakur [rapping about the gangsta lifestyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangsta_rap "Gangsta rap"), leaving behind his previous political messages. With standout production, the album has more party tracks and often a triumphant tone. Music journalist [Kevin Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Powell "Kevin Powell") noted that Shakur, once released from prison, became more aggressive, and "seemed like a completely transformed person".

As Shakur's second album to hit No. 1 on both the [Top R&B/Hip-Hop Albums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_R%26B/Hip-Hop_Albums "Top R&B/Hip-Hop Albums") chart and the pop albums chart, the *Billboard* 200, it sold 566,000 copies in its first week and was [certified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIAA_certification "RIAA certification") 5× Multi-Platinum in April. The singles "[How Do U Want It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_Do_U_Want_It "How Do U Want It")" and "[California Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Love "California Love")" reached No. 1 on the *Billboard* Hot 100. Death Row released Shakur's diss track "[Hit 'Em Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hit_%27Em_Up "Hit 'Em Up")" as the non-album B-side to "How Do U Want It". In this venomous tirade, the proclaimed "Bad Boy killer" threatens violent payback on all things Bad Boy — B.I.G., Sean Combs, Junior M.A.F.I.A., the company — and on any in the East Coast rap scene, like rap duo [Mobb Deep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobb_Deep "Mobb Deep") and rapper [Chino XL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chino_XL "Chino XL"), who allegedly had commented against Shakur about the dispute. Posthumously, *All Eyez on Me* won R&B/Soul or Rap Album of the Year at the [1997 Soul Train Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Soul_Train_Music_Awards "1997 Soul Train Music Awards"), Favorite Rap/Hip-Hop Artist at the [1997 American Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_American_Music_Awards "1997 American Music Awards"), and the album was certified 9× Multi-Platinum in June 1998, and 10× in July 2014.

At the time of his death, a fifth solo album was already finished, *[The Don Killuminati: The 7 Day Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Don_Killuminati:_The_7_Day_Theory "The Don Killuminati: The 7 Day Theory")*, under the stage name Makaveli. It had been recorded during the summer of 1996 and released that year. The lyrics were written and recorded in three days, and mixing took another four days. In 2005, MTV.com ranked *The 7 Day Theory* at No. 9 among hip-hop's greatest albums ever, and by 2006 a classic album. Its singular poignance, through hurt and rage, contemplation and vendetta, resonates with many fans. According to George "Papa G" Pryce, Death Row Records' then director of [public relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_relations "Public relations"), the album was meant to be "underground", and was not intended for release before the artist was murdered.[*[unreliable source?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Reliable_sources "Wikipedia:Reliable sources")*] It peaked at No. 1 on *[Billboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_(magazine) "Billboard (magazine)")*'s [Top R&B/Hip-Hop Albums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_R%26B/Hip-Hop_Albums "Top R&B/Hip-Hop Albums") chart and on the *Billboard* 200, with the second-highest debut-week sales total of any album that year. On June 15, 1999, it was certified 4× Multi-Platinum.

Soon after Shakur's death, three more films starring him were released, *[Bullet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullet_(1996_film) "Bullet (1996 film)")* (1996), *[Gridlock'd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gridlock%27d "Gridlock'd")* (1997), and *[Gang Related](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gang_Related "Gang Related")* (1997).

## Personal life

Jada Pinkett reported that Tupac Shakur developed [alopecia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alopecia "Alopecia") sometime around 1991.

In 1993, during a police raid of Shakur's room at the [Parker Meridian Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thompson_Central_Park_New_York_Hotel "Thompson Central Park New York Hotel") in New York City, authorities confiscated a videotape showing Shakur with his then-girlfriend, Desiree Smith. Investigators were attempting to use the tape in their case concerning allegations made by Ayanna Jackson. In 2022, Smith stated that she had neither been underage nor intoxicated at the time of the encounter. In 2011, another video featuring Shakur receiving [oral sex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_sex "Oral sex") while rapping along to an unreleased song was reportedly sold to a private collector. The recording, filmed in 1993, also included [Money B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money-B "Money-B") of Digital Underground.

### Family and relationships

Tupac had a younger [maternal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afeni_Shakur "Afeni Shakur") half-sister, Sekyiwa "Set" Shakur, and five [paternal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Garland_(activist) "Billy Garland (activist)") half-siblings: Landon, Carlos (Malik), William "Billy" III, N'Neka Garland (1973–2023), and Takerra Allen-Taylor. He also had five [stepsiblings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepsiblings "Stepsiblings") from his mother's marriage to [Mutulu Shakur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutulu_Shakur "Mutulu Shakur") (who was Tupac's stepfather and Sekyiwa's father), namely: Chinua, Talib "Tyrone", and [Maurice "Mopreme" Shakur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mopreme_Shakur "Mopreme Shakur"), Nzingha Shakur-Ali, and [Ayize Jama-Everett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayize_Jama-Everett "Ayize Jama-Everett"). Tupac had no [full siblings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_siblings "Full siblings").

On April 29, 1995, Shakur married his girlfriend Keisha Morris, then a [pre-law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-law "Pre-law") student, while he was incarcerated. The marriage was annulled ten months later.

While attending the Baltimore School for the Arts, Shakur formed a close friendship with [Jada Pinkett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jada_Pinkett_Smith "Jada Pinkett Smith"). Shakur later helped Pinkett secure her first film role in *Menace II Society* (1993), while Pinkett helped him obtain a guest-starring role on the sitcom *[A Different World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Different_World "A Different World")* the same year. Pinkett appeared in Shakur's music videos for "Keep Ya Head Up" and "Temptations". She also conceived the concept for his "California Love" music video and was originally set to direct it before withdrawing from the project. In 1995, Jada Pinkett contributed $100,000 toward Shakur's bail while he awaited an appeal of his sexual abuse conviction. She later revealed that she declined a marriage proposal from Shakur while he was incarcerated at Rikers Island that same year. Reflecting on their relationship, Shakur said, "Jada is my heart. She will be my friend for my whole life. We'll be old together. Jada can ask me to do anything and she can have it." Pinkett similarly described Shakur as "one of my best friends" and said, "He was like a brother. It was beyond friendship for us. The type of relationship we had, you only get that once in a lifetime."

In an interview with *[The Source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Source_(magazine) "The Source (magazine)")*, Shakur criticized record producer [Quincy Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quincy_Jones "Quincy Jones") for his [interracial marriages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interracial_marriage "Interracial marriage"). His daughter [Rashida Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashida_Jones "Rashida Jones") responded in an irate open letter to the magazine. Shakur later apologized to her sister [Kidada Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidada_Jones "Kidada Jones"), whom he began dating in 1996. The couple attended [Men's Fashion Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men%27s_Fashion_Week "Men's Fashion Week") in Milan and walked together for a [Romeo Gigli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romeo_Gigli "Romeo Gigli") runway show. Jones was at the Las Vegas hotel where Shakur was staying when he was fatally shot in September 1996.

### Friendships

Shakur befriended Treach when they were both roadies on Public Enemy's tour in 1990. He made a cameo in [Naughty by Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naughty_by_Nature "Naughty by Nature")'s music video "[Uptown Anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uptown_Anthem "Uptown Anthem")" in 1992. Treach collaborated on Shakur's song "5 Deadly Venomz" and appeared in the music video for Shakur's "Temptations". Treach was also a speaker at a public memorial service for Shakur in 1996.

After Tupac Shakur was shot in 1994, he recuperated at the home of actress [Jasmine Guy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasmine_Guy "Jasmine Guy"). The two had met following Shakur's guest appearance on the sitcom *A Different World* in 1993. Guy later appeared in his music video for "Temptations" and went on to write his mother's 2004 memoir, *Afeni Shakur: Evolution of a Revolutionary*. In a 1995 interview with *Vibe*, Shakur named Pinkett, Guy, Treach, Madonna, and Mickey Rourke among those who supported him during his incarceration. He also described Madonna as a supportive friend; Madonna later revealed that the two had dated in 1994. Shakur and Rourke developed a close friendship while filming the movie *Bullet* in 1994, with Rourke later recalling that Shakur "was there for me during some very hard times."

Shakur also maintained friendships with other celebrities, including [Mike Tyson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Tyson "Mike Tyson") [Chuck D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_D "Chuck D"), [Jim Carrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Carrey "Jim Carrey"), and [Alanis Morissette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alanis_Morissette "Alanis Morissette"). In April 1996, Shakur stated that he, Morissette, Snoop Dogg, and Suge Knight planned to open a restaurant together.

## Legal issues

### 1993 shooting in Atlanta

On October 31, 1993, Shakur was arrested in Atlanta for shooting two off-duty police officers, brothers Mark Whitwell and Scott Whitwell. The Atlanta police claimed the shooting occurred after the brothers were almost struck by a car carrying Shakur while they were crossing the street with their wives. As they argued with the driver, Shakur's car pulled up and he shot the Whitwells in the buttocks and the abdomen. However, there are conflicting accounts that the Whitwells were harassing a black motorist and uttered racial slurs. According to some witnesses, Shakur and his entourage had fired in self-defense as Mark Whitwell shot at them first.

Shakur was charged with two counts of aggravated assault. Mark Whitwell was charged with firing at Shakur's car and later with making false statements to investigators. Scott Whitwell admitted to possessing a gun he had taken from a [Henry County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_County,_Georgia "Henry County, Georgia") police evidence room. Prosecutors ultimately dropped all charges against both parties. Mark Whitwell resigned from the force seven months after the shooting. Both brothers filed civil suits against Shakur; Mark Whitwell's suit was settled out of court, while Scott Whitwell's $2 million lawsuit resulted in a default judgment entered against the rapper's estate in 1998.

### Sexual assault case, prison sentence, appeal and release

In November 1993, Shakur and two other men were charged in New York with [sodomizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodomy "Sodomy") a woman in Shakur's hotel room. The woman, Ayanna Jackson, alleged that after she performed oral sex on Shakur at the public dance floor of a Manhattan nightclub, she went to his hotel room on a later day, when Shakur, record executive [Jacques "Haitian Jack" Agnant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Agnant "Jacques Agnant"), Shakur's road manager Charles Fuller and an unidentified fourth man apprehended and forced her to perform non-consensual oral sex on each of them. Shakur was also charged with [illegal possession of a firearm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_possession_of_a_firearm "Illegal possession of a firearm") as two guns were found in the hotel room. Interviewed on *[The Arsenio Hall Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Arsenio_Hall_Show "The Arsenio Hall Show")*, Shakur said he was hurt that "a woman would accuse me of taking something from her", as he had been raised in a female household and surrounded by women his whole life.

On December 1, 1994, Shakur was acquitted of three counts of sodomy and the associated gun charges, but convicted of two counts of first-degree sexual abuse for "forcibly touching the woman's buttocks" in his hotel room. Jurors have said the lack of evidence stymied a sodomy conviction. Shakur's lawyer characterized the sentence as "out of line" with the groping conviction and the setting of bail at $3 million as "inhumane". Shakur's accuser later filed a civil suit against Shakur seeking $10 million for punitive damages which was subsequently [settled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settlement_(litigation) "Settlement (litigation)").

After Shakur had been convicted of sexual abuse, Jacques Agnant's case was separated and closed via misdemeanor plea without incarceration. [A. J. Benza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._J._Benza "A. J. Benza") reported in *[New York Daily News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Daily_News "New York Daily News")* Shakur's new disdain for Agnant who Shakur theorized had set him up with the case. Shakur reportedly believed his accuser was connected to and had sexual relations with Agnant and [James "Henchman" Rosemond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Rosemond "James Rosemond"), whom he considered to be behind the 1994 Quad Studios shooting.

Shakur was unable to post the $3 million bond to keep himself free until sentencing so he surrendered himself to authorities at the [Bellevue Hospital Jail Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellevue_Hospital "Bellevue Hospital") in New York City on December 23, 1994. At the time, he was still recovering from injuries he received on November 30, when he was shot five times and robbed at Quad Studios. In January 1995, Shakur was moved to the North Infirmary Command (NIC) on [Rikers Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rikers_Island "Rikers Island") in [the Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bronx "The Bronx"). On February 7, 1995, he was sentenced to 18 months to 4+1⁄2 years in prison by a judge who decried "an act of brutal violence against a helpless woman".

In March 1995, Shakur was transferred to [Clinton Correctional Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_Correctional_Facility "Clinton Correctional Facility") in [Dannemora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dannemora,_New_York "Dannemora, New York"). While imprisoned, he began reading again, which he had been unable to do as his career progressed due to his marijuana and alcohol habits. Works such as *[The Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prince "The Prince")* by Italian philosopher [Niccolò Machiavelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niccol%C3%B2_Machiavelli "Niccolò Machiavelli") and *[The Art of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Art_of_War "The Art of War")* by Chinese military strategist [Sun Tzu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Tzu "Sun Tzu") sparked Shakur's interest in [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy "Philosophy"), [philosophy of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_war "Philosophy of war") and [military strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_strategy "Military strategy"). On April 29, 1995, Shakur married his girlfriend Keisha Morris; the marriage was later annulled. While in prison, Shakur exchanged letters with celebrities such as [Jim Carrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Carrey "Jim Carrey") and [Tony Danza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Danza "Tony Danza") among others. He was also visited by [Al Sharpton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Sharpton "Al Sharpton"), who helped Shakur get released from [solitary confinement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solitary_confinement "Solitary confinement").

By October 1995, pending judicial appeal, Shakur was incarcerated in New York. On October 12, he bonded out of the maximum security [Dannemora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dannemora_(town),_New_York "Dannemora (town), New York") Clinton Correctional Facility in the process of appealing his conviction, once [Suge Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suge_Knight "Suge Knight"), CEO of Death Row Records, arranged for the posting of his $1.4 million bond.

### 1994 Quad Studios shooting

On November 30, 1994, while in New York recording verses for a mixtape of Ron G, Shakur was repeatedly distracted by his beeper. Music manager [James "Jimmy Henchman" Rosemond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Henchman "Jimmy Henchman") reportedly offered Shakur $7,000 to stop by Quad Studios, in Times Square, that night to record a verse for his client [Little Shawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawn_Pen "Shawn Pen"). Shakur was unsure, but agreed to the session as he needed the cash to offset legal costs. He arrived with [Stretch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stretch_(rapper) "Stretch (rapper)") and one or two others. In the lobby, three men robbed and beat him at gunpoint; Shakur resisted and was [pistol-whipped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pistol-whipping "Pistol-whipping") and shot five times. Shakur speculated that the shooting had been a set-up.

[Against medical advice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Against_medical_advice "Against medical advice"), Shakur checked out of [Metropolitan Hospital Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Hospital_Center "Metropolitan Hospital Center") a few hours after surgery and secretly went to the house of the actress [Jasmine Guy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasmine_Guy "Jasmine Guy") to recuperate. The next day, Shakur arrived at a Manhattan courthouse bandaged in a wheelchair to receive the jury's verdict for his sexual abuse case. Shakur spent the next few weeks being cared for by his mother and a private doctor at Guy's home. The [Fruit of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_of_Islam "Fruit of Islam") and former members of the [Black Panther Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Panther_Party "Black Panther Party") stood guard to protect him.

### Setup accusations involving the Notorious B.I.G.

In a 1995 interview with *[Vibe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vibe_(magazine) "Vibe (magazine)")*, Shakur accused [Sean Combs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Combs "Sean Combs"), [Jimmy Henchman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Rosemond "James Rosemond"), and the Notorious B.I.G. – who were at Quad Studios at the time – among others, of setting up or being privy to the November 1994 robbery and shooting. The accusations were significant to the [East Coast–West Coast hip-hop rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast%E2%80%93West_Coast_hip-hop_rivalry "East Coast–West Coast hip-hop rivalry"); in 1995, months after the robbery, Combs and B.I.G. released the track "[Who Shot Ya?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who_Shot_Ya%3F "Who Shot Ya?")", which Shakur took as a mockery of his shooting and thought they could be responsible, so he released a [diss song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diss_song "Diss song"), "[Hit 'Em Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hit_%27Em_Up "Hit 'Em Up")", in which he targeted B.I.G., Combs, [their record label](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Boy_Records "Bad Boy Records"), [Junior M.A.F.I.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_M.A.F.I.A. "Junior M.A.F.I.A."), and at the end of "Hit 'Em Up", he mentions rivals [Mobb Deep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobb_Deep "Mobb Deep") and [Chino XL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chino_XL "Chino XL").

In March 2008, [Chuck Philips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Philips "Chuck Philips"), in the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")*, reported on the 1994 ambush and shooting. The newspaper later retracted the article since it relied partially on FBI documents later discovered forged, supplied by a man convicted of fraud. In June 2011, convicted murderer Dexter Isaac, incarcerated in Brooklyn, issued a confession that he had been one of the gunmen who had robbed and shot Shakur at Henchman's order.

### Tupac Records copyright lawsuit

[Amaru Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amaru_Entertainment "Amaru Entertainment"), founded by Shakur's mother, announced that they would sue Baby Vox and their agency to stop the use of Shakur's music and trademarks without a license agreement. In response, Baby Vox appealed against the accusation, stating that "the sampling of Shakur's music was done legally after receiving an offer from a company at Bungaroo Music, the mecca of American hip-hop." They further explained that Amaru does not hold all of Shakur copyrights, so there is no legal issue, and that Shakur's copyrights are shared by several labels including Tupac State and [Interscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interscope_Records "Interscope Records"), in addition to Amaru. They also revealed that they released their last album after purchasing the rights to use music, portraits, and video for the Asian region from [Death Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_Row_Records "Death Row Records") and DTTG last November. Ultimately, the incident was concluded as a mere misunderstanding after it was confirmed that they had legally purchased the music.

### Other criminal or civil cases

#### 1991 Oakland Police Department lawsuit

On October 17, 1991, two [Oakland Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland_Police_Department "Oakland Police Department") officers stopped Shakur for [jaywalking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaywalking "Jaywalking") at a downtown intersection. According to Shakur, officers Alex Boyovic and Kevin Rogers asked him for his ID and pressed him about his name before choking him, throwing him to the ground and slamming his head on the concrete. Shakur filed a $10 million lawsuit against the officers for [police brutality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_brutality "Police brutality"). The case was [settled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settlement_(litigation) "Settlement (litigation)") for about $43,000.

#### Misdemeanor assault convictions

On April 5, 1993, charged with felonious assault, Shakur allegedly threw a microphone and swung a baseball bat at rapper Chauncey Wynn, of the group M.A.D., at a concert at [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University "Michigan State University"). Shakur claimed the bat was a part of his show, that he never swung it, and that there was no criminal intent. Nonetheless, on September 14, 1994, Shakur pleaded guilty to a [misdemeanor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misdemeanor "Misdemeanor") and was sentenced to 30 days in jail, twenty of them suspended on condition that he complete 35 hours of community service.

Slated to star as Sharif in the 1993 [Hughes Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hughes_Brothers "Hughes Brothers")' film *[Menace II Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menace_II_Society "Menace II Society")*, Shakur was replaced by actor Vonte Sweet after allegedly assaulting one of the film's directors, Allen Hughes. In early 1994, Shakur served 15 days in jail after being found guilty of the assault. The prosecution's evidence included a *[Yo! MTV Raps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yo!_MTV_Raps "Yo! MTV Raps")* interview in which Shakur boasted that he had "beat up the director of *Menace II Society*".

#### Concealed weapon case

In 1994, Shakur was arrested in Los Angeles, when he was stopped by police on suspicion of speeding. Police found a semiautomatic pistol in the car, a felony offense because of a prior conviction in 1993 in Los Angeles for carrying a concealed firearm. On April 4, 1996, Shakur was sentenced to 120 days in jail for violating his release terms and failing to appear for a road cleanup job, but was allowed to remain free awaiting appeal. On June 7, his sentence was deferred via appeals pending in other cases.

#### 1995 wrongful death suit

On August 22, 1992, in Marin City, Shakur performed outdoors at a festival. For about an hour after the performance, he signed autographs and posed for photos. A conflict broke out and Shakur allegedly drew a legally carried [Colt Mustang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colt_Mustang "Colt Mustang") but dropped it on the ground. Shakur claimed that someone with him then picked it up when it accidentally discharged.

About 100 yards (90 meters) away in a schoolyard, Qa'id Walker-Teal, a boy aged 6 on his bicycle, was fatally shot in the forehead. Police matched the bullet to a [.38-caliber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.38_caliber ".38 caliber") pistol registered to Shakur. His stepbrother [Maurice Harding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mopreme_Shakur "Mopreme Shakur") was arrested in suspicion of having fired the gun, but no charges were filed. Lack of witnesses stymied prosecution. In 1995, Qa'id's mother filed a [wrongful death suit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrongful_death_claim "Wrongful death claim") against Shakur, which was settled for about $300,000 to $500,000.

#### C. Delores Tucker lawsuit

In 1997, Civil rights activist and fierce rap critic [C. Delores Tucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._Delores_Tucker "C. Delores Tucker") sued Shakur's estate in federal court, claiming that lyrics in "How Do U Want It" and "Wonda Why They Call U Bitch" inflicted emotional distress, were slanderous, and invaded her privacy. The case was later dismissed.

## Murder and aftermath

On the night of September 7, 1996, Shakur was in [Paradise, Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise,_Nevada "Paradise, Nevada"), to attend the [Bruce Seldon vs. Mike Tyson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Seldon_vs._Mike_Tyson "Bruce Seldon vs. Mike Tyson") boxing match with Suge Knight at the [MGM Grand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MGM_Grand_Las_Vegas "MGM Grand Las Vegas"). Afterward in the lobby one of Knight's associates spotted [Orlando Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Anderson "Orlando Anderson"), a [South Side Compton Crip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Side_Compton_Crips "South Side Compton Crips"), and told Shakur he had tried to rob them earlier that year. The hotel's [surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed-circuit_television "Closed-circuit television") footage shows the ensuing assault on Anderson. Shakur soon stopped by his hotel room and then headed with Knight to his Death Row nightclub, Club 662, in a black [BMW 750iL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_E38 "BMW E38") sedan, part of a larger convoy.

At about 11 p.m. on [Las Vegas Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Boulevard "Las Vegas Boulevard"), bicycle-mounted police stopped the car for its loud music and lack of [license plates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plate "Vehicle registration plate"). The plates were found in the trunk, and the car was released without a ticket. At about 11:15 p.m. at a stop light, a white, four-door, late-model [Cadillac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadillac "Cadillac") sedan pulled up to the passenger side and an occupant rapidly fired into the car. Shakur was struck four times: once in the arm, once in the thigh, and twice in the chest with one bullet entering his right lung. Shards hit Knight's head. Frank Alexander, Shakur's bodyguard, was not in the car at the time. He would say he had been tasked to drive the car of Shakur's girlfriend, [Kidada Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidada_Jones "Kidada Jones").

Shakur was taken to the [University Medical Center of Southern Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Medical_Center_of_Southern_Nevada "University Medical Center of Southern Nevada") where he was heavily sedated and put on life support. In the [intensive-care unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intensive-care_unit "Intensive-care unit") on the afternoon of September 13, 1996, Shakur died from [internal bleeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_bleeding "Internal bleeding"). He was pronounced dead at 4:03 p.m. The official causes of death are [respiratory failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_failure "Respiratory failure") and [cardiopulmonary arrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiopulmonary_arrest "Cardiopulmonary arrest") associated with multiple gunshot wounds. Shakur's body was [cremated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremation "Cremation") the next day. Members of the [Outlawz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlawz "Outlawz"), recalling a line in his song "Black Jesus", (although uncertain of the artist's attempt at a literal meaning chose to interpret the request seriously) smoked some of his body's ashes after mixing them with [marijuana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis_(drug) "Cannabis (drug)").

In 2002, investigative journalist [Chuck Philips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Philips "Chuck Philips"), after a year of work, reported in the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")* that Anderson, a Southside Compton Crip, having been attacked by Suge and Shakur's entourage at the MGM Hotel after the boxing match, had fired the fatal gunshots, but that [Las Vegas Metropolitan Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Metropolitan_Police "Las Vegas Metropolitan Police") had interviewed him only once, briefly, before his death in an unrelated shooting. Philips's 2002 article also alleges the involvement of [Christopher "Notorious B.I.G." Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Notorious_B.I.G. "The Notorious B.I.G.") and several within New York City's criminal underworld. Both Anderson and Wallace denied involvement, while Wallace offered a confirmed alibi.[*[unreliable source?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Reliable_sources "Wikipedia:Reliable sources")*] Music journalist John Leland, in *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")*, called the evidence "inconclusive".

In 2011, via the [Freedom of Information Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_Information_Act_(United_States) "Freedom of Information Act (United States)"), the [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Investigation "Federal Bureau of Investigation") released documents related to its investigation which described an [extortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extortion "Extortion") scheme by the [Jewish Defense League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Defense_League "Jewish Defense League") (classified as "a [right wing terrorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-wing_terrorist "Right-wing terrorist") group" by the FBI) that included making [death threats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_threats "Death threats") against Shakur and other rappers, but did not indicate a direct connection to his murder.

### Murder trial

On July 18, 2023, the Las Vegas Metropolitan Police Department executed a [search warrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_warrant "Search warrant") in connection with Shakur's murder. On September 29, 2023, the [AP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_Press "Associated Press") reported that Las Vegas Metropolitan Police had arrested a suspect, [Duane "Keefe D" Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duane_Davis_(gangster) "Duane Davis (gangster)"), in Shakur's murder. Police had two months previously served a search warrant at his wife's home in the Las Vegas suburb of [Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henderson,_Nevada "Henderson, Nevada"). Davis pleaded not guilty on November 2, 2023, in [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas "Las Vegas"). On August 10, 2026, Davis's trial for Shakur's murder began. By this point in time, Davis was incarcerated at Nevada's [High Desert State Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Desert_State_Prison_(Nevada) "High Desert State Prison (Nevada)") serving a 16 to 40-month prison sentence for a separate jailhouse fight-related conviction he received in April 2025. On August 31, one week shy of the 30th anniversary of the shooting, the jury, after nearly three hours of deliberations, convicted Davis of [first-degree murder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-degree_murder "First-degree murder"). Davis was sentenced on October 13, to 40 years to life in prison.

## Artistry

### Musical style

Shakur's music and philosophical outlook were deeply influenced by a wide range of American, [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African-American_culture "African-American culture"), and global influences, including the [Black Panther Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Panther_Party "Black Panther Party"), [black nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_nationalism "Black nationalism"), [egalitarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egalitarianism "Egalitarianism"), and the concept of [liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty "Liberty"). Moreover, Shakur's artistic sensibilities were enriched by his passion for theater and admiration for the works of [William Shakespeare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Shakespeare "William Shakespeare"). Notably, he honed his theatrical skills as a student at the [Baltimore School for the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_School_for_the_Arts "Baltimore School for the Arts"), where he delved into the psychological complexities inherent in inter-gang warfare and inter-cultural conflicts, reflecting themes explored in Shakespearean dramas.

*[2Pacalypse Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2Pacalypse_Now "2Pacalypse Now")* (1991), showcased his socially conscious perspective. Through powerful tracks like "[Brenda's Got a Baby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenda%27s_Got_a_Baby "Brenda's Got a Baby")", "[Trapped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapped_(Tupac_Shakur_song) "Trapped (Tupac Shakur song)")", and "Part Time Mutha", Shakur addressed social injustice, poverty, and police brutality. In doing so, he contributed to the ongoing success of rap groups such as [Boogie Down Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boogie_Down_Productions "Boogie Down Productions"), [Public Enemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Enemy "Public Enemy"), [X-Clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_Clan "X Clan"), and [Grandmaster Flash and the Furious Five](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grandmaster_Flash_and_the_Furious_Five "Grandmaster Flash and the Furious Five"), while establishing himself as one of the pioneering socially conscious rappers from the West Coast.

Continuing his focus on the social challenges faced by African American people, Shakur's second album featured songs like "The Streetz R Deathrow" and "Last Wordz". Simultaneously, he showcased his compassionate side with the empowering anthem "[Keep Ya Head Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keep_Ya_Head_Up "Keep Ya Head Up")", and his legendary intensity with the title track from the album *[Strictly 4 My N.I.G.G.A.Z...](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strictly_4_My_N.I.G.G.A.Z... "Strictly 4 My N.I.G.G.A.Z...")* Additionally, he paid homage to his former group [Digital Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Underground "Digital Underground") by including them on the playful track "[I Get Around](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Get_Around_(Tupac_Shakur_song) "I Get Around (Tupac Shakur song)")". Throughout his career, Shakur's subsequent albums reflected a growing assertiveness in his approach.

Shakur's body of work encompassed contrasting themes, including [social inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_inequality "Social inequality"), [injustice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injustice "Injustice"), compassion, playfulness, and [hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hope "Hope"). These elements continued to shape his artistry, exemplified by his explosive 1995 album *[Me Against the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Me_Against_the_World "Me Against the World")*. The release of *All Eyez on Me* in 1996 further solidified his reputation, with tracks like "[Ambitionz az a Ridah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambitionz_az_a_Ridah "Ambitionz az a Ridah")", "[I Ain't Mad at Cha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Ain%27t_Mad_at_Cha "I Ain't Mad at Cha")", "[California Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Love "California Love")", "Life Goes On", and "Picture Me Rollin'" being hailed as classics by critics. Shakur described *All Eyez on Me* as a celebration of life, and the album achieved both critical acclaim and commercial success. According to [Eminem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eminem "Eminem"), Tupac is the greatest songwriter of all time. [Nas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nas "Nas") said in 2002: "I put Tupac beyond [Shakespeare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakespeare "Shakespeare")."

### Vocal style

In the documentary *Tupac Shakur: Thug Angel*, [Greg "Shock G" Jacobs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_G "Shock G"), one of Shakur's early producers, discusses how rappers use different bodily areas to project their voices. According to him, "Slick Rick rhymed from the nasal palate, Nas from the back of his throat, and Pac from the pit of his stomach, which is where his power came from." Shakur's influences stemmed from powerful orators like [Martin Luther King Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther_King_Jr. "Martin Luther King Jr.") and [Malcolm X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_X "Malcolm X"). Despite not being physically imposing, Shakur's voice carried immense weight and power, reminiscent of these influential speakers.

Shakur was also known for his technique of stacking or layering vocals, adding depth and rawness to his voice. This approach, demonstrated notably on tracks like "[Dear Mama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dear_Mama "Dear Mama")" from his 1995 album *[Me Against the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Me_Against_the_World "Me Against the World")*, involves overlaying multiple vocal lines to highlight rhythms and emphasize words and phrases. Mastering this technique requires precision to maintain flow and clarity, as heard in the lyrics "and even though I act crazy/I gotta thank the Lord that you made me", where Shakur's voice transitions from full to husky, underscoring the emotional depth of the lyrics. Despite its difficulty, Shakur's background in jazz, poetry, and theater endowed him with exceptional rhythm control, enabling him to layer vocals seamlessly while preserving cohesion and flow.

## Poetry and literary influences

Before and during his music career, Shakur wrote dozens of poems. According to *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")* writer Andy Greene, Shakur was "a poet and activist who became one of his era's most revolutionary voices." He had a strong passion for theater and greatly admired William Shakespeare. Years after Shakur's death, rapper [Nas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nas "Nas") remarked, "I put Tupac beyond Shakespeare."

In 1993, Shakur portrayed Lucky in the film *Poetic Justice* alongside Janet Jackson. The poems featured in the film were written by poet and civil rights activist [Maya Angelou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_Angelou "Maya Angelou").

Many of Shakur's best-known poems were later collected in the posthumous poetry book *[The Rose That Grew from Concrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rose_That_Grew_from_Concrete_(poetry_collection) "The Rose That Grew from Concrete (poetry collection)")*, released in 1999. The collection includes poems such as "Can U C the Pride in the Panther", "If I Fail", "Family Tree", "The Rose That Grew from Concrete", and "Jada". In 2021, Jada Pinkett Smith commemorated Shakur's 50th birthday by sharing a previously unreleased poem titled "Lost Soulz" on Instagram.

In April 2022, a collection of handwritten poems Shakur wrote at age 11 was offered for sale for US$300,000, but ultimately sold for $90,000. The poems were dedicated to [Jamal Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_Joseph "Jamal Joseph") and three other members of the Black Panther Party while they were incarcerated at [Leavenworth Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Correctional_Institution,_Leavenworth "Federal Correctional Institution, Leavenworth"). Even at a young age, Shakur's writing explored themes of [Black liberation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_power_movement "Black power movement"), [mass incarceration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incarceration_in_the_United_States "Incarceration in the United States"), [race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_(human_categorization) "Race (human categorization)"), and [masculinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masculinity "Masculinity"). The collection also included a self-portrait depicting Shakur asleep with a pen in hand, dreaming of the Panthers being freed from prison, signed with a heart and the phrase "Tupac Shakur, Future Freedom Fighter."

## Legacy and remembrance

Shakur is considered one of the greatest and most influential rappers of all time. He was listed as one of the greatest artists of all time by *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone%27s_100_Greatest_Artists_of_All_Time "Rolling Stone's 100 Greatest Artists of All Time")*. He is widely credited as an important figure in [hip-hop culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip-hop_culture "Hip-hop culture"), and his prominence in [pop culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_culture "Pop culture") in general has been noted. [Dotdash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dotdash "Dotdash"), formerly About.com, while ranking him fifth among the greatest rappers, nonetheless notes, "Tupac Shakur is the most influential hip-hop artist of all time. Even in death, 2Pac remains a transcendental rap figure." Yet to some, he was a "father figure" who, said rapper [YG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YG_(rapper) "YG (rapper)"), "makes you want to be better—at every level." In 2023, [Billboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200") ranked Tupac at number 4 among the top 50 rappers of all time.

[AllMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllMusic "AllMusic")'s [Stephen Thomas Erlewine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Thomas_Erlewine "Stephen Thomas Erlewine") described Shakur as "the unlikely martyr of [gangsta rap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangsta_rap "Gangsta rap")", with Shakur paying the ultimate price of a criminal lifestyle. Shakur was described as one of the top two American rappers in the 1990s, along with [Snoop Dogg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snoop_Dogg "Snoop Dogg"). The online rap magazine *[AllHipHop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllHipHop "AllHipHop")* held a 2007 roundtable at which New York rappers [Cormega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cormega "Cormega"), citing tour experience with New York rap duo [Mobb Deep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobb_Deep "Mobb Deep"), commented that B.I.G. ran New York, but Shakur ran America. Shakur emerged as a celebrated artist, earning recognition for his astonishingly prolific output and unwavering commitment to his craft. According to [Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone") writer Andy Green: "He was also a poet and activist who became one of his era's most revolutionary voices."

In 2017, American rapper [Snoop Dogg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snoop_Dogg "Snoop Dogg") called Tupac "the greatest rapper of all time" during his [Rock & Roll Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame "Rock and Roll Hall of Fame") tribute. In 2021, [Saweetie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saweetie "Saweetie") told [Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_Networks "Complex Networks") that Tupac was "the greatest rapper that ever lived".

According to British writer Rob Marriott, he deemed the act of tying a [bandana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerchief "Kerchief") into rabbit ears as one of the most distinctive and instantly recognizable style choices in the world of [hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip-hop "Hip-hop"). Regarded as a [sex symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_symbol "Sex symbol"), his unique style helped shape the fashion landscape of the 1990s and continues to influence artists and fashion enthusiasts to this day.

In 2010, writing *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")* magazine's entry on Shakur at No. 86 among the "100 greatest artists", New York rapper [50 Cent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/50_Cent "50 Cent") appraised:

> Every rapper who grew up in the Nineties owes something to Tupac. People either try to emulate him in some way, or they go in a different direction because they didn't like what he did. But whatever you think of him, he definitely developed his own style: He didn't sound like anyone who came before him.

According to music journalist [Chuck Philips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Philips "Chuck Philips"), Shakur "had helped elevate rap from a crude street fad to a complex art form, setting the stage for the current global hip-hop phenomenon." Philips writes, "The slaying silenced one of modern music's most eloquent voices—a ghetto poet whose tales of urban alienation captivated young people of all races and backgrounds." Via numerous fans perceiving him, despite his questionable conduct, as a [martyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyr "Martyr"), "the downsizing of martyrdom cheapens its use", academic [Michael Eric Dyson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Eric_Dyson "Michael Eric Dyson") concedes. But Dyson adds, "Some, or even most, of that criticism can be conceded without doing damage to Tupac's martyrdom in the eyes of those disappointed by more traditional martyrs."

In 2014, [BET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET "BET") explained that "his confounding mixture of ladies' man, thug, revolutionary and poet has forever altered our perception of what a rapper should look like, sound like and act like. In [50 Cent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/50_Cent "50 Cent"), [Ja Rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ja_Rule "Ja Rule"), [Lil Wayne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lil_Wayne "Lil Wayne"), newcomers like [Freddie Gibbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freddie_Gibbs "Freddie Gibbs") and even his friend-turned-rival B.I.G., it's easy to see that Pac is the most copied MC of all time. There are murals bearing his likeness in New York, Brazil, Sierra Leone, Bulgaria and countless other places; he even has statues in Atlanta and Germany. Quite simply, no other rapper has captured the world's attention the way Tupac did and still does." More simply, his writings, published after his death, inspired rapper YG to return to school and get his GED. In 2020, former California Senator and former vice-president [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris "Kamala Harris") called Shakur the "best rapper alive", which she explained as being because "West Coast girls think 2Pac lives on". According to writer [Kevin Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Powell "Kevin Powell"): "He deserves to be put in the same category as [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan "Bob Dylan"), [Bob Marley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Marley "Bob Marley"), as [John Lennon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lennon "John Lennon"), in terms of his global impact." Tupac is regarded as one of the most influential artists in music and popular culture in general and an icon of activism.

Palestinian rapper [Tamer Nafar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamer_Nafar "Tamer Nafar"), leader and a founding member of [DAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DAM_(band) "DAM (band)"), became passionate about hip-hop by listening to Tupac, saying, "The imagery in Shakur's videos was similar to our reality in [Lod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lod "Lod")."

### Final ash placement

In 2006, on the 10th anniversary of Tupac Shakur's death, his ashes were planned to be moved to [Soweto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soweto "Soweto"). Shakur's mother Afeni planned to transport them to the "birthplace of his ancestors" and conduct a memorial service. Afeni Shakur explained that Soweto had been selected due to its significance as the "birthplace of the South African struggle for [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy "Democracy") and against [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid "Apartheid")." The [City of Johannesburg Metropolitan Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Johannesburg_Metropolitan_Municipality "City of Johannesburg Metropolitan Municipality") donated a five-acre plot of undeveloped land in the [Zola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zola,_South_Africa "Zola, South Africa") area of Soweto to build a memorial honoring Shakur. A portion of the land was designated to be transformed into a park for the benefit of local children as well as aimed at promoting [environmental education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_education "Environmental education"), pathways, orphanages, bridges, skateboard ramps and a golf range while plans also included the construction of an [amphitheater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphitheatre "Amphitheatre") and a museum showcasing South African music and arts. The project was funded by Johannesburg city authorities and the Tupac Amaru Shakur Foundation. A week before the event, Afeni Shakur postponed the trip. The majority of his ashes were buried in his mother's native [Lumberton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumberton,_North_Carolina "Lumberton, North Carolina"), North Carolina.

### Tupac Amaru Shakur Foundation

In 1997, Shakur's mother founded the Shakur Family Foundation. Later renamed the Tupac Amaru Shakur Foundation, or TASF, it launched with a stated mission to "provide training and support for students who aspire to enhance their creative talents." The TASF sponsors essay contests, charity events, a performing arts day camp for teenagers, and undergraduate scholarships. In June 2005, the TASF opened the [Tupac Amaru Shakur Center for the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupac_Amaru_Shakur_Center_for_the_Arts "Tupac Amaru Shakur Center for the Arts"), or TASCA, in [Stone Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Mountain,_Georgia "Stone Mountain, Georgia"), Georgia. It closed in 2015.

### Tupac memorial pole in Las Vegas

The Tupac memorial pole in [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas "Las Vegas") is an unofficial memorial at the site where Shakur was murdered. Fans frequently leave messages and writings on the pole to remember him. Some visitors coat the pole with graffiti to show their appreciation for Shakur, and some well-wishers have left offerings at the base of the utility pole. Fans often lay flowers near the pole on his birthday and the day he died. The memorial is located at the intersection of East [Flamingo Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flamingo_Road_(Las_Vegas) "Flamingo Road (Las Vegas)") and Koval Lane.

### Academic appraisal

In 1997, the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), offered a course led by a student titled "History 98: Poetry and History of Tupac Shakur". In April 2003, [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") cosponsored the symposium "All Eyez on Me: Tupac Shakur and the Search for the Modern Folk Hero", where Shakur's influence as both an artist and an activist was analyzed.
The papers presented cover his ranging influence from entertainment to sociology. Calling him a "Thug Nigga Intellectual", an "organic intellectual", English scholar [Mark Anthony Neal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Anthony_Neal "Mark Anthony Neal") assessed his death as leaving a "leadership void amongst hip-hop artists", as this "walking contradiction" helps, Neal explained, "make being an intellectual accessible to ordinary people."

Tracing Shakur's [mythical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythical "Mythical") status, Murray Forman discussed him as "O.G.", or "Ostensibly Gone", with fans, using digital mediums, "resurrecting Tupac as an ethereal life force." Music scholar Emmett Price, calling him a "Black folk hero", traced his persona to Black American folklore's [tricksters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trickster "Trickster"), which, after [abolition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolition_of_slavery "Abolition of slavery"), evolved into the urban "bad-man". Yet in Shakur's "terrible sense of urgency", Price identified instead a quest to "unify mind, body, and spirit." According to Price, Tupac had surpassed the legacies of [John Coltrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Coltrane "John Coltrane") and [Mahalia Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahalia_Jackson "Mahalia Jackson") within the tradition of black music.

In 2012, the Norwegian [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo "University of Oslo") organized the course: "Tupac, hiphop og kulturhistorie (Tupac, hip-hop and cultural history)." As Knut Aukrust, a Norwegian professor and scholar of cultural studies at the University of Oslo, puts it: "Tupac Amaru Shakur (1971–1996) is one of the most famous and controversial representatives of hip-hop culture. He has become an icon with almost saint-like status far beyond his fan base. References to him and his message appear all over the world, from Barack Obama's slogan about "changes," to Palestinians and Israelis longing for peace in the Middle East, to the people of Groruddalen, who want their experiences to be taken seriously. The course highlights how a single person can fit into a wider network of cultural models and how a local storytelling tradition has become an international cultural phenomenon."

Jeffrey Ogbonna Green Ogbar, professor of History and Popular Music at the [University of Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Connecticut "University of Connecticut"), described Shakur as "one of the most iconic and influential music artists of the 20th century", and also a "politically conscious activist voice for Black America."

### Multimedia releases

In 2005, Death Row released on DVD, *[Tupac: Live at the House of Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupac:_Live_at_the_House_of_Blues "Tupac: Live at the House of Blues")*, his final recorded live performance, an event on July 4, 1996. In August 2006, *[Tupac Shakur Legacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupac_Shakur_Legacy "Tupac Shakur Legacy")*, an "interactive biography" by [Jamal Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_Joseph_(writer) "Jamal Joseph (writer)"), arrived with previously unpublished family photographs, intimate stories, and over 20 detachable copies of his handwritten song lyrics, contracts, scripts, poetry, and other papers. In 2006, the Shakur album *[Pac's Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pac%27s_Life "Pac's Life")* was released and, like the previous, was among the recording industry's most popular releases. In 2008, his estate made about $15 million.

On April 15, 2012, at the [Coachella Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella_Music_Festival "Coachella Music Festival"), rappers Snoop Dogg and [Dr. Dre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Dre "Dr. Dre") joined a Shakur "hologram" (Although the media referred to the technology as a hologram, technically it was a projection created with the [Musion Eyeliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musion_Eyeliner "Musion Eyeliner")), and, as a partly virtual trio, performed the Shakur songs "[Hail Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hail_Mary_(2Pac_song) "Hail Mary (2Pac song)")" and "[2 of Amerikaz Most Wanted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_of_Amerikaz_Most_Wanted "2 of Amerikaz Most Wanted")". There were talks of a tour, but Dre refused. Meanwhile, the *Greatest Hits* album, released in 1998, and which in 2000 had left the pop albums chart, the [Billboard 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200"), returned to the chart and reached No. 129, while also other Shakur albums and singles drew sales gains.

### Books

In 1999, Tupac's posthumous book was released called *[The Rose That Grew from Concrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rose_That_Grew_from_Concrete_(poetry_collection) "The Rose That Grew from Concrete (poetry collection)")* featuring 72 deeply personal, handwritten poems written when Tupac was 19. The poetry collection is used in schools, ranging from middle school to high school English curricula.

### Film and stage

The documentary film *[Tupac: Resurrection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupac:_Resurrection "Tupac: Resurrection")* was released in November 2003. It was nominated for [Best Documentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Documentary_Feature "Academy Award for Documentary Feature") at the [2005 Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/77th_Academy_Awards "77th Academy Awards").

In 2014, the play *Holler If Ya Hear Me*, based on Shakur's lyrics, played on Broadway, but, among Broadway's worst-selling musicals in recent years, ran only six weeks. In development since 2013, a Shakur biopic, *[All Eyez on Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Eyez_on_Me_(film) "All Eyez on Me (film)")*, began filming in [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta "Atlanta") in December 2015. It was released on June 16, 2017, on Shakur's 46th birthday, albeit to generally negative reviews.

In August 2019, a [docuseries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_documentary "Television documentary") directed by [Allen Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hughes_Brothers "Hughes Brothers"), *Outlaw: The Saga of Afeni and Tupac Shakur*, was announced.

### Video games

In June 2026, it was announced that Shakur's likeness will be featured in the video game *[Stranger Than Heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stranger_Than_Heaven "Stranger Than Heaven")* (2027), developed by [RGG Studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryu_Ga_Gotoku_Studio "Ryu Ga Gotoku Studio"). Shakur's character, Amaru, was developed with supervision from his estate.

### Awards and honors

* In 2002, Shakur was inducted into the Hip-Hop Hall of Fame. In 2004, Shakur was among the honorees at the first [Hip Hop Honors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip_Hop_Honors "Hip Hop Honors").
* In 2006, Shakur's close friend and classmate Jada Pinkett Smith donated $1 million to their high school alma mater, the Baltimore School for the Arts, and named the new theater in his honor. In 2021, Pinkett Smith honored Shakur's 50th birthday by releasing a never before seen poem she had received from him.
* In 2009, drawing praise, the Vatican added "[Changes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changes_(Tupac_Shakur_song) "Changes (Tupac Shakur song)")", a [1998 posthumous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greatest_Hits_(Tupac_Shakur_album) "Greatest Hits (Tupac Shakur album)") track, to its online playlist. On June 23, 2010, the [Library of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress "Library of Congress") added "[Dear Mama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dear_Mama "Dear Mama")" to the [National Recording Registry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Recording_Registry "National Recording Registry"), the third rap song.
* In 2015, the [Grammy Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Museum_at_L.A._Live "Grammy Museum at L.A. Live") opened an exhibition dedicated to Shakur. In his first year of eligibility, Shakur was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame on April 7, 2017.
* In January 2022, the exhibition *Tupac Shakur: Wake Me When I'm Free* opened at The Canvas at [L.A. Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L.A._Live "L.A. Live") in Los Angeles.

* On May 16, 2023, [Oakland City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland_City_Council "Oakland City Council") voted to name the section of [MacArthur Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Boulevard "MacArthur Boulevard") between Grand Avenue and Van Buren Avenue "Tupac Shakur Way".
* On June 7, 2023, Shakur received a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame "Hollywood Walk of Fame"). His half-sister, Sekyiwa "Set" Shakur, accepted the award in his honor.
* On May 8, 2026, a part of Greenmount Avenue in [Pen Lucy, Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pen_Lucy,_Baltimore "Pen Lucy, Baltimore") was named "Tupac Shakur Way". Mayor [Brandon Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandon_Scott "Brandon Scott") said "Shakur served as an inspiration to him."

* 2003: MTV's viewers voted Shakur the greatest MC.

* 2006: MTV staff placed him second on its list of "The Greatest MCs Of All Time".
* 2012: *[The Source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Source_(magazine) "The Source (magazine)")* magazine ranked him No. 5 among "The Top 50 Lyricists".
* 2007: The [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame "Rock and Roll Hall of Fame") placed *All Eyez on Me* at No. 90 and *Me Against the World* at No. 170.
* 2010: *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")* magazine placed Shakur at No. 86 among the "100 Greatest Artists".
* 2020: *All Eyez on Me* was ranked No. 436 on *Rolling Stone*'s list of the ["500 Greatest Albums Of All Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone%27s_500_Greatest_Albums_of_All_Time "Rolling Stone's 500 Greatest Albums of All Time")".
* 2023: *Billboard* ranked Shakur at number 4 of the top 50 rappers.
