# Ancient history

**Ancient history** is a [time period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_period "Time period") from the [beginning of writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_writing "History of writing") and recorded [human history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_history "Human history") through [late antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_antiquity "Late antiquity"). The span of [recorded history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recorded_history "Recorded history") is roughly 5,000 years, beginning with the development of [Sumerian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumerian_language "Sumerian language") [cuneiform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuneiform "Cuneiform") script. Ancient history covers all continents inhabited by humans in the period 3000 BC – AD 500.

The [three-age system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-age_system "Three-age system") periodises ancient history into the [Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age "Stone Age"), the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age "Bronze Age"), and the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age "Iron Age"), with recorded history usually considered to begin with the Bronze Age. The start and end of the three ages vary between world regions. In many regions, the Bronze Age is generally considered to begin a few centuries prior to 3000 BC, while the end of the Iron Age varies from the early first millennium BC in some regions to the late first millennium AD in others.

During the time period of ancient history, the [world population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_population "World population") was [exponentially increasing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponential_growth "Exponential growth") due to the [Neolithic Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_Revolution "Neolithic Revolution"), which was in full progress. In 10,000 BC, the world population stood at an estimated 2 million; it rose to 45 million by 3000 BC. By the Iron Age in 1000 BC, the population had risen to 72 million. By the end of the ancient period in AD 500, the world population is thought to have stood at 209 million. In 10,500 years, the world population increased by 100 times.

## Prehistory

Prehistory is the period before written history. Most of the knowledge of that period comes from the work of [archaeologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeologist "Archaeologist").

The [early human migrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_human_migration "Early human migration") in the [Lower Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Paleolithic "Lower Paleolithic") saw *[Homo erectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_erectus "Homo erectus")* spread across [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia "Eurasia") 1.8 million years ago. Evidence for the use of fire has been dated as early as 1.8 million years ago, a date which is contested, with generally accepted evidence for the controlled use of fire dating to 780,000 years ago. Actual use of hearths first appears 400,000 years ago. Dates for the emergence of *[Homo sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_sapiens "Homo sapiens")* (modern humans) range from 250,000 to 160,000 years ago, with the varying dates being based on [DNA studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_study "DNA study") and [fossils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil "Fossil"), respectively. Some 50,000 years ago, *Homo sapiens* migrated out of [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa "Africa"). They reached [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia") about 45,000 years ago, southwestern [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe "Europe") about the same time, southeastern Europe and [Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberia "Siberia") around 40,000 years ago, and [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan") about 30,000 years ago. Humans [migrated to the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_to_the_New_World "Migration to the New World") about 15,000 years ago.

Evidence for [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture "Agriculture") emerges in about 9000 BC in what is now eastern [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey "Turkey") and spread through the [Fertile Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertile_Crescent "Fertile Crescent"). The settlement at [Göbekli Tepe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6bekli_Tepe "Göbekli Tepe") began around 9500 BC and may have the world's oldest temple. The [Nile River Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile_River_Valley "Nile River Valley") has evidence of [sorghum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorghum "Sorghum") and [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet "Millet") cultivation starting around 8000 BC, and agricultural use of [yams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yam_(vegetable) "Yam (vegetable)") in Western Africa perhaps dates to the same time period. Cultivation of millet, [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice "Rice"), and [legumes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legume "Legume") began around 7000 BC in [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China"). [Taro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taro "Taro") cultivation in [New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Guinea "New Guinea") dates to about 7000 BC also, with [squash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cucurbita "Cucurbita") cultivation in [Mesoamerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica "Mesoamerica") perhaps sharing that date. [Animal domestication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_domestication "Animal domestication") began with the [domestication of dogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestication_of_dogs "Domestication of dogs"), which dates to at least 15,000 years ago, and perhaps even earlier. [Sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_domestic_sheep "History of the domestic sheep") and [goats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_of_domestic_goats "Evolution of domestic goats") were domesticated around 9000 BC in the Fertile Crescent, alongside the first evidence for agriculture. Other animals, such as [pigs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigs "Pigs") and [poultry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poultry "Poultry"), were later domesticated and used as food sources. [Cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle "Cattle") and [water buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_buffalo "Water buffalo") were domesticated around 7000 BC, and [horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestication_of_the_horse "Domestication of the horse"), [donkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donkey "Donkey"), and [camels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel "Camel") were domesticated by about 4000 BC. All of these animals were used not only for food, but to carry and pull people and loads, greatly increasing human ability to do work. The invention of the simple [plough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plough "Plough") by 6000 BC further increased agricultural efficiency.

Metal use in the form of hammered [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper "Copper") items predates the discovery of [smelting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smelting "Smelting") of [copper ores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_ore "Copper ore"), which happened around 6000 BC in western Asia and independently in eastern Asia before 2000 BC. [Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold "Gold") and [silver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver "Silver") use dates to between 6000 and 5000 BC. [Alloy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_alloy "Metal alloy") metallurgy began with [bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze "Bronze") in about 3500 BC in [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia "Mesopotamia") and was developed independently in China by 2000 BC. [Pottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pottery "Pottery") developed independently throughout the world, with fired pots appearing first among the [Jomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jomon "Jomon") of Japan and in West Africa at [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali "Mali"). Sometime between 5000 and 4000 BC, the [potter's wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potter%27s_wheel "Potter's wheel") was invented. By 3000 BC, the pottery wheel was adapted into [wheeled vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheeled_vehicle "Wheeled vehicle") which could be used to carry loads further and easier than with human or animal power alone.

[Writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writing "Writing") developed separately in five different locations in human history: Mesopotamia, Egypt, India, China, and Mesoamerica. By 3400 BC, "proto-literate" [cuneiform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuneiform "Cuneiform") spread in the Middle East. Egypt developed its own system of [hieroglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hieroglyph "Hieroglyph") by about 3200 BC. By 2800 BC, the [Indus Valley Civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation "Indus Valley Civilisation") had developed its [Indus script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_script "Indus script"), which remains undeciphered. [Chinese Characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_characters "Chinese characters") were independently developed in China during the [Shang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang_dynasty "Shang dynasty") in the form of the [Oracle Bone Script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle_bone_script "Oracle bone script") dating to the period 1600 to 1100 BC. Writing in Mesoamerica dates to 600 BC with the [Zapotec civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zapotec_civilization "Zapotec civilization").

## History by region

### West Asia

The ancient Near East is considered the [cradle of civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cradle_of_civilization "Cradle of civilization"). It was the first to practice intensive year-round agriculture; created one of the first [coherent writing systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_writing "History of writing"), invented the [potter's wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potter%27s_wheel "Potter's wheel") and then the vehicular [wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel "Wheel"), created the first [centralised governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralized_government "Centralized government"), [law codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_code "Law code") and empires, as well as displaying [social stratification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_stratification "Social stratification"), slavery, and organised warfare. It began the study of the stars and the sciences of astronomy and mathematics.

#### Mesopotamia

Mesopotamia is the site of some of the earliest known [civilisations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization "Civilization") in the world. Agricultural communities emerged in the area with the [Halaf culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halaf_culture "Halaf culture") around 8000 BC and continued to expand through the [Ubaid period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubaid_period "Ubaid period") around 6000 BC. Cities began in the [Uruk period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruk_period "Uruk period") (4000–3100 BC) and expanded during the [Jemdet Nasr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jemdet_Nasr_period "Jemdet Nasr period") (3100–2900 BC) and [Early Dynastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Dynastic_Period_(Mesopotamia) "Early Dynastic Period (Mesopotamia)") (2900–2350 BC) periods. The surplus of storable foodstuffs created by this economy allowed the population to settle in one place instead of migrating after crops and herds. It also allowed for a much greater population density, and in turn required an extensive labour force and division of labour. This organisation led to the necessity of record keeping and the development of writing.

[Babylonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonia "Babylonia") was an [Amorite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amorite "Amorite") state in lower Mesopotamia (modern southern [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq "Iraq")), with Babylon as its capital. Babylonia emerged when [Hammurabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammurabi "Hammurabi") created an empire out of the territories of the former kingdoms of [Sumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumer "Sumer") and [Akkad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_Empire "Akkadian Empire").

The [Neo-Babylonian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Babylonian_Empire "Neo-Babylonian Empire"), or [Chaldea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaldea "Chaldea"), was Babylonia from the 7th and 6th centuries BC. Under the reign of [Nebuchadnezzar II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebuchadnezzar_II "Nebuchadnezzar II"), it conquered [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem "Jerusalem"). This empire also created the [Hanging Gardens of Babylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanging_Gardens_of_Babylon "Hanging Gardens of Babylon") and the still-surviving [Ishtar Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishtar_Gate "Ishtar Gate") as architectural embellishments of its capital at Babylon.

[Akkad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkad_(city) "Akkad (city)") was a city and its surrounding region near Babylon. Akkad also became the capital of the Akkadian Empire. Despite an extensive search, the precise site has never been found. Akkad reached the height of its power between about 2330 and 2150 BC, following the conquests of King [Sargon of Akkad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sargon_of_Akkad "Sargon of Akkad"). Through the spread of Sargon's empire, the language of Akkad, known as [Akkadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_language "Akkadian language") from the city, spread and replaced the Sumerian language in Mesopotamia and eventually, by 1450 BC, was the main language of diplomacy in the Near East.

[Assyria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyria "Assyria") was originally a region on the Upper [Tigris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigris "Tigris"), where a small state was created in the 19th century BC. The capital was at [Assur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assur "Assur"), which gave the state its name. Later, as a nation and empire that came to control all of the Fertile Crescent, Egypt, and much of [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia "Anatolia"), the term "Assyria proper" referred to roughly the northern half of Mesopotamia (the southern half being Babylonia), with [Nineveh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineveh "Nineveh") as its capital. The Assyrian kings controlled a large kingdom at three different times in history. These are called the *Old* (20th to 18th centuries BC), *Middle* (14th to 11th centuries BC), and *[Neo-Assyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Assyrian_Empire "Neo-Assyrian Empire")* (9th to 7th centuries BC) kingdoms, or periods.

[Mitanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitanni "Mitanni") was a [Hurrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurrians "Hurrians") empire in northern Mesopotamia founded around 1500 BC. The Mitannians conquered and controlled Assyria until the 14th century BC while contending with Egypt for control of parts of modern Syria. Its capital was [Washukanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washukanni "Washukanni"), whose precise location has not been determined by archaeologists.

#### Iranian peoples

The [Medes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medes "Medes") and [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persians "Persians") were peoples who had appeared in the Iranian plateau around 1500 BC. Both peoples spoke [Indo-European languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_languages "Indo-European languages") and were mostly pastoralists with a tradition of horse archery. The Medes established their own [Median Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_Empire "Median Empire") by the 6th century BC, having defeated the Neo-Assyrian Empire with the [Chaldeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaldea "Chaldea") in 614 BC.

The [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire "Achaemenid Empire") was founded by [Cyrus the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_the_Great "Cyrus the Great"), who first became king of the Persians, then conquered the Medes, [Lydia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia "Lydia"), and Babylon by 539 BC. The empire built on earlier Mesopotamian systems of government to govern its large empire. By building roads, they improved both the ability to send governmental instructions throughout their lands and the ability of their military forces to be deployed rapidly. Increased trade and upgraded farming techniques increased wealth, but also exacerbated inequalities between social classes. The empire's location at the centre of trading networks spread its intellectual and philosophical ideas throughout a wide area, and its religion, while not itself spreading far, had an impact on later religions such as [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity"), [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam"), and [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism "Judaism"). Cyrus' son [Cambyses II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambyses_II "Cambyses II") conquered Egypt, while a later emperor, [Darius the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_the_Great "Darius the Great"), expanded the empire to the [Indus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River "Indus River"), creating the largest empire in the world to that date. But Darius and his son [Xerxes I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xerxes_I "Xerxes I") failed to expand into [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece "Greece"), with expeditions in 490 and 480 BC eventually failing. The Achaemenid dynasty and empire fell to [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great "Alexander the Great") by 330 BC, and after Alexander's death, much of the area previously ruled by Cyrus and his successors was ruled by the [Seleucid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_dynasty "Seleucid dynasty").

[Parthia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthia "Parthia") was an Iranian civilisation situated in the northeastern part of modern Iran. Their power was based on a combination of military power based on heavy cavalry with a decentralised governing structure based on a [federated system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation "Federation"). The [Parthian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthian_Empire "Parthian Empire") was led by the [Arsacid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsacid_dynasty "Arsacid dynasty"), which – by around 155 BC, under [Mithradates I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mithradates_I "Mithradates I") – had mostly conquered the [Seleucid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire "Seleucid Empire"). Parthia had many wars with the Romans, but it was rebellions within the empire that ended it in the 3rd century AD.

The [Sasanian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire "Sasanian Empire") began when the Parthian Empire ended in AD 224. Their rulers claimed the Achaemenids as ancestors and set up their capital at [Ctesiphon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ctesiphon "Ctesiphon") in Mesopotamia. Their period of greatest military expansion occurred under [Shapur I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shapur_I "Shapur I"), who by the time of his death in AD 272 had defeated Roman imperial armies and set up buffer states between the Sasanian and Roman Empires. After Shapur, the Sasanians were under more pressure from the Kushans to their east as well as the Roman (then Byzantine) Empire to their west. However, the Sasanians rebuilt and founded numerous cities; their merchants travelled widely and introduced crops such as sugar, rice, and cotton into the Iranian plateau. But in AD 651, the last Sassanid emperor was killed by the expanding Islamic Arabs.

#### Hittites

The [Hittites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittites "Hittites") first came to Anatolia about 1900 BC; during the period 1600–1500, they expanded into Mesopotamia, where they adapted the cuneiform script to their Indo-European language. By 1200, their empire stretched to [Phoenicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia "Phoenicia") and eastern [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia "Anatolia"). They improved two earlier technologies from Mesopotamia and spread these new techniques widely – improved iron working and light [chariots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chariot "Chariot") with [spoked wheels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spoked_wheel "Spoked wheel") in warfare. The Hittites introduced the casting of iron with moulds and then hammering it, which enabled weapons and tools to be made stronger and also cheaper. Although chariots had been used previously, the use of spoked wheels allowed the chariots to be much lighter and more manoeuvrable. In 1274 BC the Hittites clashed with the Egyptians at the [Battle of Kadesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kadesh "Battle of Kadesh"), where both sides claimed victory. In 1207 BC, the Hittite capital of [Hattusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hattusa "Hattusa") was sacked, ending the [Hittite Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittite_Empire "Hittite Empire").

#### Israel

[Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Israel_(Samaria) "Kingdom of Israel (Samaria)") and [Judah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Judah "Kingdom of Judah") were related Iron Age kingdoms of the ancient Levant, and they existed during the Iron Ages and the Neo-Babylonian, Persian and Hellenistic periods. The name Israel first appears in the [stele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merneptah_stele "Merneptah stele") of the Egyptian pharaoh [Merneptah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merneptah "Merneptah") around 1209 BC. This "Israel" was a cultural and probably political entity of the central highlands, well enough established to be perceived by the Egyptians as a possible challenge to their [hegemony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegemony "Hegemony"), but an ethnic group rather than an organised state.

Israel had emerged by the middle of the 9th century BC, when the Assyrian King [Shalmaneser III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalmaneser_III "Shalmaneser III") named "[Ahab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahab "Ahab") the Israelite" among his enemies at the [battle of Qarqar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Qarqar "Battle of Qarqar") (853). Judah emerged somewhat later than Israel, probably during the 9th century BC, but the subject is one of considerable controversy. Israel came into conflict with the Assyrians, who conquered Israel in 722 BC. The [Neo-Babylonian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Babylonian_Empire "Neo-Babylonian Empire") did the same to Judah in 586. After both conquests, the conquering forces deported many of the inhabitants to other regions of their respective empires.

Following the fall of Babylon to the Persian Empire, Cyrus the Great allowed the rebuilding of the temple at [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem "Jerusalem"), and some of the exiles from Judah [returned to Judea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_to_Zion "Return to Zion"), where they remained under Persian rule until the [Maccabean revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maccabean_revolt "Maccabean revolt") led to independence during the Hellenistic period until [Roman conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic "Roman Republic").

#### Phoenicia

[Phoenicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia "Phoenicia") was an ancient civilisation centred in the north of ancient [Canaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaan "Canaan"), with its heartland along the coastal regions of modern-day [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon "Lebanon"), Syria and Israel. Phoenician civilisation was an enterprising [maritime trading culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalassocracy "Thalassocracy") that spread across the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean "Mediterranean") between 1550 and 300 BC. One Phoenician colony, [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage "Carthage"), ruled an empire in the Western Mediterranean until being defeated by Rome in the [Punic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic_Wars "Punic Wars"). The Phoenicians invented the [Phoenician alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenician_alphabet "Phoenician alphabet"), the forerunner of the modern [alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphabet "Alphabet") still in use today.

#### Arabia

The history of Pre-Islamic Arabia before the rise of [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam") in the AD 630s is not known in great detail. Archaeological exploration in the [Arabian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Peninsula "Arabian Peninsula") has been sparse; indigenous written sources are limited to the many inscriptions and coins from southern Arabia. Existing material consists primarily of written sources from other traditions (such as Egyptians, Greeks, Persians, Romans, etc.) and [oral traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_tradition "Oral tradition") later recorded by [Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam") scholars.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] A number of small kingdoms existed in Arabia from around AD 100 to perhaps about AD 400.

### Africa

#### North Africa

##### Egypt

[Ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt "Ancient Egypt") was a long-lived civilisation geographically located in north-eastern Africa. It was concentrated along the middle to lower reaches of the Nile River, reaching its greatest extent during the 2nd millennium BC, which is referred to as the New Kingdom period. It reached broadly from the [Nile Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile_Delta "Nile Delta") in the north, as far south as [Jebel Barkal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jebel_Barkal "Jebel Barkal") at the [Fourth Cataract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Cataract "Fourth Cataract") of the Nile. Extensions to the geographical range of ancient Egyptian civilisation included, at different times, areas of the southern [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant "Levant"), the Eastern Desert and the Red Sea coastline, the [Sinai Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_Peninsula "Sinai Peninsula"), and the [Western Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Desert "Libyan Desert") (focused on the several [oases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oasis "Oasis")).

Ancient Egypt developed over at least three and a half millennia. It began with the incipient unification of Nile Valley polities around 3100 BC, traditionally under [Menes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menes "Menes"). The civilisation of ancient Egypt was characterised primarily by intensive agricultural use of the fertile Nile Valley; the use of the Nile itself for transportation; the development of writing systems – first [hieroglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hieroglyphs "Hieroglyphs") and then later [hieratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hieratic "Hieratic") and other derived scripts – and [literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature "Literature"); the organisation of collective projects such as the [pyramids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_pyramids "Egyptian pyramids"); [trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade "Trade") with surrounding regions; and a [polytheistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytheism "Polytheism") religious tradition that included elaborate funeral customs including [mummification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_mummy "Egyptian mummy"). Overseeing these activities were a socio-political and economic [elite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elite "Elite") under the figure of a (semi)-divine ruler from a succession of ruling [dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynasty "Dynasty").

Ancient Egyptian history is divided across various periods, beginning with the [Old Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Kingdom "Old Kingdom"), which saw pyramid building on a large scale. After 2100 BC, the Old Kingdom dissolved into smaller states during the [First Intermediate Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Intermediate_Period "First Intermediate Period"), which lasted about 100 years. The [Middle Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Kingdom_of_Egypt "Middle Kingdom of Egypt") began around 2000 BC with the reunification of Egypt under pharaohs ruling from [Thebes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thebes,_Egypt "Thebes, Egypt"). The Middle Kingdom ended with the conquest of northern Egypt by the [Hyksos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyksos "Hyksos") around 1650 BC. The Hyksos were expelled from Egypt, and the land was reunited in the [New Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Kingdom_of_Egypt "New Kingdom of Egypt") around 1550 BC. This period lasted until about 1000 BC, and saw Egypt expand its borders into Palestine and Syria. The [Third Intermediate Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Intermediate_Period "Third Intermediate Period") was marked by the rule of priests as well as the conquest of Egypt by [Nubian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubia "Nubia") kings and then later Assyria, Persia, and Macedonians.

##### Nubia

The Ta-Seti kingdom in [Nubia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubia "Nubia") to the south of [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt") was conquered by Egyptian rulers around 3100 BC, but by 2500 BC the Nubians had created a new kingdom further south, known as the [Kingdom of Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kush "Kingdom of Kush"), centred on the upper Nile with a capital at [Kerma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerma "Kerma"). In the [Egyptian New Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Kingdom_of_Egypt "New Kingdom of Egypt") period, Kush was once more conquered by Egypt. However, by 1100 BC a new kingdom of Kush had formed, with a capital at [Napata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napata "Napata"). Nubian rulers conquered Egypt around 760 BC and retained control for about a century.

##### Carthage

Carthage was founded around 814 BC by [Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia "Phoenicia") settlers. [Ancient Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Carthage "Ancient Carthage") was a city-state that ruled an empire through alliances and trade influence that stretched throughout North Africa and modern [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain "Spain"). At the height of the city's influence, its empire included most of the western Mediterranean. The empire was in a constant state of struggle with the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic "Roman Republic"), which led to a series of conflicts known as the [Punic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic_Wars "Punic Wars"). After the third and [final Punic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Punic_War "Third Punic War"), Carthage was destroyed and then occupied by Roman forces. Nearly all of the territory held by Carthage fell into Roman hands.

#### East Africa

##### Ethiopia and Eritrea

The [Kingdom of Aksum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aksum "Kingdom of Aksum") was an important trading nation in northeastern Africa centred in present-day [Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrea "Eritrea") and northern [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia "Ethiopia"); it existed from approximately AD 100 to 940, growing from the Iron Age proto-Aksumite period around the 4th century BC to achieve prominence by the 1st century AD. The Kingdom of Aksum, at its height by the early 6th century AD, extended through much of modern Ethiopia and across the [Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea "Red Sea") to Arabia. The capital city of the empire was [Aksum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axum "Axum"), now in northern Ethiopia.

#### West Africa

##### Nok culture

[The Nok culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nok_culture "Nok culture") appeared in [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria "Nigeria") around 1000 BC and mysteriously vanished around AD 200. The civilisation's [social system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_system "Social system") is thought to have been highly advanced. The Nok civilisation was considered to be the earliest sub-Saharan producer of life-sized terracotta which has been discovered by archaeologists. The Nok also used [iron smelting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smelting "Smelting") that may have been independently developed.

##### Djenné-Djenno

The civilisation of Djenné-Djenno was located in the Niger River Valley in the country of [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali "Mali") and is considered to be among the oldest urbanised centres and the best-known archaeological site in [sub-Saharan Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-Saharan_Africa "Sub-Saharan Africa"). This archaeological site is located about 3 kilometers (1.9 miles) away from the modern town and is believed to have been involved in [long-distance trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade "Trade") and possibly the domestication of African rice. The site is believed to exceed 33 hectares (82 acres); however, this is yet to be confirmed with extensive survey work. With the help of archaeological excavations mainly by [Susan and Roderick McIntosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_McIntosh "Susan McIntosh"), the site is known to have been occupied from 250 BC to AD 900. The city is believed to have been abandoned and moved to where the current city is located due to the spread of Islam and the building of the [Great Mosque of Djenné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Mosque_of_Djenn%C3%A9 "Great Mosque of Djenné"). Previously, it was assumed that advanced trade networks and complex [societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society "Society") did not exist in the region until the arrival of traders from [Southwest Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia "West Asia"). However, sites such as Djenné-Djenno disprove this, as these traditions in [West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Africa "West Africa") flourished long before. Towns similar to that at Djenné-Djenno also developed at the site of Dia, also in Mali along the [Niger River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niger_River "Niger River"), from around 900 BC.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

##### Dhar Tichitt and Oualata

Dhar Tichitt and Oualata were prominent among the early urban centres, dated to 2000 BC, in present-day Mauritania. About 500 stone settlements littered the region in the former savannah of the Sahara. Its inhabitants fished and grew millet. It has been found that the Soninke of the Mandé peoples were responsible for constructing such settlements. Around 300 BC, the region became more desiccated, and the settlements began to decline, most likely relocating to Koumbi Saleh. From the type of architecture and pottery, it is believed that Tichit was related to the subsequent Ghana Empire. Old Jenne (Djenne) began to be settled around 300 BC, producing iron and with a sizeable population, evidenced in crowded cemeteries. The inhabitants and creators of these settlements during these periods are thought to have been ancestors of the Soninke people. [*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

#### Bantu expansion

Peoples speaking precursors to the modern-day [Bantu languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_languages "Bantu languages") began to spread throughout southern Africa, and by 2000 BC they were expanding past the [Congo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_River "Congo River") and into the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Great_Lakes "African Great Lakes") area. By AD 1000, these groups had spread throughout all of southern Africa south of the equator. Iron metallurgy and agriculture spread along with these peoples, with the cultivation of millet, oil palms, sorghum, and yams as well as the use of domesticated cattle, pigs, and sheep. These technologies helped increase population, and settled communities became common in sub-Saharan Africa except in deserts or heavy forests.

### South Asia

[Paleolithic tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic "Paleolithic") have been discovered in India dating to 200,000 years ago, and Neolithic sites are known from near the [Indus Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley "Indus Valley") dating to around 8000 BC. Agriculture began in the Indus Valley around 7000 BC, and reached the [Ganges Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges_Valley "Ganges Valley") by 3000 BC. [Barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley "Barley"), [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton "Cotton"), and wheat were grown; the population had domesticated cattle, goats, and sheep.

The [Indus Valley Civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation "Indus Valley Civilisation") developed around 3000 BC in the [Indus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River "Indus River") and [Ghaggar-Hakra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaggar-Hakra_River "Ghaggar-Hakra River") river valleys of north-east [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan"), Pakistan, and western India. Another name for this civilisation is Harappan, after the first of its cities to be excavated, [Harappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harappa "Harappa") (now in the Pakistani province of [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_(Pakistan) "Punjab (Pakistan)")). Harappan civilisation grew out of the earlier agricultural communities as they evolved into cities. These communities created and traded jewelry, figurines, and seals that appear widely scattered throughout Mesopotamia, Afghanistan, and Iran. [Chickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken "Chicken") were domesticated in addition to the earlier crops and animals. They developed their own writing system, the [Indus Valley script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_script "Indus Valley script"), which is still mostly undeciphered. The exact structure of society and the way the cities were governed is not known. By about 1600 BC, the Indus Valley culture had abandoned many of its cities, including [Mohenjo-Daro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohenjo-Daro "Mohenjo-Daro"). The exact reason for this decline is not known.

Indo-European-speaking peoples began to spread into India about 1500 BC. The *[Rigveda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigveda "Rigveda")*, in [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit "Sanskrit"), dates to this period and begins a period often known as the [Vedic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_period "Vedic period"). Between 1500 and 500 BC, these peoples spread throughout most of India and had begun to found small cities. Vedic society was characterised by the *[varna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varna_(Hinduism) "Varna (Hinduism)")* system, which divided society into four broad castes, which were later elaborated. By the end of the Vedic period, this way of organising society had become central to Indian society. Religion in the late Vedic period was evolving into [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism"), which spread throughout Southeast Asia. [Siddhartha Gautama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siddhartha_Gautama "Siddhartha Gautama"), born around 560 BC in northern India, went on to found a new religion based on his ascetic life – [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"). This faith also spread throughout Eastern and Southeastern Asia after his death. This period also saw the composition of the epics *[Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana "Ramayana")* and *[Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata "Mahabharata")*.

The kingdom of [Magadha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magadha_(Mahajanapada) "Magadha (Mahajanapada)") rose to prominence under a number of dynasties that peaked in power under the reign of [Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka "Ashoka") Maurya, one of India's most legendary and famous emperors. During the reign of Ashoka, the four dynasties of [Chola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola_dynasty "Chola dynasty"), [Chera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chera_dynasty "Chera dynasty"), and [Pandya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandya "Pandya") were ruling in the South, while [Devanampiya Tissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devanampiya_Tissa "Devanampiya Tissa") (250–210 BC) controlled [Anuradhapura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anuradhapura "Anuradhapura") (now [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka")). These kingdoms, while not part of Ashoka's empire, were on friendly terms with the [Maurya Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire "Maurya Empire"). An alliance existed between Devanampiya Tissa and Ashoka of India, who sent Buddhist missionaries to Sri Lanka.

Most of [North India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_India "North India") was reunited under the [Gupta Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire "Gupta Empire") beginning under [Chandragupta I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandragupta_I "Chandragupta I") around AD 320. Under his successors, the empire spread to include much of India except for the Deccan Plateau and the very south of the peninsula. This was a period of relative peace, and the Gupta rulers generally left administration to local rulers. The Gupta Empire was weakened and ultimately ruined by the raids of [Hunas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huna_people "Huna people") (a branch of the [Hephthalites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hephthalite "Hephthalite") emanating from Central Asia), and the empire broke up into smaller regional kingdoms by the end of the fifth century AD. India would remain fragmented into smaller states until the rise of the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire "Mughal Empire") in the 1500s.

### Southeast Asia and Oceania

The [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic "Neolithic") period of [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia") was characterised by several migrations into [Mainland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Southeast_Asia "Mainland Southeast Asia") and [Island Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_Southeast_Asia "Island Southeast Asia") from southern [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") by [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_languages "Austronesian languages"), [Austroasiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austroasiatic "Austroasiatic"), [Kra-Dai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kra-Dai "Kra-Dai"), and [Hmong–Mien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmong%E2%80%93Mien "Hmong–Mien") speakers.

Territorial principalities in both Insular and Mainland Southeast Asia, characterised as "agrarian kingdoms", developed an economy by around 500 BC based on surplus crop cultivation and moderate coastal trade of domestic natural products. Several states of the Malayan-Indonesian "[thalassian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalassocracy "Thalassocracy")" zone shared these characteristics with Indochinese polities like the [Pyu city-states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyu_city-states "Pyu city-states") in the [Irrawaddy River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrawaddy_River "Irrawaddy River") valley, the [Văn Lang kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Lang "Van Lang") in the [Red River Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Delta "Red River Delta") and [Funan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funan_(Southeast_Asia) "Funan (Southeast Asia)") around the lower [Mekong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong "Mekong"). Văn Lang, founded in the 7th century BC, endured until 258 BC under the [Hồng Bàng dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%E1%BB%93ng_B%C3%A0ng_dynasty "Hồng Bàng dynasty"), as part of the [Đông Sơn culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90%C3%B4ng_S%C6%A1n_culture "Đông Sơn culture") that sustained a dense and organised population that produced an elaborate [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age "Bronze Age") industry.

[Intensive wet-rice cultivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddy_field "Paddy field") in an ideal climate enabled the farming communities to produce a regular crop surplus that was used by the ruling elite to raise, command and pay work forces for public construction and maintenance projects such as canals and fortifications.

#### Mainland Southeast Asia

The earliest known evidence of copper and bronze production in Southeast Asia was found at [Ban Chiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Chiang "Ban Chiang") in north-east Thailand and among the [Phùng Nguyên culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phung_Nguyen_culture "Phung Nguyen culture") of northern Vietnam around 2000 BC.

The [Đông Sơn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dong_Son_culture "Dong Son culture") culture established a tradition of bronze production and the manufacture of ever more refined bronze and iron objects, such as plows, axes and sickles with shaft holes, socketed arrows and spearheads and small ornamented items. By about 500 BC, large and delicately decorated bronze drums of remarkable quality, weighing more than 70 kg (150 lb), were produced in the laborious [lost-wax casting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost-wax_casting "Lost-wax casting") process. This industry of highly sophisticated metal processing was developed independently of Chinese or Indian influence. Historians relate these achievements to the presence of organised, centralised and hierarchical communities and a large population.

Between 1000 BC and 100 AD, the [Sa Huỳnh culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa_Hu%E1%BB%B3nh_culture "Sa Huỳnh culture") flourished along the south-central coast of [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam "Vietnam"). Ceramic jar burial sites that included grave goods have been discovered at various sites along the entire territory. Among large, thin-walled terracotta jars, ornamented and colourised cooking pots, glass items, [jade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nephrite "Nephrite") earrings and metal objects were deposited near the rivers and along the coast.

#### Austronesia

Around 3000 to 1500 BC, a large-scale migration of [Austronesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesians "Austronesians") – known as the [Austronesian expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_expansion "Austronesian expansion") – began from [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan"). [Population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth "Population growth") primarily fuelled this migration. These first settlers settled in northern [Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzon "Luzon"), in the archipelago of the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines"), intermingling with the earlier [Australo-Melanesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australo-Melanesian "Australo-Melanesian") population who had inhabited the islands since about 23,000 years earlier. Over the next thousand years, Austronesian peoples migrated southeast to the rest of the Philippines, and into the islands of the [Celebes Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebes_Sea "Celebes Sea") and Borneo. From southwestern Borneo, Austronesians spread further west in a single migration event to both [Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatra "Sumatra") and the coastal regions of southern Vietnam, becoming the ancestors of the speakers of the [Malayic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayic_languages "Malayic languages") and [Chamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamic_languages "Chamic languages") branches of the Austronesian language family.

Soon after reaching the Philippines, Austronesians colonised the [Northern Mariana Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Mariana_Islands "Northern Mariana Islands") by 1500 BC or even earlier, becoming the first humans to reach [Remote Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_Oceania "Remote Oceania"). The [Chamorro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamorro_people "Chamorro people") migration was also unique in that it was the only Austronesian migration to the Pacific Islands to successfully retain rice cultivation. [Palau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau "Palau") and [Yap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yap "Yap") were settled by separate voyages by 1000 BC.

Another important migration branch was by the [Lapita culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapita_culture "Lapita culture"), which rapidly spread into the islands off the coast of northern [New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Guinea "New Guinea") and into the [Solomon Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Islands "Solomon Islands") and other parts of coastal New Guinea and [Island Melanesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_Melanesia "Island Melanesia") by 1200 BC. They reached the islands of [Fiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiji "Fiji"), [Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samoa "Samoa"), and [Tonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonga "Tonga") by around 900 to 800 BC. This remained the furthest extent of the Austronesian expansion into Polynesia until around 700 AD, when there was another surge of island colonisation. It reached the [Cook Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Islands "Cook Islands"), [Tahiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahiti "Tahiti"), and the [Marquesas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquesas "Marquesas") by 700 AD; [Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii "Hawaii") by 900 AD; [Rapa Nui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapa_Nui "Rapa Nui") by 1000 AD; and New Zealand by 1200 AD. For a few centuries, the Polynesian islands were connected by bidirectional long-distance sailing, with the exception of Rapa Nui, which had limited further contact due to its isolated geographical location. Island groups like the [Pitcairns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitcairns "Pitcairns"), the [Kermadec Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kermadec_Islands "Kermadec Islands"), and the [Norfolk Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Islands "Norfolk Islands") were also formerly settled by Austronesians but later abandoned. There is also putative evidence, based on the spread of the [sweet potato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_potato "Sweet potato"), that Austronesians may have reached South America from Polynesia, where they might have traded with the [Indigenous peoples of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas "Indigenous peoples of the Americas").

Austronesians established prehistoric [maritime trade networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_maritime_trade_network "Austronesian maritime trade network") in Island Southeast Asia, including the Maritime Jade Road, a jade trade network in Southeast Asia which existed in [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan") and the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines") from 2000 BC to 1000 AD. The trade was established by links between the indigenous peoples of Taiwan and the Philippines, and later included parts of Vietnam, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand, and other areas in Southeast Asia (known as the [Sa Huynh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa_Hu%E1%BB%B3nh_culture "Sa Huỳnh culture")-[Kalanay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalanay_Cave "Kalanay Cave") Interaction Sphere). [Lingling-o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingling-o "Lingling-o") artefacts are among the notable archaeological finds originating from the Maritime Jade Road. During the operation of the Maritime Jade Road, the Austronesian [spice trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice_trade "Spice trade") networks were also established by [Islander Southeast Asians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islander_Southeast_Asians "Islander Southeast Asians") with [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka") and [Southern India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_India "Southern India") by around 1000 to 600 BC.

They also established early long-distance contacts with Africa, possibly as early as before 500 BC, based on such archaeological evidence as banana [phytoliths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytolith "Phytolith") in [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon "Cameroon") and [Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda "Uganda") and remains of Neolithic chicken bones in [Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar "Zanzibar"). An Austronesian group, originally from the [Makassar Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makassar_Strait "Makassar Strait") region around [Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalimantan "Kalimantan") and [Sulawesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulawesi "Sulawesi"), eventually [settled Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Madagascar#A_common_Austronesian_origin:_The_Vahoaka_Ntaolo "History of Madagascar"), either directly from Southeast Asia or from preexisting mixed Austronesian-[Bantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_peoples "Bantu peoples") populations from [East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Africa "East Africa"). Estimates for when this occurred vary from the 1st century AD to as late as the 6th to 7th centuries AD. It is likely that the Austronesians that settled [Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar "Madagascar") followed a coastal route through South Asia and East Africa, rather than directly across the Indian Ocean. Genetic evidence suggests that some individuals of Austronesian descent reached Africa and the [Arabian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Peninsula "Arabian Peninsula").

By around the 2nd century BC, the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic "Neolithic") Austronesian jade and spice trade networks in Southeast Asia connected with the maritime trade routes of [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia"), the [Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East "Middle East"), eastern [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa "Africa"), and the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean "Mediterranean"), becoming what is now known as the [Maritime Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Silk_Road "Maritime Silk Road"). Prior to the 10th century, the eastern part of the route was primarily used by Southeast Asian Austronesian traders using distinctive [lashed-lug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lashed_lug_boat "Lashed lug boat") ships, although [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_people "Tamil people") and [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_people "Persian people") traders also sailed the western parts of the routes. It allowed the exchange of goods from [East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia") and Southeast Asia on one end, all the way to [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe "Europe") and eastern Africa on the other.

[Srivijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivijaya "Srivijaya"), an Austronesian polity founded at [Palembang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palembang "Palembang") in 682 AD, rose to dominate the trade in the region around the Straits of [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca "Strait of Malacca") and [Sunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_Strait "Sunda Strait") and the South China Sea [emporium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emporium_(antiquity) "Emporium (antiquity)") by controlling the trade in luxury aromatics and Buddhist artefacts from West Asia to a thriving Tang market. It emerged through the conquest and subjugation of neighbouring thalassocracies. These included [Melayu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melayu_Kingdom "Melayu Kingdom"), [Kedah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kedah "Kedah"), [Tarumanagara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarumanagara "Tarumanagara"), and [Mataram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mataram_Kingdom "Mataram Kingdom"), among others. These polities controlled the sea lanes in Southeast Asia and exploited the spice trade of the [Spice Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maluku_Islands "Maluku Islands"), as well as maritime trade routes between [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") and [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China").

### East Asia

#### China

The Chinese civilisation that emerged within the [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River "Yellow River") valley is one of the earliest civilisations in the world. Prior to the formation of civilisation, Neolithic cultures such as the [Longshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longshan_culture "Longshan culture") and [Yangshao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangshao_culture "Yangshao culture") dating to 5000 BC produced sophisticated pottery, cultivated millet, and likely produced clothes woven from [hemp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemp "Hemp") and [silk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk "Silk"). Rice was also farmed, and pigs and [water buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_buffalo "Water buffalo") were kept for food. Longshan potters may have used the pottery wheel to produce their wares. Ancient Chinese traditions described three [ancient dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynasties_in_Chinese_history "Dynasties in Chinese history") that predated the unification under the [Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_dynasty "Qin dynasty") and [Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty "Han dynasty") dynasties. These were the [Xia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xia_dynasty "Xia dynasty"), the [Shang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang_dynasty "Shang dynasty"), and the [Zhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Zhou "Western Zhou"). It was not until the later 20th century that many historians considered the Shang or Xia to be anything other than legendary. Little is yet known about the Xia, which appears to have begun around 2200 BC, and may have controlled parts of the [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River "Yangtze River") valley.

The Shang dynasty traditionally is dated to 1766 to 1122 BC. Bronze was central to Shang culture and technology, with chariots and bronze weapons helping to expand Shang control over northern China. The cities at Ao and [Yinxu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yinxu "Yinxu"), near [Anyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anyang "Anyang"), have been excavated; city walls, royal palaces, and archives were found, as well as tombs and workshops. A system of writing developed, beginning with [oracle bones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle_bone "Oracle bone"), of which over 100,000 are still extant.

Towards the end of the 2nd millennium BC, the Shang were overrun by the [Zhou dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_dynasty "Zhou dynasty") from the [Wei River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wei_River "Wei River") valley to the west. The Zhou rulers at this time invoked the concept of the [Mandate of Heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_of_Heaven "Mandate of Heaven") to legitimize their rule, a concept that would be influential for almost every successive dynasty. The Zhou initially established their capital in the west near modern [Xi'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi%27an "Xi'an"), near the Yellow River, but they would preside over a series of expansions into the Yangtze River valley. Zhou administration was decentralised, with local elites responsible for collecting tribute and providing military support to the Zhou rulers.

In the 8th century BC, power became decentralised during the [Spring and Autumn period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_and_Autumn_period "Spring and Autumn period"), named after the influential *[Spring and Autumn Annals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_and_Autumn_Annals "Spring and Autumn Annals")*. In this period, local military leaders used by the Zhou began to assert their power and vie for hegemony. The situation was aggravated by the invasion of other peoples, forcing the Zhou to move their capital east to [Luoyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luoyang "Luoyang"). In each of the hundreds of states that eventually arose, local strongmen held most of the political power and continued their subservience to the Zhou kings in name only. The [Hundred Schools of Thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Schools_of_Thought "Hundred Schools of Thought") of Chinese philosophy blossomed during this period, and such influential intellectual movements as Confucianism, [Taoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism "Taoism"), [Legalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legalism_(Chinese_philosophy) "Legalism (Chinese philosophy)") and [Mohism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohism "Mohism") were founded, partly in response to the changing political world.

After further political consolidation, seven prominent states remained by the end of the 5th century BC, and the years in which these few states battled each other are known as the Warring States period. Though there remained a nominal Zhou king until 256 BC, he was largely a figurehead and held little power. As neighboring territories of these warring states, including areas of modern [Sichuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan "Sichuan") and [Liaoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liaoning "Liaoning"), were annexed by the growing power of the rulers of [Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_(state) "Qin (state)"), they were governed under the new local administrative system of [commanderies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commandery_(China) "Commandery (China)"). The final expansion in this period began during the reign of Ying Zheng, the king of Qin. His unification of the other six powers, and further annexations to the south and southeast by 213 BC, enabled him to proclaim himself the [First Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_Shi_Huang "Qin Shi Huang") (Qin Shi Huangdi).

Qin Shi Huangdi ruled the unified China directly with absolute power. In contrast to the decentralised and feudal rule of earlier dynasties, the Qin ruled directly. Nationwide, the philosophy of [legalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legalism_(Chinese_philosophy) "Legalism (Chinese philosophy)") was enforced, and publications promoting rival ideas such as Confucianism were prohibited. During his reign, he unified China and created the first continuous [Great Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Wall "Great Wall") with the use of forced labour. [Invasions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin%27s_campaign_against_the_Yue_tribes "Qin's campaign against the Yue tribes") were launched southward to annex Vietnam. The Qin period also saw the standardisation of the Chinese writing system, and the government unified the legal systems as well as setting standardised units of measurement throughout the empire. After the emperor's death, rebellions began, and the Han dynasty took power and ruled China for over four centuries with a brief interruption from AD 9 to 23. The Han dynasty promoted the spread of iron agricultural tools, which helped create a food surplus that led to a large growth of population during the Han period. Silk production also increased, and the manufacture of paper was invented. Though the Han enjoyed great military and economic success, it was strained by the rise of aristocrats who disobeyed the central government. Public frustration provoked the [Yellow Turban Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Turban_Rebellion "Yellow Turban Rebellion"); though a failure, it nonetheless accelerated the empire's downfall. After AD 208, the Han dynasty broke up into [rival kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms_period "Three Kingdoms period"). China would remain divided for almost the next 400 years.

#### Neighbours of China

The East Asian nations adjacent to China were all profoundly influenced by their interactions with [Chinese civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_cultural_sphere "East Asian cultural sphere"). Northwestern [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea "Korea") and Northern [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam "Vietnam") were brought under Han rule by [Han Wudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Wudi "Han Wudi") in the second century BC, and this rule led to cultural influences on both areas for many centuries to come. Wudi also faced a threat from the [Xiongnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiongnu "Xiongnu"), a nomadic people from the Central Asian steppes. Wudi's invasions ended the Xiongnu state.

In 108 BC, the Han dynasty of China conquered much of northern Korea, but when Han China began its decline, [three kingdoms in Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms_of_Korea "Three Kingdoms of Korea") – those of [Baekje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baekje "Baekje"), [Goguryeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goguryeo "Goguryeo") and [Silla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silla "Silla") – emerged and expelled the Chinese. Goguryeo and Baekje were eventually destroyed by a [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty "Tang dynasty") and Silla alliance. Silla then drove out the Tang dynasty in 676 to control most of the Korean peninsula undisputed.

[Jomon culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jomon_culture "Jomon culture") formed in Japan before 500 BC and, under Chinese influence, became the [Yayoi culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yayoi_culture "Yayoi culture") which built large tombs by AD 200. In the 300s, a kingdom formed in the Yamato plain, perhaps influenced by Korean refugees.

### Americas

In pre-Columbian times, several large, centralised ancient civilisations developed in the [Western Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Hemisphere "Western Hemisphere"), both in [Mesoamerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica "Mesoamerica") and western [South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_America "South America"). Beyond these areas, the use of agriculture expanded to the southwest of the continental United States by 1200 BC, and into the eastern woodlands of North America by AD 100.

#### Andean civilisations

Ancient Andean civilisation began with the rise of organised fishing communities from 3500 BC onwards. Along with a sophisticated maritime society came the construction of large monuments, which likely existed as community centres. The peoples of this area grew beans, cotton, [peanuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut "Peanut"), and [sweet potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_potatoes "Sweet potatoes"), fished in the ocean, and by about 2000 BC had added the [potato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potato "Potato") to their crops. The [Chavín culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chavin_culture "Chavin culture"), based around the [Chavín cult](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Chavin_cult&action=edit&redlink=1 "Chavin cult (page does not exist)"), emerged around 1000 BC and led to large temples and artworks as well as sophisticated textiles. Gold, silver, and copper were worked for jewelry and occasionally for small copper tools.

After the decline of Chavín culture, a number of cities formed after about 200 BC. The cities at [Huari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huari_(archaeological_site) "Huari (archaeological site)"), [Pucara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pucara "Pucara"), and [Tiahuanaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiahuanaco "Tiahuanaco") were all likely over 10,000 residents. From about AD 300, the [Mochica culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mochica_culture "Mochica culture") arose along the [Moche River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_River "Moche River"). These people left painted pottery depicting their society and culture with a wide range of varied subjects. Besides the Mochica, there were a number of other large states in the Andes after about AD 100. Included amongst these are the [Nazca culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazca_culture "Nazca culture"), who were mainly village-dwelling but left behind a large ceremonial centre at [Cahuachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cahuachi "Cahuachi") as well as the [Nazca lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazca_lines "Nazca lines"), a large number of huge designs set into the desert floor.

#### Mesoamerica

Agricultural cultivation began around 8000 BC in [Mesoamerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica "Mesoamerica"), where [avocados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avocado "Avocado"), beans, [chili peppers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chili_pepper "Chili pepper"), [gourds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gourd "Gourd"), and squashes were grown from about 7000 BC. Around 4000 BC, [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize "Maize") began to be grown, and soon after this, [tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomato "Tomato"). Settlements appeared around 3000 BC, and by 2000 BC most of Mesoamerica was practicing agriculture. Although some animals were domesticated – notably [turkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_(bird) "Turkey (bird)") and dogs – the lack of suitable large animals precluded the development of animals used for transportation or labour.

Around 1200 BC the first [Olmec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olmec_culture "Olmec culture") centre of [San Lorenzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Lorenzo_Tenochtitl%C3%A1n "San Lorenzo Tenochtitlán") was founded, which remained the centre of Olmec civilisation until around 800 BC when [La Venta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Venta "La Venta") took over before losing primacy to [Tres Zapotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tres_Zapotes "Tres Zapotes") around 400 BC. These and other Olmec centres were groups of tombs, temples, and other ceremonial sites built of stone. Their construction testifies to the complexity of Olmec society, although the exact nature of how they were governed is not known. They also erected large stone sculptures of human heads and other subjects. [Jade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jade "Jade") jewelry and other Olmec objects are found throughout Mesoamerica, likely having travelled via trade networks. The [Olmec writing system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olmec_hieroglyphs "Olmec hieroglyphs") was mainly used for recording their [calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerican_calendar "Mesoamerican calendar"), both of which influenced later Mesoamerican cultures.

After the decline of the Olmecs, other civilisations in Mesoamerica either arose or emerged from the Olmec shadow – the [Mayans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_civilization "Maya civilization"), the [Zapotecs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zapotec_civilization "Zapotec civilization"), and [Teotihuacan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teotihuacan "Teotihuacan"). The Zapotecs began around 500 BC in the [Oaxaca Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oaxaca_Valley "Oaxaca Valley") at the site of [Monte Alban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Alban "Monte Alban"). Monte Alban grew to around 25,000 residents in the period around AD 200, with the city having large stone temples and an expansive stone plaza. Like the Olmecs, they had a writing system and calendar. But by AD 900, Monte Alban was deserted for unknown reasons. Teotihuacan developed around AD 200 and centred on the city of Teotihuacan, which grew to perhaps as many as 200,000 inhabitants at its height. Teotihuacan lasted until around AD 700, when it was burned and vandalised.

[Maya culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_civilization "Maya civilization") began to emerge around AD 300 in the [Yucatan Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yucatan_Peninsula "Yucatan Peninsula") and modern-day Guatemala. During the 600 years of the [Classical Maya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_civilization#Classic_period_(c._250–900_AD) "Maya civilization") period, more than 80 Mayan sites were built, with temples, pyramids, and palaces as the focal point of each centre. The most influential was [Tikal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tikal "Tikal"), but Mayan civilisation was based on city-states which often were at war with each other. This seems not to have restricted trade, which went on between the cities. A priestly elite kept astronomical and calendrical knowledge, recording it with a [writing system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayan_glyphs "Mayan glyphs") based on the Olmec system of glyphs. History, poetry, and other records were recorded in books, most of which did not survive the [Spanish conquest of Mesoamerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_colonization_of_the_Americas "Spanish colonization of the Americas"). Mathematics was also studied, and they used the concept of zero in their calculations. The Mayan civilisation began to decline about AD 800, and most of its cities were deserted soon afterwards.

#### Northern America

Organised societies in the ancient United States or Canada were often mound-builder civilisations. One of the most significant of these was the [Poverty Point culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_Point_culture "Poverty Point culture") that existed in the U.S. state of Louisiana, and was responsible for the creation of over 100 mound sites. The Mississippi River was a core area in the development of long-distance trade and culture. Following Poverty Point, successive complex cultures such as the Hopewell emerged in the Southeastern United States in the [Early Woodland period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodland_period "Woodland period"). Before AD 500, many mound-builder societies retained a hunter-gatherer form of subsistence.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Europe

#### Greece

Greece is home to the first advanced civilisations in Europe beginning with the [Cycladic culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycladic_culture "Cycladic culture") on the islands of the [Aegean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegean_Sea "Aegean Sea") around 3200 BC, and the [Minoan civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoan_civilization "Minoan civilization") in Crete (2700–1500 BC). The Minoans built large palaces decorated with frescoes and wrote in the [undeciphered script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undeciphered_writing_systems "Undeciphered writing systems") known as [Linear A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_A "Linear A"). The [Mycenaean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenaean_Greece "Mycenaean Greece") civilisation, the first distinctively Greek civilisation, later emerged on the mainland (1600–1100 BC), consisting of a network of palace-centred states and writing the earliest [attested](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attested_language "Attested language") form of [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language "Greek language") with the [Linear B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_B "Linear B") script. The Mycenaeans gradually absorbed the Minoans, but collapsed violently around 1200 BC, along with several other civilisations in the eastern Mediterranean, during the regional event known as the [Late Bronze Age collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Bronze_Age_collapse "Late Bronze Age collapse"). This ushered in a period known as the [Greek Dark Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Dark_Ages "Greek Dark Ages"), from which written records are absent. [*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The [Archaic Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaic_Greece "Archaic Greece") in Greece is generally considered to have lasted from around the 8th century BC to the invasion by Xerxes in 480 BC. This period saw the expansion of the Greek world around the Mediterranean, with the founding of Greek city-states as far afield as Sicily in the west and the Black Sea in the east. Politically, the Archaic period in Greece saw the collapse of the power of the old aristocracies, with democratic reforms in Athens and the development of [Sparta's unique constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spartan_Constitution "Spartan Constitution"). The end of the Archaic period also saw the rise of Athens, which would come to be a dominant power in the [Classical Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Greece "Classical Greece"), after the reforms of [Solon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solon "Solon") and the tyranny of [Pisistratus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisistratus "Pisistratus").

The Classical Greek world was dominated throughout the 5th century BC by the major powers of [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Athens "Ancient Athens") and [Sparta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparta "Sparta"). Through the [Delian League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delian_League "Delian League"), Athens was able to convert pan-Hellenic sentiment and fear of the Persian threat into a powerful empire, and this, along with the conflict between Sparta and Athens culminating in the [Peloponnesian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnesian_War "Peloponnesian War"), was the major [political](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political "Political") development of the first part of the Classical period. The period in Greek history from the death of Alexander the Great until the rise of the Roman Empire and its conquest of Egypt in 30 BC is known as the [Hellenistic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period "Hellenistic period"). After Alexander's death, a series of wars between his successors eventually led to three large states being formed from parts of Alexander's conquests, each ruled by a dynasty founded by one of the successors. These were the [Antigonids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigonid "Antigonid"), the [Seleucids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid "Seleucid"), and the [Ptolemies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemies "Ptolemies"). These three kingdoms, along with smaller kingdoms, spread Greek culture and lifestyles into Asia and Egypt. These varying states were eventually conquered by Rome or the [Parthian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthian_Empire "Parthian Empire").

#### Rome

[Ancient Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome "Ancient Rome") was a civilisation that grew out of the city-state of Rome, originating as a small agricultural community founded on the Italian peninsula in the 8th century BC, with influences from Greece and other Italian civilisations, such as the [Etruscans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruscans "Etruscans"). Traditionally, Rome was founded as a [monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_monarchy "Roman monarchy") that then became a [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic "Roman Republic"). Rome expanded through the Italian peninsula through a series of wars in the fifth through the third centuries BC. This expansion brought the Roman Republic into conflict with [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage "Carthage"), leading to a series of [Punic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic_Wars "Punic Wars") that ended with the destruction of Carthage in 146 BC. Rome then expanded into Greece and the eastern Mediterranean, while a series of internal conflicts led to the republic becoming an empire ruled by an [emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_emperor "Roman emperor") by the first century AD. Throughout the first and second centuries AD, the Empire grew slightly while spreading Roman culture throughout its boundaries.

A number of factors led to the eventual [decline of the Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decline_of_the_Roman_Empire "Decline of the Roman Empire"). The western half of the empire, including [Hispania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania "Hispania"), [Gaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaul "Gaul"), and Italy, eventually broke into independent kingdoms in the 5th century AD; the Eastern Roman Empire, governed from [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople "Constantinople"), is referred to as the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire "Byzantine Empire") after AD 476, the traditional date for the "fall of Rome" and subsequent onset of the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages "Middle Ages").

#### Late antiquity

The Roman Empire underwent considerable social, cultural and organisational change starting with the reign of [Diocletian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocletian "Diocletian"), who began the custom of splitting the empire into eastern and [western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire "Western Roman Empire") halves ruled by multiple emperors. [Constantine the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_the_Great "Constantine the Great") initiated the process of [Christianisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization "Christianization") of the empire and established a new capital at [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople "Constantinople"). [Migrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_Period "Migration Period") of [Germanic tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_peoples "Germanic peoples") disrupted Roman rule from the late 4th century onwards, culminating in the eventual [collapse of the empire in the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decline_of_the_Roman_Empire "Decline of the Roman Empire") in 476, replaced by the so-called [barbarian kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_monarchy "Germanic monarchy"). The resultant cultural fusion of [Greco-Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Roman_world "Greco-Roman world"), Germanic and Christian traditions formed the cultural foundations of Europe. There has been an attempt by scholars to connect European late antiquity to other areas in Eurasia.

#### Nomads and Iron Age peoples

The Celts were a diverse group of [tribal societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribal_societies "Tribal societies") in [Iron Age Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age_Europe "Iron Age Europe"). [Proto-Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Celtic "Proto-Celtic") culture formed in the [Early Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Iron_Age "Early Iron Age") in Central Europe ([Hallstatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallstatt_culture "Hallstatt culture") period, named for the site in present-day Austria). By the later Iron Age ([La Tène](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_T%C3%A8ne_culture "La Tène culture") period), Celts had expanded over a wide range of lands: as far west as [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland "Ireland") and the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula "Iberian Peninsula"), as far east as [Galatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galatia "Galatia") (central [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia "Anatolia")), and as far north as [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland "Scotland"). By the early centuries AD, following the expansion of the Roman Empire and the Great Migrations of Germanic peoples, Celtic culture had become restricted to the [British Isles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Isles "British Isles").

The [Huns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huns "Huns") were a nomadic people who formed a large state in Eastern Europe by about AD 400, and under their leader [Attila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attila "Attila"), they fought against both sections of the Roman Empire. However, after Attila's death, the state fell apart, and the Huns' influence in history disappeared. The [Hun-Xiongnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huns "Huns") connection is controversial at best and is often disputed, but it is also not completely discredited.

Migration of Germanic peoples to Britain from what is now northern [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany") and southern [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia "Scandinavia") is attested from the 5th century. Groups of [Goths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goths "Goths") migrated into western Europe, with the [Ostrogoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogoths "Ostrogoths") eventually settling in Italy before being conquered by the [Lombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombards "Lombards"). A related people, the [Visigoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigoths "Visigoths"), settled in Spain, founding a kingdom that lasted until it was conquered by Islamic rulers in the AD 700s.

Several Indo-European-speaking peoples inhabited the [Balkan Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkans "Balkans"), including the [Thracians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thracians "Thracians") and [Illyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyrians "Illyrians"), who were divided into many tribes. The first Illyrian tribe to create its own kingdom was the [Enchelei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enchelei "Enchelei"), which formed its own state around the 8th-7th century BC and reached the height of its power under King [Bardylis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bardylis "Bardylis"). The [Ardiani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardiani "Ardiani") were infamous for their piracy and [wars against the Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyro-Roman_Wars "Illyro-Roman Wars"), for the [first time between 229 BC and 228 BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyro-Roman_Wars#First_Illyrian_War "Illyro-Roman Wars"), then for a [second time during 220 BC-219 BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyro-Roman_Wars#Second_Illyrian_War "Illyro-Roman Wars") and for a [third time during 168 BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyro-Roman_Wars#Third_Illyrian_War "Illyro-Roman Wars").

## Developments

### Religion and philosophy

The rise of civilisation corresponded with the institutional sponsorship of belief in gods, supernatural forces and the afterlife. During the Bronze Age, many civilisations adopted their own form of polytheism. Usually, polytheistic Gods manifested human personalities, strengths and failings. Early religion was often based on location, with cities or entire countries selecting a deity that would grant them preferences and advantages over their competitors. Worship involved the construction of representations of deities and the offering of sacrifices. Sacrifices could be material goods, food, or – in extreme cases – human sacrifice to please a deity. New philosophies and religions arose in both the East and West, particularly around the 6th century BC. Over time, a great variety of religions developed around the world, with some of the earliest major ones being Hinduism (around 2000 BC), [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism") (5th century BC), and [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism "Jainism") (6th century BC) in [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India"), and [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism "Zoroastrianism") in [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire "Achaemenid Empire"). The [Abrahamic religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrahamic_religion "Abrahamic religion") trace their origin to [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism "Judaism"), around 1700 BC.

In the east, three schools of thought were to dominate Chinese thinking until the modern day. These were [Taoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism "Taoism"), [Legalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legalism_(Chinese_philosophy) "Legalism (Chinese philosophy)") and [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism "Confucianism"). The Confucian tradition, which would attain dominance, looked for political [morality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morality "Morality") not to the force of law but to the power and example of tradition. Confucianism would later spread into the [Korean Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Peninsula "Korean Peninsula") and [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan").

In the West, the Greek philosophical tradition, represented by [Socrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socrates "Socrates"), [Plato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plato "Plato"), and [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle "Aristotle"), was diffused throughout Europe and the Middle East in the 4th century BC by the conquests of Alexander the Great. After the [Bronze and Iron Age religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_and_Iron_Age_religion "Bronze and Iron Age religion") formed, Christianity spread through the Roman world.

### Science and technology

Ancient technological progress began before the recording of history, with tools, use of fire, domestication of animals, and agriculture all predating recorded history. The use of metals and the ability to make metal alloys was foundational for later technologies to develop. Medical knowledge, including the use of herbs to treat illnesses and wounds as well as some surgical techniques, advanced during antiquity. An early very important development that allowed for further advancement was writing, which allowed humans to record information for later use.

The characteristics of [ancient Egyptian technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egyptian_technology "Ancient Egyptian technology") are indicated by a set of artifacts and customs that lasted for thousands of years. The Egyptians invented and used many basic machines, such as the ramp and the lever, to aid construction processes. The Egyptians also played an important role in developing Mediterranean maritime technology, including ships. The Babylonians and Egyptians were early astronomers who recorded their observations of the night sky.

Water-managing [qanats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qanat "Qanat") – which likely emerged on the [Iranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran") plateau and possibly also in the [Arabian peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_peninsula "Arabian peninsula") sometime in the early 1st millennium BC – spread from there slowly west- and eastward.

The [Hindu–Arabic numeral system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu%E2%80%93Arabic_numeral_system "Hindu–Arabic numeral system") with the concept of zero was developed in India, while modern forms of paper were invented in China in the first century AD.
