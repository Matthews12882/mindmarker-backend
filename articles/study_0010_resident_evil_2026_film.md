# Resident Evil (2026 film)

***Resident Evil*** is a 2026 American [horror film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horror_film "Horror film") directed by [Zach Cregger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zach_Cregger "Zach Cregger") and written by Cregger and [Shay Hatten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shay_Hatten "Shay Hatten"), based on the *[Resident Evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil "Resident Evil")* video game franchise by [Capcom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capcom "Capcom"). It is the eighth live-action [*Resident Evil* film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_(film_series) "Resident Evil (film series)"). [Austin Abrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Abrams "Austin Abrams") stars as Bryan Hodukavich, a medical courier who finds himself in the midst of a zombie outbreak on his way to a delivery in [Raccoon City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raccoon_City "Raccoon City"). [Zach Cherry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zach_Cherry "Zach Cherry"), [Kali Reis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kali_Reis "Kali Reis"), and [Paul Walter Hauser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Walter_Hauser "Paul Walter Hauser") feature in supporting roles.

[Constantin Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantin_Film "Constantin Film") began planning a [reboot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reboot_(fiction) "Reboot (fiction)") of the *[Resident Evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_(film_series) "Resident Evil (film series)")* film series in early 2022, following the failure of the previous reboot, *[Resident Evil: Welcome to Raccoon City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil:_Welcome_to_Raccoon_City "Resident Evil: Welcome to Raccoon City")* (2021), and the cancellation of [the Netflix television series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_(TV_series) "Resident Evil (TV series)"). Cregger was hired in January 2025, and [Sony Interactive Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Interactive_Entertainment "Sony Interactive Entertainment")'s [PlayStation Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Productions "PlayStation Productions") joined the production. Four studios, including [Warner Bros. Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures "Warner Bros. Pictures") and [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix"), bid for the film rights, with [Sony Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Pictures "Sony Pictures") winning in March. Filming began in October 2025 in Prague and concluded in January 2026.

*Resident Evil* premiered in Los Angeles on September 15, 2026, and was theatrically released in the US on September 18 by [Sony Pictures Releasing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Pictures_Releasing "Sony Pictures Releasing"). It has grossed $325 million at the box office on a $75 million budget, becoming the highest-grossing *Resident Evil* film. It received positive reviews and is the highest-rated [film based on a video game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_films_based_on_video_games "List of films based on video games") on the review aggregators [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes") and [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic").

## Plot

Medical courier Bryan Hodukavich agrees to deliver a last-minute package to [Raccoon City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raccoon_City "Raccoon City") General Hospital in the snowy Colorado mountains. While driving, he argues on the phone with his girlfriend, Michelle, about her recently discovered pregnancy. A woman steps into the road and Bryan hits her. With no mobile signal, Bryan tries to drive the woman to the hospital, but Deputy Sheriff Wilson pulls him over. Wilson places the woman in his cruiser and orders Bryan to follow. The cruiser crashes, killing Wilson. When Bryan tries to call for help on the cruiser's radio, the woman attacks him. He shoots her with Wilson's pistol, damaging his own car.

Bryan walks to a farm, where he escapes and kills a mutated dog. He enters the farmhouse and finds a shotgun. Searching for ammunition, he spots the injured woman approaching and is ambushed by her infected husband. He shoots them both, but tentacles emerge from their bodies, fusing them together. Fleeing, Bryan enters a tunnel, but the exit is blocked by an RV. Inside, an infected girl mutates and bites him before he kills her. Outside the tunnel, Bryan is confronted by the infected couple, now an amalgamation of numerous bodies. He is saved by [Umbrella Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbrella_Corporation "Umbrella Corporation") employees Paul and Maxine, who ward the creature off with gunfire.

As they drive Bryan into Raccoon City, Paul explains that Umbrella developed a serum for the US government capable of rapidly evolving organisms, and that a laboratory accident with the serum triggered the outbreak. Scientists at Umbrella's Raccoon City General Hospital laboratory need Bryan's package—a stabilizer—to develop a cure and hand it to the National Guard. In the city, Maxine is killed by a mutant and Paul is absorbed into the amalgam. The amalgam chases Bryan, who escapes into the sewers. He encounters an obese creature, whose stomach bursts open and releases mutant rats. Bryan shoots them and flees as their remains begin merging.

Near the hospital, infected people leap from rooftops and splatter around Bryan, who notices their remains show no sign of regenerating. Entering the hospital via the underground car park, he realizes his bite wound is worsening and records a tearful farewell message for Michelle. An Umbrella scientist spots him on a surveillance camera and directs him via intercom to take the stairwell to the top-floor laboratory. Exhausted, Bryan takes the elevator instead, fighting off an acid-vomiting mutant patient while the amalgam pursues him up the shaft. He forces the mutant to spray acid on the amalgam, wounding it. The amalgam flees, and Bryan kills the patient.

Bryan navigates a maternity ward overrun with mutated newborns and staff, which chase him. He escapes onto a ruined skybridge and barricades the door. Bryan spots the amalgam climbing toward him. Using his last bullet, he shoots the barricade, causing the pursuing mutants to fall onto the amalgam. The creatures crash to the ground.

Bryan delivers the package to the laboratory. As the Umbrella scientists begin developing the cure, Bryan's infection takes hold and mutant appendages burst from his body. The appendages begin killing and consuming the scientists while Bryan desperately pleads for the cure. Bryan succumbs to the infection and kills the last scientist, sending the cure plummeting off the rooftop. Fully mutated, Bryan roars as National Guard helicopters arrive.

## Production

### Development

[Johannes Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Roberts "Johannes Roberts"), the director of the previous film in the *[Resident Evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_(film_series) "Resident Evil (film series)")* film series, *[Resident Evil: Welcome to Raccoon City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil:_Welcome_to_Raccoon_City "Resident Evil: Welcome to Raccoon City")* (2021), expressed interest in directing a sequel. In April 2023, Raccoon HG Productions, which financed *Welcome to Raccoon City*, received a grant of CA$2 million from the [Northern Ontario Heritage Fund Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ontario_Heritage_Fund "Northern Ontario Heritage Fund") for a film titled *Resident Evil: The Umbrella Chronicles*. [Greater Sudbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Sudbury "Greater Sudbury") was picked as the principal location.

In January 2025, it was announced Cregger would co-write and direct a *Resident Evil* film reboot. The rights holder, [Constantin Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantin_Film "Constantin Film"), co-financed and produced, alongside [PlayStation Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Productions "PlayStation Productions") and [Vertigo Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertigo_Entertainment "Vertigo Entertainment"). A bidding war between four major studios ensued for the distribution rights, including [Warner Bros. Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures "Warner Bros. Pictures") and [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix"). In March 2025, it was revealed that [Sony Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Pictures "Sony Pictures"), which had co-produced the previous live-action films under their [Screen Gems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Gems "Screen Gems") division, won the bidding war, and would have [Columbia Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Pictures "Columbia Pictures") overseeing the production.

Cregger chose not to feature any characters from the games, saying: "I'm not going to tell [Leon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_S._Kennedy "Leon S. Kennedy")'s story, because Leon's story is told in the games. [Fans] already have that." He said it would not resemble *[Evil Dead II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evil_Dead_II "Evil Dead II")* (1987) rather than his previous films *[Barbarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbarian_(2022_film) "Barbarian (2022 film)")* (2022) and *[Weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapons_(2025_film) "Weapons (2025 film)")* (2025), and that it was a story he wanted to write whether he could use the *Resident Evil* IP or not. Cregger is a *Resident Evil* fan and described the film as a "love letter to the games". Cregger had not seen any of the previous *Resident Evil* films, and said his film would be more closely based on the games, particularly *[Resident Evil 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_2 "Resident Evil 2")* (1998), *[Resident Evil 3: Nemesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_3:_Nemesis "Resident Evil 3: Nemesis")* (1999), and *[Resident Evil 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_4 "Resident Evil 4")* (2005). The story takes place concurrently with the events of the Raccoon City outbreak in *Resident Evil 2*, in an alternative timeline set in a contemporary period.

Cregger and the co-writer, [Shay Hatten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shay_Hatten "Shay Hatten"), decided that *Resident Evil* was about "the player experience, which is you're one person guiding a character. You're up against it. They tried to create a story that could translate "trying to stumble through navigating a crazy situation" in the journey of its protagonist. The overall plot resembles a playthrough in which the character begins with one gun, gains confidence and items to help the fight, and by the time things get worse in the third act was "doing it adeptly". Bryan served as the audience surrogate, being someone "who's over his head, realizes what the situation is, and just is doing his best to scramble his way out of it", not being too competent in his actions and only managing to go through as "his survival instinct and desire to get home and see his girlfriend overcomes his natural fear". New characters would rarely appear and soon leave the story so as to keep the focus on Bryan's isolation and "the pure nightmare that he's in". The backstory of having a pregnant girlfriend and facing the fear of parenthood was not meant to be prominent, but Cregger and Hatten realized many of the situations they had written into Bryan's journey "speak to his psychological unreadiness to be a father and are reflecting his own situation back to him". The writing sessions had them doing a [FaceTime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FaceTime "FaceTime") call where each would write a scene simultaneously and then trade what they had finished, which Hatten described as "two hour spurts of concentrated energy" that helped them imbue the script with a fun tone, that like Cregger he compared to [Sam Raimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Raimi "Sam Raimi").

Cregger said, "I wanted to construct a story that could live in the world of *Resident Evil* and be maybe on the periphery of the events of *Resident Evil 2*, where [Raccoon City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raccoon_City "Raccoon City") is having its big night, but tell another story that could be happening in parallel to that that really honors the vibe and the pacing that you get when you play the games." In a September 2026 interview with *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")*, Cregger stated that he cut most of the jokes after test audiences found it "too funny".

### Casting

In March 2025, [Austin Abrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Abrams "Austin Abrams") was in talks to star, after having played a role in Cregger's previous film, *Weapons*. This was confirmed in July. [Mikey Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikey_Madison "Mikey Madison") was briefly in negotiations to star, but nothing ever came to fruition. In September, [Paul Walter Hauser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Walter_Hauser "Paul Walter Hauser") joined the cast. In October, [Zach Cherry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zach_Cherry "Zach Cherry"), [Kali Reis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kali_Reis "Kali Reis"), and Johnno Wilson were announced to have also joined the cast, with Cherry as a scientist and Reis as an ex-military character that was originally written for a male actor.

### Filming

[Principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography "Principal photography") began in [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague "Prague") on October 10, 2025, with [Dariusz Wolski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dariusz_Wolski "Dariusz Wolski") serving as [cinematographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinematographer "Cinematographer"). Filming wrapped on January 22, 2026. The film was in the editing process by February 2026.

### Music

The soundtrack was composed by Ryan and Hays Holladay, who previously co-scored *Weapons* with Cregger. The soundtrack album was released by [Milan Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Records "Milan Records") on September 18, 2026, the day of the film's release.

## Release

*Resident Evil* premiered at the United Theater on Broadway in Los Angeles on September 15, 2026, before premiering at the 21st [Fantastic Fest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantastic_Fest "Fantastic Fest") on September 17, 2026. It was released in the U.S. on September 18, 2026, having been filmed for [IMAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAX "IMAX").

Overseas, Sony opted for releasing *Resident Evil* a few weeks later in select Asian and African markets, with Thailand, the [Commonwealth of Independent States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Independent_States "Commonwealth of Independent States"), and South Africa opening on the following weekend from the American release, and Japan going on October 9.

## Reception

### Box office

As of October 14, 2026[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Resident_Evil_(2026_film)&action=edit), *Resident Evil* has grossed $142 million in the United States and Canada, and $183 million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $325 million.

In the United States and Canada, *Resident Evil* made $8.8 million in Thursday previews, and earned $60 million there on its opening weekend along with $48.3 million from other territories, generating a total of $108.3 million debut at the global box office. It set the opening records for both the franchise (surpassing *[Resident Evil: Retribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil:_Retribution "Resident Evil: Retribution")*) and Cregger's career (surpassing *Weapons*). It fell 61% in its second weekend to $23.3 million, finishing second. In its third weekend, it made $8.3 million.

### Critical response

On the [review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator "Review aggregator") website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes"), 96% of 299 critics' reviews are positive, with an average rating of 7.7/10. The website's consensus reads: "Packing unforgettable scares into a sleek runtime while Austin Abrams' crack comedic timing provides crucial levity, Zach Cregger's *Resident Evil* is a propulsive tribute to the pleasure and terror of playing a truly spooky video game." [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), which uses a [weighted average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_arithmetic_mean "Weighted arithmetic mean"), assigned it a score of 79 out of 100, based on 51 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews. It is both the best-reviewed film in the franchise and the highest-rated [film based on a video game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_films_based_on_video_games "List of films based on video games") on Rotten Tomatoes and Metacritic, surpassing *[Exit 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exit_8_(film) "Exit 8 (film)")*. Audiences polled by [CinemaScore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CinemaScore "CinemaScore") gave it an average grade of "B+" on an A+ to F scale, the highest grade ever received for a *Resident Evil* film.

William Bibbiani of *[TheWrap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TheWrap "TheWrap")* praised *Resident Evil* as both an effective horror film and a video game adaptation, describing it as an "explosive horror experience" that captures the feeling of playing the games without adapting one of their stories. Bibbiani highlighted Austin Abrams' performance as Bryan, the survival-horror structure, and Cregger's combination of frightening and comedic elements, ultimately calling it Cregger's best film to date. Clarisse Loughrey of *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent "The Independent")* praised the approach to video game adaptation, describing it as a "funny, delirious" reboot that effectively recreated the experience of playing a *Resident Evil* game. Loughrey praised Cregger's handling of the survival-horror mechanics, the balance of horror and comedy, and Abrams' performance, while noting that it remained accessible to viewers unfamiliar with the games.

Jesse Hassenger of *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* praised Abrams' portrayal of Bryan, calling his performance "a particularly fresh point-of-view character for a zombie/monster movie" while praising the comedic elements and the character of Bryan as a whole. Donald Clarke of *[The Irish Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Irish_Times "The Irish Times")* described *Resident Evil* as "hilarious, fast-moving, self-aware action romp", praising its gore, humor, and references to video game cliches. He called it "smallish in scale" and mentioned it there were fewer story elements from the games than in the other films.

Jocelyn Noveck of the [Associated Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_Press "Associated Press") praised Cregger's use of humor and gore and described Abrams as an appealing and relatable protagonist, while also highlighting the varied environments and jump scares. However, Noveck felt that the increasingly elaborate action became tiring toward the end. Phil de Semlyen of *[Time Out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Out_(magazine) "Time Out (magazine)")* gave *Resident Evil* three out of five, praising Abrams' performance and the visual style, creature design, and faithfulness to the gameplay experience, but criticized its relatively slow pace and argued that it was more "goofy and good-looking" than frightening. He also felt that the plot was thin outside of its survival-horror set pieces.
