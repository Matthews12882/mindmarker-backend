# Kenya

**Kenya**, officially the **Republic of Kenya**, is a country located in East Africa. With an estimated population of more than 54.2 million as of mid-2026, it is the [26th-most populous country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population "List of countries and dependencies by population") in the world and the [seventh-most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_African_countries_by_population "List of African countries by population") in Africa. Kenya's capital and largest city is [Nairobi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nairobi "Nairobi"). The second-largest and oldest city is [Mombasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa "Mombasa"), a port city located on [Mombasa Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa_Island "Mombasa Island"). Other major cities within the country include [Kisumu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisumu "Kisumu"), [Nakuru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakuru "Nakuru") and [Eldoret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eldoret "Eldoret"). Going clockwise, Kenya is bordered by South Sudan to the northwest, Ethiopia to the north, Somalia to the east, the Indian Ocean to the southeast, Tanzania to the southwest, and [Lake Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Victoria "Lake Victoria") and Uganda to the west. [Its geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Kenya "Geography of Kenya"), [climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Kenya "Climate change in Kenya") and [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Kenya "Demographics of Kenya") vary. In western Rift Valley counties, the landscape includes cold, snow-capped mountaintops (such as Batian, Nelion, and Point Lenana on [Mount Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kenya "Mount Kenya")) with surrounding forests, wildlife, and fertile agricultural regions in temperate climates. In other areas there are dry, [arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arid "Arid"), and [semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate "Semi-arid climate") climates, as well as absolute deserts (such as the [Chalbi Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalbi_Desert "Chalbi Desert") and [Nyiri Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyiri_Desert "Nyiri Desert")).

Kenya's earliest inhabitants included some of the first humans to evolve from ancestral members of the genus [Homo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo "Homo"). Ample fossil evidence for this evolutionary history has been found at [Koobi Fora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koobi_Fora "Koobi Fora"). Later, Kenya was inhabited by [hunter-gatherers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer "Hunter-gatherer") similar to the present-day [Hadza people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadza_people "Hadza people"). According to archaeological dating of associated artefacts and skeletal material, [Cushitic speakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Cushitic_languages "South Cushitic languages") first settled in the region's lowlands between 3,200 and 1,300 BC, a phase known as the Lowland [Savanna Pastoral Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savanna_Pastoral_Neolithic "Savanna Pastoral Neolithic"). [Nilotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilo-Saharan_languages "Nilo-Saharan languages")-speaking [pastoralists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastoralism "Pastoralism") (ancestral to Kenya's Nilotic speakers) began migrating from present-day South Sudan into Kenya around 500 BC. [Bantu people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_languages "Bantu languages") settled at the coast and the interior between 250 BC and 500 AD. European contact began in 1500 AD with the [Portuguese Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire "Portuguese Empire"), and effective colonisation of Kenya began in the 19th century during the [European exploration of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_exploration_of_Africa "European exploration of Africa"). Modern-day Kenya emerged from [a protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Africa_Protectorate "East Africa Protectorate"), established by the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire") in 1895, and the subsequent [Kenya Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Colony "Kenya Colony"), which began in 1920. Mombasa was the capital of the [British East Africa Protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_Africa_Protectorate "British East Africa Protectorate"), which included most of what is now Kenya and southwestern Somalia, from 1889 to 1907. Disputes between the UK and the colony led to the [Mau Mau revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mau_Mau_revolution "Mau Mau revolution"), which began in 1952, and the declaration of Kenya's independence in 1963. After independence, Kenya remained a member of the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations "Commonwealth of Nations").

Kenya is a [presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system "Presidential system") [representative democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democratic "Representative democratic") republic, in which elected officials represent the people and the president is the head of state and government. The country is a member of the United Nations, [the Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations "Commonwealth of Nations"), [World Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank "World Bank"), [International Monetary Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund "International Monetary Fund"), [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization "World Trade Organization"), [COMESA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COMESA "COMESA"), [International Criminal Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court "International Criminal Court"), as well as several other international organisations. It is also a [major non-NATO ally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_non-NATO_ally "Major non-NATO ally") of the United States. [Kenya's economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya%27s_economy "Kenya's economy") is the largest in East and Central Africa, with Nairobi serving as a major regional commercial hub. With a per-capita [Gross National Income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_National_Income "Gross National Income") of $2,110, the country is a [lower-middle-income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_country "Developing country") economy. Agriculture is the country's largest economic sector; tea and coffee are the sector's traditional cash crops, while fresh flowers are a fast-growing export. The [service industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_industry "Service industry"), particularly tourism, is also one of the country's major economic drivers. Kenya is a member of the [East African Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_Community "East African Community") trade bloc, though some international trade organisations categorise it as part of the [Greater Horn of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Horn_of_Africa "Greater Horn of Africa"). Africa is Kenya's largest export market, followed by the European Union.

## Etymology

The Republic of Kenya is named after [Mount Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kenya "Mount Kenya"). The earliest recorded version of the modern name was written by German explorer [Johann Ludwig Krapf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Ludwig_Krapf "Johann Ludwig Krapf") in the 19th century. While travelling with a Kamba caravan led by the long-distance trader [Chief Kivoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Kivoi_Mwendwa "Chief Kivoi Mwendwa"), Krapf spotted the mountain peak and asked what it was called. Kivoi told him "*Kĩ-Nyaa*" or "*Kĩlĩma- Kĩinyaa*", probably because the pattern of black rock and white snow on its peaks reminded him of the feathers of the male ostrich. In archaic [Kikuyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gikuyu_language "Gikuyu language"), the word 'nyaga' or more commonly 'manyaganyaga' is used to describe an extremely bright object. The Agikuyu, who inhabit the slopes of Mt. Kenya, call it Kĩrĩma Kĩrĩnyaga (literally 'the mountain with brightness') in [Kikuyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kikuyu_language "Kikuyu language"), while the [Embu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embu_people "Embu people") call it "*Ki-nyaga*" or Kirima Kinyaga. All three names have the same meaning.

Ludwig Krapf recorded the name as both *Kenia* and *Kegnia*. Some have said that this was a precise notation of the African pronunciation [/ˈkɛnjə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"). An 1882 map drawn by Joseph Thompsons, a Scottish geologist and naturalist, indicated Mt. Kenya as Mt. Kenia. The mountain's name was accepted, *[pars pro toto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pars_pro_toto "Pars pro toto")*, as the name of the country. It did not come into widespread official use during the early colonial period, when the country was referred to as the [East African Protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Africa_Protectorate "East Africa Protectorate"). The official name was changed to the [Colony of Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Colony "Kenya Colony") in 1920.

## History

### Prehistory

[Hominid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominid "Hominid") species, such as *[Homo habilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_habilis "Homo habilis")* (fl. 1.8 to 2.5 million years ago) and *[Homo erectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_erectus "Homo erectus")* (fl. 1.9 million to 350,000 years ago), possibly the direct ancestors of modern *[Homo sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_sapiens "Homo sapiens")*, had lived in Kenya in the [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene "Pleistocene") epoch. East Africa, including Kenya, is one of the earliest regions where modern humans (*Homo sapiens*) are believed to have lived. In 1984, during excavations at Lake Turkana palaeoanthropologist [Richard Leakey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Leakey "Richard Leakey"), assisted by [Kamoya Kimeu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamoya_Kimeu "Kamoya Kimeu"), discovered the [Turkana Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkana_Boy "Turkana Boy"), a 1.6-million-year-old *Homo erectus* fossil. Further evidence of Kenya's prehistory was found in 2018, namely the early emergence of [modern behaviours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavioral_modernity "Behavioral modernity"), including long-distance trade networks (involving goods such as [obsidian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obsidian "Obsidian")), the use of pigments, and possibly the making of [projectile points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projectile_points "Projectile points"), about 320,000 years ago. The authors of three 2018 studies on the site suggest that complex and modern behaviours had already begun in Africa around the time of the emergence of *Homo sapiens*.

The first inhabitants of present-day Kenya were ancestral hunter-gatherer groups, akin to the modern Khoisan speakers. They were subsequently joined by Southern Nilotic hunter-gatherers, such as the [Ogiek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okiek_people "Okiek people"), who established a deep, indigenous presence in the highland forests long before the arrival of later migrating groups. These people were later largely replaced by agropastoralist [Cushitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cushitic "Cushitic") (ancestral to Kenya's Cushitic speakers), who originated from the [Horn of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horn_of_Africa "Horn of Africa"). During the early [Holocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene "Holocene"), the region's climate shifted from drier to wetter conditions. This provided an opportunity for the development of cultural traditions such as agriculture and [herding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herding "Herding") in a more favourable environment.

[Nilotic-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilotic_peoples "Nilotic peoples") pastoralists began migrating into Kenya from the [Nile Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile_Valley "Nile Valley") and the [Ethiopian Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Highlands "Ethiopian Highlands") as early as 2000 BC. The earliest arrivals were [Southern Nilotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Nilotes "Southern Nilotes") (ancestors of the [Kalenjin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalenjin_people "Kalenjin people")), followed by subsequent waves of Eastern and Western Nilotes.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] Today, the country's Nilotic ethnic groups include the Kalenjin, [Samburu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samburu_people "Samburu people"), [Luo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luo_(family_of_ethnic_groups) "Luo (family of ethnic groups)"), [Turkana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkana_people "Turkana people"), [Maasai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maasai_people "Maasai people") and others.

By the first millennium AD, [Bantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_languages "Bantu languages")-speaking farmers had moved into the region, initially along the Kenyan coast. The Bantus had originated in West Africa along the [Benue River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benue_River "Benue River") in what is now eastern Nigeria and western Cameroon. The [Bantu migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_migration "Bantu migration") brought new developments in agriculture and [ironworking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_ferrous_metallurgy "History of ferrous metallurgy") to the region. Today, the country's Bantu groups include the [Kikuyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kikuyu_people "Kikuyu people"), [Luhya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhya_people "Luhya people"), [Kamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamba_people "Kamba people"), [Kisii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gusii_people "Gusii people"), [Meru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meru_people "Meru people"), [Kuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuria_people "Kuria people"), [Aembu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aembu "Aembu"), [Ambeere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambeere "Ambeere"), [Wadawida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadawida "Wadawida")-Watuweta, Wapokomo, and [Mijikenda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mijikenda_peoples "Mijikenda peoples"), among many others.

Notable prehistoric sites in the interior of Kenya include the (possibly archaeoastronomical) site [Namoratunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namoratunga "Namoratunga") on the west side of [Lake Turkana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Turkana "Lake Turkana") and the walled settlement of [Thimlich Ohinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thimlich_Ohinga "Thimlich Ohinga") in [Migori County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migori_County "Migori County").

### Swahili trade period

The coastline of Kenya was home to communities of [ironworkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironwork "Ironwork") and Bantu subsistence farmers, hunters, and fishers who supported the region's economy with agriculture, fishing, metal production, and trade with foreign countries. These communities formed the earliest [city-states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City-states "City-states") in the region, which were collectively known as [Azania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azania "Azania"). The Swahili people were of mixed African and Asian (particularly Persian) ancestry, as [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA") evidence has revealed.

By the 1st century CE, many of the area's city-states, such as [Mombasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa "Mombasa"), [Malindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malindi "Malindi"), and [Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar "Zanzibar"), began to establish trading relations with the [Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabs "Arabs"). This led to increased economic growth of the Swahili states, the introduction of [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam"), [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic "Arabic") influences on the [Swahili language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_language "Swahili language"), [cultural diffusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_diffusion "Cultural diffusion"), as well as the Swahili city-states becoming members of a larger trade network. Many historians had long believed that the city-states were established by Arab or Persian traders, but archaeological evidence has led scholars to recognise the city-states as an indigenous development which, though subjected to foreign influence due to trade, retained a Bantu cultural core.

The earliest native written sources date to this period. For example, there is a dedicatory inscription in the Msikiti wa Pwani in [Lamu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamu "Lamu") dating to 1370 or 1371 AD (772 [AH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hijri_era "Hijri era")). These pre-colonial sources are in Arabic but contain Swahili loanwords, as illustrated by the 1462 AD (866 AH) funerary inscription of Mwana wa Bwana binti Mwidani on [Mombasa Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa_Island "Mombasa Island").

The [Kilwa Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilwa_Sultanate "Kilwa Sultanate") was a medieval [sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate "Sultanate") centred at [Kilwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilwa_Kisiwani "Kilwa Kisiwani"), in modern-day Tanzania. At its height, its authority stretched over the entire length of the [Swahili Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_Coast "Swahili Coast"), including Kenya. Beginning in the 10th century, the rulers of Kilwa would go on to build elaborate coral mosques and introduce copper coinage.

Swahili, a [Bantu language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_language "Bantu language") with Arabic, [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language "Persian language"), and other Middle-Eastern and South Asian [loanwords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loanword "Loanword"), later developed as a *[lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca "Lingua franca")* for trade between the different peoples. Since the turn of the 20th century, Swahili has also adopted numerous loanwords and [calques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calque "Calque") from English, many of which originated during the era of British colonial rule.

### Early Portuguese presence

The Swahili built Mombasa into a major port city and established trade links with other nearby city-states, as well as commercial centres in Persia, Arabia, and even India. By the 15th century, Portuguese voyager [Duarte Barbosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duarte_Barbosa "Duarte Barbosa") wrote that "Mombasa is a place of great traffic and has a good harbour in which there are always moored small craft of many kinds and also great ships, both of which are bound from [Sofala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofala "Sofala") and others which come from [Cambay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambay "Cambay") and [Melinde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malindi "Malindi") and others which sail to the island of Zanzibar."

One major city on the Kenyan coast is Malindi. It has been an important Swahili settlement since the 14th century, and the city once rivalled Mombasa for dominance in the [African Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Great_Lakes "African Great Lakes") region. Malindi has traditionally been a friendly port city for foreign powers. In 1414, the Chinese trader and explorer [Zheng He](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zheng_He "Zheng He"), representing the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty "Ming dynasty"), visited the East African coast on one of his last '[treasure voyages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_treasure_voyages "Ming treasure voyages")'. Malindi also welcomed the Portuguese explorer [Vasco da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_da_Gama "Vasco da Gama") in 1498.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

In the 17th century, the Swahili coast was conquered by the [Omani Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omani_Arabs "Omani Arabs"), who expanded the [slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean_slave_trade "Indian Ocean slave trade") to meet the demands of plantations in Oman and Zanzibar. Initially, these traders came mainly from Oman, but later many came from Zanzibar (such as [Tippu Tip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tippu_Tip "Tippu Tip")). In addition, the Portuguese started buying slaves from the Omani and Zanzibari traders in response to the interruption of the [transatlantic slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transatlantic_slave_trade "Transatlantic slave trade") by [British abolitionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism_in_the_United_Kingdom "Abolitionism in the United Kingdom").[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### 18th and 19th centuries

During the 18th and 19th century, the [Masai people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masai_people "Masai people") moved into the central and southern [Rift Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Rift_Valley,_Kenya "Great Rift Valley, Kenya") plains of Kenya, from a region north of Lake Rudolf (now [Lake Turkana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Turkana "Lake Turkana")). Although there weren't many of them, they managed to conquer a great amount of land in the plains, where people did not put up much resistance.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] The [Nandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nandi_people "Nandi people") peoples managed to oppose the Masai, while the Taveta peoples fled to the forests on the eastern edge of [Mount Kilimanjaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kilimanjaro "Mount Kilimanjaro"), although they later were forced to leave the land due to the threat of [smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox "Smallpox"). An outbreak of either [rinderpest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rinderpest "Rinderpest") or [pleuropneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleuropneumonia "Pleuropneumonia") greatly affected the Masai's cattle, while an epidemic of smallpox affected the Masai themselves. After the death of the Masai [Mbatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbatian "Mbatian"), the chief *laibon* (medicine man), the Masai split into warring factions. The Masai caused much strife in the areas they conquered; however, cooperation between such groups as the [Luo people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luo_people "Luo people"), [Luhya people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhya_people "Luhya people"), and [Gusii people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gusii_people "Gusii people") is evidenced by shared vocabulary for modern implements and similar economic regimes. Although Arab traders remained in the area, trade routes were disrupted by the hostile Masai, though there was trade in ivory between these factions.

The first foreigners to successfully get past the Masai were [Johann Ludwig Krapf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Ludwig_Krapf "Johann Ludwig Krapf") and [Johannes Rebmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Rebmann "Johannes Rebmann"), two German missionaries who established a mission in [Rabai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabai "Rabai"), not too far from [Mombasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa "Mombasa"). The pair were the first Europeans to sight [Mount Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kenya "Mount Kenya").

### German Protectorate (1885–1890)

The [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire "German Empire") established a protectorate over the [Sultan of Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_of_Zanzibar "Sultan of Zanzibar")'s coastal possessions in 1885, followed by the arrival of the [Imperial British East Africa Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_British_East_Africa_Company "Imperial British East Africa Company") in 1888. Imperial rivalry was prevented by the [Heligoland–Zanzibar Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heligoland%E2%80%93Zanzibar_Treaty "Heligoland–Zanzibar Treaty"), so Germany handed its East African coastal holdings to Britain in 1890.

### Nandi Resistance (1890–1906)

The **Nandi Resistance** (1890–1906), *also known as the Kalenjin Resistance*, was the most prolonged military challenge to British [colonial establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_expansion "Colonial expansion") in East Africa. For nearly two decades, the [Nandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nandi_people "Nandi people") and [Kipsigis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kipsigis_people "Kipsigis people") peoples—led by the Orkoiyot [Koitalel Arap Samoei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koitalel_Arap_Samoei "Koitalel Arap Samoei") defended their territories in the western highlands of [Kenya's Rift Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Rift_Valley "Kenya Rift Valley"). By using effective [guerrilla warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerrilla_warfare "Guerrilla warfare") and sabotage against the construction of the [Uganda Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_Railway "Uganda Railway"), they successfully stalled British administrative control for nearly two decades.

Despite several "punitive expeditions," the British were unable to achieve a conventional military victory over the Kalenjin warriors. The resistance was broken through an act of [treachery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betrayal "Betrayal") on 19 October 1905, when [Colonel Richard Meinertzhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Meinertzhagen "Richard Meinertzhagen") lured Samoei to a purported truce meeting and assassinated him. Following this, Samoei's body was [decapitated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decapitated "Decapitated") and his head was taken to London as a colonial trophy, marking the end of the fierce military opposition by the Kalenjin and allowing the British to finally establish administrative control over the region.

### British Kenya (1888–1962)

The transfer by Germany to Britain was followed by the building of the [Uganda Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_Railway "Uganda Railway") passing through the country.

The building of the railway was resisted by some ethnic groups—notably the [Nandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nandi_people "Nandi people"), led by *[Orkoiyot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orkoiyot "Orkoiyot")* [Koitalel Arap Samoei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koitalel_Arap_Samoei "Koitalel Arap Samoei") from 1890 to 1900—but the British eventually built it. The *Nandi* were the first ethnic group to be put in a native reserve to stop them from disrupting the building of the railway.

During the railway construction era, there was a significant influx of Indian workers, who provided the bulk of the skilled labour required for construction. They and most of their descendants later remained in Kenya and formed the core of several distinct Indian communities, such as the [Ismaili Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismailism "Ismailism") and [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh "Sikh") communities. While building the railway through [Tsavo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsavo "Tsavo"), a number of the Indian railway workers and local African labourers were attacked by two lions known as the [Tsavo maneaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsavo_maneaters "Tsavo maneaters").

At the outbreak of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I") in August 1914, the governors of [British East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_Africa "British East Africa") (as the protectorate was generally known) and [German East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_East_Africa "German East Africa") initially agreed on a truce in an attempt to keep the young colonies out of direct hostilities. But Lieutenant Colonel [Paul von Lettow-Vorbeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_von_Lettow-Vorbeck "Paul von Lettow-Vorbeck"), the German military commander, determined to tie down as many British resources as possible. Completely cut off from Germany, Lettow-Vorbeck conducted [an effective guerrilla warfare campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_campaign_(World_War_I) "East African campaign (World War I)"), living off the land, capturing British supplies, and remaining undefeated. He eventually surrendered in [Northern Rhodesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Rhodesia "Northern Rhodesia") (today Zambia) 14 days after the Armistice was signed in 1918.

To chase von Lettow, the British deployed the [British Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Army "British Indian Army") troops from India but needed large numbers of porters to overcome the formidable logistics of transporting supplies far into the interior on foot. The [Carrier Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_Corps "Carrier Corps") was formed and ultimately mobilised over 400,000 Africans, contributing to their long-term politicisation.

In 1920, the East Africa Protectorate was turned into a colony and renamed Kenya after its highest mountain.

During the early part of the 20th century, the interior central highlands were settled by British and other European farmers, who became wealthy farming coffee and tea. One depiction of this period of change from a colonist's perspective is found in the memoir *[Out of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out_of_Africa "Out of Africa")* by Danish author Baroness Karen von Blixen-Finecke, published in 1937. By the 1930s, approximately 30,000 white settlers lived in the area and gained a political voice because of their contribution to the market economy.

The central highlands were already home to over a million members of the Kikuyu people, most of whom had no land claims in European terms and lived as itinerant farmers. To protect their own interests the settlers banned the growing of coffee and introduced a hut tax, and the landless were granted less and less land in exchange for their labour. A massive exodus to the cities ensued as their ability to make a living from the land dwindled. By the 1950s there were 80,000 [white settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whites_in_Kenya "Whites in Kenya") living in Kenya.

Throughout [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), [Kenya was](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_in_World_War_II "Kenya in World War II") an important source of manpower and agriculture for the United Kingdom. Kenya itself was the site of [fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_Campaign_(World_War_II) "East African Campaign (World War II)") between Allied forces and Italian troops in 1940–41, when Italian forces invaded. [Wajir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wajir "Wajir") and [Malindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malindi "Malindi") were bombed as well.

### Mau Mau Uprising

From October 1952 to December 1959, Kenya was in a state of emergency arising from the [Mau Mau rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mau_Mau_Uprising "Mau Mau Uprising") against British rule. The Mau Mau, also known as the Kenya Land and Freedom Army, were primarily [Kikuyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kikuyu_people "Kikuyu people"), [Embu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embu_people "Embu people") and [Meru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meru_people "Meru people") people. During the colonial administration's crackdown, over 11,000 freedom fighters had been killed, along with 100 British troops and 2,000 Kenyan loyalist soldiers. War crimes were committed on both sides of the conflict, including the publicised [Lari massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lari_massacre "Lari massacre") and the [Hola massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hola_massacre "Hola massacre"). The governor requested and obtained British and African troops, including the [King's African Rifles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King%27s_African_Rifles "King's African Rifles"). The British began counter-insurgency operations. In May 1953, General Sir [George Erskine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Erskine "George Erskine") took charge as commander-in-chief of the colony's armed forces, with the personal backing of [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill "Winston Churchill").

The capture of [Waruhiu Itote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waruhiu_Itote "Waruhiu Itote") (nom de guerre *"General China"*) on 15 January 1954 and the subsequent interrogation led to a better understanding of the Mau Mau command structure for the British. [Operation Anvil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mau_Mau_Uprising#British_gain_the_initiative "Mau Mau Uprising") opened on 24 April 1954, after weeks of planning by the army with the approval of the War Council. The operation effectively placed Nairobi under military siege. Nairobi's occupants were screened, and suspected Mau Mau supporters moved to detention camps. More than 80,000 Kikuyu were [held in detention camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_British_Detention_Camps_during_the_Mau_Mau_Uprising "List of British Detention Camps during the Mau Mau Uprising") without trial, often subject to brutal treatment. The Home Guard formed the core of the government's strategy as it was composed of loyalist Africans, not foreign forces such as the [British Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army "British Army") and King's African Rifles.

The capture of [Dedan Kimathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedan_Kimathi "Dedan Kimathi") on 21 October 1956 in [Nyeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyeri "Nyeri") signified the ultimate defeat of the Mau Mau and essentially ended the military offensive. During this period, substantial governmental changes to land tenure occurred. The most important of these was the [Swynnerton Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swynnerton_Plan "Swynnerton Plan"), which was used to both reward loyalists and punish Mau Mau. This left roughly 1/3rd of Kikuyu bereft of any tenancy land arrangement and thus propertyless at the time of independence.

### Somalis of Kenya referendum, 1962

Before Kenya got its independence, [Somali ethnic people in present-day Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somalis_in_Kenya "Somalis in Kenya") in the areas of [Northern Frontier Districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Frontier_Districts "Northern Frontier Districts") petitioned Her Majesty's Government not to be included in Kenya. The colonial government decided to hold Kenya's first referendum in 1962 to check the willingness of Somalis in Kenya to join Somalia.

The result of the referendum showed that 86% of Somalis in Kenya wished to join Somalia, yet the British colonial administration rejected the result and the Somalis remained in Kenya.

### Early independence period

The first direct elections for native Kenyans to the Legislative Council took place in 1957.

Despite British hopes of handing power to "moderate" local rivals, it was the [Kenya African National Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_African_National_Union "Kenya African National Union") (KANU) of [Jomo Kenyatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jomo_Kenyatta "Jomo Kenyatta") that formed a government. The Colony of Kenya and the Protectorate of Kenya each came to an end on 12 December 1963, with independence conferred on all of Kenya. The UK ceded sovereignty over the Colony of Kenya. The Sultan of Zanzibar agreed that simultaneously with the independence of the Colony he would cease to have sovereignty over the Protectorate of Kenya, so that all of Kenya would become one sovereign state. In this way, Kenya became an independent country under the Kenya Independence Act 1963 of the United Kingdom. On 12 December 1964, Kenya became a republic under the name "Republic of Kenya".

Concurrently, the Kenyan army fought the [Shifta War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shifta_War "Shifta War") against ethnic Somali rebels inhabiting the [Northern Frontier District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Frontier_District "Northern Frontier District") who wanted to join their kin in the [Somali Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Republic "Somali Republic") to the north. A ceasefire was eventually reached with the signing of the Arusha Memorandum in October 1967, but relative insecurity prevailed through 1969. To discourage further invasions, Kenya signed a defence pact with Ethiopia in 1969, which is still in effect.

### First presidency

On 12 December 1964, the Republic of Kenya was proclaimed, and [Jomo Kenyatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jomo_Kenyatta "Jomo Kenyatta") became Kenya's first president. Under Kenyatta, corruption became widespread throughout the government, civil service, and business community. Kenyatta and his family were tied up with this corruption as they enriched themselves through the mass purchase of property after 1963. Their acquisitions in the Central, Rift Valley, and Coast Provinces aroused great anger among landless Kenyans. His family used his presidential position to circumvent legal or administrative obstacles to acquiring property. The Kenyatta family also heavily invested in the coastal hotel business, with Kenyatta personally owning the Leonard Beach Hotel.

Kenyatta's mixed legacy was highlighted at the 10-year anniversary of Kenya's independence. A December 1973 article in *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* praised Kenyatta's leadership and Kenya for emerging as a model of pragmatism and conservatism. Kenya's GDP had increased at an annual rate of 6.6%, higher than the population growth rate of more than 3%. But [Amnesty International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amnesty_International "Amnesty International") responded to the article by stating the cost of the stability in terms of human rights abuses. The opposition party started by [Oginga Odinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oginga_Odinga "Oginga Odinga")—[Kenya People's Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_People%27s_Union "Kenya People's Union") (KPU)—was banned in 1969 after the [Kisumu Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisumu_Massacre "Kisumu Massacre") and KPU leaders were still in detention without trial in gross violation of the [U.N. Declaration of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.N._Declaration_of_Human_Rights "U.N. Declaration of Human Rights"). The Kenya Students Union, [Jehovah Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah_Witnesses "Jehovah Witnesses") and all opposition parties were outlawed. Kenyatta ruled until his death on 22 August 1978.

### Moi era

After Kenyatta died, [Daniel arap Moi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_arap_Moi "Daniel arap Moi") became president. He retained the presidency, running unopposed in elections held in 1979, 1983 ([snap elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snap_election "Snap election")), and 1988, all of which were held under the single-party constitution. The 1983 elections were held a year early, and were a direct result of [a failed military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Kenyan_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat_attempt "1982 Kenyan coup d'état attempt") on 2 August 1982.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The 1982 coup was masterminded by a low-ranking Air Force serviceman, Senior Private [Hezekiah Ochuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hezekiah_Ochuka "Hezekiah Ochuka"), and was staged mainly by enlisted men of the Air Force. It was quickly suppressed by forces commanded by Chief of General Staff [Mahamoud Mohamed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahamoud_Mohamed "Mahamoud Mohamed"), a veteran Somali military official. They included the General Service Unit (GSU)—a paramilitary wing of the police—and later the regular police.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

On the heels of the [Garissa Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garissa_Massacre "Garissa Massacre") of 1980, Kenyan troops committed the [Wagalla massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagalla_massacre "Wagalla massacre") in 1984 against thousands of civilians in [Wajir County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wajir_County "Wajir County"). An official probe into the atrocities was later ordered in 2011.[*[clarification needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")*]

The election held in 1988 saw the advent of the *mlolongo* (queuing) system, where voters were supposed to line up behind their favoured candidates instead of casting a secret ballot. This was seen as the climax of a very undemocratic regime and led to widespread agitation for constitutional reform. Several contentious clauses, including the one that allowed for only one political party, were changed in the following years.

### Transition to multiparty democracy

In 1991, Kenya transitioned to a multiparty political system after 26 years of single-party rule. On 28 October 1992, Moi dissolved parliament, five months before the end of his term. As a result, preparations began for all elective seats in parliament as well as the president. The election was scheduled to take place on 7 December 1992, but delays led to its postponement to 29 December. Apart from KANU, the ruling party, other parties represented in the elections included [FORD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forum_for_the_Restoration_of_Democracy "Forum for the Restoration of Democracy") Kenya and FORD Asili. This election was marked by large-scale intimidation of opponents and harassment of election officials. It resulted in an economic crisis propagated by ethnic violence as the president was accused of rigging electoral results to retain power. This election was a turning point for Kenya as it signified the beginning of the end of Moi's leadership and the rule of KANU. Moi retained the presidency and [George Saitoti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Saitoti "George Saitoti") became vice-president. Although it held on to power, KANU won 100 seats and lost 88 seats to the six opposition parties.

The 1992 elections marked the beginning of multiparty politics after more than 25 years of KANU rule. Following skirmishes in the aftermath of the elections, 5,000 people were killed and another 75,000 displaced from their homes. In the next five years, many political alliances were formed in preparation for the next elections. In 1994, [Jaramogi Oginga Odinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaramogi_Oginga_Odinga "Jaramogi Oginga Odinga") died and several coalitions joined his FORD Kenya party to form a new party, United National Democratic Alliance. This party was plagued with disagreements. In 1995, [Richard Leakey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Leakey "Richard Leakey") formed the Safina party, but it was denied registration until November 1997.

In 1996, KANU revised the constitution to allow Moi to remain president for another term. Subsequently, Moi stood for reelection and won a 5th term in 1997. His win was strongly criticised by his major opponents, [Kibaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mwai_Kibaki "Mwai Kibaki") and [Odinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raila_Odinga "Raila Odinga"), as fraudulent. Following this win, Moi was constitutionally barred from another presidential term. Beginning in 1998, he attempted to influence the country's succession politics to have [Uhuru Kenyatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uhuru_Kenyatta "Uhuru Kenyatta") elected in the 2002 elections.

### President Kibaki and the road to a new constitution

Moi's plan to be replaced by Uhuru Kenyatta failed, and [Mwai Kibaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mwai_Kibaki "Mwai Kibaki"), running for the opposition coalition "National Rainbow Coalition" ([NARC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Rainbow_Coalition "National Rainbow Coalition")), was elected president. David Anderson (2003) reports the elections were judged free and fair by local and international observers, and seemed to mark a turning point in Kenya's democratic evolution.

In 2005, Kenyans rejected a plan to replace the 1963 independence constitution with a new one. As a result, the elections of 2007 took place following the procedure set by the old constitution. Kibaki was reelected in highly contested elections marred by [political and ethnic violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_Kenyan_crisis "2007–08 Kenyan crisis"). The main opposition leader, Raila Odinga, claimed the election was rigged and that he was the rightfully elected president. In the ensuing violence, 1,500 people were killed and another 600,000 internally displaced, making it the worst post-election violence in Kenya. To stop the death and displacement of people, Kibaki and Odinga agreed to work together, with the latter taking the position of a prime minister. This made Odinga [the second prime minister of Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Kenya "Prime Minister of Kenya").

In July 2010, Kenya partnered with other East African countries to form the new East African Common Market within the [East African Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_Community "East African Community"). In 2011, [Kenya began sending troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Linda_Nchi "Operation Linda Nchi") to Somalia to fight the terror group [Al-Shabaab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shabaab_(militant_group) "Al-Shabaab (militant group)"). In mid-2011, two consecutive missed rainy seasons precipitated the worst [drought in East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_East_Africa_drought "2011 East Africa drought") in 60 years. The northwestern [Turkana region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkana_District "Turkana District") was especially affected, with local schools shut down as a result. The crisis was reportedly over by early 2012 because of coordinated relief efforts. Aid agencies subsequently shifted their emphasis to recovery initiatives, including digging irrigation canals and distributing plant seeds.

In August 2010, Kenyans held a [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Kenyan_constitutional_referendum "2010 Kenyan constitutional referendum") and passed [a new constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Kenya "Constitution of Kenya"), which limited presidential powers and devolved the central government. Following the passage of the new constitution, Kenya became a [presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system "Presidential system") [representative democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy "Representative democracy") republic, whereby the [President of Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Kenya "President of Kenya") is both [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state "Head of state") and [head of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_government "Head of government"), and of a [multi-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system "Multi-party system"). The new constitution also states that [executive powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_power "Executive power") are exercised by the executive branch of government, headed by the president, who chairs a cabinet composed of people chosen from outside parliament. [Legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_power "Legislative power") is vested exclusively in the [Parliament of Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Kenya "Parliament of Kenya"). The [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary "Judiciary") is independent of the executive and the legislature.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Kenyatta and Ruto presidencies

In 2013, Kenya held its [first general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Kenyan_general_election "2013 Kenyan general election") under the 2010 constitution. [Uhuru Kenyatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uhuru_Kenyatta "Uhuru Kenyatta") won, though the result was [unsuccessfully challenged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Kenyan_presidential_election_petition "2013 Kenyan presidential election petition") at the [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Kenya "Supreme Court of Kenya") by opposition leader [Raila Odinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raila_Odinga "Raila Odinga"). Kenyatta was re-elected in 2017 in [another disputed election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Kenyan_general_election "2017 Kenyan general election"). Odinga accused the [Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Electoral_and_Boundaries_Commission "Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission") of mismanaging the election and Kenyatta and his party of rigging. The Supreme Court overturned the results in a landmark ruling that established its position as an independent check on the president. In the repeated election, Kenyatta won after Odinga refused to participate, citing irregularities.

In March 2018, a historic [handshake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Kenya_handshake "2018 Kenya handshake") between Kenyatta and Odinga signalled a period of reconciliation followed by economic growth and increased stability. Between 2019 and 2021, the two combined efforts to promote major changes to the Kenyan constitution, labelled the "Building Bridges Initiative" (BBI). They said their efforts were to improve inclusion and overcome the country's election system that often resulted in post-election violence. The BBI proposal called for broad expansion of the legislative and executive branches, including the creation of a prime minister with two deputies and an official leader of the opposition, reverting to selecting cabinet ministers from among the elected members of parliament, establishing up to 70 new [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituencies "Constituencies"), and adding up to 300 unelected members of Parliament (under an "[affirmative action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affirmative_action "Affirmative action")" plan).

Critics saw this as an attempt to reward political dynasties and blunt Deputy President [William Ruto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Ruto "William Ruto")'s efforts to seek the presidency, and bloat the government at a high cost to the debt-laded country. Ultimately, in May 2021, the [Kenyan High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_(Kenya) "High Court (Kenya)") ruled the BBI unconstitutional, because it was an effort of the government rather than a [popular initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_initiative "Popular initiative"). The court sharply criticised Kenyatta, laying out grounds for his being sued or even [impeached](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeachment "Impeachment"). The ruling was seen as a major defeat for both Kenyatta and Odinga, but a boon to Odinga's future presidential-election rival, Ruto. In August 2021, Kenya's [Court of Appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Appeal_of_Kenya "Court of Appeal of Kenya") upheld the High Court's judgement in an appeal from the BBI Secretariat .

Ruto narrowly won the [2022 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Kenyan_general_election "2022 Kenyan general election"), defeating Odinga to become Kenya's fifth president. In 2024, Ruto and the [Kenya Kwanza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Kwanza "Kenya Kwanza") coalition faced [popular protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Protests_(2024) "Kenya Protests (2024)") over the [Kenyan Finance Bill 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Finance_Bill_2024 "Kenya Finance Bill 2024").

## Geography

At 580,367 km2 (224,081 sq mi), Kenya is the world's 47th-largest country (after Madagascar). It lies between latitudes [5°N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_parallel_north "5th parallel north") and [5°S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_parallel_south "5th parallel south"), and longitudes [34°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/34th_meridian_east "34th meridian east") and [42°E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42nd_meridian_east "42nd meridian east"). From the coast on the Indian Ocean, the low plains rise to central highlands which are bisected by the [Great Rift Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenyan_Rift_Valley "Kenyan Rift Valley"), and fertile plateaus lie on either side, around [Lake Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Victoria "Lake Victoria") and to the east.

The Kenyan Highlands are one of the most successful agricultural production regions in Africa. The highlands are the site of the highest point in Kenya and the second highest peak on the continent: [Mount Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kenya "Mount Kenya"), which reaches a height of 5,199 m (17,057 ft) and is the site of glaciers. [Mount Kilimanjaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kilimanjaro "Mount Kilimanjaro") (5,895 m or 19,341 ft) can be seen from Kenya to the south of the Tanzanian border.

### Climate

Kenya's climate varies from tropical along the coast, to temperate inland, to [arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arid "Arid") in the north and northeast parts of the country. The area receives a great deal of sunshine every month. It is usually cool at night and early in the morning inland at higher elevations.

The "long rains" season occurs from March/April to May/June. The "short rains" season occurs from October to November/December. The rainfall is sometimes heavy and often falls in the afternoons and evenings. Climate change is altering the natural pattern of the rainfall period, causing an extension of the short rains – which has led to floods – and reducing the drought cycle from every ten years to annual events, producing strong droughts such as the [2008–09 Kenya Drought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_Kenya_Drought "2008–09 Kenya Drought").

The temperature remains high throughout these months of tropical rain. The hottest period is February and March, leading into the season of the long rains, and the coldest is in July, until mid-August.

[Climate Change Impact on Real Estate](https://vorsagroup.co.ke/how-climate-change-is-impacting-nairobi-real-estate/)

[Climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change "Climate change") is posing an increasing threat to global socioeconomic development and environmental sustainability. Developing countries with low adaptive capacity and high vulnerability to the phenomenon are disproportionately affected. [Climate change in Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Kenya "Climate change in Kenya") is increasingly impacting the lives of Kenya's citizens and the [environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_environment "Natural environment"). Climate change has led to more frequent [extreme weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_weather "Extreme weather") events like [droughts](https://www.c2es.org/content/drought-and-climate-change/) which last longer than usual, irregular and unpredictable rainfall, flooding and increasing temperatures.

### Wildlife

Kenya has extensive land area devoted to [wildlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife "Wildlife") habitats, including the [Tsavo game park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsavo "Tsavo") and [Masai Mara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masai_Mara "Masai Mara"), where [Blue Wildebeest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Wildebeest "Blue Wildebeest") and other [bovids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bovid "Bovid") participate in a large-scale [annual migration](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/migration "wikt:migration"). More than one million wildebeest and 200,000 zebras participate in the migration across the [Mara River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mara_River "Mara River").

The ["Big Five"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Five_Game "Big Five Game") game animals of Africa, that is the lion, [leopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopard "Leopard"), [buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_buffalo "African buffalo"), [rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinoceros "Rhinoceros"), and [elephant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_elephant "African elephant"), can be found in Kenya and in the Masai Mara in particular. A significant population of other wild animals, reptiles, and birds can be found in the [national parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Kenya "List of national parks of Kenya") and game reserves in the country. The annual animal migration occurs between June and September, with millions of animals taking part, attracting valuable foreign tourism. Two million wildebeest migrate a distance of 2,900 kilometres (1,802 mi) from the [Serengeti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serengeti "Serengeti") in neighbouring Tanzania to the Masai Mara in Kenya, in a constant clockwise fashion, searching for food and water supplies. This Serengeti Migration of the wildebeest is listed among the [Seven Natural Wonders of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Natural_Wonders_of_Africa "Seven Natural Wonders of Africa").

### Conservation

Kenya had a 2019 [Forest Landscape Integrity Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Landscape_Integrity_Index "Forest Landscape Integrity Index") mean score of 4.2/10, ranking it 133rd globally out of 172 countries. Conservation International operates in Kenya through [Conservation International Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_International_Kenya "Conservation International Kenya"), a country programme founded in 2014 that reports work spanning nearly 4.9 million hectares, including forest and rangeland conservation and restoration, ecotourism, and climate-mitigation activities such as [REDD+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/REDD%2B "REDD+").

## Government and politics

Kenya is a [presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system "Presidential system") [representative democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy "Representative democracy") republic with a [multi-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system "Multi-party system"). The president is [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state "Head of state") and [head of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_government "Head of government"). [Executive power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_power "Executive power") is exercised by the government. [Legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_power "Legislative power") is vested in the government, the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Kenya "National Assembly of Kenya") and the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Kenya "Senate of Kenya"). The [Judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary "Judiciary") is independent of the executive and the legislature. There has been growing concern, especially during former president Daniel arap Moi's tenure, that the executive was increasingly meddling with the affairs of the judiciary.

Kenya has high levels of corruption according to [Transparency International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transparency_International "Transparency International")'s [Corruption Perceptions Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_Perceptions_Index "Corruption Perceptions Index") (CPI), a metric which attempts to gauge the prevalence of [public-sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_sector "Public sector") corruption in various countries. In 2019, the nation placed 137th out of 180 countries in the index, with a score of 28 out of 100. But there are several rather significant developments with regard to curbing corruption from the Kenyan government, for instance the establishment of a new and independent [Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission (EACC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics_and_Anti-Corruption_Commission "Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission").

Following general elections held in 1997, the Constitution of Kenya Review Act, designed to pave the way for more comprehensive amendments to the Kenyan constitution, was passed by the national parliament.

In December 2002, Kenya held democratic and open elections, which were judged free and fair by most international observers. The 2002 elections marked an important turning point in Kenya's democratic evolution in that power was transferred peacefully from the [Kenya African National Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_African_National_Union "Kenya African National Union") (KANU), which had ruled the country since independence, to the [National Rainbow Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Rainbow_Coalition "National Rainbow Coalition") (NARC), a coalition of political parties.

Under the presidency of [Mwai Kibaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mwai_Kibaki "Mwai Kibaki"), the new ruling coalition promised to focus its efforts on generating economic growth, combating corruption, improving education, and rewriting its constitution. A few of these promises have been met. There is free primary education. In 2007, the government issued a statement declaring that from 2008, secondary education would be heavily subsidised, with the government footing all tuition fees.

### 2013 elections and new government

Under the new constitution and with President Kibaki prohibited by term limits from running for a third term, Deputy Prime Minister [Uhuru Kenyatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uhuru_Kenyatta "Uhuru Kenyatta") ran for office. He won with 50.51% of the vote in March 2013.

In December 2014, President Kenyatta signed a Security Laws Amendment Bill, which supporters of the law suggested was necessary to guard against armed groups. Opposition politicians, human rights groups, and nine Western countries criticised the security bill, arguing that it infringed on democratic freedoms. The governments of the United States, the United Kingdom, Germany, and France also collectively issued a press statement cautioning about the law's potential impact. Through the Jubilee Coalition, the Bill was later passed on 19 December in the National Assembly under acrimonious circumstances.

### Foreign relations

Relations with Somalia have historically been tense, although there has been some military co-ordination against Islamist insurgents. Kenya has good relations with the United Kingdom. Kenya is one of the most [pro-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-American "Pro-American") nations in Africa, and the wider world.

With [International Criminal Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court "International Criminal Court") trial dates scheduled in 2013 for both President Kenyatta and Deputy President [William Ruto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Ruto "William Ruto") related to the 2007 election aftermath, US president [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama"), who is half-Kenyan, chose not to visit the country during his [mid-2013 African trip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_presidential_visits_to_Sub-Saharan_Africa#Table_of_visits "United States presidential visits to Sub-Saharan Africa"). Later in the summer, Kenyatta visited China at the invitation of President [Xi Jinping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi_Jinping "Xi Jinping") after a stop in Russia and not having visited the United States as president. In July 2015, Obama visited Kenya, the first American president to visit the country while in office.

The [British Army Training Unit Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army_Training_Unit_Kenya "British Army Training Unit Kenya") (BATUK) is used for the training of [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army "British Army") infantry battalions in the arid and rugged terrain of the [Great Rift Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Rift_Valley "Great Rift Valley").

### Armed forces

The Kenya Defence Forces are the armed forces of Kenya. The [Kenya Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Army "Kenya Army"), [Kenya Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Navy "Kenya Navy"), and [Kenya Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Air_Force "Kenya Air Force") compose the National Defence Forces. The current Kenya Defence Forces were established, and its composition laid out, in Article 241 of the 2010 [Constitution of Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Kenya "Constitution of Kenya"); the KDF is governed by the Kenya Defence Forces Act of 2012. The [President of Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Kenya "President of Kenya") is the [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief "Commander-in-chief") of all the armed forces.

The armed forces are regularly deployed in peacekeeping missions around the world. Further, in the aftermath of the national elections of December 2007 and the violence that subsequently engulfed the country, a commission of inquiry, the [Waki Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waki_Commission "Waki Commission"), commended its readiness and adjudged it to "have performed its duty well." Nevertheless, there have been serious allegations of human rights violations, most recently while conducting counter-insurgency operations in the [Mt Elgon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mt_Elgon "Mt Elgon") area and also in the district of [Mandera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandera "Mandera") central.

Kenya's armed forces, like many government institutions in the country, have been tainted by [corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_Kenya "Corruption in Kenya") allegations. Because the operations of the armed forces have been traditionally cloaked by the ubiquitous blanket of "state security", the corruption has been hidden from public view, and thus less subject to public scrutiny and notoriety. This has changed recently. In what are by Kenyan standards unprecedented revelations, in 2010, credible claims of corruption were made with regard to recruitment and procurement of armoured personnel carriers. Further, the wisdom and prudence of certain decisions of procurement have been publicly questioned.

### Administrative divisions

Kenya is divided into [47 semi-autonomous counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_Kenya "Counties of Kenya") that are headed by governors. These 47 counties form the first-order divisions of Kenya.

The smallest administrative units in Kenya are called [locations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locations_of_Kenya "Locations of Kenya"). Locations often coincide with electoral wards. Locations are usually named after their central villages/towns. Many larger towns consist of several locations. Each location has a chief, appointed by the state.

Constituencies are an electoral subdivision, with each county comprising a whole number of constituencies. An interim boundaries commission was formed in 2010 to review the constituencies and in its report, it recommended the creation of an additional 80 constituencies. Previous to the 2013 elections, there were 210 [constituencies in Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituencies_of_Kenya "Constituencies of Kenya").

### Human rights

[Homosexual acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosexuality "Homosexuality") are illegal in Kenya and typically punishable by up to 14 years in prison. According to a 2020 survey by the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center "Pew Research Center"), 83% of Kenyans believe that homosexuality should not be accepted by society. While addressing a joint press conference together with President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") in 2015, President Kenyatta declined to assure Kenya's commitment to gay rights, saying that "the issue of gay rights is really a non-issue... But there are some things that we must admit we don't share. Our culture, our societies don't accept."

In November 2008, [WikiLeaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WikiLeaks "WikiLeaks") brought wide international attention to *The Cry of Blood* report, which documents the extrajudicial killing of gangsters by the Kenyan police. In the report, the [Kenya National Commission on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_National_Commission_on_Human_Rights "Kenya National Commission on Human Rights") (KNCHR) reported these in their key finding "e)", stating that the [forced disappearances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_disappearance "Forced disappearance") and [extrajudicial killings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrajudicial_killing "Extrajudicial killing") appeared to be official policy sanctioned by the political leadership and the police.

## Economy

Kenya's [macroeconomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroeconomics "Macroeconomics") outlook has steadily posted robust growth over the past few decades mostly from road, rail, [air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-cost_carrier "Low-cost carrier") and [water transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_transport "Water transport") infrastructure projects as well as massive investments in [Information and Communication Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_and_communications_technology "Information and communications technology"). The Kenyan economy is the largest in East Africa. After independence, Kenya promoted rapid economic growth through public investment, encouraged smallholder agricultural production and provided incentives for private industrial investment. Kenya is East Africa's regional transportation and financial hub. Kenya's financial sector is vibrant, well developed and diversified boasting the highest financial inclusion in the region and globally.

Foreign investments in Kenya remain relatively weak considering the size of its economy and its level of development. As of 2022, Kenya's total FDI stock stood at US$10.4 billion, accounting for a mere 9.5% of the country's GDP.

Kenya has a [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index "Human Development Index") (HDI) of 0.628 (medium), ranked 143 out of 193 in the world as of 2023[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kenya&action=edit). As of 2022, 25.4% of Kenyans lived on less than $2.15 a day. Based on the most recent data from 2022, 25.4% of the population is affected by [multidimensional poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multidimensional_Poverty_Index "Multidimensional Poverty Index") and an additional 26.4% vulnerable to it. In 2017, Kenya ranked 92nd in the World Bank [ease of doing business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ease_of_doing_business_index "Ease of doing business index") rising from 113th in 2016 (of 190 countries). The important agricultural sector employs around 30% of the workforce. Kenya is usually classified as a [frontier market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontier_market "Frontier market") or occasionally an [emerging market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerging_market "Emerging market"), and is one of the [Developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_country "Developing country").

The economy has seen much expansion, seen by strong performance in tourism, higher education and [telecommunications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_in_Kenya "Telecommunications in Kenya") and decent post-drought results in agriculture, especially the vital tea sector. Kenya's economy grew by more than 7% in 2007, and its foreign debt was greatly reduced. This changed immediately after the disputed presidential election of December 2007, following the chaos which engulfed the country.

Telecommunications and financial activity over the last decade now comprise 62% of GDP. 22% of GDP still comes from the unreliable agricultural sector which employs 75% of the labour force (a characteristic of under-developed economies that have not attained [food security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_security "Food security")). A small portion of the population relies on food aid. Industry and manufacturing is the smallest sector, accounting for 16% of GDP. The services, industry and manufacturing sectors only employ 25% of the labour force but contribute 75% of GDP.
Kenya also exports textiles worth over $400 million under [AGOA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGOA "AGOA").

Privatisation of state corporations like the defunct Kenya Post and Telecommunications Company, which resulted in East Africa's most profitable company—[Safaricom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safaricom "Safaricom"), has led to their revival because of massive private investment.

As of May 2011[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kenya&action=edit), economic prospects were positive with 4–5% GDP growth expected, largely because of expansions in tourism, telecommunications, transport, construction, and a recovery in agriculture. The [World Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank "World Bank") estimated growth of 4.3% in 2012.

In March 1996 the presidents of Kenya, Tanzania, and Uganda re-established the [East African Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_Community "East African Community") (EAC). The EAC's objectives include harmonising tariffs and customs regimes, free movement of people and improving regional infrastructures. In March 2004, the three East African countries signed a [Customs Union Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs_union "Customs union").

Kenya has a more developed financial services sector than its neighbours. The [Nairobi Securities Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nairobi_Securities_Exchange "Nairobi Securities Exchange") (NSE) is ranked 4th in Africa in terms of market capitalisation. The Kenyan banking system is supervised by the [Central Bank of Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_Kenya "Central Bank of Kenya") (CBK). As of late July 2004, the system consisted of 43 commercial banks (down from 48 in 2001) and several [non-bank financial institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-bank_financial_institution "Non-bank financial institution") including mortgage companies, four savings and loan associations and several core foreign-exchange bureaus.

The inaugural Kenya Innovation Week (KIW) was started in 2021, from 6 to 10 December 2021, at the Kenya School of Government in Lower Kabete, Nairobi.

### Tourism

Tourism contributes around 6% to Kenya's economy.[Tourism in Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Kenya "Tourism in Kenya") is the third-largest source of foreign exchange revenue, behind diaspora remittances and agriculture. The Kenya Tourism Board is responsible for maintaining information pertaining to tourism in Kenya. The main tourist attractions are photo safaris through the [60 national parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Kenya "List of national parks of Kenya") and game reserves. Game reserves include [Maasai Mara Game Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maasai_Mara_Game_Reserve "Maasai Mara Game Reserve"), [Samburu National Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samburu_National_Reserve "Samburu National Reserve"), [Solio Game Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solio_Game_Reserve "Solio Game Reserve"), [Shaba National Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaba_National_Reserve "Shaba National Reserve"), [Buffalo Springs National Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Springs_National_Reserve "Buffalo Springs National Reserve") among others. Other attractions include the [wildebeest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildebeest "Wildebeest") migration at the [Masaai Mara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masaai_Mara "Masaai Mara"); historical mosques, and colonial-era forts at [Mombasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa "Mombasa"), [Malindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malindi "Malindi"), and [Lamu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamu "Lamu"); renowned scenery such as the white-capped [Mount Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kenya "Mount Kenya") and the [Great Rift Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Rift_Valley,_Kenya "Great Rift Valley, Kenya"); tea plantations at [Kericho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kericho "Kericho"); coffee plantations at [Thika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thika "Thika"); a splendid view of [Mount Kilimanjaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kilimanjaro "Mount Kilimanjaro") across the border into Tanzania; and the beaches along the [Swahili Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_Coast "Swahili Coast"), in the Indian Ocean. Tourists, the largest number being from Germany and the United Kingdom, are attracted mainly to the coastal beaches and the [game reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_reserve "Game reserve"), notably, the expansive [East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsavo_East_National_Park "Tsavo East National Park") and [Tsavo West National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsavo_West_National_Park "Tsavo West National Park"), 20,808 square kilometres (8,034 sq mi) to the southeast.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Agriculture

Agriculture is the second-largest contributor to Kenya's gross domestic product (GDP) after the service sector. In 2005, agriculture, including [forestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry "Forestry") and fishing, accounted for 24% of GDP, as well as for 18% of wage employment and 50% of revenue from exports. The principal [cash crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_crop "Cash crop") are tea, horticultural produce, and coffee. Horticultural produce and tea are the main growth sectors and the two most valuable of all of Kenya's exports. The production of major food staples such as [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize "Maize") is subject to sharp weather-related fluctuations. Production downturns periodically necessitate food aid—for example in 2004, due to one of Kenya's intermittent [droughts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Droughts "Droughts").

A consortium led by the [International Crops Research Institute for the Semi-Arid Tropics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Crops_Research_Institute_for_the_Semi-Arid_Tropics "International Crops Research Institute for the Semi-Arid Tropics") ([ICRISAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICRISAT "ICRISAT")) has had some success in helping farmers grow new [pigeon pea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigeon_pea "Pigeon pea") varieties instead of maize, in particularly dry areas. Pigeon peas are very drought-resistant, so can be grown in areas with less than 650 millimetres (26 in) annual rainfall. Successive projects encouraged the commercialisation of legumes by stimulating the growth of local seed production and agro-dealer networks for distribution and marketing. This work, which included linking producers to wholesalers, helped to increase local producer prices by 20–25% in Nairobi and Mombasa. The commercialisation of the pigeon pea is now enabling some farmers to buy assets ranging from mobile phones to productive land and livestock, and is opening pathways for them to move out of poverty.

Tea, coffee, sisal, pyrethrum, corn, and wheat are grown in the fertile highlands, one of the most successful agricultural production regions in Africa. Livestock predominates in the semi-arid savanna to the north and east. [Coconuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconuts "Coconuts"), [pineapples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineapples "Pineapples"), [cashew nuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cashew_nuts "Cashew nuts"), cotton, [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane "Sugarcane"), [sisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisal "Sisal"), and corn are grown in the lower-lying areas. Kenya has not attained the level of investment and efficiency in agriculture that can guarantee food security, and coupled with resulting poverty (53% of the population lives below the poverty line), a significant portion of the population regularly starves and is heavily dependent on food aid. Poor roads, an inadequate railway network, under-used water transport, and expensive air transport have isolated mostly [arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arid "Arid") and [semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid "Semi-arid") areas, and farmers in other regions often leave food to rot in the fields because they cannot access markets. This was last seen in August and September 2011, prompting the [Kenyans for Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenyans_for_Kenya "Kenyans for Kenya") initiative by the [Red Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Cross "Red Cross").

Kenya's [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation "Irrigation") sector is categorised into three organizational types: [smallholder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallholding "Smallholding") schemes, centrally-managed public schemes, and private/commercial irrigation schemes.

The smallholder schemes are owned, developed, and managed by individuals or groups of farmers operating as water users or self-help groups. Irrigation is carried out on individual or on group farms averaging 0.1–0.4 ha. There are about 3,000 smallholder irrigation schemes covering a total area of 47,000 ha.
The country has seven large, centrally managed irrigation schemes, namely Mwea, [Bura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bura_irrigation_and_Settlement_Project_(Kenya) "Bura irrigation and Settlement Project (Kenya)"), [Hola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hola,_Kenya "Hola, Kenya"), [Perkera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perkerra_River "Perkerra River"), West Kano, Bunyala, and [Ahero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahero "Ahero"), covering a total area of 18,200 ha and averaging 2,600 ha per scheme. These schemes are managed by the National Irrigation Board and account for 18% of irrigated land area in Kenya.
Large-scale private commercial farms cover 45,000 hectares, accounting for 40% of irrigated land. They use high technology and produce high-value crops for the export market, especially flowers and vegetables.

Kenya is the world's 3rd largest exporter of [cut flowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cut_flowers "Cut flowers"). Roughly half of Kenya's 127 flower farms are concentrated around [Lake Naivasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Naivasha "Lake Naivasha"), 90 kilometres northwest of Nairobi. To speed their export, Nairobi airport has a terminal dedicated to the transport of flowers and vegetables.

### Industry and manufacturing

Although Kenya is a low middle-income country, manufacturing accounts for 14% of the GDP, with industrial activity concentrated around the three largest urban centres of [Nairobi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nairobi "Nairobi"), [Mombasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa "Mombasa"), and [Kisumu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisumu "Kisumu"), and is dominated by food-processing industries such as grain milling, beer production, sugarcane crushing, and the fabrication of consumer goods, e.g., vehicles from kits.

Kenya also has a cement production industry. Kenya has an [oil refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_refinery "Oil refinery") that processes imported crude petroleum into petroleum products, mainly for the domestic market. In addition, a substantial and expanding [informal sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informal_sector "Informal sector") commonly referred to as *jua kali* engages in small-scale manufacturing of household goods, auto parts, and farm implements.

Kenya's inclusion among the beneficiaries of the US Government's African Growth and Opportunity Act ([AGOA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGOA "AGOA")) has given a boost to manufacturing in recent years. Since AGOA took effect in 2000, Kenya's clothing sales to the United States increased from US$44 million to US$270 million (2006). Other initiatives to strengthen manufacturing have been the new government's favourable tax measures, including the removal of duty on capital equipment and other raw materials.

In 2023, Kenya is in the process of constructing five industrial parks that will operate tax-free, with an anticipated completion date set for 2030. There are intentions to develop an additional 20 industrial parks in the future.

### Transport

Two trans-African automobile routes pass through Kenya: the [Cairo-Cape Town Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo-Cape_Town_Highway "Cairo-Cape Town Highway") and the [Lagos-Mombasa Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos-Mombasa_Highway "Lagos-Mombasa Highway"), so the country has an extensive road network of paved and unpaved roads. Kenya's railway system links the nation's ports and major cities, connecting it with neighbouring Uganda. There are 15 airports which have paved runways.

### Energy

The largest share of Kenya's electricity supply comes from geothermal energy, followed by hydroelectric stations at dams along the upper [Tana River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tana_River_(Kenya) "Tana River (Kenya)"), as well as the Turkwel Gorge Dam in the west. A petroleum-fired plant on the coast, [geothermal facilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_power_in_Kenya "Geothermal power in Kenya") at [Olkaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olkaria "Olkaria") (near Nairobi), and electricity imported from Uganda make up the rest of the supply. A [2,000 MW powerline from Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodo%E2%80%93Moyale%E2%80%93Suswa_High_Voltage_Power_Line "Sodo–Moyale–Suswa High Voltage Power Line") was completed in 2022.

Kenya's installed capacity increased from 1,142 [megawatts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megawatts "Megawatts") between 2001 and 2003 to 2,341 in 2016. The state-owned [Kenya Electricity Generating Company (KenGen)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Electricity_Generating_Company "Kenya Electricity Generating Company"), established in 1997 under the name of Kenya Power Company, handles the generation of electricity, [Kenya Electricity Transmission Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Electricity_Transmission_Company "Kenya Electricity Transmission Company") handles long distance distribution of electricity, while [Kenya Power and Lighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Power_and_Lighting "Kenya Power and Lighting") handles the electricity transmission and distribution system in the country. Shortfalls of electricity occur periodically, when drought reduces water flow. To become energy sufficient, Kenya has installed [wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_in_Kenya "Wind power in Kenya") and [solar power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_power_in_Kenya "Solar power in Kenya") (over 300 MW each), and aims to build a nuclear power plant by 2027.

Kenya has proven deposits of oil in [Turkana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkana_County "Turkana County"). [Tullow Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tullow_Oil "Tullow Oil") estimates the country's oil reserves to be around one billion barrels. Exploration is still continuing to determine whether there are more reserves. Kenya currently imports all crude petroleum requirements. Since 2013 the government resorted to importing processed white petroleum. [Kenya Pipeline Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Pipeline_Company "Kenya Pipeline Company") is the state-owned parastatal mandated with the storage and transportation of white petroleum. The company was recently privatised by the government with the government owning a minor stake. Kenya has no strategic reserves and relies solely on oil marketers' 21-day oil reserves required under industry regulations. Petroleum accounts for 20%-to-25% of the national import bill.

### Chinese investment and trade

Published comments on Kenya's [Capital FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/98.4_Capital_FM "98.4 Capital FM") website by Liu Guangyuan, [China's ambassador to Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Ambassador_to_Kenya "Chinese Ambassador to Kenya"), at the time of President Kenyatta's 2013 trip to Beijing, said, "Chinese investment in Kenya ... reached $474 million, representing Kenya's largest source of foreign direct investment, and ... bilateral trade ... reached $2.84 billion" in 2012. Kenyatta was "[a]ccompanied by 60 Kenyan business people [and hoped to] ... gain support from China for a planned $2.5 billion railway from the southern Kenyan port of Mombasa to neighbouring Uganda, as well as a nearly $1.8 billion dam", according to a statement from the president's office, also at the time of the trip.

Base Titanium, a subsidiary of Base resources of Australia, shipped its first major consignment of minerals to China. About 25,000 tonnes of [ilmenite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilmenite "Ilmenite") was flagged off the Kenyan coastal town of Kilifi. The first shipment was expected to earn Kenya about KSh.15–20 billion/= in earnings. In 2014, the Chinese contracted railway project from Nairobi to Mombasa was suspended due to a dispute over compensation for land acquisition.

### Vision 2030

In 2007, the Kenyan government unveiled [Vision 2030](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Vision_2030 "Kenya Vision 2030"), an economic development programme it hopes will put the country in the same league as the [Asian Economic Tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Asian_Tigers "Four Asian Tigers") by 2030. In 2013, it launched a National Climate Change Action Plan, having acknowledged that omitting climate as a key development issue in Vision 2030 was an oversight failure. The 200-page Action Plan, developed with support from the [Climate & Development Knowledge Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_%26_Development_Knowledge_Network "Climate & Development Knowledge Network"), sets out the Government of Kenya's vision for a 'low-carbon climate resilient development pathway'. At the launch in March 2013, the Secretary of the Ministry of Planning, National Development, and Vision 2030 emphasised that climate would be a central issue in the renewed Medium-Term plan that would be launched in the coming months. This would create a direct and robust delivery framework for the Action Plan and ensure climate change is treated as an economy-wide issue. Furthermore, Kenya submitted an updated, more ambitious NDC on 24 December 2020, with a commitment to abate greenhouse gases by 32 per cent by 2030 relative to the business-as-usual scenario and in line with its sustainable development agenda and national circumstances.

### Oil exploration

Kenya has proven oil deposits in [Turkana County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkana_County "Turkana County"). President Mwai Kibaki announced on 26 March 2012 that [Tullow Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tullow_Oil "Tullow Oil"), an Anglo-Irish [oil exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_exploration "Oil exploration") firm, had struck oil, but its commercial viability and subsequent production would take about three years to confirm.

Early in 2006, Chinese president [Hu Jintao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hu_Jintao "Hu Jintao") signed an oil exploration contract with Kenya, part of a series of deals designed to keep Africa's natural resources flowing to China's rapidly expanding economy.

The deal allowed for China's state-controlled offshore oil and gas company, [CNOOC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Offshore_Oil_Corporation "China National Offshore Oil Corporation"), to prospect for oil in Kenya, which is just beginning to drill its first exploratory wells on the borders of Sudan and the disputed area of [North Eastern Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Eastern_Province,_Kenya "North Eastern Province, Kenya"), on the border with Somalia and in coastal waters. There are formal estimates of the possible reserves of oil discovered.

### Action against pollution

In 2017, Kenya banned single-use plastic bags. According to the national environmental authority, 80% of the public has adhered to this ban. Subsequently, in 2020, the prohibition of single-use plastics was extended to protected areas, including parks and forests.

A law passed in July 2023 mandates companies to actively reduce the pollution and environmental impact caused by the products they introduce into the Kenyan market, either individually or through collective schemes. Unlike previous practices, businesses are now obligated to participate in waste collection and recycling initiatives, such as the Petco initiative established by the government in 2018.

### Labour and human capital

Kenya has a labour force of around 24 million and a total labour force participation rate of 74%, the unemployment rate in 2022 was estimated at 5.6%

According to the World Bank's 2019 Human Capital Index (HCI), which measured human capital of the next generation, Kenya ranked first in sub-Saharan Africa with an HCI score of 0.52. The index combined several key indicators, which are school enrolment, child survival, quality of learning, healthy growth and adult survival into a single index ranging between 0–1.

### Microfinance

More than 20 institutions offer business loans on a large scale, specific agriculture loans, education loans, and loans for other purposes. Additionally, there are:

* emergency loans, which are more expensive in respect to interest rates, but are quickly available
* group loans for smaller groups (four to five members) and larger groups (up to 30 members)
* women's loans, which are also available to groups of women

Out of approximately 40 million Kenyans, about 14 million are unable to receive financial service through formal loan application services, and an additional 12 million have no access to financial service institutions at all. Further, one million Kenyans are reliant on informal groups for receiving financial aid.

To mitigate this problem, the mobile banking service [M-Pesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-Pesa "M-Pesa") was launched in 2007 by [Vodafone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vodafone "Vodafone") and [Safaricom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safaricom "Safaricom"), in collaboration from the Financial Deepening Challenge Fund competition established by the UK government's Department for International Development. M-Pesa allows users to deposit, withdraw, transfer money, pay for goods and services (Lipa na M-Pesa), access credit and savings, all with a mobile device, has provided access to digital transactions to millions of Kenyans in poverty situation.

## Demographics

The census carried out by the [Kenya National Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_National_Bureau_of_Statistics "Kenya National Bureau of Statistics") in 2019 reported Kenya's population as 47,564,296. The country has a young population, with 73% of residents under 30 because of rapid [population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth "Population growth"), from 2.9 million to 40 million inhabitants over the last century.

Nairobi is home to [Kibera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kibera "Kibera"), one of the world's largest slums. The shantytown is believed to house between 170,000 and one million people. The UNHCR base in [Dadaab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadaab "Dadaab") in the north houses around 500,000.

### Ethnic groups

Kenya has a diverse population that includes many of Africa's major ethnoracial and linguistic groups. Although there is no official list of Kenyan ethnic groups, the number of ethnic categories and sub-categories recorded in the country's census has changed significantly over time, expanding from 42 in 1969 to more than 120 in 2019. Most residents are [Bantus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_peoples "Bantu peoples") (60%) or [Nilotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilotic_peoples "Nilotic peoples") (30%). [Cushitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cushitic_languages "Cushitic languages") groups also form a small ethnic minority, as do Arabs, Indians, and Europeans.

According to the Kenya National Bureau of Statistics (KNBS), the largest native ethnic groups in 2019 were the [Kikuyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kikuyu_people "Kikuyu people") (8,148,668), [Luhya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhya_people "Luhya people") (6,823,842), [Kalenjin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalenjin_people "Kalenjin people") (6,358,113), [Luo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luo_people "Luo people") (5,066,966), [Kamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamba_people "Kamba people") (4,663,910), [Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somalis "Somalis") (2,780,502), [Kisii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisii_people "Kisii people") (2,703,235), [Mijikenda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mijikenda_peoples "Mijikenda peoples") (2,488,691), [Meru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meru_people "Meru people") (1,975,869), [Maasai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maasai_people "Maasai people") (1,189,522), and [Turkana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkana_people "Turkana people") (1,016,174). The [North Eastern Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Eastern_Province,_Kenya "North Eastern Province, Kenya") of Kenya, formerly known as NFD, is predominantly inhabited by ethnic Somalis. Foreign-rooted populations include [Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omanis "Omanis"), [Asians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indians_in_Kenya "Indians in Kenya"), and [Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_people_in_Kenya "White people in Kenya").

### Languages

Kenya's ethnic groups typically speak their mother tongues within their own communities. The two [official languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language "Official language"), English and [Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_language "Swahili language"), are used in varying degrees of fluency for communication with other populations. English is widely spoken in commerce, schooling, and government. [Peri-urban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peri-urbanisation "Peri-urbanisation") and rural dwellers are less multilingual, with many in rural areas speaking only their native languages.

[British English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English "British English") is primarily used in Kenya. Additionally, a distinct local dialect, [Kenyan English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenyan_English "Kenyan English"), is used by some communities and individuals in the country, and contains features unique to it that were derived from local [Bantu languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_languages "Bantu languages") such as Kiswahili and [Kikuyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gikuyu_language "Gikuyu language"). It has been developing since colonisation and also contains certain elements of [American English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English "American English"). In January 2026, Kenya was ranked 3rd best English-speaking nation in Africa behind South Africa and Zimbabwe and 9th in the world according to English Proficiency Index (2025). [Sheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheng_slang "Sheng slang") is a Kiswahili-based [cant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cant_(language) "Cant (language)") spoken in some urban areas. Primarily a mixture of Swahili and English, it is an example of linguistic [code-switching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code-switching "Code-switching").

69 languages are spoken in Kenya. Most belong to two broad language families: [Niger-Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niger-Congo_languages "Niger-Congo languages") ([Bantu branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_languages "Bantu languages")) and [Nilo-Saharan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilo-Saharan_languages "Nilo-Saharan languages") ([Nilotic branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilotic_languages "Nilotic languages")), spoken by the country's Bantu and Nilotic populations respectively. The Cushitic and Arab ethnic minorities speak languages belonging to the separate [Afroasiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afroasiatic_languages "Afroasiatic languages") family, with the Indian and European residents speaking languages from the [Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_languages "Indo-European languages") family.

### Religion

Most Kenyans are [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christians "Christians") (85.5%), with 33.4% [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant "Protestant"), 20.6% [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Kenya "Catholic Church in Kenya") and 20.4% [Evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism "Evangelicalism"). The Catholic Church is the largest single Christian church in Kenya, with about 10 million members. The [Presbyterian Church of East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian_Church_of_East_Africa "Presbyterian Church of East Africa") has 3 million followers in Kenya and surrounding countries. There are smaller conservative [Reformed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformed_tradition "Reformed tradition") churches, the [Africa Evangelical Presbyterian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa_Evangelical_Presbyterian_Church "Africa Evangelical Presbyterian Church"), the [Independent Presbyterian Church in Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Presbyterian_Church_in_Kenya "Independent Presbyterian Church in Kenya"), and the [Reformed Church of East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformed_Church_of_East_Africa "Reformed Church of East Africa"). [Orthodox Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church "Eastern Orthodox Church") has 621,200 adherents. Kenya has by far the highest number of [Quakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaker "Quaker") of any country in the world, with around 146,300. The only [Jewish synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_synagogue "Jewish synagogue") in the country is in Nairobi.

Islam is the [second largest religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Kenya "Islam in Kenya"), comprising 11% of the population. 60% of Kenyan Muslims live in the [Coastal Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Province "Coast Province"), comprising 50% of the total population there, while the upper part of Kenya's [Eastern Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Province_(Kenya) "Eastern Province (Kenya)") is home to 10% of the country's Muslims, where they are the majority religious group. [Indigenous beliefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_traditional_religion "African traditional religion") are practised by 0.7% of the population, although many self-identifying Christians and Muslims [maintain some traditional beliefs and customs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_syncretism "Religious syncretism"). Nonreligious Kenyans are 1.6% of the population.

Some Hindus also live in Kenya. The numbers are estimated to be around 60,287, or 0.13% of the population.

### Health

Health care is one of the low-priority sectors in Kenya and was allocated 4.8% of the national budget in 2019/2020 or just 4.59% of GDP compared to high-priority sectors such as education which was allocated more than 25%. This is below the 4.98% average in [Sub-Saharan Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-Saharan_Africa "Sub-Saharan Africa") and 9.83% spent globally.

According to the National and County Health Budget Analysis FY 2020/21, the breakdown of county health expenditure was 58% on Policy Planning and Administrative Support Services, 28% on Curative and Rehabilitative Health Services, 8% on Preventive and Promotive Health Services and 7% on Other Programmes.

Health care is largely funded by private individuals and their families or employers through direct payments to [health care providers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care_provider "Health care provider"), to the [National Health Insurance Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Hospital_Insurance_Fund "National Hospital Insurance Fund") or to medical insurance companies. Additional funding comes from local, international and some government [social safety net](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_safety_net "Social safety net") schemes. Public hospitals are [fee-for-service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fee-for-service "Fee-for-service") establishments that generate large amounts of county and national government revenues making them highly political and corrupt enterprises.

Private health facilities are diverse, highly dynamic, and difficult to classify, unlike public health facilities, which are easily grouped in classes that consist of community-based (level I) services, run by [community health workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_health_worker "Community health worker"); dispensaries (level II facilities), run by nurses; health centres (level III facilities), run by [clinical officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_officer "Clinical officer"); sub-county hospitals (level IV facilities), which may be run by a clinical officer or a [medical officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_practitioner "General practitioner"); county hospitals (level V facilities), which may be run by a medical officer or a [medical practitioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consultant_(medicine) "Consultant (medicine)"); and national referral hospitals (level VI facilities), which are run by fully qualified medical practitioners.

[Nurses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nursing "Nursing") are by far the largest group of [front-line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front-line "Front-line") health care providers in all sectors, followed by clinical officers, medical officers, and medical practitioners. These are absorbed and deployed into government service in accordance with the Scheme of Service for Nursing Personnel (2014), the Revised Scheme of Service for Clinical Personnel (2020) and the Revised Scheme of Service for Medical Officers and Dental Officers (2016).

[Traditional healers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_healer "Traditional healer") ([herbalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbalist "Herbalist"), [witch doctors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witch_doctor "Witch doctor"), and [faith healers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith_healer "Faith healer")) are readily available, trusted, and widely consulted as practitioners of first or last choice by both rural and urban dwellers.

Despite major achievements in the health sector, Kenya still faces many challenges. The estimated [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy "Life expectancy") dropped in 2009 to approximately 55 years – five years below the 1990 level. The [infant mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality "Infant mortality") rate was high at approximately 44 deaths per 1,000 children in 2012. The WHO estimated in 2011 that only 42% of births were attended by a skilled health professional.

[Diseases of poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diseases_of_poverty "Diseases of poverty") directly correlate with a country's [economic performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy "Economy") and [wealth distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth_distribution "Wealth distribution"): In 2015/16, 35.6% of Kenyans lived below the poverty line. Preventable diseases like malaria, HIV/AIDS, pneumonia, diarrhoea, and malnutrition are the biggest burden, major child-killers, and responsible for much morbidity; weak policies, corruption, inadequate health workers, weak management, and poor leadership in the public health sector are largely to blame. According to 2009 estimates, [HIV/AIDS prevalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_HIV/AIDS_adult_prevalence_rate "List of countries by HIV/AIDS adult prevalence rate") is about 6.3% of the adult population. However, the 2011 UNAIDS Report suggests that the HIV epidemic may be improving in Kenya, as HIV prevalence is declining among young people (ages 15–24) and pregnant women. Kenya had an estimated 15 million cases of [malaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaria "Malaria") in 2006. [Tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculosis "Tuberculosis") is a major public health problem. The per capita incidence of TB in Kenya more than quadrupled between 1990 and 2015.

The 2024 Global Hunger Index gave Kenya a 25.0 score, indicating that the severity of hunger is "serious".

### Women

The [total fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate "Total fertility rate") in Kenya was estimated to be 4.49 children per woman in 2012. According to a 2008–09 survey by the Kenyan government, the total fertility rate was 4.6% and the contraception usage rate among married women was 46%. [Maternal mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternal_mortality "Maternal mortality") is high, partly because of [female genital mutilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Female_genital_cutting "Female genital cutting"), with about 27% of women having undergone it. However, this practice is on the decline as the country becomes more modernised, and in 2011 it was banned in Kenya. Women were economically empowered before colonialisation. By colonial land alienation, women lost access and control of land. They became more economically dependent on men. A colonial order of gender emerged where males dominated females.
Median age at first marriage increases with increasing education.
Rape, defilement, and battering are not always seen as serious crimes.
Reports of sexual assault are not always taken seriously.

### Youth

Article 260 of the [Kenyan Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Kenyan_constitutional_referendum "2010 Kenyan constitutional referendum") of 2010 defines youth as those between the ages of 18 and 34. According to the 2019 Population and Census results, 75 per cent of the 47.6 million population is under the age of 35, making Kenya a country of the [youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth "Youth"). Youth unemployment and underemployment in Kenya has become a problem. According to the Kenya National Bureau of Statistics (KNBS), approximately 1.7 million people lost their jobs as a result of the COVID-19 pandemic, which eliminated some informal jobs and caused the economy to slow. The Kenyan government has made progress in addressing the high youth unemployment by implementing various affirmative action programs and projects which include: the [National Youth Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Youth_Service_(Kenya) "National Youth Service (Kenya)"), The National Youth Enterprise Development Fund, The Women Enterprise Fund, *Kazi Mtaani, Ajira Digital, Kikao Mtaani*, *Uwezo fund*, Future Bora and *Studio mashinani* that [empower youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_empowerment "Youth empowerment"), offer job opportunities and to raise one's standard of living.

### Education

Children attend nursery school, or kindergarten in the private sector, until they are five years old. This lasts one to three years (KG1, KG2 and KG3) and is financed privately because there has been no government policy on pre-schooling until recently.

Basic formal education starts at the age of six and lasts 12 years, consisting of eight years in primary school and four in high school or secondary. Primary school is free in public schools and those attending can join a vocational youth/village polytechnic, or make their own arrangements for an apprenticeship program and learn a trade such as tailoring, carpentry, motor vehicle repair, brick-laying and masonry for about two years.

Those who complete high school can join a [polytechnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_technology "Institute of technology") or other technical college and study for three years, or proceed directly to university and study for four years. Graduates from the polytechnics and colleges can then join the workforce and later obtain a specialised higher diploma qualification after a further one to two years of training, or join the university—usually in the second or third year of their respective course. The [higher diploma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_diploma "Higher diploma") is accepted by many employers in place of a bachelor's degree and direct or accelerated admission to post-graduate studies is possible in some universities.

Public universities in Kenya are highly commercialised institutions and only a small fraction of qualified high school graduates are admitted on limited government-sponsorship into programs of their choice. Most are admitted into the social sciences, which are cheap to run, or as self-sponsored students paying the full cost of their studies. Most qualified students who miss out opt for middle-level diploma programs in public or private universities, colleges, and polytechnics.

In 2018, 18.5 per cent of the Kenyan adult population was illiterate, which was the highest rate of literacy in East Africa. There are very wide regional disparities: for example, Nairobi had the highest level of literacy at 87.1 per cent, compared to North Eastern Province, the lowest, at 8.0 per cent. Preschool, which targets children from age three to five, is an integral component of the education system and is a key requirement for admission to Standard One (First Grade). At the end of primary education, pupils sit the [Kenya Certificate of Primary Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Certificate_of_Primary_Education "Kenya Certificate of Primary Education") (KCPE), which determines those who proceed to secondary school or vocational training. The result of this examination is needed for placement at secondary school.

Primary school is for students aged 6/7-13/14 years. For those who proceed to the secondary level, there is a national examination at the end of Form Four – the [Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Certificate_of_Secondary_Education "Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education") (KCSE), which determines those proceeding to the universities, other professional training, or employment. Students sit [examinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Examinations "Examinations") in eight subjects of their choosing. However, English, Kiswahili, and mathematics are compulsory subjects.

The Kenya Universities and Colleges Central Placement Service (KUCCPS), formerly the Joint Admissions Board (JAB), is responsible for selecting students joining the public universities. Other than the public schools, there are many private schools, mainly in urban areas. Similarly, there are a number of international schools catering to various overseas educational systems.

Kenya was ranked 102nd in the [Global Innovation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Innovation_Index "Global Innovation Index") in 2025.

## Culture

The [culture of Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Kenya "Culture of Kenya") comprises multiple traditions from 42 (+) tribes. Kenya has no single prominent culture. It instead consists of the various cultures of the country's different communities.

Notable populations include the [Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_people "Swahili people") on the coast, several other [Bantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_peoples "Bantu peoples") communities in the central and western regions, and Nilotic communities in the northwest. The [Maasai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maasai_people "Maasai people") culture is well known to tourism, despite constituting a relatively small part of Kenya's population. They are renowned for their elaborate upper-body adornment and jewellery.

Kenya has an extensive music, television, comedy and theatre scene.

### Literature

[Ngũgĩ wa Thiong'o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ng%C5%A9g%C4%A9_wa_Thiong%27o "Ngũgĩ wa Thiong'o") is one of Kenya's best-known writers. His novel *[Weep Not, Child](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weep_Not,_Child "Weep Not, Child")* depicts life in Kenya during the British occupation. The story details the effects of the Mau Mau on the lives of Kenyans. Its combination of themes—[colonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonialism "Colonialism"), education, and love—helped make it one of the best-known African novels.

[M.G. Vassanji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.G._Vassanji "M.G. Vassanji")'s 2003 novel *The In-Between World of Vikram Lall* won the [Giller Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotiabank_Giller_Prize "Scotiabank Giller Prize") in 2003. It is the fictional memoir of a Kenyan of Indian heritage and his family as they adjust to the changing political climates in colonial and post-colonial Kenya.

Since 2003, the literary journal *[Kwani?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwani%3F "Kwani?")* has been publishing Kenyan contemporary literature. Kenya has also nurtured emerging versatile authors such as Paul Kipchumba (Kipwendui, Kibiwott) who demonstrate a pan-African outlook.

### Music

Kenya has a diverse assortment of popular music forms, in addition to multiple types of [folk music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") based on the variety of over 40 regional languages.

Drums are the most dominant instrument in popular Kenyan music. Drum beats are very complex and include both native rhythms and imported ones, especially the Congolese [cavacha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavacha "Cavacha") rhythm. Popular Kenyan music usually involves the interplay of multiple parts, and more recently, showy guitar solos as well. There are also a number of local hip-hop artists, including [Jua Cali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jua_Cali "Jua Cali"); Afro-pop bands such as [Sauti Sol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauti_Sol "Sauti Sol"); and musicians who play local genres like Benga, such as [Akothee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akothee "Akothee").

Lyrics are most often in Kiswahili or English. There is also some emerging aspect of [Lingala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingala_language "Lingala language") borrowed from Congolese musicians. Lyrics are also written in local languages. Urban radio generally only plays English music, though there also exist a number of vernacular radio stations.

*Zilizopendwa* is a genre of local urban music that was recorded in the 1960s, 70s, and 80s by musicians such as [Daudi Kabaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daudi_Kabaka "Daudi Kabaka"), [Fadhili William](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fadhili_William "Fadhili William"), and Sukuma Bin Ongaro, and is particularly enjoyed by older people—having been popularised by the Kenya Broadcasting Corporation's Kiswahili service (formerly called Voice of Kenya or VOK).

The Isukuti is a vigorous dance performed by the [Luhya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhya_people "Luhya people") sub-tribes to the beat of a traditional drum called the Isukuti during many occasions such as the birth of a child, marriage, or funeral. Other traditional dances include the [Ohangla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohangla "Ohangla") among the [Luo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luo_peoples "Luo peoples"), Nzele among the [Mijikenda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mijikenda_peoples "Mijikenda peoples"), [Mugithi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mugithi "Mugithi") among the [Kikuyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kikuyu_people "Kikuyu people"), and [Taarab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taarab "Taarab") among the [Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_people "Swahili people").

Kenya has a growing Christian gospel music scene. Prominent local gospel musicians include the [Kenyan Boys Choir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenyan_Boys_Choir "Kenyan Boys Choir").

[Benga music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benga_music "Benga music") has been popular since the late 1960s, especially in the area around [Lake Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Victoria "Lake Victoria"). The word *benga* is occasionally used to refer to any kind of pop music. Bass, guitar, and percussion are the usual instruments.

### Sports

Kenya is active in several sports, among them [cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket "Cricket"), [rallying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rallying "Rallying"), [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football "Association football"), [rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_union "Rugby union"), [field hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_hockey "Field hockey"), and [boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxing "Boxing"). The country is known chiefly for its dominance in [middle-distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle-distance_running "Middle-distance running") and [long-distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-distance_running "Long-distance running") athletics, having consistently produced Olympic and [Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Games "Commonwealth Games") champions in various distance events, especially in 800 m, 1,500 m, 3,000 m steeplechase, 5,000 m, 10,000 m, and the marathon. Kenyan athletes (particularly [Kalenjin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalenjin_people "Kalenjin people")), continue to dominate the world of distance running, although competition from Morocco and Ethiopia has reduced this supremacy. Some of Kenya's best-known athletes include the four-time women's [Boston Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Marathon "Boston Marathon") winner and two-time world champion [Catherine Ndereba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_Ndereba "Catherine Ndereba"), 800m world record holder [David Rudisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rudisha "David Rudisha"), former [marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathon_(sport) "Marathon (sport)") world record-holder [Paul Tergat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Tergat "Paul Tergat"), and 5000m Olympic gold medalist [John Ngugi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ngugi "John Ngugi"). Kenya's most decorated athlete is three-time Olympic gold medalist and eleven-time world marathon major champion, [Eliud Kipchoge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliud_Kipchoge "Eliud Kipchoge").

Kenya won several medals during the Beijing Olympics: six gold, four silver, and four bronze, making it Africa's most successful nation in the 2008 Olympics. New athletes gained attention, such as [Pamela Jelimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamela_Jelimo "Pamela Jelimo"), the women's 800m gold medalist who went on to win the [IAAF Golden League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAAF_Golden_League "IAAF Golden League") jackpot, and [Samuel Wanjiru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Wanjiru "Samuel Wanjiru"), who won the men's marathon. Retired Olympic and Commonwealth Games champion [Kipchoge Keino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kipchoge_Keino "Kipchoge Keino") helped usher in Kenya's ongoing distance dynasty in the 1970s and was followed by Commonwealth Champion [Henry Rono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Rono "Henry Rono")'s spectacular string of world record performances. Lately, there has been controversy in Kenyan athletics circles, with the defection of a number of Kenyan athletes to represent other countries, chiefly Bahrain and Qatar. The Kenyan Ministry of Sports has tried to stop the defections, but they have continued anyway, with [Bernard Lagat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Lagat "Bernard Lagat") being the latest, choosing to represent the United States. Most of these defections occur because of economic or financial factors. Decisions by the Kenyan government to tax athletes' earnings may also be a motivating factor. Some elite Kenyan runners who cannot qualify for their country's strong national team find it easier to qualify by running for other countries.

Kenya has been a dominant force in women's volleyball within Africa, with both the clubs and the national team winning various continental championships in the past decade. The women's team has competed at the Olympics and [World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIVB_Volleyball_Women%27s_World_Championship "FIVB Volleyball Women's World Championship"), though without any notable success. Cricket is another popular sport, also ranking as the most successful team sport. [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenyan_cricket_team "Kenyan cricket team") has competed in the [Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_World_Cup "Cricket World Cup") since [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Cricket_World_Cup "1996 Cricket World Cup"). They upset some of the world's best teams and reached the semi-finals of the [2003 tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Cricket_World_Cup "2003 Cricket World Cup"). They won the inaugural World Cricket League Division 1 hosted in Nairobi and participated in the World T20. They also participated in the [ICC Cricket World Cup 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICC_Cricket_World_Cup_2011 "ICC Cricket World Cup 2011"). Their current captain is [Rakep Patel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakep_Patel "Rakep Patel").

Rugby is increasing in popularity, especially with the annual Safari Sevens tournament. The [Kenya Sevens team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_national_rugby_sevens_team "Kenya national rugby sevens team") ranked 9th in the IRB Sevens World Series for the 2006 season. In 2016, the team beat Fiji at the Singapore Sevens finals, making Kenya the second African nation after South Africa to win a World Series championship. [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_national_football_team "Kenya national football team") was once also a regional powerhouse in football. However, its dominance has been eroded by wrangles within the now defunct [Kenya Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Football_Federation "Kenya Football Federation"), leading to a suspension by [FIFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA "FIFA") which was lifted in March 2007.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

In the motor rallying arena, Kenya is home to the world-famous [Safari Rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safari_Rally "Safari Rally"), commonly acknowledged as one of the toughest rallies in the world. First held in 1953, it was a part of the [World Rally Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Rally_Championship "World Rally Championship") for many years until its exclusion after the 2002 event owing to financial difficulties. Some of the best rally drivers in the world have taken part in and won the rally, such as [Björn Waldegård](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B6rn_Waldeg%C3%A5rd "Björn Waldegård"), [Hannu Mikkola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannu_Mikkola "Hannu Mikkola"), [Tommi Mäkinen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommi_M%C3%A4kinen "Tommi Mäkinen"), [Shekhar Mehta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shekhar_Mehta "Shekhar Mehta"), [Carlos Sainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Sainz_Sr. "Carlos Sainz Sr."), and [Colin McRae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_McRae "Colin McRae"). The Safari Rally returned to the world championship in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Safari_Rally "2021 Safari Rally"), after the 2003–2019 events ran as part of the [African Rally Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Rally_Championship "African Rally Championship").[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Nairobi has hosted several major continental sports events, including the [FIBA Africa Championship 1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIBA_Africa_Championship_1993 "FIBA Africa Championship 1993"), where [Kenya's national basketball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_national_basketball_team "Kenya national basketball team") finished in the top four, its best performance to date.

Kenya also has its own [ice hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey "Ice hockey") team, the Kenya Ice Lions. The team's home ground is the [Solar Ice Rink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_Ice_Rink "Solar Ice Rink") at the Panari Sky Centre in Nairobi, which is the first and largest [ice rink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_rink "Ice rink") in all of Africa.

[Kenya men's national field hockey team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team "Kenya men's national field hockey team") was considered one of the good teams in the world during 1960s and 1970s.

Kenya took 6th position in [1964 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Summer_Olympics "1964 Summer Olympics") hockey tournament and 4th in 1971 [Men's FIH Hockey World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men%27s_FIH_Hockey_World_Cup "Men's FIH Hockey World Cup").

### Cuisine

Kenyans generally have three meals in a day—breakfast (*kiamsha kinywa*), lunch (*chakula cha mchana*), and supper (*chakula cha jioni* or simply *chajio*). In between, they have the 10-o'clock tea (*chai ya saa nne*) and 4 p.m. tea (*chai ya saa kumi*). Breakfast is usually tea or porridge with bread, [chapati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapati "Chapati"), [mahamri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahamri "Mahamri"), boiled sweet potatoes, or [yams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yam_(vegetable) "Yam (vegetable)"). [Githeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Githeri "Githeri") is a common lunchtime dish in many households, while [Ugali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugali "Ugali") with vegetables, sour milk ([mursik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mursik "Mursik")), meat, fish, or any other stew is generally eaten by much of the population for lunch or supper. Regional variations and dishes also exist.

In western Kenya, among the [Luo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luo_(Kenya_and_Tanzania) "Luo (Kenya and Tanzania)"), fish is a common dish; among the [Kalenjin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalenjin_people "Kalenjin people"), who dominate much of the Rift Valley Region, mursik—sour milk—is a common drink.

In cities such as Nairobi, there are fast-food restaurants, including [Steers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steers_(restaurant) "Steers (restaurant)"), [KFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KFC "KFC"), and [Subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subway_(restaurant) "Subway (restaurant)"). There are also many fish-and-chips shops.

[Cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese_in_Kenya "Cheese in Kenya") is becoming more popular in Kenya, with consumption increasing particularly among the middle class.
