# Chris Hansen

**Christopher Edward Hansen** (born September 13, 1959) is an American television presenter and journalist. During his tenure as a [correspondent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correspondent "Correspondent") for *[Dateline NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dateline_NBC "Dateline NBC")*, he hosted the program's segment *[To Catch a Predator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Catch_a_Predator "To Catch a Predator")* (2004–2007), in which [online predators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_predator "Online predator") would be caught using [sting operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sting_operation "Sting operation").

After parting ways with NBC in 2013, he began hosting *Killer Instinct* on [Investigation Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigation_Discovery "Investigation Discovery") in 2015, a short-lived series that documented homicide investigations. That same year, he replaced [Matt Doran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Doran "Matt Doran") as the host of the syndicated series *[Crime Watch Daily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_Watch_Daily "Crime Watch Daily")*, which he hosted for two seasons and had his own segment similar to his *Dateline* feature, *Hansen vs. Predator*. He co-founded the [true crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_crime "True crime")–oriented streaming service TruBlu in 2020, for which he produced and starred in the web series *Takedown with Chris Hansen*.

Hansen has earned several accolades, including ten [Emmy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Award "Emmy Award"), five [Edward R. Murrow Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTNDA_Edward_R._Murrow_Award "RTNDA Edward R. Murrow Award"), three [Clarion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Women_in_Communications "Association for Women in Communications") awards, the [National Press Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Press_Club_(United_States) "National Press Club (United States)") award, and awards for excellence from the [Associated Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_Press "Associated Press") and [United Press International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Press_International "United Press International"). Hansen and numerous subjects of his investigations have garnered online [cult followings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult_following "Cult following"), and Hansen is considered a progenitor in content centered around sting operations.

In 2025, a documentary on his career, titled *[Predators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predators_(2025_film) "Predators (2025 film)")*, was distributed by [MTV Entertainment Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Entertainment_Studios "MTV Entertainment Studios"). In 2026, [A24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A24 "A24") released the film *[Primetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_(film) "Primetime (film)")*, starring [Robert Pattinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pattinson "Robert Pattinson") as Hansen. *Primetime* is based on Hansen's work on *To Catch a Predator*, and a 2009 *[Esquire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esquire_(magazine) "Esquire (magazine)")* story on the show, "Tonight on Dateline This Man Will Die".

## Early life and education

Christopher Edward Hansen was born in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago"), [Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois "Illinois"), on September 13, 1959, and grew up in [West Bloomfield Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bloomfield_Township,_Michigan "West Bloomfield Township, Michigan") and [Birmingham, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham,_Michigan "Birmingham, Michigan"). In an interview with *[City Pulse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Pulse "City Pulse")*, Hansen said that he first wanted to be a journalist when he was 14 years old, watching and following the police and [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI "FBI") investigation of the disappearance of [Jimmy Hoffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Hoffa "Jimmy Hoffa").

Hansen attended [Brother Rice High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brother_Rice_High_School_(Michigan) "Brother Rice High School (Michigan)") in [Bloomfield Hills, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomfield_Hills,_Michigan "Bloomfield Hills, Michigan"). Hansen graduated from [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University "Michigan State University") with a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") in [telecommunication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunication "Telecommunication") in 1981.

## Career

### 1981–2013: Career with NBC

In 1981, Hansen became a reporter for [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC "NBC") affiliate [WILX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WILX "WILX") in [Lansing, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lansing,_Michigan "Lansing, Michigan") during his senior year at Michigan State University. He later reported for [WFLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WFLA-TV "WFLA-TV") in [Tampa, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa,_Florida "Tampa, Florida"), various newspapers and radio stations in Michigan and [WXYZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WXYZ-TV "WXYZ-TV") in Detroit. In 1988, he was hired as an investigative reporter and anchor at [WDIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WDIV-TV "WDIV-TV") in Detroit. In May 1993, Hansen joined [NBC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_News "NBC News") as a correspondent for the short-lived news magazine *[Now with Tom Brokaw and Katie Couric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Now_with_Tom_Brokaw_and_Katie_Couric "Now with Tom Brokaw and Katie Couric")*.

#### *Dateline NBC*

Hansen's notable work for *Dateline* includes coverage of the [Columbine High School massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbine_High_School_massacre "Columbine High School massacre"), the [Oklahoma City bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma_City_bombing "Oklahoma City bombing"), the [Unabomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Kaczynski "Ted Kaczynski") and the [TWA Flight 800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TWA_Flight_800 "TWA Flight 800") disaster, and investigative reports on Indian child slave labor and counterfeit prescription drug sales in China. Hansen was responsible for most of *Dateline's* coverage of the [September 11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_attacks "September 11 attacks"), as well as stories on terrorist groups and the operations of [Al-Qaeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda "Al-Qaeda"). He also exposed how a group linked to [Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden "Osama bin Laden") had attempted to buy missiles and nuclear weapons components, and he also worked on an exclusive report on the [Air France Flight 8969 hijacking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_France_Flight_8969 "Air France Flight 8969"). His series on deficient airport security resulted in the [Federal Aviation Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Aviation_Administration "Federal Aviation Administration") investigating and ultimately revising its policies.

#### *To Catch a Predator*

In conjunction with the website [Perverted-Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perverted-Justice "Perverted-Justice"), Hansen hosted a series of *Dateline NBC* reports under the title *To Catch a Predator*. Volunteers from Perverted-Justice impersonated minors such as pre-teens to young-mid teenagers (usually 11–15 years old) in chat rooms online and agreed to meet with adults for sex. The meeting places were "sting houses", where camera crews from NBC, and in later episodes local police, awaited potential sexual predators.

Capitalizing on the success of Hansen and his *Predator* investigations, *[Dateline NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dateline_NBC "Dateline NBC")* created three Tuesday night spin-offs of its original concept; Hansen hosted *[To Catch a Con Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Catch_a_Con_Man "To Catch a Con Man")* and *To Catch an I.D. Thief*. In March 2007, Hansen's book, *To Catch a Predator: Protecting Your Kids from Online Enemies Already in Your Home*, was released in the American market.

In August 2013, NBC decided not to renew its contract with Hansen, ultimately ending his tenure of 20 years.

### 2015–present: Career post-NBC

#### Independent television shows

In 2015, Hansen hosted *Killer Instinct*, a show on [Investigation Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigation_Discovery "Investigation Discovery") chronicling homicide cases. The show's initial 10-episode season premiered on August 17, 2015.

In 2015, Hansen planned a new independent television show called *Hansen vs. Predator*, a spin-off of his original show *To Catch a Predator*. The program was intended to premiere online, crowdfunded via [Kickstarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickstarter "Kickstarter") with Hansen hoping to raise $400,000; however the Kickstarter only raised $89,000. Hansen offered promotional memorabilia as part of the campaign that donors claimed to have never received, and he was arrested in January 2019 for paying for $13,000 worth of promotional items with a [bad check](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_check "Bad check"). Later broadcast rights were sold, with *Hansen vs. Predator* becoming a recurring segment on *Crime Watch Daily*.

#### Work with *Crime Watch Daily*

On August 22, 2016, Hansen was introduced as the new host of the syndicated news program *[Crime Watch Daily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_Watch_Daily "Crime Watch Daily")* beginning with its second-season premiere on September 12. Hansen anchored the program from New York City while the program maintained its Los Angeles–based newsroom. The second season also saw the premiere of *Hansen vs. Predator*, a revival of his previous *To Catch a Predator* series. Hansen's entry brought further ratings gains to the program, with household ratings increasing by 20% to 1.0, and a 23% gain among women 18–34. On January 4, 2017, *Crime Watch Daily* was renewed for a third season. The show ended its run in June 2018. Chris Hansen debuted the first episode of *Hansen vs. Predator* on September 12, 2016, on *Crime Watch Daily*.

#### *Have a Seat with Chris Hansen*

On October 9, 2019, Hansen started a [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube") channel under the name *Have a Seat with Chris Hansen*, featuring weekly podcasts tackling different topics and interviews with special guests. In 2019, this channel investigated claims of pedophilia and grooming against YouTube creator James Jackson, commonly known as [Onision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onision "Onision"). In January 2020, Hansen visited Jackson's home in an attempt to interview him. Jackson called the police on Hansen, though the police did not take action against him. Jackson also filed a lawsuit against Hansen, which took place on January 24, 2020. Jackson later voluntarily dismissed the claims, claiming that he was advised to consider an "alternative, more effective legal action". This dismissal request was granted.

#### *Unseamly* & *Takedown with Chris Hansen*

Hansen was named as an executive producer for *Unseamly: The Investigation of Peter Nygård*, a [Discovery+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery%2B "Discovery+") show which examines [Peter Nygård](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Nyg%C3%A5rd "Peter Nygård")'s life from his birth to his downfall and arrest.

Hansen returned to television in 2022 with the series *Takedown with Chris Hansen* on his streaming service TruBlu, which once more finds him investigating and interviewing online predators. The show granted him greater creative control, allowing him to use profanity and work further with local law enforcement entities.

#### Podcasting

Since 2020, Hansen has hosted a podcast called *Predators I've Caught* in which he revisits the various predator investigations he has conducted from his perspective, in addition to offering any new information regarding the suspects of the investigations and answering submitted questions. Each installment covers a different investigation, with episodes released on a weekly basis.

#### *Dangerous Games: Investigating Roblox*

In August 2025, in the wake of the [Roblox Schlep ban controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roblox_Schlep_ban_controversy "Roblox Schlep ban controversy") in which the [YouTuber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTuber "YouTuber") Schlep was banned from [Roblox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roblox "Roblox") for conducting stings on alleged child predators, rumors spread on social media that Chris Hansen would create a project focusing on the state of [child safety on Roblox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_safety_on_Roblox "Child safety on Roblox"). Hansen confirmed the rumors on August 15, stating that he had interviewed law enforcement, abuse victims, and Schlep for a documentary film. Chris Hansen was interviewed by Schlep on August 22 about the documentary. During the discussion, he praised Schlep for his efforts against alleged child predators and said that he had "expanded on his plans" for the documentary. The documentary, *Dangerous Games: Investigating Roblox — A Chris Hansen Special*, was released on February 27, 2026 on the streaming platform TruBlu.

## Personal life

Hansen married his first wife, Mary Joan, in 1989, and raised two sons with her.

Mary Joan filed for divorce in 2018 after nearly thirty years of marriage; the couple divorced in 2020.

Hansen maintained a [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") apartment; however, in January 2019 it was reported that he was evicted after failing to pay rent for the apartment since the previous August.

Hansen's mother, Patricia Hansen, died on March 1, 2020.

On November 13, 2021, Hansen married his second wife, Gabrielle Gagnon.

### Legal issues

In January 2019, Hansen was charged with [larceny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larceny "Larceny") after multiple checks he had written to a vendor who delivered promotional items bounced. Hansen turned himself in to police in Connecticut on January 14, 2019, for allegedly bouncing checks for nearly $13,000 worth of promotional materials, according to [Stamford Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamford,_Connecticut#Police_Department "Stamford, Connecticut"). The charges were dropped after a fan of Hansen's—who had never met him—paid off the amount owed.

In relation to a sting operation that occurred in October 2020, Hansen was [subpoenaed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subpoena "Subpoena") to appear and present evidence in a [Shiawassee County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiawassee_County,_Michigan "Shiawassee County, Michigan") courtroom in Michigan on July 1, 2021. Hansen did not appear, and so a warrant was issued for his arrest. Hansen turned himself in the next day, stating that his failure to appear in court was because of a misunderstanding, and the matter was resolved.

## Awards and honors

Hansen has received 10 [Emmy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Award "Emmy Award") for his work in investigative reporting, outstanding coverage of a news story and outstanding coverage of breaking news.

He has received 5 [Edward R. Murrow Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTNDA_Edward_R._Murrow_Award "RTNDA Edward R. Murrow Award") for his news series investigating aviation security and safety as well as his coverage of the Ford/Firestone case.

He has won three [Clarion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Women_in_Communications "Association for Women in Communications") awards for his coverage of aviation security, Indian child slave labor and the Unabomber case, also receiving a first place medal for an undercover investigation of airport security.

Hansen has also been presented the [Overseas Press Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Press_Club "Overseas Press Club") award, an [IRE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigative_Reporters_and_Editors "Investigative Reporters and Editors") award, the [National Press Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Press_Club_(United_States) "National Press Club (United States)") award, and the [International Consortium of Investigative Journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Consortium_of_Investigative_Journalists "International Consortium of Investigative Journalists") award, as well as awards for excellence from the [Associated Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_Press "Associated Press") and [United Press International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Press_International "United Press International"), also receiving numerous Headliner awards. In 2015 The KidSafe Foundation honored Hansen with their Child Advocate of the year award, for raising awareness among the dangers of online child predators.

## In popular culture

### In television

Over the years, Hansen has both played himself and been parodied in various television series. In 2006, Hansen appeared as himself in the sitcom *[30 Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30_Rock "30 Rock")*, in the episode "[The Break-Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Break-Up_(30_Rock) "The Break-Up (30 Rock)")". In 2010, Hansen voiced himself in an episode of *[The Simpsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simpsons "The Simpsons")* titled "[Loan-a Lisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loan-a_Lisa "Loan-a Lisa")". In 2015, Hansen appeared on the hidden camera prank show *[Fameless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fameless "Fameless")*, where a victim is pranked into thinking he has been mistaken for an online predator by being catfished and is confronted by Hansen himself. In 2016, Hansen appeared as himself in an episode of the television show *[Black-ish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-ish "Black-ish")*, and had a cameo appearance in *[The Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Boys_(TV_series) "The Boys (TV series)")* episode "[The Big Ride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Big_Ride_(The_Boys_episode) "The Big Ride (The Boys episode)")" in 2020.

In the 2008 *[South Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Park "South Park")* episode "[Le Petit Tourette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Petit_Tourette "Le Petit Tourette")", [Eric Cartman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Cartman "Eric Cartman") links up with a parody version of Hansen to do a feature on his fake [Tourette syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourette_syndrome "Tourette syndrome"). Cartman agrees to interview with the intention of spouting hate speech on air, but once he realizes that he's lost the ability to censor himself after keeping the schtick going for so long he attempts to back out. Hansen then intimidates Cartman into going ahead with it by recounting an incident where a predator backed out of the *To Catch a Predator* series by shooting himself.

In 2010, Hansen was parodied in an episode of *[The Boondocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Boondocks_(TV_series) "The Boondocks (TV series)")* titled "[A Date with the Booty Warrior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Boondocks_season_3 "The Boondocks season 3")". In the episode, Hansen performs a sting operation just like in *To Catch a Predator*.

Hansen has been parodied in the television show *[Family Guy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Guy "Family Guy")* three times, first in the 2013 episode "[Valentine's Day in Quahog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentine%27s_Day_in_Quahog "Valentine's Day in Quahog")", where [Mr. Herbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_(Family_Guy) "Herbert (Family Guy)") is watching his favorite show, *[To Catch a Predator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Catch_a_Predator "To Catch a Predator")*. He has also appeared in "[Oscar Guy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Guy_season_21 "Family Guy season 21")" and "[Supermarket Pete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Guy_season_22 "Family Guy season 22")".

### In music

Hansen has been referenced in multiple songs, including "F.I.F.A.", by [Pusha T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pusha_T "Pusha T"), and "[Poppin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poppin_(KSI_song) "Poppin (KSI song)")", by [KSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KSI "KSI"). Most prominently, [Insane Clown Posse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insane_Clown_Posse "Insane Clown Posse")'s 11th studio album *[Bang! Pow! Boom!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bang!_Pow!_Boom! "Bang! Pow! Boom!")* features a track entitled "To Catch a Predator", with the lyrics featuring many phrases that Hansen used on the show.

### In film

Hansen featured in the documentary film [*Predators*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predators_(2025_film) "Predators (2025 film)") (2025), which covers *To Catch a Predator*, the imitations it inspired, and Hansen's subsequent work in true crime entertainment, including his founding of TruBlu. Hansen received the film positively. Hansen also appeared in independent filmmaker [Mike Clum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Clum "Mike Clum")'s documentary *The Sick Mind of EDP445*, in which he interviewed Bryant Moreland, known online as EDP445, a former [YouTuber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTuber "YouTuber") who lost his career after he was caught trying to meet minors for sex.

[Robert Pattinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pattinson "Robert Pattinson") portrayed Hansen in [Lance Oppenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Oppenheim "Lance Oppenheim")'s film *[Primetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_(film) "Primetime (film)")* (2026), which focuses on the origins of *To Catch a Predator* and its rise in television ratings. The film was released on September 25, 2026 by [A24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A24 "A24"). It was partly based on a 2009 *Esquire* article, "Tonight on Dateline This Man Will Die", which focused on the 2006 [suicide of Bill Conradt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_of_Bill_Conradt "Suicide of Bill Conradt"). Hansen called the film "an insult to the thousands of men and women who work hard every day to protect children from predators." Hansen also told *[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")* that he and Pattinson never met or talked about the role.
