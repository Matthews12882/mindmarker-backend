# Mirzapur (film)

***Mirzapur: The Movie*** is a 2026 Indian [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi "Hindi")-language [gangster drama film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangster_film "Gangster film") directed by Gurmeet Singh and written by Puneet Krishna. Produced by [Ritesh Sidhwani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritesh_Sidhwani "Ritesh Sidhwani") and [Farhan Akhtar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farhan_Akhtar "Farhan Akhtar") under [Excel Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excel_Entertainment "Excel Entertainment") and [Amazon MGM Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_MGM_Studios "Amazon MGM Studios"), the film stars [Pankaj Tripathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pankaj_Tripathi "Pankaj Tripathi"), [Ali Fazal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Fazal "Ali Fazal"), [Divyenndu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divyenndu "Divyenndu"), [Jitendra Kumar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jitendra_Kumar "Jitendra Kumar") and [Ravi Kishan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravi_Kishan "Ravi Kishan"). It acts as an interquel set during the first season of the [Amazon Prime Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Prime_Video "Amazon Prime Video") television series *[Mirzapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirzapur_(TV_series) "Mirzapur (TV series)")* (2018).

The film was theatrically released on 4 September 2026 and received largely positive reviews from critics. The film became a commercial success. It is the [7th highest-grossing Indian film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_films_of_2026#Box_office_collection "List of Indian films of 2026") and [5th highest-grossing Hindi film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Hindi_films_of_2026#Box_office_collection "List of Hindi films of 2026") of 2026.

## Plot

Babban, a druglord operating out of Jaisalmer in Rajasthan, steals a large order of opium from rival gang leader, Bhairav Singh. Bhairav tracks Babban down but he manages to escape with the help of his right-hand man, Birju Lohar. Meanwhile, in Mirzapur, Akhandanand aka Kaleen Bhaiyya is asked by the state Chief Minister's brother, JP Yadav, to deliver funds to the party for upcoming elections. Kaleen Bhaiyya hopes to increase the distribution of drugs in the state to raise money, but local druglords are colluding with JP Yadav against him, and will not immediately increase drug supply to his gang. Kaleen Bhaiyya entrusts this task to Bablu and Guddu Pandit.

Kaleen Bhaiyya's wife, Beena, visits her former lover, Birju, and conspires with him to assassinate her husband. Meanwhile, Bablu and Guddu remain at odds with Kaleen Bhaiyya's son, Munna, who resents their involvement in the family mafia operations. Guddu also has a crush on Sweety, not knowing that Munna chases after her too. Bablu wants to strike a favourable drug deal, hoping to ask Kaleen Bhaiyya for a portion of the empire for the brothers to run independently. Babban approaches their gang with an offer to sell opium, and Bablu secures a heavily discounted rate from him for their first purchase. In return, Babban asks that Kaleen Bhaiyya personally finalize the deal with him in Jaisalmer. He plans to murder Kaleen Bhaiyya in the desert by removing him from his stronghold of Mirzapur.

En route to meet Babban, Guddu buys some illegal steroid injections from a camel owner who races animals in local competitions. Guddu intends to use them due to his obsession with bodybuilding. Upon their arrival, Babban entertains Kaleen Bhaiyya's gang and uses the distraction to subdue his trusted bodyguard, Maqbool. Kaleen Bhaiyya is kidnapped, but Bablu manages to figure out his whereabouts. Guddu and Munna put aside their differences to rescue Kaleen Bhaiyya. A gunfight ensues between the two gangs, with Kaleen Bhaiyya's men aided by Bhairav Singh's gang. In the final fight, Bablu administers the steroids on Guddu, who succeeds in overpowering Babban. He then kills Babban on Kaleen Bhaiyya's orders, also avenging his mother who was slighted by Babban one afternoon in Mirzapur.

Kaleen Bhaiyya congratulates Bablu for the deal, but stops short of carving out an empire for the brothers. He instead wishes them to support Munna when he eventually takes over the gang. It is revealed that Babban was related to JP Yadav, who receives his body parts in a box, along with party funds as promised from Kaleen Bhaiyya. In an epilogue, Birju breaks into Kaleen Bhaiyya's house to stab him, but is subdued. Before he can implicate Beena, she shoots him dead, sacrificing her lover to guarantee her own safety inside the Tripathi household.

## Production

### Development

The film was officially announced by [Amazon MGM Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_MGM_Studios "Amazon MGM Studios"), with Gurmmeet Singh serving as [director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_director "Film director"), and the script is penned by Puneet Krishna, while [Excel Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excel_Entertainment "Excel Entertainment") managed the [production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filmmaking#Production "Filmmaking"). The [muhurat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhurat_shot "Muhurat shot") puja was held on 14 August 2025 at Excel office in Mumbai with the presence of the film's cast and crew.

### Casting

In August 2025, [Ravi Kishan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravi_Kishan "Ravi Kishan") joined the cast. In September 2025, [Mohit Malik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohit_Malik "Mohit Malik") was added to the cast. In the same month, [Jitendra Kumar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jitendra_Kumar "Jitendra Kumar") was cast to replace [Vikrant Massey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikrant_Massey "Vikrant Massey"), who exited the project. In October 2025, [Sonal Chauhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonal_Chauhan "Sonal Chauhan") and [Anjum Sharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anjum_Sharma "Anjum Sharma") were added to the cast.

### Filming

The script reading session was held on 15 August 2025. [Principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography "Principal photography") began the following month in [Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai "Mumbai"). Later production moved to [Varanasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varanasi "Varanasi") in October 2025 for its second schedule, which was completed within the same month. The next schedule was held in [Jaisalmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaisalmer "Jaisalmer") followed by [Jodhpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jodhpur "Jodhpur"). Following a short break, the team filmed a concurrent schedule in December 2025 in [Rajasthan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajasthan "Rajasthan") where Fazal's portions were filmed. Filming [wrapped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrap_(filming) "Wrap (filming)") in January 2026. The film was shot extensively in multiple locations including [Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai "Mumbai"), [Varanasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varanasi "Varanasi"), and various parts of [Rajasthan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajasthan "Rajasthan").

## Music

The soundtrack for *Mirzapur: The Movie* is the first [Excel Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excel_Entertainment "Excel Entertainment") film soundtrack to be released under the company's newly founded label, Excel Records, with distribution and licence partner [Universal Music India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Music_India "Universal Music India"), which had acquired a significant stake in the company the previous year, owning the publishing rights as a label partner. This also marked the end of Excel's nearly decade-long audio rights deal with [Zee Music Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zee_Music_Company "Zee Music Company"), which began with *[Baar Baar Dekho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baar_Baar_Dekho "Baar Baar Dekho")* (2016).

The first song from the soundtrack, "Do Numbari", performed by Haryanvi rapper Dhanda Nyoliwala, released on 3 August 2026.

The second song, "Vaaroon Forever", is an extended recreation of the song "Vaaroon" from the series' soundtrack, which [Zee Music Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zee_Music_Company "Zee Music Company") holds the rights to. Like the original, it was composed by Anand Bhaskar, written by Ginny Diwan and sung by Romy, with [Shreya Ghoshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreya_Ghoshal "Shreya Ghoshal") contributing the female vocals.

## Release

### Theatrical

In March 2026, [Amazon Prime Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Prime_Video "Amazon Prime Video") revealed its slate line-up for 2026. The film was theatrically released on 4 September 2026.

### Certification

The film received an [A certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Board_of_Film_Certification#Certificates_and_guidelines "Central Board of Film Certification") on 29 August 2026 by the [Central Board of Film Certification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Board_of_Film_Certification "Central Board of Film Certification") (CBFC) after several cuts. The CBFC's Revising Committee asked the makers to mute profanity in 10 places, modify a dialogue, replace a word in the first half of the film with the word, 'kamar' (meaning 'waist'), modify a word, 'lanth' (meaning 'idiot') in the second half of the film and modify a caste reference.

The makers were also asked to reduce the intimate scenes by 50% through suitable modifications in visuals and sound. This led to 35 seconds of intimate scenes in the film being deleted by the makers. None of the violent or gory scenes were censored by the CBFC.

The runtime of the film is 197.19 minutes.

### Legal proceedings

A PIL was filed before the Allahabad High Court on 3 September 2026 seeking a stay on the film's theatrical release over alleged obscenity, abusive language, excessive violence and derogatory references to Mirzapur district in Uttar Pradesh. The court declined to grant interim relief as the respondents had not been served with sufficient notice. Counsel for Excel Entertainment received the notice only on the evening before the film's scheduled release. The hearing was adjourned for four weeks and is expected to resume in October 2026.

## Reception

### Box office

*Mirzapur* grossed ₹324.75 crore (US$34 million) worldwide. It grossed ₹259.55 crore (US$27 million) in India and ₹65.2 crore (US$6.8 million) in [overseas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaspora "Diaspora") market.

On its opening day, the film earned ₹24.47 crore net at the domestic box office.

The film entered [100 Crore Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100_Crore_Club "100 Crore Club") on 7 September 2026, collecting over ₹100 crore domestic box office net.

On the [review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator "Review aggregator") website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes"), 80% of 20 critics' reviews are positive, with an average rating of 6.5/10.

A critic at *[Bollywood Hungama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bollywood_Hungama "Bollywood Hungama")* gave the film 4 stars out of 5, concluding, "On the whole, MIRZAPUR THE MOVIE is a [paisa-vasool](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/paisa_vasool "wikt:paisa vasool") big-screen entertainer that successfully transports the much-loved world of MIRZAPUR from [OTT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over-the-top_media_services_in_India "Over-the-top media services in India") to cinemas. The powerhouse performances, whistle-worthy dialogues, gripping confrontations, massy moments and a rousing finale compensate for the stretched portions and predictable nature of the narrative."

Rishabh Suri of *[Hindustan Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustan_Times "Hindustan Times")* also gave the film 4 stars out of 5, appreciating Gurmeet Singh's direction, Puneet Krishna's writing and him bringing out the 'peculiar flavour of the [Mirzapur universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirzapur_(TV_series) "Mirzapur (TV series)")' [world-building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worldbuilding "Worldbuilding") to the film and acting performances of the [ensemble cast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensemble_cast "Ensemble cast"). He also noted in his review, "It's the fact that the makers have ensured there is enough freshness by adding [subplots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subplot "Subplot"). The action is larger-than-life and, thankfully, has none of the [AI slop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_slop "AI slop") that many filmmakers might resort to, to cut costs. Shot largely at real-life locations, the film benefits from the scale and authenticity this brings."

Mayur Sanap of *[Rediff.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rediff.com "Rediff.com")* gave the film 4 stars out of 5 as well. With praise for the director of the film, he wrote, "Gurmmeet Singh keeps the film on track with his assured direction. He gives the film a bigger canvas without losing the earthy flavour of the series. The pacing is mostly smooth, the action set pieces are presented well, and he keeps the focus on the characters and drama rather than letting the scale take over."

Zinia Bandyopadhyay of *[Firstpost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firstpost "Firstpost")* gave the film 3.5 stars out of 5 and wrote, "What absolutely works in its favour is the cast. The chemistry between the actors remains almost exactly what it was eight years ago, when the show first landed. Yes, the storyline is long, stretching to 190 minutes, but the arcs are interesting and, importantly, nothing is left hanging. By the end, the threads come together while also hinting at a sequel that could take this story somewhere very different from the series."

Tanmayi Savadi of *[Times Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Now "Times Now")* also gave the film 3.5 stars out of 5 and summed up in her review, "With the massier and cinematic elements, Mirzapur: The Movie is a thoroughly engaging and thrilling watch that, at times, has edge-of-the-seat moments.

Nandini Ramnath of *[Scroll.in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scroll.in "Scroll.in")* had praise for the film, writing, "The film version stands on its own bloodied feet. The extraction of the show's most entertaining aspects while also introducing a couple of new arcs, and the decision to use the same actors with one notable exception, give *Mirzapur The Movie* both the element of comforting familiarity and the thrill of surprise.

Sana Farzeen of *[India Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_Today "India Today")* gave the film 3 stars out of 5, writing, "The humour still lands, the action packs a punch, and the Rajasthan portions look particularly gorgeous. There is even room for romance amid all the bloodshed, giving the otherwise dark world a different shade. But here is where *Mirzapur: The Movie* loses its grip. The makers are so invested in preserving the show's complicated relationships, layered characters and multiple storylines that they forget one small thing: this is a film, not a 10-episode season. There is simply too much going on."

Abhishek Srivastava of *[The Times of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times_of_India "The Times of India")* also gave the film 3 stars out of 5, concluding, "'Mirzapur: The Movie' loses the charm it builds in the first half, and whatever charm survives fades fast after the interval. Its grip on the setting is shaky, but the cast keeps things watchable even when the story doesn't. The film touches on [political corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_corruption "Political corruption") involving the chief minister's brother without ever really digging into how ugly that world gets. At 197 minutes, it overstays its welcome, and chopping thirty minutes of its runtime would have helped a lot. This one exists mainly to cash in on the show's popularity, and it never quite matches it. This is a film that serves primarily the fans of the series."

Sneha Hiro Tare of *[News18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News18 "News18")* gave the film 3 stars out of 5 as well and observed, "The set pieces, however, lack the real scale and impact you hope for on the big screen. The story itself stays thin and the film runs long. Some stretches drag and the pacing dips in the middle before the last half-hour picks up again. It is the towering background score that makes up for the technical limitations."

[Shubhra Gupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shubhra_Gupta "Shubhra Gupta") of *[The Indian Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Indian_Express "The Indian Express")*, too, gave the film 3 stars out of 5 and concluded in her review, "Net net, I quite enjoyed Mirzapur The Movie because it knows how to stay in its lane, papering over its flabby bits with continual speedy spurts and an intriguing [climax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climax_(narrative) "Climax (narrative)"). I know how things will pan out, but I’m still in for the ride. Will there be a Mirzapur The Movie part 2? Questions, questions."

Rahul Desai of *[The Hollywood Reporter India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter_India "The Hollywood Reporter India")* wrote, "The thing about *Mirzapur: The Movie* is that it seems to know that it's a film wooing its OTT loyalists to the big screen. It arrives in a landscape that suffers from the very genre fatigue it kickstarted; every second show now uses the [testosterone-dominated excesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypermasculinity "Hypermasculinity") of *Mirzapur* as a blueprint. Initially, it straddles the awkward bridge between the induction of new fans and the service of old ones (if that sounds like a cheap innuendo, blame the movie). There are plenty of clunky expository exchanges and 'intro' moments — you know, where either Kaleen or the two brothers or Babban are carrying out gruesome executions on traitors; where power struggles are spelt out; where everyone is not as edgy as they should be. But the movie comes into its own only as a tribute to itself: when Munna is being the OG heir yearning for papa's love before [Ranbir Kapoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranbir_Kapoor "Ranbir Kapoor") in *[Animal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_(2023_Indian_film) "Animal (2023 Indian film)")*; when the colourful language (the b-words and c-words) makes a hero-style comeback on the right lips; and when misogynistic gangsters face off against moral gangsters. It leans into the [fan service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fan_service "Fan service"), treating gestures and threats and abuses as musical cameos from a bygone era.

[Saibal Chatterjee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saibal_Chatterjee "Saibal Chatterjee") of *[NDTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDTV "NDTV")* gave the film 2.5 stars out of 5, summing up in his review, "A *Mirzapur* movie franchise might not be such a bad idea provided the makers find enough narrative meat to pad it with."
