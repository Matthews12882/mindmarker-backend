# Dancing with the Stars (American TV series)

***Dancing with the Stars*** is an American [reality dance competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reality_competition "Reality competition") television series that premiered on [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company "American Broadcasting Company") on June 1, 2005. It is the American version of the British contest program *[Strictly Come Dancing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strictly_Come_Dancing "Strictly Come Dancing")* and is part of [its international franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars "Dancing with the Stars"). The series pairs celebrities with professional dancers to perform primarily [ballroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballroom_dance "Ballroom dance") and [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_dance "Latin dance") routines. Each couple competes against the others for judges' points and audience votes. The couple receiving the lowest combined total of judges' points and audience votes is usually eliminated each week until only the champion dance pair remains. Since the thirty-second season in 2023, the series has been hosted by [Alfonso Ribeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_Ribeiro "Alfonso Ribeiro") and [Julianne Hough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julianne_Hough "Julianne Hough"), with [Carrie Ann Inaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrie_Ann_Inaba "Carrie Ann Inaba"), [Derek Hough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_Hough "Derek Hough"), and [Bruno Tonioli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Tonioli "Bruno Tonioli") serving as judges.

In April 2022, it was announced that, beginning with the [thirty-first season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_31 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 31"), *Dancing with the Stars* would move from ABC to [Disney+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney%2B "Disney+"). However, for [season thirty-two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_32 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 32"), the series returned to ABC while continuing to be streamed simultaneously on Disney+. The [thirty-fifth season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_35 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 35") premiered in September 2026.

## Cast

### Hosts

[Tom Bergeron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Bergeron "Tom Bergeron") (who was also hosting *[America's Funniest Home Videos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America%27s_Funniest_Home_Videos "America's Funniest Home Videos")*, also on [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company "American Broadcasting Company"), at the time of the show's premiere) was the host for the show's first 28 seasons, beginning with its premiere in 2005. In season 1, his co-host was [Lisa Canning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Canning "Lisa Canning"). She was subsequently replaced by [Samantha Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samantha_Harris "Samantha Harris") for seasons 2 through 9 (2006–2009), who was then replaced by [Brooke Burke-Charvet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooke_Burke "Brooke Burke") from seasons 10 through 17 (2010–2013). [Erin Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erin_Andrews "Erin Andrews") served as co-host from season 18 through 28 (2014–2019).

On July 13, 2020, Bergeron announced in a tweet that he had been dismissed from the series. ABC and BBC Studios made an official announcement shortly afterward saying Andrews would also be leaving the program. The following day, model and host [Tyra Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyra_Banks "Tyra Banks") was announced to be joining the show as host in addition to serving as an executive producer for the [twenty-ninth season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_29 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 29"). On July 14, 2022, it was announced that [Alfonso Ribeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_Ribeiro "Alfonso Ribeiro") would join Banks as co-host for the [thirty-first season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_31 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 31"). On March 17, 2023, it was revealed that Banks would be leaving the show prior to the [thirty-second season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_32 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 32"). Three days later, it was announced that [Julianne Hough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julianne_Hough "Julianne Hough") would be joining as co-host for season 32, while Ribeiro was elevated to main host.

### Judges

The regular judges included [Len Goodman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Len_Goodman "Len Goodman"), who served as head judge, [Carrie Ann Inaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrie_Ann_Inaba "Carrie Ann Inaba"), and [Bruno Tonioli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Tonioli "Bruno Tonioli"). Goodman was absent for much of [season 19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_19 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 19"), the entirety of [season 21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_21 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 21"), much of [season 23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_23 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 23"), and the entirety of [season 29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_29 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 29"). [Julianne Hough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julianne_Hough "Julianne Hough"), who had been a professional dancing partner for seasons 4 through 8, was added as a full-time judge for seasons 19 through 21, after having been a guest judge in the previous two seasons. She did not return for season 22, but she did return as a full-time judge for seasons 23 and 24, but did not return subsequently. Julianne's brother, [Derek Hough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_Hough "Derek Hough"), replaced Len Goodman for season 29, as Goodman was unable to travel from London to Los Angeles due to [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19 "COVID-19") travel restrictions, though he was able to "share his ballroom expertise" during the season via pre-taped clips. Goodman announced during the season 31 semifinals that he would be retiring from the show at the end of the season.

Following Goodman's death in April 2023, the Mirrorball Trophy was renamed the Len Goodman Mirrorball Trophy beginning in [season 32](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_32 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 32").

### Musicians

For 17 seasons, the [Harold Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Wheeler_(musician) "Harold Wheeler (musician)") orchestra and singers provided the live music for the show. Starting in season 18, [Ray Chew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Chew "Ray Chew") replaced Wheeler as the new band leader, bringing with him a new group of instrumentalists and singers.

### Professional dancers

Each season, celebrities are paired with professional dance partners who instruct them in the various dance styles, design their choreography, and perform with them each week in the competition.

### Dance troupe

In season 12, the show introduced the dance troupe, which consisted of professional dancers who performed on the show but were not paired with celebrity partners. The first troupe in season 12 included [Oksana Dmytrenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oksana_Dmytrenko "Oksana Dmytrenko"), [Tristan MacManus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tristan_MacManus "Tristan MacManus"), [Peta Murgatroyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peta_Murgatroyd "Peta Murgatroyd"), Kiki Nyemchek, Nicole Volynets, and Ted Volynets.

The troupe was eliminated in seasons 28, 29, 30 and 32. For season 33, Marcquet Hill, Roman Nevinchanyi, Kailyn Rogers, and Stephani Sosa joined the show as first time troupe members. For season 34, Hailey Bills, Onye Stevenson, Carter Williams, and Jaxon Willard joined the show as first-time troupe members. For season 35, [Kamri Peterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamri_Peterson "Kamri Peterson"), a former pro on *[Dancing with the Stars: Juniors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars:_Juniors "Dancing with the Stars: Juniors")*, joined the troupe as a first-time member. In addition, Jaxon Willard, Carter Williams, and Onye Stevenson returned as second-year troupe members.

Other former troupe members include Brandon Armstrong, Lindsay Arnold, Alan Bersten, Sharna Burgess, Henry Byalikov, Witney Carson, Brittany Cherry, Daria Chesnokova, Artem Chigvintsev, Sasha Farber, Shannon Holtzapffel, Dennis Jauch, Jenna Johnson, Kiril Kulish, Tristan MacManus, Keo Motsepe, Sonny Fredie-Pedersen, Gleb Savchenko, Emma Slater, Julz Tocker, Artur Adamski, Hayley Erbert, Britt Stewart, Morgan Larson, Vladislav Kvartin, D'Angelo Castro, Kateryna Klishyna, Ezra Sosa, and [Alexis Warr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexis_Warr "Alexis Warr").

## Scoring and voting procedure

In the first two seasons, only the overall ranking between competitors by the judges and the public was relevant. In the third and subsequent seasons, the scoring system has also made the exact scores relevant.

The scoring begins with the judges' marks. Each judge gives a numeric score from 1 to 10, for a total score of 3 to 30 or 40. The scoring was altered for the "all-star" season 15, during which judges could give scores at 1/2-point intervals from 0.5 to 10, for a total score of 1.5 to 30. When multiple performances are scored, only the cumulative total counts. The contestants' judges' shares are calculated as the percentage of the total number of points awarded to all contestants that evening. (For example, if a team earned 20 points on a night when the judges awarded 200 points, their judges' share would be 20/200 = 10%.) This percentage is then added to the percentage of North American votes received by each contestant. The two couples with the lowest scores are identified at the end of the show, and the couple with the lowest combined total gets eliminated. Season 8 added an occasional "dance-off", in which contestants could re-perform one of their dances, to improve their judges' score. This was later discontinued.

Public voting is conducted via a toll-free number, the ABC website, and, most recently, text messages and Facebook; contestants can vote during and immediately after each performance show. The maximum number of votes per voter per medium is equal to the number of couples performing that night, or five votes, whichever is larger. In April 2010, it was revealed that former contestant [Kate Gosselin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Gosselin "Kate Gosselin") had e-mailed friends and family asking them to vote as many as 10 times each. In November 2010, *The Washington Post* reported that online voting appeared not to require a valid email address, and accordingly that numerous votes apparently could be cast by one person.

In several cases where [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN "ESPN") coverage of *[Monday Night Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monday_Night_Football "Monday Night Football")* airs instead on an ABC affiliate in an [NFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL "NFL") team's home market, the program is delayed to air immediately after that station's local news, *[Jimmy Kimmel Live!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Kimmel_Live! "Jimmy Kimmel Live!")*, and *[Nightline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightline "Nightline")*, and a voting window confined only to the [area codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_code "Area code") of the pre-empted market is opened up to allow affected viewers to still put their votes in for the competition, though this is on a market-by-market basis (in some markets, an alternate sister station or [digital subchannel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_subchannel "Digital subchannel") carries the program live as scheduled).

Seasons 1 and 3 featured only two couples in the final week instead of three. Starting with season 16, four couples made it into the final week, although the top three finalists proceeded to dance one more time for the judges the next night after the fourth-place couple was announced. In seasons 20, 22, 24, and 26 there were three couples in the final week, but in seasons 21, 23, 25, 27–31 there were four, however in season 32, there was a change and five couples made it to the final.

Starting in season 28, the two couples with the lowest combined total of judges' scores and viewer voting percentages are in the bottom two and in jeopardy of being eliminated. The judges can save one of the bottom two couples from elimination in these seasons so that more talented competitors are kept. In situations of a double elimination in which three teams are at risk of elimination, the couple with the lowest combined total of judges' scores and viewer votes is eliminated, and the other two couples will face the judges' decision, who have to vote to save one couple from elimination. However, for season 32, this was removed and reverted to the original rules used in the first 27 seasons.

## General information

### Payment

On the April 18, 2006, episode of the *[Howard Stern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Stern "Howard Stern") [Radio Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Stern_Radio_Show "Howard Stern Radio Show")*, Stern's wife [Beth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beth_Ostrosky_Stern "Beth Ostrosky Stern") said that she was guaranteed to earn $125,000 for just appearing on *DWTS* (in season 3) and could earn up to more than double the original sum, depending on how long she lasted on the program.

In season 21, [Bindi Irwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bindi_Irwin "Bindi Irwin") had her payments withheld by a [Los Angeles County Superior Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_County_Superior_Court "Los Angeles County Superior Court") judge because she was a minor, which therefore required her parents to sign-off on the contract. But although [her mother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terri_Irwin "Terri Irwin") signed, the contract lacked her father's signature, so the judge refused to validate the contract, despite Irwin's father being naturalist and television personality [Steve Irwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Irwin "Steve Irwin"), who died in a stingray incident in 2006. The judge later validated the contract once Steve Irwin's death certificate was presented to the court, resulting in Bindi receiving a $350,000 paycheck from the show.

### Withdrawals

The first person to withdraw from competition was [Romeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romeo_Miller "Romeo Miller") in [season 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_2 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 2"). His father, [Master P](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_P "Master P"), replaced him in the competition before the beginning of broadcasts, being partnered with [Ashly DelGrosso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashly_DelGrosso "Ashly DelGrosso"). However, Romeo later competed in [season 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_12 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 12") and finished in 5th place. He was partnered with Chelsie Hightower.

On week six in [season three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_3 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 3"), [Sara Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Evans "Sara Evans") withdrew during the competition, citing her divorce as the reason for her departure. No one was eliminated that week. Another withdrawal occurred during preparations for [season four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_4 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 4") on February 28, when [Vincent Pastore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Pastore "Vincent Pastore") withdrew from the competition after only one week of training. Pastore said he did not realize how much work was needed during a ten-week period, and that he was not up to the physical demands of the show. He was replaced on March 2 by actor [John Ratzenberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ratzenberger "John Ratzenberger") who was partnered with [Edyta Śliwińska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edyta_%C5%9Aliwi%C5%84ska "Edyta Śliwińska").

In season seven, [Misty May-Treanor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misty_May-Treanor "Misty May-Treanor") withdrew from the competition in week three, after rupturing her [Achilles tendon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achilles_tendon "Achilles tendon") when rehearsing her [jive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jive_(dance) "Jive (dance)") with her partner, Maksim Chmerkovskiy. She did not perform the routine at all nor was she scored for it; no one else was eliminated that week.

In season eight, [Jewel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewel_(singer) "Jewel (singer)") and [Nancy O'Dell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_O%27Dell "Nancy O'Dell") were injured before the season began and could not compete. Jewel was diagnosed with fractured [tibias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibia "Tibia") in both legs; she would perform "[Somewhere Over the Rainbow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somewhere_Over_the_Rainbow "Somewhere Over the Rainbow")" on a results show. O'Dell suffered from a torn knee cartilage. They were replaced by [Holly Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holly_Madison "Holly Madison") and [Melissa Rycroft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melissa_Rycroft "Melissa Rycroft") who would be dancing with their partners for the rest of the season ([Dmitry Chaplin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Chaplin "Dmitry Chaplin") and [Tony Dovolani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Dovolani "Tony Dovolani")).

[Tom DeLay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_DeLay "Tom DeLay"), in [season nine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_9 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 9"), withdrew in week three of competition due to a full stress fracture that had developed in both feet from an earlier pre-stress fracture in one foot. DeLay was declared safe before he announced his withdrawal during the October 6, 2009, results show. [Debi Mazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debi_Mazar "Debi Mazar") was still eliminated that night despite DeLay's departure.

In [season sixteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_16 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 16"), Olympic figure skating champion [Dorothy Hamill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothy_Hamill "Dorothy Hamill") had to withdraw from the competition due to doctor's orders for a spinal injury. A cyst had developed near her lower spine, and was pinching off the nerve in that area. Either boxing champion [Victor Ortiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Ortiz "Victor Ortiz") or reality television star [Lisa Vanderpump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Vanderpump "Lisa Vanderpump") would have been eliminated, but Hamill withdrew before the results could be announced, meaning that no one was eliminated that week.

In [season eighteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_18 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 18"), week three, actor [Billy Dee Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Dee_Williams "Billy Dee Williams") withdrew, by advice from a doctor, due to a chronic back problem, which resulted in no elimination that week.

In [season twenty-one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_21 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 21"), week three, reality television star [Kim Zolciak-Biermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Zolciak "Kim Zolciak") was forced to withdraw from the competition after suffering a blood clot which resulted in a mini stroke, which resulted in no elimination that week. [Tamar Braxton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamar_Braxton "Tamar Braxton") also withdrew from the season due to blood clots in her lungs making it the first season to have two withdrawals in it.

In [season twenty eight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_28 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 28"), [Christie Brinkley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christie_Brinkley "Christie Brinkley") withdrew from the competition a week before the season premiere, due to injuring her arm during rehearsal and needing sudden surgery. She was replaced by her daughter, [Sailor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailor_Brinkley_Cook "Sailor Brinkley Cook"), with only a few days to practice prior to the season premiere. Later that season, [Ray Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Lewis "Ray Lewis") withdrew from the competition in the third week due to a toe injury he sustained during rehearsal that needed surgery.

In [Season 29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_29 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 29") during the 8th week, television host [Jeannie Mai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeannie_Mai "Jeannie Mai") withdrew from the show after being hospitalized for [epiglottitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiglottitis "Epiglottitis"). As a result, the double elimination that was supposed to happen did not take place. Only one couple, Chrishell Stause and Gleb Savchenko, were eliminated that night.

In [Season 31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_31 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 31") during the 5th week, movie star [Selma Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selma_Blair "Selma Blair") withdrew from the show to prevent her health from further deteriorating due to her [multiple sclerosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_sclerosis "Multiple sclerosis"). This marks the first time where a celebrity got the first perfect score of the season and withdrew on the same episode. As a result, there was no elimination that first night of the 5th week.

## Special episodes

### Season 1 Dance Off

Following controversy over Kelly Monaco surging from behind to win the first mirror ball trophy over the consistently strong John O'Hurley, ABC arranged for a "Dance Off" episode for a rematch on September 20, 2005. Both contestants were reunited with their professional dance partners, Alec Mazo (Monaco) and Charlotte Jørgensen (O'Hurley). Judges Len Goodman, Carrie Ann Inaba, and Bruno Tonioli awarded O'Hurley and Jørgensen with 77 points and Monaco and Mazo with 74, but for this specific competition only the audience vote counted. As announced by Tom Bergeron on September 22, the audience vote gave the rematch victory to O'Hurley with a slim 1% edge.

Many considered this rematch a mistake and Kelly Monaco retains her title as the winner of season 1. A rematch episode was never staged again.

## Merchandise, tours, and spin-offs

### Cardio dance DVDs

A DVD titled *Dancing with the Stars: Cardio Dance* was released on April 3, 2007, featuring [Kym Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kym_Johnson "Kym Johnson"), [Maksim Chmerkovskiy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maksim_Chmerkovskiy "Maksim Chmerkovskiy") and [Ashly DelGrosso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashly_DelGrosso "Ashly DelGrosso"). The program contains [cardiovascular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiovascular "Cardiovascular") workouts adapted from [cha-cha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cha-cha-cha_(dance) "Cha-cha-cha (dance)"), [paso doble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paso_doble "Paso doble"), [samba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samba_(ballroom_dance) "Samba (ballroom dance)"), and [jive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jive_(dance) "Jive (dance)") dance routines.

A second DVD titled *Dancing with the Stars: Latin Cardio Dance* was released on September 13, 2008, featuring Maksim Chmerkovskiy and [Cheryl Burke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheryl_Burke "Cheryl Burke"). The program contains cardiovascular workouts adapted from cha-cha, [Merengue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merengue_(dance) "Merengue (dance)"), samba and [mambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mambo_(dance) "Mambo (dance)") dance routines.

### Spin-offs

The first *Dancing* spin-off, *[Dance War: Bruno vs. Carrie Ann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance_War:_Bruno_vs._Carrie_Ann "Dance War: Bruno vs. Carrie Ann")*, premiered on January 7, 2008, on ABC. The show's format was similar to the [BBC Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Television "BBC Television") series, *[DanceX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DanceX "DanceX")*. The show was canceled after one season.

A figure skating spin-off similar to [ITV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_(TV_network) "ITV (TV network)")'s *[Dancing on Ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_on_Ice "Dancing on Ice")* called *[Skating with the Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skating_with_the_Stars "Skating with the Stars")* aired on ABC on November 22, 2010. The series was canceled after one season.

On May 16, 2017, another spin-off, *[Dancing with the Stars: Juniors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars:_Juniors "Dancing with the Stars: Juniors")*, was announced. The dance competition spin-off was originally set to premiere on ABC in spring 2018. The format of the show featured celebrity children and the children of celebrities paired with professional junior ballroom dancers. In January 2018, ABC said that *Dancing with the Stars: Juniors* was still in development, but did not progress as quickly as planned. Instead, *[Dancing with the Stars: Athletes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_26 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 26")* aired in its place, while *Juniors* had initially been scheduled for a summer release. In May 2018, it was announced that the show would premiere on October 7, 2018, with the episodes filmed in August 2018. Professional skateboarder [Sky Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Brown "Sky Brown"), junior pro JT Church and pro mentor [Alan Bersten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Bersten "Alan Bersten") were announced as the winners on December 9, 2018. The show was canceled after one season.

A fourth spin-off, *[Dancing with the Stars: The Next Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars:_The_Next_Pro "Dancing with the Stars: The Next Pro")*, premiered on July 13, 2026. It featured up-and-coming professional dancers living in a house together who competed in an audition process to determine who would join the main series as a pro, beginning with [season 35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_35 "Dancing with the Stars (American TV series) season 35"). The sixth episode of the series marked the first time that fans of the series participated as partners for a potential *Dancing with the Stars* professional dancer. The five fans who participated were notable [social media influencers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Influencer "Influencer"), Jeremy Urann, Zaira Yzabelle, Jay Wyson, [Bailey McKnight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bailey_McKnight "Bailey McKnight") and Anthony Riccardo.

### *Dancing with the Stars: The Game*

In 2016, [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company "American Broadcasting Company"), together with [BBC Worldwide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Worldwide "BBC Worldwide"), commissioned a companion mobile game. The [match-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Match-3 "Match-3") game, published by Donut Publishing and developed by [Exient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exient "Exient"), uses a mix of hand animation and motion-captured data for the dances in the game. The game uses pro dancers from *[Strictly Come Dancing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strictly_Come_Dancing "Strictly Come Dancing")*, and features nine dances: Quickstep, Jive, Tango, Salsa, Charleston, Viennese Waltz, Rumba, Cha Cha Cha, and Paso Doble.

### Tours

An unofficial live tour show called *Dancing Pros: Live!* toured with several *Dancing* pros since 2010. A *Dancing with the Stars: Live!* official tour was announced on November 3, 2014, for the 2014–2015 season starting on December 27, 2014, in [Niagara Falls, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niagara_Falls,_New_York "Niagara Falls, New York"), and ending February 15, 2015, performing in 33 cities. A second tour, *Dancing with the Stars Live!: Dance All Night*, was announced. A third tour, *Dancing with the Stars: Live! – We Came to Dance*, was announced on October 3, 2016, for 43 cities from December 16, 2016, to February 14, 2017. ## Reception

In 2016, a *[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times "New York Times")* study of the 50 television shows with the most [Facebook Likes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook_Like "Facebook Like") found that "unlike *[So You Think You Can Dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/So_You_Think_You_Can_Dance "So You Think You Can Dance")*, which is generally more of a city show, *Dancing with the Stars* is most popular in the countryside. It also has a higher share of 'likes' from viewers aged 65 and up than any other show."

Coverage has noted the evolving reception of *Dancing with the Stars* among younger audiences, particularly Generation Z. *The New York Times* reported that the show has gained visibility on platforms such as TikTok, where performance clips attract younger viewers. Coverage has also emphasized its continued cross-generational appeal, combining renewed interest from long-time viewers with attention from younger audiences through challenging stereotypes, entry to dance, trends in dance styles, iconic choreography, and lasting impact.

The mid-2020s brought on a resurgence in viewership and popularity for *Dancing with the Stars*. The season 34 finale drew in 9.24 million viewers, which is a 45% increase from the previous season's finale and the series' largest finale audience in nine years. Social media coverage, such as on TikTok, attributed to the show's renewed popularity among younger viewers. The casting of contestants with established younger online audiences was also cited as a factor in the increase in viewership.
