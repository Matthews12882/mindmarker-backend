# Death

**Death** is the end of an organism's [life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life "Life"). It is the [irreversible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreversible_process "Irreversible process") cessation of [biological functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_process "Biological process") that sustain a living [organism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organism "Organism"). [Entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy "Entropy"), nature's tendency toward disorder, can contribute to the breakdown of living systems over time. Upon death, organisms stop exhibiting [behavior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavior "Behavior") and generally begin to [decompose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decompose "Decompose") through various physical and [chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-mortem_chemistry "Post-mortem chemistry") processes. Death can occur at any stage of [development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developmental_biology "Developmental biology"), including [in infancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality "Infant mortality"). A person may experience [clinical death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_death "Clinical death") that is reversible, resulting in some cases in a [near-death experience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-death_experience "Near-death experience"). In [humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humans "Humans"), establishing [death of the brain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_death "Brain death") determines that an individual has irreversibly died.

[Natural selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_selection "Natural selection") of organisms which successfully pass on their [genes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genes "Genes") to the next generation drives life to engage in a [struggle for existence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Struggle_for_existence "Struggle for existence") against death. Because of this, living things, including humans, normally [fear death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_anxiety "Death anxiety") and have strong [instincts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-preservation "Self-preservation") to avoid it. Some organisms may [kill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_types_of_killing "List of types of killing") other organisms, causing them to die through [herbivory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbivory "Herbivory"), [predation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predation "Predation"), or [parasitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasitism "Parasitism") in order to take [resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resource_(biology) "Resource (biology)") from the bodies of other organisms into their own. Organisms can also kill others through [competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competition_(biology) "Competition (biology)"), in which they struggle with each other for control of external resources. Sometimes, organisms will take resources from organisms which have already died, either through [scavenging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scavenging "Scavenging") or [decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decomposition "Decomposition"). However, despite the widespread instinct to survive, sometimes animals may commit [suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide "Suicide"), being intentionally self-caused death, which in humans may be attributed to a variety of [risk factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide#Risk_factors "Suicide") including [mental illness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_illness "Mental illness"). Besides intentional killing, organisms can die due to failure to secure necessary resources such as [starvation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starvation "Starvation") or [dehydration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dehydration "Dehydration"), or through harmful physical factors such as [natural disasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_disasters "Natural disasters") or [diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diseases "Diseases"). Organisms that do not die for these reasons usually succumb to [death from old age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senescence "Senescence"); however, some organisms, such as the [immortal jellyfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immortal_jellyfish "Immortal jellyfish"), are [biologically immortal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_immortality "Biological immortality") while still being able to die from other causes. Organisms have varying [life histories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_history_theory "Life history theory"), with differing [life spans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_life_span "Maximum life span") between [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species "Species"). Death affects [populations of organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_(biology) "Population (biology)"), with [selective pressures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_pressure "Evolutionary pressure") resulting in organisms with [traits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenotypic_trait "Phenotypic trait") that are unfavorable in a given environment to die off; changes to an environment such as [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change "Climate change") or [invasive species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasive_species "Invasive species") can cause entire [ecosystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystems "Ecosystems") to die at higher rates than usual. As opposed to intentional suicide, sometimes species will commit [evolutionary suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_suicide "Evolutionary suicide") in which existing adaptations lead to the deaths of all of their members. When all individuals of a [clade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clade "Clade") have died, that clade is said to be [extinct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinct "Extinct"). [Extinction events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinction_events "Extinction events") are periods of time when rates of extinction on [Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth "Earth") result in global loss of [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity "Biodiversity") due to [mass death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_mortality_event "Mass mortality event") within many species worldwide.

As human beings have evolved the mental faculties to be [aware of death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortality_salience "Mortality salience"), knowledge of death is a central part of the [human condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_condition "Human condition") and therefore [is universal to human culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_culture "Death and culture"). Knowledge of death has motivated the development of a variety of [religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious "Religious"), [spiritual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirituality "Spirituality"), [ethical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethical "Ethical"), and [legal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal "Legal") methods for understanding and responding to it. Many cultures have a concept of the survival of individuals after death, whether in an [afterlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afterlife "Afterlife"), as [spirits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_(supernatural_entity) "Spirit (supernatural entity)"), or through [reincarnation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reincarnation "Reincarnation"). While such [dualistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dualistic "Dualistic") notions of survival past death are common to the [world's major religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups "Major religious groups"), other [monist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monist "Monist") forms of thought such as [materialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materialism "Materialism") and [physicalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicalism "Physicalism") generally maintain that death is the irreversible end of [personal identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_identity "Personal identity"). Some societies have [taboos on the dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taboo_on_the_dead "Taboo on the dead"), whereby information about a person who has died is censored. To avoid death, people have sometimes sought to achieve [immortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immortality "Immortality"), or eternal life, such as through [cryogenics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryogenics "Cryogenics"). Once a person has died, their [community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community "Community") will generally perform [funerary rituals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funeral "Funeral") to process the [cadaver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadaver "Cadaver") and honor the decedent according to practices specific to their culture. If a person is understood to have most likely died without direct evidence, they can be legally declared [dead in absentia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_in_absentia "Dead in absentia") in many countries. Surviving people close to a decedent normally experience [grief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grief "Grief"), a set of powerful mental and emotional processes associated with the loss of the person. For a human being to kill another is called [homicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homicide "Homicide"), an act that has profound legal implications in most societies. Homicide considered unjustified is known as [murder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder "Murder"), and is generally considered one of the most serious crimes in any society. However, states claim a [monopoly on violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopoly_on_violence "Monopoly on violence") by which they may legally kill people, whether as [punishment for crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment "Capital punishment"), through [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police "Police") violence, or through [war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War "War"). The study of death is known as [thanatology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanatology "Thanatology").

## Background

As of the early 21st century, 56 million people die per year. As of 2022, an estimated total of almost 110 billion humans have died, or roughly 94% of all humans to have ever lived. The [cause of death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cause_of_death "Cause of death") is usually considered important, and an [autopsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopsy "Autopsy") can be done to determine it. There are many causes, from accidents to diseases or crime and war. The most common reason is [aging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aging "Aging"); the most common cause is [cardiovascular disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiovascular_disease "Cardiovascular disease") (CVD), which is a [disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disease "Disease") that affects the [heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart "Heart") or [blood vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_vessel "Blood vessel"). A substudy of [gerontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerontology "Gerontology") known as [biogerontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biogerontology "Biogerontology") seeks to eliminate death by natural aging in humans, often through the application of natural processes found in certain organisms. However, as humans do not have the means to apply this to themselves, they have to use other ways to reach the [maximum lifespan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_lifespan "Maximum lifespan") for a human, often through [lifestyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifestyle_(social_sciences) "Lifestyle (social sciences)") changes, such as [calorie reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calorie_restriction "Calorie restriction"), [dieting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieting "Dieting"), and [exercise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise "Exercise"). The idea of [lifespan extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifespan_extension "Lifespan extension") is considered and studied as a way for people to live longer.

Determining when a person has definitively died has proven difficult. Initially, death was defined as occurring when breathing and the heartbeat ceased, a status still known as [clinical death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_death "Clinical death"). However, the development of [cardiopulmonary resuscitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiopulmonary_resuscitation "Cardiopulmonary resuscitation") (CPR) meant that such a state was no longer strictly irreversible. For all organisms with a [brain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain "Brain"), death can instead be focused on this organ. [Brain death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_death "Brain death") was then considered a more fitting option, but several definitions exist for this: some people believe that all brain functions must cease; others believe that even if the [brainstem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brainstem "Brainstem") is still alive, the [personality and identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_identity "Personal identity") are irretrievably lost, so therefore the person should be considered entirely dead. Brain death is sometimes used as a legal definition of death.

There are different customs for honoring the lifeless body, such as a [funeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funeral "Funeral"), [cremation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremation "Cremation"), or [sky burial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_burial "Sky burial"). After a death, an [obituary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obituary "Obituary") may be posted in a newspaper, and the family and friends of the dead person usually go through the [grieving process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grieving_process "Grieving process").

## Definition

There are many scientific approaches and various interpretations of the concept. Additionally, the advent of life-sustaining therapy and the numerous criteria for defining death from both a medical and legal standpoint have made it difficult to create a single unifying definition.

### Defining life to define death

One of the challenges in defining death is in distinguishing it from life. As a point in time, death seems to refer to the moment when life ends. Determining when death has occurred is difficult, as cessation of life functions is often not simultaneous across organ systems. Such determination, therefore, requires drawing precise conceptual boundaries between life and death. This is difficult due to there being little consensus on how to define life.

It is possible to define life in terms of [consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consciousness "Consciousness"). When consciousness ceases, an organism can be said to have died. One of the flaws in this approach is that there are many organisms that are alive but probably not conscious. Another problem is in defining consciousness, which has many different definitions given by modern scientists, psychologists and philosophers. Additionally, many religious traditions, including [Abrahamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrahamic_religions "Abrahamic religions") and [Dharmic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmic_religions "Dharmic religions") traditions, hold that death does not (or may not) entail the end of consciousness. In certain cultures, death is more of a process than a single event. It implies a slow shift from one spiritual state to another.

Other definitions for death focus on the character of cessation of organismic functioning and human death, which refers to irreversible loss of personhood. More specifically, death occurs when a living entity experiences irreversible cessation of all functioning. As it pertains to human life, death is an irreversible process where someone loses their existence as a person.

### Definition of death by heartbeat and breath

Historically, attempts to define the exact moment of a human's death have been subjective or imprecise. Death was defined as the cessation of [heartbeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart "Heart") (cardiac arrest) and [breathing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breath "Breath"), but the development of [CPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiopulmonary_resuscitation "Cardiopulmonary resuscitation") and prompt [defibrillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defibrillation "Defibrillation") have rendered that definition inadequate because breathing and heartbeat can sometimes be restarted. This type of death where circulatory and respiratory arrest happens is known as the circulatory definition of death (CDD). Proponents of the CDD believe this definition is reasonable because a person with permanent loss of circulatory and respiratory function should be considered dead. Critics of this definition state that while cessation of these functions may be permanent, it does not mean the situation is irreversible because if CPR is applied fast enough, the person could be revived. Thus, the arguments for and against the CDD boil down to defining the actual words "permanent" and "irreversible," which further complicates the challenge of defining death. Furthermore, events [causally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causality "Causality") linked to death in the past no longer kill in all circumstances; without a functioning heart or lungs, life can sometimes be sustained with a combination of [life support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_support "Life support") devices, [organ transplants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_transplants "Organ transplants"), and [artificial pacemakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_pacemaker "Artificial pacemaker").

### Brain death

Today, where a definition of the moment of death is required, doctors and coroners usually turn to "brain death" or "biological death" to define a person as being dead; people are considered dead when the electrical activity in their brain ceases. It is presumed that an end of electrical activity indicates the end of [consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consciousness "Consciousness"). Suspension of consciousness must be permanent and not transient, as occurs during certain [sleep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-rapid_eye_movement_sleep "Non-rapid eye movement sleep") stages, and especially a coma. In the case of sleep, [electroencephalograms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electroencephalography "Electroencephalography") (EEGs) are used to tell the difference.

The category of "brain death" is seen as problematic by some scholars. For instance, Dr. Franklin Miller, a senior faculty member at the Department of Bioethics, National Institutes of Health, notes: "By the late 1990s... the equation of brain death with death of the human being was increasingly challenged by scholars, based on evidence regarding the array of biological functioning displayed by patients correctly diagnosed as having this condition who were maintained on mechanical ventilation for substantial periods of time. These patients maintained the ability to sustain circulation and respiration, control temperature, excrete wastes, heal wounds, fight infections and, most dramatically, to gestate fetuses (in the case of pregnant "brain-dead" women)."

While "brain death" is viewed as problematic by some scholars, there are proponents of it[*[who?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Words_to_watch#Unsupported_attributions "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Words to watch")*] that believe this definition of death is the most reasonable for distinguishing life from death. The reasoning behind the support for this definition is that brain death has a set of criteria that is reliable and reproducible. Also, the brain is crucial in determining our identity or who we are as human beings. The distinction should be made that "brain death" cannot be equated with one in a vegetative state or coma, in that the former situation describes a state that is beyond recovery.

EEGs can detect spurious electrical impulses, while certain drugs, [hypoglycemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypoglycemia "Hypoglycemia"), [hypoxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypoxia_(medical) "Hypoxia (medical)"), or [hypothermia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothermia "Hypothermia") can suppress or even stop brain activity temporarily; because of this, hospitals have protocols for determining brain death involving EEGs at widely separated intervals under defined conditions.

#### Neocortical brain death

One view is that the [neocortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neocortex "Neocortex") of the brain is necessary for consciousness, and that therefore only electrical activity of the neocortex should be considered when defining death. Eventually, the criterion for death may be the permanent and irreversible loss of [cognitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognition "Cognition") function, as evidenced by the death of the [cerebral cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebral_cortex "Cerebral cortex"). All hope of recovering human thought and [personality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personality_psychology "Personality psychology") is then gone, given current and foreseeable medical technology. Even by whole-brain criteria, the determination of brain death can be complicated.

#### Total brain death

There is a more conservative definition of death: irreversible cessation of all functions of the whole brain, as opposed to just in the neo-cortex.

#### United States

One example is the [Uniform Determination Of Death Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_Determination_Of_Death_Act "Uniform Determination Of Death Act") in the United States. In the past, the adoption of this whole-brain definition was a conclusion of the President's Commission for the Study of Ethical Problems in Medicine and Biomedical and Behavioral Research in 1980. They concluded that this approach to defining death sufficed in reaching a uniform definition nationwide. A multitude of reasons was presented to support this definition, including uniformity of standards in law for establishing death, consumption of a family's fiscal resources for artificial life support, and legal establishment for equating brain death with death to proceed with [organ donation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_donation "Organ donation").

### Problems in medical practice

Aside from the issue of support of or dispute against brain death, there is another inherent problem in this categorical definition: the variability of its application in medical practice. In 1995, the American Academy of Neurology (AAN) established the criteria that became the medical standard for diagnosing neurologic death. At that time, three clinical features had to be satisfied to determine "irreversible cessation" of the total brain, including coma with clear etiology, cessation of breathing, and lack of brainstem reflexes. These criteria were updated again, most recently in 2010, but substantial discrepancies remain across hospitals and medical specialties.

## Legal issues

The death of a person has legal consequences that may vary between jurisdictions. Most countries follow the whole-brain death criteria, where all functions of the brain must have completely ceased. However, in other jurisdictions, some follow the brainstem version of brain death. Afterward, a [death certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_certificate "Death certificate") is issued in most jurisdictions, either by a doctor or by an administrative office, upon presentation of a doctor's declaration of death.

### Donations

The problem of defining death is especially imperative as it pertains to the [dead donor rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_donor_rule "Dead donor rule"), which could be understood as one of the following interpretations of the rule: there must be an official declaration of death in a person before starting organ procurement, or that organ procurement cannot result in the death of the donor. A great deal of controversy has surrounded the definition of death and the dead donor rule. Advocates of the rule believe that the rule is legitimate in protecting organ donors while also countering any moral or legal objection to organ procurement. Critics, on the other hand, believe that the rule does not uphold the best interests of the donors and that the rule does not effectively promote organ donation.

## Signs

Signs of death or strong indications that a [warm-blooded animal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endotherm "Endotherm") is no longer alive are:

* [Respiratory arrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_arrest "Respiratory arrest") (no [breathing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breathing "Breathing"))
* [Cardiac arrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiac_arrest "Cardiac arrest") (no [pulse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_rate "Heart rate"))
* [Brain death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_death "Brain death") (no neuronal activity)

The stages that follow after death are:

* *[Pallor mortis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pallor_mortis "Pallor mortis")*, paleness which happens in 15–120 minutes after death
* *[Livor mortis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livor_mortis "Livor mortis")*, a settling of the blood in the lower (dependent) portion of the body
* *[Algor mortis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algor_mortis "Algor mortis")*, the reduction in body temperature following death. This is generally a steady decline until matching ambient temperature
* *[Rigor mortis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigor_mortis "Rigor mortis")*, the limbs of the corpse become stiff (Latin *rigor*) and difficult to move or manipulate
* [Putrefaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putrefaction "Putrefaction"), the beginning signs of decomposition
* [Decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decomposition "Decomposition"), the reduction into simpler forms of matter, accompanied by a strong, unpleasant odor.
* [Skeletonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeletonization "Skeletonization"), the end of decomposition, where all soft tissues have decomposed, leaving only the skeleton.
* [Fossilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil "Fossil"), the natural preservation of the skeletal remains formed over a very long period

## Causes

The leading cause of human death in [developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_countries "Developing countries") is [infectious disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectious_disease "Infectious disease"). The leading causes in [developed countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_countries "Developed countries") are [atherosclerosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atherosclerosis "Atherosclerosis") ([heart disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_disease "Heart disease") and stroke), cancer, and other diseases related to [obesity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obesity "Obesity") and [aging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aging "Aging"). By an extremely wide margin, the largest unifying cause of death in the developed world is biological aging, leading to various complications known as [aging-associated diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aging-associated_diseases "Aging-associated diseases"). These conditions cause loss of [homeostasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeostasis "Homeostasis"), leading to cardiac arrest, causing loss of [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen "Oxygen") and nutrient supply, causing irreversible deterioration of the brain and other [tissues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tissue_(biology) "Tissue (biology)"). Of the roughly 150,000 people who die each day across the globe, about two thirds die of age-related causes. In industrialized nations, the proportion is much higher, approaching 90%. With improved medical capability, [dying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dying "Dying") has become [a condition to be managed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respite_care "Respite care").

In [developing nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_nations "Developing nations"), inferior sanitary conditions and lack of access to modern [medical technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_technology "Medical technology") make death from [infectious diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectious_diseases "Infectious diseases") more common than in [developed countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_countries "Developed countries"). One such disease is [tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculosis "Tuberculosis"), a bacterial disease that killed 1.8 million people in 2015. In 2004, [malaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaria "Malaria") caused about 2.7 million deaths annually. The [AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS "HIV/AIDS") death toll in Africa may reach 90–100 million by 2025.

According to [Jean Ziegler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Ziegler "Jean Ziegler"), the United Nations Special Reporter on the Right to Food from 2000 to March 2008, mortality due to [malnutrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malnutrition "Malnutrition") accounted for 58% of the total mortality rate in 2006. Ziegler says worldwide, approximately 62 million people died from all causes and of those deaths, more than 36 million died of hunger or diseases due to deficiencies in [micronutrients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronutrient "Micronutrient").

Tobacco smoking killed 100 million people worldwide in the 20th century and could kill 1 billion people worldwide in the 21st century, a [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization "World Health Organization") report warned.

Many leading developed world causes of death can be postponed by [diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diet_(nutrition) "Diet (nutrition)") and [physical activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_fitness "Physical fitness"), but the accelerating incidence of disease with age still imposes limits on human [longevity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longevity "Longevity"). The [evolutionary cause of aging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_of_aging "Evolution of aging") is, at best, only beginning to be understood. It has been suggested that direct intervention in the aging process may now be the most effective intervention against major causes of death.

[Selye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Selye "Hans Selye") proposed a unified non-specific approach to many causes of death. He demonstrated that [stress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stress_(biology) "Stress (biology)") decreases the adaptability of an organism and proposed to describe adaptability as a special resource in itself, *adaptation energy*. The animal dies when this resource is exhausted. Selye assumed that adaptability is a finite supply presented at birth. But later, Goldstone proposed the concept of production or income of adaptation energy which may be stored (up to a limit) as a capital reserve of adaptation. In recent works, adaptation energy is considered an internal coordinate on the "dominant path" in the model of adaptation. It is demonstrated that oscillations of well-being appear when the reserve of adaptability is almost exhausted.

In 2012, [suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide "Suicide") overtook [car crashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_collision "Traffic collision") as the leading cause of human injury deaths in the U.S., followed by poisoning, falls, and murder.

Accidents and disasters, from [nuclear disasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_and_radiation_accidents_and_incidents "Nuclear and radiation accidents and incidents") to [structural collapses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_integrity_and_failure "Structural integrity and failure"), also claim lives. One of the deadliest incidents of all time is the [1975 Banqiao Dam Failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Banqiao_Dam_failure "1975 Banqiao Dam failure"), with varying estimates, up to 240,000 dead. Other incidents with high death tolls are the [Wanggongchang explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanggongchang_Explosion "Wanggongchang Explosion") (when a gunpowder factory ended up with 20,000 deaths), a collapse of a wall of [Circus Maximus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circus_Maximus "Circus Maximus") that killed 13,000 people, and the [Chernobyl disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_disaster "Chernobyl disaster") that killed between 95 and 4,000 people.

[Natural disasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_disaster "Natural disaster") kill around 45,000 people annually, although this number can vary from thousands to millions on a per-decade basis. Some of the deadliest natural disasters are the [1931 China floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_China_floods "1931 China floods"), which killed an estimated 4 million people, although estimates widely vary; the [1887 Yellow River flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1887_Yellow_River_flood "1887 Yellow River flood"), which killed an estimated 2 million people in China; and the [1970 Bhola cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_Bhola_cyclone "1970 Bhola cyclone"), which killed as many as 500,000 people in [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan "East Pakistan") (present-day [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh")). If naturally occurring [famines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famine "Famine") are considered natural disasters, the [Chinese famine of 1906–1907](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_famine_of_1906%E2%80%931907 "Chinese famine of 1906–1907"), which killed 15–20 million people, can be considered the deadliest natural disaster in recorded history.

In animals, [predation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predation "Predation") can be a common cause of death. Livestock have a 6% death rate from predation. However, younger animals are more susceptible to predation. For example, 50% of young foxes die to [birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_of_prey "Bird of prey"), [bobcats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobcat "Bobcat"), [coyotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coyote "Coyote"), and [other foxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannibalism "Cannibalism") as well. Young bear cubs in the [Yellowstone National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellowstone_National_Park "Yellowstone National Park") only have a 40% chance to survive to adulthood from other bears and predators.

### Autopsy

An autopsy, also known as a *postmortem examination* or an *obduction*, is a [medical procedure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_procedure "Medical procedure") that consists of a thorough [examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_examination "Medical examination") of a human [corpse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_body "Dead body") to determine the cause and manner of a person's death and to evaluate any disease or injury that may be present. It is usually performed by a specialized [medical doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physician "Physician") called a [pathologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathologist "Pathologist").

Autopsies are either performed for legal or medical purposes. A forensic autopsy is carried out when the cause of death may be a criminal matter, while a clinical or academic autopsy is performed to find the medical cause of death and is used in cases of unknown or uncertain death, or for research purposes. Autopsies can be further classified into cases where external examination suffices, and those where the body is dissected and an internal examination is conducted. Permission from [next of kin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_of_kin "Next of kin") may be required for internal autopsy in some cases. Once an internal autopsy is complete the body is generally reconstituted by sewing it back together.

A necropsy, which is not always a medical procedure, was a term previously used to describe an unregulated postmortem examination. In modern times, this term is more commonly associated with the corpses of animals.

## Misdiagnosis

There are many anecdotal references to people being declared dead by physicians and then "coming back to life," sometimes days later in their coffin or when [embalming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embalming "Embalming") procedures are about to begin. From the mid-18th century onwards, there was an upsurge in the public's fear of being mistakenly buried alive and much debate about the uncertainty of the signs of death. Various suggestions were made to test for signs of life before burial, ranging from pouring vinegar and pepper into the corpse's mouth to applying red hot pokers to the feet or into the [rectum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectum "Rectum"). Writing in 1895, the physician J.C. Ouseley claimed that as many as 2,700 people were buried prematurely each year in England and Wales, although some estimates peg the figure to be closer to 800.

In cases of [electric shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_shock "Electric shock"), [cardiopulmonary resuscitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiopulmonary_resuscitation "Cardiopulmonary resuscitation") (CPR) for an hour or longer can allow stunned [nerves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve "Nerve") to recover, allowing an apparently dead person to survive. People found unconscious under icy water may survive if their faces are kept continuously cold until they arrive at an [emergency room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_room "Emergency room"). This "diving response," in which [metabolic activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolism "Metabolism") and oxygen requirements are minimal, is something humans share with [cetaceans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cetacea "Cetacea") called the [mammalian diving reflex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammalian_diving_reflex "Mammalian diving reflex").

As medical technologies advance, ideas about when death occurs may have to be reevaluated in light of the ability to restore a person to vitality after longer periods of apparent death (as happened when CPR and defibrillation showed that cessation of heartbeat is inadequate as a decisive indicator of death). The lack of electrical brain activity may not be enough to consider someone scientifically dead. Therefore, the concept of information-theoretic death has been suggested as a better means of defining when true death occurs, though the concept has few practical applications outside the field of [cryonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryonics "Cryonics").

## Death before birth

Death before birth can happen in several ways: [stillbirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stillbirth "Stillbirth"), when the [fetus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetus "Fetus") dies before or during the delivery process; [miscarriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscarriage "Miscarriage"), when the [embryo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryo "Embryo") dies before independent survival; and [abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion "Abortion"), the artificial termination of the pregnancy. Stillbirth and miscarriage can happen for various reasons, while abortion is carried out purposely.

### Stillbirth

Stillbirth can happen right before or after the delivery of a fetus. It can result from [defects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_defect "Birth defect") of the fetus or [risk factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_factor_(epidemiology) "Risk factor (epidemiology)") present in the mother. Reductions of these factors, [caesarean sections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesarean_section "Caesarean section") when risks are present, and early detection of birth defects have lowered the rate of stillbirth. However, 1% of births in the United States end in a stillbirth.

### Miscarriage

A miscarriage is defined by the [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization "World Health Organization") as, "The expulsion or extraction from its mother of an embryo or fetus weighing 500g or less." Miscarriage is one of the most frequent problems in pregnancy, and is reported in around 12–15% of all [clinical pregnancies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pregnancy "Pregnancy"); however, by including pregnancy losses during [menstruation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menstruation "Menstruation"), it could be up to 17–22% of all pregnancies. There are many risk-factors involved in miscarriage; consumption of [caffeine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caffeine "Caffeine"), [tobacco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco "Tobacco"), [alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_beverage "Alcoholic beverage"), drugs, having a previous miscarriage, and the use of abortion can increase the chances of having a miscarriage.

### Abortion

An abortion may be performed for many reasons, such as [pregnancy from rape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pregnancy_from_rape "Pregnancy from rape"), financial constraints of having a child, [teenage pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teenage_pregnancy "Teenage pregnancy"), and the lack of support from a [significant other](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Significant_other "Significant other"). There are two forms of abortion: a [medical abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_abortion "Medical abortion") and an in-clinic abortion or sometimes referred to as a surgical abortion. A medical abortion involves taking a pill that will terminate the pregnancy no more than 11 weeks past the last [period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menstrual_cycle "Menstrual cycle"), and an in-clinic abortion involves a medical procedure using suction to empty the uterus; this is possible after 12 weeks, but it may be more difficult to find an operating doctor who will go through with the procedure.

## Related issues

### Senescence

*Senescence* refers to a scenario when a living being can survive all calamities but eventually dies due to causes relating to old age. Conversely, ***premature death*** can refer to a death that occurs before old age arrives, for example, human death before a person reaches the age of 75. Animal and plant cells normally reproduce and function during the whole period of natural existence, but the aging process derives from the deterioration of cellular activity and the ruination of regular functioning. The aptitude of cells for gradual deterioration and mortality means that cells are naturally sentenced to stable and long-term loss of living capacities, even despite continuing metabolic reactions and viability. In the United Kingdom, for example, nine out of ten of all the deaths that occur daily relates to senescence, while around the world, it accounts for two-thirds of 150,000 deaths that take place daily.

Almost all animals who survive external hazards to their biological functioning eventually die from [biological aging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senescence "Senescence"), known in life sciences as *senescence*. Some organisms experience [negligible senescence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negligible_senescence "Negligible senescence"), even exhibiting [biological immortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_immortality "Biological immortality"). These include the jellyfish *[Turritopsis dohrnii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turritopsis_dohrnii "Turritopsis dohrnii")*, the [hydra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydra_(genus) "Hydra (genus)"), and the [planarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planarian "Planarian"). Unnatural causes of death include [suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide "Suicide") and [predation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predation "Predation"). Of all causes, roughly 150,000 people die around the world each day. Of these, two-thirds die directly or indirectly due to senescence, but in industrialized countries – such as the United States, the United Kingdom, and Germany – the rate approaches 90% (i.e., nearly nine out of ten of all deaths are related to senescence).

[Physiological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiological "Physiological") death is now seen as a process, more than an event as conditions once considered indicative of death are now reversible. Where in the process, a dividing line is drawn between life and death depends on factors beyond the presence or absence of [vital signs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vital_signs "Vital signs"). In general, [clinical death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_death "Clinical death") is neither necessary nor sufficient for a determination of [legal death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_death "Legal death"). A patient with working [heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_heart "Human heart") and [lungs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_lung "Human lung") determined to be [brain dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_death "Brain death") can be pronounced legally dead without clinical death occurring.

### Life extension

Life extension refers to an increase in [maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_life_span "Maximum life span") or [average lifespan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy "Life expectancy"), especially in humans, by slowing or reversing [aging processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senescence "Senescence") through [anti-aging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-aging "Anti-aging") measures. Aging is the most common cause of death worldwide. Aging is seen as inevitable, so according to [Aubrey de Grey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aubrey_de_Grey "Aubrey de Grey") little is spent on research into anti-aging therapies, a phenomenon known as [pro-aging trance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-aging_trance "Pro-aging trance").

The average lifespan is determined by vulnerability to [accidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accident "Accident") and age or lifestyle-related afflictions such as cancer or [cardiovascular disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiovascular_disease "Cardiovascular disease"). Extension of lifespan can be achieved by good [diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diet_(nutrition) "Diet (nutrition)"), exercise, and avoidance of hazards such as [smoking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_smoking "Tobacco smoking"). Maximum lifespan is determined by the rate of aging for a species inherent in its [genes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene "Gene"). A recognized method of extending maximum lifespan is [calorie restriction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calorie_restriction "Calorie restriction"). Theoretically, the extension of the maximum lifespan can be achieved by reducing the rate of aging damage, by [periodic replacement of damaged tissues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tissue_engineering "Tissue engineering"), [molecular repair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanobiotechnology "Nanobiotechnology"), or [rejuvenation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rejuvenation_(aging) "Rejuvenation (aging)") of deteriorated cells and tissues.

A United States poll found religious and irreligious people, as well as men and women and people of different economic classes, have similar rates of support for life extension, while Africans and Hispanics have higher rates of support than white people. 38% said they would desire to have their aging process cured.

Researchers of life extension can be known as "biomedical [gerontologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerontologist "Gerontologist")." They try to understand aging, and develop treatments to reverse aging processes, or at least slow them for the improvement of health and maintenance of youthfulness. Those who use life extension findings and apply them to themselves are called "life extensionists" or "longevists." The primary life extension strategy currently is to apply anti-aging methods to attempt to live long enough to benefit from a cure for aging.

#### Cryonics

Cryonics (from [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language "Greek language") κρύος 'kryos-' meaning 'icy cold') is the [low-temperature preservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryopreservation "Cryopreservation") of animals, including humans, who cannot be sustained by contemporary medicine, with the hope that healing and [resuscitation](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/resuscitate "wikt:resuscitate") may be possible in the future.

[Cryopreservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryopreservation "Cryopreservation") of people and other large animals is not reversible with current technology. The stated rationale for cryonics is that people who are considered dead by current legal or medical definitions, may not necessarily be dead according to the more stringent 'information-theoretic' definition of death.

Some scientific literature is claimed to support the feasibility of cryonics. Medical science and [cryobiologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryobiology "Cryobiology") generally regard cryonics with skepticism.

## Psychology

Death studies is a field within [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology "Psychology"). To varying degrees people inherently fear death, both the process and the eventuality; it is hard wired and part of the 'survival instinct' of all animals. Discussing, thinking about, or planning for their deaths causes them discomfort. This fear may cause them to put off financial planning, preparing a [will and testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_and_testament "Will and testament"), or requesting help from a [hospice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospice "Hospice") organization.

[Mortality salience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortality_salience "Mortality salience") is the belief that death is inevitable. However, [self-esteem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-esteem "Self-esteem") and culture are ways to reduce the [anxiety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anxiety "Anxiety") this effect can cause. The awareness of someone's own death can cause a deepened bond in their [in-group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In-group_and_out-group "In-group and out-group") as a [defense mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_mechanism "Defence mechanism"). This can also cause the person to become very judging. In a study, two groups were formed; one group was asked to reflect upon their mortality, the other was not, afterwards, the groups were told to set a [bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bail_bond "Bail bond") for a [prostitute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostitute "Prostitute"). The group that did not reflect on death had an average of $50, the group who was reminded about their death had an average of $455.

Different people have different responses to the idea of their deaths. Philosopher [Galen Strawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galen_Strawson "Galen Strawson") writes that the death that many people wish for is an instant, painless, unexperienced annihilation. In this unlikely scenario, the person dies without realizing it and without being able to fear it. One moment the person is walking, eating, or sleeping, and the next moment, the person is dead. Strawson reasons that this type of death would not take anything away from the person, as he believes a person cannot have a legitimate claim to ownership in the future.

## Society and culture

In society, the nature of death and humanity's [awareness of its mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortality_salience "Mortality salience") has, for millennia, been a concern of the world's [religious traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_traditions "Religious traditions") and [philosophical inquiry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy "Philosophy"). This includes belief in [resurrection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resurrection "Resurrection") or an [afterlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afterlife "Afterlife") (associated with [Abrahamic religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrahamic_religions "Abrahamic religions")), [reincarnation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reincarnation "Reincarnation") or rebirth (associated with [Dharmic religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmic_religions "Dharmic religions")), or that consciousness permanently ceases to exist, known as [eternal oblivion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternal_oblivion "Eternal oblivion") (associated with [secular humanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secular_humanism "Secular humanism")).

Commemoration ceremonies after death may include various [mourning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mourning "Mourning"), funeral practices, and ceremonies for honoring the deceased. The physical remains of a person, commonly known as a *corpse* or *body*, are usually [interred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burial "Burial") whole or [cremated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremated "Cremated"), though among the world's cultures, there are a variety of other methods of [mortuary disposal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disposal_of_human_corpses "Disposal of human corpses"). In the English language, blessings directed towards a dead person include *[rest in peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rest_in_peace "Rest in peace")* (originally the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin"), *requiescat in pace*) or its [initialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initialism "Initialism") RIP.

Death is the center of many traditions and organizations; customs relating to death are a feature of every culture around the world. Much of this revolves around the care of the dead, as well as the [afterlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afterlife "Afterlife") and the disposal of bodies upon the onset of death. The [disposal of human corpses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disposal_of_human_corpses "Disposal of human corpses") does, in general, begin with the [last offices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_offices "Last offices") before significant time has passed, and ritualistic ceremonies often occur, most commonly interment or cremation. This is not a unified practice; in [Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet "Tibet"), for instance, the body is given a [sky burial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_burial "Sky burial") and left on a mountain top. Proper preparation for death and techniques and ceremonies for producing the ability to transfer one's spiritual attainments into another body (reincarnation) are subjects of detailed study in Tibet. [Mummification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mummification "Mummification") or [embalming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embalming "Embalming") is also prevalent in some cultures to slow the rate of [decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decomposition "Decomposition"). The rise of [secularism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularism "Secularism") resulted in material mementos of death declining.

Some parts of death in culture are legally based, having laws for when death occurs, such as the receiving of a death certificate, the settlement of the deceased [estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estate_(law) "Estate (law)"), and the issues of [inheritance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inheritance "Inheritance") and, in some countries, [inheritance taxation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inheritance_tax "Inheritance tax").

[Capital punishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment "Capital punishment") is also a culturally divisive aspect of death. In most jurisdictions where capital punishment is carried out today, the death penalty is reserved for premeditated murder, espionage, [treason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treason "Treason"), or as part of [military justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_justice "Military justice"). In some countries, sexual crimes, such as [adultery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adultery "Adultery") and [sodomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodomy "Sodomy"), carry the death penalty, as do religious crimes, such as [apostasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostasy "Apostasy"). In many [retentionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Use_of_capital_punishment_by_nation "Use of capital punishment by nation") countries, drug trafficking is also a capital offense. In China, [human trafficking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trafficking_in_human_beings "Trafficking in human beings") and serious cases of corruption are also punished by the death penalty. In militaries around the world, [courts-martial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courts-martial "Courts-martial") have imposed death sentences for offenses such as [cowardice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowardice "Cowardice"), [desertion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desertion "Desertion"), [insubordination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insubordination "Insubordination"), and [mutiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutiny "Mutiny"). Mutiny is punishable by death in the United States.

Death in warfare and [suicide attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_attack "Suicide attack") also have cultural links, and the ideas of *[dulce et decorum est pro patria mori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulce_et_decorum_est_pro_patria_mori "Dulce et decorum est pro patria mori"),* which translates to "It is sweet and proper to die for one's country", is a concept that dates to antiquity. Additionally, grieving relatives of dead soldiers and [death notification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_notification "Death notification") are embedded in many cultures. Recently in the [Western world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_world "Western world")—with the increase in terrorism following the [September 11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_attacks "September 11 attacks") but also further back in time with suicide bombings, [kamikaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamikaze "Kamikaze") missions in [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), and suicide missions in a host of other conflicts in history—death for a cause by way of suicide attack, including [martyrdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyrdom "Martyrdom"), have had significant cultural impacts.

Suicide, in general, and particularly [euthanasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euthanasia "Euthanasia"), are also points of cultural debate. Both acts are understood very differently in different cultures. In Japan, for example, ending a life with honor by [seppuku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seppuku "Seppuku") was considered a desirable death, whereas according to traditional Christian and Islamic cultures, suicide is viewed as a sin.

[Death is personified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personifications_of_death "Personifications of death") in many cultures, with such symbolic representations as the [Grim Reaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_(personification) "Death (personification)"), [Azrael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azrael "Azrael"), the [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu "Hindu") god [Yama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yama "Yama"), and [Father Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Father_Time "Father Time"). In western cultures, the Grim Reaper, or figures similar to it, is the most popular depiction of death.

In Brazil, death is counted officially when it is registered by existing family members at a cartório, a government-authorized registry. Before being able to file for an official death, the deceased must have been registered for an official birth at the cartório. Though a Public Registry Law guarantees all Brazilian citizens the right to register deaths, regardless of the financial means of their family members (often children), the Brazilian government has not taken away the burden, hidden costs, and fees of filing for a death. For many impoverished families, the indirect costs and burden of filing for a death lead to a more appealing unofficial, local, and cultural burial, which in turn raises the debate about inaccurate [mortality rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortality_rates "Mortality rates").

Talking about death and witnessing it is a [difficult issue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grief "Grief") in most cultures. Western societies may like to treat the dead with the utmost material respect, with an official embalmer and associated rites. Eastern societies like India may be more open to accepting it as a *fait accompli*, with a funeral procession of the dead body ending in an open-air burning-to-ashes.

### Place of death

Around 1930, most people in Western countries died in their own homes, surrounded by family, and comforted by clergy, neighbors, and doctors making [house calls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_call "House call"). By the mid-20th century, half of all Americans died in a hospital. By the start of the 21st century, only about 20 to 25% of people in developed countries died outside of a medical institution. The shift from dying at home towards dying in a professional medical environment has been termed the "Invisible Death." This shift occurred gradually over the years, and most deaths now occur outside the home.

### Origins of death in mythology

The [origin of death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_of_death "Origin of death") is a theme or myth of how death came to be. It is present in nearly all cultures across the world, as death is a universal happening. This makes it an [origin myth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_myth "Origin myth"), a myth that describes how a feature of the natural or social world appeared. There can be some similarities between myths and cultures. In [North American mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythologies_of_the_Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas "Mythologies of the Indigenous peoples of the Americas"), the theme of a man who wants to be immortal and a man who wants to die can be seen across many [Indigenous people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples "Indigenous peoples"). In Christianity, death is the result of the [fall of man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_man "Fall of man") after eating the fruit from the [tree of the knowledge of good and evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_of_the_knowledge_of_good_and_evil "Tree of the knowledge of good and evil"). In [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology "Greek mythology"), the opening of [Pandora's box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandora%27s_box "Pandora's box") releases death upon the world.

### Religious views

#### Afterlife

Much interest and debate surround the question of what happens to one's consciousness as one's body dies. The belief in the permanent loss of consciousness after death is often called *[eternal oblivion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternal_oblivion "Eternal oblivion")*. The belief that the [stream of consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stream_of_consciousness_(psychology) "Stream of consciousness (psychology)") is preserved after physical death is described by the term *[afterlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afterlife "Afterlife")*.

[Near-death experiences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-death_experience "Near-death experience") (NDEs) describe the [subjective experiences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subjective_character_of_experience "Subjective character of experience") associated with impending death. Some survivors of such experiences report it as "seeing the afterlife while they were dying". Seeing a being of light and talking with it, [life flashing before the eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_review "Life review"), and the confirmation of cultural beliefs of the afterlife are common themes in NDEs.

Many religious traditions regard funeral and end-of-life rituals as important practices that help mark or support an individual's passage from life to afterlife.

#### Buddhism

In Buddhist doctrine and practice, death plays an important role. Awareness of death motivated [Prince Siddhartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautama_Buddha "Gautama Buddha") to strive to find the ["deathless"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amrta#Theravada_Buddhism "Amrta") and finally attain [enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlightenment_(Buddhism) "Enlightenment (Buddhism)"). In Buddhist doctrine, death functions as a reminder of the value of [having been born as a human being](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_beings_in_Buddhism "Human beings in Buddhism"). Rebirth as a human being is considered the only state in which one can attain enlightenment. Therefore, death helps remind oneself that one should not take life for granted. The belief in rebirth among Buddhists does not necessarily remove [death anxiety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existential_angst "Existential angst") since all existence in the [cycle of rebirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsara_(Buddhism) "Samsara (Buddhism)") is considered filled with [suffering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dukkha "Dukkha"), and being reborn many times does not necessarily mean that one progresses.

Death is part of several key Buddhist tenets, such as the [Four Noble Truths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Noble_Truths "Four Noble Truths") and [dependent origination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prat%C4%ABtyasamutp%C4%81da "Pratītyasamutpāda").

In Buddhist traditions, preparation for death is regarded as an important aspect of spiritual practice. Practitioners are encouraged to contemplate the inevitability of death as a means of developing awareness of the value and rarity of human life. Since death is unavoidable and its timing uncertain, individuals are advised to live ethically by avoiding unwholesome actions of body, speech, and mind while cultivating wholesome actions that support spiritual progress and liberation.

The mental state at the time of death is considered especially significant because it is believed to influence the transition to a future existence. According to Buddhist teachings, the final moment of consciousness in one life conditions the first moment of consciousness in the next. For this reason, practitioners seek to cultivate calmness, mindfulness, and emotional stability throughout life so that fear, anger, or agitation do not dominate the mind at the moment of death and negatively affect future rebirth.

#### Christianity

While [Christian denominations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_denomination "Christian denomination") have varying beliefs, they generally agree that all humans are [souls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul "Soul"), a unity of their physical body and their [spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_(animating_force) "Spirit (animating force)").

After one dies, Christians believe that during this time, their spirit would separate from their body, and enter the [afterlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afterlife "Afterlife"). Additionally, they hold that when the [Second Coming of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Coming "Second Coming") occurs, [resurrection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resurrection "Resurrection") would follow, and the spirit would be reunified with the body.

#### Hinduism

In [Hindu texts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_texts "Hindu texts"), death is described as the individual eternal spiritual *[jiva-atma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiva "Jiva")* (soul or conscious self) exiting the current temporary material body. The soul exits this body when the body can no longer sustain the conscious self (life), which may be due to mental or physical reasons or, more accurately, the inability to act on one's *[kama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kama#In_Hinduism "Kama")* (material desires). During conception, the soul enters a compatible new body based on the remaining merits and demerits of one's *[karma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karma#In_Hinduism "Karma")* (good/bad material activities based on *[dharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharma#Hinduism "Dharma")*) and the state of one's mind (impressions or last thoughts) at the time of death. Liberation from the cycle of births and deaths ([samsara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa%E1%B9%83s%C4%81ra "Saṃsāra")), known as [moksa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moksha "Moksha"), is regarded as the ultimate goal of human life.

Usually, the process of [reincarnation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reincarnation#Hinduism "Reincarnation") makes one forget all memories of one's previous life. Because nothing really dies and the temporary material body is always changing, both in this life and the next, death means forgetfulness of one's previous experiences.

#### Islam

The Islamic view is that death is the separation of the soul from the body as well as the beginning of the afterlife. The afterlife, or *[akhirah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhirah "Akhirah"),* is one of the six main beliefs in Islam. Muslims consider death as a continuation of life in another form. In Islam, life on earth right now is a short, temporary life and a testing period for every soul. True life begins with the Day of Judgement when all people will be divided into two groups. The righteous will be welcomed to *[jannah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jannah "Jannah")* and others will be punished.

Muslims believe death to be wholly natural and predetermined by God. Only God knows the exact time of a person's death. [The Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran "Quran") emphasizes that death is inevitable. ([Q50:16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaf_(surah) "Qaf (surah)")) Life on earth is the one and only chance for people to prepare themselves for the life to come and choose to either believe or not believe in God, and death is the end of that learning opportunity.

#### Judaism

There are a [variety of beliefs about the afterlife within Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_eschatology#World_to_come "Jewish eschatology"), but none of them contradict the preference for life over death. This is partially because death puts a cessation to the possibility of fulfilling any [commandments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitzvah "Mitzvah").

### Grief

A common human response to death is [grief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grief "Grief") and [mourning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mourning "Mourning"). While it is commonly discussed as an emotion, grief also has physical, cognitive, behavioral, social, cultural, spiritual, political and philosophical aspects. The state of being in grief is called "bereavement".

## In biology

Death plays a role in [extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinction "Extinction"), which is generally considered to be the death of the last individual of a [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species "Species"). After death, the remains of a former organism become part of the [biogeochemical cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biogeochemical_cycle "Biogeochemical cycle"), during which animals may be [consumed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necrophagy "Necrophagy") by a [predator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predator "Predator") or a [scavenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scavenger "Scavenger"). [Organic material](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_material "Organic material") may then be further [decomposed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decomposition "Decomposition") by [detritivores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detritivore "Detritivore"), organisms that recycle [detritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detritus "Detritus"), returning it to the environment for reuse in the [food chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_chain "Food chain"), where these chemicals may eventually end up being consumed and assimilated into the cells of an organism. Examples of detritivores include [earthworms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthworm "Earthworm"), [woodlice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodlice "Woodlice"), and [millipedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millipede "Millipede").

[Microorganisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microorganism "Microorganism") also play a vital role, raising the temperature of the decomposing matter as they break it down into yet simpler molecules. Not all materials need to be fully decomposed. [Coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal "Coal"), a [fossil fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel "Fossil fuel") formed over vast tracts of time in [swamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swamp "Swamp") ecosystems, is one example.

### Natural selection

The contemporary [evolutionary theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_evolutionary_thought "History of evolutionary thought") sees death as an important part of the process of [natural selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_selection "Natural selection"). It is considered that organisms less [adapted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptation "Adaptation") to their environment are more likely to die, having produced fewer offspring, thereby reducing their contribution to the [gene pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_pool "Gene pool"). Their genes are thus eventually bred out of a population, leading at worst to [extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinction "Extinction") and, more positively, making the process possible, referred to as [speciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speciation "Speciation"). Frequency of [reproduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_reproduction "Biological reproduction") plays an equally important role in determining species survival: an organism that dies young but leaves numerous offspring displays, according to [Darwinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin "Charles Darwin") criteria, much greater [fitness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwinian_fitness "Darwinian fitness") than a long-lived organism leaving only one.

Death also has a role in [competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competition_(biology) "Competition (biology)"), where if a species out-competes another, there is a risk of death for the population, especially in the case where they are directly fighting over resources.

### Evolution of aging and mortality

Inquiry into the evolution of aging aims to explain why so many living things and the vast majority of animals weaken and die with age. However, there are exceptions, such as *[Hydra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydra_(genus) "Hydra (genus)")* and the jellyfish *[Turritopsis dohrnii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turritopsis_dohrnii "Turritopsis dohrnii")*, which research shows to be [biologically immortal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_immortality "Biological immortality").

Organisms showing only [asexual reproduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asexual_reproduction "Asexual reproduction"), such as bacteria, some [protists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protist "Protist"), like the [euglenoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euglenoid "Euglenoid") and many [amoebozoans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amoebozoan "Amoebozoan"), and [unicellular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicellular "Unicellular") organisms with [sexual reproduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_reproduction "Sexual reproduction"), [colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_(biology) "Colony (biology)") or not, like the [volvocine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvocales "Volvocales") algae *[Pandorina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandorina "Pandorina")* and *[Chlamydomonas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlamydomonas "Chlamydomonas"),* are "immortal" at some extent, dying only due to external hazards, like being eaten or meeting with a fatal accident. In [multicellular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multicellular "Multicellular") organisms and also in [multinucleate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinucleate "Multinucleate") [ciliates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciliates "Ciliates") with a [Weismannist development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weismann_barrier "Weismann barrier"), that is, with a division of labor between mortal [somatic (body) cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somatic_cells "Somatic cells") and "immortal" [germ (reproductive) cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germ_cell "Germ cell"), death becomes an essential part of life, at least for the somatic line.

The *[Volvox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvox "Volvox")* algae are among the simplest organisms to exhibit that division of labor between two completely different cell types, and as a consequence, include the death of somatic line as a regular, genetically regulated part of its [life history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_life_cycle "Biological life cycle").

### Cell death

[Cell death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_death "Cell death") is the event of a [biological cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_cell "Biological cell") ceasing to carry out its functions. This may be the result of the natural process of old cells dying and being replaced by new ones, as in [programmed cell death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programmed_cell_death "Programmed cell death"), or may result from factors such as [diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disease "Disease"), localized [injury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injury "Injury"), or the death of the organism of which the cells are part. [Apoptosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apoptosis "Apoptosis") or type I cell-death, and [autophagy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autophagy_(cellular) "Autophagy (cellular)") or type II cell-death are both forms of programmed cell death, while [necrosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necrosis "Necrosis") is a non-physiological process that occurs as a result of infection or injury.

The term "cell necrobiology" has been used to describe the life processes associated with morphological, biochemical, and molecular changes which predispose, precede, and accompany cell death, as well as the consequences and tissue response to cell death. The word is derived from the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language "Greek language") [νεκρό](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%CE%BD%CE%B5%CE%BA%CF%81%CF%8C#Ancient_Greek "wikt:νεκρό") meaning "death", βìο meaning "life", and [λόγος](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logos "Logos") meaning "the study of". The term was initially coined to broadly define investigations of the changes that accompany cell death, detected and measured by multiparameter flow- and laser scanning- cytometry. It has been used to describe the real-time changes during cell death, detected by flow cytometry.

## Language

The word "death" comes from [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English "Old English") *dēaþ*, which in turn comes from [Proto-Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Germanic_language "Proto-Germanic language") \**dauþuz* (reconstructed by etymological analysis). This comes from the [Proto-Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European_language "Proto-Indo-European language") stem \**dheu-* meaning the "process, act, condition of [dying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dying "Dying")."

The concept and symptoms of death, and varying degrees of delicacy used in discussion in public forums, have generated numerous scientific, legal, and socially acceptable terms or euphemisms. When a person has died, it is also said they have "passed away", "passed on", "expired", or "gone", among other socially accepted, religiously specific, slang, and irreverent terms. [*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

As a formal reference to a dead person, it has become common practice to use the [participle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Participle "Participle") form of "decease", as in "the deceased"; another noun form is "[decedent](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/decedent "wikt:decedent")".

Bereft of life, the dead person is a "corpse", "[cadaver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadaver "Cadaver")", "body", "set of remains", or when all flesh is gone, a "[skeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeleton "Skeleton")". The terms "[carrion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrion "Carrion")" and "carcass" are also used, usually for dead non-human animals. The ashes left after a [cremation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremation "Cremation") are lately called "cremains".
