# Taylor Swift

**Taylor Alison Swift** (born December 13, 1989) is an American singer-songwriter. [An influential figure in popular culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_impact_of_Taylor_Swift "Cultural impact of Taylor Swift"), Swift is known for her autobiographical songwriting and artistic reinventions. She is the [highest-grossing live music artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_live_music_artists "List of highest-grossing live music artists"), the [wealthiest female musician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_music_artists_by_net_worth "List of music artists by net worth"), and one of the [best-selling music artists of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best-selling_music_artists_of_all_time "Best-selling music artists of all time").

Swift signed with [Big Machine Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Machine_Records "Big Machine Records") in 2005 and debuted as a [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music") singer with the albums *[Taylor Swift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift_(album) "Taylor Swift (album)")* (2006) and *[Fearless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fearless_(Taylor_Swift_album) "Fearless (Taylor Swift album)")* (2008). The singles "[Teardrops on My Guitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teardrops_on_My_Guitar "Teardrops on My Guitar")", "[Love Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Story_(Taylor_Swift_song) "Love Story (Taylor Swift song)")", and "[You Belong with Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_Belong_with_Me "You Belong with Me")" found [crossover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossover_music "Crossover music") success on [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_radio "Country radio") and [pop radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_radio "Pop radio") formats. Her songs began incorporating stronger [rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_music "Rock music") elements with *[Speak Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speak_Now "Speak Now")* (2010) and [electronic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_music "Electronic music") styles with *[Red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_(Taylor_Swift_album) "Red (Taylor Swift album)")* (2012). She subsequently recalibrated her artistic identity from country to [pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_music "Pop music") with the [synth-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synth-pop "Synth-pop") album *[1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_(album) "1989 (album)")* (2014), while ensuing media scrutiny inspired the [trap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trap_music "Trap music")-imbued *[Reputation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reputation_(album) "Reputation (album)")* (2017). Through the 2010s, Swift accumulated the US [*Billboard* Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100 "Billboard Hot 100") number-one singles "[We Are Never Ever Getting Back Together](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Are_Never_Ever_Getting_Back_Together "We Are Never Ever Getting Back Together")", "[Shake It Off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shake_It_Off "Shake It Off")", "[Blank Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blank_Space "Blank Space")", "[Bad Blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Blood_(Taylor_Swift_song) "Bad Blood (Taylor Swift song)")", and "[Look What You Made Me Do](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Look_What_You_Made_Me_Do "Look What You Made Me Do")".

Shifting to [Republic Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Records "Republic Records") in 2018, Swift released the [eclectic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclecticism_in_music "Eclecticism in music") pop album *[Lover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lover_(album) "Lover (album)")* (2019) and [re-recorded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Re-recorded "Re-recorded") four of her first six albums due to [a dispute with Big Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift_masters_dispute "Taylor Swift masters dispute"). She explored [indie folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_folk "Indie folk") on the 2020 albums *[Folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore_(Taylor_Swift_album) "Folklore (Taylor Swift album)")* and *[Evermore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evermore "Evermore")*, followed by mellower pop subgenres on *[Midnights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnights "Midnights")* (2022) and *[The Tortured Poets Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tortured_Poets_Department "The Tortured Poets Department")* (2024), as well as [soft rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_rock "Soft rock") on *[The Life of a Showgirl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Life_of_a_Showgirl "The Life of a Showgirl")* (2025). The singles "[Cardigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardigan_(song) "Cardigan (song)")", "[Willow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willow_(song) "Willow (song)")", "[All Too Well (Taylor's Version)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Too_Well_(Taylor%27s_Version) "All Too Well (Taylor's Version)")", "[Anti-Hero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Hero_(song) "Anti-Hero (song)")", "[Cruel Summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruel_Summer_(Taylor_Swift_song) "Cruel Summer (Taylor Swift song)")", "[Is It Over Now?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Is_It_Over_Now%3F "Is It Over Now?")", "[Fortnight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortnight_(song) "Fortnight (song)")", "[The Fate of Ophelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fate_of_Ophelia "The Fate of Ophelia")", "[Opalite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opalite_(song) "Opalite (song)")", "[I Knew It, I Knew You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Knew_It,_I_Knew_You "I Knew It, I Knew You")", and "[Patient Zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patient_Zero_(song) "Patient Zero (song)")" topped the Hot 100. Her [Eras Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eras_Tour "Eras Tour") (2023–2024) and its associated film, *[Taylor Swift: The Eras Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift:_The_Eras_Tour "Taylor Swift: The Eras Tour")* (2023), are the [highest-grossing concert tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_concert_tours "List of highest-grossing concert tours") and [concert film of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concert_film#Highest-grossing_concert_films "Concert film").

Swift is the only artist to have been named the [IFPI Global Recording Artist of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFPI_Global_Recording_Artist_of_the_Year "IFPI Global Recording Artist of the Year") six times. A record eight of her albums have each sold over a million copies first-week in the US. Publications such as *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")* and [*Billboard*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_(magazine) "Billboard (magazine)") have ranked Swift among the greatest artists of all time. She is the first individual from [the arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_arts "The arts") to be named [*Time* Person of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Person_of_the_Year "Time Person of the Year") (2023) and the youngest female inductee into the [Songwriters Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songwriters_Hall_of_Fame "Songwriters Hall of Fame") (2026). [Her accolades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Taylor_Swift "List of awards and nominations received by Taylor Swift") include 14 [Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Awards "Grammy Awards")—including a record four [Album of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Album_of_the_Year "Grammy Award for Album of the Year") wins—and a [Primetime Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award "Primetime Emmy Award"). Swift is the most-awarded artist of the [American Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Music_Awards "American Music Awards"), the [*Billboard* Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Music_Awards "Billboard Music Awards"), and the [MTV Video Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Awards "MTV Video Music Awards"). A subject of [extensive media coverage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_image_of_Taylor_Swift "Public image of Taylor Swift"), she has a global fanbase known as [Swifties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swifties "Swifties").

## Life and career

### Early life

Taylor Alison Swift was born on December 13, 1989, in [West Reading, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Reading,_Pennsylvania "West Reading, Pennsylvania"). She is named after the singer-songwriter [James Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Taylor "James Taylor"); her parents chose a unisex name, hoping it would help her succeed in business. Her father, Scott Kingsley Swift, was a [stockbroker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockbroker "Stockbroker") for [Merrill Lynch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merrill_Lynch "Merrill Lynch"), and her mother, Andrea Gardner Swift ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") Finlay), worked as a [mutual fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_fund "Mutual fund") marketing executive. Swift's younger brother, [Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Swift "Austin Swift"), is an actor who manages her film projects. Taylor and Austin are of Scottish, English, and German descent, with distant Irish and Italian ancestry; they are paternal great-great-grandchildren of [Charles Carmine Antonio Baldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Carmine_Antonio_Baldi "Charles Carmine Antonio Baldi"), a prominent Philadelphia businessman. Their maternal grandmother, [Marjorie Finlay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjorie_Finlay "Marjorie Finlay") ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") Moehlenkamp), was an opera singer whose singing in church became one of Swift's earliest memories of music.

During childhood, Swift spent her [holiday seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holiday_season "Holiday season") on a [Christmas tree farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_tree_farm "Christmas tree farm") in Pennsylvania, and summers at her family's vacation home in [Stone Harbor, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Harbor,_New_Jersey "Stone Harbor, New Jersey"), where she occasionally performed acoustic songs at a local coffee shop. Raised Christian, she attended preschool and kindergarten at a [Montessori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montessori "Montessori") school run by the [Bernardine Sisters of St. Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardine_Sisters_of_St._Francis "Bernardine Sisters of St. Francis") before transferring to the Wyndcroft School in [Pottstown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pottstown,_Pennsylvania "Pottstown, Pennsylvania"). When her family moved to [Wyomissing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyomissing "Wyomissing"), she attended [Wyomissing Area Junior/Senior High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyomissing_Area_Junior/Senior_High_School "Wyomissing Area Junior/Senior High School"). At age nine, she aspired to a career in [musical theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_theater "Musical theater"), performing at local festivals and in Berks Youth Theatre Academy productions, and traveling regularly to New York City for vocal and acting lessons. After watching a documentary about [Faith Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith_Hill "Faith Hill"), she changed her goal and became determined to pursue a [country music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music") career in [Nashville, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville,_Tennessee "Nashville, Tennessee").

At the age of 11, Swift traveled to Nashville with her mother to visit record labels and submit [demo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demo_(music) "Demo (music)") tapes of [Dolly Parton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_Parton "Dolly Parton") and [Dixie Chicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dixie_Chicks "Dixie Chicks") karaoke covers. She was rejected by all the labels, which led her to focus on songwriting. She started learning the guitar at the age of 12 with the help of a computer repairman and local musician who assisted Swift in writing an original song. In 2003, she and her parents started working with the talent manager Dan Dymtrow. With his help, Swift modeled for [Abercrombie & Fitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abercrombie_%26_Fitch "Abercrombie & Fitch"), had an original song included on a [Maybelline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maybelline "Maybelline") compilation CD, and was given an [artist development deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artist_development_deal "Artist development deal") from [RCA Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCA_Records "RCA Records") at 13. To help Swift break into the country music scene, her father transferred to Merrill Lynch's Nashville office when she was 14 years old, and the family relocated to [Hendersonville, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendersonville,_Tennessee "Hendersonville, Tennessee"). Swift attended [Hendersonville High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendersonville_High_School_(Tennessee) "Hendersonville High School (Tennessee)") for two years before transferring to Aaron Academy, which offered homeschooling.

### 2004–2008: Career beginnings and *Taylor Swift*

Swift signed with [Sony/ATV Tree Music Publishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony/ATV_Music_Publishing "Sony/ATV Music Publishing") in 2004; at 14, she became the youngest person in that publishing company's history. In Nashville, she worked with experienced [Music Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_Row "Music Row") songwriters, including [Liz Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liz_Rose "Liz Rose"). Rose and Swift would write songs every Tuesday afternoon after school. After one year on the development deal, she left RCA Records, which was planning to keep her in development until she turned 18. Swift made this decision because she wanted to release the songs immediately to ensure that they still resonated with her teenage experiences.

Swift organized a showcase concert at the [Bluebird Cafe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluebird_Cafe "Bluebird Cafe") on November 3, 2004; among the attendees was [Scott Borchetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Borchetta "Scott Borchetta"), a music executive who was planning to establish an [independent record label](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_record_label "Independent record label") called [Big Machine Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Machine_Records "Big Machine Records"). She signed a [recording contract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_contract "Recording contract") with Big Machine two weeks after the concert, on the condition that her albums would be self-written; her father purchased a three-percent stake in the company. The contract was finalized by July 2005, when Swift ended her working relationship with Dymtrow. She spent four months near the end of 2005 recording her debut album, [*Taylor Swift*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift_(album) "Taylor Swift (album)"), with the producer [Nathan Chapman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Chapman_(record_producer) "Nathan Chapman (record producer)").

Swift's debut single, "[Tim McGraw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_McGraw_(song) "Tim McGraw (song)")", was released in June 2006. She and her mother spent mid-2006 sending promotional copies of the song to [country radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_radio "Country radio") stations across the US. *Taylor Swift* was released on October 24, 2006. On the US [*Billboard* 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200") chart, the album peaked at number five and spent 157 weeks on the chart—the longest chart run by an album in the 2000s. With *Taylor Swift*, she became the first female country music artist to write or co-write every track on a [platinum-certified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum-certified "Platinum-certified") debut album. Swift promoted the album with a six-month radio tour and by [opening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opening_act "Opening act") for other country artists, including [Rascal Flatts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rascal_Flatts "Rascal Flatts") in 2006, and [George Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Strait "George Strait"), [Brad Paisley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Paisley "Brad Paisley"), and [Tim McGraw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_McGraw "Tim McGraw") and Faith Hill in 2007. She opened for Rascal Flatts again in 2008, when she dated the singer [Joe Jonas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Jonas "Joe Jonas").

*Taylor Swift* was supported by four more singles in 2007 and 2008: "[Teardrops on My Guitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teardrops_on_My_Guitar "Teardrops on My Guitar")", "[Our Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Song "Our Song")", "[Picture to Burn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picture_to_Burn "Picture to Burn")", and "[Should've Said No](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Should%27ve_Said_No "Should've Said No")". "Our Song" and "Should've Said No" reached number one on the [Hot Country Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Country_Songs "Hot Country Songs") chart; with the former single, Swift became the youngest person to single-handedly write and sing a number-one country single. "Teardrops on My Guitar" was Swift's breakthrough single on mainstream radio and charts, reaching the top 10 of the [Pop Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_Songs "Pop Songs"), [Adult Pop Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_Pop_Songs "Adult Pop Songs"), and [Adult Contemporary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_Contemporary_(chart) "Adult Contemporary (chart)") charts. Her next releases were the [Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_music "Christmas music") [extended play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_play "Extended play") (EP) *[The Taylor Swift Holiday Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Taylor_Swift_Holiday_Collection "The Taylor Swift Holiday Collection")* in October 2007, and the [Walmart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walmart "Walmart")-exclusive EP *[Beautiful Eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beautiful_Eyes "Beautiful Eyes")* in July 2008. Swift became the youngest person to be awarded with the [Nashville Songwriters Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_Songwriters_Association_International "Nashville Songwriters Association International")'s Songwriter/Artist of the Year in 2007.

### 2008–2010: *Fearless*

Swift's second album, *[Fearless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fearless_(Taylor_Swift_album) "Fearless (Taylor Swift album)")*, was released on November 11, 2008, in North America, and in March 2009 in other markets. *Fearless* spent eleven weeks at number one on the *Billboard* 200, becoming her first chart topping album and the longest-running number-one female country album; it was the best-selling album of 2009 in the US. The album's lead single, "[Love Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Story_(Taylor_Swift_song) "Love Story (Taylor Swift song)")", became the first country song to top the Pop Songs chart, and its third single, "[You Belong with Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_Belong_with_Me "You Belong with Me")", was the first country song to top *[Billboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_(magazine) "Billboard (magazine)")*'s all-genre [Radio Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Songs_(chart) "Radio Songs (chart)") chart; both reached the top five of the [*Billboard* Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100 "Billboard Hot 100") chart and peaked atop the Hot Country Songs chart. Three other singles—"[White Horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Horse_(Taylor_Swift_song) "White Horse (Taylor Swift song)")", "[Fifteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifteen_(song) "Fifteen (song)")", "[Fearless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fearless_(Taylor_Swift_song) "Fearless (Taylor Swift song)")"—all reached the top 10 of Hot Country Songs. In 2009, Swift opened for [Keith Urban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Urban "Keith Urban")'s tour and embarked on her first headlining concert tour, the [Fearless Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fearless_Tour "Fearless Tour") (2009–2010).

*Fearless* became the most-awarded country album of all time. It won the three highest awards for a country album: Album of the Year by both the [Country Music Association Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Music_Association_Awards "Country Music Association Awards") and [Academy of Country Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Country_Music_Awards "Academy of Country Music Awards") in 2009, and [Best Country Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Country_Album "Best Country Album") by the [Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Awards "Grammy Awards") in 2010. At the Grammy Awards, *Fearless* also won [Album of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Album_of_the_Year "Grammy Award for Album of the Year"), and "White Horse" won [Best Country Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Country_Song "Best Country Song") and [Best Female Country Vocal Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Female_Country_Vocal_Performance "Best Female Country Vocal Performance"). Also in 2009, Swift was named Artist of the Year by both the [American Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Music_Awards_of_2009 "American Music Awards of 2009") and *Billboard*, and Entertainer of the Year by the Country Music Association Awards, becoming the youngest person to receive the honor. "You Belong with Me" won [Best Female Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Female_Video "Best Female Video") at the [MTV Video Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Awards "MTV Video Music Awards"). Her acceptance speech was interrupted by the rapper [Kanye West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanye_West "Kanye West"), [an incident that became the subject of controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_MTV_Video_Music_Awards#Kanye_West–Taylor_Swift_incident "2009 MTV Video Music Awards") and widespread media coverage.

Swift featured in 2009 on "[Half of My Heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_of_My_Heart "Half of My Heart")" by [John Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Mayer "John Mayer"), with whom she was romantically linked later that year. She wrote "[Best Days of Your Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Days_of_Your_Life "Best Days of Your Life")" for [Kellie Pickler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kellie_Pickler "Kellie Pickler"), co-wrote and featured on [Boys Like Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boys_Like_Girls "Boys Like Girls")' "[Two Is Better Than One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Is_Better_Than_One "Two Is Better Than One"), and wrote "[You'll Always Find Your Way Back Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You%27ll_Always_Find_Your_Way_Back_Home "You'll Always Find Your Way Back Home")" and wrote and recorded "[Crazier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crazier "Crazier")" for the [soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannah_Montana:_The_Movie_(soundtrack) "Hannah Montana: The Movie (soundtrack)") of *[Hannah Montana: The Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannah_Montana:_The_Movie "Hannah Montana: The Movie")*, in which she also had a cameo appearance. She made her acting debut in the 2010 rom-com *[Valentine's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentine%27s_Day_(2010_film) "Valentine's Day (2010 film)")* and wrote "[Today Was a Fairytale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Today_Was_a_Fairytale "Today Was a Fairytale")" for [its soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentine%27s_Day_(soundtrack) "Valentine's Day (soundtrack)"). "Today Was a Fairytale" reached number one on the [Canadian Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Hot_100 "Canadian Hot 100"). While shooting *Valentine's Day* in October 2009, Swift dated co-star [Taylor Lautner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Lautner "Taylor Lautner"). She made her television debut as a rebellious teenager in a *[CSI: Crime Scene Investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSI:_Crime_Scene_Investigation "CSI: Crime Scene Investigation")* episode and hosted and performed as the musical guest on *[Saturday Night Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live "Saturday Night Live")*; she was the first host to write their own opening monologue.

### 2010–2014: *Speak Now* and *Red*

Swift wrote her third album, *[Speak Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speak_Now "Speak Now")*, entirely by herself. Released on October 25, 2010, it debuted atop the US *Billboard* 200 with over one million first-week copies sold—the highest single-week tally for a female country artist. Five of its singles—"[Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mine_(Taylor_Swift_song) "Mine (Taylor Swift song)")", "[Back to December](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_December "Back to December")", "[Mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_(song) "Mean (song)")", "[Sparks Fly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparks_Fly_(song) "Sparks Fly (song)")", and "[Ours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ours_(song) "Ours (song)")"—charted in the top three of Hot Country Songs; "Sparks Fly" and "Ours" reached number one. "Mine" peaked at number three and was the album's highest-charting single on the *Billboard* Hot 100, and "Mean" became its best-selling track. Swift supported the album with the [Speak Now World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speak_Now_World_Tour "Speak Now World Tour") (2011–2012) and its accompanying live album, *[Speak Now World Tour – Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speak_Now_World_Tour_%E2%80%93_Live "Speak Now World Tour – Live")* (2011).

In 2011, Swift was honored as Woman of the Year by *Billboard*, Entertainer of the Year by both the Academy of Country Music Awards and the Country Music Association Awards, and Artist of the Year at the American Music Awards. She again won Entertainer of the Year at the Academy of Country Music Awards in 2012. At the [54th Annual Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/54th_Annual_Grammy_Awards "54th Annual Grammy Awards") in 2012, "Mean" won Best Country Song and [Best Country Solo Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Country_Solo_Performance "Best Country Solo Performance"). After the release of *Speak Now*, Swift briefly dated the actor [Jake Gyllenhaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Gyllenhaal "Jake Gyllenhaal").

On October 22, 2012, Swift released her fourth album, *[Red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_(Taylor_Swift_album) "Red (Taylor Swift album)")*, which she produced with Chapman and several new collaborators, including [Max Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Martin "Max Martin"), [Shellback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shellback_(record_producer) "Shellback (record producer)"), [Dan Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Wilson_(musician) "Dan Wilson (musician)"), [Jeff Bhasker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Bhasker "Jeff Bhasker"), [Dann Huff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dann_Huff "Dann Huff"), and [Butch Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butch_Walker "Butch Walker"). It debuted at number one on the *Billboard* 200 with 1.21 million sales, becoming the fastest-selling country album in US history. *Red* was Swift's first number-one album in the UK. During the promotion of *Red*, Swift was romantically involved with the political heir [Conor Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_family "Kennedy family"), and subsequently the singer [Harry Styles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Styles "Harry Styles").

The two most successful singles from *Red*, "[We Are Never Ever Getting Back Together](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Are_Never_Ever_Getting_Back_Together "We Are Never Ever Getting Back Together")" and "[I Knew You Were Trouble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Knew_You_Were_Trouble "I Knew You Were Trouble")", peaked at numbers one and two on the *Billboard* Hot 100. Both songs also reached the top five on the [UK singles chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_singles_chart "UK singles chart"), and the former was Swift's first chart topper in the US. The singles "[Begin Again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Begin_Again_(Taylor_Swift_song) "Begin Again (Taylor Swift song)")" and "[Red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_(Taylor_Swift_song) "Red (Taylor Swift song)")" peaked in the top 10 of the *Billboard* Hot 100, while "[Everything Has Changed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everything_Has_Changed "Everything Has Changed")" and "[22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22_(Taylor_Swift_song) "22 (Taylor Swift song)")" reached the top 10 on the UK singles chart. [The Red Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Red_Tour "The Red Tour") ran from March 2013 to June 2014 and became the highest-grossing country tour with revenue of $150.2 million upon completion. Swift was named Artist of the Year at the American Music Awards in 2013.

Swift wrote and recorded two songs for [the soundtrack album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games:_Songs_from_District_12_and_Beyond "The Hunger Games: Songs from District 12 and Beyond") to the 2012 dystopian film *[The Hunger Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games_(film) "The Hunger Games (film)")*: "[Eyes Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyes_Open_(song) "Eyes Open (song)")" and "[Safe & Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safe_%26_Sound_(Taylor_Swift_song) "Safe & Sound (Taylor Swift song)")". The latter, co-written with [the Civil Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Civil_Wars "The Civil Wars") and [T-Bone Burnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-Bone_Burnett "T-Bone Burnett"), won the [Grammy Award for Best Song Written for Visual Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Song_Written_for_Visual_Media "Grammy Award for Best Song Written for Visual Media") in 2013. She wrote and recorded "[Sweeter than Fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweeter_than_Fiction "Sweeter than Fiction")" for the soundtrack to the 2013 biographical film *[One Chance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Chance_(film) "One Chance (film)")*, and featured as a guest vocalist on [B.o.B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.o.B "B.o.B")'s 2012 single "[Both of Us](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Both_of_Us "Both of Us")" and Tim McGraw's 2013 single "[Highway Don't Care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_Don%27t_Care "Highway Don't Care")". Her acting roles included a voice acting role in the 2012 animated film *[The Lorax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lorax_(film) "The Lorax (film)")* and a supporting role in the 2014 dystopian film [*The Giver*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Giver_(film) "The Giver (film)").

### 2014–2018: *1989* and *Reputation*

Swift relocated from Nashville to New York City in March 2014 and transformed her image from country to [pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_music "Pop music") with her fifth album, *[1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_(album) "1989 (album)")*. She produced *1989* with Martin, Shellback, Chapman, and new collaborators [Jack Antonoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Antonoff "Jack Antonoff"), [Imogen Heap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imogen_Heap "Imogen Heap"), [Ryan Tedder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Tedder "Ryan Tedder"), and [Ali Payami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Payami "Ali Payami"). Released on October 27, 2014, the album spent eleven weeks at number one and one year in the top 10 of the *Billboard* 200. It has sold 14 million copies worldwide, becoming Swift's best-selling album. Three of *1989*'s singles—"[Shake It Off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shake_It_Off "Shake It Off")", "[Blank Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blank_Space "Blank Space")", and "[Bad Blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Blood_(Taylor_Swift_song) "Bad Blood (Taylor Swift song)")"—reached number one on the *Billboard* Hot 100; the first two made Swift [the first woman to replace herself at the top spot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Billboard_Hot_100_chart_achievements_and_milestones "List of Billboard Hot 100 chart achievements and milestones"). Two other singles—"[Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Style_(Taylor_Swift_song) "Style (Taylor Swift song)")" and "[Wildest Dreams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildest_Dreams "Wildest Dreams")"—peaked at numbers six and five, making *1989* the first album by Swift to have five consecutive top 10 singles on the Hot 100.

Swift supported *1989* with [the 1989 World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_1989_World_Tour "The 1989 World Tour"), the [highest-grossing tour of 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_concert_tours#Highest-grossing_tours_by_year "List of highest-grossing concert tours") with $250 million in revenue. She was named *Billboard*'s Woman of the Year and received the inaugural [Dick Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Clark "Dick Clark") Award for Excellence at the American Music Awards in 2014, and "Bad Blood" won [Video of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Award_for_Video_of_the_Year "MTV Video Music Award for Video of the Year") and [Best Collaboration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Award_for_Best_Collaboration "MTV Video Music Award for Best Collaboration") at the MTV Video Music Awards in 2015. At the [58th Annual Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/58th_Annual_Grammy_Awards "58th Annual Grammy Awards") in 2016, *1989* made Swift the first woman to win Album of the Year twice and also won [Best Pop Vocal Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Pop_Vocal_Album "Best Pop Vocal Album"), while "Bad Blood" won [Best Music Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Music_Video "Best Music Video"). The most-awarded pop album of all time, *1989* was selected for preservation in the [National Recording Registry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Recording_Registry "National Recording Registry") in 2026.

During the promotion of *1989*, Swift publicly opposed free [music streaming services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_streaming_service "Music streaming service") and their compensation of artists. She published an [op-ed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op-ed "Op-ed") in *[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")* in July 2014 to stress the importance of albums as a creative medium for artists, and, in November, removed her discography from ad-supported, free streaming platforms such as [Spotify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify "Spotify"). Big Machine kept her music only on paid, subscription-required platforms. In a June 2015 open letter, Swift criticized [Apple Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Music "Apple Music") for not offering royalties to artists during its free three-month trial period and threatened to withdraw her music from the platform, prompting [Apple Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Inc. "Apple Inc.") to announce that it would pay artists during the free trial period. Big Machine returned Swift's catalog to Spotify and other free streaming platforms in June 2017.

Swift dated the DJ [Calvin Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Harris "Calvin Harris") from March 2015 to June 2016. They co-wrote "[This Is What You Came For](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Is_What_You_Came_For "This Is What You Came For")", an [EDM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_dance_music "Electronic dance music") single by Harris featuring [Rihanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rihanna "Rihanna"); Swift was initially credited under the pseudonym Nils Sjöberg. Swift also wrote "[Better Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Better_Man_(Little_Big_Town_song) "Better Man (Little Big Town song)")", recorded by [Little Big Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Big_Town "Little Big Town") in 2016, which won the Country Music Association Award for Song of the Year. She wrote and recorded "[I Don't Wanna Live Forever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Don%27t_Wanna_Live_Forever "I Don't Wanna Live Forever")" with [Zayn Malik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zayn_Malik "Zayn Malik") for [the soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifty_Shades_Darker:_Original_Motion_Picture_Soundtrack "Fifty Shades Darker: Original Motion Picture Soundtrack") to the 2017 film *[Fifty Shades Darker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifty_Shades_Darker_(film) "Fifty Shades Darker (film)")*; it peaked at number two on the *Billboard* Hot 100, the highest for a [*Fifty Shades*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifty_Shades_(film_series) "Fifty Shades (film series)") single.

In April 2016, Kanye West released the single "[Famous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famous_(Kanye_West_song) "Famous (Kanye West song)")", in which he references Swift in the line, "I made that bitch famous." Swift criticized West and said she never consented to the lyric, but West claimed that he had received her approval, and his then-wife, [Kim Kardashian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Kardashian "Kim Kardashian"), released video clips of Swift and West discussing the song amicably over the phone. Although the clips were proven to be purposefully edited, the controversy made Swift a subject of an online "[cancel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancel_culture "Cancel culture")" movement, in which her critics denounced her as a fake and calculating "snake". In late 2016, after briefly dating the actor [Tom Hiddleston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Hiddleston "Tom Hiddleston"), Swift began a six-year relationship with the actor [Joe Alwyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Alwyn "Joe Alwyn") and went on hiatus.

In August 2017, Swift [countersued and won a case against David Mueller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift_sexual_assault_trial "Taylor Swift sexual assault trial"), a former [radio jockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_jockey "Radio jockey"), who sued her for damages from loss of employment. Four years earlier, she informed Mueller's employer that he had sexually assaulted her by groping her at an event. Public controversies influenced Swift's sixth album, *[Reputation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reputation_(album) "Reputation (album)")*, which explores themes of fame and finding love amidst the tumultuous affairs. Released on November 10, 2017, *Reputation* debuted atop the *Billboard* 200 with 1.21 million US sales and recorded first-week worldwide sales of two million copies.

*Reputation*'s lead single, "[Look What You Made Me Do](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Look_What_You_Made_Me_Do "Look What You Made Me Do")", topped the *Billboard* Hot 100 with the highest sales and streaming week of 2017, and was Swift's first UK number-one single. The singles "[...Ready for It?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/...Ready_for_It%3F "...Ready for It?")", "[End Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_Game_(song) "End Game (song)")", and "[Delicate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delicate_(Taylor_Swift_song) "Delicate (Taylor Swift song)")" were released to pop radio and all peaked in the top 20 of the *Billboard* Hot 100. In 2018, Swift featured on [Sugarland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarland "Sugarland")'s "[Babe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babe_(Sugarland_song) "Babe (Sugarland song)")" and embarked on the [Reputation Stadium Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reputation_Stadium_Tour "Reputation Stadium Tour"), which grossed $345.7 million worldwide.

### 2018–2021: *Lover*, *Folklore*, and *Evermore*

In November 2018, Swift signed a record deal with [Universal Music Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Music_Group "Universal Music Group"), which promoted her albums under [Republic Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Records "Republic Records")' imprint. The contract included a provision allowing Swift to maintain ownership of her [master recordings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_recording "Master recording"). In addition, if Universal sold any part of its stake in Spotify, it agreed to distribute a non-recoupable portion of the proceeds to its artists.

Swift's first album with Republic Records and seventh overall, *[Lover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lover_(album) "Lover (album)")*, was released on August 23, 2019. She produced the album with Antonoff, [Louis Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Bell "Louis Bell"), [Frank Dukes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Dukes "Frank Dukes"), and [Joel Little](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Little "Joel Little"). *Lover* peaked atop the charts of such countries as Australia, Canada, Ireland, Mexico, Norway, Sweden, the UK, and the US, and was the global best-selling album by a solo artist of 2019. Three of its singles—"[Me!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Me! "Me!")", "[You Need to Calm Down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_Need_to_Calm_Down "You Need to Calm Down")", and "[Lover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lover_(Taylor_Swift_song) "Lover (Taylor Swift song)")"—were released in 2019 and peaked in the top 10 of the *Billboard* Hot 100. "[The Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Man_(Taylor_Swift_song) "The Man (Taylor Swift song)")" was released in 2020 and charted highly, and "[Cruel Summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruel_Summer_(Taylor_Swift_song) "Cruel Summer (Taylor Swift song)")" became a resurgent success in 2023, reaching number one. Swift promoted *Lover* with the one-off [City of Lover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Lover "City of Lover") concert, [its accompanying television special](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Lover#Video_and_music_releases "City of Lover"), and the live album *[Lover (Live from Paris)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lover_(Live_from_Paris) "Lover (Live from Paris)")*, which set the record for the largest vinyl sales week by a live album in the US.

In 2019, Swift was named Artist of the Decade by the American Music Awards and Woman of the Decade by *Billboard*, and became the first female artist to win the MTV Video Music Award for Video of the Year for a self-directed video, with "You Need to Calm Down". During the promotion of *Lover*, Swift became embroiled in [a public dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift_masters_dispute "Taylor Swift masters dispute") with the talent manager [Scooter Braun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scooter_Braun "Scooter Braun") after he purchased Big Machine Records, including the masters of her albums under the label. Swift said that Big Machine would allow her to acquire the masters only if she exchanged one new album for each older one under a new contract, which she declined. In November 2020, Swift began [re-recording](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Re-recording_(music) "Re-recording (music)") her back catalog to control the licensing of her songs for [commercial use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synchronization_rights "Synchronization rights").

In February 2020, Swift signed a global publishing deal with [Universal Music Publishing Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Music_Publishing_Group "Universal Music Publishing Group") after her 16-year contract with Sony/ATV expired. During the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"), Swift collaborated with Antonoff and [Aaron Dessner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Dessner "Aaron Dessner") on two "sister albums" [surprise-released](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surprise_album "Surprise album") in 2020: *[Folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore_(Taylor_Swift_album) "Folklore (Taylor Swift album)")* on July 24, and *[Evermore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evermore "Evermore")* on December 11. Joe Alwyn co-wrote and co-produced several songs under the pseudonym William Bowery. Both albums incorporate atmospheric [indie folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_folk "Indie folk") and [indie rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_rock "Indie rock") sounds with [orchestrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestration "Orchestration"); each had three singles sent to US pop, country, and [adult alternative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_album_alternative "Adult album alternative") radio—"[Cardigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardigan_(song) "Cardigan (song)")", "[Betty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_(Taylor_Swift_song) "Betty (Taylor Swift song)")", and "[Exile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exile_(Taylor_Swift_song) "Exile (Taylor Swift song)")" from *Folklore*, and "[Willow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willow_(song) "Willow (song)")", "[No Body, No Crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Body,_No_Crime "No Body, No Crime")", and "[Coney Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coney_Island_(Taylor_Swift_song) "Coney Island (Taylor Swift song)")" from *Evermore*. *Folklore* and "Cardigan" made Swift the first artist to debut a number-one album and a number-one song in the same week in the US; she achieved the feat again with *Evermore* and "Willow". Swift promoted *Folklore* with the documentary concert film *[Folklore: The Long Pond Studio Sessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore:_The_Long_Pond_Studio_Sessions "Folklore: The Long Pond Studio Sessions")* (2020); [its accompanying live album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore:_The_Long_Pond_Studio_Sessions_(From_the_Disney%2B_Special) "Folklore: The Long Pond Studio Sessions (From the Disney+ Special)") became the first [Record Store Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_Store_Day "Record Store Day")-exclusive album to reach the top 10 of the *Billboard* 200.

Swift won Artist of the Year at the American Music Awards in 2020 and Album of the Year for *Folklore* at the [63rd Annual Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/63rd_Annual_Grammy_Awards "63rd Annual Grammy Awards") in 2021, becoming the first woman to win the Grammy Award for Album of the Year three times. She played [Bombalurina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombalurina "Bombalurina") in the musical fantasy film *[Cats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cats_(2019_film) "Cats (2019 film)")* (2019), for which she co-wrote and recorded the original song "[Beautiful Ghosts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beautiful_Ghosts "Beautiful Ghosts")". The documentary *[Miss Americana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_Americana "Miss Americana")*, which chronicled parts of Swift's life and career, premiered at the [2020 Sundance Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Sundance_Film_Festival "2020 Sundance Film Festival") and received positive reviews.

### 2021–2023: Re-recordings and *Midnights*

Swift released two re-recorded albums in 2021: *[Fearless (Taylor's Version)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fearless_(Taylor%27s_Version) "Fearless (Taylor's Version)")* on April 9, and *[Red (Taylor's Version)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_(Taylor%27s_Version) "Red (Taylor's Version)")* on November 12. Both peaked atop the *Billboard* 200; the former was the first re-recorded album to do so. The latter helped Swift become the female musician with the most weeks at number one on the [Top Country Albums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Country_Albums "Top Country Albums") chart, and its song "[All Too Well (Taylor's Version)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Too_Well_(Taylor%27s_Version) "All Too Well (Taylor's Version)")" became the longest song to top the *Billboard* Hot 100.

Swift's tenth album, *[Midnights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnights "Midnights")*, was released on October 21, 2022. *Midnights* was Swift's fifth album to open atop the *Billboard* 200 chart with US first-week sales of one million. Its tracks, led by the single "[Anti-Hero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Hero_(song) "Anti-Hero (song)")", made her the first artist to occupy the entire top 10 of the *Billboard* Hot 100 the same week. The album peaked atop the charts of at least 14 other countries, including Australia, Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Norway, and Sweden. Two other singles, "[Lavender Haze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavender_Haze "Lavender Haze")" and "[Karma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karma_(Taylor_Swift_song) "Karma (Taylor Swift song)")", both peaked at number two on the *Billboard* Hot 100.

In 2023, Swift released two re-recorded albums: *[Speak Now (Taylor's Version)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speak_Now_(Taylor%27s_Version) "Speak Now (Taylor's Version)")* on July 7, and *[1989 (Taylor's Version)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_(Taylor%27s_Version) "1989 (Taylor's Version)")* on October 27. Both debuted atop the *Billboard* 200, and the former made Swift the female artist with the most number-one albums on the chart. The single "[Is It Over Now?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Is_It_Over_Now%3F "Is It Over Now?")" from *1989 (Taylor's Version)* reached number one on the *Billboard* Hot 100, making Swift the first woman to have three number-one songs from three different albums in a calendar year. Swift featured on [Big Red Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Red_Machine_(band) "Big Red Machine (band)")'s "[Renegade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renegade_(Big_Red_Machine_song) "Renegade (Big Red Machine song)")" and "Birch" (2021), [Haim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haim_(band) "Haim (band)")'s "[Gasoline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gasoline_(Haim_song) "Gasoline (Haim song)")" (2021), [Ed Sheeran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Sheeran "Ed Sheeran")'s "[The Joker and the Queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Joker_and_the_Queen "The Joker and the Queen")" (2022), and [the National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_National_(band) "The National (band)")'s "[The Alcott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Alcott "The Alcott")" (2023). She wrote and recorded "[Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolina_(Taylor_Swift_song) "Carolina (Taylor Swift song)")" for [the soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where_the_Crawdads_Sing_(soundtrack) "Where the Crawdads Sing (soundtrack)") to the 2022 mystery film *[Where the Crawdads Sing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where_the_Crawdads_Sing_(film) "Where the Crawdads Sing (film)")*, and had a supporting role in the 2022 period comedy film *[Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam_(2022_film) "Amsterdam (2022 film)")*. After splitting from Alwyn in April 2023, Swift dated the musician [Matty Healy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matty_Healy "Matty Healy") in May–June.

In 2022, Swift won Artist of the Year at the American Music Awards and Video of the Year for *[All Too Well: The Short Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Too_Well:_The_Short_Film "All Too Well: The Short Film")*, her self-directed short film that accompanies "All Too Well (10 Minute Version)", at the MTV Video Music Awards; *All Too Well* also won the Grammy Award for Best Music Video. The following year, she again won the MTV Video Music Award for Video of the Year with "Anti-Hero", became the first musician to rank at number one on *Billboard*'s year-end top artists list in three different decades (2009, 2015 and 2023), and had five out of the 10 best-selling albums of the year in the US, a record since [Luminate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luminate_(company) "Luminate (company)") began tracking US music sales in 1991. At the [66th Annual Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/66th_Annual_Grammy_Awards "66th Annual Grammy Awards") in 2024, *Midnights* made Swift the first artist to win Album of the Year four times; it also won Best Pop Vocal Album.

### 2023–present: The Eras Tour, *The Tortured Poets Department*, and *The Life of a Showgirl*

In March 2023, Swift embarked on [the Eras Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Eras_Tour "The Eras Tour"), which she conceived as a tribute to her entire discography. The tour spanned five continents through December 2024. It exerted [a global cultural, economic, and political impact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_of_the_Eras_Tour "Impact of the Eras Tour") and culminated in an unprecedented height of popularity for Swift, resulting in a phenomenon that the media dubbed "Swiftmania". The Eras Tour became the first tour to gross $1 billion and the [highest-grossing tour in history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_concert_tours "List of highest-grossing concert tours"), with $2 billion in revenue. [Its 2023 concert film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift:_The_Eras_Tour "Taylor Swift: The Eras Tour") became the highest-grossing of its kind with $250 million, and [its 2024 photobook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Eras_Tour_Book "The Eras Tour Book") sold nearly one million copies in its first week in the US, followed by [a 2025 documentary miniseries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift:_The_End_of_an_Era "Taylor Swift: The End of an Era") on [Disney+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney%2B "Disney+"). The events that happened during the tour inspired Swift's eleventh and twelfth albums, *[The Tortured Poets Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tortured_Poets_Department "The Tortured Poets Department")* (2024) and *[The Life of a Showgirl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Life_of_a_Showgirl "The Life of a Showgirl")* (2025).

During the run of the Eras Tour, there were controversies surrounding [Ticketmaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticketmaster "Ticketmaster")'s monopoly that led to [political scrutiny in the US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift%E2%80%93Ticketmaster_controversy "Taylor Swift–Ticketmaster controversy"), venue mismanagement that led to [a concertgoer's death in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Ana_Clara_Benevides "Death of Ana Clara Benevides"), and Singapore's exclusivity deal that led to political tension in Southeast Asia. In January 2024, [AI-generated pornographic images portraying Swift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift_deepfake_pornography_controversy "Taylor Swift deepfake pornography controversy") were posted to [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter") and spread to other social media platforms, spurring criticism and demands for legal reform. In July 2024, [three children were killed in a stabbing attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Southport_stabbing "2024 Southport stabbing") at a Swift-themed workshop in [Southport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southport "Southport"), England, leading to [civil unrest in the UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Kingdom_riots "2024 United Kingdom riots"). *The Tortured Poets Department* was released on April 19, 2024. The same day, it was expanded into a [double album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_album "Double album"), subtitled *The Anthology*. ## Artistry

### Musical styles

With continuous musical reinventions, Swift has been described as a "musical chameleon" by journalists and music analysts alike. Her discography primarily spans country, pop, and [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") genres, incorporating various styles such as [country pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_pop "Country pop"), [country rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_rock "Country rock"), [electropop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electropop "Electropop"), indie rock, [pop rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_rock "Pop rock"), [soft rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_rock "Soft rock"), and [synth-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synth-pop "Synth-pop"), with elements of [electronica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronica "Electronica"), [hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip-hop "Hip-hop"), [R&B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_R%26B "Contemporary R&B"), and [rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_music "Rock music"). Swift self-identified as a country musician with her first four albums, influenced by 1990s female country artists such as Shania Twain, Faith Hill, [LeAnn Rimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeAnn_Rimes "LeAnn Rimes"), and the Dixie Chicks, as well as Keith Urban's incorporation of rock, pop, and [blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues "Blues") into country music. The albums feature a country pop sound defined by instruments such as the [six-string banjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six-string_banjo "Six-string banjo"), [mandolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandolin "Mandolin"), and [fiddle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiddle "Fiddle"), a slight [twang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twang "Twang") in Swift's vocals, and pop rock melodies; *Speak Now* expanded this sound with 1970s and 1980s rock influences, including [pop-punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop-punk "Pop-punk") and [arena rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_rock "Arena rock"). Some critics argued that country signified Swift's songwriting more than her musical style, and accused her of causing mainstream country to stray from its roots.

*Red*'s incorporation of [eclectic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclecticism_in_music "Eclecticism in music") pop and rock styles, including [Britrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britrock "Britrock"), [dance-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance-pop "Dance-pop"), and [dubstep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubstep "Dubstep"), led to a critical debate over Swift's status as a country musician. Swift then chose 1980s synth-pop to recalibrate her artistic identity, inspired by the music of [Phil Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Collins "Phil Collins"), [Annie Lennox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_Lennox "Annie Lennox"), [Peter Gabriel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Gabriel "Peter Gabriel"), and [Madonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna "Madonna"). *1989*, the first album in this direction, incorporates [electronic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_music "Electronic music") arrangements consisting of dense [synthesizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthesizers "Synthesizers") and [drum machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drum_machine "Drum machine"). Swift expanded on the electronic production on her next albums. *Reputation* features a [trap-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trap-pop "Trap-pop") production with elements of hip-hop, R&B, and EDM, and [maximalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximalism_(music) "Maximalism (music)") arrangements of heavy [bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bass_(sound) "Bass (sound)") and manipulated vocals. *Lover* is a pop album with a 1980s-influenced sound combining [dream pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream_pop "Dream pop"), synth-pop, and pop rock. *Midnights* and *The Tortured Poets Department* both have a [minimalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_music "Minimal music") synth-pop sound characterized by [analog synthesizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_synthesizers "Analog synthesizers"), sustained [bass notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bass_note "Bass note"), and simple [drum machine patterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drum_pattern "Drum pattern"), although *The Tortured Poets Department* features a more understated production. *The Life of a Showgirl* incorporates pop and [soft rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_rock "Soft rock"), featuring [new wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_wave_music "New wave music")-leaning compositions and prominent acoustic guitars. When Swift embraced a pop identity, [rockist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockist "Rockist") critics regarded it as an erosion of her country songwriting authenticity, while others considered it necessary for her artistic evolution and defended her as a pioneer of [poptimism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poptimism "Poptimism").

Her 2020 albums *Folklore* and *Evermore*, described by some critics as "alternative", explore indie folk and rock styles. They incorporate a subtle, stripped-back soundscape with orchestrations, muted synthesizers, and [drum pads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drum_pad "Drum pad"). *Evermore* experiments with varied [song structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_structure "Song structure"), asymmetric [time signatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_signature "Time signature"), and diverse instruments. Critics deemed the indie styles a mature representation of Swift's singer-songwriter status and credited her with popularizing "alternative" music, although there was disagreement over this description.

Swift possesses a [mezzo-soprano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mezzo-soprano "Mezzo-soprano") singing voice, but she mostly sings in her [alto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alto "Alto") range. Her [pitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitch_(music) "Pitch (music)"), [dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialect "Dialect"), and [accent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accent_(sociolinguistics) "Accent (sociolinguistics)") have undergone many changes throughout her career. Reviews of her early country albums criticized her vocals as weak and strained compared to those of other female country singers. Defenders of Swift appreciated that she refrained from correcting her pitch with [Auto-Tune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auto-Tune "Auto-Tune") and that she prioritized intimacy and emotion to communicate the messages of her songs with her audience, a style that critics have described as conversational. According to the critic [Ann Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Powers "Ann Powers"), this singing style is demonstrated through Swift's attention to detail, which conveys an exact feeling—"the subtle adjustment of words and phrases to suggest moods like doubt, hope, and intimacy".

On *Red* and *1989*, Swift's vocals are processed with electronic effects, such as synthesizer tweaking, looping, and multitracking, to accompany the pop production. Her voice on *Reputation* and *Midnights* incorporates hip-hop and R&B influences, resulting in a near-[rap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rap "Rap") delivery that emphasizes rhythm and [cadence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadence "Cadence") over melody. She uses her lower [register](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Register_(music) "Register (music)") vocals extensively in *Folklore* and both her lower and upper registers in *Evermore*; the [musicologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musicologist "Musicologist") Alyssa Barca described her [timbre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbre "Timbre") in the upper register as "breathy and bright" and the lower register as "full and dark".

Swift's vocals have received more positive assessments since *Folklore*. The critic [Amanda Petrusich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Petrusich "Amanda Petrusich") commented in 2023 that her singing had become richer, with greater clarity and a stronger tone, even in live performances. *Rolling Stone* ranked her 102nd on their 2023 list "[The 200 Greatest Singers of All Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone%27s_200_Greatest_Singers_of_All_Time "Rolling Stone's 200 Greatest Singers of All Time")"; the magazine argued that her breathy timbre allows for a broad range of delivery and commented: "A decade ago, including her on this list would have been a controversial move, but recent releases like *Folklore*, *Evermore*, and *Midnights* officially settled the argument." Powers attributed Swift's vocal versatility to her evolving artistry, mentioning her use of hip-hop cadences and country-influenced timbre.

### Songwriting

Swift is credited as the lead songwriter on every song she has released and as the sole songwriter on over 60 tracks. Her fascination with songwriting began in her childhood; she credited her mother with igniting her early interest by helping her prepare for class presentations, and she would make up lyrics to [Disney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney "Disney") soundtrack songs once she had run out of words while singing them. Her early influences included the country musicians [Patsy Cline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patsy_Cline "Patsy Cline"), [Loretta Lynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loretta_Lynn "Loretta Lynn"), [Tammy Wynette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tammy_Wynette "Tammy Wynette"), and Dolly Parton; the singer-songwriters [Joni Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joni_Mitchell "Joni Mitchell"), [Melissa Etheridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melissa_Etheridge "Melissa Etheridge"), [Sarah McLachlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_McLachlan "Sarah McLachlan"), and [Alanis Morissette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alanis_Morissette "Alanis Morissette"); and the [emo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emo "Emo") musicians [Pete Wentz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Wentz "Pete Wentz") of [Fall Out Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_Out_Boy "Fall Out Boy") and [Chris Carrabba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Carrabba "Chris Carrabba") of [Dashboard Confessional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dashboard_Confessional "Dashboard Confessional"). She credited songs by the Dixie Chicks, [Jeannie C. Riley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeannie_C._Riley "Jeannie C. Riley"), and [Kenny Chesney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenny_Chesney "Kenny Chesney") with influencing her approach to narrative songwriting, and listed [Paul McCartney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_McCartney "Paul McCartney"), [Bruce Springsteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Springsteen "Bruce Springsteen"), [Emmylou Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmylou_Harris "Emmylou Harris"), and [Kris Kristofferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kris_Kristofferson "Kris Kristofferson") as career role models for their consistent and evolving songwriting.

Swift considers herself a songwriter first and foremost. She divides her lyrics into three types: "[quill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quill "Quill") lyrics", which feature antiquated words and phrasing; "[fountain pen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountain_pen "Fountain pen") lyrics", which combine modern storylines with poetic twists and vivid imagery; and "glitter [gel pen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gel_pen "Gel pen") lyrics", which are lively and frivolous. Swift has used songwriting to cope with personal experiences; her songs are largely autobiographical and feature narratives that mostly revolve around love and romantic relationships. In her songwriting process, she would start by identifying an emotion she wanted to convey, and the story and melody would follow. Swift has characterized her songwriting as "confessional", and academics have linked her style to [confessional poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confessional_poetry "Confessional poetry"), noting that her songs reference personal events and express her internal feelings to her audience.

Where *Taylor Swift* and *Fearless* are rooted in adolescent feelings and detail an optimistic romance inspired by fairy tales, *Speak Now* reflects Swift's young adulthood, offering newfound wisdom on real-life heartbreak. *Speak Now* marked the beginning of increasingly intense media speculation about the subjects of her songs, particularly her dating history; Swift has described such scrutiny as sexist. *Red* explores the tumult of an intense breakup, and *1989* reflects on failed relationships with a wistful perspective; both incorporate lyrics that hint at sex, reflecting her personal growth. *Reputation* addresses the public controversies that tarnished Swift's wholesome image while portraying a blossoming romance with intimacy and vulnerability; its extensive references to sex and alcohol marked a departure from the youthful innocence that had informed her past albums. Swift described *Lover* as a "love letter to love", inspired by her realization of "love that was very real".

On *Folklore* and *Evermore*, Swift was inspired by [escapism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escapism "Escapism") and [romanticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanticism "Romanticism") to explore fictional narratives. She imposed her emotions onto imagined characters and story arcs, inspired by authors and poets of [romantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romantic_literature "Romantic literature") and [modernist literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernist_literature "Modernist literature") like [F. Scott Fitzgerald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._Scott_Fitzgerald "F. Scott Fitzgerald"), [Robert Frost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Frost "Robert Frost"), [William Wordsworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Wordsworth "William Wordsworth"), and [Emily Dickinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Dickinson "Emily Dickinson"); the last of whom was a distant cousin of Swift. The albums' characters construct their narratives from fragments of memory, symbolizing the nature of folktales and oral traditions that endure over time. Swift returned to autobiographical songwriting on *Midnights*, whose nocturnal ruminations encompass regrets and fantasies informed by her awareness of fame. *The Tortured Poets Department* explores heartbreak and other themes to extremes, including erotic desire, forbidden love, and escaping the public spotlight, while *The Life of a Showgirl* draws on Swift's experiences during the Eras Tour as informed by her fame and romantic life.

Critical reception of Swift's songwriting has been largely positive, and her melodic compositions have been highlighted for optimizing the [verse-chorus form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verse-chorus_form "Verse-chorus form") with memorable [bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridge_(music) "Bridge (music)"). Several scholars have credited her with taking the confessional singer-songwriter tradition to new heights, and journalists have variously described her as a "[poet laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poet_laureate "Poet laureate")"—of puberty, of romance, and of her generation. In the view of the literary critic [Stephanie Burt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephanie_Burt "Stephanie Burt"), Swift's writing is proficient at "placing inventive, evocative language into pop melodies designed to be sung". Some critics have dismissed her "confessional" style as material for [tabloid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabloid_journalism "Tabloid journalism") gossip. Objection to the perceived poetic value of her songs, mostly from rockist critics, views her as a pop star using literary subtexts as a commercial ploy, with metaphors that are at times imprecise or self-indulgent. Scholars have attributed such criticisms to sexism. The musicologist Travis Stimeling argued that while Swift's autobiographical authenticity fits country and rock standards, her mostly male detractors view her depictions of young women's experiences as trivial and unworthy of serious merit. According to the English-language academic Ryan Hibbett, this gendered criticism bars Swift from receiving the artistic credentials granted to figures such as [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan "Bob Dylan"), whose romantic themes and occasional literary imprecisions are not as harshly criticized.

### Performances and stage

Swift's concerts feature elaborate settings that incorporate elements of [Broadway theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_theatre "Broadway theatre") and [high tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_tech "High tech"). She does not rely on elaborate choreography and instead emphasizes connecting emotionally with her audience through storytelling and vocal delivery. Swift has used special guests and surprise songs or covers as recurring elements of her concerts; the acoustic "surprise song" segment, which originated during the Speak Now World Tour, has become a fan favorite and one of the most anticipated parts of her shows. Since 2007, she has toured with the same live band. She plays four instruments live: guitar (including [electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_guitar "Electric guitar"), acoustic six-string, and [twelve-string](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-string_guitar "Twelve-string guitar")), six-string banjo, piano, and [ukulele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukulele "Ukulele").

Critics have praised her stage presence, stamina, and ability to bring forth an intimate atmosphere for her audience, even in stadium settings. The critic [Sasha Frere-Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasha_Frere-Jones "Sasha Frere-Jones") hailed her in 2008 as a "preternaturally skilled" entertainer who exerted professionalism with a vibrant energy. The journalist [Sam Lansky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Lansky "Sam Lansky") wrote in 2023: "Swift is many things onstage—vulnerable and triumphant, playful and sad—but the intimacy of her songcraft is front and center."

### Videos and filmmaking

Swift emphasizes visuals as a key creative component of her music. She established her production company, [Taylor Swift Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift_Productions "Taylor Swift Productions"), in 2008. Her directorial debut was the music video for "Mine", co-directed with [Roman White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_White "Roman White"), in 2010; and she developed the concept and [treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_treatment "Film treatment") for "Mean" in 2011. For the music videos of the *1989* and *Reputation* singles, she collaborated extensively with the director [Joseph Kahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Kahn_(director) "Joseph Kahn (director)") on eight videos; among them, she produced "Bad Blood", which won [Best Music Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Music_Video "Best Music Video") at the Grammy Awards in 2016. She worked with [American Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Express "American Express") for the "Blank Space" music video (which Kahn directed) and served as executive producer for the interactive app *AMEX Unstaged: Taylor Swift Experience*, for which she won a [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Interactive Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Interactive_Program "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Interactive Program") in 2015.

Swift has [directed 16 of her music videos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift_videography "Taylor Swift videography"). "The Man" was her first solo directorial work and made her the first female artist to win the [MTV Video Music Award for Best Direction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Award_for_Best_Direction "MTV Video Music Award for Best Direction"). *All Too Well: The Short Film*, her filmmaking debut, made her the first artist to win the Grammy Award for Best Music Video as a solo director. In June 2023, Swift was invited to the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences "Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences"). She has cited Joseph Kahn, [Chloé Zhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlo%C3%A9_Zhao "Chloé Zhao"), [Greta Gerwig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greta_Gerwig "Greta Gerwig"), [Nora Ephron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nora_Ephron "Nora Ephron"), [Guillermo del Toro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillermo_del_Toro "Guillermo del Toro"), [John Cassavetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cassavetes "John Cassavetes"), and [Noah Baumbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah_Baumbach "Noah Baumbach") as filmmaking influences.

## Achievements

Swift has won 14 Grammy Awards, including a record four [Album of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Album_of_the_Year "Grammy Award for Album of the Year") wins; she has also received a record eight nominations for [Song of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_of_the_Year "Song of the Year"). Swift has additionally won 12 Country Music Association Awards, 8 Academy of Country Music Awards, 2 [Brit Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brit_Awards "Brit Awards"), and an [Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Award "Emmy Award"). She is the most-awarded artist of the American Music Awards (40 wins), the [*Billboard* Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Music_Awards "Billboard Music Awards") (49), and the MTV Video Music Awards (33). Swift is the first woman to be honored with the Pinnacle Award by the Country Music Association Awards in 2013 and with the Global Icon Award by the Brit Awards in 2021. At the 2026 MTV Video Music Awards, she became the first artist to receive the MTV VMA Artist Director Honors.

The youngest person on *Rolling Stone*'s list "[The 100 Greatest Songwriters of All Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone%27s_100_Greatest_Songwriters_of_All_Time "Rolling Stone's 100 Greatest Songwriters of All Time")" (2015), Swift was also named to *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")*' list "The 30 Greatest Living American Songwriters" (2026), determined by votes from six of its critics and over 250 music experts. At the 64th [BMI Pop Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMI_Pop_Awards "BMI Pop Awards") in 2016, Swift became the first female songwriter to receive an award named after its recipient. She received the Songwriter Icon Award from the [National Music Publishers' Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Music_Publishers%27_Association "National Music Publishers' Association") in 2021 and was named Songwriter-Artist of the Decade by the Nashville Songwriters Association International in 2022. Swift is the youngest female inductee into the [Songwriters Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songwriters_Hall_of_Fame "Songwriters Hall of Fame") and the youngest inductee into the [Nashville Songwriters Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_Songwriters_Hall_of_Fame "Nashville Songwriters Hall of Fame") (both 2026), entering each in her first year of eligibility. She is set to be inducted into the [Central Pennsylvania Music Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Pennsylvania_Music_Hall_of_Fame "Central Pennsylvania Music Hall of Fame") in 2027.

The [International Federation of the Phonographic Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Federation_of_the_Phonographic_Industry "International Federation of the Phonographic Industry") (IFPI) has named Swift the [Global Recording Artist of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Recording_Artist_of_the_Year "Global Recording Artist of the Year") a record six times (2014, 2019, 2022–2025). She is the most-streamed artist of all time on Spotify as of April 2026, the most-streamed female artist of all time on Apple Music as of June 2026, and the [highest-grossing touring act of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_live_music_artists#All-time_ranking "List of highest-grossing live music artists"), with cumulative revenue at $3.12 billion as of December 2024. Her chart records include the most number-one albums in the UK for a female artist and for a non-British artist; the most number ones on the [Irish Albums Chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Albums_Chart "Irish Albums Chart"); the first artist to occupy the top five of the [Australian albums chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARIA_Charts "ARIA Charts"), doing so twice, and the top 10 positions on the Australian singles chart; the most number-one songs on the Canadian Hot 100; and the most entries, most top-10 entries, and most number-one entries (tied with [BTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BTS "BTS")) on the [*Billboard* Global 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Global_200 "Billboard Global 200").

In the US, Swift has sold 116.7 million album units, including 54 million pure sales, as of May 2025. She is the solo artist with the most *Billboard* 200 number-one albums (15, tied with [Drake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_(musician) "Drake (musician)")) and the most weeks at number one on the chart (100); the artist with the most *Billboard* Hot 100 number-one debuts (10, tied with Drake) and the most number-one songs on [Adult Pop Airplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_Pop_Airplay "Adult Pop Airplay") and [Pop Airplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_Airplay "Pop Airplay") (both 16); the first living artist to chart five albums in the top 10 of the *Billboard* 200; and the first artist to spend one hundred weeks atop the [*Billboard* Artist 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Artist_100 "Billboard Artist 100"). Swift is the first woman to have both an album and a song receive diamond certifications from the [Recording Industry Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America "Recording Industry Association of America") (RIAA), and also to have 100 million RIAA-certified album units. *Billboard* ranked Swift at number eight on its list "Greatest of All Time Artists" (2019), number three on "Top Country Artists of the 21st Century" (2025), number two on "[Greatest Pop Stars of the 21st Century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard%27s_Greatest_Pop_Stars "Billboard's Greatest Pop Stars")" (2024), and number one on "Top 100 Women Artists of the 21st Century" and "Top Artists of the 21st Century" (both 2025).

Swift has appeared in power listings. In 2024, she became the first solo artist to top *Billboard*'s annual Power 100 ranking of the top music industry executives. *Time* included her on its annual list of the [100 most influential people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100 "Time 100") in 2010, 2015, and 2019. Swift was one of the "Silence Breakers" that the magazine spotlighted as [Person of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Person_of_the_Year "Time Person of the Year") in 2017 for speaking up about sexual assault. In 2023, she became the first individual from [the arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_arts "The arts") to be named *Time* Person of the Year and the first woman to appear on a Person of the Year cover more than once. Swift received an honorary [Doctor of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Fine_Arts "Doctor of Fine Arts") degree from [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University "New York University") and served as its [commencement speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commencement_speaker "Commencement speaker") on May 18, 2022.

## Public image

Swift is an enduring figure of 21st-century [popular culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_culture "Popular culture"). Her career trajectory from a country singer-songwriter to a pop star in the 2000s and 2010s was the subject of extensive commentary. Deemed "America's Sweetheart" in her early career, she was described in the press as a "media darling" with a [girl-next-door](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girl-next-door "Girl-next-door")'s polite demeanor and open-hearted conversations. Swift displayed a feminine image but refrained from the "aggressively sexualized feminist pop" of her contemporaries, leading publications to comment that her [sex appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_appeal "Sex appeal") was modest, subtle, and sophisticated. The adolescent themes of Swift's music contributed to her status as a [teen idol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teen_idol "Teen idol"), although several [feminist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminist "Feminist") authors took issue with her songs about romantic relationships as narrow-minded and detrimental to girls and women, who made up the majority of her fanbase known as [Swifties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swifties "Swifties").

Upon recalibrating her artistry to pop music, Swift has identified as a feminist and achieved a [pop icon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_icon "Pop icon") status. In 2013, the author [Jody Rosen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jody_Rosen "Jody Rosen") labelled Swift the "Queen of Pop" due to her popularity that defied traditional boundaries between "genres, eras, demographics, paradigms, trends". Her feminist public image garnered varied reactions from the public, including praise for her success in a male-dominated music industry as an inspiration for girls and women, and criticism that dismissed her feminist adoption as superficial and self-interested.

The 2016 dispute with Kanye West bolstered the narrative among Swift's detractors that she was a calculating and manipulative woman despite her sweetheart image, and deepened [their feud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift%E2%80%93Kanye_West_feud "Taylor Swift–Kanye West feud") that has resonated in their respective careers. Her artistic reinventions in the 2020s turned her into an acclaimed singer-songwriter. Buoyed by her enduring pop stardom, she has been recognized as a rare phenomenon that combines the pop star and singer-songwriter archetypes with unprecedented success. Swift is a known [triskaidekaphile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triskaidekaphobia#Lucky_13 "Triskaidekaphobia"), using the number [13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13_(number) "13 (number)") in her various works.

## Cultural impact

Swift is one of the few artists to have consistently sold millions of albums across two decades despite the post-[album era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Album_era "Album era") decline in record sales. Academics and journalists have therefore described her as "the last pop superstar" and "the last great rock star" of the 21st century. Her commercial strategies for albums and concert tickets have earned her a reputation as a savvy businesswoman, and her use of physical album variants and [Easter eggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_egg_(media) "Easter egg (media)") have become indicative of music marketing trends. The economist [Alan Krueger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Krueger "Alan Krueger") described Swift as an "economic genius". *Billboard* has considered her one of the few artists who can simultaneously achieve chart success, critical acclaim, and fan support, and popularize any sound in mainstream music.

Swift's success in country music has been credited with popularizing the genre beyond the US and introducing it to adolescent women, a previously ignored demographic. The critic [Kelefa Sanneh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelefa_Sanneh "Kelefa Sanneh") described her as the biggest country star since [Garth Brooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garth_Brooks "Garth Brooks"), "and maybe since before him, too". Swift's guitar performances has resulted in increasing sales of guitars among women, which the media dubbed the "Taylor Swift factor". Her transition from country to pop has been credited as the catalyst for poptimism, and her songwriting and musical transitions have been credited with influencing a new generation of artists.

Swift's enduring popularity, particularly among women, has contributed to her status as a representation of [millennials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennials "Millennials"), or, more broadly, of her generation's *[zeitgeist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeitgeist "Zeitgeist")*. Her fanbase has been described by academics and journalists as one of the most loyal and dedicated. *Time*'s Cady Lang credited Swift's sustained superstardom to her "savvy manipulation of both the industry and [her] personal brand". According to the popular-culture scholars Mary Fogarty and [Gina Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gina_Arnold "Gina Arnold"), Swift is arguably the singular artist "whose story encapsulates many of the urgent conflicts in early twenty-first-century American culture". In a 2024 article for *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")*, Joe Coscarelli wrote that her lasting popularity provoked debates comparing her not only to contemporaries like [Drake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_(musician) "Drake (musician)") or Beyoncé, but also to veteran artists like [the Beatles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beatles "The Beatles"), [Michael Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jackson "Michael Jackson"), [Elton John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elton_John "Elton John"), or Madonna.

Swift's advocacy for artists' rights and re-recording projects have contributed to industry-wide discourses and reforms. Her artistry and career maneuvers have been the subject of various university courses in literary, cultural, and [sociopolitical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociopolitical "Sociopolitical") contexts. Swift's legacy has been both embraced and critiqued by groups ranging from feminist and [queer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queer "Queer") communities to [far-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-right "Far-right") and religious organizations, and studied by experts across various fields including musicology, literature, sociology, media theory, linguistics, and culture studies.

## Wealth and endorsements

Through her management company, TAS Rights Management, LLC, Swift has filed over 300 trademark applications in the US and holds more than 400 registrations worldwide, as of August 2025[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Taylor_Swift&action=edit). Her filings include her name "Taylor Swift", her fanbase name "Swifties", album and tour titles, lyrics, slogans, artwork, and her cats' names. In 2026, Swift filed to trademark her voice and appearance against [AI-generated reproductions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_AI "Generative AI"), following unauthorized [uses of her likeness in deepfake pornography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift_deepfake_pornography_controversy "Taylor Swift deepfake pornography controversy") and presidential election advertisements.

Swift was listed by *Forbes* as the [world's highest-paid musician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_list_of_the_world%27s_highest-paid_musicians "Forbes list of the world's highest-paid musicians") in 2016 and 2019, the highest-paid female musician of the 2010s, and the highest-paid female musician in 2021, 2022, and 2025. In October 2023, it was reported that Swift had become a [billionaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billionaire "Billionaire"). She was recognized as the first billionaire "primarily based on her songs and performances", with the majority of her sum coming from music royalties and touring. As of July 2026,[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Taylor_Swift&action=edit) *Forbes* estimated her net worth at $2.1 billion, making her the [world's richest female musician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_music_artists_by_net_worth "List of music artists by net worth"); the estimate included a $900 million music catalog and a $100 million real estate portfolio that spans Nashville, New York City, Los Angeles ([Samuel Goldwyn Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Goldwyn_Estate "Samuel Goldwyn Estate")), and [Rhode Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island "Rhode Island") ([High Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Watch "High Watch")).

Swift's use of [private jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_jet "Private jet") has drawn scrutiny for its [carbon emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_emissions "Carbon emissions"). In 2023, a spokesperson for Swift stated that she had purchased more than double the required [carbon credits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_credits "Carbon credits") to offset all tour travel and personal flights. In December 2023, Swift's lawyers sent a [cease and desist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cease_and_desist "Cease and desist") letter to the American programmer [Jack Sweeney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sweeney "Jack Sweeney"), who uses publicly available data to track her private jet, alleging stalking and safety risks. In February 2024, it was reported that Swift had sold one of her two private jets.

Swift's album rollouts typically consist of multimedia promotional activities involving corporate tie-ins and product endorsements. [Target](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_Corporation "Target Corporation") is a long-standing business partner with Swift, offering exclusive physical albums and merchandise. In 2008–2011, her albums and tours were promoted with self-designed dolls with [Jakks Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakks_Pacific "Jakks Pacific"), fragrances with [Elizabeth Arden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Arden,_Inc. "Elizabeth Arden, Inc."), clothes with [L.E.I.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L.E.I. "L.E.I."), and holiday cards with [American Greetings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Greetings "American Greetings"). Her partnership deals also included makeup products for [CoverGirl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CoverGirl "CoverGirl") and [Cyber-shot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyber-shot "Cyber-shot") cameras for [Sony Electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Electronics "Sony Electronics"). In 2012–2015, she had tie-ins with [Starbucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starbucks "Starbucks"), [Keds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keds "Keds"), [Subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subway_(restaurant) "Subway (restaurant)"), [Diet Coke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diet_Coke "Diet Coke"), [Walgreens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walgreens "Walgreens"), [Walmart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walmart "Walmart"), and [Papa John's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papa_John%27s "Papa John's"). She became the first global ambassador for [Record Store Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_Store_Day "Record Store Day") in 2022.

## Social activism

Swift avoided discussing politics in her early career. She said that she kept herself as educated as possible but did not want to influence others with her political opinions, later attributing her reluctance to the [2003 Dixie Chicks controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dixie_Chicks_controversy "Dixie Chicks controversy") and its impact on country musicians, especially women. Due to her apolitical stance, she was appropriated by the [alt-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alt-right "Alt-right") movement in the US, which sparked controversy. Swift voted for [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") in the [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_US_presidential_election "2008 US presidential election") and [2012 US presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_US_presidential_election "2012 US presidential election"), and for [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton "Hillary Clinton") in the [2016 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_US_presidential_election "2016 US presidential election"); she did not publicly endorse Clinton because she believed that the backlash she was facing at the time could make her endorsement harmful to Clinton's likability.

Since 2018, Swift has been public about her political views and has abandoned her once-apolitical stance. She publicly endorsed two [Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)") politicians for the [2018 US midterm elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_US_midterm_elections "2018 US midterm elections"), which resulted in headlines about how she finally dissociated herself from the alt-right and [conservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States "Conservatism in the United States"). In 2020, she urged her fans to check their voter registration ahead of elections, which resulted in 65,000 people registering to vote within one day of her post. Swift endorsed the Democratic ticket of [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden") and [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris "Kamala Harris") in the [2020 US presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_US_presidential_election "2020 US presidential election"), and later endorsed Harris and [Tim Walz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Walz "Tim Walz") for the [2024 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_US_presidential_election "2024 US presidential election"). She has openly criticized President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") and his policies.

A [pro-choice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-choice "Pro-choice") feminist, Swift is a founding signatory of the [Time's Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time%27s_Up_(organization) "Time's Up (organization)") movement against sexual harassment and has criticized the [US Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Supreme_Court "US Supreme Court")'s 2022 [decision to end federal abortion rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobbs_v._Jackson_Women%27s_Health_Organization "Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization"). A supporter of [LGBTQ rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_rights "LGBTQ rights"), she has donated to [GLAAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GLAAD "GLAAD") and the [Tennessee Equality Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_Equality_Project "Tennessee Equality Project"), advocated for the [Equality Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equality_Act_(United_States) "Equality Act (United States)"), and performed during [WorldPride NYC 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WorldPride_NYC_2019 "WorldPride NYC 2019") at the [Stonewall Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonewall_Inn "Stonewall Inn"), a [gay rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_rights "Gay rights") monument. Swift has spoken up against [white supremacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_supremacy "White supremacy"), racism, and [police brutality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_brutality_in_the_United_States "Police brutality in the United States") in the US. She has donated to [March for Our Lives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_for_Our_Lives "March for Our Lives") and supported [gun control and reform in the US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_politics_in_the_United_States "Gun politics in the United States"), donated to the [NAACP Legal Defense and Educational Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAACP_Legal_Defense_and_Educational_Fund "NAACP Legal Defense and Educational Fund") and supported the [Black Lives Matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Lives_Matter "Black Lives Matter") movement, called for the [removal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Removal_of_Confederate_monuments_and_memorials "Removal of Confederate monuments and memorials") of [Confederate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America "Confederate States of America") monuments in Tennessee, and advocated for [Juneteenth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juneteenth "Juneteenth") to become a national holiday.

Swift's political engagements have been met with mixed reception: they have provoked further public discussion of political issues and empowered Swifties, but critics have questioned whether her political alignments were strategic in her career. While some publications have argued that Swift's stardom had a significant impact on political involvement in the US, others have viewed her influence as sizable but overstated. According to the popular culture scholar Simone Driessen, her political impact extended beyond the US to Australia and Europe.

## Philanthropy

Swift ranked first on [DoSomething](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DoSomething "DoSomething")'s "Gone Good" list in 2012, 2013, 2014, and 2015. She received the Star of Compassion from the Tennessee Disaster Services and the Big Help Award from the [Nickelodeon Kids' Choice Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_Kids%27_Choice_Awards "Nickelodeon Kids' Choice Awards") for her "dedication to helping others" and "inspiring others through action". Especially early in her career, Swift donated to various relief funds following natural disasters. In 2009, she donated $100,000 to the Red Cross to help the victims of the [Iowa flood of 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_flood_of_2008 "Iowa flood of 2008"). The same year, she performed at Sydney's [Sound Relief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_Relief "Sound Relief") concert, which raised money for those impacted by bushfires and flooding. In 2011, Swift used a [dress rehearsal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dress_rehearsal "Dress rehearsal") of her Speak Now tour as a benefit concert for victims of [recent tornadoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Super_Outbreak "2011 Super Outbreak") in the US, raising more than $750,000. In response to the [May 2010 Tennessee floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_2010_Tennessee_floods "May 2010 Tennessee floods"), she donated $500,000. In 2009, Swift sang at the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC")'s [Children in Need](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children_in_Need "Children in Need") concert and raised [£](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pound_sterling "Pound sterling")13,000 for the cause. In 2016, she donated $1 million to [Louisiana flood relief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Louisiana_floods "2016 Louisiana floods") efforts and $100,000 to the Dolly Parton Fire Fund. Swift donated to [food banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_bank "Food bank") after [Hurricane Harvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Harvey "Hurricane Harvey") struck [Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston "Houston") in 2017. She donated $1 million to [Tennessee tornado relief in 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Nashville_tornado_outbreak "2020 Nashville tornado outbreak") and [again in 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/December_2023_Tennessee_tornado_outbreak "December 2023 Tennessee tornado outbreak"), as well as $5 million to relief efforts after [Hurricane Helene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Helene "Hurricane Helene") and [Hurricane Milton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Milton "Hurricane Milton") in 2024.

She has also donated to [cancer research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_research "Cancer research"). As the recipient of the [Academy of Country Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Country_Music "Academy of Country Music")'s Entertainer of the Year in 2011, Swift donated $25,000 to [St. Jude Children's Research Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Jude_Children%27s_Research_Hospital "St. Jude Children's Research Hospital"), Tennessee. In 2012, she participated in the [Stand Up to Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand_Up_to_Cancer "Stand Up to Cancer") telethon, performing the charity single "[Ronan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronan_(song) "Ronan (song)")", which she wrote in memory of a four-year-old boy who died of [neuroblastoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroblastoma "Neuroblastoma"). She has also donated $100,000 to the [V Foundation for Cancer Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V_Foundation_for_Cancer_Research "V Foundation for Cancer Research") and $50,000 to the [Children's Hospital of Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children%27s_Hospital_of_Philadelphia "Children's Hospital of Philadelphia"). She has made donations to her fans several times to help cover their medical or academic expenses. During the COVID-19 pandemic, Swift donated to the [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization "World Health Organization") and [Feeding America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feeding_America "Feeding America") and supported independent record stores. She performed "[Soon You'll Get Better](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soon_You%27ll_Get_Better "Soon You'll Get Better")" on the *[One World: Together At Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Together_at_Home#Television_special "Together at Home")* television special, a [benefit concert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benefit_concert "Benefit concert") curated by [Lady Gaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Gaga "Lady Gaga") for [Global Citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Poverty_Project#Global_Citizen "Global Poverty Project") to raise funds for the World Health Organization's [COVID-19 Solidarity Response Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_Solidarity_Response_Fund "COVID-19 Solidarity Response Fund").

Swift is a supporter of the arts. A benefactor of the [Nashville Songwriters Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_Songwriters_Hall_of_Fame "Nashville Songwriters Hall of Fame"), she has donated $75,000 to Hendersonville High School to help refurbish its auditorium; $4 million to the [Country Music Hall of Fame and Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Music_Hall_of_Fame_and_Museum "Country Music Hall of Fame and Museum") in Nashville—the largest donation the museum had received from an artist—to establish the Taylor Swift Education Center, an exhibit and classroom space that opened in 2013; and $100,000 to the [Nashville Symphony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_Symphony "Nashville Symphony"). In 2012, Swift partnered with Chegg for Good to donate $10,000 to the music departments of six US colleges.

Swift has also provided one-off donations. She has donated items, including signed guitars and concert tickets, to various charities for auction, among them [MusiCares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MusiCares "MusiCares"), [Global's Make Some Noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global%27s_Make_Some_Noise "Global's Make Some Noise"), and [Rare Impact Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare_Impact_Fund "Rare Impact Fund"). A promoter of children's literacy, Swift has donated money and books to schools across the US. In April 2018, Swift donated to the [Rape, Abuse & Incest National Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rape,_Abuse_%26_Incest_National_Network "Rape, Abuse & Incest National Network") in honor of [Sexual Assault Awareness Month](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_Assault_Awareness_Month "Sexual Assault Awareness Month"). She also donated to fellow singer-songwriter [Kesha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kesha "Kesha") to help with [her legal battles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kesha_v._Dr._Luke "Kesha v. Dr. Luke") against [Dr. Luke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Luke "Dr. Luke") and to actress [Mariska Hargitay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariska_Hargitay "Mariska Hargitay")'s [Joyful Heart Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyful_Heart_Foundation "Joyful Heart Foundation").

Swift donated the equivalent of tens of thousands of meals to food banks at each stop of the Eras Tour; she also employed local businesses throughout the tour and gave $197 million in [bonus payments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonus_payment "Bonus payment") to her entire crew. In February 2024, she donated $100,000 to the family of a woman who died in a [shooting at the Kansas City Chiefs' Super Bowl parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Kansas_City_parade_shooting "2024 Kansas City parade shooting"). Swift donated $250,000 to Operation Breakthrough in December 2024; the funds were directed to workforce development, childcare, and early learning programs. In December 2025, she made donations to eight organizations including MusiCares, Operation Breakthrough, Feeding America, and the [American Heart Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Heart_Association "American Heart Association").
