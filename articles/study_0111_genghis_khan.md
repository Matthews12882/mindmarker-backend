# Genghis Khan

**Genghis Khan** (born **Temüjin**; c. 1162 – 18 or 25 August 1227), also known as **Chinggis Khan**, was the founder and first [khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_(title) "Khan (title)") of the [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire "Mongol Empire"). After spending most of his life uniting the [Mongol tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_tribes "Mongol tribes"), he launched [a series of military campaigns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_conquests "Mongol conquests"), conquering large parts of [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_conquest_of_China "Mongol conquest of China") and [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_campaigns_in_Central_Asia "Mongol campaigns in Central Asia").

Genghis Khan was born Temüjin between 1155 and 1167 as the eldest child of [Yesugei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yesugei "Yesugei"), a Mongol chieftain of the [Borjigin clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borjigin_clan "Borjigin clan"), and his wife [Hö'elün](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B6%27el%C3%BCn "Hö'elün"). When Temüjin was eight, his father died and his family was abandoned by its tribe. Reduced to near-poverty, Temüjin killed his older half-brother [Behter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behter "Behter") to secure his familial position. His charismatic personality helped to attract his first followers and to form alliances with two prominent [steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Steppe "Eurasian Steppe") leaders named [Jamukha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamukha "Jamukha") and [Toghrul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toghrul "Toghrul"); they worked together to retrieve Temüjin's newlywed wife [Börte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B6rte "Börte"), who had been kidnapped by raiders. As his reputation grew, his relationship with Jamukha deteriorated into open warfare. Temüjin was [badly defeated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Dalan_Baljut "Battle of Dalan Baljut") in c. 1187, and may have spent the following years as a subject of the [Jin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_dynasty_(1115%E2%80%931234) "Jin dynasty (1115–1234)"); upon reemerging in 1196, he swiftly began gaining power. Toghrul came to view Temüjin as a threat and [launched a surprise attack on him](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Khalakhaljid_Sands "Battle of Khalakhaljid Sands") in 1203. [Temüjin regrouped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baljuna_Covenant "Baljuna Covenant") and overpowered Toghrul. After defeating the [Naiman tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naiman_tribe "Naiman tribe") and executing Jamukha, he was left as the sole ruler on the Mongolian steppe.

Temüjin formally adopted the title "Genghis Khan", the meaning of which is uncertain, at an assembly in 1206. Carrying out reforms designed to ensure long-term stability, he transformed the Mongols' tribal structure into an integrated [meritocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meritocracy "Meritocracy") dedicated to the service of the ruling family. After thwarting a [coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup "Coup") attempt from a powerful [shaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaman "Shaman"), Genghis began to consolidate his power. In 1209, he led a large-scale raid against the neighbouring [Western Xia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Xia "Western Xia"), who agreed to Mongol terms the following year. He then launched [a campaign against the Jin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_conquest_of_the_Jin_dynasty "Mongol conquest of the Jin dynasty"), which lasted for four years and ended in 1215 with [the capture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sieges_of_Zhongdu "Sieges of Zhongdu") of the Jin capital [Zhongdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhongdu "Zhongdu"). His general [Jebe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jebe "Jebe") annexed the Central Asian state of [Qara Khitai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qara_Khitai "Qara Khitai") in 1218. The following year, Genghis invaded the [Khwarazmian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khwarazmian_Empire "Khwarazmian Empire") following the execution of his envoys; [the campaign toppled the Khwarazmian state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_the_Khwarazmian_Empire "Mongol invasion of the Khwarazmian Empire") and devastated the regions of [Transoxiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transoxiana "Transoxiana") and [Khorasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khorasan "Khorasan"), while Jebe and his colleague [Subutai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subutai "Subutai") led an expedition that reached [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasions_of_Georgia "Mongol invasions of Georgia") and [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Kievan_Rus%27 "Mongol invasion of Kievan Rus'"). In 1227, Genghis died while subduing the rebellious Western Xia. Following a two-year [interregnum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interregnum "Interregnum"), his third son and heir [Ögedei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96gedei_Khan "Ögedei Khan") acceded to the throne in 1229.

Genghis Khan remains a controversial figure. He was generous and intensely loyal to his followers, but ruthless towards his enemies. He welcomed advice from diverse sources in his quest for world domination, for which he believed the shamanic supreme deity [Tengri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tengri "Tengri") had destined him. The [Mongol army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_the_Mongol_Empire "Military of the Mongol Empire") under Genghis [killed millions of people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destruction_under_the_Mongol_Empire "Destruction under the Mongol Empire"), yet his conquests also facilitated unprecedented commercial and cultural exchange over a vast geographical area. He is remembered as a backward, savage tyrant in [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia "Russia") and the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world "Arab world") while Western scholarship since the 1990s has begun to reassess the previous view of him as a barbarian warlord. He was posthumously [deified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apotheosis "Apotheosis") in Mongolia; modern Mongolians recognise him as the [founding father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_founders#Mongolia "List of national founders") of their nation.

## Name and title

There is no universal [romanisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization "Romanization") system used for [Mongolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_language "Mongolian language"); as a result, modern spellings of Mongolian names vary greatly and may result in considerably different pronunciations from the original. The [honorific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorific "Honorific") most commonly rendered as "Genghis" ultimately derives from the Mongolian ᠴᠢᠩᠭᠢᠰ, which may be romanised as *Činggis*. This was adapted into Chinese as [成吉思](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%88%90%E5%90%89%E6%80%9D "wikt:成吉思") *Chéngjísī*, and into Persian as چنگیز *Čəngīz*. As [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic "Arabic") lacks a sound similar to [[tʃ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiceless_postalveolar_affricate "Voiceless postalveolar affricate")], represented in the Mongolian and Persian romanisations by ⟨č⟩, writers transcribed the name as *J̌ingiz*, while [Syriac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_language "Syriac language") authors used *Šīngīz*.

In addition to "Genghis", introduced into English during the 18th century based on a misreading of Persian sources, modern English spellings include "Chinggis", "Chingis", "Jinghis", and "Jengiz". His birth name "Temüjin" (ᠲᠡᠮᠦᠵᠢᠨ; [鐵木真](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E9%90%B5%E6%9C%A8%E7%9C%9F "wikt:鐵木真") *Tiěmùzhēn*) is sometimes also spelled "Temuchin" in English.

When Genghis's grandson [Kublai Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kublai_Khan "Kublai Khan") established the [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty "Yuan dynasty") in 1271, he bestowed the [temple name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_name "Temple name") *Taizu* ([太祖](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%A4%AA%E7%A5%96#Chinese "wikt:太祖"), meaning 'Supreme Progenitor') and the [posthumous name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posthumous_name "Posthumous name") *Shengwu Huangdi* ([聖](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E8%81%96#Chinese "wikt:聖")[武](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%AD%A6#Chinese "wikt:武")[皇帝](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E7%9A%87%E5%B8%9D#Chinese "wikt:皇帝"), meaning 'Holy-Martial Emperor') upon his grandfather. Kublai's great-grandson [Külüg Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%BCl%C3%BCg_Khan "Külüg Khan") later expanded this title into *Fatian Qiyun Shengwu Huangdi* ([法](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%B3%95#Chinese "wikt:法")[天](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%A4%A9#Chinese "wikt:天")[啟](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%95%9F#Chinese "wikt:啟")[運](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E9%81%8B#Chinese "wikt:運")[聖](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E8%81%96#Chinese "wikt:聖")[武](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%AD%A6#Chinese "wikt:武")[皇帝](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E7%9A%87%E5%B8%9D#Chinese "wikt:皇帝"), meaning 'Interpreter of the Heavenly Law, Initiator of the Good Fortune, Holy-Martial Emperor').
