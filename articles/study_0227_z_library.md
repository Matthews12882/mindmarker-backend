# Z-Library

**Z-Library** (abbreviated as **z-lib**, formerly **BookFinder**) is a [shadow library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_library "Shadow library") project for [file-sharing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File-sharing "File-sharing") access to [scholarly journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholarly_journal "Scholarly journal") articles, academic texts and general-interest books. It began as a [mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_site "Mirror site") of [Library Genesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_Genesis "Library Genesis") but has expanded dramatically.

According to the website's own data released in February 2023, its collection comprised over 13.35 million books and over 84.8 million articles. Z-Library is particularly popular in [emerging economies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerging_economies "Emerging economies") and among academics. In June 2020, Z-Library was visited by around 2.84 million users, of whom 14.76% were from the United States. According to the [Alexa Traffic Rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexa_Traffic_Rank "Alexa Traffic Rank") service, Z-Library was ranked as the 2,758th most active website in October 2021.

The organization describes itself as "the world's largest [e-book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-book "E-book") library" and "the world's largest scientific articles store;" however, this is contestable as [Anna's Archive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna%27s_Archive "Anna's Archive") also claims this, with its slogan claiming a collection containing "63,632,048 books" and "95,689,475 [scientific] papers" that includes a full mirror of Z-Library. It operates as a non-profit organization sustained by donations. Besides sharing ebooks, Z-Library announced plans to expand their offerings to include physical paperback books at dedicated "Z-Points" around the globe.

Z-Library and its activities are illegal in many jurisdictions. While website seizures reduce the accessibility of the content, it remains available on the [dark web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_web "Dark web"). The legal status of the project, as well as its potential impact on the publishing industry and authors' rights, is a matter of ongoing debate.

## Website

The site is financed by user donations that are collected twice a year (September and March) through fundraising. Over the years, various [URLs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/URL "URL") and [IP addresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_address "IP address") have been used for Z-Library, as domain names have been confiscated by various legal authorities.

### Functionality

Little is known about Z-Library in terms of its operation, management, and commercial status. Notably, Z-Library does not open its full database to the public. Despite that, its database, excluding books from [Library Genesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_Genesis "Library Genesis"), was mirrored by [Anna's Archive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna%27s_Archive "Anna's Archive") in 2022 and continues to be updated.

According to a *Financial Times* interview with an anonymous member of the site's staff, about 20 people were responsible for the site's development and maintenance, with the biggest expense being infrastructure maintenance, followed by the salaries of personnel.

In March 2019, the Z-Library team claimed to have servers in Finland, Germany, Luxembourg, Malaysia, Panama, Russia and the United States. The size of their database is over 220 TB.

In an effort to prevent blacklisting of domains (oftentimes by internet providers at the [DNS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNS "DNS")-level in accordance with legal procedures), Z-Library used a homepage that did not contain any infringing content but instead listed many working mirror domains for different regions. This strategy proved ineffective, as the domain "z-lib.org" was seized in 2022. In August 2023, Z-Library announced the possible use of browser extensions to help mitigate challenges if the domain name has to change.

### Fraudulent domains

Some [phishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phishing "Phishing") scams have attempted to impersonate Z-Library by using similar domain names and an identical visual design in order to extract login credentials and ask for donations. In June 2024, the news outlet Cybernews reported the discovery of a leaked database containing the personal data of nearly 10 million users, which had been collected by the scam clone site Z-lib.is.

## Legal status

Z-Library has cycled through domain names, some of which have been blocked by [domain registry operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_name_registry "Domain name registry"). Z-Library remains accessible via alternative domains and through the [.onion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.onion ".onion")-linked [Tor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tor_(anonymity_network) "Tor (anonymity network)") network.

### United Kingdom

In mid-2015, [The Publishers Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Publishers_Association "The Publishers Association"), a UK organization, attempted to enact [internet service provider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_service_provider "Internet service provider")-level blocks on Z-Library. In late 2015, publisher [Elsevier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elsevier "Elsevier") filed a successful court request that ordered the registrar of bookfi.org to seize the site's [internet domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_domain "Internet domain").

### United States

Some of Z-Library's domains (bookfi.org, booksc.org and b-ok.org) were included in the 2017 [Office of the United States Trade Representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_United_States_Trade_Representative "Office of the United States Trade Representative") report on [notorious markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notorious_market "Notorious market").

Z-Library's domains were temporarily blocked in 2021 after a [DMCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DMCA "DMCA") notice issued by [Harvard Business Publishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Business_Publishing "Harvard Business Publishing"). The domain suspensions were lifted.

In October 2022, [TikTok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TikTok "TikTok") blocked hashtags related to Z-Library after gaining popularity on the platform, and the [Authors Guild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authors_Guild "Authors Guild") submitted a complaint to the [United States Trade Representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Trade_Representative "United States Trade Representative"). On November 3, 2022, over 240 domain names of Z-Library's were seized by the [United States Department of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Justice "United States Department of Justice") and [Federal Bureau of Investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Investigation "Federal Bureau of Investigation") in response to a [court order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_order "Court order"), and two Russian nationals associated with the project were arrested on charges related to copyright infringement and money laundering in Argentina.

When the domains z-lib.org, b-ok.org, and 3lib.net were seized, the DNS servers utilized switched to ns1.seizedservers.com and ns2.seizedservers.com, used commonly in US law enforcement seizures. These servers have switched to [Njalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Njalla "Njalla"), an anonymous [hosting provider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_hosting_service "Internet hosting service"). The website continues to be active and accessible through the [Tor network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tor_network "Tor network") and the [I2P network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I2P "I2P"), before returning to the regular Internet through private personal domains issued to each user on February 11, 2023.

On November 16, 2022, U.S. Attorneys for the Eastern District of New York of the Department of Justice unsealed the indictment for two Russian nationals, Anton Napolsky and Valeriia Ermakova, who had been placed under house arrest in Argentina on November 3, 2022, pending an extradition hearing. They were charged with [criminal copyright infringement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_copyright_infringement "Criminal copyright infringement"), [wire fraud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wire_fraud "Wire fraud") and [money laundering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money_laundering "Money laundering") for operating the Z-Library website. The indictment pertains to alleged criminal activity taking place from 2018 to 2022, though the pair are suspected to have operated Z-Library for "over a decade". The arrests were accomplished by the FBI with data from Google and Amazon (among other sites), accessed with [search warrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_warrants "Search warrants"), that helped identify the founders of the website. The U.S. lawyers retained as official representatives requested a dismissal of the criminal indictment in June 2023. The two escaped their house arrest in Argentina in July 2024. The presiding judge issued an [Interpol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpol "Interpol") warrant for their arrest, and their whereabouts are unknown.

The law enforcement efforts were formally assisted by [The Publishers Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Publishers_Association "The Publishers Association") along with the Authors Guild, and reportedly, indirectly by [BREIN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BREIN "BREIN"), a Dutch anti-piracy group. The Authors Guild issued a statement supporting the arrests and commented that it was "one of the biggest breakthroughs in the fight against online criminal e-book piracy to date". The executive director for the [Authors Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authors_Alliance "Authors Alliance"), a group dedicated to increasing access for literature, said, "I certainly don't condone illegal behavior, but I think this seizure and press release highlight how broken our copyright system is". Some authors like [Alison Rumfitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alison_Rumfitt "Alison Rumfitt") have also defended the project, arguing that it provides a valuable service by increasing access to knowledge and promoting education in underprivileged communities.

Decreased accessibility to Z-Library and its services has substantially impacted students and researchers in underfunded institutions who rely on its resources for their studies and work. Days after the law enforcement action, a group of anonymous archivists launched [Anna's Archive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna%27s_Archive "Anna's Archive"), a search engine that provides access to the contents of Z-Library and other shadow libraries. Many other workarounds to the recent attempts to take down Z-Library have been reported. Some of these purported alternative sites have taken up the top search results and submitted bogus [DMCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DMCA "DMCA") takedown requests of their own, according to news reports.

In May 2023, a new round of domain name seizures was carried out by U.S. authorities. In November 2023, dozens of domains were seized by authorities from the United States and Austria. In January 2024, additional Z-Library domains were reported to have been targeted by publishers. In May 2024, several domain name seizures were carried out by U.S. authorities, including the site's email domain z-lib.se.

#### Meta lawsuit

In July 2023, authors [Richard Kadrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Kadrey "Richard Kadrey"), [Sarah Silverman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Silverman "Sarah Silverman"), and [Christopher Golden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Golden "Christopher Golden") filed a class action lawsuit in the Northern District of California Court accusing [Meta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta_Platforms "Meta Platforms") of [copyright infringement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright_infringement "Copyright infringement") and violation of the [Digital Millennium Copyright Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Millennium_Copyright_Act "Digital Millennium Copyright Act") by using copyrighted works to train its [Llama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llama_(language_model) "Llama (language model)") AI model. The District Court dismissed without prejudice the authors' claims, and in February 2025 the plaintiffs filed an amended complaint citing internal emails from Meta employees which revealed that the company had torrented 81.7 terabytes of data from [shadow libraries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_libraries "Shadow libraries"), contradicting testimony given in the previous hearing. The dataset included 35.7 terabytes of data from Z-Library and [Library Genesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_Genesis "Library Genesis").

### India

Z-Library's website was banned in India in August 2022 following a court order from the [Tis Hazari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tis_Hazari "Tis Hazari") [district court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_courts_of_India "District courts of India") after a complaint stated that the copyrights of ten books (pertaining to the topics of tax and corporate law) were being violated by Z-Library. Internet service providers in India were directed to block the site. The decision to block Z-Library and other shadow libraries has been criticized by some Indian authors, students, academics, and [freedom of information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_information "Freedom of information") activists. On November 5, 2022, the Hindu right wing group [Swadeshi Jagran Manch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swadeshi_Jagran_Manch "Swadeshi Jagran Manch") formally objected that the FBI's seizure of the Indian domain name 1lib.in (used by Z-lib) by the District Judge of New York's order without jurisdiction had violated India's sovereignty.

### France

In September 2022, it was announced that the [Syndicat national de l'édition](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Syndicat_national_de_l%27%C3%A9dition&action=edit&redlink=1 "Syndicat national de l'édition (page does not exist)") [[fr](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syndicat_national_de_l%27%C3%A9dition "fr:Syndicat national de l'édition")] (National Publishing Union) in France succeeded in a legal challenge to Z-Library, having filed a complaint against 209 domains and mirror site domains associated with it. They were joined in the challenge by twelve major publishing houses: [Actes Sud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actes_Sud "Actes Sud"), [Albin Michel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albin_Michel "Albin Michel"), [Cairn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairn.info "Cairn.info"), [Editis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editis "Editis"), [Hachette Livre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hachette_Livre "Hachette Livre"), [Humensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Humensis&action=edit&redlink=1 "Humensis (page does not exist)") [[fr](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humensis "fr:Humensis")], [Lefebvre-Sarrut](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Lefebvre-Sarrut&action=edit&redlink=1 "Lefebvre-Sarrut (page does not exist)") [[fr](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lefebvre-Sarrut "fr:Lefebvre-Sarrut")], [LexisNexis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LexisNexis "LexisNexis"), [Madrigall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groupe_Madrigall "Groupe Madrigall"), [Maison des Langues](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Maison_des_Langues&action=edit&redlink=1 "Maison des Langues (page does not exist)") [[fr](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maison_des_Langues "fr:Maison des Langues")], [Odile Jacob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odile_Jacob "Odile Jacob"), and [Presses de Science Po](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sciences_Po "Sciences Po"). The decision was made by the [Tribunal Judiciaire de Paris](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribunal_judiciaire_de_Paris "fr:Tribunal judiciaire de Paris"), which is Paris's civil court; internet service providers in France were directed to block the domains.

Z-library sites and content began to reappear online after the 2022 court decision. In September 2024, the [Tribunal Judiciaire de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribunal_de_Paris "Tribunal de Paris") issued another court order requiring internet service providers to block an additional 98 domains.

### China

The site is targeted and blocked by the [Great Firewall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Firewall "Great Firewall"). In April 2024, Z-Library announced that their Chinese social media accounts, including those on [WeChat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WeChat "WeChat") and [Bilibili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilibili "Bilibili"), would be shut down in response to alleged "attacks" against volunteers by the Chinese judicial system.[*[better source needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")*]
