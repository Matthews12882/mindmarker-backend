# Lane Kiffin

**Lane Monte Kiffin** (born May 9, 1975) is an American [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_football "American football") coach who is the head coach for the [LSU Tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LSU_Tigers_football "LSU Tigers football"). He previously served as the head coach for the [Oakland Raiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland_Raiders "Oakland Raiders") from 2007 to 2009, [Tennessee Volunteers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_Volunteers_football "Tennessee Volunteers football") in 2009, [USC Trojans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USC_Trojans_football "USC Trojans football") from 2010 to 2013, [Florida Atlantic Owls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Atlantic_Owls_football "Florida Atlantic Owls football") from 2017 to 2019, and [Ole Miss Rebels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ole_Miss_Rebels_football "Ole Miss Rebels football") from 2020 to 2025.

Kiffin was the youngest head coach in modern NFL history when he became the head coach of the Raiders and was the youngest head coach of a [power conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_conference "Power conference") team in college football when he was at Tennessee and USC. Kiffin later served as the offensive coordinator at the [University of Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabama_Crimson_Tide_football "Alabama Crimson Tide football") from 2014 until 2016, when he was hired to be the head coach at [Florida Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Atlantic_Owls_football "Florida Atlantic Owls football"), a position he held until 2019, when he became the head coach at Ole Miss. In 2025, as his team was preparing for the playoffs, Kiffin left to become the head coach at LSU.

## Playing career

Kiffin is the son of longtime NFL [defensive coordinator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defensive_coordinator "Defensive coordinator") [Monte Kiffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Kiffin "Monte Kiffin"). He graduated from [Bloomington Jefferson High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomington_Jefferson_High_School "Bloomington Jefferson High School") in [Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota "Minnesota") in 1994, and committed to [Fresno State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_University,_Fresno "California State University, Fresno") to play college football. He played backup [quarterback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarterback "Quarterback") for the [Bulldogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresno_State_Bulldogs_football "Fresno State Bulldogs football"), giving up his senior season to become a Student Assistant Coach for position coach [Jeff Tedford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tedford "Jeff Tedford"). According to teammate [David Carr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Carr_(American_football) "David Carr (American football)"), Monte Kiffin forbade his son from quitting the team because of lack of playing time, so Lane became a coach instead. Kiffin graduated from Fresno State in 1998.

## Coaching career

### Early positions

Kiffin worked as a graduate assistant for one year at [Colorado State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_State_University "Colorado State University"). In 1999, while he was working with the offensive line, the Rams played in the [Liberty Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Liberty_Bowl "1999 Liberty Bowl"). Kiffin secured a job with the [Jacksonville Jaguars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville_Jaguars "Jacksonville Jaguars") of the NFL as a quality control assistant for one year. He was then hired by head coach [Pete Carroll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Carroll "Pete Carroll") as a tight ends coach at USC.

#### USC Trojans assistant coach

Kiffin began working with the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California "University of Southern California") (USC) staff during the [2001 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_NCAA_Division_I-A_football_season "2001 NCAA Division I-A football season") and became the [wide receivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide_receiver "Wide receiver") coach prior to the [2002 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_NCAA_Division_I-A_football_season "2002 NCAA Division I-A football season"). For the [2004 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_NCAA_Division_I-A_football_season "2004 NCAA Division I-A football season"), he added the duties of passing game coordinator, and he was promoted to [offensive coordinator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offensive_coordinator "Offensive coordinator") along with [Steve Sarkisian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Sarkisian "Steve Sarkisian") who returned as USC's assistant head coach and quarterbacks coach for the [2005 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_USC_Trojans_football_team "2005 USC Trojans football team") after [Norm Chow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norm_Chow "Norm Chow") left USC for the same position with the NFL's [Tennessee Titans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_Titans "Tennessee Titans"). In addition to his duties as offensive coordinator, Kiffin took the reins as recruiting coordinator that year. Along with these duties, Kiffin continued as the wide receivers coach. Under Kiffin and Sarkisian, the 2005 USC offense produced numerous school records, averaging 49.1 points and 579 yards per game and becoming the first in NCAA history to have a 3,000 yard passer ([Matt Leinart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Leinart "Matt Leinart")), two 1,000 yard rushers ([Reggie Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggie_Bush "Reggie Bush") and [LenDale White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LenDale_White "LenDale White")), and a 1,000 yard receiver ([Dwayne Jarrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwayne_Jarrett "Dwayne Jarrett")). [Steve Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Smith_(wide_receiver,_born_1985) "Steve Smith (wide receiver, born 1985)") fell a few yards short of also surpassing 1,000 yards in receiving. In Kiffin's three years as recruiting coordinator at USC, the Trojans had the top ranked recruiting class in college football every year. In 2006, the Trojans finished first in the Pac-10 in passing efficiency by averaging 142.8 passer rating, produced two, 1,000-yard receivers – Dwayne Jarrett (1,105) and Steve Smith (1,083) – and a 3,000-yard passer [John David Booty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_David_Booty "John David Booty"), with 3,347 yards. The team produced top 20 statistics in most NCAA offensive categories and concluded with a 32–18 win over the then #3 ranked team the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Wolverines_football "Michigan Wolverines football") in the [Rose Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Rose_Bowl "2007 Rose Bowl").

Kiffin helped guide USC to a 23–3 record during his tenure as offensive coordinator, an 88.5% win percentage; however, in June 2010, the NCAA retroactively declared Bush ineligible for the entire 2005 season, and forced USC to vacate all of its 2005 wins. Litigation from former coach [Todd McNair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_McNair "Todd McNair") to fight his defamation and to overturn those vacancies went on for ten years, before the defamation suit finally was settled through mediation in July 2021. The wins remained vacated.

### Oakland Raiders

Raiders' owner [Al Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Davis "Al Davis") hired the 31-year-old Kiffin on January 23, 2007, making him the youngest head coach in Oakland Raiders history, and signed Kiffin to a two-year contract worth about $4 million with a team option for 2009. Pro Football Hall of Fame Coach [John Madden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Madden "John Madden") was 32 when he was elevated to the head post by Davis in 1969. Davis had been known to select young, up-and-coming coaches in their thirties; those hires who fared well include Madden, [Mike Shanahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Shanahan "Mike Shanahan"), and [Jon Gruden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Gruden "Jon Gruden"). All have won [Super Bowls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowls "Super Bowls"), though Madden is the only one of the three to win a championship with the Raiders. Davis himself was 34 when he began his tenure as coach of the Raiders. Age 31 at the time of his hiring by the Raiders (32 when he coached his first game), Kiffin became the youngest head coach in modern NFL history (i.e. since 1946); he also surpassed the [New York Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Jets "New York Jets")' [Eric Mangini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Mangini "Eric Mangini") and the [Pittsburgh Steelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers "Pittsburgh Steelers")' [Mike Tomlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Tomlin "Mike Tomlin") as the youngest head coach since the [AFL–NFL merger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL%E2%80%93NFL_merger "AFL–NFL merger") in 1970.

On August 12, 2007, in his NFL head coaching debut, Kiffin and the Raiders won their preseason opener 27–23 over the [Arizona Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Arizona_Cardinals_season "2007 Arizona Cardinals season"). Kiffin opposed the selection of [LSU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LSU_Tigers_football "LSU Tigers football") quarterback [JaMarcus Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JaMarcus_Russell "JaMarcus Russell") in the [2007 NFL draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_NFL_draft "2007 NFL draft"), who would eventually be regarded as one of the biggest draft busts in NFL history.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] Russell held out until September 12, and did not make his first start until December 2, long after the season was effectively over. Kiffin recorded his first regular season win as an NFL head coach on September 23, 2007; the Raiders defeated the [Cleveland Browns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Cleveland_Browns_season "2007 Cleveland Browns season") by a score of 26–24 when defensive lineman [Tommy Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Kelly_(American_football) "Tommy Kelly (American football)") blocked a late Cleveland field goal. At his end-of-the-season press conference, Kiffin told the media and his players that he had many plans and changes he was going to make in the 2008 offseason. When asked by his players about rumors that Kiffin was interested in open coaching positions in college football, he told them he never thought the rumors were important enough to address because he was never planning to leave.

#### Departure from the Raiders

On January 25, 2008, [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN "ESPN") NFL analyst [Chris Mortensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Mortensen "Chris Mortensen") reported that Davis, who was not known for being patient with his coaches, tried to force Kiffin to resign after his first season ended with a 4–12 record. A source allegedly close to Kiffin told Mortensen that Kiffin would not resign, and would not sign the letter of resignation that would cause him to forfeit his $2 million salary for the remaining guaranteed year of his contract. However, the Raiders denied the story, while Kiffin refused to comment.

On September 15, 2008, [NBC Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_Sports "NBC Sports") reported Davis was unhappy with Kiffin, and intended to fire him as soon as the following Monday or Tuesday. On September 30, 2008, Davis fired Kiffin over the telephone. At the televised news conference announcing the firing, Davis called Kiffin "a flat-out liar" and said he was guilty of "bringing disgrace to the organization". The Raiders said the move was made for cause, meaning they would terminate his contract immediately without paying the $2.6 million that was left on it for 2008. Kiffin later added in an interview with ESPN that he was not proud to be associated with Davis's accusations and was actually more embarrassed for Davis than himself. The Raiders subsequently released a letter Davis sent to Kiffin on September 12 that warned him that he was on the verge of being fired for "conduct detrimental to the Raiders", including repeated instances of making excuses and outright lies. Kiffin's post-firing press conference was canceled. Kiffin filed a grievance against the Raiders, claiming that he was fired without cause, but on November 15, 2010, an arbitrator ruled that Davis did indeed have cause to fire Kiffin. Kiffin's short tenure as the Raiders' head coach ended with a 5–15 record. Offensive line coach [Tom Cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cable "Tom Cable") was given interim head coaching duties for the remainder of the [2008 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Oakland_Raiders_season "2008 Oakland Raiders season") and was later made their permanent head coach on February 4, 2009.

Several of his former Raiders staff expressed interest as Kiffin began assembling his new staff at the University of Tennessee. On December 15, 2008, Raiders head coach Cable lashed out at Kiffin for hiring one of his assistants, [James Cregg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cregg "James Cregg"), with two weeks remaining in the NFL season. Cable called the timing of Cregg's departure "wrong in the business of coaching" and indicated he had lost respect for Kiffin and planned to confront him about it. Nothing further was said publicly regarding the incident.

Despite the animosity between the Raiders and Kiffin, he released a statement following Al Davis's death in October 2011 stating that although their relationship had not ended well, he appreciated the opportunity Davis had given him and had "nothing but the greatest respect" for the late Raiders owner.

### Tennessee Volunteers

On November 28, 2008, multiple media outlets reported that Kiffin would be the next head football coach for the [University of Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tennessee "University of Tennessee") [Volunteers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_Volunteers_football "Tennessee Volunteers football") in 2009, replacing head coach [Phillip Fulmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillip_Fulmer "Phillip Fulmer"), who was fired. Tennessee formally introduced Kiffin as the school's 21st head football coach on December 1, 2008, in a 2:00 p.m. news conference.

At the age of 33, Kiffin was hired by Tennessee and became the youngest active head coach in [Division I FBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_I_FBS "Division I FBS"), surpassing [Northwestern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_Wildcats_football "Northwestern Wildcats football")'s [Pat Fitzgerald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Fitzgerald "Pat Fitzgerald").

Kiffin signed a [memorandum of understanding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorandum_of_understanding "Memorandum of understanding") with the University of Tennessee on November 30, 2008. The deal included $2 million in 2009, with additional performance bonuses, including a $300,000 bonus if Tennessee was to compete for the national championship. His salary was set to increase over the six-year-deal, reaching a high of $2.75 million in 2014. The average salary of the deal was $2.375 million. If Kiffin had been fired in 2009 or 2010, the school would have to pay him $7.5 million under a buyout clause; after the 2012 season, the buyout clause decreased to $5 million. Kiffin's contract stated that if he resigned, he would have to pay UT $1 million in 2009, with the sum decreasing by $200,000 each year of his contract.

Kiffin led the Volunteers to a 7–6 record in [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Tennessee_Volunteers_football_team "2009 Tennessee Volunteers football team"), an improvement from their 5–7 record in [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Tennessee_Volunteers_football_team "2008 Tennessee Volunteers football team"). Highlights included wins over [South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_South_Carolina_Gamecocks_football_team "2009 South Carolina Gamecocks football team"), [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Georgia_Bulldogs_football_team "2009 Georgia Bulldogs football team"), and [Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Kentucky_Wildcats_football_team "2009 Kentucky Wildcats football team"). However, the season was marred by losses to [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_UCLA_Bruins_football_team "2009 UCLA Bruins football team"), [Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Florida_Gators_football_team "2009 Florida Gators football team"), [Auburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Auburn_Tigers_football_team "2009 Auburn Tigers football team"), [Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Alabama_Crimson_Tide_football_team "2009 Alabama Crimson Tide football team"), and [Ole Miss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Ole_Miss_Rebels_football_team "2009 Ole Miss Rebels football team"), as well as a 23-point blow-out loss to [Virginia Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Virginia_Tech_Hokies_football_team "2009 Virginia Tech Hokies football team") in the [Chick-fil-A Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Chick-fil-A_Bowl "2009 Chick-fil-A Bowl"). After one season as coach, Kiffin left the Volunteers during the 2010 recruiting season to accept the head coaching job at the University of Southern California after Carroll left for the [Seattle Seahawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Seahawks "Seattle Seahawks").

#### Remarks and accusations

On February 5, 2009, during a Tennessee booster breakfast at the Knoxville Convention Center, Kiffin accused [Urban Meyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_Meyer "Urban Meyer"), then head coach of the [Florida Gators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Gators_football "Florida Gators football") and subsequently head coach of the [Ohio State Buckeyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_Buckeyes_football "Ohio State Buckeyes football"), of violating NCAA recruiting rules.

> I'm going to turn Florida in right here in front of you," Kiffin told the crowd. "As Nu'Keese (Richardson) was here on campus, his phone keeps ringing. And so one of our coaches is sitting in the meeting with him and says, 'Who is that?' And he looks at the phone and says, 'Urban Meyer.' Just so you know, you can't call a recruit on another campus. But I love the fact that Urban had to cheat and still didn't get him.

While Kiffin accused Meyer of violating NCAA rules, he incidentally violated a [Southeastern Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeastern_Conference "Southeastern Conference") rule that prevented coaches from mentioning a recruit by name. Kiffin's accusations against Meyer were mistaken.
Southeastern Conference commissioner [Mike Slive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Slive "Mike Slive") issued a public reprimand to Kiffin because of the comment. In addition to the SEC's public reprimand, Florida Athletic Director [Jeremy Foley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Foley_(athletic_director) "Jeremy Foley (athletic director)") demanded a public apology from Kiffin. Kiffin issued a public apology one day after making the comment. In a statement released by the University of Tennessee, Kiffin wrote, "In my enthusiasm for our recruiting class, I made some statements that were meant solely to excite those at the breakfast. If I offended anyone at the University of Florida, including Mr. Foley and Urban Meyer, I sincerely apologize. That was not my intention."

Kiffin generated further controversy when he told wide receiver recruit [Alshon Jeffery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alshon_Jeffery "Alshon Jeffery") that if Jeffery chose the [University of South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_South_Carolina "University of South Carolina") [Gamecocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_Gamecocks_football "South Carolina Gamecocks football"), "he would end up pumping gas for the rest of his life like all the other players from that state who had gone to South Carolina." Jeffery went on to sign with the Gamecocks, became the second round, 45th pick overall in the [2012 NFL draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_NFL_draft "2012 NFL draft") by the [Chicago Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Bears "Chicago Bears"), and subsequently a member of the Super Bowl LII champions, the [Philadelphia Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Eagles "Philadelphia Eagles"). Kiffin denied making the statement, however the incident was corroborated by Jeffery's high school coach Walter Wilson, who was listening to Kiffin's remarks on speakerphone.

#### Departure from Tennessee

Kiffin's departure for USC in 2010 after just one season as head coach of the Volunteers upset many students and fans of the University of Tennessee. When Tennessee athletic director Mike Hamilton was asked for an assessment of Kiffin's tenure coaching the Volunteers, he responded with just one word: "Brief." Hundreds of students rioted on campus at the news of Kiffin's departure. Knoxville police and fire department were brought in after students blocked the exit from the Neyland Thompson Sports Center and started several small fires.

### USC Trojans

On January 12, 2010, Kiffin returned to USC to become the [Trojans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USC_Trojans_football "USC Trojans football")' head coach. This came following Carroll's departure from USC to become the head coach of the Seattle Seahawks.

In June 2010, after a prolonged four-year investigation into whether former USC [running back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_back "Running back") Reggie Bush and his family had accepted financial benefits and housing from two [sports agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_agents "Sports agents") in [San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego "San Diego") while he was a student athlete at USC, the [NCAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA "NCAA") imposed sanctions against the Trojan football program for a "lack of institutional control", including a two-year postseason ban, the loss of 30 scholarships over three years, and the vacating of all wins in which Bush participated as an "ineligible" player, including the [2005 Orange Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Orange_Bowl "2005 Orange Bowl"), in which the Trojans won the [BCS National Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BCS_National_Championship "BCS National Championship"). The severity of these sanctions has been criticized by some NCAA football writers, including ESPN's Ted Miller, who wrote, "It's become an accepted fact among informed college football observers that the NCAA sanctions against USC were a travesty of justice, and the NCAA's refusal to revisit that travesty are a massive act of cowardice on the part of the organization."

#### 2010 season

In 2010, his first season at USC, Kiffin's Trojans team finished the season with an 8–5 record but were ineligible for post-season play due to the NCAA sanctions. After the NCAA issued a guideline allowing current USC juniors and seniors to automatically transfer from USC without having to sit out a year, several USC players left before the start of the 2010 season, including [Malik Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malik_Jackson_(defensive_lineman) "Malik Jackson (defensive lineman)") and Byron Moore to Tennessee, Travon Patterson to Colorado, D.J. Shoemate to Connecticut, Uona Kaveinga to BYU, and Blake Ayles to Miami, among others. [Seantrel Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seantrel_Henderson "Seantrel Henderson"), who had signed a [letter of intent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_of_intent "Letter of intent") to USC, was granted a release by Kiffin and immediately enrolled at Miami. Kiffin and former head coach Pete Carroll publicly referred to this NCAA-transfer exception as "free agency" because it allowed current USC players to be targeted for transfer opportunities and granted them immediate eligibility at their transfer destination. USC played the 2010 season with just 71 scholarship players, some of whom were redshirt candidates who did not play, instead of the normal NCAA allowance of 85 scholarship players.

Season highlights included a 48–14 win over the [California Golden Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_California_Golden_Bears_football_team "2010 California Golden Bears football team") in which quarterback [Matt Barkley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Barkley "Matt Barkley") tied the USC record for touchdown passes in a game by completing five in just the first half to put the Trojans up 42–0 at halftime. After losing to rival [Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Notre_Dame_Fighting_Irish_football_team "2010 Notre Dame Fighting Irish football team") for the first time in eight years, USC bounced back to close their season with a win over cross-town rival [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_UCLA_Bruins_football_team "2010 UCLA Bruins football team") to retain the [Victory Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_Bell_(UCLA%E2%80%93USC) "Victory Bell (UCLA–USC)"). Quarterback Matt Barkley returned after missing the previous week and threw one of the team's two touchdown passes. Allen Bradford led the Trojans by gaining 212 yards rushing and catching a 47-yard touchdown pass.

#### 2011 season

In 2011, Kiffin coached the Trojans to a 10–2 record (7–2 in the [Pac-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pac-12_Conference "Pac-12 Conference")), despite being ineligible for post-season play for the second consecutive season. On May 26, 2011, the NCAA's Appeals Committee upheld the sanctions against USC, after ruling that the use of [precedent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precedent "Precedent") was not allowed under NCAA Bylaws, so the USC football team could not participate in the [Pac-12 Football Championship Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pac-12_Football_Championship_Game "Pac-12 Football Championship Game") (although they held the best record in the South division) or play in a bowl game during the 2011–12 season. The BCS stripped USC of the 2004 title, though USC still retains the 2003 and 2004 AP National Championships.

Season highlights included road wins against the [California Golden Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_California_Golden_Bears_football_team "2011 California Golden Bears football team"), [Notre Dame Fighting Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Notre_Dame_Fighting_Irish_football_team "2011 Notre Dame Fighting Irish football team"), and [Oregon Ducks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Oregon_Ducks_football_team "2011 Oregon Ducks football team"). Kiffin's Trojans lost in triple overtime to the [Stanford Cardinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Stanford_Cardinal_football_team "2011 Stanford Cardinal football team"), who were led by quarterback [Andrew Luck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Luck "Andrew Luck"), but they bounced back by winning their last four games and defeating the [UCLA Bruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_UCLA_Bruins_football_team "2011 UCLA Bruins football team") 50–0 at the [Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Memorial_Coliseum "Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum"), which extended the Trojans' victory streak against the Bruins to five.

USC ended the season with two thousand-yard receivers ([Robert Woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Woods_(wide_receiver,_born_1992) "Robert Woods (wide receiver, born 1992)") and [Marqise Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marqise_Lee "Marqise Lee")), a thousand-yard rusher ([Curtis McNeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtis_McNeal "Curtis McNeal")), and a 3,000-yard passer (Matt Barkley) for the first time since the [2005 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_USC_Trojans_football_team "2005 USC Trojans football team"), when Kiffin was the offensive coordinator.

#### 2012 season

Kiffin for the first time became a voting member of the [*USA Today* Coaches' Poll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Today_Coaches%27_Poll "USA Today Coaches' Poll"), but he resigned after just one vote amidst controversy over his preseason selection of USC as No. 1. After being informed that [Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Wildcats_football "Arizona Wildcats football") coach [Rich Rodriguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rich_Rodriguez "Rich Rodriguez") had voted the Trojans as the top team, Kiffin told reporters, "I would not vote USC No. 1, I can tell you that much." However, *[USA Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Today "USA Today")*, citing the need to "protect the poll's integrity", revealed that Kiffin had voted his team for the top spot. Kiffin apologized and explained that his comments were from the perspective of an opposing coach voting for USC.
The Trojans finished the season with a 7–6 record overall and a 5–4 record in Pac-12 conference play. The Trojans were ranked #1 in both major polls at the start of the season, but a lackluster season (including a .500 record in conference play and a loss to archrival UCLA) left them unranked by the end of the season. Prior to 2012, the last time a team that was the pre-season ranked #1 finished the season unranked was the [1964 Ole Miss Rebels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Ole_Miss_Rebels_football_team "1964 Ole Miss Rebels football team").

#### 2013 season and firing

The Trojans lost their first two conference games of the 2013 season against [Washington State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Washington_State_Cougars_football_team "2013 Washington State Cougars football team") and [Arizona State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Arizona_State_Sun_Devils_football_team "2013 Arizona State Sun Devils football team"), making Kiffin's record 4–7 in his last eleven games. During the Washington State opener, USC fans began filling the Coliseum with boos and, late in the game, chants to "fire Kiffin". On September 28, 2013, after the 62–41 loss to Arizona State, USC Athletics Director [Pat Haden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Haden "Pat Haden") fired Kiffin hours after the game, when the team arrived back in Los Angeles at 3 a.m. Kiffin was called off the team bus that was preparing to head back to campus from [Los Angeles International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_International_Airport "Los Angeles International Airport") and taken to a small room inside the terminal where Haden told Kiffin he was being dismissed. After the meeting, Haden rejoined the team bus, and they headed back to campus with Kiffin's bags, leaving Kiffin behind at the airport. Haden supposedly met with USC president Max Nikias in the third quarter and they decided Kiffin should be terminated. Haden formally announced the decision to dismiss Kiffin the next day. Assistant coach [Ed Orgeron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Orgeron "Ed Orgeron") took over for Kiffin and led the team to a 6–2 finish, including an upset win against [Stanford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Stanford_Cardinal_football_team "2013 Stanford Cardinal football team") at the Coliseum. USC won the [2013 Las Vegas Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Las_Vegas_Bowl "2013 Las Vegas Bowl") under interim head coach [Clay Helton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_Helton "Clay Helton") against Fresno State. Former USC associate head coach and [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Huskies_football "Washington Huskies football") head coach Steve Sarkisian was hired by Haden following the season.

### Alabama Crimson Tide

In December 2013, Kiffin spent eight days in [Tuscaloosa, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscaloosa,_Alabama "Tuscaloosa, Alabama") reviewing the [Alabama Crimson Tide football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabama_Crimson_Tide_football "Alabama Crimson Tide football") team's offense. On January 9, 2014, after [Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Wolverines_football "Michigan Wolverines football") hired Alabama offensive coordinator [Doug Nussmeier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Nussmeier "Doug Nussmeier"), Kiffin interviewed for the vacant coordinator job. Kiffin was offered the job as offensive coordinator at Alabama and accepted on January 10.

In 2014, Kiffin was a finalist for the [Broyles Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broyles_Award "Broyles Award"), given annually to the nation's top college football assistant coach.

On January 2, 2017, three weeks after having accepted the head coaching job at Florida Atlantic, but electing to remain as the Alabama Offensive Coordinator through the playoffs, Kiffin was instead relieved of his duties as OC. He was replaced by another former USC head coach and his successor at that job, Steve Sarkisian, for the [2017 College Football Playoff National Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_College_Football_Playoff_National_Championship "2017 College Football Playoff National Championship") 35–31 loss against [Clemson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Clemson_Tigers_football_team "2016 Clemson Tigers football team") and for the upcoming season.

### Florida Atlantic Owls

On December 12, 2016, Kiffin accepted the head coaching position at [Florida Atlantic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Atlantic_University "Florida Atlantic University"). After a 1–3 start, the FAU Owls reeled off ten straight wins, culminating in the [Conference USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conference_USA "Conference USA") (C-USA) football championship against [University of North Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Texas "University of North Texas"), 41–17, on their home field. FAU was slated to play the [University of Akron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Akron "University of Akron") in the Boca Raton Bowl on December 19, 2017. Before the game against Akron, Kiffin and FAU agreed to a new ten-year deal. John Kelly, the president of FAU, stated, "This is further proof of FAU's unbridled ambition [...] I thought we could be a Top-25 program and we need a coach who can do that, he's on the verge of doing that. We're obviously looking toward keeping Lane long term." Kiffin led the Owls to a 50–3 victory over Akron in the Boca Raton Bowl, culminating in an 11–3 season in his first year. The 11–3 season was the first season over .500 for the FAU Owls since 2008, and the first time they have achieved over ten wins while competing at Division 1 football. The 2017 FAU football season is only the 4th time in school history they had a winning record in Division 1 football. In 2019, Kiffin once again led FAU to a 10-win season and a second C-USA championship.

### Ole Miss Rebels

On December 7, 2019, following FAU's blowout win in the C-USA championship game over [UAB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAB_Blazers_football "UAB Blazers football") (49–6), it was confirmed by Ole Miss AD [Keith Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Carter_(basketball) "Keith Carter (basketball)") that Kiffin would be the next head coach at Ole Miss.
On December 9, Kiffin was officially introduced as the 39th head football coach at Ole Miss. Kiffin's four-year contract totaled $16.2 million and would pay him $3.9 million in 2020 with a $100,000 yearly increment thereafter. Kiffin won his first game at Ole Miss in the second game of the [2020 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Ole_Miss_Rebels_football_team "2020 Ole Miss Rebels football team") at [Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Kentucky_Wildcats_football_team "2020 Kentucky Wildcats football team"), a 42–41 win in overtime. In Kiffin's first season Ole Miss finished 5–5 with a 4–5 record in the SEC leading to a [2021 Outback Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Outback_Bowl "2021 Outback Bowl") invitation. After winning the Outback Bowl Kiffin was given a one-year contract extension by Ole Miss (the maximum extension Ole Miss could offer since Mississippi state law only allows four-year total contracts for university employees), however financial details were not immediately released. New contract details were released in August 2021 and amounted to $21 million in base pay through 2024, with $4.5 million paid out in 2021 and over $5 million in each of the remaining three seasons.

Prior to the 2021 season, Kiffin led Ole Miss to become the first NCAA football team [100% vaccinated against COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_vaccination_in_the_United_States "COVID-19 vaccination in the United States"). This was particularly notable because at the time of Ole Miss' announcement the state of [Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi "Mississippi") ranked 49th out of 50 in COVID-19 vaccination rates. Kiffin tested positive for COVID-19 two days before the 2021 Ole Miss opener in the [Chick-fil-A Kickoff Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chick-fil-A_Kickoff_Game "Chick-fil-A Kickoff Game") with [Louisville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisville_Cardinals_football "Louisville Cardinals football") and would not make the trip with the team. With a 31–21 victory in the [Egg Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egg_Bowl "Egg Bowl") against rival [Mississippi State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Mississippi_State_Bulldogs_football_team "2022 Mississippi State Bulldogs football team"), the Rebels would finish the [2021 regular season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Ole_Miss_Rebels_football_team "2021 Ole Miss Rebels football team") 10–2. This was only the second time in half a century that Ole Miss had won 10 games, and the first time ever that it finished the regular season with 10 wins. By comparison, the Rebels' winningest coach, Hall of Famer [Johnny Vaught](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Vaught "Johnny Vaught"), never won more than nine games in a regular season (though he added a 10th win in a bowl game four times).

The Rebels slipped to 8–5 in 2022, but rebounded strongly to finish 11–2 in 2023 with a victory in the Peach Bowl, the first 11-win season in school history.

In the 2024 season, Kiffin led the Rebels to a 9–3 record, highlighted with an upset victory over #2 Georgia. In the Gator Bowl, Ole Miss defeated Duke 52–20 and finished the season 10–3. Additionally, Jaxson Dart became the winningest quarterback in program history and set the Ole Miss single-season passing yards record.

In the 2025 season, Kiffin led the Rebels to an 11-win regular season for the first time in school history, highlighted by victories over #4 [LSU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_LSU_Tigers_football_team "2025 LSU Tigers football team") and #11 [Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Oklahoma_Sooners_football_team "2025 Oklahoma Sooners football team") in Norman.

Kiffin's record with Ole Miss was 55–19, making him the third-winningest [head coach in program history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ole_Miss_Rebels_head_football_coaches "List of Ole Miss Rebels head football coaches").

#### Departure from Ole Miss

For much of the 2025 season, Kiffin was mentioned as a potential hire to fill head-coaching vacancies at LSU, Florida, and [Penn State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penn_State_Nittany_Lions_football "Penn State Nittany Lions football"). On November 22, 2025, Ole Miss Athletic Director [Keith Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Carter_(basketball) "Keith Carter (basketball)") confirmed that "an announcement on Coach Kiffin's future is expected" on November 29, a day after the annual [Egg Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egg_Bowl "Egg Bowl") game against Mississippi State. On the same day, it was reported that LSU was finalizing an offer that would make Kiffin one of the highest-paid college football coaches.

On November 30, 2025, Kiffin was to meet with the team at 9 a.m., but the meeting was postponed after reports of issues regarding staff retention, as Kiffin was seeking to bring a large contingent with him to Baton Rouge, and whether Kiffin would be allowed to coach the team for a potential [College Football Playoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_Football_Playoff "College Football Playoff") run. The meeting went on at 1 p.m., with Kiffin absent at the request of Carter. After the meeting, the team announced that defensive coordinator [Pete Golding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Golding "Pete Golding") would take over as the head coach for the playoffs, and was expected to become the full-time head coach.

Shortly after, Kiffin confirmed reports that he was leaving Ole Miss to become the head coach at LSU via a post on [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter"). Kiffin added that his request to coach the team during a potential College Football Playoff run "was denied" by Carter, making the previous day's Egg Bowl victory over Mississippi State Kiffin's final game with the Rebels program. Boarding the plane taking him to Baton Rouge from [University-Oxford Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University-Oxford_Airport "University-Oxford Airport"), Kiffin and his traveling contingent were loudly booed and jeered with obscenities by Ole Miss fans.

### LSU Tigers

On the evening of November 30, 2025, Kiffin was named as the school's 34th head football coach. He signed a seven-year, $91 million deal, making him one of the highest-paid coaches in the sport. It includes bonuses that could net him up to $1 million if the Tigers win the National Championship.

## Personal life

Lane Kiffin's father was football coach [Monte Kiffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Kiffin "Monte Kiffin"). Kiffin's brother, [Chris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Kiffin "Chris Kiffin"), was a defensive lineman at [Colorado State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_State_Rams_football "Colorado State Rams football") and was the linebackers coach for the [Houston Texans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Texans "Houston Texans"). Kiffin's former father-in-law, [John Reaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Reaves "John Reaves"), was a former starting [NFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL "NFL") and [USFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USFL "USFL") quarterback who played college football for the Florida Gators.

Lane and his ex-wife Layla Reaves, a [University of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Florida "University of Florida") alumna, have three children. On February 28, 2016, the couple announced they would divorce. Kiffin's daughter, Presley, plays volleyball at [USC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California "University of Southern California").

In 2024, Kiffin said he had been sober and [abstinent from alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teetotalism "Teetotalism") for three and a half years.
