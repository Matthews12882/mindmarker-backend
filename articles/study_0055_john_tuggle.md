# John Tuggle

**John Davis Tuggle** (January 13, 1961 – August 30, 1986) was an American professional [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_football "American football") player who was a [running back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_back "Running back") in the [National Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League "National Football League") (NFL) for one season with the [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Giants "New York Giants"). He played [college football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_football "College football") for the [California Golden Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Golden_Bears_football "California Golden Bears football") and was selected by the Giants as the [final pick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Irrelevant "Mr. Irrelevant") of the [1983 NFL draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_NFL_draft "1983 NFL draft"). Tuggle's professional career was cut short after his rookie season by a [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer "Cancer") diagnosis and he died two years later.

## Life and career

Tuggle played high school football for [Independence High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_High_School_(San_Jose,_California) "Independence High School (San Jose, California)") in [San Jose, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose,_California "San Jose, California"). The school now holds the annual John Tuggle Memorial Golf Tournament as a sports program fundraiser. He went on to play four seasons for the [California Golden Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Golden_Bears "California Golden Bears") as their starting fullback, rushing for 1,813 yards and 16 touchdowns, while also catching 108 passes for 914 yards and three scores. Tuggle earned a spot in the starting lineup in the fourth game of his true freshman season in 1979, helping the team reach the [1979 Garden State Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Garden_State_Bowl "1979 Garden State Bowl"), the school's first bowl game appearance since 1959. Cal lost the game 28–17 and finished with a 6–6 record. Cal won a combined total of just four games during Tuggle's next two seasons, but their 1980 game against [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University") turned out to be one of the highlights of his career. With a 2–8 record, Cal entered the game as 15-point underdogs against the 6–4 [Cardinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_Cardinal_football "Stanford Cardinal football") led by future NFL Hall of Famer [John Elway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Elway "John Elway"). Tuggle rushed 27 times for 110 yards and two touchdowns in the game, while also catching two passes for 37 yards as his team upset Stanford 28–23 and ruined the Cardinal's chance of playing in a bowl game. In Tuggle's senior season of 1982, he led the team in rushing and helped them achieve a 7–4 record. In their final game against Stanford, Tuggle had 28 carries for 97 yards, a performance that was largely overshadowed by the game's dramatic ending, known as [The Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Play_(American_football) "The Play (American football)").

Selected by the Giants with the last pick of the 1983 draft, granting him the unenviable title of [Mr. Irrelevant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Irrelevant "Mr. Irrelevant") for that season, Tuggle fought hard to make the roster and secured himself a spot on [special teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_football_positions#Special_teams "American football positions") after some solid plays in the pre-season. Tuggle played most of the 1983 season on special teams, but was promoted to starting fullback in week 12 when [Rob Carpenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Carpenter_(running_back) "Rob Carpenter (running back)") was injured. He finished the season with 17 carries for 49 yards and a touchdown, three receptions for 50 yards, and nine kickoff returns for 156 yards. He was voted special teams player of the year by his teammates.

The offseason in 1984 turned out to be less positive. Tuggle got divorced from his wife and suffered a knee injury during a workout, resulting in minor surgery. During the summer, Tuggle and some teammates[*[who?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Words_to_watch#Unsupported_attributions "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Words to watch")*] were involved in a minor car crash when their vehicle struck a telephone pole. Tuggle noticed pain in his shoulder that seemed too significant for the relatively minor collision. Advised to see a doctor by Carpenter, who was also in the car, Tuggle was soon diagnosed with cancer. He stayed on the team and continued to work out with them, hoping he could one day return to the field. "You could tell what a terrible thing his chemotherapy was," said Giants conditioning coach Johnny Parker, "but John actually got stronger, and although it broke your heart to do it, he wanted to be pushed, he didn't want sympathy. In the weight room, John Tuggle was not sick. John Tuggle was a standard." The Giants advised Tuggle not to report to training camp in 1985, as that way they would not have to cut him from the team and he could continue to collect his salary until his contract expired in February 1986.

In the final months of his life, Tuggle moved in with a college teammate from his days at Cal, telling him he wanted to die in California. He spent his remaining days undergoing cancer treatment in San Diego and Mexico until his death on August 30, 1986. Though they did not keep him on the team after the 1985 season, the Giants paid for Tuggle's health insurance for the remainder of his life, and wore his number 38 on their helmets during their [1986–87 Super Bowl winning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_New_York_Giants_season#Season_facts "1986 New York Giants season") season.

## In popular culture

Tuggle is the subject of the *[30 for 30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30_for_30 "30 for 30")* short, *The Irrelevant Giant*. In October 2024, it was reported that Tuggle will be the subject of the biopic *[Mr. Irrelevant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Irrelevant_(film) "Mr. Irrelevant (film)")*, portrayed by [David Corenswet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Corenswet "David Corenswet"). The film will be released in theaters on December 25, 2026.
