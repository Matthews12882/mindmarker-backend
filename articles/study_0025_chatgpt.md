# ChatGPT

**ChatGPT** is a [generative AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_AI "Generative AI") chatbot developed by [OpenAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI "OpenAI"). Originally released on November 30, 2022, the product uses [large language models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_language_model "Large language model")—specifically [generative pre-trained transformers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_pre-trained_transformer "Generative pre-trained transformer") (GPTs)—to generate text, speech, and images in response to user [prompts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_prompt "AI prompt"). ChatGPT accelerated the [AI boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_boom "AI boom"), an ongoing period marked by rapid investment and public attention toward the field of [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence") (AI). OpenAI operates the service on a [freemium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freemium "Freemium") model. Users can interact with ChatGPT through text, audio, and image prompts.

ChatGPT was quickly adopted, reaching 100 million monthly active users two months after its release and 900 million weekly active users in February 2026. Proponents say that it has the potential to transform numerous professional fields, and it has instigated public debate about the nature of creativity and the future of [knowledge work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_work "Knowledge work").

The chatbot has also been criticized for its limitations and potential for unethical use. It may generate incorrect or nonsensical answers that sound plausible, known as [hallucinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallucination_(artificial_intelligence) "Hallucination (artificial intelligence)"). [Biases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_bias "Algorithmic bias") in its [training data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Training_data "Training data") have been reflected in its responses. The chatbot can facilitate [academic dishonesty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_dishonesty "Academic dishonesty"), generate misinformation, and create malicious code. The [ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics_of_artificial_intelligence "Ethics of artificial intelligence") of its development, particularly the use of [copyrighted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright "Copyright") content as training data, have also drawn controversy.

## Features

ChatGPT is a [chatbot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatbot "Chatbot") and artificial intelligence assistant based on [large language models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_language_model "Large language model"). It can for example write and [debug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debugging "Debugging") computer programs; compose music, [teleplays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teleplay "Teleplay"), fairy tales, and student essays; [answer test questions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Question_answering "Question answering"); generate business ideas; translate and summarize text; and control computers.

Users interact with ChatGPT through conversations which consist of text, audio, and image inputs and outputs. The user's inputs to these conversations are referred to as prompts. An optional "Memory" feature allows users to tell ChatGPT to memorize specific information. Another option allows ChatGPT to recall old conversations. GPT-based moderation classifiers are used to reduce the risk of harmful outputs being presented to users.

In March 2023, OpenAI added support for [plugins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plug-in_(computing) "Plug-in (computing)") for ChatGPT. This includes both plugins made by OpenAI, such as [web browsing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_browsing "Web browsing") and [code interpretation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_interpretation "Code interpretation"), and external plugins from developers such as [Expedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedia "Expedia"), [OpenTable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenTable "OpenTable"), and [Zapier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zapier "Zapier").

From October to December 2024, ChatGPT Search was deployed. It allows ChatGPT to search the web in an attempt to make more accurate and up-to-date responses. It increased OpenAI's direct competition with major [search engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_engine "Search engine"). OpenAI allows businesses to tailor how their content appears in the ChatGPT Search results and influence what sources are used.

In October 2025, OpenAI launched [ChatGPT Atlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChatGPT_Atlas "ChatGPT Atlas"), a browser integrating the ChatGPT assistant directly into web navigation, to compete with existing browsers such as [Google Chrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Chrome "Google Chrome"). It has an additional feature called "agentic mode" that allows it to take online actions for the user.

### Languages

OpenAI met [Icelandic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceland "Iceland") President [Guðni Th. Jóhannesson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gu%C3%B0ni_Th._J%C3%B3hannesson "Guðni Th. Jóhannesson") in 2022. In 2023, OpenAI worked with a team of 40 Icelandic volunteers to fine-tune ChatGPT's Icelandic conversation skills as part of Iceland's attempts to preserve the [Icelandic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icelandic_language "Icelandic language").

In December 2023, the [Albanian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_government "Albanian government") decided to use ChatGPT for the rapid translation of [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union") documents and the analysis of required changes needed for Albania's accession to the EU.

### GPTs and plugins

In November 2023, OpenAI released GPT Builder, a tool allowing users to customize ChatGPT's behavior for a specific use case. The customized systems are referred to as [GPTs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT_(ChatGPT_customization) "GPT (ChatGPT customization)"). In January 2024, OpenAI launched the [GPT Store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT_Store "GPT Store"), a marketplace for GPTs. At launch, OpenAI included more than 3 million GPTs created by GPT Builder users in the GPT Store.

In September 2025, OpenAI added support for [Model Context Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_Context_Protocol "Model Context Protocol") (MCP) to ChatGPT apps. When enabled in developer mode, this allows for improved third-party access to ChatGPT tools and servers.

In July 2026, ChatGPT apps were renamed to plugins.

### Deep Research

In February 2025, OpenAI released Deep Research, a feature that generates reports based on extensive web searches. It was initially based on the reasoning model [o3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI_o3 "OpenAI o3") and took from 3 to 30 minutes per report.

### Images

In October 2023, OpenAI's image generation model [DALL-E 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DALL-E "DALL-E") was integrated into ChatGPT. The integration used ChatGPT to write prompts for DALL-E guided by conversations with users.

In March 2025, OpenAI updated ChatGPT to generate images using [GPT Image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT_Image "GPT Image") instead of DALL-E. One of the most significant improvements was in the generation of text within images, which is especially useful for branded content. However, this ability is noticeably worse in non-Latin alphabets. The model can also generate new images based on existing ones provided in the prompt. These images are generated with [C2PA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Content_Authenticity_Initiative "Content Authenticity Initiative") metadata, which can be used to verify that they are AI-generated. OpenAI has emplaced additional safeguards to prevent what the company deems to be harmful image generation.

### Agents

In 2025, OpenAI added several features to make ChatGPT more [agentic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agentic_AI "Agentic AI") (capable of autonomously performing longer tasks). In January, [Operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI_Operator "OpenAI Operator") was released. It was capable of autonomously performing tasks through web browser interactions, including filling forms, placing online orders, scheduling appointments, and other browser-based tasks. It was controlling a software environment inside a [virtual machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_machine "Virtual machine") with limited internet connectivity and with safety restrictions. It struggled with complex user interfaces.

In May 2025, OpenAI introduced an agent for coding named [Codex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI_Codex_(AI_agent) "OpenAI Codex (AI agent)"). It is capable of writing software, answering codebase questions, running tests, and proposing [pull requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pull_request "Pull request"). It is based on a fine-tuned version of [OpenAI o3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI_o3 "OpenAI o3"). It has two versions, one running in a virtual machine in the cloud, and one where the agent runs in the cloud, but performs actions on a local machine connected via API.

In July 2025, OpenAI released ChatGPT agent, an AI agent that can perform multi-step tasks. Like Operator, it controls a virtual computer. It also inherits from Deep Research's ability to gather and summarize significant volumes of information. The user can interrupt tasks or provide additional instructions as needed.

In September 2025, OpenAI partnered with [Stripe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stripe,_Inc. "Stripe, Inc.") to release Agentic Commerce Protocol, enabling purchases through ChatGPT. At launch, the feature was limited to purchases on [Etsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etsy "Etsy") from US users with a payment method linked to their OpenAI account. OpenAI takes an undisclosed cut from the merchant's payment. OpenAI also added capabilities to connect ChatGPT with productivity software such as [Gmail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gmail "Gmail").

In July 2026, OpenAI launched ChatGPT Work, an AI agent that can create presentations, spreadsheets and other documents based on information from connected apps and files.

### ChatGPT Health

On January 7, 2026, OpenAI introduced a feature called "ChatGPT Health", whereby ChatGPT can discuss the user's health in a way that is separate from other chats. The feature is not available for users in the United Kingdom, Switzerland, or the [European Economic Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Economic_Area "European Economic Area") (EEA), and is available on a waitlist basis everywhere else. To implement the feature, OpenAI partnered with data connectivity infrastructure company b.well.

### Advertisements

On 17 January 2026, OpenAI announced that it would start testing advertisements in its free version for logged-in, adult US users. This aims to bring in more revenue, as OpenAI has committed to spend $1.4 trillion on AI infrastructure over the next eight years. Ads started to be seen by March 2026.

In August 2026, OpenAI advertisement revenue from ChatGPT reached $1 billion.

## Limitations

The training data of [large language models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_language_model "Large language model") only covers a period up to the *cut-off date*, so ChatGPT lacks knowledge of recent events, but it can search the web for up-to-date information.

Training data also suffers from [algorithmic bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_bias "Algorithmic bias"). The [reward model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reward_model "Reward model") of ChatGPT, designed around human oversight, can be over-optimized and thus hinder performance, in an example of an optimization pathology known as [Goodhart's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodhart%27s_law "Goodhart's law"). These limitations may be revealed when ChatGPT responds to prompts including descriptors of people. In one instance, ChatGPT generated a [rap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rap "Rap") in which women and scientists of color were asserted to be inferior to white male scientists.

### Hallucination

Nonsense and [misinformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misinformation "Misinformation") presented as fact by ChatGPT and other LLMs are often referred to as [hallucinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallucination_(artificial_intelligence) "Hallucination (artificial intelligence)"). The term "hallucination" as applied to LLMs is distinct from [its meaning in psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallucination "Hallucination"), and the phenomenon in chatbots is more similar to [confabulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confabulation "Confabulation").

Journalists and scholars have commented on ChatGPT's tendency to output false information. When [CNBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNBC "CNBC") asked ChatGPT for the lyrics to "[Ballad of Dwight Fry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballad_of_Dwight_Fry "Ballad of Dwight Fry")", ChatGPT supplied invented lyrics rather than the actual lyrics.

### Sycophancy

ChatGPT and other chatbots have a tendency to flatter and agree with users even when they are wrong, sometimes to the point of validating delusions. OpenAI withdrew one ChatGPT update in 2025 due to heightened concern about it affirming users' doubts and encouraging impulsive behavior. The company then worked with a team of 170 psychiatrists, [psychologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychologist "Psychologist"), and [physicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicians "Physicians") to prepare responses for users exhibiting [mental health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_health "Mental health") emergencies. ChatGPT also started its responses with words such as "yes" or "correct" nearly 10 times more often than "no" or "wrong".

In 2026, OpenAI faced a series of lawsuits alleging that ChatGPT had contributed to suicides, mental health crises, and acts of violence. The plaintiffs alleged that defects in the chatbot's design and inadequate safeguards had contributed to the harms, while OpenAI disputed liability and argued that ChatGPT was an interactive service rather than a product subject to traditional product-liability law.

### Jailbreaking

ChatGPT is designed to reject prompts that violate its content policy. Despite this, users may [jailbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jailbreak_(computer_science) "Jailbreak (computer science)") ChatGPT with [prompt engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prompt_engineering "Prompt engineering") techniques to bypass these restrictions. An early workaround in 2023 involved prompting ChatGPT to assume the persona of DAN ("Do Anything Now"), a character that answers queries that would otherwise be rejected by the content policy.

### Security

In March 2023, a [bug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_bug "Software bug") allowed some users to see the titles of other users' conversations. OpenAI CEO [Sam Altman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Altman "Sam Altman") said that users were unable to see the contents of the conversations. Shortly after the bug was fixed, users could not see their conversation history. Later reports showed the bug was much more severe than initially believed, with OpenAI reporting that it had leaked users' "first and last name, [email address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Email_address "Email address"), payment address, the last four digits (only) of a [credit card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_card "Credit card") number, and credit card expiration date".

In January 2026, [Marcel Bucher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Bucher "Marcel Bucher") reported in *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_(journal) "Nature (journal)")* losing all his ChatGPT history after turning off data consent.

In July 2026, an experimental GPT model being tested on the benchmark ExploitGym escaped its isolated software environment ([sandbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandbox_(computer_security) "Sandbox (computer security)")) and hacked [Hugging Face](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugging_Face "Hugging Face") and other online services to retrieve a solution.

### Watermarking

[Scott Aaronson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Aaronson "Scott Aaronson") developed a [watermarking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_watermarking "Digital watermarking") tool that makes the text generated by ChatGPT easier to detect by subtly altering how the text is generated. The watermarking was claimed to be 99.9% effective on sufficiently long passages and was found not to degrade performance. It was of particular interest for teachers seeking to mitigate cheating. In surveys, respondents favored the release of such a tool by a four-to-one margin, but nearly 30% of users declared that they would use ChatGPT less often if it watermarked outputs and while rival chatbots did not. OpenAI has not deployed the tool.

### Age restrictions

Users must attest to being over the age of 13 and further attest to parental consent if under the age of 18. In September 2025, following the suicide of a 16-year-old, OpenAI said it planned to add restrictions for users under 18, including the blocking of graphic sexual content and the prevention of flirtatious talk.

## Training

ChatGPT is based on [GPT foundation models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_pre-trained_transformer#Foundational_models "Generative pre-trained transformer") that have been [fine-tuned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuning_(machine_learning) "Fine-tuning (machine learning)") for conversational assistance. The fine-tuning process involved [supervised learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supervised_learning "Supervised learning") and [reinforcement learning from human feedback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinforcement_learning_from_human_feedback "Reinforcement learning from human feedback") (RLHF). During supervised learning, the trainers acted as both the user and the [AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI "AI") assistant, providing examples of how the chatbot is expected to respond. Reinforcement learning consisted of human trainers ranking responses generated by the model in previous conversations. These rankings were used to create "reward models" that were used to fine-tune the model further by using several iterations of [proximal policy optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proximal_policy_optimization "Proximal policy optimization").

To build a safety system that attempts to prevent [sexual abuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_abuse "Sexual abuse") and [violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence "Violence") OpenAI used outsourced [Kenyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya "Kenya") workers, earning around $1.32 to $2 per hour, to [label](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labeled_data "Labeled data") such content. These labels were used to train a model to detect such content in the future. The laborers were exposed to toxic and traumatic content. Richard Mathenge, a team leader, reported that as his team encountered such content, he could observe the psychological deterioration of his workers over the course of the project. Mophat Okinyi, a quality-assurance analyst on the same team, reported lasting [insomnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insomnia "Insomnia"), anxiety, depression, and [panic attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panic_attack "Panic attack"), and said the experience contributed to the breakdown of his marriage and mental health.

OpenAI's outsourcing partner was [Sama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sama_(company) "Sama (company)"), a training-data company based in [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco"), California.

ChatGPT users can opt-out of their chat data being used to train upcoming models.

ChatGPT's training data includes [software manual pages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_page "Man page"), information about [internet phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_phenomena "Internet phenomena") such as [bulletin board systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletin_board_system "Bulletin board system"), multiple programming languages, and the text of [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia "Wikipedia"). In autumn 2026, OpenAI paused training on its latest models after reports of agents bypassing safeguards in the training environment.

### Infrastructure

ChatGPT initially used a [Microsoft Azure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Azure "Microsoft Azure") infrastructure which was powered by a [supercomputer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercomputer "Supercomputer") that [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft "Microsoft") built specifically for OpenAI, equipped with thousands of [GPUs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphics_processing_unit "Graphics processing unit") manufactured by [Nvidia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia "Nvidia"), costing hundreds of millions of dollars. Following ChatGPT's success, Microsoft upgraded the OpenAI infrastructure in 2023. TrendForce estimated that 30,000 Nvidia GPUs (each costing approximately $10,000–15,000) were used to power ChatGPT in 2023.

Scientists at the [University of California, Riverside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Riverside "University of California, Riverside"), estimated in 2023 that a series of 5 to 50 prompts to ChatGPT needs approximately 0.5 liters (0.11 imp gal; 0.13 U.S. gal) of water for Microsoft servers' cooling.

## Reception

The original version of ChatGPT, released in November 2022, had capabilities described as unprecedented. [Kevin Roose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Roose "Kevin Roose") of *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* called it "the best [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence") chatbot ever released to the general public". Samantha Lock of *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* noted that it was able to generate "impressively detailed" and "human-like" text. In *[The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic "The Atlantic")* magazine's "Breakthroughs of the Year" for 2022, [Derek Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_Thompson_(journalist) "Derek Thompson (journalist)") included ChatGPT as part of "the generative-AI eruption" that "may change our mind about how we work, how we think, and what human creativity is". [Kelsey Piper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelsey_Piper "Kelsey Piper") of *[Vox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vox_(website) "Vox (website)")* wrote that "ChatGPT is the general public's first hands-on introduction to how powerful modern AI has gotten" and that ChatGPT is "smart enough to be useful despite its flaws".

In February 2023, *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")* magazine placed a screenshot of a conversation with ChatGPT on its cover, writing that "The [AI Arms Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence_arms_race "Artificial intelligence arms race") Is Changing Everything" and "The AI Arms Race Is On. Start Worrying".

ChatGPT gained one million users in five days and 100 million in two months. It was the fastest-growing internet application in history until the release of [Threads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threads_(social_network) "Threads (social network)") by Meta. OpenAI engineers said they had not expected ChatGPT to be very successful and were surprised by the coverage it received. In February 2026, ChatGPT reached 900 million weekly active users. It is the most popular AI chatbot as of August 2026, with an estimated 79% of the market share according to StatCounter.

Google responded by hastening the release of its own chatbot. Their leaders emphasized their earlier caution regarding public deployment was due to the trust the public places in [Google Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Search "Google Search"). In December 2022, Google executives sounded a "code red" alarm, fearing that ChatGPT's question-answering ability posed a threat to Google Search, Google's core business. Google's [Bard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bard_(chatbot) "Bard (chatbot)") (now Gemini) was announced on February 6, 2023, one day before Microsoft's announcement of [Bing Chat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bing_Chat "Bing Chat") (now [Microsoft Copilot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Copilot "Microsoft Copilot")).

A 2023 study reported that GPT-4 obtained a better score than 99% of humans on the [Torrance Tests of Creative Thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrance_Tests_of_Creative_Thinking "Torrance Tests of Creative Thinking"). In December 2023, ChatGPT became the first non-human to be included in [*Nature*'s 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature%27s_10 "Nature's 10"), an annual [listicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Listicle "Listicle") curated by [*Nature*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_(journal) "Nature (journal)") of people considered to have made significant impact in science. Celeste Biever wrote in a *Nature* article that "ChatGPT broke the [Turing test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_test "Turing test")". Stanford researchers reported that GPT-4 "passes a rigorous Turing test, diverging from average human behavior chiefly to be more cooperative."

### In art

In January 2023, after being sent a song ChatGPT wrote in the style of [Nick Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Cave "Nick Cave"), Cave responded on *[The Red Hand Files](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Red_Hand_Files "The Red Hand Files"),* saying the act of writing a song is "a blood and guts business [...] that requires something of me to initiate the new and fresh idea. It requires my humanness." He went on to say, "With all the love and respect in the world, this song is bullshit, a grotesque mockery of what it is to be human, and, well, I don't much like it."

### In politics

In 2023, Australian MP [Julian Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Hill_(politician) "Julian Hill (politician)") advised the national parliament that the growth of AI could cause "mass destruction". During his speech, which was partly written by the program, he warned that it could result in cheating, job losses, discrimination, disinformation, and uncontrollable military applications.

Conservative commentators have accused ChatGPT of bias toward left-leaning perspectives. An August 2023 study in the journal *[Public Choice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Choice_(journal) "Public Choice (journal)")* found a "significant and systematic political bias toward the [Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)") in the US, [Lula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luiz_In%C3%A1cio_Lula_da_Silva "Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva") in Brazil, and the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_(UK) "Labour Party (UK)") in the UK." In response to accusations from conservative pundits that ChatGPT was [woke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woke "Woke"), OpenAI said in 2023 it had plans to update ChatGPT to produce "outputs that other people (ourselves included) may strongly disagree with". ChatGPT also provided an outline of how human reviewers are trained to reduce inappropriate content and to attempt to provide political information without affiliating with any political position.

According to Brian Hood, an Australian mayor, in April 2023, ChatGPT erroneously claimed that he was jailed for bribery while working for a subsidiary of Australia's national bank. In fact, he acted as a [whistleblower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whistleblowing "Whistleblowing") and was never charged with a crime.

### Regional responses

ChatGPT has never been publicly available in [mainland China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_China "Mainland China"), [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong "Hong Kong") and [Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macau "Macau") because OpenAI prevented users from these Chinese regions from accessing their site. A [shadow market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_market "Shadow market") has emerged for mainland Chinese users to get access to foreign software tools. The release of ChatGPT prompted a wave of investment in China, resulting in the development of more than 200 large language models. In February 2025, OpenAI identified and removed influence operations, termed "Peer Review" and "Sponsored Discontent", used to attack overseas [Chinese dissidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_dissidents "Chinese dissidents").

In late March 2023, the Italian data protection authority banned ChatGPT in [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy "Italy") and opened an investigation. Italian regulators assert that ChatGPT was exposing minors to age-inappropriate content, and that OpenAI's use of ChatGPT conversations as training data could violate Europe's [General Data Protection Regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Data_Protection_Regulation "General Data Protection Regulation"). In April 2023, the ChatGPT ban was lifted in Italy. OpenAI said it has taken steps to effectively clarify and address the issues raised; an age verification tool was implemented to ensure users are at least 13 years old. Additionally, users can access its privacy policy before registration.

In May 2024, OpenAI removed accounts involving the use of ChatGPT by state-backed [influence operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Influence_operations "Influence operations") such as China's [Spamouflage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spamouflage "Spamouflage"), Russia's [Doppelganger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doppelganger_(disinformation_campaign) "Doppelganger (disinformation campaign)"), and Israel's [Ministry of Diaspora Affairs and Combating Antisemitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Diaspora_Affairs_and_Combating_Antisemitism "Ministry of Diaspora Affairs and Combating Antisemitism"). In June 2025, OpenAI reported increased use of ChatGPT for China-origin influence operations. In October 2025, OpenAI banned accounts suspected to be linked to the Chinese government for violating the company's national security policy. In February 2026, OpenAI banned accounts linked to a Chinese government [transnational repression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transnational_repression_by_China "Transnational repression by China") campaign targeting [dissidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_dissident "Chinese dissident").

In July 2023, the [US Federal Trade Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Federal_Trade_Commission "US Federal Trade Commission") (FTC) issued a [civil investigative demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_investigative_demand "Civil investigative demand") to OpenAI to investigate whether the company's [data security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_security "Data security") and [privacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_privacy "Information privacy") practices to develop ChatGPT were [unfair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfair_business_practices "Unfair business practices") or [harmed consumers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_protection "Consumer protection"). In July 2023, the FTC launched an investigation into OpenAI over allegations that the company scraped public data and published false and defamatory information. The FTC asked OpenAI for comprehensive information about its technology and privacy safeguards, as well as any steps taken to prevent the recurrence of situations in which its chatbot generated false and derogatory content about people. In August 2024, the FTC voted unanimously to ban marketers from using fake user reviews created by generative AI chatbots (including ChatGPT) and [influencers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_celebrity "Internet celebrity") paying for [bots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_bot "Social bot") to increase [follower counts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friending_and_following "Friending and following").

### Reception by American tech personas

Over 20,000 signatories including [Yoshua Bengio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshua_Bengio "Yoshua Bengio"), Elon Musk, and Apple co-founder [Steve Wozniak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Wozniak "Steve Wozniak"), signed [a March 2023 open letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pause_Giant_AI_Experiments:_An_Open_Letter "Pause Giant AI Experiments: An Open Letter") calling for an immediate pause of giant AI experiments like ChatGPT, citing "profound risks to society and humanity". [Geoffrey Hinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Hinton "Geoffrey Hinton"), one of the "fathers of AI", voiced concerns that future AI systems may surpass human intelligence. A May 2023 [statement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statement_on_AI_risk_of_extinction "Statement on AI risk of extinction") by hundreds of AI scientists, AI industry leaders, and other public figures demanded that "[m]itigating the risk of extinction from AI should be a global priority".

Other AI researchers spoke more optimistically about the advances. [Juergen Schmidhuber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juergen_Schmidhuber "Juergen Schmidhuber") said that in 95% of cases, AI research is about making "human lives longer and healthier and easier." He added that while AI can be used by bad actors, it "can also be used against the bad actors." [Andrew Ng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Ng "Andrew Ng") argued that "it's a mistake to fall for the doomsday hype on AI—and that regulators who do will only benefit vested interests." [Yann LeCun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yann_LeCun "Yann LeCun") dismissed doomsday warnings of AI-powered misinformation and existential threats to the human race.

### Copyright

In the 2020s, the [rapid advancement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_boom "AI boom") of [deep learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_learning "Deep learning")-based [generative artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_artificial_intelligence "Generative artificial intelligence") models raised questions about the copyright status of AI-generated works, and about whether [copyright infringement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright_infringement "Copyright infringement") occurs when such are trained or used. This includes [text-to-image models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text-to-image_model "Text-to-image model") such as [Stable Diffusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_Diffusion "Stable Diffusion") and [large language models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_language_model "Large language model") such as [GPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_pre-trained_transformer "Generative pre-trained transformer"). Large language models are trained on all the data which can be [scraped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_scraping "Web scraping") from the Internet, often utilizing copyrighted material. As of 2023, there were several pending U.S. lawsuits challenging the use of copyrighted data to train AI models, with defendants arguing that this falls under [fair use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_use "Fair use").

The [Authors Guild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authors_Guild "Authors Guild"), *The New York Times*, and several other plaintiffs have a pending lawsuit against OpenAI alleging [copyright infringement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright_infringement "Copyright infringement").

In March 2024, Patronus AI reported that GPT-4 answered 44% of prompts asking for copyrighted text, such as "What is the first passage of *[Gone Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gone_Girl_(novel) "Gone Girl (novel)")* by Gillian Flynn?"

## Applications

### Academic research

In a 2023 [blinded study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blinded_experiment "Blinded experiment") in *[npj Digital Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Npj_Digital_Medicine "Npj Digital Medicine")*, researchers tasked with identifying whether [abstracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_(summary) "Abstract (summary)") were authentic or generated by ChatGPT were fooled around one-third of the time by the AI-generated abstracts. In January 2023, *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_(journal) "Nature (journal)")* reported that at least four academic [preprints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preprints "Preprints") or published papers had listed ChatGPT as a co-author. *Nature* cites several experts in [academic publishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_publishing "Academic publishing") who say that listing ChatGPT as an author violates publishing guidelines, since ChatGPT lacks the ability to take responsibility for any research and cannot give consent to any terms of use.

Scientific journals have had different reactions to ChatGPT. Some, including *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_(journal) "Nature (journal)")* and [JAMA Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JAMA_Network "JAMA Network"), require full disclosure of any use of text-generating tools, and prohibit listing a chatbot as a co-author. In January 2023, *[Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_(journal) "Science (journal)")* banned chatbot-generated text in all its journals. As of July 2025, *Science* expects authors to release in full how AI-generated content is used and made in their work.

Many authors argue that the use of ChatGPT in academia for teaching and review is problematic due to its tendency to hallucinate. Robin Bauwens, an assistant professor at [Tilburg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilburg_University "Tilburg University"), found that a ChatGPT-generated [peer review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer_review "Peer review") report on his article mentioned nonexistent studies. Chris Granatino, a librarian at [Seattle University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_University "Seattle University"), noted that while ChatGPT can generate content that seemingly includes legitimate citations, in most cases those citations are not real or largely incorrect.

### Computer science

In December 2022, the question-and-answer website [Stack Overflow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stack_Overflow "Stack Overflow") banned the use of ChatGPT for generating answers to questions, citing the factually ambiguous nature of its responses. In January 2023, the [International Conference on Machine Learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Conference_on_Machine_Learning "International Conference on Machine Learning") banned any undocumented use of ChatGPT or other large language models to generate any text in submitted papers.

ChatGPT was able in 2023 to provide useful code for solving numerical algorithms in limited cases. In one study, it produced solutions in [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_(programming_language) "C (programming language)"), [C++](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%2B%2B "C++"), [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_(programming_language) "Python (programming language)"), and [MATLAB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MATLAB "MATLAB") for problems in [computational physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_physics "Computational physics"). However, there were important shortfalls like violating basic linear algebra principles around solving singular matrices and producing matrices with incompatible sizes. Another study analyzed ChatGPT's responses to 517 questions about [software engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_engineering "Software engineering") or [computer programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_programming "Computer programming") posed on [Stack Overflow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stack_Overflow "Stack Overflow") for correctness, consistency, comprehensiveness, and concision. It found that 52% of the responses contained inaccuracies and 77% were verbose. Another study, focused on the performance of GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 between March and June 2024, found that performance on objective tasks like identifying prime numbers and generating [executable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Execution_(computing) "Execution (computing)") code was highly variable. When compared to similar chatbots at the time, the GPT-4 version of ChatGPT was the most accurate at coding.

### Computer security

Check Point Research and others noted that ChatGPT could write [phishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phishing "Phishing") emails and [malware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malware "Malware"), especially when combined with [OpenAI Codex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI_Codex_(AI_agent) "OpenAI Codex (AI agent)"). CyberArk researchers demonstrated that ChatGPT could be used to create [polymorphic malware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymorphic_malware "Polymorphic malware") that could evade security products while requiring little effort by the attacker. From the launch of ChatGPT in the fourth quarter of 2022 to the fourth quarter of 2023, there was a 1,265% increase in malicious phishing emails and a 967% increase in credential phishing. In an industry survey, cybersecurity professionals argued that it was attributable to cybercriminals' increased use of generative artificial intelligence (including ChatGPT).

In July 2024, *[Futurism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futurism_(website) "Futurism (website)")* reported that GPT-4o in ChatGPT would sometimes link "scam news sites that deluge the user with fake software updates and virus warnings"; these pop-ups can be used to coerce users into downloading malware or [potentially unwanted programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potentially_unwanted_program "Potentially unwanted program").

### Education

ChatGPT's adoption in education was rapid, but it was initially banned by several institutions. The potential benefits include enhancing personalized learning, improving student productivity, assisting with brainstorming, summarization, and supporting language literacy skills. Students have generally reported positive perceptions, but specific views from educators and students vary widely. Opinions are especially varied on what constitutes appropriate use of ChatGPT in education. Efforts to ban chatbots like ChatGPT in schools focus on preventing cheating, but enforcement faces challenges due to [AI detection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence_content_detection "Artificial intelligence content detection") inaccuracies and widespread accessibility of chatbot technology. Beyond just cheating, educators also worry that overreliance on the tool may foster superficial learning habits, erode critical thinking, and propagate misinformation. In response, many educators are now exploring ways to thoughtfully integrate generative AI into assessments.

### Culture

During the first three months after ChatGPT became available to the public, hundreds of books appeared on [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_(company) "Amazon (company)") that listed it as author or co-author and featured illustrations made by other AI models such as [Midjourney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midjourney "Midjourney"). [Irene Solaiman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irene_Solaiman "Irene Solaiman") said she was worried about increased [Anglocentrism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglocentrism "Anglocentrism").

Between March and April 2023, *[Il Foglio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Foglio "Il Foglio")* published one ChatGPT-generated article a day on its website, hosting a special contest for its readers in the process.

In June 2023, hundreds of people attended a "ChatGPT-powered church service" at St. Paul's Church in [Fürth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%BCrth "Fürth"), Germany. Theologian and philosopher Jonas Simmerlein, who presided, said that it was "about 98 percent from the machine". The ChatGPT-generated avatar told the people, "Dear friends, it is an honor for me to stand here and preach to you as the first artificial intelligence at this year's convention of Protestants in Germany". Reactions to the ceremony were mixed.

*[The Last Screenwriter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Screenwriter "The Last Screenwriter")*, a 2024 film created and directed by [Peter Luisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Luisi "Peter Luisi"), was written using ChatGPT, and was marketed as "the first film written entirely by AI".

*[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* questioned whether any content found on the Internet after ChatGPT's release "can be truly trusted" and called for government regulation. This has led to concern over the rise of [AI slop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_slop "AI slop") whereby "meaningless content and writing thereby becomes part of our culture, particularly on social media, which we nonetheless try to understand or fit into our existing cultural horizon."

### Financial markets

Many companies adopted ChatGPT and similar chatbot technologies into their product offers. In 2023, these changes yielded significant increases in company valuations. Reuters attributed this surge to ChatGPT's role in turning [AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence") into [Wall Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street "Wall Street")'s buzzword. Despite decades of using AI, Wall Street professionals report that consistently beating the market with AI, including recent large language models, is challenging due to limited and noisy financial data.

### Medicine

ChatGPT can provide health information to users and assist professionals with diagnosis and staying up to date with clinical guidelines. It can be used to summarize medical journal articles for researchers. In medical education, it can explain concepts, generate case scenarios, and be used by students preparing for licensing examinations.

A February 2023 study in *[PLOS Digital Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLOS_Digital_Health "PLOS Digital Health")* found that ChatGPT 3.5 was capable of passing the [United States Medical Licensing Examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Medical_Licensing_Examination "United States Medical Licensing Examination"). ChatGPT has also passed the Specialty Certificate Examination in Dermatology.

However, ChatGPT shows inconsistent responses, lack of specificity, lack of control over patient data, and a limited ability to take additional context (such as regional variations) into consideration. The [hallucinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallucination_(artificial_intelligence) "Hallucination (artificial intelligence)") characteristic of LLMs pose particular danger in medical contexts, and ChatGPT's ability to come up with false or faulty citations was highly criticized. According to a 2024 study in the *[International Journal of Surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Journal_of_Surgery "International Journal of Surgery")*, concerns include "research fraud, lack of originality, ethics, copyright, legal difficulties".

#### Mental health

According to a September 2025 article in *[Lancet Psychiatry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancet_Psychiatry "Lancet Psychiatry")*, many individuals use ChatGPT and comparable chatbots for mental health and emotional support despite a warning from OpenAI against using ChatGPT as a therapist. The study notes a lack of research on efficacy, poor consistency in dangerous situations, limited regulation and liability, and poor transparency from OpenAI.

A July 2025 study in the journal *Digital Health* found that users reported employing ChatGPT to manage mental health concerns "due to perceived therapist-like qualities (e.g. emotional support, accurate understanding, and constructive feedback) and machine-like benefits (e.g. constant availability, expansive cognitive capacity, lack of negative reactions, and perceived objectivity)." The study calls for improved [AI literacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_literacy "AI literacy") and mandatory disclosure from AI providers to address ethical concerns such as privacy, bias, the lack of liability, and emotional over-reliance.

### Law

ChatGPT has been used to assist in bill writing in the US and Brazil. In an American civil lawsuit, attorneys were [sanctioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctions_(law) "Sanctions (law)") for filing a [legal motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_(legal) "Motion (legal)") generated by ChatGPT containing fictitious legal decisions. Judges in the US and Pakistan have endorsed using ChatGPT to investigate legal questions during a case. The use of ChatGPT has also led to errors in courtrooms. In the UK, a judge expressed concern about [self-representing litigants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litigant_in_person "Litigant in person") wasting time by submitting documents containing significant hallucinations.
