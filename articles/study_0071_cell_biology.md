# Cell (biology)

The **cell** is the basic structural and functional unit of all [forms of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life "Life") or [organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organism "Organism"). The term comes from the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin") word *cellula* meaning 'small room'. A biological cell basically consists of a [semipermeable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semipermeable_membrane "Semipermeable membrane") [cell membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_membrane "Cell membrane") enclosing [cytoplasm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytoplasm "Cytoplasm") that contains genetic material. Most cells are only visible under a [microscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microscopic_scale "Microscopic scale"). Except for highly-[differentiated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_differentiation "Cellular differentiation") cell types (examples include [red blood cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_blood_cell "Red blood cell") and [gametes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamete "Gamete")) most cells are capable of [replication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-replication "Self-replication"), and [protein synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_synthesis "Protein synthesis"). Some types of cell are [motile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_motility "Cell motility"). Cells [emerged on Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abiogenesis "Abiogenesis") about four billion years ago.

All organisms are grouped into [prokaryotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prokaryote "Prokaryote") and [eukaryotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eukaryote "Eukaryote"). Prokaryotes are [single-celled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-celled "Single-celled") and include [archaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaea "Archaea") and [bacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteria "Bacteria"). Eukaryotes can be single-celled or [multicellular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multicellular "Multicellular"). Single-celled eukaryotes include most [protists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protist "Protist"), some species of [fungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fungi "Fungi") ([yeasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeast "Yeast")), and some species of [algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae "Algae"). Multicellular eukaryotes include [animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal "Animal"), [plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryophyte "Embryophyte"), some species of fungi, and some species of algae. All multicellular organisms are made up of many different [types of cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_type "Cell type"). The [diploid cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diploid_cell "Diploid cell") that make up the body of an animal or plant are known as [somatic cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somatic_cell "Somatic cell"), which excludes the [haploid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haploid "Haploid") [gametes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamete "Gamete").

Prokaryotes lack a [membrane-bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane-bound "Membrane-bound") [nucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_nucleus "Cell nucleus") and have a [nucleoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleoid "Nucleoid") instead. In eukaryotic cells, the nucleus is enclosed in the [nuclear membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_membrane "Nuclear membrane"). Eukaryotic cells contain other [membrane-bound organelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane-bound_organelle "Membrane-bound organelle") such as [mitochondria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondria "Mitochondria"), which provide energy for cell functions, and [chloroplasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloroplast "Chloroplast"), in plants that create sugars by [photosynthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photosynthesis "Photosynthesis"). Other [membrane-less organelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organelle#Types "Organelle") may be [proteinaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proteinaceous "Proteinaceous"), such as the [ribosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribosome "Ribosome") present (though different) in both groups. A unique membrane-bound [prokaryotic organelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prokaryotic_organelle "Prokaryotic organelle"), the [magnetosome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetosome "Magnetosome") has been discovered in [magnetotactic bacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetotactic_bacteria "Magnetotactic bacteria").

Cells were discovered by [Robert Hooke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hooke "Robert Hooke") in 1665, who named them after their resemblance to [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monastic_cell "Monastic cell") in a [monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monastery "Monastery"). [Cell theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_theory "Cell theory") was developed through the work of [Matthias Jakob Schleiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Jakob_Schleiden "Matthias Jakob Schleiden") in 1838 and [Theodor Schwann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Schwann "Theodor Schwann") in 1839, who established the cell as the basic structural unit of plants and animals. It states that all organisms are composed of one or more cells, that cells are the fundamental unit of structure and function in all organisms, and that all cells come from pre-existing cells.

## Types

Organisms are broadly grouped into [eukaryotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eukaryote "Eukaryote") and [prokaryotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prokaryote "Prokaryote"). Eukaryotic cells possess a [membrane-bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane-bound "Membrane-bound") [nucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_nucleus "Cell nucleus"), and [prokaryotic cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prokaryotic_cell "Prokaryotic cell") lack a nucleus but have a [nucleoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleoid "Nucleoid") region. Prokaryotes are [single-celled organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-celled_organism "Single-celled organism"), whereas eukaryotes can be either single-celled or [multicellular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multicellular_organism "Multicellular organism"). Single-celled eukaryotes include [microalgae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microalgae "Microalgae") such as [diatoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diatom "Diatom"), and some [yeasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeast "Yeast") , species of [fungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fungi "Fungi"). Multicellular eukaryotes include all [animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal "Animal"), and [plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant "Plant"), most fungi, and some species of [algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae "Algae").

### Prokaryotes

All [prokaryotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prokaryote "Prokaryote") are single-celled and include [bacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteria "Bacteria") and [archaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaea "Archaea"), two of the [three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_domain_system "Three domain system") [domains of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_(taxonomy) "Domain (taxonomy)"). Prokaryotic cells were likely the first form of [life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life "Life") on Earth, characterized by having vital [biological processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_process "Biological process") including [cell signaling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_signaling "Cell signaling"). They are simpler and smaller than eukaryotic cells, lack a [nucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_nucleus "Cell nucleus"), and the other usually present [membrane-bound organelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane-bound_organelle "Membrane-bound organelle"). [Prokaryotic organelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prokaryotic_organelle "Prokaryotic organelle") are less complex, and are typically [membrane-less](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane-less_organelle "Membrane-less organelle"). All prokaryotic cells [secrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secrete "Secrete") different substances from their membranes, including [exoenzymes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exoenzyme "Exoenzyme"), and [extracellular polymeric substances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extracellular_polymeric_substance "Extracellular polymeric substance").

Most prokaryotes are the smallest of all organisms, ranging from 0.1 to 5.0 μm in diameter. The largest bacterium known, *[Thiomargarita magnifica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiomargarita_magnifica "Thiomargarita magnifica")*, is visible to the naked eye with an average length of 1 cm, but can reach 2 cm.

#### Bacteria

[Bacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteria "Bacteria") are enclosed in a [cell envelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_envelope "Cell envelope"), that protects the interior from the exterior. It generally consists of a [plasma membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_membrane "Plasma membrane") covered by a [cell wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_wall "Cell wall") which, for some bacteria, is covered by a third layer, a gelatinous [bacterial capsule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacterial_capsule "Bacterial capsule"). The capsule may be [polysaccharide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polysaccharide "Polysaccharide") as in [pneumococci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumococci "Pneumococci"), [meningococci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meningococci "Meningococci") or [polypeptide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polypeptide "Polypeptide") as *[Bacillus anthracis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacillus_anthracis "Bacillus anthracis")* or [hyaluronic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyaluronic_acid "Hyaluronic acid") as in [streptococci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streptococci "Streptococci"). *[Mycoplasma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycoplasma "Mycoplasma")* only possess the cell membrane. The cell envelope gives rigidity to the cell and separates the interior of the cell from its environment, serving as a protective mechanical and chemical filter. The cell wall consists of [peptidoglycan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peptidoglycan "Peptidoglycan") and acts as an additional barrier against exterior forces. The cell wall acts to protect the cell mechanically and chemically from its environment, and is an additional layer of protection to the cell membrane. It also prevents the cell from expanding and bursting ([cytolysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytolysis "Cytolysis")) from [osmotic pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osmotic_pressure "Osmotic pressure") due to a [hypotonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypotonic "Hypotonic") environment.

The [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA") of a bacterium typically consists of a single [circular chromosome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circular_chromosome "Circular chromosome") that is in direct contact with the [cytoplasm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytoplasm "Cytoplasm") in a region called the [nucleoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleoid "Nucleoid"). Some bacteria contain multiple circular or even [linear chromosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_chromosome "Linear chromosome"). The cytoplasm also contains [ribosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribosome#Prokaryotic_ribosomes "Ribosome") and various inclusions where [transcription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacterial_transcription "Bacterial transcription") takes place alongside [translation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacterial_translation "Bacterial translation"). [Extrachromosomal DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrachromosomal_DNA#Prokaryotic "Extrachromosomal DNA") as [plasmids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasmid "Plasmid"), are usually circular and encode additional genes, such as those of [antibiotic resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibiotic_resistance "Antibiotic resistance"). Linear bacterial plasmids have been identified in several species of [spirochete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirochete "Spirochete") bacteria, including species of *[Borrelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borrelia "Borrelia")* which causes [Lyme disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyme_disease "Lyme disease"). The [prokaryotic cytoskeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prokaryotic_cytoskeleton "Prokaryotic cytoskeleton") in bacteria is involved in the maintenance of cell shape, [polarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_polarity "Cell polarity") and cytokinesis.

[Compartmentalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_compartment "Cellular compartment") is a key feature of eukaryotic cells but some species of bacteria, have protein-based organelle-like [microcompartments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacterial_microcompartment "Bacterial microcompartment") such as [gas vesicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_vesicle "Gas vesicle"), and [carboxysomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carboxysome "Carboxysome"), and [encapsulin nanocompartments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encapsulin_nanocompartment "Encapsulin nanocompartment"). Certain membrane-bound [prokaryotic organelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prokaryotic_organelle "Prokaryotic organelle") have also been discovered. They include the [magnetosome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetosome "Magnetosome") of [magnetotactic bacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetotactic_bacteria "Magnetotactic bacteria"), and the [anammoxosome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anammoxosome "Anammoxosome") of [anammox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anammox "Anammox") bacteria.

[Cell-surface appendages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell-surface_appendage "Cell-surface appendage") can include [flagella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacterial_flagella "Bacterial flagella"), and [pili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilus "Pilus"), protein structures that facilitate movement and communication between cells. The flagellum stretches from the cytoplasm through the cell membrane and extrudes through the cell wall. [Fimbriae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fimbria_(bacteriology) "Fimbria (bacteriology)") are short attachment pili, the other type of pilus is the longer [conjugative type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacterial_conjugation "Bacterial conjugation"). Fimbriae are formed of an [antigenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigenic "Antigenic") protein called [pilin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilin "Pilin"), and are responsible for the attachment of bacteria to specific receptors on host cells.

#### Archaea

Archaea are enclosed in a cell envelope consisting of a plasma membrane and a cell wall.
An exception to this is the *[Thermoplasma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermoplasma "Thermoplasma")* that only has the cell membrane. The cell membranes of archaea are unique, consisting of [ether-linked lipids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ether_lipid "Ether lipid"). The [prokaryotic cytoskeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prokaryotic_cytoskeleton "Prokaryotic cytoskeleton") has homologues of eukaryotic [actin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actin "Actin") and [tubulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tubulin "Tubulin"). A unique form of metabolism in the archaean is [methanogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methanogenesis "Methanogenesis"). Their [cell-surface appendage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell-surface_appendage "Cell-surface appendage") equivalent of the flagella is the differently structured and unique [archaellum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaellum "Archaellum"). The DNA is contained in a circular chromosome in direct contact with the cytoplasm, in a region known as the nucleoid. Ribosomes are also found freely in the cytoplasm, or attached to the cell membrane where DNA processing takes place.

The archaea are noted for their [extremophile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremophile "Extremophile") species, and many are selectively evolved to thrive in conditions of extreme heat, cold, acidity, alkalinity, or high salt. There are no known archaean pathogens.

### Eukaryotes

A eukaryotic cell can be 2 to 100 times larger in diameter than a typical prokaryotic cell. Eukaryotes can be single-celled, as in [diatoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diatom "Diatom") ([microscopic algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microalgae "Microalgae")), or [microfungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microfungi "Microfungi") such as [yeasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeast "Yeast"), or multicellular, as in [animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal "Animal"), [plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant "Plant"), most [fungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fungi "Fungi"), and [seaweed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaweed "Seaweed") (macroalgae). [Multicellular organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multicellular_organism "Multicellular organism") are made up of many different [types of cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_types "Cell types") known overall as [somatic cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somatic_cell "Somatic cell"). Eukaryotes are distinguished by the presence of a membrane-bound [nucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_nucleus "Cell nucleus") that gives the eukaryote its name, which means "true nut" or "true kernel", where "nut" means the nucleus.

The nucleus is the largest [membrane-bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organelle#Types "Organelle") [organelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organelle "Organelle") in the eukaryotic cell. Other organelles present in all eukaryotic cells are the [endoplasmic reticulum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoplasmic_reticulum "Endoplasmic reticulum"), [ribosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribosome "Ribosome"), the [Golgi apparatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golgi_apparatus "Golgi apparatus"), [mitochondria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondria "Mitochondria"), [lysosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysosome "Lysosome"), [peroxisomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peroxisome "Peroxisome"), [endosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endosome "Endosome"), and vesicles. The contents of the cell are contained within a [cell membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_membrane "Cell membrane"), and together with all the membranes of the organelles (except the mitochondria) are known as the [endomembrane system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endomembrane_system "Endomembrane system"). All of these membranes are involved in the [secretory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretion "Secretion") and [endocytic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocytosis "Endocytosis") pathways, modifying, packaging, and transporting proteins and lipids to and from the [trans Golgi network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans_Golgi_network "Trans Golgi network"). In [mammalian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammalian "Mammalian") cells, endocytosis includes [early](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_endosome "Early endosome"), [late](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_endosome "Late endosome"), and [recycling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recycling_endosome "Recycling endosome") endosomes.

Some eukaryotes cells including [plant cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_cell "Plant cell") and [fungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fungi "Fungi") have a [cell wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_wall "Cell wall"). [Plastids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastid "Plastid") including [chloroplasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloroplast "Chloroplast") feature mainly in plant cells. Most cell types include [vaults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vault_(organelle) "Vault (organelle)"). There are many cell variations among the different eukaryote groups. Many cells project one or more [cellular extensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_extensions "Cellular extensions"). And some organelles also project extensions such as [stromules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stromule "Stromule") from plastids in plant cells, peroxules from [peroxisomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peroxisome "Peroxisome"), and matrixules from [mitochondria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondria "Mitochondria").

Most distinct cell types arise from a single [totipotent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totipotent "Totipotent") cell, called a [zygote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zygote "Zygote"), that [differentiates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_differentiation "Cellular differentiation") into hundreds of different cell types during the course of [development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_(biology) "Development (biology)"). Differentiation of cells is driven by different environmental cues (such as cell–cell interaction) and intrinsic differences (such as those caused by the uneven distribution of [molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecule "Molecule") during [division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_division "Cell division")).

Eukaryotic cell types include those that make up [animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal "Animal"), [plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant "Plant"), [fungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fungi "Fungi"), [algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae "Algae"), and [protists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protist "Protist"). All of which have many different species and cell differences. A separate grouping of animals and fungi are known as [opisthokonts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opisthokont "Opisthokont").

#### Structure

A eukaryotic cell has a [cell membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_membrane "Cell membrane") that surrounds a gel-like [cytoplasm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytoplasm "Cytoplasm"). The cytoplasm contains the [cytoskeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytoskeleton "Cytoskeleton"), the cell nucleus, the endoplasmic reticulum, ribosomes, the Golgi apparatus, mitochondria, [peroxisomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peroxisome "Peroxisome"), [endosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endosome "Endosome"), vacuoles and vesicles, and [vaults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vault_(organelle) "Vault (organelle)"). A eukaryotic cell structure, includes an [endomembrane system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endomembrane_system "Endomembrane system") encompassing the cell membrane, and all the membranes of the organelles excluding those of the mitochondria. The whole system cooperates in the modification, packaging, and transport of proteins and lipids.

##### Cell membrane

The [cell membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_membrane "Cell membrane"), or plasma membrane, is a [selectively permeable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selectively_permeable "Selectively permeable") [membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_membrane "Biological membrane") as an outer boundary of the cell that encloses the cytoplasm. Underlying, and attached to the cell membrane is the [cell cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_cortex "Cell cortex"), the outermost part of the actin [cytoskeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytoskeleton "Cytoskeleton"). The membrane serves to separate and protect a cell from its surrounding environment and is made mostly from a [lipid bilayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipid_bilayer "Lipid bilayer") of [phospholipids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phospholipid "Phospholipid"), which are [amphiphilic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphiphilic "Amphiphilic") (partly [hydrophobic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrophobic "Hydrophobic") and partly [hydrophilic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrophilic "Hydrophilic")). It has been best described in the [fluid mosaic model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid_mosaic_model "Fluid mosaic model").

Embedded within the cell membrane are [secretory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretory "Secretory") [macromolecular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macromolecular_assembly "Macromolecular assembly") [lipoprotein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipoprotein "Lipoprotein") structures called [porosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porosome "Porosome"); and a number of different channels and pumps involved in [actively transporting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_transport "Active transport") molecules into and out of the cell. The membrane is semi-permeable, and selectively permeable, in that it can either let a substance ([molecule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecule "Molecule") or [ion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion "Ion")) pass through freely, to a limited extent or not at all. [Cell surface receptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_surface_receptor "Cell surface receptor") in the membrane allow cells to detect external [signaling molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signaling_molecule "Signaling molecule") such as [hormones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormone "Hormone").

##### Cytoplasm

The cell membrane encloses the [cytoplasm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytoplasm "Cytoplasm") of the cell that surrounds all of the cell's organelles. It is made up of two main components, the [cytoskeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytoskeleton "Cytoskeleton") made up of [protein filaments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_filament "Protein filament"), and the [cytosol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytosol "Cytosol"). The network of filaments and microtubules of the cytoskeleton gives shape and support to the cell, and has a part in organising the cell components.

The cytosol is a gel-like substance made up of water, ions, and non-essential biomolecules, and is the main site of [protein synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_synthesis "Protein synthesis"), and [degradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_degradation "Protein degradation"). The acidity (pH) of the cytosol is near neutral, and is regulated by transporters in the cell membrane. Different proteins in the cytoplasm operate optimally at different pHs. The cytosol forms 30%–50% of the cell's volume.

##### Cytoskeleton

The [cytoskeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytoskeleton "Cytoskeleton") acts to organize and maintain the cell's shape; anchors organelles in place; helps during [endocytosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocytosis "Endocytosis"), and in the uptake of external materials by a cell. The cytoskeleton is composed of [microtubules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microtubule "Microtubule"), [intermediate filaments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermediate_filament "Intermediate filament") and [microfilaments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microfilament "Microfilament"). There are a great number of proteins associated with them, each controlling a cell's structure by directing, bundling, and aligning filaments. The outermost part of the cytoskeleton is the [cell cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_cortex "Cell cortex"), or [actin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actin "Actin") cortex, a thin layer of cross-linked [actomyosins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actomyosin "Actomyosin"). Its thickness varies with cell type and physiology. It directs the transport through the [ER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoplasmic_reticulum "Endoplasmic reticulum") and the [Golgi apparatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golgi_apparatus "Golgi apparatus"). The cytoskeleton in the animal cell also plays a part in [cytokinesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytokinesis "Cytokinesis"), in the formation of the [spindle apparatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spindle_apparatus "Spindle apparatus") during [cell division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_division "Cell division"), the separation of daughter cells.

##### Organelles

[Organelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organelle "Organelle") are [compartments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_compartment "Cellular compartment") of the cell that are specialized for carrying out one or more functions, analogous to the [organs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_(biology) "Organ (biology)"), such as the heart, and lungs. There are several types of organelles held in the cytoplasm. Most organelles are membrane-bound, and vary in size and number based on the growth of the host cell. Organelles include the [nucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_nucleus "Cell nucleus"), [mitochondria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondria "Mitochondria"), [endoplasmic reticulum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoplasmic_reticulum "Endoplasmic reticulum"), [Golgi apparatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golgi_apparatus "Golgi apparatus"), [lysosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysosome "Lysosome"), [peroxisomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peroxisome "Peroxisome"), [vesicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesicle_(biology_and_chemistry) "Vesicle (biology and chemistry)"), and [vacuoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuole "Vacuole"). [Membrane-less organelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane-less_organelle "Membrane-less organelle") include the [nucleolus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleolus "Nucleolus") in the nucleus, [centrosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrosome "Centrosome"), [ribosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribosome "Ribosome"), [proteasomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proteasome "Proteasome"), and [vaults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vault_(organelle) "Vault (organelle)").

##### Nucleus

The [cell nucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_nucleus "Cell nucleus") is the largest organelle in the animal cells and many other eukaryotic cells. It houses the cell's [chromosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromosomes "Chromosomes"), and is the place where almost all [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA") replication and [RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA "RNA") synthesis ([transcription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eukaryotic_transcription "Eukaryotic transcription")) occur. The nucleus is spherical and separated from the cytoplasm by a double-membraned [nuclear envelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_envelope "Nuclear envelope"). A space between the membranes is called the perinuclear space. The nuclear envelope isolates and protects a cell's DNA from various molecules that could accidentally damage its structure or interfere with its processing. During processing, DNA is [transcribed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcription_(genetics) "Transcription (genetics)"), or copied into a special RNA, called [messenger RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messenger_RNA "Messenger RNA") (mRNA). This mRNA is then transported out of the nucleus, where it is translated into a specific protein molecule. The [nucleolus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleolus "Nucleolus") is a specialized [biomolecular condensate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomolecular_condensate "Biomolecular condensate") within the nucleus where ribosome subunits are assembled. It is one of several types of membrane-less [nuclear bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_bodies "Nuclear bodies"). Cells use DNA for their long-term information storage that is [encoded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_code "Genetic code") in its [DNA sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_sequence "DNA sequence"). RNA is used for information transport (e.g., [mRNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRNA "MRNA")) and [enzymatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzyme "Enzyme") functions (e.g., [ribosomal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribosome "Ribosome") RNA). [Transfer RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_RNA "Transfer RNA") (tRNA) molecules are used to add amino acids during protein [translation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translation_(biology) "Translation (biology)").

The [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA") of each cell is its genetic material, and is organized in multiple linear molecules, called [chromosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromosome "Chromosome"), that are coiled around [histone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histone "Histone") [proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein") and housed in the [cell nucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_nucleus "Cell nucleus"). In humans, the nuclear genome is divided into 46 linear chromosomes, including 22 [homologous chromosome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homologous_chromosome "Homologous chromosome") pairs and a pair of [sex chromosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_chromosomes "Sex chromosomes").
The nucleus is a [membrane-bound organelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane-bound_organelle "Membrane-bound organelle").

##### Endoplasmic reticulum

The [endoplasmic reticulum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoplasmic_reticulum "Endoplasmic reticulum") (ER) is a transport network for molecules targeted for certain modifications and specific destinations, as compared to molecules that float freely in the cytoplasm. The ER has two forms: the [rough endoplasmic reticulum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rough_endoplasmic_reticulum "Rough endoplasmic reticulum") (RER), which has [ribosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribosome "Ribosome") on its surface that secrete proteins into the ER, and the [smooth endoplasmic reticulum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_endoplasmic_reticulum "Smooth endoplasmic reticulum") (SER), which lacks ribosomes. The smooth ER plays a role in calcium sequestration and release, and helps in synthesis of [lipid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipid "Lipid").

##### Golgi apparatus

The [Golgi apparatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golgi_apparatus "Golgi apparatus") processes and packages [proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein"), and [lipids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipid "Lipid"), that are synthesized by the cell. It is organized as a stack of plate-like structures known as [cisternae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisternae "Cisternae").

##### Mitochondria

[Mitochondria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondria "Mitochondria") are self-replicating double membrane-bound organelles that occur in various numbers, shapes, and sizes in the cytoplasm of the cell. [Aerobic respiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerobic_respiration "Aerobic respiration") in the mitochondria generates the cell's energy by [oxidative phosphorylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidative_phosphorylation "Oxidative phosphorylation"), using [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen "Oxygen") to release energy stored in cellular nutrients (typically pertaining to [glucose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glucose "Glucose")) to generate [adenosine triphosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adenosine_triphosphate "Adenosine triphosphate") (ATP). Mitochondria are descended from bacteria that formed an [endosymbiotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endosymbiont "Endosymbiont") relationship with ancient prokaryotes. Mitochondria multiply by [binary fission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_fission "Binary fission") and have their own DNA contained in multiple small circular chromosomes. The mitochondrial DNA ([mtDNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MtDNA "MtDNA")) is very small compared to [nuclear DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_DNA "Nuclear DNA"), but it codes for 13 proteins involved in mitochondrial energy production and specific [transfer RNAs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_RNA "Transfer RNA") (tRNAs). Mitochondria also have their own ribosomes known as [mitoribosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitoribosome "Mitoribosome").

##### Lysosomes

A [lysosome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysosome "Lysosome") is the most acidic compartment in the cell. It contains over 60 different [hydrolytic enzymes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrolase "Hydrolase") that need an acidic environment. They digest excess or worn-out organelles, food particles, and engulfed [viruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virus "Virus") or [bacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteria "Bacteria"). The cell could not house these destructive enzymes if they were not contained in a membrane-bound compartment.

##### Peroxisomes

[Peroxisomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peroxisome "Peroxisome") are [microbodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbodies "Microbodies") bounded by a single membrane. A peroxisome has no DNA or ribosomes and the proteins that it needs are encoded in the nucleus, and selectively imported from the cytosol. Some proteins enter via the endomembrane reticulum. They have enzymes that rid the cell of toxic [peroxides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peroxide "Peroxide"). The enzymatic content of the peroxisomes varies widely across the species, as it can in an individual organism. The peroxisomes in animal cells are concentrated in the [liver cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liver_cell "Liver cell") and [adipocytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adipocyte "Adipocyte").

##### Vacuoles

[Vacuoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuole "Vacuole") sequester waste products. Some cells, most notably *[Amoeba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amoeba_(genus) "Amoeba (genus)")*, have contractile vacuoles, which can pump water out of the cell if there is too much water.

##### Ribosomes

A [ribosome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribosome "Ribosome") is a large complex of [RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA "RNA") and [protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein") molecules considered as a [membrane-less organelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane-less_organelle "Membrane-less organelle") when freely found in the cytosol, or as a [membrane-bound organelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organelle#Types "Organelle") when located in the [rough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rough_endoplasmic_reticulum "Rough endoplasmic reticulum") type of [endoplasmic reticulum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoplasmic_reticulum "Endoplasmic reticulum"). They each consist of two subunits, one larger than the other, and act as an assembly line where [messenger RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messenger_RNA "Messenger RNA") from the nucleus is used to [synthesise proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_biosynthesis "Protein biosynthesis") from amino acids.

##### Vaults

A [vault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vault_(organelle) "Vault (organelle)") is a large ribonuclear protein particle, a [membrane-less organelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane-less_organelle "Membrane-less organelle"), three times the size of a ribosome but with only three proteins in contrast to the near hundred in the ribosome. Most human cells have around 10,000 vaults, and in some types of [immune cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immune_cell "Immune cell") there may be up to 100,000. [Macrophages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrophage "Macrophage") have the greatest number of vaults of any human cell. Vaults are largely overlooked because their functions are purely speculative. They may play a role in transport from the nucleus to the cytoplasm, and may serve as scaffolds for signal transduction proteins. They are present in normal tissues, and more so in secretory and excretory epithelial cells.

##### Proteasome

[Proteasomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proteasome "Proteasome") are intricate membrane-less protein complexes responsible for the degradation of regulatory proteins, and damaged proteins in the cytosol and nucleus. They also function in [protein homeostasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proteostasis "Proteostasis"), [stress response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_stress_response "Cellular stress response"), and in the control of cell division and signal transduction.

### Animal cells

All the cells in an animal body develop from one [totipotent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totipotent "Totipotent") [diploid cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diploid_cell "Diploid cell") called a [zygote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zygote "Zygote"). During the [embryonic development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_embryonic_development "Animal embryonic development") of an [animal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal "Animal"), the cells [differentiate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_differentiation "Cellular differentiation") into the specialised [tissues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_tissue "Animal tissue") and [organs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organogenesis "Organogenesis") of the organism. Different groups of cells differentiate from the [germ layers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germ_layer "Germ layer"). The [sponge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sponge "Sponge") has only one layer. Some other animals known as [diploblasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diploblast "Diploblast") have two germ layers the [ectoderm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ectoderm "Ectoderm"), and the [endoderm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoderm "Endoderm"). More advanced animals have an extra layer, the middle [mesodermal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoderm "Mesoderm") layer, and are known as [triploblastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triploblast "Triploblast"). Triploblastic animals make up the large [clade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clade "Clade") of [Bilateria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilateria "Bilateria"). Differentiation results in structural or functional changes to [stem cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stem_cell "Stem cell"), and [progenitor cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progenitor_cell "Progenitor cell").

The ectoderm gives rise to several different types of [epithelial tissues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epithelial_tissues "Epithelial tissues") including the [skin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin "Skin"), and [glands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gland "Gland"), and to the [nervous tissue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nervous_tissue "Nervous tissue"). Epithelium as [mesothelium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesothelium "Mesothelium") forms the lining of many organs, and [inner cavities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_cavity "Body cavity"). Epithelial cells are joined in sheets by way of [cell junctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_junction "Cell junction"); [adherens junctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adherens_junction "Adherens junction"), and [desmosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desmosome "Desmosome") bind the cells together, and [hemidesmosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemidesmosome "Hemidesmosome") bind the cells to the [basement membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basement_membrane "Basement membrane"). All three types are linked to the cell cytoskeleton.

There are an estimated 200 different [cell types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_type "Cell type") in the human body. The estimated [cell count](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_count "Cell count") in a typical adult human body is around 30 trillion cells, 36 trillion in an adult male, and 28 trillion in a female.

#### Structure

Like other eukaryotic cells, animal cells are made up of a cell membrane containing a gel like cytoplasm. In this cytoplasm are a variety of membrane organelles, including all the shared eukaryotic organelles: the cell nucleus, the endoplasmic reticulum, ribosomes, the Golgi apparatus, mitochondria, peroxisomes, endosomes, vacuoles and vesicles, and vaults; as well and [centrosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrosome "Centrosome"), which are unique to animal cells.

##### Centrosome

The [centrosome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrosome "Centrosome") is a membrane-less organelle composed of [pericentriolar material](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pericentriolar_material "Pericentriolar material") and the two [centrioles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centriole "Centriole"). The centrosome is the main [microtubule organizing center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microtubule_organizing_center "Microtubule organizing center") in the animal cell that produces the [microtubules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microtubules "Microtubules") key components of the cytoskeleton. Centrosomes are composed of two [centrioles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrioles "Centrioles") which lie perpendicular to each other in which each has an organization like a [cartwheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cart_wheel "Cart wheel"), which separate during [cell division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_division "Cell division") and help in the formation of the [mitotic spindle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitotic_spindle "Mitotic spindle"). While most other eukaryotes do have centrioles, plants do not and centrosomes are unique to animal cells.

#### Animal cell types

Some types of specialized cell are localized to a particular animal group. [Vertebrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebrate "Vertebrate") for example have specialized, structurally changed cells including [muscle cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscle_cell "Muscle cell"). The cell membrane of a [skeletal muscle cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeletal_muscle_cell "Skeletal muscle cell") or of a [cardiac muscle cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiac_muscle_cell "Cardiac muscle cell") is termed the [sarcolemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarcolemma "Sarcolemma"). And the cytoplasm is termed the [sarcoplasm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarcoplasm "Sarcoplasm"). Skeletal muscle cells also become [multinucleated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinucleated "Multinucleated"). Populations of animal groups [evolve to become distinct species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speciation "Speciation"), where sexual reproduction is isolated. The many [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species "Species") of vertebrates for example have other unique characteristics by way of additional specialized cells. In some species of [electric fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_fish "Electric fish") for example modified muscle cells or nerve cells have specialized to become [electerocytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electroreception_and_electrogenesis "Electroreception and electrogenesis") capable of creating and storing electrical energy for future release, as in stunning prey, or use in [electrolocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrolocation "Electrolocation"). These are large flat cells in the [electric eel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_eel "Electric eel"), and [electric ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_ray "Electric ray") in which thousands are stacked into an [electric organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_organ_(fish) "Electric organ (fish)") comparable to a [voltaic pile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltaic_pile "Voltaic pile").

Many animal cells are [ciliated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilium "Cilium") and most cells except red blood cells have [primary cilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_cilia "Primary cilia"). Primary cilia play important roles in [chemosensation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemoreceptor "Chemoreceptor") and [mechanosensation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanosensation "Mechanosensation").
Each cilium may be "viewed as a sensory cellular [antennae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antenna_(biology) "Antenna (biology)") that coordinates a large number of cellular signaling pathways, sometimes coupling the signaling to ciliary motility or alternatively to cell division and differentiation." The cilia in other cells are motile organelles, and in the [respiratory epithelium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_epithelium "Respiratory epithelium") play an important role in the [movement of mucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mucociliary_clearance "Mucociliary clearance"). In the reproductive system ciliated epithelium in the [fallopian tubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallopian_tube "Fallopian tube") move the egg from the uterus to the ovary. Motile cilia also known as flagella, drive the sperm cells. [Invertebrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invertebrate "Invertebrate") [planarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planarian "Planarian") have ciliated excretory [flame cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flame_cell "Flame cell"). Other excretory cells also found in planarians are [solenocytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solenocyte "Solenocyte") that are long and flagellated.

### Plant cells

Plant cells have [cell walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_wall "Cell wall") composed of [cellulose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellulose "Cellulose"), [hemicelluloses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemicellulose "Hemicellulose"), and [pectin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pectin "Pectin") that are constructed outside the [cell membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_membrane "Cell membrane"). In [sclerenchyma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sclerenchyma "Sclerenchyma") tissue [lignin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lignin "Lignin") is secreted to form a secondary wall inside the primary cell wall. [Cutin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutin "Cutin") is secreted outside the primary cell wall and into the outer layers of the secondary cell wall of the epidermal cells of leaves, stems and other above-ground organs to form the [plant cuticle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_cuticle "Plant cuticle").[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] Cell walls perform many essential functions, they provide shape to form the tissue and organs of the plant, and play an important role in intercellular communication and plant-microbe interactions.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] Specialized cell-to-cell communication pathways known as [plasmodesmata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasmodesmata "Plasmodesmata"), occur in the form of pores in the primary cell wall through which the cell membrane and [endoplasmic reticulum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoplasmic_reticulum "Endoplasmic reticulum") of adjacent cells are continuous.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Organelles in [plant cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_cell "Plant cell"), include [pigment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_pigment#Pigments_in_plants "Biological pigment")-containing [plastids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastid "Plastid"), especially [chloroplasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloroplast "Chloroplast") that contain [chlorophyll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorophyll "Chlorophyll") (also found in [algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae "Algae")), large water-storage [vacuoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuole "Vacuole"), and two types of peroxisome. Chloroplasts capture the sun's energy to make carbohydrates through [photosynthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photosynthesis "Photosynthesis"). [Chromoplasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromoplast "Chromoplast") contain fat-soluble [carotenoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carotenoid "Carotenoid") pigments such as orange [carotene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carotene "Carotene") and yellow [xanthophylls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xanthophyll "Xanthophyll") which helps in synthesis and storage. [Leucoplasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leucoplast "Leucoplast") are non-pigmented plastids and helps in storage of nutrients. Plastids divide by binary fission. The vacuoles are larger than those in animal cells, and their membrane transports ions against concentration gradients.
One type of peroxisome is in the leaves where it takes part in [photorespiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photorespiration "Photorespiration"). The other type is in germinating seeds where it takes part in the conversion of fatty acids into sugars for the plant's growth. In this peroxisome type the enzymatic content is so different from other groups that it has an alternative name of [glyoxysome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyoxysome "Glyoxysome"). The enzymes are of the [glyoxylate cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyoxylate_cycle "Glyoxylate cycle").

The plant cytoskeleton is a dynamic structure that has a scaffold of microtubules and microfilaments, but no intermediate filaments. The [microtubule organizing center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microtubule_organizing_center "Microtubule organizing center") in plant cells is often sited underneath the cell membrane where nucleated microtubules often form sheet-like semi-parallel arrays.

[Plant hormones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_hormones "Plant hormones") are produced by all plant cells. [Different hormones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_hormone#Classes "Plant hormone") act as [signaling molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signaling_molecules "Signaling molecules") to control all aspects of the plant's growth and development including embryogenesis and reproduction, and in pathogen defense.

### Algal cells

[Algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae "Algae") members are [photoautotrophs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photoautotroph "Photoautotroph") able to use [photosynthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photosynthesis "Photosynthesis") to produce energy. In eukaryotes, photosynthesis is made possible by the use of [plastids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastid "Plastid"), organelles in the cytoplasm known as [chloroplasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloroplast "Chloroplast").

Photosynthesis is found in both prokaryotic [cyanobacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanobacteria "Cyanobacteria") and multiple [clades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clade "Clade") of [eukaryotic algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eukaryotic_algae "Eukaryotic algae"); [red algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_algae "Red algae"), [brown algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_algae "Brown algae"), and [green algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_algae "Green algae"), which are closely related to [land plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryophyte "Embryophyte").

[Alginate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alginate "Alginate") is a [polysaccharide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polysaccharide "Polysaccharide") found in the matrix of the cell walls of [brown algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_algae "Brown algae"), and has many important uses in the food industry, and in [pharmacology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacology "Pharmacology").

### Fungal cells

The cells of [fungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fungi "Fungi") have in addition to the shared eukaryotic organelles a [spitzenkörper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spitzenk%C3%B6rper "Spitzenkörper") in their endomembrane system, associated with [hyphal tip growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypha "Hypha"). It is a [phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_contrast "Phase contrast")-dark body that is composed of an aggregation of membrane-bound vesicles containing cell wall components, serving as a point of assemblage and release of such components intermediate between the Golgi and the cell membrane. The spitzenkörper is motile and generates new hyphal tip growth as it moves forward.

The cell walls of fungi are uniquely made of a [chitin-glucan complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitin-glucan_complex "Chitin-glucan complex"). [Chloroplasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloroplast "Chloroplast") are not found in fungal cells, their pigments are instead associated with their cell walls.

### Protist cells

The cells of [protists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protist "Protist") may be bounded only by a [cell membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_membrane "Cell membrane"), or may in addition have a [cell wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_wall "Cell wall"), or may be covered by a [pellicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pellicle_(biology) "Pellicle (biology)") (in [ciliates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciliates "Ciliates")), a [test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_(biology) "Test (biology)") (in [testate amoebae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testate_amoebae "Testate amoebae")), or a [frustule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frustule "Frustule") (in [diatoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diatom "Diatom")).

Some protists such as [amoebae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amoebae "Amoebae") may [feed on other organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterotroph "Heterotroph") and ingest food by [phagocytosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phagocytosis "Phagocytosis"). Vacuoles known as [phagosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phagosome "Phagosome") in the cytoplasm may be used to draw in and incorporate the captured particles. Other types of protists are [photoautotrophs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photoautotroph "Photoautotroph"), providing themselves with energy by photosynthesis. Most single-celled protists are motile, and [generate movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protist_locomotion "Protist locomotion") with cilia, flagella, or [pseudopodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudopodia "Pseudopodia").

[Ciliates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciliate "Ciliate") have two different sorts of [nuclei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_nucleus "Cell nucleus"): a tiny, [diploid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diploid "Diploid") [micronucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronucleus "Micronucleus") (the "generative nucleus", which carries the [germline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germline "Germline") of the cell), and a large, [ampliploid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampliploid "Ampliploid") [macronucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macronucleus "Macronucleus") (the "vegetative nucleus", which takes care of general cell regulation.

## Cellular processes

### Replication

During [cell division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_division "Cell division"), a single cell, the *mother cell* divides into two daughter cells. This leads to the growth of [tissue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tissue_(biology) "Tissue (biology)") in [multicellular organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multicellular_organism "Multicellular organism"). [Prokaryotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prokaryote "Prokaryote") cells divide by [binary fission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_fission "Binary fission"), while [eukaryotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eukaryote "Eukaryote") cells usually undergo a process of nuclear division, called [mitosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitosis "Mitosis"), followed by division of the cell, called [cytokinesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytokinesis "Cytokinesis"). A [diploid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diploid "Diploid") cell may undergo [meiosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiosis "Meiosis") to produce haploid cells, usually four. [Haploid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haploid "Haploid") cells serve as [gametes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamete "Gamete") in multicellular organisms, fusing to form new diploid cells.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

[DNA replication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_replication "DNA replication"), or the process of duplicating a cell's genome, always happens when a cell divides through mitosis or binary fission.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] This occurs during the [S (synthesis) phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S_phase "S phase") of the [cell cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_cycle "Cell cycle").

In meiosis, the DNA is replicated only once, while the cell divides twice. DNA replication only occurs before [meiosis I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiosis_I "Meiosis I"). DNA replication does not occur when the cells divide the second time, in [meiosis II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiosis_II "Meiosis II"). Replication, like all cellular activities, requires specialized proteins.

### Signaling

Cell signaling is the process by which a cell interacts with itself, other cells, and the environment. Typically, the signaling process involves three components: the first messenger (the [ligand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligand#Biochemnistry "Ligand")), the [receptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Receptor_(biochemistry) "Receptor (biochemistry)"), and the signal itself. Most cell signaling is chemical in nature, and can occur with neighboring cells or more distant targets. Signal receptors are complex proteins or tightly bound multimer of proteins, [located in the plasma membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_surface_receptor "Cell surface receptor") or [within the interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intracellular_receptor "Intracellular receptor").

Each cell is programmed to respond to specific extracellular signal molecules, and this process is the basis of [development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developmental_biology "Developmental biology"), [tissue repair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tissue_repair "Tissue repair"), [immunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunity_(medicine) "Immunity (medicine)"), and [homeostasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeostasis "Homeostasis"). Individual cells are able to manage receptor sensitivity including turning them off, and receptors can become less sensitive when they are occupied for long durations. Errors in signaling interactions may cause diseases such as [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer "Cancer"), [autoimmunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoimmunity "Autoimmunity"), and [diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes "Diabetes").

### Protein targeting

[Protein targeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_targeting "Protein targeting") or protein sorting is the [biological mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanism_(biology) "Mechanism (biology)") by which [proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein") are transported to their appropriate destinations within or outside the cell.
Proteins can be targeted to the inner space of an [organelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organelle "Organelle"), different intracellular [membranes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_membrane "Biological membrane"), the [plasma membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_membrane "Cell membrane"), or to the exterior of the cell via [secretion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretion "Secretion"). Information contained in the protein itself directs this delivery process. Correct sorting is crucial for the cell; errors or dysfunction in sorting have been linked to multiple diseases.

### DNA repair

All cells contain enzyme systems that scan for [DNA damage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_damage_(naturally_occurring) "DNA damage (naturally occurring)") and carry out [repair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_repair "DNA repair"). Diverse repair processes have evolved in all organisms. Repair is vital to maintain DNA integrity, avoid cell death and errors of replication that could lead to [mutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutation "Mutation"). Repair processes include [nucleotide excision repair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleotide_excision_repair "Nucleotide excision repair"), [DNA mismatch repair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_mismatch_repair "DNA mismatch repair"), [non-homologous end joining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-homologous_end_joining "Non-homologous end joining") of double-strand breaks, [recombinational repair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homologous_recombination "Homologous recombination") and light-dependent repair ([photoreactivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photolyase "Photolyase")).

### Growth and metabolism

Between successive cell divisions, cells grow through the functioning of cellular metabolism. Cell metabolism is the process by which individual cells process nutrient molecules. Metabolism has two distinct divisions: [catabolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catabolism "Catabolism"), in which the cell breaks down complex molecules to produce energy and [reducing power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reducing_agent "Reducing agent"), and [anabolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anabolism "Anabolism"), in which the cell uses energy and reducing power to construct complex molecules and perform other biological functions.

Complex sugars can be broken down into simpler sugar molecules called [monosaccharides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monosaccharides "Monosaccharides") such as [glucose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glucose "Glucose"). Once inside the cell, glucose is broken down to make adenosine triphosphate ([ATP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adenosine_triphosphate "Adenosine triphosphate")), a molecule that possesses readily available energy, through two different pathways. In plant cells, [chloroplasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloroplast "Chloroplast") create sugars by [photosynthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photosynthesis "Photosynthesis"), using the energy of light to join molecules of water and [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide "Carbon dioxide").

### Protein synthesis

Cells are capable of [synthesizing new proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_biosynthesis "Protein biosynthesis"), which are essential for the modulation and maintenance of cellular activities. This process involves the formation of new protein molecules from [amino acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amino_acid "Amino acid") building blocks based on information encoded in DNA/RNA. Protein synthesis generally consists of two major steps: [transcription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcription_(biology) "Transcription (biology)") and [translation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translation_(biology) "Translation (biology)").

Transcription is the process where genetic information in DNA is used to produce a complementary RNA strand. This RNA strand is then processed to give [messenger RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messenger_RNA "Messenger RNA") (mRNA), which is free to migrate into the cytoplasm. mRNA molecules bind to protein-RNA complexes called [ribosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribosome "Ribosome") located in the [cytosol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytosol "Cytosol"), where they are translated into polypeptide sequences. The ribosome mediates the formation of a polypeptide sequence based on the mRNA sequence. The mRNA sequence directly relates to the polypeptide sequence by binding to [transfer RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_RNA "Transfer RNA") (tRNA) adapter molecules in binding pockets within the ribosome. The new polypeptide chain then [folds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_folding "Protein folding") into a functional [three-dimensional protein molecule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_structure "Tertiary structure").

### Motility

Unicellular organisms can move in order to find food or escape predators. Common mechanisms of motion include [flagella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagella "Flagella") and [cilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilia "Cilia"), and the projection of [pseudopodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudopodia "Pseudopodia") in [amoeboid movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amoeboid_movement "Amoeboid movement"). Cells in multicellular organisms can move during processes such as [wound healing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wound_healing "Wound healing"), the immune response, and [cancer metastasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_metastasis "Cancer metastasis"). In wound healing in animals, [white blood cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_blood_cell "White blood cell") move to the wound site to kill the [pathogens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathogen "Pathogen") causing infection. Cell motility involves many receptors, crosslinking, bundling, binding, adhesion, motor and other proteins. The process is divided into three steps: protrusion of the leading edge of the cell, adhesion of the leading edge and de-adhesion at the cell body and rear, and cytoskeletal contraction to pull the cell forward. Each step is driven by physical forces generated by unique segments of the cytoskeleton.

#### Navigation, control and communication

In August 2020, scientists described one way cells—in particular cells of a slime mold and mouse pancreatic cancer-derived cells—are able to [navigate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemotaxis "Chemotaxis") efficiently through a body and identify the best routes through complex mazes: generating gradients after breaking down diffused [chemoattractants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemoattractant "Chemoattractant") which enable them to sense upcoming maze junctions before reaching them, including around corners.

### Cell death

[Cell death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_death "Cell death") occurs when a cell ceases to carry out its functions, as a result of ageing, or types of [cell injury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_injury "Cell injury") ([necrosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necrosis "Necrosis")). [Programmed cell death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programmed_cell_death "Programmed cell death"), including [apoptosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apoptosis "Apoptosis"), and [autophagy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autophagy "Autophagy") is a natural process of replacing dead cells with new ones.

A separate mode of cellular death is known as a [mitotic catastrophe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitotic_catastrophe#Mechanism_of_cellular_death "Mitotic catastrophe"), which occurs during [mitosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitosis "Mitosis"), following the improper progression of, or entrance to the [cell cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_cycle "Cell cycle"). This mechanism operates to prevent genomic instability. Other cell death pathways are described, and include [anoikis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anoikis "Anoikis"), [pyroptosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyroptosis "Pyroptosis"), mitoptosis, [parthanatos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthanatos "Parthanatos"), and [necroptosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necroptosis "Necroptosis").

## Origins

The origin of cells has to do with the [origin of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abiogenesis "Abiogenesis"), which began the [history of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_evolution "Timeline of evolution") on Earth. Small molecules needed for life may have been carried to Earth on meteorites, created at deep-sea [hydrothermal vents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrothermal_vent "Hydrothermal vent"), or [synthesized by lightning in a reducing atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller%E2%80%93Urey_experiment "Miller–Urey experiment"). There is little experimental data defining what the first self-replicating forms were. [RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA "RNA") may have been [the earliest self-replicating molecule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA_world_hypothesis "RNA world hypothesis"), as it can both store genetic information and catalyze chemical reactions. This process required an enzyme to [catalyze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalysis "Catalysis") the RNA reactions, which may have been the early [peptides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peptide "Peptide") that formed in hydrothermal vents.

Cells emerged around 4 billion years ago. The first cells were most likely [heterotrophs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterotroph "Heterotroph"). The early cell membranes were probably simpler and more permeable than later ones, with only a single fatty acid chain per lipid. Lipids spontaneously form bilayered [vesicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesicle_(biology_and_chemistry) "Vesicle (biology and chemistry)") in water, and could have preceded RNA.

[Eukaryotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eukaryote "Eukaryote") cells were created some 2.2 billion years ago in a process called [eukaryogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eukaryogenesis "Eukaryogenesis"). This is widely agreed to have involved [symbiogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbiogenesis "Symbiogenesis"), in which an [archaean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaea "Archaea") and a [bacterium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacterium "Bacterium") came together to create the [first eukaryotic common ancestor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_eukaryotic_common_ancestor "First eukaryotic common ancestor"). It evolved into a population of single-celled organisms that included the [last eukaryotic common ancestor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_eukaryotic_common_ancestor "Last eukaryotic common ancestor"), gaining capabilities along the way.

This cell had a new level of complexity, with a nucleus and facultatively aerobic [mitochondria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondrion "Mitochondrion"). It featured at least one [centriole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centriole "Centriole") and [cilium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilium "Cilium"), sex ([meiosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiosis "Meiosis") and [syngamy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syngamy "Syngamy")), [peroxisomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peroxisome "Peroxisome"), and a dormant [cyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyst "Cyst") with a cell wall of [chitin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitin "Chitin") and/or [cellulose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellulose "Cellulose"). The last eukaryotic common ancestor gave rise to the eukaryotes' [crown group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_group "Crown group"), containing the ancestors of [animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal "Animal"), [fungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fungus "Fungus"), [plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant "Plant"), and a diverse range of single-celled organisms. The [green plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_plant "Green plant") were created around 1.6 billion years ago with a second episode of symbiogenesis that added [chloroplasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloroplast "Chloroplast"), derived from [cyanobacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanobacteria "Cyanobacteria").

### Multicellularity

Multicellular behavior is demonstrated by [microorganisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microorganism "Microorganism") that are [cloned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clone_(cell_biology) "Clone (cell biology)") from a single cell and form visible [microbial colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbial_colonies "Microbial colonies"). A [microbial consortium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbial_consortium "Microbial consortium") of two or more species can form a [biofilm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biofilm "Biofilm") by the secretion of [extracellular polymeric substances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extracellular_polymeric_substance "Extracellular polymeric substance") (EPSs). [Slime molds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slime_mold "Slime mold") consist of different groups of microorganisms grouped together in a multicellular-like fashion.

The first evidence of multicellularity in an organism comes from [cyanobacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanobacteria "Cyanobacteria")-like organisms that lived between 3 and 3.5 billion years ago. Cyanobacteria are variable in morphology, [filamentous forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanobacteria#Filamentous "Cyanobacteria") exhibit functional cell differentiation such as [heterocysts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterocyst "Heterocyst") (for nitrogen fixation), [akinetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akinetes "Akinetes") (resting stage cells), and [hormogonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormogonia "Hormogonia") (reproductive, motile filaments). These, together with the intercellular connections they possess, are considered the first signs of multicellularity.

Multicellularity was made possible by the development of the [extracellular matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extracellular_matrix "Extracellular matrix") (ECM) similar in function to the bacterial EPS that consists of extracellular polymeric substances. EPS enables microbial cell adhesion, and is believed to be the first evolutionary step toward multicellular organisms. [Basement membranes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basement_membrane "Basement membrane") are a type of specialized extracellular matrix that surrounds most [animal tissues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_tissue "Animal tissue"), and are essential in their formation. Extracellular matrix components of [laminin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laminin "Laminin") domains, integrated with other proteins such as [cadherins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadherin "Cadherin") have been described in single-celled motile [choanoflagellates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choanoflagellate "Choanoflagellate") that pre-dates the evolutionary emergence of basement membranes, one of the two types of ECM. The emergence of the basement membrane coincided with the origin of multicellularity. The other type of ECM is the [interstial matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstitium "Interstitium").

The evolution of multicellularity from unicellular ancestors has been replicated in the laboratory, in [evolution experiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_evolution "Experimental evolution") using predation as the [selective pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selective_pressure "Selective pressure").

## History of research

In 1665, [Robert Hooke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hooke "Robert Hooke") examined a thin slice of cork under his [microscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microscope "Microscope"), and saw a structure of small enclosures. He wrote "I could exceeding plainly perceive it to be all perforated and porous, much like a [honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honeycomb "Honeycomb"), but that the pores of it were not regular". To further support his theory, [Matthias Schleiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Schleiden "Matthias Schleiden") and [Theodor Schwann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Schwann "Theodor Schwann") studied cells of both animal and plants. What they discovered were significant differences between the two types of cells. This put forth the idea that cells were fundamental to both plants and animals.

* 1632–1723: [Antonie van Leeuwenhoek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonie_van_Leeuwenhoek "Antonie van Leeuwenhoek") taught himself to make [lenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lens_(optics) "Lens (optics)"), constructed basic [optical microscopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_microscope "Optical microscope") and drew protozoa, such as *[Vorticella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vorticella "Vorticella")* from rain water, and [bacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteria "Bacteria") from his own mouth.
* 1665: [Robert Hooke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hooke "Robert Hooke") discovered cells in [cork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cork_(material) "Cork (material)"), then in living plant tissue using an early microscope. In his book *[Micrographia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micrographia "Micrographia")* he coined the term *cell* (from [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin") *cellula*, meaning "small room") since they resembled the cells of a [monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monastery "Monastery").
* 1839: [Theodor Schwann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Schwann "Theodor Schwann") and [Matthias Jakob Schleiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Jakob_Schleiden "Matthias Jakob Schleiden") elucidated the principle that plants and animals are made of cells, concluding that cells are a common unit of structure and development, founding the [cell theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_theory "Cell theory").
* 1855: [Rudolf Virchow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Virchow "Rudolf Virchow") stated that new cells come from pre-existing cells by [cell division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_division "Cell division") (*omnis cellula ex cellula*).
* 1931: [Ernst Ruska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Ruska "Ernst Ruska") built the first [transmission electron microscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmission_electron_microscope "Transmission electron microscope") at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin "University of Berlin"). By 1935, he had built an [electron microscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_microscope "Electron microscope") with twice the resolution of a light microscope, revealing previously unresolvable organelles.
* 1981: [Lynn Margulis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynn_Margulis "Lynn Margulis") published *Symbiosis in Cell Evolution* detailing how eukaryotic cells were created by [symbiogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbiogenesis "Symbiogenesis").
