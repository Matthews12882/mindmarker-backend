# Brad Pitt

**William Bradley Pitt** (born December 18, 1963) is an American actor and film producer. In a [film career](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Pitt_filmography "Brad Pitt filmography") spanning more than thirty-five years, Pitt has received [numerous accolades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Brad_Pitt "List of awards and nominations received by Brad Pitt"), including two [Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards"), two [British Academy Film Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Awards "British Academy Film Awards"), two [Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards "Golden Globe Awards"), two [Primetime Emmy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Awards "Primetime Emmy Awards"), and one [Volpi Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volpi_Cup_for_Best_Actor "Volpi Cup for Best Actor"). His films as a leading actor have grossed over $7.5 billion worldwide.

Pitt first gained recognition as a [cowboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowboy "Cowboy") hitchhiker in the [Ridley Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridley_Scott "Ridley Scott") road film *[Thelma & Louise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thelma_%26_Louise "Thelma & Louise")* (1991). Pitt emerged as a star taking on leading roles in films such as the drama *[A River Runs Through It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_River_Runs_Through_It_(film) "A River Runs Through It (film)")* (1992), the western *[Legends of the Fall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legends_of_the_Fall "Legends of the Fall")* (1994), the horror film *[Interview with the Vampire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interview_with_the_Vampire_(film) "Interview with the Vampire (film)")* (1994), the crime thriller *[Seven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_(1995_film) "Seven (1995 film)")* (1995), the [cult film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult_film "Cult film") *[Fight Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fight_Club "Fight Club")* (1999), and the crime comedy *[Snatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snatch_(film) "Snatch (film)")* (2000).
He cemented his [leading man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leading_man "Leading man") status by starring in [blockbusters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockbuster_(entertainment) "Blockbuster (entertainment)"), such as the [*Ocean's* film trilogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean%27s "Ocean's") (2001–2007), *[Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy_(film) "Troy (film)")* (2004) and *[Mr. & Mrs. Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._%26_Mrs._Smith_(2005_film) "Mr. & Mrs. Smith (2005 film)")* (2005). His other major successes include *[Inglourious Basterds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inglourious_Basterds "Inglourious Basterds")* (2009), *[World War Z](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_Z_(film) "World War Z (film)")* (2013), *[Bullet Train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullet_Train_(film) "Bullet Train (film)")* (2022) and [*F1*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F1_(film) "F1 (film)") (2025).

Pitt earned Academy Award nominations for his performances in the science fiction drama *[12 Monkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12_Monkeys "12 Monkeys")* (1995), the fantasy romance *[The Curious Case of Benjamin Button](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Curious_Case_of_Benjamin_Button_(film) "The Curious Case of Benjamin Button (film)")* (2008) and the sports drama *[Moneyball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moneyball_(film) "Moneyball (film)")* (2011). For his portrayal of a [stuntman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuntman "Stuntman") in [Quentin Tarantino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quentin_Tarantino "Quentin Tarantino")'s *[Once Upon a Time in Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Once_Upon_a_Time_in_Hollywood "Once Upon a Time in Hollywood")* (2019), he won the [Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor "Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor"). He also starred in acclaimed films such as *[Babel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babel_(2006_film) "Babel (2006 film)")* (2006), *[The Assassination of Jesse James by the Coward Robert Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Assassination_of_Jesse_James_by_the_Coward_Robert_Ford "The Assassination of Jesse James by the Coward Robert Ford")* (2007), *[Burn After Reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burn_After_Reading "Burn After Reading")* (2008), *[The Tree of Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tree_of_Life_(film) "The Tree of Life (film)")* (2011), *[Fury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fury_(2014_film) "Fury (2014 film)")* (2014), *[The Big Short](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Big_Short_(film) "The Big Short (film)")* (2015), *[Ad Astra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad_Astra_(film) "Ad Astra (film)")* (2019) and *[Babylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylon_(2022_film) "Babylon (2022 film)")* (2022).

In 2001, Pitt co-founded the production company [Plan B Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plan_B_Entertainment "Plan B Entertainment"). As a producer, he won the [Academy Award for Best Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture") for *[12 Years a Slave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12_Years_a_Slave_(film) "12 Years a Slave (film)")* (2013) and was nominated for *[Moneyball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moneyball_(film) "Moneyball (film)")* (2011) and *[The Big Short](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Big_Short_(film) "The Big Short (film)")* (2015). An influential figure in popular culture, Pitt appeared on *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes "Forbes")*' annual [Celebrity 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_Celebrity_100 "Forbes Celebrity 100") list from 2006 to 2008, and the [*Time* 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100 "Time 100") list in 2007. Regarded as a [sex symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_symbol "Sex symbol"), Pitt was named *People's* [Sexiest Man Alive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexiest_Man_Alive "Sexiest Man Alive") in 1995 and 2000. Pitt's relationships have been subject to widespread media attention, particularly his marriages to actresses [Jennifer Aniston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Aniston "Jennifer Aniston") and [Angelina Jolie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelina_Jolie "Angelina Jolie").

## Early life

William Bradley Pitt was born on December 18, 1963, in [Shawnee, Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawnee,_Oklahoma "Shawnee, Oklahoma"), to William Alvin Pitt, the proprietor of a trucking company, and Jane Etta ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") Hillhouse; 1940–2025), a school counselor. The family soon moved to [Springfield, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springfield,_Missouri "Springfield, Missouri"), where Pitt lived with his younger siblings, [Doug Pitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Pitt "Doug Pitt") (born 1966) and Julie Neal ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") Pitt; born 1969). Born into a conservative Christian household, he was raised as [Southern Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Baptist_Convention "Southern Baptist Convention") and later "oscillate[d] between [agnosticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnosticism "Agnosticism") and [atheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism "Atheism")". He later returned to a belief "that we're all connected".

Pitt attended [Kickapoo High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickapoo_High_School_(Springfield,_Missouri) "Kickapoo High School (Springfield, Missouri)"), where he was a member of the golf, swimming, and tennis teams. He participated in the school's [Key](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Key_Club "Key Club") and [Forensics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forensics_(public_speaking) "Forensics (public speaking)") clubs, in school debates, and in musicals. Pitt enrolled at the [University of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Missouri "University of Missouri") in 1982, majoring in journalism with a focus on advertising. As graduation approached, Pitt did not feel ready to settle down. He loved films, and since films were not made in Missouri, he decided to go to where they were made. Two weeks before completing the coursework for a degree, Pitt left the university and moved to Los Angeles, where he took acting lessons and worked odd jobs. He has named [Gary Oldman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Oldman "Gary Oldman"), [Sean Penn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Penn "Sean Penn"), and [Mickey Rourke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickey_Rourke "Mickey Rourke") as his early acting heroes.

## Career

### Early work (1987–1993)

While struggling to establish himself in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles"), Pitt took lessons from acting coach [Roy London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_London "Roy London"). His acting career began in 1987, with uncredited roles in the films *[No Way Out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Way_Out_(1987_film) "No Way Out (1987 film)")*, *[No Man's Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Man%27s_Land_(1987_film) "No Man's Land (1987 film)")* and *[Less than Zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Less_than_Zero_(film) "Less than Zero (film)")*. In May of the same year, he made his television debut in a two-episode role on the [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC "NBC") soap opera *[Another World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Another_World_(TV_series) "Another World (TV series)")*. That November, he had guest appearances on the [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Broadcasting_System "Columbia Broadcasting System") sitcom *[Trial and Error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trial_and_Error_(TV_series) "Trial and Error (TV series)")* and the [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company "American Broadcasting Company") sitcom *[Growing Pains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growing_Pains "Growing Pains")*. He appeared in four episodes of the [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS "CBS") primetime series *[Dallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_(TV_series) "Dallas (TV series)")* between December 1987 and February 1988 as Randy, the boyfriend of Charlotte (played by [Shalane McCall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalane_McCall "Shalane McCall")). Later in 1988, Pitt made a guest appearance on the [Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company "Fox Broadcasting Company") police drama *[21 Jump Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21_Jump_Street "21 Jump Street")* and appeared in a [Pringles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pringles "Pringles") commercial directed by [Lawrence Bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Bridges "Lawrence Bridges"). The same year, he had his first leading film role in the Yugoslavian–American co-production *[The Dark Side of the Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dark_Side_of_the_Sun_(film) "The Dark Side of the Sun (film)")*, as a young American taken by his family to the [Adriatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adriatic_Sea "Adriatic Sea") to find a remedy for a skin condition. The film was shelved at the outbreak of the [Croatian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_War_of_Independence "Croatian War of Independence"), and was not released until 1997. Pitt made two film appearances in 1989: the first in a supporting role in the comedy *[Happy Together](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happy_Together_(1989_American_film) "Happy Together (1989 American film)")*, and the second in a major role in the horror film *[Cutting Class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutting_Class "Cutting Class")*, which was the first of Pitt's films to reach theaters. That year, he made guest appearances on the television series *[Head of the Class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_the_Class "Head of the Class")*, *[Freddy's Nightmares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freddy%27s_Nightmares "Freddy's Nightmares")*, *[Thirtysomething](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirtysomething "Thirtysomething")*, and *Growing Pains*.

Pitt appeared as Billy Canton, a drug addict who takes advantage of a young runaway (played by [Juliette Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juliette_Lewis "Juliette Lewis")) in the 1990 NBC television film *[Too Young to Die?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Too_Young_to_Die%3F "Too Young to Die?")*, the story of an abused teenager sentenced to death for a murder. [Ken Tucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Tucker "Ken Tucker") of *[Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")* described Pitt as a "magnificent slimeball" in his role as the "hoody" and "really scary" boyfriend of Lewis' character. The same year, Pitt co-starred in six episodes of the short-lived Fox drama *[Glory Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glory_Days_(1990_TV_series) "Glory Days (1990 TV series)")* and had a supporting role in the [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO "HBO") television film *[The Image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Image_(TV) "The Image (TV)")*. His next appearance came in the 1991 film *[Across the Tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Across_the_Tracks "Across the Tracks")*, in which he portrays Joe Maloney, a high school runner. The same year he featured in a [Levi's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi%27s "Levi's") jeans commercial.

Pitt attracted wider recognition in his supporting role in [Ridley Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridley_Scott "Ridley Scott")'s 1991 film *[Thelma & Louise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thelma_%26_Louise "Thelma & Louise")*. He plays J.D., a small-time criminal who befriends Thelma ([Geena Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geena_Davis "Geena Davis")). His love scene with Davis has been cited as the event that defined Pitt as a [sex symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_symbol "Sex symbol"). Pitt subsequently starred in *[Johnny Suede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Suede "Johnny Suede")* (1991) a low-budget film about an aspiring rock star, and the live-action/animated fantasy film *[Cool World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cool_World "Cool World")* (1992), both of which fared poorly at the box office and with critics.

Pitt appeared as Paul Maclean in the 1992 biographical film *[A River Runs Through It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_River_Runs_Through_It_(film) "A River Runs Through It (film)")*, directed by [Robert Redford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Redford "Robert Redford"). Although Pitt thought his portrayal of Maclean was one of his weakest performances, the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")* described it as a career-making performance. Jeff Giles of [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes") said Pitt's portrayal proved he could be more than a "cowboy-hatted hunk." In 1993, Pitt reunited with Juliette Lewis for the road film *[Kalifornia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalifornia "Kalifornia")*, playing the [serial killer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serial_killer "Serial killer") husband of her character. His performance was described by [Peter Travers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Travers "Peter Travers") of *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")* as "outstanding, all boyish charm and then a snort that exudes pure menace." Pitt also garnered attention for a brief appearance in the cult hit *[True Romance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Romance "True Romance")* as a stoner named Floyd. He finished the year by winning a [ShoWest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_of_Theatre_Owners "National Association of Theatre Owners") Award for Male Star of Tomorrow.

### Breakthrough (1994–1998)

In 1994, Pitt portrayed the vampire [Louis de Pointe du Lac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_de_Pointe_du_Lac "Louis de Pointe du Lac") in the horror film *[Interview with the Vampire: The Vampire Chronicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interview_with_the_Vampire_(film) "Interview with the Vampire (film)")*, based on [Anne Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Rice "Anne Rice")'s 1976 novel [Interview with the Vampire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interview_with_the_Vampire "Interview with the Vampire"). Pitt was part of an [ensemble cast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensemble_cast "Ensemble cast") that included [Tom Cruise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cruise "Tom Cruise"), [Kirsten Dunst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirsten_Dunst "Kirsten Dunst"), [Christian Slater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Slater "Christian Slater"), and [Antonio Banderas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Banderas "Antonio Banderas"). Despite winning two [MTV Movie Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Movie_Awards "MTV Movie Awards") at the [1995 ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_MTV_Movie_Awards "1995 MTV Movie Awards"), his performance was poorly received by critics.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] Matt Zoller Seitz of the *[Dallas Observer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Observer "Dallas Observer")* felt Pitt was dull in the role because he failed to display the self-awareness and inner torment necessary for the character.

Pitt next starred in *[Legends of the Fall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legends_of_the_Fall "Legends of the Fall")* (1994), based on the novel *Legends of the Fall* by [Jim Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Harrison "Jim Harrison") and set in the American West during the first four decades of the twentieth century. He portrays Tristan Ludlow, son of Colonel William Ludlow ([Anthony Hopkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Hopkins "Anthony Hopkins")). For his performance, Pitt received his first [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award "Golden Globe Award") nomination, in the [Best Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actor_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Drama "Golden Globe Award for Best Actor – Motion Picture Drama") category. [Janet Maslin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Maslin "Janet Maslin") of *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* wrote, "Pitt's diffident mix of acting and attitude works to such heartthrob perfection it's a shame the film's superficiality gets in his way." Although the film received mixed reviews, the *[Deseret News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deseret_News "Deseret News")* predicted that *Legends of the Fall* would solidify Pitt's reputation as a lead actor.

In 1995, Pitt starred alongside [Morgan Freeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Freeman "Morgan Freeman"), [Gwyneth Paltrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwyneth_Paltrow "Gwyneth Paltrow"), and [Kevin Spacey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Spacey "Kevin Spacey") in the crime thriller *[Seven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_(1995_film) "Seven (1995 film)")*, playing a detective on the trail of a serial killer who preys on people he considers guilty of the [Seven Deadly Sins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Deadly_Sins "Seven Deadly Sins"). Pitt said the role would expand his acting horizons, expressing an intention to move on from playing the "pretty boy" to playing someone with flaws." *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* wrote that Pitt did a "determined, energetic, creditable job" in his role, and that his performance "was screen acting at its best". Following the box-office success of *Seven*, Pitt played the psychotic [anarchist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchy "Anarchy") Jeffrey Goines in [Terry Gilliam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Gilliam "Terry Gilliam")'s 1995 science fiction film *[12 Monkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12_Monkeys "12 Monkeys")*. The film received predominantly positive reviews, with Pitt praised in particular. Janet Maslin of *The New York Times* called *Twelve Monkeys* "fierce and disturbing" and remarked on Pitt's "startlingly frenzied performance", concluding that he "electrifies Jeffrey with a weird magnetism that becomes important later in the film." He won a Golden Globe Award for [Best Supporting Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture "Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actor – Motion Picture") for the film and received his first [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award "Academy Award") nomination for [Best Supporting Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor "Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor").

The following year, he appeared in the legal drama *[Sleepers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleepers_(film) "Sleepers (film)")* (1996), based on [Lorenzo Carcaterra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Carcaterra "Lorenzo Carcaterra")'s novel of the same name. The film received mixed reviews. In the 1997 film *[The Devil's Own](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Devil%27s_Own "The Devil's Own")* Pitt starred, opposite [Harrison Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrison_Ford "Harrison Ford"), as [Irish Republican Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Irish_Republican_Army "Provisional Irish Republican Army") terrorist Rory Devany, a role for which he was required to learn an Irish accent. Critical opinion was divided on his accent; "Pitt finds the right tone of moral ambiguity, but at times his Irish brogue is too convincing – it's hard to understand what he's saying", wrote the *[San Francisco Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Chronicle "San Francisco Chronicle").* *[The Charleston Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Charleston_Gazette "The Charleston Gazette")* opined that it had favored Pitt's accent over the movie. *The Devil's Own* grossed $140 million worldwide.

Later that year, he led as Austrian mountaineer [Heinrich Harrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Harrer "Heinrich Harrer") in the [Jean-Jacques Annaud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Jacques_Annaud "Jean-Jacques Annaud") film *[Seven Years in Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years_in_Tibet_(1997_film) "Seven Years in Tibet (1997 film)")*. Pitt trained for months for the role, which demanded significant mountain climbing and trekking practice, including rock climbing in California and the European [Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alps "Alps") with his co-star [David Thewlis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Thewlis "David Thewlis"). Pitt had the lead role in 1998's fantasy romance film *[Meet Joe Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meet_Joe_Black "Meet Joe Black")*. He portrayed a [personification of death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_(personification) "Death (personification)") inhabiting the body of a young man to learn what it is like to be human. The film received mixed reviews, and many were critical of Pitt's performance. According to [Mick LaSalle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mick_LaSalle "Mick LaSalle") of the *[San Francisco Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Chronicle "San Francisco Chronicle")*, Pitt was unable to "make an audience believe that he knows all the mysteries of death and eternity." [Roger Ebert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ebert "Roger Ebert") remarked, "Pitt is a fine actor, but this performance is a miscalculation."

### Rise to prominence (1999–2003)

In 1999, Pitt portrayed [Tyler Durden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Narrator_(Fight_Club) "The Narrator (Fight Club)") in *[Fight Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fight_Club "Fight Club")*, a film adaptation of [Chuck Palahniuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Palahniuk "Chuck Palahniuk")'s [novel of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fight_Club_(novel) "Fight Club (novel)"), directed by [David Fincher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Fincher "David Fincher"). Pitt prepared for the part with lessons in [boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxing "Boxing"), [taekwondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taekwondo "Taekwondo"), and [grappling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grappling "Grappling"). To look the part, Pitt consented to the removal of pieces of his front teeth which were restored when filming ended. While promoting *Fight Club*, Pitt said that the film explored not taking one's aggressions out on someone else but to "have an experience, take a punch more and see how you come out on the other end." *Fight Club* premiered at the 1999 [Venice International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice_International_Film_Festival "Venice International Film Festival"). Despite divided critical opinion on the film as a whole, Pitt's performance was widely praised. [Paul Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Clinton "Paul Clinton") of [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN "CNN") noted the risky yet successful nature of the film, while *Variety* remarked upon Pitt's ability to be "cool, charismatic and more dynamically physical, perhaps than [...] his breakthrough role in *Thelma and Louise*". In spite of a worse-than-expected box office performance, *Fight Club* became a [cult classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult_following "Cult following") after its DVD release in 2000.

Pitt was cast as an [Irish Traveller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Travellers "Irish Travellers") [boxer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxing "Boxing") with a barely intelligible accent in [Guy Ritchie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Ritchie "Guy Ritchie")'s 2000 gangster film *[Snatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snatch_(film) "Snatch (film)")*. Several reviewers were critical of *Snatch*; however, most praised Pitt. Mick LaSalle of the *San Francisco Chronicle* said Pitt was "ideally cast as an Irishman whose accent is so thick even Brits can't understand him", going on to say that, before *Snatch*, Pitt had been "shackled by roles that called for brooding introspection, but recently he has found his calling in black comic outrageousness and flashy extroversion;" while [Amy Taubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Taubin "Amy Taubin") of *[The Village Voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Village_Voice "The Village Voice")* claimed that "Pitt gets maximum comic mileage out of a one-joke role".

The following year Pitt starred opposite [Julia Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Roberts "Julia Roberts") in the [romantic comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romantic_comedy "Romantic comedy") *[The Mexican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mexican "The Mexican")*, a film that garnered a range of reviews but enjoyed box office success. Pitt's next role, in 2001's $143 million-grossing [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War") thriller *[Spy Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spy_Game "Spy Game")*, was as Tom Bishop, an operative of the [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Intelligence_Agency "Central Intelligence Agency")'s [Special Activities Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Activities_Division "Special Activities Division"), mentored by [Robert Redford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Redford "Robert Redford")'s character. Mark Holcomb of [Salon.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salon.com "Salon.com") enjoyed the film, although he noted that neither Pitt nor Redford provided "much of an emotional connection for the audience".

On November 22, 2001, Pitt made a guest appearance in the [eighth season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_One_with_the_Rumor "The One with the Rumor") of the television series *[Friends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends "Friends")*, playing a man with a grudge against [Rachel Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Green "Rachel Green"), played by [Jennifer Aniston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Aniston "Jennifer Aniston") (to whom Pitt was married at the time). For this performance, he was nominated for an [Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Guest_Actor_in_a_Comedy_Series "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Guest Actor in a Comedy Series"). In December 2001, Pitt played [Rusty Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusty_Ryan_(character) "Rusty Ryan (character)") in the heist film *[Ocean's Eleven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean%27s_Eleven_(2001_film) "Ocean's Eleven (2001 film)")*, a remake of the 1960 [Rat Pack original](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean%27s_Eleven_(1960_film) "Ocean's Eleven (1960 film)"). He joined an ensemble cast including [George Clooney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Clooney "George Clooney"), [Matt Damon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Damon "Matt Damon"), [Andy García](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Garc%C3%ADa "Andy García"), and Julia Roberts. Well received by critics, *Ocean's Eleven* was highly successful at the box office, earning $450 million worldwide.

Pitt appeared in two episodes of MTV's reality series *[Jackass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackass_(franchise) "Jackass (franchise)")* in February 2002, first running through the streets of Los Angeles with several cast members in gorilla suits, and in a subsequent episode participating in his own staged abduction. In the same year, Pitt had a cameo role in George Clooney's directorial debut *[Confessions of a Dangerous Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confessions_of_a_Dangerous_Mind_(film) "Confessions of a Dangerous Mind (film)")*. He took on his first voice-acting roles in 2003, speaking as the titular character of the [DreamWorks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DreamWorks_Pictures "DreamWorks Pictures") animated film *[Sinbad: Legend of the Seven Seas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinbad:_Legend_of_the_Seven_Seas "Sinbad: Legend of the Seven Seas")* and playing [Boomhauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boomhauer "Boomhauer")'s brother, [Patch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patch_Boomhauer "Patch Boomhauer"), in an episode of the animated television series *[King of the Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_the_Hill "King of the Hill")*.

### Worldwide recognition (2004–2008)

Pitt had two major film roles in 2004, starring as [Achilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achilles "Achilles") in *[Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy_(film) "Troy (film)")*, and reprising his role, [Rusty Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusty_Ryan_(character) "Rusty Ryan (character)"), in the sequel *[Ocean's Twelve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean%27s_Twelve "Ocean's Twelve")*. He spent six months sword training before the filming of *Troy*, based on the *[Iliad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iliad "Iliad")*. An on-set injury to his [Achilles tendon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achilles_tendon "Achilles tendon") delayed production on the picture for several weeks. [Stephen Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Hunter "Stephen Hunter") of *[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")* stated that Pitt excelled at such a demanding role. *Troy* was the first film produced by [Plan B Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plan_B_Entertainment "Plan B Entertainment"), a film production company he had founded two years earlier with Jennifer Aniston and [Brad Grey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Grey "Brad Grey"), CEO of [Paramount Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures "Paramount Pictures"). *Ocean's Twelve* earned $362 million worldwide, and Pitt and Clooney's dynamic was described by CNN's Paul Clinton as "the best male chemistry since Paul Newman and Robert Redford." In 2005, Pitt starred as John Smith in the [Doug Liman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Liman "Doug Liman")-directed action comedy *[Mr. & Mrs. Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._%26_Mrs._Smith_(2005_film) "Mr. & Mrs. Smith (2005 film)")*, in which a bored married couple discover that each is an assassin for a competing agency. The feature received reasonable reviews but was generally lauded for the chemistry between Pitt and [Angelina Jolie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelina_Jolie "Angelina Jolie"), who played his character's wife Jane Smith. The *[Star Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Tribune "Star Tribune")* noted that "while the story feels haphazard, the movie gets by on gregarious charm, galloping energy and the stars' thermonuclear screen chemistry". *Mr. & Mrs. Smith* earned $478 million worldwide, making it one of the biggest hits of 2005.

For his next film, Pitt starred opposite [Cate Blanchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett "Cate Blanchett") in [Alejandro González Iñárritu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Gonz%C3%A1lez_I%C3%B1%C3%A1rritu "Alejandro González Iñárritu")'s [multi-narrative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiperspectivity "Multiperspectivity") drama *[Babel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babel_(2006_film) "Babel (2006 film)")* (2006). Pitt's performance was critically well-received, and the *[Seattle Post-Intelligencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Post-Intelligencer "Seattle Post-Intelligencer")* said that he was credible and gave the film visibility. Pitt later said he regarded taking the part as one of the best decisions of his career. The film was screened at a special presentation at the [2006 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Cannes_Film_Festival "2006 Cannes Film Festival") and was later featured at the 2006 [Toronto International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_International_Film_Festival "Toronto International Film Festival"). *Babel* received seven Academy Award and Golden Globe nominations, winning the [Golden Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Drama "Golden Globe Award for Best Motion Picture – Drama"), and earned Pitt a [Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actor – Motion Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture "Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actor – Motion Picture") nomination. That same year, Pitt's company Plan B Entertainment produced *[The Departed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Departed "The Departed")*, which won the [Academy Award for Best Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture"). Pitt was credited on-screen as a producer; however, only [Graham King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_King "Graham King") was ruled eligible for the Oscar win.

Reprising his role as Rusty Ryan in a third picture, Pitt starred in 2007's *[Ocean's Thirteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean%27s_Thirteen "Ocean's Thirteen")*. While less lucrative than the first two films, this sequel earned $311 million at the international box office. Pitt's next film role was as American outlaw [Jesse James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_James "Jesse James") in the 2007 [Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_(genre) "Western (genre)") drama *[The Assassination of Jesse James by the Coward Robert Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Assassination_of_Jesse_James_by_the_Coward_Robert_Ford "The Assassination of Jesse James by the Coward Robert Ford")*, adapted from [Ron Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Hansen_(novelist) "Ron Hansen (novelist)")'s [1983 novel of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Assassination_of_Jesse_James_by_the_Coward_Robert_Ford_(novel) "The Assassination of Jesse James by the Coward Robert Ford (novel)"). Directed by [Andrew Dominik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Dominik "Andrew Dominik") and produced by Pitt's company Plan B Entertainment, the film premiered at the [2007 Venice Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/64th_Venice_International_Film_Festival "64th Venice International Film Festival"), with Pitt playing a "scary and charismatic" role, according to Lewis Beale of *[Film Journal International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_Journal_International "Film Journal International")*, and earning Pitt the [Volpi Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volpi_Cup "Volpi Cup") award for Best Actor at the 64th Venice International Film Festival. He eventually collected the award one year later at the [2008 festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/65th_Venice_International_Film_Festival "65th Venice International Film Festival"). As of January 2019, it was his own favorite of his films.

Pitt's next appearance was in the 2008 black comedy *[Burn After Reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burn_After_Reading "Burn After Reading")*, his first collaboration with the [Coen brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coen_brothers "Coen brothers"). The film received a positive reception from critics, with *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* calling it "a tightly wound, slickly plotted spy comedy", noting that Pitt's performance was one of the funniest. He was later cast as Benjamin Button, the lead in [David Fincher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Fincher "David Fincher")'s 2008 film *[The Curious Case of Benjamin Button](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Curious_Case_of_Benjamin_Button_(film) "The Curious Case of Benjamin Button (film)")*, a loosely adapted version of a 1921 [short story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Curious_Case_of_Benjamin_Button_(short_story) "The Curious Case of Benjamin Button (short story)") by [F. Scott Fitzgerald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._Scott_Fitzgerald "F. Scott Fitzgerald"). The story follows a man who is born an [octogenarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octogenarian "Octogenarian") and ages in reverse, with Pitt's "sensitive" performance making *Benjamin Button* a "timeless masterpiece", according to [Michael Sragow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Sragow "Michael Sragow") of *[The Baltimore Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Baltimore_Sun "The Baltimore Sun")*. The performance earned Pitt his first [Screen Actors Guild Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actor_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Male_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role "Actor Award for Outstanding Performance by a Male Actor in a Leading Role") nomination, as well as a fourth [Golden Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Drama "Golden Globe Award for Best Actor in a Motion Picture – Drama") and second [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor "Academy Award for Best Actor") nomination. The film received 13 Academy Award nominations and grossed $329 million at the box office worldwide.

### Artistic resurgence and production success (2009–2018)

Pitt's next leading role came in 2009 with the [Quentin Tarantino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quentin_Tarantino "Quentin Tarantino")-directed [war film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_film "War film") *[Inglourious Basterds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inglourious_Basterds "Inglourious Basterds")*, which premiered at the [2009 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Cannes_Film_Festival "2009 Cannes Film Festival"). Pitt played Lieutenant Aldo Raine, "The Apache", an American resistance fighter battling [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi "Nazi") in German-occupied France. The film was a box office hit, taking $311 million worldwide, and garnered generally favorable reviews. The film received multiple [awards and nominations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_accolades_received_by_Inglourious_Basterds "List of accolades received by Inglourious Basterds"), including eight [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards") nominations and seven [MTV Movie Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Movie_Award "MTV Movie Award") nominations, including [Best Male Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Movie_Award_for_Best_Performance "MTV Movie Award for Best Performance") for Pitt. He next voiced the superhero Metro Man in the 2010 animated feature *[Megamind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megamind "Megamind")*. Pitt produced and appeared in [Terrence Malick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrence_Malick "Terrence Malick")'s experimental drama *[The Tree of Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tree_of_Life_(film) "The Tree of Life (film)")*, co-starring [Sean Penn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Penn "Sean Penn"), which won the [Palme d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palme_d%27Or "Palme d'Or") at the [2011 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Cannes_Film_Festival "2011 Cannes Film Festival"). In a performance that attracted strong praise, he portrayed [Oakland Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland_Athletics "Oakland Athletics") general manager [Billy Beane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Beane "Billy Beane") in the drama *[Moneyball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moneyball_(film) "Moneyball (film)")*, which is based on the 2003 [book of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moneyball:_The_Art_of_Winning_an_Unfair_Game "Moneyball: The Art of Winning an Unfair Game") written by [Michael Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Lewis "Michael Lewis"). *Moneyball* received six Academy Award nominations, including [Best Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture") and [Best Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor "Academy Award for Best Actor") for Pitt.

His next role was as mob hitman Jackie Cogan in [Andrew Dominik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Dominik "Andrew Dominik")'s *[Killing Them Softly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_Them_Softly "Killing Them Softly")* (2012), based on the novel *[Cogan's Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cogan%27s_Trade "Cogan's Trade")* by [George V. Higgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_V._Higgins "George V. Higgins"). In 2013, Pitt starred in *[World War Z](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_Z_(film) "World War Z (film)")*, a thriller about a [zombie apocalypse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zombie_apocalypse "Zombie apocalypse"), based on [Max Brooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Brooks "Max Brooks")'s [novel of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_Z "World War Z"). Pitt also produced the film. *World War Z* grossed $540 million at the box office worldwide, becoming Pitt's then highest grossing picture, until it was overtaken by [*F1*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F1_(film) "F1 (film)") in 2025. Next in 2013, he produced, and played a small role in, *[12 Years a Slave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12_Years_a_Slave_(film) "12 Years a Slave (film)")*, a historical drama based on the autobiography of [Solomon Northup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Northup "Solomon Northup"). The film received critical acclaim and was nominated for nine Academy Awards and won three, including [Best Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture"). Also in 2013, Pitt had a supporting role in [Ridley Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridley_Scott "Ridley Scott")'s *[The Counselor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Counselor "The Counselor")*. [Plan B Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plan_B_Entertainment "Plan B Entertainment") landed its first television series on the 2013–2014 schedule, as their joint venture with [ABC Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_Studios "ABC Studios"), the sci-fi/fantasy drama *[Resurrection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resurrection_(U.S._TV_series) "Resurrection (U.S. TV series)")*, was picked up by [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company "American Broadcasting Company").

Pitt starred in *[Fury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fury_(2014_film) "Fury (2014 film)")*, a [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") film directed and written by [David Ayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ayer "David Ayer"), and co-starring [Shia LaBeouf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_LaBeouf "Shia LaBeouf"), [Logan Lerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_Lerman "Logan Lerman"), [Jon Bernthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Bernthal "Jon Bernthal"), [Michael Peña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Pe%C3%B1a "Michael Peña"), and [Jason Isaacs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Isaacs "Jason Isaacs"). The film was released on October 17, 2014. By the end of its run, *Fury* proved to be a commercial and critical success; it grossed $211 million worldwide and received highly positive reviews from critics.

In 2015, Pitt starred opposite his wife, Jolie, in her third directorial effort, *[By the Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/By_the_Sea_(2015_film) "By the Sea (2015 film)")*, a romantic drama about a marriage in crisis, based on her screenplay. The film was their first collaboration since 2005's *Mr. & Mrs. Smith*. Pitt's next role came with the biographical comedy-drama *[The Big Short](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Big_Short_(film) "The Big Short (film)")*, which he also produced and also co-starred alongside [Christian Bale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Bale "Christian Bale"), [Steve Carell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Carell "Steve Carell"), and [Ryan Gosling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Gosling "Ryan Gosling"). The film was a commercial and critical success. It went on to gross over $102 million worldwide and received positive reviews from critics. The film was nominated for five Academy Awards, including Best Picture, earning Pitt his third Academy Award nomination as producer. In 2016, Pitt starred in [Robert Zemeckis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Zemeckis "Robert Zemeckis")'s romantic thriller *[Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_(film) "Allied (film)")*, in which he plays an assassin who falls in love with a French spy (played by [Marion Cotillard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marion_Cotillard "Marion Cotillard")) during a mission to kill a German official in [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). In 2017, he starred in the [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") satirical war comedy *[War Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Machine_(2017_film) "War Machine (2017 film)")*, which he also produced. Pitt played a recurring role as a weatherman on the late-night talk show *[The Jim Jefferies Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jim_Jefferies_Show "The Jim Jefferies Show")* throughout 2017.

A 2017 sequel to *World War Z* was announced in 2016, then briefly delayed, then its director confirmed to be David Fincher, and finally shelved because of budget problems.

### Established legacy (2019–present)

Pitt starred as veteran stunt double Cliff Booth opposite [Leonardo DiCaprio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_DiCaprio "Leonardo DiCaprio"), in [Quentin Tarantino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quentin_Tarantino "Quentin Tarantino")'s 2019 film *[Once Upon a Time in Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Once_Upon_a_Time_in_Hollywood "Once Upon a Time in Hollywood")*, reuniting with DiCaprio after *The Departed*, which Pitt produced and DiCaprio starred in. For his performance in the film, he won the [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress "Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress"), [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture "Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actor – Motion Picture"), [BAFTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Supporting_Role "BAFTA Award for Best Actor in a Supporting Role"), [Screen Actors Guild Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Male_Actor_in_a_Supporting_Role "Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Male Actor in a Supporting Role"), and [Critics' Choice Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Movie_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor "Critics' Choice Movie Award for Best Supporting Actor"). This is the second Oscar for Pitt, his first that he received for acting. In 2019, he also starred in [James Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gray_(film_director) "James Gray (film director)")'s deep space epic *[Ad Astra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad_Astra_(film) "Ad Astra (film)")*, in which he played Roy McBride, a space engineer searching the galaxy for his father. Pitt's performance was praised as one of his career-best turn, delivering a performance "that weaponizes passivity into a lethal form of self-defense". In [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live_season_45 "Saturday Night Live season 45"), Pitt portrayed [Dr. Anthony Fauci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Anthony_Fauci "Dr. Anthony Fauci") in the cold open on *[Saturday Night Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live "Saturday Night Live")*, earning him a [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Guest Actor in a Comedy Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Guest_Actor_in_a_Comedy_Series "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Guest Actor in a Comedy Series") nomination.

In 2021, Pitt entered the recording business by creating a company with French record producer [Damien Quintard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damien_Quintard "Damien Quintard"). Set in Pitt's [Chateau Miraval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C3%A2teau_Miraval,_Correns-Var "Château Miraval, Correns-Var") in South of France, Miraval Studios re-opened in 2022 after two decades of inactivity. The previous version of the studio was one of the most iconic studios in the world, producing the records for [Pink Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_Floyd "Pink Floyd"), [the Cranberries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cranberries "The Cranberries"), [AC/DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC/DC "AC/DC"), [Sade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sade_(singer) "Sade (singer)") and [Muse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muse_(band) "Muse (band)"), among others. In 2022, Pitt starred in *[Bullet Train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullet_Train_(film) "Bullet Train (film)")*, directed by [David Leitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Leitch "David Leitch"), and reunited with his *Once Upon a Time in Hollywood* co-star [Margot Robbie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margot_Robbie "Margot Robbie") in *[Babylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylon_(2022_film) "Babylon (2022 film)")*, directed by [Damien Chazelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damien_Chazelle "Damien Chazelle"). He reteamed with George Clooney in *[Wolfs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfs_(film) "Wolfs (film)")*, a comedy-thriller written and directed by [Jon Watts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Watts "Jon Watts"), which was released in September 2024. In 2025, Pitt produced and starred in the [Formula One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_One "Formula One") racing film *[F1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F1_(film) "F1 (film)")*, directed by [Joseph Kosinski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Kosinski "Joseph Kosinski"), for which he earned $30 million. *F1* quickly became Apple's biggest hit at the box office, and Pitt's highest-grossing film. In 2026, Pitt portrayed a wandering lost war soldier and dog owner in *[Heart of the Beast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_of_the_Beast "Heart of the Beast")* and reprises his role as Cliff Booth in *[The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Further_Mis-Adventures_of_Cliff_Booth "The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth")*.

## Acting credits and accolades

Over his career, Pitt has received numerous awards including two [Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards") (as a producer and actor), two [BAFTA Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Awards "BAFTA Awards"), two [Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards "Golden Globe Awards"), two [Primetime Emmy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Awards "Primetime Emmy Awards"), and two [Screen Actors Guild Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Awards "Screen Actors Guild Awards").

Pitt has been recognized by the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences "Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences") for the following performances:

* [81st Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/81st_Academy_Awards "81st Academy Awards") (2008): [Best Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor "Academy Award for Best Actor"), nomination, *[The Curious Case of Benjamin Button](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Curious_Case_of_Benjamin_Button_(film) "The Curious Case of Benjamin Button (film)")*

## Philanthropy and activism

Pitt visited the [University of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Missouri "University of Missouri") campus in October 2004 to encourage students to vote in the [2004 U.S. presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_United_States_presidential_election "2004 United States presidential election"), in which he supported [John Kerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kerry "John Kerry"). Later in October, he publicly supported the principle of public funding for [embryonic stem-cell research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stem_cell "Stem cell"). "We have to make sure that we open up these avenues so that our best and our brightest can go find these cures that they believe they will find", he said. In support of this he endorsed [Proposition 71](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Constitution_Article_XXXV "California Constitution Article XXXV"), a California ballot initiative intended to provide state government funding for stem-cell research.

Pitt supports [One Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ONE_Campaign "ONE Campaign"), an organization aimed at combating AIDS and poverty in the developing world. He narrated the 2005 [PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Broadcasting_Service "Public Broadcasting Service") public television series *Rx for Survival: A Global Health Challenge*, which discusses current global health issues. The following year Pitt and Jolie flew to [Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiti "Haiti"), where they visited a school supported by Yéle Haïti, a charity founded by Haitian-born hip hop musician [Wyclef Jean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyclef_Jean "Wyclef Jean"). In May 2007, Pitt and Jolie donated $1 million to three organizations in [Chad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad "Chad") and [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan "Sudan") dedicated to those affected by the crisis in the Darfur region. Along with Clooney, Damon, [Don Cheadle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Cheadle "Don Cheadle"), [David Pressman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Pressman "David Pressman"), and [Jerry Weintraub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Weintraub "Jerry Weintraub"), Pitt is one of the founders of Not On Our Watch, an organization that focuses global attention on stopping "mass atrocities".

Pitt has a sustained interest in architecture, even taking time away from film to study computer-aided design at the Los Angeles offices of renowned architect [Frank Gehry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Gehry "Frank Gehry"). He narrated *e2 design*, a PBS television series focused on worldwide efforts to build environmentally friendly structures through sustainable architecture and design. In 2000, he co-authored an architectural book on the [Blacker House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_R._Blacker_House "Robert R. Blacker House") with the architects Thomas A. Heinz and Randell Makinson. In 2006, he founded the [Make It Right Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Make_It_Right_Foundation_New_Orleans "Make It Right Foundation New Orleans"), organizing housing professionals in New Orleans to finance and construct 150 sustainable, affordable new houses in New Orleans's [Ninth Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninth_Ward_of_New_Orleans "Ninth Ward of New Orleans") following the devastation caused by [Hurricane Katrina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Katrina "Hurricane Katrina").

The project involves 13 architectural firms and the environmental organization [Global Green USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Green_USA "Global Green USA"), with several of the firms donating their services. Pitt and philanthropist [Steve Bing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Bing "Steve Bing") have each committed $5 million in donations. The first six homes were completed in October 2008, and in September 2009 Pitt received an award in recognition of the project from the [U.S. Green Building Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Green_Building_Council "United States Green Building Council"), a non-profit trade organization that promotes sustainability in how buildings are designed, built and operated. Pitt met with U.S. [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States "President of the United States") [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") and [Speaker of the House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_United_States_House_of_Representatives "Speaker of the United States House of Representatives") [Nancy Pelosi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Pelosi "Nancy Pelosi") in March 2009 to promote his concept of *green housing* as a national model and to discuss federal funding possibilities.

In September 2006, Pitt and Jolie established a charitable organization, the Jolie-Pitt Foundation, to aid humanitarian causes around the world. The foundation made initial donations of $1 million each to [Global Action for Children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Action_for_Children "Global Action for Children") and [Doctors Without Borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctors_Without_Borders "Doctors Without Borders"), followed by an October 2006 donation of $100,000 to the Daniel Pearl Foundation, an organization created in memory of the late American journalist [Daniel Pearl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Pearl "Daniel Pearl"). According to federal filings, Pitt and Jolie invested $8.5 million into the foundation in 2006; it gave away $2.4 million in 2006 and $3.4 million in 2007. In June 2009, the Jolie-Pitt Foundation donated $1 million to a U.N. refugee agency to help Pakistanis displaced by fighting between troops and [Taliban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taliban "Taliban") militants. In January 2010, the foundation donated $1 million to Doctors Without Borders for emergency medical assistance to help victims of the [Haiti earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Haiti_earthquake "2010 Haiti earthquake").

Pitt is a supporter of [same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_the_United_States "Same-sex marriage in the United States"). In an October 2006 interview with *[Esquire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esquire_(magazine) "Esquire (magazine)")*, Pitt said that he would marry Jolie when everyone in America is legally able to marry. In September 2008, he donated $100,000 to the campaign against California's 2008 ballot proposition [Proposition 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Proposition_8_(2008) "California Proposition 8 (2008)"), an initiative to overturn [the state Supreme Court decision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_re_Marriage_Cases "In re Marriage Cases") that had [legalized same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_California "Same-sex marriage in California"). In March 2012, Pitt was featured in a performance of [Dustin Lance Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dustin_Lance_Black "Dustin Lance Black")'s play, *[8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8_(play) "8 (play)")* – a staged reenactment of the [federal trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perry_v._Brown "Perry v. Brown") that overturned California's [Prop 8 ban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proposition_8 "Proposition 8") on same-sex marriage – as Judge [Vaughn Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaughn_Walker "Vaughn Walker"). In September 2012, Pitt reaffirmed his support for Obama, saying, "I am an Obama supporter and I'm backing his [U.S. election campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama_2012_presidential_campaign "Barack Obama 2012 presidential campaign")." In October 2020, he narrated an advertisement for [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden")'s [2020 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden_2020_presidential_campaign "Joe Biden 2020 presidential campaign").

## Personal life

### Relationships and marriages

From the late 1980s to early 1990s, Pitt was romantically involved with several of his co-stars, including [Shalane McCall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalane_McCall "Shalane McCall") (*Dallas*), [Robin Givens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Givens "Robin Givens") (*Head of the Class*), [Jill Schoelen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jill_Schoelen "Jill Schoelen") (*Cutting Class*), [Geena Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geena_Davis "Geena Davis") (*Thelma & Louise*), and [Juliette Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juliette_Lewis "Juliette Lewis") (*Too Young to Die?* and *Kalifornia*). McCall and Lewis were 15 and 17, respectively, when Pitt started dating them. Subsequently, Pitt had a much-publicized romance with, and engagement to, his *Seven* co-star, [Gwyneth Paltrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwyneth_Paltrow "Gwyneth Paltrow"), whom he dated from 1994 to 1997.

Pitt met actress [Jennifer Aniston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Aniston "Jennifer Aniston") in 1998; they married in a private wedding ceremony in [Malibu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malibu,_California "Malibu, California") on July 29, 2000. In January 2005, Pitt and Aniston announced they had decided to separate. Two months later, Aniston filed for divorce, citing irreconcilable differences. Pitt and Aniston's divorce was finalized by the [Los Angeles Superior Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Superior_Court "Los Angeles Superior Court") on October 2, 2005. Despite media reports that Pitt and Aniston had an acrimonious relationship, Pitt said in a February 2009 interview that he and Aniston "check in with each other", adding that they were both big parts of each other's lives.

During Pitt's divorce proceedings, his involvement with his *Mr. & Mrs. Smith* co-star [Angelina Jolie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelina_Jolie "Angelina Jolie") attracted media attention. Jolie and Pitt stated that they fell in love on the set and that there was no infidelity. In April 2005, one month after Aniston filed for divorce, a set of paparazzi photographs emerged showing Pitt, Jolie, and her son Maddox at a beach in [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya "Kenya"); the press interpreted the pictures as evidence of a relationship between Pitt and Jolie. Throughout 2005, the two were seen together with increasing frequency, and the entertainment media dubbed the couple "[Brangelina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brangelina "Brangelina")". During the ensuing media circus, shirts with the text "Team Jolie" and "Team Aniston" became a fashion trend. On January 11, 2006, Jolie confirmed to *People* that she was pregnant with Pitt's child, thereby publicly acknowledging their relationship for the first time. Pitt and Jolie announced their engagement in April 2012 after seven years together. They were legally married on August 14, 2014, and had their wedding in a private ceremony at the [Château Miraval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C3%A2teau_Miraval,_Correns-Var "Château Miraval, Correns-Var"), France, on August 23, 2014. On September 19, 2016, Jolie filed for divorce from Pitt, citing irreconcilable differences. On April 12, 2019, the divorce became legal. After Pitt sued Jolie for selling her share of a winery they owned to a third party, she filed a countersuit, in which she alleged that he physically and verbally abused her and their children in an outburst while on a plane in September 2016.

In 2022, he began dating Ines de Ramon (b. 1992), a jewelry executive and the ex-wife of actor [Paul Wesley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Wesley "Paul Wesley").

### Children

In July 2005, Pitt accompanied Jolie to [Addis Ababa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addis_Ababa "Addis Ababa"), Ethiopia, where she adopted her second child, a girl named Zahara. On December 3, 2005, Pitt was in the process of becoming the adoptive father of Jolie's daughter, and Jolie's first adopted child, a boy named Maddox. On January 19, 2006, a California judge granted Jolie's request to change the children's surnames from "Jolie" to "Jolie-Pitt". The adoptions were finalized soon after.

Jolie gave birth to their daughter Shiloh in [Swakopmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swakopmund "Swakopmund"), Namibia, on May 27, 2006. Pitt confirmed that she would qualify for a Namibian passport. The couple sold the first pictures of their daughter through the distributor [Getty Images](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getty_Images "Getty Images"); the North American rights were purchased by *People* for over $4.1 million, while *[Hello!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hello!_(magazine) "Hello! (magazine)")* obtained the British rights for approximately $3.5 million. The proceeds from the sale were donated to charities serving African children. [Madame Tussauds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madame_Tussauds "Madame Tussauds") in New York unveiled a [wax figure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wax_figure "Wax figure") of the two-month-old; it marked the first time an infant had been honoured with a statue in the museum.

On March 15, 2007, Jolie adopted a 3-year-old boy, Pax, from an orphanage in [Ho Chi Minh City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_City "Ho Chi Minh City"), Vietnam. Pitt adopted the boy in the United States on February 21, 2008. At the [Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Cannes_Film_Festival "2008 Cannes Film Festival") in May 2008, Jolie confirmed that she was expecting twins. She gave birth to son and daughter, Knox and Vivienne, on July 12, 2008, in [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice "Nice"), France. The rights for the first images of the twins were jointly sold to *People* and *Hello!* for $14 million—[the most expensive celebrity pictures ever taken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_expensive_celebrity_photographs "List of most expensive celebrity photographs"). The couple donated the proceeds to the Jolie-Pitt Foundation.

Since Jolie filed for divorce from Pitt on September 19, 2016, they have been embroiled in a custody battle over their children. Jolie had full custody until May 2021 when Pitt was granted joint custody, over four and a half years after proceedings began. However, in July, Los Angeles superior court judge John W. Ouderkirk was removed from the case due to concerns over his impartiality as he did not sufficiently disclose business relationships with Pitt's lawyers. This resulted in the custody arrangement reverting to a previous November 2018 agreement where Jolie has primary physical custody while Pitt has "custodial time" with their minor children.

In 2024, Pitt's then 18-year-old second-eldest daughter with Jolie changed her name legally from Shiloh Nouvel Jolie-Pitt to Shiloh Nouvel Jolie. In 2026, Maddox Chivan Jolie, Zahara Marley Jolie and Vivienne Marcheline Jolie filed paperwork to legally drop Pitt from their last names.

### Religion

Pitt was raised in a conservative Christian household in [Springfield, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springfield,_Missouri "Springfield, Missouri"), and brought up in the [Southern Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Baptist_Convention "Southern Baptist Convention") tradition. He attended church regularly during his childhood but later became critical of organized religion and its doctrines.

He distanced himself from institutional religion in adulthood, and described his beliefs as evolving. He identified at various times as [agnostic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnosticism "Agnosticism") and [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism "Atheism"), once stating that he was "probably 20 percent atheist and 80 percent agnostic," reflecting skepticism toward traditional religious claims. He stated in later interviews that his earlier atheism was not a fixed position and that his views had continued to change over time. He described his spirituality as personal and experiential rather than tied to any organized religious system.

He has shown interest in spiritual and philosophical traditions associated with Buddhism. His role in the film *[Seven Years in Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years_in_Tibet_(1997_film) "Seven Years in Tibet (1997 film)")* (1997) contributed to public discussion of [Tibetan Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism "Tibetan Buddhism") in Western popular culture, with commentary noting the film's emphasis on Buddhist themes and imagery.
He appeared on the cover of an October 1997 issue of *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")* magazine in a religion-themed issue that referenced Buddhism in connection with his work and public image. He visited a [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo "Tokyo") [Buddhist temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_temple "Buddhist temple") in 2022, and participated in a prayer ritual during the promotion of his film *[Bullet Train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullet_Train_(film) "Bullet Train (film)")*.

### Abuse allegation

In September 2016, the [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI "FBI") and the Los Angeles Department of Children and Family Services investigated Pitt for [child abuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_abuse "Child abuse") following an incident on a plane, where Pitt was accused by an anonymous person of being "verbally abusive" and "physical" towards one of his children. Pitt was not criminally charged for the incident by the Los Angeles County Department of Children and Family Services, or by the FBI.

In 2022, Jolie filed a countersuit against Pitt during a legal battle over their formerly co-owned vineyard. Jolie's lawyers stated that during negotiations, Pitt had asked Jolie to sign "a [nondisclosure agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondisclosure_agreement "Nondisclosure agreement") prohibiting her from speaking outside of court about his abuse of her and their children." Her complaint stated that he grabbed and shook her during the plane ride, [strangled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strangulation_in_domestic_violence "Strangulation in domestic violence") one of their children, and struck another in the face, among other allegations.

### Alcohol and drug use

In September 2016, Pitt got sober and began attending [Alcoholics Anonymous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholics_Anonymous "Alcoholics Anonymous") meetings. In December 2019, he wrote an article for *[Interview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interview_(magazine) "Interview (magazine)")* in which he talked with his *Legends of the Fall* and *Meet Joe Black* costar and fellow recovering alcoholic [Anthony Hopkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Hopkins "Anthony Hopkins") about their experiences with addiction and recovery. Pitt credits fellow actor [Bradley Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradley_Cooper "Bradley Cooper") with helping him in his sobriety. In an interview with *[Esquire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esquire_Magazine "Esquire Magazine")* in August 2026, Pitt said that he had been sober for seven years before beginning to drink again "in a more restrained manner."

Pitt has admitted to using [cannabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis "Cannabis") in the late 1990s as a way to deal with his increasing fame. According to Pitt: "I was hiding out from the celebrity thing; I was smoking way too much dope; I was sitting on the couch and just turning into a doughnut." He reduced his cannabis use and focused on his work after a trip to Morocco, where he witnessed extreme poverty and suffering.

### Face blindness

In 2022, Pitt said that he had struggled for years to recognize people's faces due to [prosopagnosia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosopagnosia "Prosopagnosia") (face blindness), for which he has not been formally diagnosed. In a 2013 interview, he said that his inability to recognize people's faces had become so severe that he often wanted to stay home.

### Artwork

Pitt has an interest in art, learned pottery, and has created sculptures. Nine of his sculptures were exhibited together with works by musician [Nick Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Cave "Nick Cave") and artist [Thomas Houseago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Houseago "Thomas Houseago") at the Sara Hildén Art Museum in [Tampere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampere "Tampere"), Finland, in 2022 and 2023.

### Business ventures

In 2022, Pitt co-founded the skincare products company Beau Domaine.

## Public image

Pitt has been frequently cited as one of the most attractive actors of all time and has been described as a [sex symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_symbol "Sex symbol") by many sources, including *[Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_(film_magazine) "Empire (film magazine)")*, who named him one of the 25 sexiest stars in film history in 1995. The same year, he was named *People*'s [Sexiest Man Alive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexiest_Man_Alive "Sexiest Man Alive"), an accolade he received again in 2000. Pitt appeared on *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes "Forbes")*' annual [Celebrity 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_Celebrity_100 "Forbes Celebrity 100") list of the 100 most powerful celebrities from 2006 to 2008 placing at numbers 20, 5 and 10, respectively. In 2007, he appeared on the [*Time* 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100 "Time 100") list, a compilation of the 100 most influential people in the world, as selected annually by *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")* magazine. The magazine credited Pitt for using "his star power to get people to look at places and stories that cameras don't usually catch". He was again included on the *Time* 100 in 2009, this time in the "Builders and Titans" list. As of 2025[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Brad_Pitt&action=edit), Pitt's films have grossed over $7.5 billion worldwide, according to *[The Numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Numbers_(website) "The Numbers (website)")*.

Beginning in 2005, Pitt's relationship with Angelina Jolie became one of the world's most reported celebrity stories. After Jolie was confirmed to be pregnant in early 2006, the intense media hype surrounding the couple reached what [Reuters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuters "Reuters"), in a story titled "The Brangelina fever," called "the point of insanity". To avoid media attention, the couple flew to [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia "Namibia") for the birth of their daughter, which was described by a paparazzi blog as "the most anticipated baby since Jesus Christ." Similarly, intense media interest greeted the announcement of Jolie's second pregnancy two years later; for the two weeks Jolie spent in a seaside hospital in [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice "Nice"), reporters and photographers camped outside on the promenade to report on the birth.

In a 2006 global industry survey by [ACNielsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACNielsen "ACNielsen") in 42 international markets, Pitt, together with Jolie, were found to be the favorite celebrity endorsers for brands and products worldwide. Pitt has appeared in several television commercials. For the U.S. market, he starred in a [Heineken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heineken_Pilsener "Heineken Pilsener") commercial aired during the [2005 Super Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXIX "Super Bowl XXXIX"); it was directed by David Fincher, who had directed Pitt in *Seven* and *Fight Club*. Other commercial appearances came in television spots including [Acura Integra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Integra "Honda Integra"), in which he was featured opposite Russian model [Tatiana Sorokko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatiana_Sorokko "Tatiana Sorokko"), as well as [SoftBank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SoftBank "SoftBank"), and [Edwin Jeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_(clothing_brand) "Edwin (clothing brand)"). On June 2, 2015, the [minor planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_planet "Minor planet") 29132 Bradpitt was named in his honor.

Pitt does not use [social media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_media "Social media") and has no accounts on any social media platform.
