# Sarah Paulson

**Sarah Catharine Paulson** (born December 17, 1974) is an American actress. Known for her [performances on stage and screen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Paulson_on_screen_and_stage "Sarah Paulson on screen and stage"), she has received [numerous accolades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Sarah_Paulson "List of awards and nominations received by Sarah Paulson"), including an [Actor Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actor_Award "Actor Award"), a [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award "Golden Globe Award"), a [Primetime Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award "Primetime Emmy Award"), and a [Tony Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award "Tony Award").

Paulson began her acting career starring in the television series *[American Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Gothic_(1995_TV_series) "American Gothic (1995 TV series)")* (1995–1996) and *[Jack & Jill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_%26_Jill_(TV_series) "Jack & Jill (TV series)")* (1999–2001). She played [Harriet Hayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harriet_Hayes "Harriet Hayes") in the [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC "NBC") drama series *[Studio 60 on the Sunset Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio_60_on_the_Sunset_Strip "Studio 60 on the Sunset Strip")* (2006–2007). Paulson gained fame for her collaborations with showrunner [Ryan Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Murphy_(producer) "Ryan Murphy (producer)"), notably starring in ten seasons of his [anthology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthology_series "Anthology series") series *[American Horror Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story "American Horror Story")* from 2011 to 2026.

She is also known for her portrayal of [Marcia Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcia_Clark "Marcia Clark") in the [FX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FX_(TV_channel) "FX (TV channel)") limited series *[The People v. O. J. Simpson: American Crime Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_People_v._O._J._Simpson:_American_Crime_Story "The People v. O. J. Simpson: American Crime Story")* (2016), her roles as [Nicolle Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolle_Wallace "Nicolle Wallace") in the [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO "HBO") television film *[Game Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Change_(film) "Game Change (film)")* (2012) and [Linda Tripp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Tripp "Linda Tripp") in the FX miniseries *[Impeachment: American Crime Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeachment:_American_Crime_Story "Impeachment: American Crime Story")* (2021). She has also played a conservative activist in the [FX on Hulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FX_on_Hulu "FX on Hulu") limited series *[Mrs. America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mrs._America_(miniseries) "Mrs. America (miniseries)")* (2020) and [Nurse Ratched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nurse_Ratched "Nurse Ratched") in the [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") thriller series *[Ratched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratched_(TV_series) "Ratched (TV series)")* (2020).

On film, she has had leading roles in *[Blue Jay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Jay_(film) "Blue Jay (film)")* (2016), *[Glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass_(2019_film) "Glass (2019 film)")* (2019), *[Run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Run_(2020_American_film) "Run (2020 American film)")* (2020), and *[Hold Your Breath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hold_Your_Breath_(2024_film) "Hold Your Breath (2024 film)")* (2024), as well as supporting roles in *[What Women Want](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_Women_Want "What Women Want")* (2000), *[Down with Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Down_with_Love "Down with Love")* (2003), *[Martha Marcy May Marlene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Marcy_May_Marlene "Martha Marcy May Marlene")* (2011), *[Mud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mud_(2012_film) "Mud (2012 film)")* (2012), *[12 Years a Slave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12_Years_a_Slave_(film) "12 Years a Slave (film)")* (2013), *[Carol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_(film) "Carol (film)")* (2015), *[The Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Post_(film) "The Post (film)")* (2017), *[Ocean's 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean%27s_8 "Ocean's 8")* (2018), and *[Bird Box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_Box_(film) "Bird Box (film)")* (2018). On [Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_theatre "Broadway theatre"), she acted in the [Tennessee Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_Williams "Tennessee Williams") revival *[The Glass Menagerie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Glass_Menagerie "The Glass Menagerie")* (2005), the [Donald Margulies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Margulies "Donald Margulies") play *[Collected Stories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collected_Stories_(play) "Collected Stories (play)")* (2010), and the [Branden Jacobs-Jenkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branden_Jacobs-Jenkins "Branden Jacobs-Jenkins") play *[Appropriate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appropriate_(play) "Appropriate (play)")* (2023).

## Early life and education

Sarah Catharine Paulson was born in [Tampa, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa,_Florida "Tampa, Florida"), on December 17, 1974, the daughter of Catharine Gordon (née Dolcater) and Douglas Lyle Paulson II. She spent her early life in [South Tampa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Tampa "South Tampa") until her parents' divorce when she was five. After her parents' separation, she relocated with her mother and sister to [Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine "Maine"), then to New York City. Her mother worked as a waitress, and Paulson lived in [Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens "Queens") and [Gramercy Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gramercy_Park "Gramercy Park") before settling in [Park Slope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Slope "Park Slope").

She recalled of this period, "My mom was 27 years old [when we moved]. She didn't know a single person in New York City. She got a job at [Sardi's Restaurant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardi%27s "Sardi's")." Throughout her childhood, Paulson spent her summers in Florida with her father, who was an executive at a Tampa door manufacturing company. She attended [P.S. 29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_public_elementary_schools_in_New_York_City "List of public elementary schools in New York City") and [Berkeley Carroll School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley_Carroll_School "Berkeley Carroll School") in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn "Brooklyn") before attending [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan "Manhattan")'s [Fiorello H. LaGuardia High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiorello_H._LaGuardia_High_School "Fiorello H. LaGuardia High School") and the [American Academy of Dramatic Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Dramatic_Arts "American Academy of Dramatic Arts").

## Career

### 1994–2007: Early roles and career beginnings

Paulson began working as an actress immediately after high school. She made her [Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_theatre "Broadway theatre") debut in 1994 as a replacement understudy for the role of Tessin, played by Amy Ryan in [Wendy Wasserstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendy_Wasserstein "Wendy Wasserstein")'s play *[The Sisters Rosensweig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sisters_Rosensweig "The Sisters Rosensweig")* at the [Ethel Barrymore Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethel_Barrymore_Theatre "Ethel Barrymore Theatre"). She appeared in the [Horton Foote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horton_Foote "Horton Foote") play *Talking Pictures* at the [Signature Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signature_Theatre_Company "Signature Theatre Company"), and in an episode of *[Law & Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_%26_Order "Law & Order")* in 1994. The next year, Paulson appeared in the [Hallmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallmark "Hallmark") television film *Friends at Last* (1995) opposite [Kathleen Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Turner "Kathleen Turner"), playing the adult version of Turner's character's daughter. She also starred in the short-lived television series *[American Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Gothic_(1995_TV_series) "American Gothic (1995 TV series)")* (also 1995), playing the ghost of a murdered woman.

In 1997, Paulson made her feature film debut in the independent thriller film *[Levitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levitation_(film) "Levitation (film)")*, playing a woman who discovers she is pregnant after an anonymous sexual encounter. Leonard Klady of *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* noted that Paulson and her co-stars are "not supported by the script", concluding: "*Levitation* is a grim, convoluted saga of identity and belonging. An ill-fitting combination of melodrama and [magic realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_realism "Magic realism"), the indie effort will have a decidedly difficult time in the theatrical arena." In 1997, Paulson was a featured actress (Janice/Nina) in the two-part episode "True Romance" of *[Cracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cracker_(American_TV_series) "Cracker (American TV series)")*, which starred Robert Pastorelli. In 1998, she returned to the stage acting in the [Off-Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-Broadway "Off-Broadway") production of *[Killer Joe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killer_Joe_(play) "Killer Joe (play)")* (1998).

In 1999, she played Elisa Cronkite in [The WB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_WB "The WB") comedy-drama series *[Jack & Jill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_%26_Jill_(TV_series) "Jack & Jill (TV series)")*. The same year, she was also cast opposite [Juliette Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juliette_Lewis "Juliette Lewis") and [Diane Keaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diane_Keaton "Diane Keaton") in the drama *[The Other Sister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Other_Sister "The Other Sister")*, playing the [lesbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesbian "Lesbian") sister of a [developmentally-disabled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developmental_disability "Developmental disability") woman in [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco"), and in a supporting part playing a hostage in the comedy *[Held Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Held_Up "Held Up")*, opposite [Jamie Foxx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Foxx "Jamie Foxx"). In 2000, she had a small supporting role in the [Nancy Meyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Meyers "Nancy Meyers")–directed romantic comedy *[What Women Want](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_Women_Want "What Women Want")*, starring [Mel Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mel_Gibson "Mel Gibson") and [Helen Hunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Hunt "Helen Hunt").

She portrayed [Luci Baines Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luci_Baines_Johnson "Luci Baines Johnson") in the [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO "HBO") film *[Path to War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_to_War "Path to War")* (2002) starring [Michael Gambon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Gambon "Michael Gambon"), [Donald Sutherland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Sutherland "Donald Sutherland") and [Alec Baldwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alec_Baldwin "Alec Baldwin"). That same year she starred as the main character, Faith Wardwell, an advertising executive, in the [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC "NBC") series *[Leap of Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leap_of_Faith_(TV_series) "Leap of Faith (TV series)")* (2002). Phil Gallo compared the show unfavorably to *[Sex and the City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_and_the_City "Sex and the City")*. She was then cast in the period romantic comedy *[Down with Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Down_with_Love "Down with Love")* (2003) in a central supporting role, portraying the friend and editor of a writer (portrayed by [Renee Zellweger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renee_Zellweger "Renee Zellweger")). Paulson had a minor recurring role in the HBO series *[Deadwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadwood_(TV_series) "Deadwood (TV series)")* (2005) and was a focal character in a 2004 episode of the [FX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FX_Networks "FX Networks") series *[Nip/Tuck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nip/Tuck "Nip/Tuck")*.

In 2004, she had a supporting role in the [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company "American Broadcasting Company") series *The D.A.*, which was cancelled after only four episodes. In the spring of 2005, Paulson starred in a [Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_theatre "Broadway theatre") revival of the [Tennessee Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_Williams "Tennessee Williams") play *[The Glass Menagerie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Glass_Menagerie "The Glass Menagerie")* opposite co-star [Jessica Lange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Lange "Jessica Lange"). [Ben Brantley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Brantley "Ben Brantley") of *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* deemed the production as "misdirected and miscast ... reality never makes an appearance in this surreally blurred production." Later that year, Paulson appeared Off-Broadway in a production of *Colder Than Here*, opposite [Lily Rabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lily_Rabe "Lily Rabe") (also her future co-star on *American Horror Story*). The production received an unfavorable review from *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")*, with critic Dave Rooney writing: "Rabe speaks in an affected monotone while Sarah Paulson has the measured, upward-inflected delivery of a children's TV presenter ... this mannered, melancholy play elicits a mainly impassive response, which is no small obstruction in a work dealing with loss."

Also in 2005, Paulson had a small role in the [Joss Whedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joss_Whedon "Joss Whedon")-directed science fiction film *[Serenity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serenity_(2005_film) "Serenity (2005 film)")*. In the 2006–07 television season, Paulson co-starred in the [Aaron Sorkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Sorkin "Aaron Sorkin") created [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC "NBC") comedy-drama *[Studio 60 on the Sunset Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio_60_on_the_Sunset_Strip "Studio 60 on the Sunset Strip")*, playing [Harriet Hayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harriet_Hayes "Harriet Hayes"), one of the stars of the show-within-a-show. This role earned her a nomination for [Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actress – Series, Miniseries or Television Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress_%E2%80%93_Series,_Miniseries_or_Television_Film "Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actress – Series, Miniseries or Television Film"). In December 2008, Paulson appeared in the [screen adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Spirit_(2008_film) "The Spirit (2008 film)") of [Will Eisner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Eisner "Will Eisner")'s comic book *[The Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Spirit_(character) "The Spirit (character)")*, playing an updated version of the character [Ellen Dolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellen_Dolan_(character) "Ellen Dolan (character)").

### 2008–2015: Breakthrough and critical acclaim

In 2008, [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company "American Broadcasting Company") cast Paulson in the pilot *[Cupid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cupid_(2009_TV_series) "Cupid (2009 TV series)")*, which was ordered to series. It was a remake of the [1998 series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cupid_(1998_TV_series) "Cupid (1998 TV series)") starring [Jeremy Piven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Piven "Jeremy Piven") and [Paula Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paula_Marshall "Paula Marshall"). In the new version, Paulson starred opposite [Bobby Cannavale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Cannavale "Bobby Cannavale"). It debuted in late March 2009 on ABC but was cancelled on May 19, 2009, after six episodes. In February 2010, Paulson was cast as the [circa](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/circa "wikt:circa") 1982 mother of main character [Meredith Grey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meredith_Grey "Meredith Grey"), on the ABC drama *[Grey's Anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey%27s_Anatomy "Grey's Anatomy")*, appearing in a flashback sequence in a season-six episode. She then played [Nicolle Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolle_Wallace "Nicolle Wallace") in the [HBO film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO_Films "HBO Films") *[Game Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Change_(film) "Game Change (film)")* (2012), based on events of the [2008 U.S. presidential election campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election "2008 United States presidential election"). For her performance, she earned [Primetime Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award "Primetime Emmy Award") and [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award "Golden Globe Award") nominations.

In the spring of 2010, she starred in the [Donald Margulies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Margulies "Donald Margulies") play *[Collected Stories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collected_Stories_(play) "Collected Stories (play)")* alongside [Linda Lavin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Lavin "Linda Lavin") at the [Samuel J. Friedman Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_J._Friedman_Theatre "Samuel J. Friedman Theatre") on Broadway. The same year, Paulson filmed the independent drama *[Martha Marcy May Marlene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Marcy_May_Marlene "Martha Marcy May Marlene")*, in which she starred opposite [Elizabeth Olsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Olsen "Elizabeth Olsen") and [Hugh Dancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Dancy "Hugh Dancy"), portraying the wealthy sister of a woman who has escaped a [cult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult "Cult"). The film was released in the fall of 2011. Simultaneously, Paulson guest-starred in three episodes of the [FX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FX_(TV_channel) "FX (TV channel)") [anthology series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthology_series "Anthology series") *[American Horror Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story "American Horror Story")*, playing medium Billie Dean Howard.

In 2012, Paulson returned for season two, *[American Horror Story: Asylum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story:_Asylum "American Horror Story: Asylum")*, in which she played a new character, Lana Winters, a writer who is committed to an asylum for being a [lesbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesbian "Lesbian"). For her performance she was nominated for the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Supporting Actress in a Limited Series or Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Supporting_Actress_in_a_Limited_or_Anthology_Series_or_Movie "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Supporting Actress in a Limited or Anthology Series or Movie") at the [65th Primetime Emmy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/65th_Primetime_Emmy_Awards "65th Primetime Emmy Awards"). During this time, she also played the supporting role of Mary Lee in the acclaimed [Jeff Nichols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Nichols "Jeff Nichols") drama film *[Mud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mud_(2012_film) "Mud (2012 film)")* (2012), starring [Matthew McConaughey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_McConaughey "Matthew McConaughey").

Paulson returned to theater in March 2013, appearing in an Off-Broadway production of the [Lanford Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanford_Wilson "Lanford Wilson") play *[Talley's Folly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talley%27s_Folly "Talley's Folly")* opposite [Danny Burstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Burstein "Danny Burstein"). She then starred in the third season of *American Horror Story*, titled *[Coven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story:_Coven "American Horror Story: Coven")* (2013) as [Cordelia Foxx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordelia_Foxx "Cordelia Foxx"), a witch who runs an academy for other young witches. The same year, she starred as Mary Epps, an abusive slave-owner, in the [Steve McQueen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_McQueen_(director) "Steve McQueen (director)") directed historical drama film *[12 Years a Slave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12_Years_a_Slave_(film) "12 Years a Slave (film)")*. The film was a critical success, earning numerous accolades. She was nominated along with the ensemble for the [Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Cast in a Motion Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Cast_in_a_Motion_Picture "Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Cast in a Motion Picture").

In 2014, Paulson appeared in the fourth season of series of *American Horror Story*, titled *[Freak Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story:_Freak_Show "American Horror Story: Freak Show")*, playing the roles of [conjoined twin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjoined_twin "Conjoined twin") sisters Bette and Dot Tattler, who are members of a circus [freak show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freak_show "Freak show"). She returned for the fifth season, subtitled *[Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story:_Hotel "American Horror Story: Hotel")*, in the role of Hypodermic Sally, the ghost of a drug addict trapped in a Hollywood hotel. For her roles in *Freak Show* and *Hotel* she received nominations for the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Supporting Actress in a Limited or Anthology Series or Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Supporting_Actress_in_a_Limited_or_Anthology_Series_or_Movie "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Supporting Actress in a Limited or Anthology Series or Movie") in 2014 and 2016. She also reprised the character of Billie Dean Howard in the last episode of the season, making a [crossover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossover_(fiction) "Crossover (fiction)") appearance. During this time, Paulson also took on the role of Abby Gerhard in the [Todd Haynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Haynes "Todd Haynes")-directed romantic drama *[Carol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_(film) "Carol (film)")* (2015), a period piece in which she played the supporting role of [Cate Blanchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett "Cate Blanchett")'s close friend.

### 2016–present: Career expansion and return to Broadway

Beginning in February 2016, Paulson starred in the first season of the true crime [anthology series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthology_series "Anthology series") *[American Crime Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Crime_Story "American Crime Story")*, subtitled *[The People v. O.J. Simpson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_People_v._O._J._Simpson:_American_Crime_Story "The People v. O. J. Simpson: American Crime Story")*, portraying prosecutor [Marcia Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcia_Clark "Marcia Clark"). Maureen Ryan of *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* wrote, "Marcia Clark's summation was clipped, precise and underlined by the simmering, quietly controlled anger that Sarah Paulson brought to the role. She was framed with geometric precision and squared edges." She garnered widespread critical acclaim for her performance and won numerous awards, the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Limited Series or Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actress_in_a_Limited_Series_or_Movie "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Limited Series or Movie") as well as the [Screen Actors Guild Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actor_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Female_Actor_in_a_Miniseries_or_Television_Movie "Actor Award for Outstanding Performance by a Female Actor in a Miniseries or Television Movie"), [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_%E2%80%93_Miniseries_or_Television_Film "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress – Miniseries or Television Film"), [Critics' Choice Television Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Television_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Movie/Miniseries "Critics' Choice Television Award for Best Actress in a Movie/Miniseries"), and [TCA Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TCA_Award_for_Individual_Achievement_in_Drama "TCA Award for Individual Achievement in Drama") for the role.

In September 2016, she starred opposite [Mark Duplass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Duplass "Mark Duplass") in [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") romantic drama *[Blue Jay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Jay_(film) "Blue Jay (film)")* (2016) which premiered at the [Toronto International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_International_Film_Festival "Toronto International Film Festival"). Jon Frosch of *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")* wrote of her performance, "If the film proves more stirring than you expect, it’s thanks to Paulson, who, with her crisp delivery and teasing eyes welling up with confusion and heartache, gives Amanda an inner life far beyond what's written for her."

In June 2016, the [Human Rights Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Campaign "Human Rights Campaign") released a video in tribute to the victims of the [Orlando nightclub shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_nightclub_shooting "Orlando nightclub shooting"); in the video, Paulson and others told the stories of the people killed there. In the fall of 2016, she starred in the sixth iteration of *American Horror Story*, subtitled *[Roanoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story:_Roanoke "American Horror Story: Roanoke")*; in it, she was cast in the dual roles of British actress Audrey Tindall and tortured wife and yoga instructor Shelby Miller, the latter of whom is portrayed by Tindall's character in *My Roanoke Nightmare*, a [documentary within the series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Story_within_a_story "Story within a story"). She also reprised her role of Lana Winters in the final episode of *Roanoke*, in which the character makes a crossover appearance.

After completing *Roanoke*, Paulson appeared in the series' seventh season, *[Cult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story:_Cult "American Horror Story: Cult")* (2017), in which she played restaurant owner Ally Mayfair-Richards, and Susan Atkins in the 10th episode of the season. She starred as [Geraldine Page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geraldine_Page "Geraldine Page") in one episode of the first season of drama anthology series *[Feud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feud_(American_TV_series) "Feud (American TV series)")* (2017), which chronicles the turbulent working relationship between actresses [Bette Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bette_Davis "Bette Davis") and [Joan Crawford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Crawford "Joan Crawford").

Paulson was then cast in the heist film *[Ocean's 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean%27s_8 "Ocean's 8")* (2018), co-starring with [Cate Blanchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett "Cate Blanchett"), [Sandra Bullock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_Bullock "Sandra Bullock"), [Anne Hathaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hathaway "Anne Hathaway"), [Mindy Kaling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindy_Kaling "Mindy Kaling"), [Awkwafina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awkwafina "Awkwafina"), and [Rihanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rihanna "Rihanna"). The film was a commercial success, grossing nearly $300 million at the worldwide box office. In 2017, she was invited to join the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences "Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences") and named by *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")* as one of the [100 most influential people in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100 "Time 100"). In 2018, Paulson was ranked one of the best dressed women by fashion website [Net-a-Porter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YOOX_Net-a-Porter_Group "YOOX Net-a-Porter Group"). Paulson returned for the eighth season of *American Horror Story*, titled *[Apocalypse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story:_Apocalypse "American Horror Story: Apocalypse")*, which premiered on September 12, 2018. In *Apocalypse*, Paulson reprised both the *Murder House* and *Coven* roles of Billie Dean Howard and Cordelia Foxx, respectively, and also starred as the villainous Miss Wilhemina Venable. In addition to appearing as three characters, Paulson also directed one of the season's episodes, marking her directorial debut. She played Sandra Bullock's character's sister, Jessica, in the drama horror film *[Bird Box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_Box_(film) "Bird Box (film)")* (2018), which was released on [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") in December.

In 2019, Paulson starred as Dr. Ellie Staple in the superhero thriller film *[Glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass_(2019_film) "Glass (2019 film)")*, Xandra in the drama film *[The Goldfinch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Goldfinch_(film) "The Goldfinch (film)")*, and Dr. Zara in the animated adventure film *[Abominable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abominable_(2019_film) "Abominable (2019 film)")*. Paulson then starred as Alice Macray in the [FX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FX_(TV_channel) "FX (TV channel)") limited drama series *[Mrs. America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mrs._America_(miniseries) "Mrs. America (miniseries)")* (2020) about the unsuccessful political movement to pass the [Equal Rights Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal_Rights_Amendment "Equal Rights Amendment"). Paulson acted opposite Cate Blanchett, [Rose Byrne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Byrne "Rose Byrne"), [Uzo Aduba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzo_Aduba "Uzo Aduba"), [Melanie Lynskey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanie_Lynskey "Melanie Lynskey") and [Margo Martindale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margo_Martindale "Margo Martindale"). She also starred as Clarissa Montgomery in the [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO "HBO") satirical comedy television film *[Coastal Elites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Elites "Coastal Elites")*, which premiered in September 2020.

In September 2017, it was announced that Paulson would star as a younger version of [Nurse Mildred Ratched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nurse_Ratched "Nurse Ratched"), the villain of the novel *[One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Flew_Over_the_Cuckoo%27s_Nest_(novel) "One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest (novel)")* and its [acclaimed 1975 film adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Flew_Over_the_Cuckoo%27s_Nest_(film) "One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest (film)"), in the [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") drama series *[Ratched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratched_(TV_series) "Ratched (TV series)")*, a [prequel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prequel "Prequel") to the novel which would portray the character's origins. The first season was released on September 18, 2020. For her performance she was nominated for the [Golden Globe Award for Best Actress – Television Series Drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_%E2%80%93_Television_Series_Drama "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress – Television Series Drama"). In November 2020, Paulson starred in the psychological thriller film *[Run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Run_(2020_American_film) "Run (2020 American film)")*, opposite [Kiera Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiera_Allen "Kiera Allen"). It became the most watched original film on the streaming platform [Hulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulu "Hulu").

In 2021, she portrayed [Linda Tripp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Tripp "Linda Tripp") in the third season of the true crime anthology series *American Crime Story*, subtitled *[Impeachment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeachment:_American_Crime_Story "Impeachment: American Crime Story")*. For her performance in the series, Paulson received a [Primetime Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actress_in_a_Limited_or_Anthology_Series_or_Movie "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Limited or Anthology Series or Movie") nomination. In 2021, she returned to *American Horror Story* for its [tenth season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story:_Double_Feature "American Horror Story: Double Feature"), after being absent for the [ninth season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story:_1984 "American Horror Story: 1984"). She portrayed the character Tuberculosis Karen and a fictional version of former [First Lady of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Lady_of_the_United_States "First Lady of the United States"), [Mamie Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamie_Eisenhower "Mamie Eisenhower").

In February 2022, it was announced that Paulson had been tapped to portray author [Glennon Doyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glennon_Doyle "Glennon Doyle") in a television series based on Doyle's memoir, *[Untamed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Untamed_(memoir) "Untamed (memoir)")*. In 2023, she guest-starred in the [Hulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulu "Hulu") comedy drama series *[The Bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bear_(TV_series) "The Bear (TV series)")*, playing Michelle Berzatto in the episode "[Fishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bear_(TV_series)#Season_2 "The Bear (TV series)")". Paulson returned to Broadway in 2023 to star in [Branden Jacobs-Jenkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branden_Jacobs-Jenkins "Branden Jacobs-Jenkins")'s play *[Appropriate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appropriate_(play) "Appropriate (play)")* acting alongside [Corey Stoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corey_Stoll "Corey Stoll"), [Natalie Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalie_Gold "Natalie Gold"), [Elle Fanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elle_Fanning "Elle Fanning"), and [Michael Esper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Esper "Michael Esper"). In the play she portrays Toni, the eldest sibling who tries to reconnect with her siblings after the death of their late father. While at their father's home in rural [Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas "Arkansas") they all discover his shocking past. Adrian Horton of *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* declared that Paulson was "the top reason among many to see this play" adding, "Part of the play's delight is relishing Paulson's delivery of some truly scathing burns". Paulson won the [Tony Award for Best Actress in a Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Play "Tony Award for Best Actress in a Play").

## Personal life

Paulson lives in Los Angeles. She is a supporter of the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)"). She was diagnosed with [melanoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanoma "Melanoma") on her back when she was 25, and the growth was removed before the cancer could spread.

Addressing her sexuality, Paulson called it "a [fluid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_fluidity "Sexual fluidity") situation" and later said, "If my life choices had to be predicated based on what was expected of me from a community on either side, that's going to make me feel really straitjacketed, and I don't want to feel that." She dated actress [Cherry Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry_Jones "Cherry Jones") from 2004 to 2009. She had dated only men before this relationship, including her former fiancé, playwright [Tracy Letts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracy_Letts "Tracy Letts").

In March 2016, Paulson confirmed during an interview that she had been dating actress [Holland Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holland_Taylor "Holland Taylor") since early 2015, after the two had met at a dinner party approximately in 2006. Paulson appeared on the red carpet at the [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Tony_Awards "2025 Tony Awards") and [2026 Tony Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Tony_Awards "2026 Tony Awards") with [Ella Beatty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ella_Beatty "Ella Beatty"), the daughter of [Warren Beatty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Beatty "Warren Beatty") and [Annette Bening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annette_Bening "Annette Bening").
