# The Buddha

**Siddhartha Gautama**, most commonly referred to as **the Buddha** (lit. 'the awakened one'), was a [wandering religious teacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Arama%E1%B9%87a "Śramaṇa") who lived in the eastern [Indo-Gangetic Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Gangetic_Plain "Indo-Gangetic Plain") during the 6th or 5th century [BCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Era "Common Era") and founded [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"). According to Buddhist legends, he was born in [Lumbini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumbini "Lumbini"), in what is now [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal "Nepal"), to royal parents of the [Shakya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakya "Shakya") clan, but [renounced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Renunciation "Great Renunciation") his [home life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Householder_(Buddhism) "Householder (Buddhism)") to live as a wandering [ascetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascetic "Ascetic"). After leading a life of [mendicancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendicancy "Mendicancy"), [asceticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asceticism "Asceticism"), and [meditation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meditation "Meditation"), he attained [nirvana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirvana_(Buddhism) "Nirvana (Buddhism)") at [Bodh Gaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodh_Gaya "Bodh Gaya") in what is now [Bihar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bihar "Bihar"), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India"). The Buddha then wandered through the lower Indo-Gangetic Plain, teaching and building a monastic order (*[sangha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangha "Sangha")*). Buddhist tradition holds he died in [Kushinagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushinagar "Kushinagar") (in modern day [Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttar_Pradesh "Uttar Pradesh"), India) and reached *[parinirvana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parinirvana "Parinirvana")* ("final release from conditioned existence").

According to Buddhist tradition, the Buddha taught a [Middle Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Way "Middle Way") between sensual indulgence and severe asceticism, leading to [freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vimutti "Vimutti") from [ignorance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avidy%C4%81_(Buddhism) "Avidyā (Buddhism)"), [craving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Up%C4%81d%C4%81na "Upādāna"), [rebirth, and suffering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa%E1%B9%83s%C4%81ra_(Buddhism) "Saṃsāra (Buddhism)"). His core teachings are summarised in the [Four Noble Truths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Noble_Truths "Four Noble Truths") and the [Noble Eightfold Path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noble_Eightfold_Path "Noble Eightfold Path"), a [training of the mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhavana "Bhavana") that includes [ethical training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_ethics "Buddhist ethics") and [kindness toward others](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmavihara "Brahmavihara"), and [meditative practices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_meditation "Buddhist meditation") such as [sense restraint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_effort "Right effort"), [mindfulness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sati_(Buddhism) "Sati (Buddhism)"), and [dhyana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhyana_in_Buddhism "Dhyana in Buddhism") (meditation proper). Another key element of his teachings are the concepts of the [five skandhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skandha "Skandha") and [dependent origination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dependent_origination "Dependent origination"), describing how all *dharmas* (both mental states and concrete 'things') come into being, and cease to be, depending on other *dharmas*, lacking an existence on their own (*[svabhava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svabhava "Svabhava")*).

While in the [Nikayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikayas "Nikayas"), he frequently refers to himself as the [Tathāgata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tath%C4%81gata "Tathāgata"); the earliest attestation of the title [Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddha_(title) "Buddha (title)") is from the 3rd century BCE, meaning 'Awakened One' or 'Enlightened One'. His teachings were compiled by the Buddhist community into the [Vinaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinaya "Vinaya") Piṭaka, containing codes for monastic discipline, and the [Sūtra Piṭaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutta_Pi%E1%B9%ADaka "Sutta Piṭaka"), a collection of discourses attributed to him. These were passed down in [Middle Indo-Aryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Indo-Aryan_languages "Middle Indo-Aryan languages") dialects through an [oral tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_tradition "Oral tradition"). Later generations composed additional texts, such as systematic treatises known as *[Abhidharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhidharma "Abhidharma")*, biographies of the Buddha, collections of stories about his past lives known as *[Jataka tales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jataka_tales "Jataka tales")*, and additional discourses, i.e., the [Mahāyāna sūtras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mah%C4%81y%C4%81na_s%C5%ABtras "Mahāyāna sūtras").

Buddhism evolved into a variety of traditions and practices, represented by [Theravāda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Therav%C4%81da "Theravāda"), [Mahāyāna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mah%C4%81y%C4%81na "Mahāyāna") and [Vajrayāna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vajray%C4%81na "Vajrayāna"), and spread beyond the Indian subcontinent. While Buddhism declined in India, and largely disappeared after the 8th century CE due to a loss of popular and economic support, it grew more prominent in [Southeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia") and [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia").

## Etymology, names and titles

### Siddhārtha Gautama and Buddha Shakyamuni

According to Donald Lopez Jr. "he tended to be known as either Buddha or Sakyamuni in China, Korea, Japan, and Tibet, and as either Gotama Buddha or Samana Gotama ('the ascetic Gotama') in Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia."

*Buddha*, "Awakened One" or "Enlightened One", is the masculine form of *[budh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhi "Buddhi")* (बुध्), "to wake, be awake, observe, heed, attend, learn, become aware of, to know, be conscious again", "to awaken" "'to open up' (as does a flower)", "one who has awakened from the deep sleep of ignorance and opened his consciousness to encompass all objects of knowledge". It is not a personal name, but a title for those who have attained [bodhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlightenment_in_Buddhism "Enlightenment in Buddhism") (awakening, enlightenment). *Buddhi*, the power to "form and retain concepts, reason, discern, judge, comprehend, understand", is the faculty which discerns truth (*[satya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satya "Satya")*) from falsehood.

The name of his clan was Gautama (Pali: Gotama). His given name, "Siddhārtha" (the Sanskrit form); the Pali rendering is "Siddhattha"; in Tibetan it is "Dhondup"; in Chinese "Xidaduo"; in Japanese "Shiddatta/Shittatta"; in Korean "Siltalta" means "He Who Achieves His Goal" ([Siddhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siddhi "Siddhi")). The clan name of [Gautama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautama_(name) "Gautama (name)") means "descendant of Gotama", "Gotama" meaning "one who has the most light", or "one who has most cows" and comes from the fact that [Kshatriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kshatriya "Kshatriya") clans adopted the names of their house priests.

While the term *Buddha* is used in the Agamas and the Pali Canon, the oldest surviving written records of the term *Buddha* is from the middle of the 3rd century BCE, when several [Edicts of Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edicts_of_Ashoka "Edicts of Ashoka") (reigned c. 269–232 BCE) mention the Buddha and Buddhism. [Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka "Ashoka")'s [Lumbini pillar inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumbini_pillar_inscription "Lumbini pillar inscription") commemorates the Emperor's pilgrimage to Lumbini as the Buddha's birthplace, calling him the *Buddha Shakyamuni*‍ ([Brahmi script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmi_script "Brahmi script"): 𑀩𑀼𑀥 [𑀲𑀓𑁆𑀬𑀫𑀼𑀦𑀻](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%F0%91%80%B2%F0%91%80%93%F0%91%81%86%F0%91%80%AC%F0%91%80%AB%F0%91%80%BC%F0%91%80%A6%F0%91%80%BB "wikt:𑀲𑀓𑁆𑀬𑀫𑀼𑀦𑀻") *Bu-dha Sa-kya-mu-nī*. *Śākyamuni,* *Sakyamuni,* or *Shakyamuni* ([Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit_language "Sanskrit language"): [शाक्यमुनि](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E0%A4%B6%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%A8%E0%A4%BF "wikt:शाक्यमुनि"), [[ɕaːkjɐmʊnɪ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Sanskrit "Help:IPA/Sanskrit")) means "Sage of the [Shakyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakya "Shakya")".

### Tathāgata

*Tathāgata* ([Pali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pali "Pali"); Pali: [[tɐˈtʰaːɡɐtɐ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Sanskrit "Help:IPA/Sanskrit")) is a term the Buddha commonly used when referring to himself or other Buddhas in the [Pāli Canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C4%81li_Canon "Pāli Canon"). The exact meaning of the term is unknown, but it is often thought to mean either "one who has thus gone" (*tathā-gata*), "one who has thus come" (*tathā-āgata*), or sometimes "one who has thus not gone" (*tathā-agata*). This is interpreted as signifying that the Tathāgata is beyond all coming and going—beyond all [transitory phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impermanence "Impermanence"). A *tathāgata* is "immeasurable", "inscrutable", "hard to fathom", and "not apprehended".

### Other epithets

A list of other [epithets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epithet "Epithet") is commonly seen together in canonical texts and depicts some of his perfected qualities:

* *Bhagavato ([Bhagavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhagavan "Bhagavan"))* – The Blessed one, one of the most used epithets, together with *tathāgata*
* *Sammasambuddho* – [Perfectly self-awakened](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhahood#Samyaksambuddha "Buddhahood")
* *Vijja-carana-sampano* – Endowed with higher knowledge and ideal conduct.
* *[Sugata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugata "Sugata")* – Well-gone or well-spoken.
* *Lokavidu* – Knower of the [many worlds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_cosmology "Buddhist cosmology").
* *Anuttaro Purisa-damma-sarathi* – Unexcelled trainer of untrained people.
* *Satthadeva-Manussanam* – Teacher of [gods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deva_(Buddhism) "Deva (Buddhism)") and humans.
* *Araham* – Worthy of homage. An [Arahant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arahant "Arahant") is "one with taints destroyed, who has lived the holy life, done what had to be done, laid down the burden, reached the true goal, destroyed the fetters of being, and is completely liberated through final knowledge".
* *[Jina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jina_(Jainism) "Jina (Jainism)")* – Conqueror. Although the term is more commonly used to name an individual who has attained [liberation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moksha "Moksha") in the religion [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism "Jainism"), it is also an alternative title for the Buddha.

The [Pali Canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pali_Canon "Pali Canon") also contains numerous other titles and epithets for the Buddha, including: All-seeing, All-transcending sage, Bull among men, The Caravan leader, Dispeller of darkness, The Eye, Foremost of charioteers, Foremost of those who can cross, King of the Dharma (*Dharmaraja*), Kinsman of the Sun, Helper of the World (*Lokanatha*), Lion (*Siha*), Lord of the Dhamma, Of excellent wisdom (*Varapañña*), Radiant One, Torchbearer of mankind, Unsurpassed doctor and surgeon, Victor in battle, and Wielder of power. Another epithet, used at inscriptions throughout South and Southeast Asia, is *Maha sramana*, "great *sramana*" (ascetic, renunciate).
