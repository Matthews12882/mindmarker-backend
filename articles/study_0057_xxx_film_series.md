# XXX (film series)

***XXX*** (stylized as ***xXx*** and pronounced ***Triple X***) is an American [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_film "Action film") [spy film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spy_film "Spy film") series created by [Rich Wilkes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rich_Wilkes "Rich Wilkes"). It consists of three full-length feature films: *[XXX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XXX_(2002_film) "XXX (2002 film)")* (2002), *[XXX: State of the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XXX:_State_of_the_Union "XXX: State of the Union")* (2005) and *[XXX: Return of Xander Cage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XXX:_Return_of_Xander_Cage "XXX: Return of Xander Cage")* (2017), and a short film: *The Final Chapter: The Death of Xander Cage*. The series has grossed $694 million worldwide.

### *XXX* (2002)

The film was released on August 9, 2002, which stars [Vin Diesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vin_Diesel "Vin Diesel") as Xander Cage, a [thrill seeking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrill_seeking "Thrill seeking") [extreme sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_sports "Extreme sports") enthusiast, [stuntman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuntman "Stuntman") and [rebellious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-establishment "Anti-establishment") athlete-turned-reluctant spy for the [National Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Agency "National Security Agency") who is sent on a dangerous mission to [infiltrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infiltration_tactics "Infiltration tactics") a group of potential Russian [terrorists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist "Terrorist") in [Central Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Europe "Central Europe"). The film also stars [Asia Argento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia_Argento "Asia Argento"), [Marton Csokas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marton_Csokas "Marton Csokas") and [Samuel L. Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_L._Jackson "Samuel L. Jackson"). It was directed by [Rob Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Cohen "Rob Cohen"), who previously directed *[The Fast and the Furious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fast_and_the_Furious_(2001_film) "The Fast and the Furious (2001 film)")* (2001), in which Diesel also starred.

### *XXX: State of the Union* (2005)

The film was released on April 29, 2005, which stars [Ice Cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_Cube "Ice Cube") as Darius Stone, a new agent in the Triple X program, who is sent to [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C.") to defuse a power struggle amongst national leaders.

### *XXX: Return of Xander Cage* (2017)

The film was released on January 20, 2017, and sees the return of Diesel as Xander Cage, who comes out of self-imposed exile, thought to be long dead, and is set on a collision course with a deadly alpha warrior and his team in a race to recover a sinister and seemingly unstoppable weapon known as Pandora's Box. Recruiting an all-new group of thrill-seeking cohorts, Xander finds himself enmeshed in a deadly conspiracy that points to collusion at the highest levels of world governments.

### Untitled fourth *XXX* film (TBA)

In September 2018, it was announced that a fourth film is in development. The project will be a joint-venture production, with [The H Collective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_H_Collective "The H Collective") and [iQiyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IQiyi "IQiyi") after the former acquired franchise rights from [Revolution Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_Studios "Revolution Studios"). [D. J. Caruso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._J._Caruso "D. J. Caruso") was believed to return as director, while [Vin Diesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vin_Diesel "Vin Diesel") would reprise his role as Xander Cage. Production was scheduled to begin in early-2019. In November 2018, [Jay Chou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Chou "Jay Chou") and Zoe Zhang joined the cast. Japanese rock star and musician [Yoshiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshiki_(musician) "Yoshiki (musician)") was set to serve as the film's composer. In September 2023, it was reiterated that a fourth film was still in development, though production would not be able to start until Diesel completed his work on *[Fast Forever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_Forever "Fast Forever")* (2028). In 2024, an article by [Deadline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadline_Hollywood "Deadline Hollywood") reported that the film was [stalled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_hell "Development hell") due to The H Collective's financial problems and litigation with the *xXx* franchise film rights.

## Short film

### *The Final Chapter: The Death of Xander Cage* (2005)

Included with the 2005 Director's Cut DVD of the first film is a four-minute short titled *The Final Chapter: The Death of Xander Cage*, that serves as a prequel to *XXX: State of the Union* by detailing the alleged death of Xander Cage before the events of that film.

In the short film, Xander is played by Vin Diesel's stunt double Khristian Lupo (who never shows his face or speaks) while reusing some archival lines spoken by Diesel. It also features [Leila Arcieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leila_Arcieri "Leila Arcieri") as Jordan King from the first film and John G. Connolly as [Lt. Colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_colonel_(Australia) "Lieutenant colonel (Australia)") Alabama "Bama" Cobb, one of the villains from *xXx: State of the Union* who is Deckert's right-hand man, as the man behind the attack on Xander.

The sequence opens with Xander driving in a car with Jordan King. He stops next to his apartment building. King makes sexual overtures to him and they get intimate. Suddenly they hear a noise and Xander goes to check it out. Cobb's men show up and abduct King. They plant a bomb in the building and drop her coat on the steps to trick Xander to his death. After confronting a homeless man, Xander returns to the building. He takes the bait left by Cobb and his henchmen and is apparently blown apart by a huge explosion. His trademark coat survives the blast. Cobb shows up and picks up a piece of burnt skin from Xander's neck which has the Triple X tattoo on it. He remarks "Poor Xander, you never had very much between the ears." His men pick him up and drive off in their car. Cobb's motives for killing Xander are obvious; he doesn't want him to interfere in Deckert's plans. "[Feuer Frei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feuer_Frei "Feuer Frei")" by [Rammstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rammstein "Rammstein") plays in the background during the sequence.

### Critical and public response

| Film | [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes") | [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic") | [CinemaScore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CinemaScore "CinemaScore") |

The first film received mixed reviews from critics. Roger Ebert called it "as good as a James Bond movie". Adam Smith of [Empire magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_(film_magazine) "Empire (film magazine)") called the movie, "Sporadically entertaining, but seriously hampered by a very choppy screenplay", and rating it three out of five stars. The film was nominated for a [Razzie Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23rd_Golden_Raspberry_Awards "23rd Golden Raspberry Awards") for Most Flatulent Teen-Targeted Movie, but lost to *[Jackass: The Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackass:_The_Movie "Jackass: The Movie")*.

The second film in the series was panned by critics, Boo Allen of the Denton Record Chronicle called it "a chubby, surly, incomprehensible action hero".
Brian Orndorf of FilmJerk.com compared watching the film to running "headfirst at top speed into a brick wall".
David Hiltbrand of the *[Philadelphia Inquirer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Inquirer "Philadelphia Inquirer")* said "the plot swings between pathetically implausible and aggressively stupid".
Some critics liked the film. Owen Gleiberman of *[Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")* called it "that rare B movie that's rooted in gut-level stirrings of power and retaliation". Paul Arendt of the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") said, "Viewed on its own trashy terms, it succeeds brilliantly".

The third film received mixed reviews from critics. Dan Jolin of Empire magazine said, "We've seen all these stunts pulled before, and seen them done better, but there's some pleasure to be had here — even if it's of the extremely guilty kind.", rating it three out of five stars. Andrew Lapin of *[Uproxx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uproxx "Uproxx")* gave the film a negative review, saying: "There is an intellectual argument to be made in favor of the [*Fast & Furious* franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_%26_Furious "Fast & Furious"), which features diverse casts, operatic plotlines, and cartoon setpieces that often look like a child assembled them out of Hot Wheels sets. *xXx* is aiming for a much lower bar, striving only to be marketable, not inventive. The series is no longer interested in aping James Bond, lacking as it does a decent gadget or supervillain and often highlighting the sidekicks at the expense of Xander himself."

## Video games

A video game featuring Xander Cage was produced for [Game Boy Advance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Boy_Advance "Game Boy Advance"), released in [North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_America "North America") and [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe "Europe") in 2002. The Game Boy Advance game received a rating of E in North America and 3+ rating in Europe unlike the film's PG-13 rating. In 2004, a *XXX* game was in development for the [Xbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_(console) "Xbox (console)") by [Warthog Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warthog_Games "Warthog Games"), but it was cancelled.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]
