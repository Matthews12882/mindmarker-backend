# History of Asia

The **history of Asia** can be seen as the collective history of [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia"), [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia"), [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Southeast_Asia "History of Southeast Asia"), [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia"), [North Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Asia "North Asia") and [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia "West Asia"). The continent is home to two of the world's oldest, continuous civilizations, Chinese civilization and Indian civilization.

Asia was also home to the [Mesopotamian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia "Mesopotamia"), [Ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt "Ancient Egypt"), [Indus Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation "Indus Valley Civilisation") and [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River_civilization "Yellow River civilization") civilizations. These civilizations were among the first in the world, and developed around fertile river valleys as they were conducive to agriculture. They shared similarities and likely exchanged technologies and ideas such as mathematics and the wheel. Other inventions such as writing likely developed independently as did cities, states, and empires.

The [Eurasian steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Steppe "Eurasian Steppe") had long been inhabited by nomads, and from the central steppes, they could reach all parts of the Asian continent. The northern part of the continent, covering much of [Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberia "Siberia") was inaccessible to the steppe nomads due to the dense forests and the [tundra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra "Tundra"). These areas in Siberia were very sparsely populated. Mountains and deserts such as the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_Mountains "Caucasus Mountains"), [Himalayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayas "Himalayas"), [Karakum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakum_Desert "Karakum Desert") and [Gobi Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gobi_Desert "Gobi Desert") formed natural barriers against the steppe nomads. The urban centers were technologically and culturally more advanced, but could do little militarily to defend against the mounted hordes of the steppe. However, the lowlands did not have enough open grassland to support a large horse mounted force. Thus the nomads who conquered states in [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia "West Asia") were soon forced to adopt local customs.

Asia has been the birthplace of many religions. They include [Abrahamic religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrahamic_religions "Abrahamic religions") such as [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism "Judaism"), [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity") and [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam"), [Indian religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_religions "Indian religions") such as [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism"), [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"), [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism "Jainism") and [Sikhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism "Sikhism"), and [Iranian religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_religions "Iranian religions") such as [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism "Zoroastrianism") and the now extinct religion of [Manichaeism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manichaeism "Manichaeism"). The [spread of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spread_of_Islam "Spread of Islam") ushered in the [Islamic Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Golden_Age "Islamic Golden Age") and the [Timurid Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_Renaissance "Timurid Renaissance"), which later went on to influence the [Islamic gunpowder empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunpowder_empires "Gunpowder empires").

The history of Asia includes major developments such as the invention of [gunpowder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunpowder "Gunpowder") in medieval China, which was later developed by the gunpowder empires, mainly the [Mughals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_tribe "Mughal tribe") and [Safavids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid "Safavid"), and led to significant advancements in warfare. The [Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road "Silk Road"), helped spread cultures, languages, religions, as well as diseases throughout Asia and Europe.

## Prehistory

A report by archaeologist Rakesh Tewari on Lahuradewa, [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") shows new C14 datings that range between 9000 and 8000 BC associated with rice, making Lahuradewa the earliest [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic "Neolithic") site in entire South Asia. Settled life emerged on the subcontinent in the western margins of the [Indus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River "Indus River") alluvium approximately 9,000 years ago, evolving gradually into the [Indus Valley Civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation "Indus Valley Civilisation") of the third millennium BC.

[Göbekli Tepe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6bekli_Tepe "Göbekli Tepe") is a [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic "Neolithic") site in the [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia "Anatolia") of [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey "Turkey"). Dated to the [Pre-Pottery Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Pottery_Neolithic "Pre-Pottery Neolithic"), between c. 9500 and 8000 BC, the site comprises a number of large circular structures supported by massive stone pillars – the world's oldest known [megaliths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalith "Megalith").

The [prehistoric Beifudi site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_Beifudi_site "Prehistoric Beifudi site") near Yixian in [Hebei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebei "Hebei") Province, China, contains relics of a culture contemporaneous with the [Cishan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cishan_culture "Cishan culture") and [Xinglongwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinglongwa_culture "Xinglongwa culture") cultures of about 8000–7000 BC, Neolithic cultures east of the [Taihang Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taihang_Mountains "Taihang Mountains"), filling in an archaeological gap between the two Northern Chinese cultures. The total excavated area is more than 1,200 square meters and the collection of Neolithic findings at the site consists of two phases.

Around 5500 BC the [Halafian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Halaf "Tel Halaf") culture appeared in the modern-day countries of [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon "Lebanon"), [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_of_Israel "Land of Israel"), [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria "Syria"), [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia "Anatolia"), and northern [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia "Mesopotamia"), based upon dryland agriculture.

In southern Mesopotamia were the alluvial plains of [Sumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumer "Sumer") and [Elam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elam "Elam"). Since there was little rainfall, [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation "Irrigation") systems were necessary. The [Ubaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubaid_period "Ubaid period") culture flourished from 5500 BC.

## Ancient

### Bronze Age

The [Chalcolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcolithic "Chalcolithic") period (or Copper Age) began about 4500 BC, then the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age "Bronze Age") began about 3500 BC, replacing the Neolithic cultures.

The [Indus Valley civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_civilization "Indus Valley civilization") (IVC) was a Bronze Age civilization (3300–1300 BC; mature period 2600–1900 BC) which was centered mostly in the western part of the Indian subcontinent; it is considered that an early form of Hinduism was performed during this civilization. Some of the great cities of this civilization include [Harappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harappa "Harappa") and [Mohenjo-daro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohenjo-daro "Mohenjo-daro"), which had a high level of town planning and arts. The cause of the destruction of these regions around 1700 BC is debatable, although evidence suggests it was caused by natural disasters (especially flooding). This era marks [Vedic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_period "Vedic period") in India, which lasted from roughly 1500 to 500 BC. During this period, the [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit "Sanskrit") language developed and the [Vedas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedas "Vedas") were written, epic hymns that told tales of gods and wars. This was the basis for the Vedic religion, which would eventually sophisticate and develop into [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism").

China and Vietnam were also centres of metalworking. Dating back to the Neolithic Age, the first bronze drums, the [Dong Son drums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dong_Son_drum "Dong Son drum"), have been uncovered in and around the Red River Delta regions of Vietnam and Southern China. These relate to the prehistoric Dong Son Culture of Vietnam.

In Ban Chiang, Thailand, bronze artifacts have been discovered dating to 2100 BC. In Nyaunggan, Burma bronze tools have been excavated along with ceramics and stone artifacts. Dating is still currently broad (3500–500 BC).

### Iron and Axial Age

The Iron Age saw the widespread use of iron tools, weaponry, and armor throughout the major civilizations of Asia.

#### West Asia

The [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire "Achaemenid Empire") founded by [Cyrus the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_the_Great "Cyrus the Great"), ruled a vast area stretching from [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece "Greece") to the [Indus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River "Indus River") and the [Aral Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aral_Sea "Aral Sea") from the 6th to 4th centuries BC. Persian politics included a tolerance for other cultures, a highly [centralized government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralized_government "Centralized government"), and significant infrastructure developments. Later, during the reign of [Darius the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_the_Great "Darius the Great"), the territories were integrated, a bureaucracy was developed, nobility were assigned military positions, tax collection was carefully organized, and spies were used to ensure the loyalty of regional officials.

The primary religion of Persia at this time was [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism "Zoroastrianism"). Developed by the philosopher [Zoroaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroaster "Zoroaster"), the religion introduced an early form of [monotheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotheism "Monotheism") to the area and tenets such as spiritual salvation through personal moral action, an [end time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eschatology "Eschatology"), and both [general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_judgment "General judgment") and [Particular judgment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particular_judgment "Particular judgment") with a [heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heaven "Heaven") or [hell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hell "Hell"). Rooted in ancient religious practices predating known history, these concepts would heavily influence later emperors and the masses throughout West Asia and adjacent regions under Persian rule.

The Persian Empire was successful in establishing peace and stability throughout the region, and was a major influence in art, politics (affecting Hellenistic leaders), and religion.

[Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great "Alexander the Great") conquered this dynasty in the 4th century BC, creating the brief [Hellenistic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period "Hellenistic period"). However, he was unable to set stability; after his death, his empire fragmented into successor states, including the [Seleucid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire "Seleucid Empire") and the [Ptolemaic Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_Kingdom "Ptolemaic Kingdom") under [Ptolemy I Soter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy_I_Soter "Ptolemy I Soter"). Persia itself later broke into smaller, weaker dynasties, including periods of Seleucid control, followed by the [Parthian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthian_Empire "Parthian Empire"). By the end of the [Classical age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_age "Classical age"), Persia had been reconsolidated into the [Sasanian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire "Sasanian Empire"), also known as the second Persian Empire.

The [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire "Roman Empire") would later control parts of Western Asia. During the Hellenistic period, the Seleucid Empire and the Ptolemaic Kingdom were major powers in West Asia and the [Eastern Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Mediterranean "Eastern Mediterranean"), while the later Parthian and Sasanian dynasties of Persia dominated much of West Asia for centuries.

#### India

The Maurya and Gupta empires are called the Golden Age of India and were marked by extensive inventions and discoveries in science, technology, art, religion, and philosophy that crystallized the elements of what is generally known as Indian culture. The religions of [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism") and [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"), which originated in the Indian subcontinent, were important influences on South, East and Southeast Asia.

By 600 BC, the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent") was politically fragmented into numerous states, including the sixteen major [Mahājanapadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahajanapada "Mahajanapada") (Sanskrit: महाजनपद) and many smaller kingdoms and republics that often competed and feuded with one another.

In 327 BC, [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great "Alexander the Great") advanced through [Bactria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bactria "Bactria") and crossed into northwestern India. He campaigned across the Punjab to the [Beas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beas_River "Beas River"), but his army refused to march farther east into the Ganges basin, forcing him to retreat. The Macedonian withdrawal weakened Greek control in the region and opened the way for new powers.

Shortly afterward, [Chandragupta Maurya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandragupta_Maurya "Chandragupta Maurya"), with the counsel of [Chanakya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanakya "Chanakya"), overthrew the [Nanda dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanda_Empire "Nanda Empire") around 321 BC and established the [Maurya Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire "Maurya Empire") (Sanskrit: मौर्य राजवंश, *Maurya Rājavaṃśa*). The Mauryan state became one of the world’s largest empires of its time, stretching north to the [Himalayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayas "Himalayas"), east into what is now [Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam "Assam"), west beyond modern [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan"), and annexing [Balochistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochistan "Balochistan") and much of present-day [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan") at its greatest extent. South of the empire lay [Tamilakam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamilakam "Tamilakam"), an independent region dominated by the [Cholas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola_dynasty "Chola dynasty"), [Pandyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandya_dynasty "Pandya dynasty"), and [Cheras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chera_dynasty "Chera dynasty").

The Mauryan Empire was notable for its sophisticated administration. Chandragupta and his successors ruled through an autocratic monarchy supported by a standing army, provincial governors, a complex bureaucracy, a regulated taxation system, state monopolies in key commodities, standardized coinage, and even a postal network.

Chandragupta’s grandson, [Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka "Ashoka") (r. 268–232 BC), expanded Mauryan control across almost the entire subcontinent (except for the southern tip). Following his conversion to Buddhism, Ashoka promoted dhamma (Sanskrit: धर्म, Pali: धम्म, *dhamma* = righteousness), non-violence, and public welfare through his famous [edicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edicts_of_Ashoka "Edicts of Ashoka"), leaving a lasting impact on Indian society and religion. After Ashoka’s death, the empire gradually fragmented; by 185 BC the last Mauryan ruler was overthrown, and successor states arose in the north and northwest, including the [Śuṅgas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shunga_Empire "Shunga Empire"), [Indo-Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Greek_Kingdom "Indo-Greek Kingdom"), and [Śakas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Scythians "Indo-Scythians").

The [Kushan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushan_Empire "Kushan Empire") (Sanskrit: कुषाण राजवंश, *Kuṣāṇa Rājavaṃśa*; 1st–3rd centuries AD), founded by Central Asian invaders from the northwest, became a major power across northern India and Central Asia. Under Emperor [Kanishka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanishka "Kanishka") (c. 127–150 AD), the Kushans were notable patrons of Buddhism, supporting its spread along the Silk Roads into Central Asia and China. While Buddhism flourished under their rule, in later centuries its close association with foreign dynasties contributed to perceptions of it as an “outside” religion, a factor in its eventual decline within India.

The [Gupta Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire "Gupta Empire") (Sanskrit: गुप्त राजवंश, *Gupta Rājavaṃśa*), founded by [Chandragupta I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandragupta_I "Chandragupta I") around AD 320, later unified much of northern India through conquest and alliance. Gupta rule covered less territory than the Mauryas but brought long-lasting stability and is remembered as a classical age of Indian civilization, marked by advances in art, science, and literature. By the mid-6th century AD, sustained invasions by the [Hūṇas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huna_people "Huna people") and internal divisions contributed to Gupta decline and fragmentation.

#### Classical China

##### Zhou dynasty

Since 1029 BC, the [Zhou dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_dynasty "Zhou dynasty") ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language "Chinese language"): 周朝; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin "Pinyin"): *Zhōu Cháo*; [Wade–Giles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wade%E2%80%93Giles "Wade–Giles"): *Chou Ch'ao* [[tʂóʊ tʂʰɑ̌ʊ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Mandarin "Help:IPA/Mandarin")), had existed in China and it would continue to until 258 BC. The Zhou dynasty had been using a [feudal system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudalism "Feudalism") by giving power to local nobility and relying on their loyalty in order to control its large territory. As a result, the Chinese government at this time tended to be very decentralized and weak, and there was often little the emperor could do to resolve national issues. Nonetheless, the government was able to retain its position with the creation of the [Mandate of Heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_of_Heaven "Mandate of Heaven"), which could establish an emperor as divinely chosen to rule. The Zhou additionally discouraged the [human sacrifice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_sacrifice "Human sacrifice") of the preceding eras and unified the [Chinese language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language "Chinese language"). Finally, the Zhou government encouraged settlers to move into the [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze "Yangtze") valley, thus creating the Chinese Middle Kingdom.

But by 500 BC, its political stability began to decline due to repeated nomadic incursions and internal conflict derived from the fighting princes and families. This was lessened by the many philosophical movements, starting with the life of [Confucius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucius "Confucius"). His philosophical writings (called [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism "Confucianism")) concerning the respect of elders and of the state would later be popularly used in the Han dynasty. Additionally, [Laozi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laozi "Laozi")'s concepts of [Taoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism "Taoism"), including [yin and yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yin_and_yang "Yin and yang") and the innate duality and balance of nature and the universe, became popular throughout this period. Nevertheless, the Zhou dynasty eventually disintegrated as the local nobles began to gain more power and their conflict devolved into the [Warring States period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warring_States_period "Warring States period"), from 402 to 201 BC.

##### Qin dynasty

One leader eventually came on top, [Qin Shi Huang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_Shi_Huang "Qin Shi Huang") ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language "Chinese language"): 始皇帝, *Shǐ Huángdì*), who overthrew the last Zhou emperor and established the Qin dynasty. The [Qin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_dynasty "Qin dynasty") (Chinese: 秦朝; pinyin: Qín Cháo) was the first ruling dynasty of Imperial China, lasting from 221 to 207 BC. The new Emperor abolished the feudal system and directly appointed a bureaucracy that would rely on him for power. Huang's imperial forces crushed any regional resistance, and they furthered the Chinese empire by expanding down to the [South China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_Sea "South China Sea") and northern [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam "Vietnam"). Greater organization brought a uniform tax system, a national census, regulated road building (and cart width), standard measurements, standard coinage, and an official written and spoken language. Further reforms included new irrigation projects, the encouragement of [silk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk "Silk") manufacturing, and most famously, the beginning of the construction of the [Great Wall of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Wall_of_China "Great Wall of China")—designed to keep out the nomadic raiders who'd constantly badger the Chinese people. However, Shi Huang was infamous for his tyranny, forcing laborers to build the Wall, ordering heavy taxes, and severely punishing all who opposed him. He oppressed Confucians and promoted [Legalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legalism_(Chinese_philosophy) "Legalism (Chinese philosophy)"), the idea that people were inherently evil, and that a strong, forceful government was needed to control them. Legalism was infused with realistic, logical views and rejected the pleasures of educated conversation as frivolous. All of this made Shi Huang extremely unpopular with the people. As the Qin began to weaken, various factions began to fight for control of China.

##### Han dynasty

The [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty "Han dynasty") ([simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters "Simplified Chinese characters"): 汉朝; [traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters "Traditional Chinese characters"): 漢朝; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin "Pinyin"): *Hàn Cháo*; 206 BC – 220 AD) was the second imperial dynasty of China, preceded by the Qin dynasty and succeeded by the Three Kingdoms (220–265 AD). Spanning over four centuries, the period of the Han dynasty is considered a golden age in Chinese history. One of the Han dynasty's greatest emperors, [Emperor Wu of Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Wu_of_Han "Emperor Wu of Han"), established a peace throughout China comparable to the [Pax Romana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pax_Romana "Pax Romana") seen in the Mediterranean a hundred years later. To this day, China's majority ethnic group refers to itself as the "Han Chinese". The Han dynasty was established when two peasants succeeded in rising up against Shi Huang's significantly weaker successor-son. The new Han government retained the centralization and bureaucracy of the Qin, but greatly reduced the repression seen before. They expanded their territory into [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea "Korea"), [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam "Vietnam"), and [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia"), creating an even larger empire than the Qin.

The Han developed contacts with the Persian Empire in the Middle East and the Romans, through the [Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road "Silk Road"), with which they were able to trade many commodities—primarily silk. Many ancient civilizations were influenced by the Silk Road, which connected China, India, the Middle East and Europe. Han emperors like Wu also promoted Confucianism as the national "religion" (although it is debated by theologians as to whether it is defined as such or as a philosophy). Shrines devoted to Confucius were built and Confucian philosophy was taught to all scholars who entered the Chinese bureaucracy. The bureaucracy was further improved with the introduction of an examination system that selected scholars of high merit. These bureaucrats were often upper-class people educated in special schools, but whose power was often checked by the lower-class brought into the bureaucracy through their skill. The Chinese imperial bureaucracy was very effective and highly respected by all in the realm and would last over 2,000 years. The Han government was highly organized and it commanded the military, judicial law (which used a system of courts and strict laws), agricultural production, the economy, and the general lives of its people. The government also promoted intellectual philosophy, scientific research, and detailed historical records.

However, despite all of this impressive stability, central power began to lose control by the turn of the [Common Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Era "Common Era"). As the Han dynasty declined, many factors continued to pummel it into submission until China was left in a state of chaos. By 100 AD, philosophical activity slowed, and corruption ran rampant in the bureaucracy. Local landlords began to take control as the scholars neglected their duties, and this resulted in heavy taxation of the peasantry. Taoists began to gain significant ground and protested the decline. They started to proclaim magical powers and promised to save China with them; the Taoist [Yellow Turban Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Turban_Rebellion "Yellow Turban Rebellion") in 184 (led by rebels in yellow scarves) failed but was able to weaken the government. The aforementioned Huns combined with diseases killed up to half of the population and officially ended the Han dynasty by 220. The ensuing period of chaos was so terrible it lasted for three centuries, where many weak regional rulers and dynasties failed to establish order in China. This period of chaos and attempts at order is commonly known as that of the [Six Dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Dynasties "Six Dynasties"). The first part of this included the [Three Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms "Three Kingdoms") which started in 220 and describes the brief and weak successor "dynasties" that followed the Han. In 265, the [Jin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_dynasty_(265%E2%80%93420) "Jin dynasty (265–420)") of China was started and this soon split into two different empires in control of northwestern and southeastern China. In 420, the conquest and abdication of those two dynasties resulted in the first of the [Southern and Northern dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_and_Northern_dynasties "Southern and Northern dynasties"). The Northern and Southern dynasties passed through until finally, by 557, the [Northern Zhou dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Zhou "Northern Zhou") ruled the north and the [Chen dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_dynasty "Chen dynasty") ruled the south.

## Medieval

During this period, the [Eastern world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_world "Eastern world") empires continued to expand through trade, migration and conquests of neighboring areas. Gunpowder was widely used as early as the 11th century and they were using moveable type printing five hundred years before Gutenberg created his press. Buddhism, Taoism, Confucianism were the dominant philosophies of the Far East during the Middle Ages. [Marco Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Polo "Marco Polo") was not the first Westerner to travel to the Orient and return with amazing stories of this different culture, but his accounts published in the late 13th and early 14th centuries were the first to be widely read throughout Europe.

### West Asia (Middle East)

The [Arabian peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Peninsula "Arabian Peninsula") and the wider [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia "West Asia") saw dramatic change during the Medieval era caused primarily by the spread of [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam") and the establishment of the early Islamic empires.

In the 5th century, West Asia was generally separated into small, weakened states; with the two major powers, the [Sasanian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire "Sasanian Empire") and the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire "Byzantine Empire") dominating the region. The Byzantines and Sasanians fought each other continually, reflecting the long-standing rivalry between the Roman Empire and the Persian Empire during the previous five hundred years. The fighting weakened both states, leaving the stage open to a new power. Meanwhile, the nomadic [Bedouin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedouin "Bedouin") tribes who dominated the Arabian desert experienced a period of tribal stabilization, expanding trade networks, and increasing familiarity with [Abrahamic religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrahamic_religions "Abrahamic religions") and forms of monotheism.

While the Byzantine Roman and Sasanian Persian empires were both severely weakened by the [Byzantine–Sasanian War of 602–628](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine%E2%80%93Sasanian_War_of_602%E2%80%93628 "Byzantine–Sasanian War of 602–628"), a new power in the form of [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam") emerged in Arabia under [Muhammad in Medina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_in_Medina "Muhammad in Medina"). In a series of rapid [Muslim conquests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_conquests "Early Muslim conquests"), the [Rashidun army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidun_army "Rashidun army"), led by the [Caliphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliph "Caliph") and commanders such as [Khalid ibn al-Walid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalid_ibn_al-Walid "Khalid ibn al-Walid"), swept through much of the Middle East, taking significant Byzantine territories in the [Arab–Byzantine wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab%E2%80%93Byzantine_wars "Arab–Byzantine wars"), including Syria and Egypt, and completely engulfing Persia in the [Muslim conquest of Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Persia "Muslim conquest of Persia"). The succeeding [Caliphates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate "Caliphate") of the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages "Middle Ages") unified much of West Asia and North Africa as a distinct political sphere and fostered the consolidation of a broader [Arab cultural identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab "Arab"). These Caliphates included the [Rashidun Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidun_Caliphate "Rashidun Caliphate"), [Umayyad Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate "Umayyad Caliphate"), [Abbasid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate "Abbasid Caliphate"), and later the [Seljuk Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Empire "Seljuk Empire").

Following the rise of Islam, the region entered a period often referred to as the [Islamic Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Golden_Age "Islamic Golden Age"), marked by major developments in [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_architecture "Islamic architecture"), preservation and advancement of classical knowledge, and innovations in [medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_medicine "History of medicine"), [algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_elementary_algebra "History of elementary algebra"), [geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_geometry "History of geometry"), [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_astronomy "History of astronomy"), [anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_anatomy "History of anatomy"), and [ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_ethics "History of ethics"). Scholars working in centers such as [Baghdad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdad "Baghdad"), and [Cairo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo "Cairo") played key roles in transmitting knowledge that later influenced Western Europe.

The dominance of Arab political power declined in the mid-11th century with the arrival of the [Seljuq Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuq_Turks "Seljuq Turks"), who migrated south from Central Asia. They conquered Persia, Iraq, Syria, Palestine, and parts of Anatolia. This period was followed by a series of invasions from Western Europe during the [Crusades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades "Crusades"). In 1099, the knights of the [First Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Crusade "First Crusade") captured [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem "Jerusalem") and founded the [Kingdom of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Jerusalem "Kingdom of Jerusalem"), which survived until 1187 when [Saladin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saladin "Saladin") retook the city. Smaller crusader states endured until 1291. In the early 13th century, a new wave of invaders, the armies of the [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire "Mongol Empire"), swept through the region, sacking Baghdad in the [Siege of Baghdad (1258)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Baghdad_(1258) "Siege of Baghdad (1258)") and advancing as far south as the border of Egypt, where they were halted by the [Mamluk Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Sultanate "Mamluk Sultanate") at the [Battle of Ain Jalut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ain_Jalut "Battle of Ain Jalut") in 1260. The Mongols eventually withdrew, but the instability that followed reshaped regional power structures. In 1401, the region was further destabilized by the campaigns of the Turco-Mongol ruler [Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur "Timur"). By this time, another Turkic power had risen in northwestern Anatolia: the [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire "Ottoman Empire").

### South Asia

#### India

The Indian early medieval age, 600 to 1200, is defined by regional kingdoms and cultural diversity. When [Harsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harsha "Harsha") of [Kannauj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannauj "Kannauj"), who ruled much of the Indo-Gangetic Plain from 606 to 647, attempted to expand southwards, he was defeated by the [Chalukya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalukya_dynasty "Chalukya dynasty") ruler of the Deccan. When his successor attempted to expand eastwards, he was defeated by the [Pala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pala_Empire "Pala Empire") king of [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal "Bengal"). When the Chalukyas attempted to expand southwards, they were defeated by the [Pallavas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pallava "Pallava") from farther south, who in turn were opposed by the [Pandyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandyas "Pandyas") and the [Cholas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholas "Cholas") from still farther south. The Cholas could under the rule of [Raja Raja Chola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Raja_Chola "Raja Raja Chola") defeat their rivals and rise to a regional power. Cholas expanded northward and defeated [Eastern Chalukya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Chalukya "Eastern Chalukya"), [Kalinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Ganga_dynasty "Eastern Ganga dynasty") and the Pala. Under [Rajendra Chola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajendra_Chola "Rajendra Chola") the Cholas created the first notable navy of Indian subcontinent. The [Chola navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola_navy "Chola navy") extended the influence of Chola Empire to Southeast Asia. During this time, pastoral peoples whose land had been cleared to make way for the growing agricultural economy were accommodated within caste society, as were new non-traditional ruling classes.
The [Muslim conquest in the Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_in_the_Indian_subcontinent "Muslim conquest in the Indian subcontinent") mainly took place from the 12th century onwards, though earlier Muslim conquests include the limited inroads into modern Afghanistan and Pakistan and the Umayyad campaigns in India, during the time of the Rajput kingdoms in the 8th century.

Major economic and military powers like the [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate "Delhi Sultanate") and [Bengal Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Sultanate "Bengal Sultanate"), were seen to be established. The search of their wealth led the [Voyages of Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyages_of_Christopher_Columbus "Voyages of Christopher Columbus").

[Sher Shah Suri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sher_Shah_Suri "Sher Shah Suri") of the [Sur Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sur_Empire "Sur Empire") of [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent") introduced the standardised [rūpya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupee "Rupee") coin in the 16th century now used as the currency of [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia"), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India"), [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan"), [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh"), [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka") and [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal "Nepal").

The [Vijayanagara Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayanagara_Empire "Vijayanagara Empire") based in the Deccan Plateau region of South India, was established in 1336 by the brothers [Harihara I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harihara_I "Harihara I") and [Bukka Raya I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukka_Raya_I "Bukka Raya I") of the [Sangama dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangama_dynasty "Sangama dynasty"), patronized by saint [Vidyaranya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidyaranya "Vidyaranya"), the 12th [Shankaracharya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shankaracharya "Shankaracharya") of [Sringeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sringeri "Sringeri") in [Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnataka "Karnataka"). The empire rose to prominence as a result of attempts by the southern powers to resist and ward off Turkic Islamic invasions by the end of the 13th century. At its peak, it subjugated almost all of South India's rulers and pushed the sultans of the Deccan beyond the Tungabhadra-Krishna river region. After annexing modern day Odisha (ancient Kalinga) from the Gajapati Empire, became a notable power. The empire lasted until 1646 after a major military defeat in the [Battle of Talikota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Talikota "Battle of Talikota") in 1565 by the combined armies of the [Deccan sultanates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_sultanates "Deccan sultanates").

### East Asia

#### China

China saw the rise and fall of the Sui, Tang, Song, and Yuan dynasties and therefore improvements in its bureaucracy, the spread of [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"), and the advent of [Neo-Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Confucianism "Neo-Confucianism"). It was an unsurpassed era for Chinese ceramics and painting. Medieval architectural masterpieces the Great South Gate in Todaiji, Japan, and the Tienning Temple in Peking, China are some of the surviving constructs from this era.

##### Sui dynasty

A new powerful dynasty began to rise in the 580s, amongst the divided factions of China. This was started when an aristocrat named Yang Jian married his daughter into the Northern Zhou dynasty. He proclaimed himself [Emperor Wen of Sui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Wen_of_Sui "Emperor Wen of Sui") and appeased the nomadic military by abandoning the Confucian scholar-gentry. Emperor Wen soon led the conquest of the southern Chen dynasty and united China once more under the [Sui dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_dynasty "Sui dynasty"). The emperor lowered taxes and constructed granaries that he used to prevent famine and control the market. Later Wen's son would murder him for the throne and declare himself [Emperor Yang of Sui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Yang_of_Sui "Emperor Yang of Sui"). Emperor Yang revived the Confucian scholars and the bureaucracy, much to anger of the aristocrats and nomadic military leaders. Yang became an excessive leader who overused China's resources for personal luxury and perpetuated exhaustive attempts to [conquer Goguryeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goguryeo%E2%80%93Sui_War "Goguryeo–Sui War"). His military failures and neglect of the empire forced his own ministers to assassinate him in 618, ending the Sui dynasty.

##### Tang dynasty

Fortunately, one of Yang's most respectable advisors, Li Yuan, was able to claim the throne quickly, preventing a chaotic collapse. He proclaimed himself [Emperor Gaozu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Gaozu_of_Tang "Emperor Gaozu of Tang"), and established the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty "Tang dynasty") in 618. The Tang saw expansion of China through conquest to Tibet in the west, [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam "Vietnam") in the south, and Manchuria in the north. Tang emperors also improved the education of scholars in the Chinese bureaucracy. A Ministry of Rites was established and the examination system was improved to better qualify scholars for their jobs. In addition, Buddhism became popular in China with two different strains between the peasantry and the elite, the [Pure Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_Land_Buddhism "Pure Land Buddhism") and [Zen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen "Zen") strains, respectively. Greatly supporting the spread of Buddhism was [Empress Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Zetian "Wu Zetian"), who additionally claimed an unofficial "Zhou dynasty" and displayed China's tolerance of a woman ruler, which was rare at the time. However, Buddhism would also experience some backlash, especially from Confucianists and Taoists. This would usually involve criticism about how it was costing the state money, since the government was unable to tax Buddhist monasteries, and additionally sent many grants and gifts to them.

The Tang dynasty began to decline under the rule of [Emperor Xuanzong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Xuanzong_of_Tang "Emperor Xuanzong of Tang"), who began to neglect the economy and military and caused unrest amongst the court officials due to the excessive influence of his concubine, [Yang Guifei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Guifei "Yang Guifei"), and her family. This eventually sparked a revolt in 755. Although the revolt failed, subduing it required involvement with the unruly nomadic tribes outside of China and distributing more power to local leaders—leaving the government and economy in a degraded state. The Tang dynasty officially ended in 907 and various factions led by the aforementioned nomadic tribes and local leaders would fight for control of China in the [Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Dynasties_and_Ten_Kingdoms_period "Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms period").

#### Mongol Empire

The [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire "Mongol Empire") was the largest contiguous land empire in history. Founded by [Genghis Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan "Genghis Khan") in 1206 after the unification of separate Mongol tribes, it rapidly expanded across Asia and into Europe. The Mongol conquests became known for both their military effectiveness and large-scale [destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destruction_under_the_Mongol_Empire "Destruction under the Mongol Empire"). The era of [Pax Mongolica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pax_Mongolica "Pax Mongolica") facilitated trade, cultural exchange, and communication throughout Eurasia.

##### Liao, Song and Jin dynasties

By 960, most of China proper had been reunited under the [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty "Song dynasty"), although it lost territories in the north and could not defeat one of the nomadic tribes there—the [Liao dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liao_dynasty "Liao dynasty") of the highly sinicized [Khitan people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khitan_people "Khitan people"). From then on, the Song would have to pay tribute to avoid invasion and thus set the precedent for other nomadic kingdoms to oppress them. The Song also saw the revival of Confucianism in the form of [Neo-Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Confucianism "Neo-Confucianism"). This had the effect of putting the Confucian scholars at a higher status than aristocrats or Buddhists and also intensified the reduction of power in women. The infamous practice of [foot binding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foot_binding "Foot binding") developed in this period as a result. Eventually the Liao dynasty in the north was overthrown by the [Jin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_dynasty_(1115%E2%80%931234) "Jin dynasty (1115–1234)") of the Manchu-related [Jurchen people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurchen_people "Jurchen people"). The new Jin kingdom [invaded northern China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin%E2%80%93Song_Wars "Jin–Song Wars"), leaving the Song to flee farther south and creating the [Southern Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Song_dynasty "Southern Song dynasty") in 1126. There, cultural life flourished.

##### Yuan dynasty

By 1227, the Mongols had conquered the [Western Xia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Xia "Western Xia") kingdom northwest of China. Soon the Mongols incurred upon the Jin empire of the Jurchens. Chinese cities were soon besieged by the Mongol hordes that showed little mercy for those who resisted and the Southern Song Chinese were quickly losing territory. In 1271 the current [Great Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Khan "Great Khan"), [Kublai Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kublai_Khan "Kublai Khan"), claimed himself Emperor of China and officially established the Yuan dynasty. By 1290, all of China was under control of the Mongols, marking the first time they were ever completely conquered by a foreign invader; the new capital was established at [Khanbaliq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanbaliq "Khanbaliq") (modern-day [Beijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing "Beijing")). Kublai Khan segregated Mongol culture from Chinese culture by discouraging interactions between the two peoples, separating living spaces and places of worship, and reserving top administrative positions to Mongols, thus preventing Confucian scholars to continue the bureaucratic system. Nevertheless, Kublai remained fascinated with Chinese thinking, surrounding himself with Chinese Buddhist, Taoist, or Confucian advisors.

Mongol women displayed a contrasting independent nature compared to the Chinese women who continued to be suppressed. Mongol women often rode out on hunts or even to war. Kublai's wife, [Chabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chabi "Chabi"), was a perfect example of this; Chabi advised her husband on several political and diplomatic matters; she convinced him that the Chinese were to be respected and well-treated in order to make them easier to rule. However, this was not enough to affect Chinese women's position, and the increasingly Neo-Confucian successors of Kublai further repressed Chinese and even Mongol women.

The Black Death, which would later ravage Western Europe, had its beginnings in Asia, where it wiped out large populations in China in 1331.

#### Japan

##### Asuka period

Japan's medieval history began with the [Asuka period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asuka_period "Asuka period"), from around 600 to 710. The time was characterized by the [Taika Reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taika_Reform "Taika Reform") and imperial centralization, both of which were a direct result of growing Chinese contact and influences. In 603, [Prince Shōtoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Sh%C5%8Dtoku "Prince Shōtoku") of the [Yamato dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_House_of_Japan "Imperial House of Japan") began significant political and cultural changes. He issued the [Seventeen-article constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventeen-article_constitution "Seventeen-article constitution") in 604, centralizing power towards the emperor (under the title *tenno*, or heavenly sovereign) and removing the power to levy taxes from provincial lords. Shōtoku was also a patron of Buddhism and he encouraged building temples competitively.

##### Nara period

Shōtoku's reforms transitioned Japan to the [Nara period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nara_period "Nara period") (c. 710 to c. 794), with the moving of the Japanese capital to [Nara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nara,_Nara "Nara, Nara") in [Honshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honshu "Honshu"). This period saw the culmination of Chinese-style writing, etiquette, and architecture in Japan along with Confucian ideals to supplement the already present Buddhism. Peasants revered both Confucian scholars and Buddhist monks. However, in the wake of the [735–737 Japanese smallpox epidemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/735%E2%80%93737_Japanese_smallpox_epidemic "735–737 Japanese smallpox epidemic"), Buddhism gained the status of state religion and the government ordered the construction of numerous Buddhist temples, monasteries, and statues. The lavish spending combined with the fact that many aristocrats did not pay taxes, put a heavy burden on peasantry that caused poverty and famine. Eventually the Buddhist position got out of control, threatening to seize imperial power and causing [Emperor Kanmu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Kanmu "Emperor Kanmu") to move the capital to [Heian-kyō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heian-ky%C5%8D "Heian-kyō") to avoid a Buddhist takeover. This marked the beginning of the [Heian period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heian_period "Heian period") and the end of Taika reform.

##### Heian period

With the Heian period (from 794 to 1185) came a decline of imperial power. Chinese influence also declined, as a result of its correlation with imperial centralization and the [heavenly mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_of_Heaven "Mandate of Heaven"), which came to be regarded as ineffective. By 838, the Japanese court discontinued its embassies in China; only traders and Buddhist monks continued to travel to China. Buddhism itself came to be considered more Japanese than Chinese, and persisted to be popular in Japan. Buddhists monks and monasteries continued their attempts to gather personal power in courts, along with aristocrats. One particular noble family that dominated influence in the imperial bureaucracy was the [Fujiwara clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujiwara_clan "Fujiwara clan"). During this time cultural life in the imperial court flourished. There was a focus on beauty and social interaction and writing and literature was considered refined. Noblewomen were cultured the same as noblemen, dabbling in creative works and politics. A prime example of both [Japanese literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_literature "Japanese literature") and women's role in high-class culture at this time was *[The Tale of Genji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tale_of_Genji "The Tale of Genji")*, written by the [lady-in-waiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady-in-waiting "Lady-in-waiting") [Murasaki Shikibu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murasaki_Shikibu "Murasaki Shikibu"). Popularization of wooden palaces and [shōji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dji "Shōji") sliding doors amongst the nobility also occurred.

Loss of imperial power also led to the rise of provincial warrior elites. Small lords began to function independently. They administered laws, supervised public works projects, and collected revenue for themselves instead of the imperial court. Regional lords also began to build their own armies. These warriors were loyal only their local lords and not the emperor, although the imperial government increasingly called them in to protect the capital. The regional warrior class developed into the [samurai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samurai "Samurai"), which created its own culture: including specialized weapons such as the [katana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katana "Katana") and a form of chivalry, [bushido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bushido "Bushido"). The imperial government's loss of control in the second half of the Heian period allowed banditry to grow, requiring both feudal lords and Buddhist monasteries to procure warriors for protection. As imperial control over Japan declined, feudal lords also became more independent and seceded from the empire. These feudal states squandered the peasants living in them, reducing the farmers to an almost [serfdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serfdom "Serfdom") status. Peasants were also rigidly restricted from rising to the samurai class, being physically set off by dress and weapon restrictions. As a result of their oppression, many peasants turned to Buddhism as a hope for reward in the afterlife for upright behavior.

With the increase of feudalism, families in the imperial court began to depend on alliances with regional lords. The Fujiwara clan declined from power, replaced by a rivalry between the [Taira clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taira_clan "Taira clan") and the [Minamoto clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minamoto_clan "Minamoto clan"). This rivalry grew into the [Genpei War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genpei_War "Genpei War") in the early 1180s. This war saw the use of both samurai and peasant soldiers. For the samurai, battle was ritual and they often easily cut down the poorly trained peasantry. The Minamoto clan proved successful due to their rural alliances. Once the Taira was destroyed, the Minamoto established a military government called the [shogunate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shogunate "Shogunate") (or bakufu), centered in [Kamakura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamakura,_Kanagawa "Kamakura, Kanagawa").

##### Kamakura period

The end of the Genpei War and the establishment of the [Kamakura shogunate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamakura_shogunate "Kamakura shogunate") marked the end of the Heian period and the beginning of the [Kamakura period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamakura_period "Kamakura period") in 1185, solidifying feudal Japan with an emphasis on military feudalism through the [daimyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daimyo "Daimyo") system.

#### Korea

##### [Three Kingdoms of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms_of_Korea "Three Kingdoms of Korea")

The three Kingdoms of Korea involves [Goguryeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goguryeo "Goguryeo") in north, [Baekje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baekje "Baekje") in southwest, and [Silla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silla "Silla") in southeast Korean peninsula. These three kingdoms act as a bridge of cultures between China and Japan. [Prince Shōtoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Sh%C5%8Dtoku "Prince Shōtoku") of Japan had been taught by two teachers. One was from Baekje, the other was from Goguryeo. Once Japan invaded Silla, Goguryeo helped Silla to defeat Japan. Baekje met the earliest heyday of them. Its heyday was the 5th century AD. Its capital was [Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul "Seoul"). During its heyday, the kingdom made colonies overseas. Liaodong, China and Kyushu, Japan were the colonies of Baekje during its short heyday. Goguryeo was the strongest kingdom of all. They sometimes called themselves as an Empire. Its heyday was 6th century. King Gwanggaeto widened its territory to north. So Goguryeo dominated from Korean peninsula to Manchuria. ### Southeast Asia

#### Khmers

In 802, [Jayavarman II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayavarman_II "Jayavarman II") consolidated his rule over neighboring peoples and declared himself [chakravartin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakravartin "Chakravartin"), or "universal ruler". The Khmer Empire effectively dominated all [mainland Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Southeast_Asia "Mainland Southeast Asia") from the early 9th until the 15th century, during which time they developed a sophisticated monumental architecture of most exquisite expression and mastery of composition at [Angkor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angkor "Angkor").

#### Vietnam

The history of Vietnam can be traced back to around 20,000 years ago, as the first modern humans arrived and settled on this land, known as the [Hoabinhians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoabinhian "Hoabinhian"), which can be traced back to the modern-day Negritos. Archaeological findings from 1965, which are still under research, show the remains of two hominins closely related to the Sinanthropus, dating as far back as the [Middle Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Pleistocene_Transition "Middle Pleistocene Transition") era, roughly half a million years ago.

Pre-historic Vietnam was home to some of the world's earliest civilizations and societies—making them one of the world's first people who had practiced agriculture. The Red River valley formed a natural geographic and economic unit, bounded to the north and west by mountains and jungles, to the east by the sea and to the south by the [Red River Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Delta "Red River Delta"). The need to have a single authority to prevent floods of the Red River, to cooperate in constructing hydraulic systems, trade exchange, and to repel invaders, led to the creation of the first legendary Vietnamese states approximately 2879 BC. While in the later times, ongoing research from archaeologists have suggested that the Vietnamese [Đông Sơn culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dong_Son_culture "Dong Son culture") were traceable back to Northern Vietnam, Guangxi and Laos around 700 BC.

Vietnam's long coastal and narrowed lands, rugged mountainous terrains, with two major deltas, were soon home to several different ancient cultures and civilizations. In the north, the Đông Sơn culture and its indigenous chiefdoms of [Văn Lang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%E1%BB%93ng_B%C3%A0ng_dynasty "Hồng Bàng dynasty") and [Âu Lạc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%82u_L%E1%BA%A1c "Âu Lạc") started to flourish by 500 BC. In Central, [Sa Huỳnh culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa_Hu%E1%BB%B3nh_culture "Sa Huỳnh culture") of [Austronesian Chamic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chams "Chams") also thrived. Both were swept by the Chinese [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty "Han dynasty") expansion from the north – the Han conquest of [Nanyue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanyue "Nanyue") brought parts of Vietnam under the Chinese rule in 111 BC. Traditional Chinese became the official script as well as the later developed independent [Nôm script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%E1%BB%AF_N%C3%B4m "Chữ Nôm") of Vietnamese.

In 40 AD, the [Trưng Sisters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tr%C6%B0ng_sisters "Trưng sisters") led the first uprising of indigenous tribes and peoples against [Chinese domination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_under_Chinese_rule "Vietnam under Chinese rule"). The rebellion was however defeated, but as the Han dynasty began to weaken by late 2nd century and China (中国) started to descend into state of turmoil, the indigenous peoples of Vietnam rose again and some became free. In 192 AD, the Chams of Central Vietnam revolted against the Chinese and subsequently became independent [Kingdom of Champa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champa "Champa"), while the Red River Delta saw loosening Northern control. At that time, with the introduction of [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism") and [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism") by the second century AD, Vietnam was the first place in Southeast Asia which shared influences of both [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indosphere "Indosphere") and [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinosphere "Sinosphere") cultures, and the rise of first Indianized kingdoms Champa and [Funan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funan "Funan").

During these 1,000 years there were many uprisings against Chinese domination, and at certain periods Vietnam was independently governed under the Trưng Sisters, [Early Lý](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_L%C3%BD_dynasty "Early Lý dynasty"), [Khúc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kh%C3%BAc_clan "Khúc clan") and [Dương Đình Nghệ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C6%B0%C6%A1ng_%C4%90%C3%ACnh_Ngh%E1%BB%87 "Dương Đình Nghệ")—although their triumphs and reigns were temporary.

When [Ngô Quyền](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ng%C3%B4_Quy%E1%BB%81n "Ngô Quyền") ([Emperor of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_monarchs_of_Vietnam "List of monarchs of Vietnam"), 938–944) restored sovereign power in the country with the victory at [Battle of Bạch Đằng (938)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_B%E1%BA%A1ch_%C4%90%E1%BA%B1ng_(938) "Battle of Bạch Đằng (938)"), the next millennium was advanced by the accomplishments of successive local dynasties: [Ngô](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ng%C3%B4_dynasty "Ngô dynasty"), [Đinh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90inh_dynasty "Đinh dynasty"), [Early Lê](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_L%C3%AA_dynasty "Early Lê dynasty"), [Lý](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BD_dynasty "Lý dynasty"), [Trần](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tr%E1%BA%A7n_dynasty "Trần dynasty"), [Hồ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%E1%BB%93_dynasty "Hồ dynasty"), [Later Trần](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_Tr%E1%BA%A7n_dynasty "Later Trần dynasty"), [Later Lê](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%AA_dynasty "Lê dynasty"), [Mạc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%E1%BA%A1c_dynasty "Mạc dynasty"), [Revival Lê](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revival_L%C3%AA_dynasty "Revival Lê dynasty"), [Tây Sơn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A2y_S%C6%A1n_dynasty "Tây Sơn dynasty") and [Nguyễn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguy%E1%BB%85n_dynasty "Nguyễn dynasty"). Nôm script (Chữ Nôm) of the Vietnamese started to develop and become more sophisticated, with literature being published and written in Nôm. At various points during the imperial dynasties, Vietnam was ravaged and divided by civil wars and witnessed interventions by the [Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty "Song dynasty"), [Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty "Yuan dynasty"), [Cham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champa "Champa"), [Ming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty "Ming dynasty"), [Siamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand"), [Qing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty "Qing dynasty"), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire "French colonial empire"), and [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan "Empire of Japan").

The [Ming Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty "Ming dynasty") conquered the Red River valley for a while before [native Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_people "Vietnamese people") regained control and the French Empire reduced Vietnam to a French dependency for nearly a century, followed by brief but brutal occupation by the Japanese Empire. During the French period, widespread brutality, inequality and cultural remnants of [Hán-Nôm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_writing_in_Vietnam "History of writing in Vietnam") were being destroyed, with the French wishing to rid the Vietnamese of their [Confucian legacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism "Confucianism") from the 1880s. French was the official language during this period. The [Vietnamese Latin script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_alphabet "Vietnamese alphabet"), seen to be a Latin transliteration of Hán-Nôm, superseded the Hán-Nôm logographic scripts and became the main mode of written as well as spoken language since the 20th century.

Japan invaded in 1940, creating deep resentment that fuelled resistance to post-World War II military-political efforts by the returning power of France, and the United States who had viewed themselves as fighters for liberty and democracy against the red waves of [communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism "Communism"). In the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War"), the United States or the [Western Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Bloc "Western Bloc") supported [South Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Vietnam "South Vietnam") and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") or the [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc "Eastern Bloc") supported [North Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vietnam "North Vietnam"). Political upheaval, a period of intense fighting and war, followed by Communist insurrection and victory further put an end to the monarchy after World War II, and the country was proclaimed a Socialist Republic. Vietnam suffered heavy sanctions as well as political and economic isolation following brutal wars with China and Cambodia in the successive years. Following that era, the [Đổi Mới](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90%E1%BB%95i_M%E1%BB%9Bi "Đổi Mới") (renovation/innovation) reformations were enacted. The forces of market liberalisation and globalisation has shaped [Vietnam's economic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Vietnam "Economy of Vietnam") and political circumstances since.

## Early modern

The [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire") began to expand into Asia from the 17th century, and would eventually take control of all of [Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberia "Siberia") and most of Central Asia by the end of the 19th century. The [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire "Ottoman Empire") controlled Anatolia, the Middle East, North Africa and the Balkans from the 16th century onwards. In the 17th century, the [Manchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_people "Manchu people") conquered China and established the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty "Qing dynasty"). In the 16th century, the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire "Mughal Empire") controlled much of India and initiated the second golden age for India. China was the largest economy in the world for much of the time, followed by India until the 18th century.

### Ming China

By 1368, [Zhu Yuanzhang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhu_Yuanzhang "Zhu Yuanzhang") had claimed himself [Hongwu Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongwu_Emperor "Hongwu Emperor") and established the Ming dynasty of China. Immediately, the new emperor and his followers drove the Mongols and their culture out of China and beyond the Great Wall. The new emperor was somewhat suspicious of the scholars that dominated China's bureaucracy, for he had been born a peasant and was uneducated. Nevertheless, Confucian scholars were necessary to China's bureaucracy and were reestablished as well as reforms that would improve the exam systems and make them more important in entering the bureaucracy than ever before. The exams became more rigorous, cut down harshly on cheating, and those who excelled were more highly appraised. Finally, Hongwu also directed more power towards the role of emperor so as to end the corrupt influences of the bureaucrats.

#### Society and economy

The Hongwu emperor, perhaps for his sympathy of the common-folk, had built many irrigation systems and other public projects that provided help for the peasant farmers. They were also allowed to cultivate and claim unoccupied land without having to pay any taxes and labor demands were lowered. However, none of this was able to stop the rising landlord class that gained many privileges from the government and slowly gained control of the peasantry. Moneylenders foreclosed on peasant debt in exchange for mortgages and bought up farmer land, forcing them to become the landlords' tenants or to wander elsewhere for work. Also during this time, [Neo-Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Confucianism "Neo-Confucianism") intensified even more than the previous two dynasties (the Song and Yuan). Focus on the superiority of elders over youth, men over women, and teachers over students resulted in minor discrimination of the "inferior" classes. The fine arts grew in the Ming era, with improved techniques in brush painting that depicted scenes of court, city or country life; people such as scholars or travelers; or the beauty of mountains, lakes, or marshes. The Chinese novel fully developed in this era, with such classics written such as *[Water Margin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_Margin "Water Margin")*, *[Journey to the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_to_the_West "Journey to the West")*, and *[Jin Ping Mei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_Ping_Mei "Jin Ping Mei")*.

Economics grew rapidly in the Ming dynasty as well. The introduction of American crops such as [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize "Maize"), [sweet potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_potatoes "Sweet potatoes"), and [peanuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut "Peanut") allowed for cultivation of crops in infertile land and helped prevent famine. The population boom that began in the Song dynasty accelerated until China's population went from 80 or 90 million to 150 million in three centuries, culminating in 1600. This paralleled the market economy that was growing both internally and externally. Silk, tea, ceramics, and lacquer-ware were produced by artisans that traded them in Asia and to Europeans. Westerners began to trade (with some Chinese-assigned limits), primarily in the port-towns of [Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macau "Macau") and [Canton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou "Guangzhou"). Although merchants benefited greatly from this, land remained the primary symbol of wealth in China and traders' riches were often put into acquiring more land. Therefore, little of these riches were used in private enterprises that could've allowed for China to develop the [market economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_economy "Market economy") that often accompanied the highly-successful Western countries.

#### Foreign interests

In the interest of national glory, the Chinese began sending impressive [junk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junk_(ship) "Junk (ship)") ships across the [South China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_Sea "South China Sea") and the [Indian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean "Indian Ocean"). From 1403 to 1433, the [Yongle Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongle_Emperor "Yongle Emperor") commissioned [expeditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_voyages "Treasure voyages") led by the admiral [Zheng He](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zheng_He "Zheng He"), a Muslim [eunuch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eunuch "Eunuch") from China. Chinese junks carrying hundreds of soldiers, goods, and animals for zoos, traveled to Southeast Asia, Persia, southern Arabia, and east Africa to show off Chinese power. Their prowess exceeded that of current Europeans at the time, and had these expeditions not ended, the world economy may be different from today. In 1433, the Chinese government decided that the cost of a navy was an unnecessary expense. The Chinese navy was slowly dismantled and focus on interior reform and military defense began. It was China's longstanding priority that they protect themselves from nomads and they have accordingly returned to it. The growing limits on the Chinese navy would leave them vulnerable to foreign invasion by sea later on.

As was inevitable, Westerners arrived on the Chinese east coast, primarily [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit "Jesuit") missionaries which reached the mainland in 1582. They attempted to [convert the Chinese people to Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit_China_missions "Jesuit China missions") by first converting the top of the social hierarchy and allowing the lower classes to subsequently convert. To further gain support, many Jesuits adopted Chinese dress, customs, and language. Some Chinese scholars were interested in certain Western teachings and especially in Western technology. By the 1580s, Jesuit scholars like [Matteo Ricci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Ricci "Matteo Ricci") and [Adam Schall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Schall "Adam Schall") amazed the Chinese elite with technological advances such as European clocks, improved calendars and cannons, and the accurate prediction of eclipses. Although some the scholar-gentry converted, many were suspicious of the Westerners whom they called "barbarians" and even resented them for the embarrassment they received at the hand of Western correction. Nevertheless, a small group of Jesuit scholars remained at the court to impress the emperor and his advisors.

#### Decline

Near the end of the 1500s, the extremely centralized government that gave so much power to the emperor had begun to fail as more incompetent rulers took the mantle. Along with these weak rulers came increasingly corrupt officials who took advantage of the decline. Once more the public projects fell into disrepair due to neglect by the bureaucracy and resulted in floods, drought, and famine that rocked the peasantry. The famine soon became so terrible that some peasants resorted to selling their children to slavery to save them from starvation, or to eating bark, the feces of geese, or [other people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_cannibalism "Human cannibalism"). Many landlords abused the situation by building large estates where desperate farmers would work and be exploited. In turn, many of these farmers resorted to flight, banditry, and open rebellion.

All of this corresponded with the usual dynastic decline of China seen before, as well as the growing foreign threats. In the mid-16th century, Japanese and ethnic Chinese pirates began to raid the southern coast, and neither the bureaucracy nor the military were able to stop them. The threat of the northern [Manchu people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_people "Manchu people") also grew. The Manchu were an already large state north of China, when in the early 17th century a local leader named [Nurhaci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nurhaci "Nurhaci") suddenly united them under the [Eight Banners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight_Banners "Eight Banners")—armies that the opposing families were organized into. The Manchus adopted many Chinese customs, specifically taking after their bureaucracy. Nevertheless, the Manchus still remained a Chinese [vassal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassal "Vassal"). In 1644 Chinese administration became so weak, the 16th and last emperor, the [Chongzhen Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongzhen_Emperor "Chongzhen Emperor"), did not respond to the severity of an ensuing rebellion by local dissenters until the enemy had invaded the [Forbidden City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbidden_City "Forbidden City") (his personal estate). He soon hanged himself in the imperial gardens. For a brief amount of time, the [Shun dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shun_dynasty "Shun dynasty") was claimed, until a loyalist Ming official called support from the Manchus to put down the new dynasty. The Shun dynasty ended within a year and the Manchu were now within the Great Wall. Taking advantage of the situation, the Manchus marched on the Chinese capital of Beijing. [Within two decades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_conquest_of_China "Manchu conquest of China") all of China belonged to the Manchu and the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty "Qing dynasty") was established.

### Korea: Joseon dynasty (1392–1897)

In early-modern Korea, the 500-year-old kingdom, [Goryeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goryeo "Goryeo") fell and the new dynasty [Joseon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseon "Joseon") rose in August 5, 1392. [Taejo of Joseon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taejo_of_Joseon "Taejo of Joseon") changed the country's name from Goryeo to Joseon. [Sejong the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sejong_the_Great "Sejong the Great") created [Hangul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangul "Hangul"), the modern Korean alphabet, in 1443; likewise the Joseon dynasty saw several improvements in science and technology, like Sun Clocks, Water Clocks, Rain-Measuring systems, Star Maps, and detailed records of Korean small villages. The ninth king, [Seongjong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seongjong_of_Joseon "Seongjong of Joseon") accomplished the first complete Korean [law code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongguk_daejeon "Gyeongguk daejeon") in 1485. So the culture and people's lives were improved again.

In 1592, Japan under [Toyotomi Hideyoshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyotomi_Hideyoshi "Toyotomi Hideyoshi") invaded Korea in the [Imjin War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imjin_War "Imjin War"). Before that war, Joseon was in a long peace like Pax Romana. So Joseon was unprepared for the war and faced multiple defeats. The Japanese army conquered [Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul "Seoul") and the whole [Korean peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_peninsula "Korean peninsula") was in danger. But [Yi Sun-sin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_Sun-sin "Yi Sun-sin"), the most renowned Korean general, led 13 ships to victory against the Japanese fleet of 133 ships off the southern coast of Korea. This incredible battle is called "[Battle of Myeongnyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Myeongnyang "Battle of Myeongnyang")". Thereafter, the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty "Ming dynasty") helped Joseon, and Japan lost the battle. So Toyotomi Hideyoshi's campaign in Korea failed, and the [Tokugawa Shogunate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokugawa_Shogunate "Tokugawa Shogunate") has later began. Korea was hurt a lot at Imjin war. ### Japan: Tokugawa or Edo period (1603–1867)

In early-modern Japan following the [Sengoku period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sengoku_period "Sengoku period") of "warring states", central government had been largely reestablished by [Oda Nobunaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oda_Nobunaga "Oda Nobunaga") and [Toyotomi Hideyoshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyotomi_Hideyoshi "Toyotomi Hideyoshi") during the [Azuchi–Momoyama period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azuchi%E2%80%93Momoyama_period "Azuchi–Momoyama period"). After the [Battle of Sekigahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sekigahara "Battle of Sekigahara") in 1600, central authority fell to [Tokugawa Ieyasu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokugawa_Ieyasu "Tokugawa Ieyasu") who completed this process and received the title of *[shōgun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dgun "Shōgun")* in 1603.

Society in the Japanese "[Tokugawa period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokugawa_shogunate "Tokugawa shogunate")" (see [Edo society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_society "Edo society")), unlike the shogunates before it, was based on the strict class [hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hierarchy "Hierarchy") originally established by [Toyotomi Hideyoshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyotomi_Hideyoshi "Toyotomi Hideyoshi"). The *[daimyōs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daimy%C5%8D "Daimyō")* (feudal lords) were at the top, followed by the warrior-caste of [samurai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samurai "Samurai"), with the farmers, artisans, and merchants ranking below. The country was strictly closed to foreigners with few exceptions with the *[Sakoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakoku "Sakoku")* policy. Literacy rose in the two centuries of isolation.

In some parts of the country, particularly smaller regions, *daimyōs* and samurai were more or less identical, since *daimyōs* might be trained as samurai, and samurai might act as local lords. Otherwise, the largely inflexible nature of this [social stratification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_stratification "Social stratification") system unleashed disruptive forces over time. Taxes on the [peasantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peasantry "Peasantry") were set at fixed amounts which did not account for inflation or other changes in monetary value. As a result, the tax revenues collected by the samurai landowners were worth less and less over time. This often led to numerous confrontations between noble but impoverished samurai and well-to-do peasants. None, however, proved compelling enough to seriously challenge the established order until the arrival of foreign powers.

### India

In the early 18th century, the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire "Mughal Empire") ruled most parts of the Indian subcontinent. Founded by [Babur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur "Babur") in 1526, the empire was notable for its a highly centralized administration connecting a diverse region. All the significant monuments of the Mughals, their most visible legacy, date to this period which was characterised by the expansion of Persian cultural influence in the Indian subcontinent, with brilliant literary, artistic, and [architectural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_architecture "Mughal architecture") results. During emperor [Shah Jahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Jahan "Shah Jahan") and his son [Aurangzeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurangzeb "Aurangzeb")'s Islamic [sharia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharia "Sharia") reigns, the empire reached its architectural and economic zenith, and became the world's largest economy, worth over 25% of world GDP. In the mid-18th century it was a major [proto-industrializing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-industrialization "Proto-industrialization") region.

The "classic period" ended with the death of [Mughal Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire "Mughal Empire") Aurangzeb, although the dynasty continued for another 150 years. Following major events such as the [Nader Shah's invasion of the Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah%27s_invasion_of_the_Mughal_Empire "Nader Shah's invasion of the Mughal Empire"), [Battle of Plassey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Plassey "Battle of Plassey"), [Battle of Buxar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Buxar "Battle of Buxar") and the long [Anglo-Mysore Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Mysore_Wars "Anglo-Mysore Wars"), most of South Asia was colonised and governed by the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire"), thus establishing the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj"). The Maratha Empire was located in the south west of present-day India and expanded greatly under the rule of the [Peshwas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshwa "Peshwa"), the prime ministers of the Maratha empire. In 1761, the Maratha army lost the [Third Battle of Panipat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Battle_of_Panipat "Third Battle of Panipat") against [Ahmad Shah Durrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_Durrani "Ahmad Shah Durrani") which halted imperial expansion and the empire was then divided into a confederacy of Maratha states.

### British and Dutch colonization

The European economic and naval powers pushed into Asia, initially to do trading, and gradually to take over major colonies. The Dutch led the way followed by the British. Portugal had arrived first, but was too weak to maintain its small holdings and was largely pushed out, retaining only [Goa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goa "Goa") and [Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macau "Macau"). The British set up a private organization, the [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company "East India Company"), which handled both trade and imperial control of much of India.

The [commercial colonization of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_rule_in_India "Company rule in India") commenced in 1757, after the [Battle of Plassey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Plassey "Battle of Plassey"), when the [Nawab of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawab_of_Bengal "Nawab of Bengal") surrendered his dominions to the British East India Company,[*[citation not found](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Template:Sfn#Wikilink_to_citation_does_not_work "Template:Sfn")*] in 1765, when the company was granted the *diwani*, or the right to collect revenue, in [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal "Bengal") and [Bihar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bihar "Bihar"), or in 1772, when the company established a capital in [Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta "Calcutta"), appointed its first [Governor-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_India "Governor-General of India"), [Warren Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Hastings "Warren Hastings"), and became directly involved in governance.

The [Maratha states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire "Maratha Empire"), following the [Anglo-Maratha wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Maratha_Wars_(disambiguation) "Anglo-Maratha Wars (disambiguation)"), eventually lost to the [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company "British East India Company") in 1818 with the [Third Anglo-Maratha War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Anglo-Maratha_War "Third Anglo-Maratha War"). The rule lasted until 1858, when, after the [Indian rebellion of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_rebellion_of_1857 "Indian rebellion of 1857") and consequent of the [Government of India Act 1858](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India_Act_1858 "Government of India Act 1858"), the [British government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_Office "India Office") assumed the task of directly administering India in the new [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj"). In 1819 [Stamford Raffles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamford_Raffles "Stamford Raffles") established [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore") as a key trading post for Britain in their rivalry with the Dutch. However, their rivalry cooled in 1824 when an [Anglo-Dutch treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Dutch_Treaty_of_1824 "Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824") demarcated their respective interests in Southeast Asia. From the 1850s onwards, the pace of colonization shifted to a significantly higher gear.

The [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company "Dutch East India Company") (1800) and [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company "British East India Company") (1858) were dissolved by their respective governments, who took over the direct administration of the colonies. Only [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand") was spared the experience of foreign rule, although, Thailand itself was also greatly affected by the power politics of the Western powers. Colonial rule had a profound effect on Southeast Asia. While the colonial powers profited much from the region's vast resources and large market, colonial rule did develop the region to a varying extent.

## Late modern

### Central Asia: The Great Game, Russia vs Great Britain

[The Great Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Game "The Great Game") was a political and diplomatic confrontation between Great Britain and Russia over [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Afghanistan "History of Afghanistan") and neighbouring territories in [Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia") and [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia"). It lasted from 1828 to 1907. There was no war, but there were many threats. Russia was fearful of British commercial and military inroads into Central Asia, and Britain was fearful of Russia threatening its largest and most important possession, India. This resulted in an atmosphere of distrust and the constant threat of war between the two empires. Britain made it a high priority to protect all the approaches to India, and the "great game" is primarily how the British did this in terms of a possible Russian threat. Historians with access to the archives have concluded that Russia had no plans involving India, as the Russians repeatedly stated.

The Great Game began in 1838 when Britain decided to gain control over the [Emirate of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Afghanistan "Emirate of Afghanistan") and make it a protectorate, and to use the [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire "Ottoman Empire"), [Qajar Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_Iran "Qajar Iran"), the [Khanate of Khiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanate_of_Khiva "Khanate of Khiva"), and the [Emirate of Bukhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Bukhara "Emirate of Bukhara") as buffer states between both empires. This would protect India and also key British sea trade routes by stopping Russia from gaining a port on the [Persian Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf "Persian Gulf") or the [Indian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean "Indian Ocean"). Russia proposed Afghanistan as the neutral zone, and the final result was diving up Afghanistan with a neutral zone in the middle between Russian areas in the north and British in the South. Important episodes included the failed [First Anglo-Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Anglo-Afghan_War "First Anglo-Afghan War") of 1838, the [First Anglo-Sikh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Anglo-Sikh_War "First Anglo-Sikh War") of 1845, the [Second Anglo-Sikh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Anglo-Sikh_War "Second Anglo-Sikh War") of 1848, the [Second Anglo-Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Anglo-Afghan_War "Second Anglo-Afghan War") of 1878, and the annexation of [Kokand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanate_of_Kokand "Khanate of Kokand") by Russia. The 1901 novel [*Kim*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_(novel) "Kim (novel)") by [Rudyard Kipling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudyard_Kipling "Rudyard Kipling") made the term popular and introduced the new implication of great power rivalry. It became even more popular after the 1979 advent of the [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War "Soviet–Afghan War").

### Qing China

By 1644, the northern [Manchu people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_people "Manchu people") had conquered [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty "Ming dynasty") and established a foreign dynasty—the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty "Qing dynasty")—once more. The Manchu Qing emperors, especially Confucian scholar [Kangxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangxi_Emperor "Kangxi Emperor"), remained largely conservative—retaining the bureaucracy and the scholars within it, as well as the Confucian ideals present in Chinese society. However, changes in the economy and new attempts at resolving certain issues occurred too. These included increased trade with Western countries that brought large amounts of silver into the Chinese economy in exchange for tea, [porcelain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcelain "Porcelain"), and silk textiles. This allowed for a new merchant-class, the [compradors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprador "Comprador"), to develop. In addition, repairs were done on existing [dikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levee "Levee"), canals, roadways, and [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation "Irrigation") works. This, combined with the lowering of taxes and government-assigned labor, was supposed to calm peasant unrest. However, the Qing failed to control the growing landlord class which had begun to exploit the peasantry and abuse their position.

By the late 18th century, both internal and external issues began to arise in Qing China's politics, society, and economy. The exam system with which scholars were assigned into the bureaucracy became increasingly corrupt; bribes and other forms of cheating allowed for inexperienced and inept scholars to enter the bureaucracy and this eventually caused rampant neglect of the peasantry, military, and the previously mentioned infrastructure projects. Poverty and banditry steadily rose, especially in rural areas, and mass migrations looking for work throughout China occurred. The perpetually conservative government refused to make reforms that could resolve these issues.

#### Opium Wars

China saw its status reduced by what it perceived as parasitic trade with Westerners. Originally, European traders were at a disadvantage because the Chinese cared little for their goods, while European demand for Chinese commodities such as tea and porcelain only grew. In order to tip the trade imbalance in their favor, British merchants began to sell Indian [opium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium "Opium") to the Chinese. Not only did this sap Chinese bullion reserves, it also led to widespread drug addiction amongst the [bureaucracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholar_official "Scholar official") and society in general. A ban was placed on opium as early as 1729 by the [Yongzheng Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongzheng_Emperor "Yongzheng Emperor"), but little was done to enforce it. By the early 19th century, under the new [Daoguang Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daoguang_Emperor "Daoguang Emperor"), the government began serious efforts to eradicate opium from Chinese society. Leading this endeavour were respected scholar-officials including [Imperial Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Commissioner_(China) "Imperial Commissioner (China)") [Lin Zexu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lin_Zexu "Lin Zexu").

After Lin [destroyed more than 20,000 chests of opium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destruction_of_opium_at_Humen "Destruction of opium at Humen") in the summer of 1839, Europeans demanded compensation for what they saw as unwarranted Chinese interference in their affairs. When it was not paid, the British declared war later the same year, starting what became known as the [First Opium War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Opium_War "First Opium War"). The outdated Chinese [junks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junk_(ship) "Junk (ship)") were no match for the advanced British gunboats, and soon the [Yangzi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangzi_River "Yangzi River") region came under threat of British bombardment and invasion. The emperor had no choice but to sue for peace, resulting in the exile of Lin and the making of the [Treaty of Nanking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Nanking "Treaty of Nanking"), which ceded the British control of [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong "Hong Kong") and opened up trade and diplomacy with other European countries, including Germany, France, and the USA.

Following the First Opium War, Western powers sought to expand their commercial and diplomatic privileges in China through a series of treaties. Dissatisfied with the Qing government's implementation of existing agreements, Britain launched the [Second Opium War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Opium_War "Second Opium War") (1856–1860), forcing China to sign the [Treaties of Tianjin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaties_of_Tianjin "Treaties of Tianjin") in 1858. Under the treaties, additional ports were opened to [foreign trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_trade "Foreign trade"), foreigners gained access to China's interior, [diplomats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomats "Diplomats") were permitted to reside in [Beijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing "Beijing"), then known as Peking, and import tariffs were reduced. The [most-favoured-nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most-favoured-nation "Most-favoured-nation") clause ensured that concessions obtained by one power were extended to others, allowing France, Russia, and the United States to secure similar privileges.

Chinese resistance to the treaties led to further conflict, culminating in the capture of Beijing by British and French forces in 1860 and the destruction of the Summer Palace. China subsequently ratified the agreements through the [Convention of Beijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_of_Beijing "Convention of Beijing"). Collectively known as the "[Unequal Treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unequal_Treaties "Unequal Treaties")," these agreements weakened Chinese sovereignty through [extraterritorial rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extraterritorial_rights "Extraterritorial rights") and foreign economic influence, while replacing the traditional [Chinese tribute system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_tribute_system "Chinese tribute system") with Western diplomatic norms. The treaties facilitated foreign trade and presence in China but also fuelled widespread resentment towards [Western imperialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_imperialism "Western imperialism") and contributed to the growth of [Chinese nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_nationalism "Chinese nationalism").

#### Manchuria

Manchuria came under influence of Russia with the building of the [Chinese Eastern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Eastern_Railway "Chinese Eastern Railway") through [Harbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbin,_China "Harbin, China") to [Vladivostok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladivostok "Vladivostok"). The [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan "Empire of Japan") replaced Russian influence in the region as a result of the [Russo-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Japanese_War "Russo-Japanese War") in 1904–1905, and Japan laid the [South Manchuria Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Manchuria_Railway "South Manchuria Railway") in 1906 to [Port Arthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BCshunkou "Lüshunkou"). During the [Warlord Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warlord_Era "Warlord Era") in China, [Zhang Zuolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Zuolin "Zhang Zuolin") established himself in Northeast China, but was murdered by the Japanese for being too independent. The former Chinese emperor, [Puyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puyi "Puyi"), was then placed on the throne to lead a Japanese puppet state of [Manchukuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchukuo "Manchukuo"). In August 1945, the Soviet Union invaded the region. From 1945 to 1948, Northeast China was a base area for Mao Zedong's [People's Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Liberation_Army "People's Liberation Army") in the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War "Chinese Civil War"). With the encouragement of the Kremlin, the area was used as a staging ground during the Civil War for the [Chinese Communists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_China "Communist Party of China"), who were victorious in 1949 and have controlled ever since.

## Contemporary

The European powers had control of other parts of Asia by the early 20th century, such as [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj"), [French Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina "French Indochina"), [Spanish East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_East_Indies "Spanish East Indies"), and Portuguese [Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macau "Macau") and [Goa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goa "Goa"). The [Great Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Game "Great Game") between Russia and Britain was the struggle for power in the Central Asian region in the nineteenth century. The [Trans-Siberian Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Siberian_Railway "Trans-Siberian Railway"), crossing Asia by train, was complete by 1916. Parts of Asia remained free from European control, although not influence, such as [Qajar Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_Iran "Qajar Iran"), [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand") and majority of China. In the 20th century, the [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan "Empire of Japan") expanded into China and Southeast Asia during the [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War "Second Sino-Japanese War") and [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). After the war, many Asian countries became independent from European powers. During the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War"), the northern parts of Asia were communist controlled with the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") and [People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China"), while western allies formed pacts such as [CENTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CENTO "CENTO") and [SEATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEATO "SEATO"). Conflicts such as the [Korean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_War "Korean War"), [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War") and [Soviet invasion of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War "Soviet–Afghan War") were fought between communists and anti-communists. In the decades after the Second World War, a massive restructuring plan drove Japan to become the world's second-largest economy, a phenomenon known as the [Japanese post-war economic miracle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_post-war_economic_miracle "Japanese post-war economic miracle"). The [Arab–Israeli conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_conflict "Arab–Israeli conflict") has dominated much of the recent history of West Asia. After the Soviet Union's collapse in 1991, there were many new independent nations in Central Asia and Eastern Europe.

### China

Prior to [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), China faced a civil war between [Mao Zedong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao_Zedong "Mao Zedong")'s Communist party and [Chiang Kai-shek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Kai-shek "Chiang Kai-shek")'s nationalist party; the nationalists appeared to be in the lead. However, once the [Japanese invaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War "Second Sino-Japanese War") in 1937, the two parties were forced to form a temporary ceasefire in order to defend China. The nationalists faced many military failures that caused them to lose territory and subsequently, respect from the Chinese masses. In contrast, the communists' use of guerilla warfare (led by [Lin Biao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lin_Biao "Lin Biao")) proved effective against the Japanese's conventional methods and put the Communist Party on top by 1945. They also gained popularity for the reforms they were already applying in controlled areas, including land redistribution, education reforms, and widespread health care. For the next four years, the nationalists would be forced to retreat to the small island east of Fujian province, known as [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Taiwan "Geography of Taiwan") (formerly known as Formosa), where they remain today. In mainland China, [People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") was established by the Communist Party, with Mao Zedong as its [state chairman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_China "President of China").

The communist government in China was defined by the party [cadres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_revolutionaries "Professional revolutionaries"). These hard-line officers controlled the [People's Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Liberation_Army "People's Liberation Army"), which itself controlled large amounts of the bureaucracy. This system was further controlled by the [Central Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Committee_of_the_Communist_Party_of_China "Central Committee of the Communist Party of China"), which additionally supported the state chairman who was considered the head of the government. The People's Republic's foreign policies included the repressing of [secession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secession "Secession") attempts in Mongolia and Tibet and supporting of [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea "North Korea") and [North Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vietnam "North Vietnam") in the [Korean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_War "Korean War") and [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War"), respectively. By 1960 China and the USSR became adversaries, battling worldwide for control of local communist movements.

In modern times, China plays important roles in world economics and politics. China today is the world's second largest economy and the second fastest growing economy.

### South Asia

From the mid-18th century to the mid-19th century, large regions of India were gradually annexed by the [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company "East India Company"), a chartered company acting as a sovereign power on behalf of the British government. Dissatisfaction with [company rule in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_rule_in_India "Company rule in India") led to the [Indian Rebellion of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857 "Indian Rebellion of 1857"), which rocked parts of north and central India, and led to the dissolution of the company. India was afterwards ruled directly by the [British Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crown "The Crown"), in the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj"). After [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), a nationwide struggle for independence was launched by the [Indian National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Congress "Indian National Congress"), led by [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi "Mahatma Gandhi"), and noted for [nonviolence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonviolent_resistance "Nonviolent resistance"). Later, the [All-India Muslim League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-India_Muslim_League "All-India Muslim League") would advocate for a separate Muslim-majority [nation state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation_state "Nation state").

In August 1947, the British Indian Empire was [partitioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India "Partition of India") into the [Dominion of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_India "Dominion of India") and [Dominion of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Pakistan "Dominion of Pakistan"). In particular, the partition of [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_(British_India) "Punjab (British India)") and [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal,_British_India "Bengal, British India") led to rioting between Hindus, Muslims, and Sikhs in these provinces and spread to other nearby regions, leaving some 500,000 dead. The police and army units were largely ineffective. The British officers were gone, and the units were beginning to tolerate if not actually indulge in violence against their religious enemies. Also, this period saw one of the largest mass migrations anywhere in modern history, with a total of 12 million Hindus, Sikhs, and Muslims moving between the newly created nations of India and Pakistan (which gained independence on 15 and 14 August 1947 respectively). In 1971, [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh"), formerly [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan "East Pakistan") and [East Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bengal "East Bengal"), seceded from Pakistan through an [armed conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War "Bangladesh Liberation War") sparked by the rise of the [Bengali nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_nationalism "Bengali nationalism") and [self-determination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-determination "Self-determination") movement.

### Korea

During the period when the [Korean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_War "Korean War") occurred, Korea divided into North and South. [Syngman Rhee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syngman_Rhee "Syngman Rhee") became the first president of [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea "South Korea"), and [Kim Il Sung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Il_Sung "Kim Il Sung") became the supreme leader of [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea "North Korea"). After the war, the president of South Korea, Syngman Rhee attempts to become a dictator. The [April Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_Revolution "April Revolution") occurred, and eventually Syngman Rhee was exiled from his country. In 1963, [Park Chung Hee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Chung_Hee "Park Chung Hee") was empowered with a military coup d'état. He dispatched [Republic of Korea Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Korea_Army "Republic of Korea Army") to [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War"). And during this age, the economy of South Korea outran that of North Korea.

Although [Park Chung Hee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Chung_Hee "Park Chung Hee") improved the nation's economy, he was a dictator, so people didn't like him. Eventually, he was murdered by [Kim Jae-gyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Jae-gyu "Kim Jae-gyu"). In 1979, [Chun Doo-hwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chun_Doo-hwan "Chun Doo-hwan") was empowered by another coup d’état by military. He oppressed the resistances in the city of [Gwangju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwangju "Gwangju"), named the [Gwangju Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwangju_Uprising "Gwangju Uprising"). Despite the Gwangju Uprising, Chun Doo-hwan became the president. But the people resisted again in 1987, starting the [June Struggle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_Struggle "June Struggle"). As a result of Gwangju Uprising and June Struggle, South Korea finally became a democratic republic in 1987.

[Roh Tae-woo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roh_Tae-woo "Roh Tae-woo") (1988–93), [Kim Young-sam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Young-sam "Kim Young-sam") (1993–98), [Kim Dae-jung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Dae-jung "Kim Dae-jung") (1998–2003), [Roh Moo-hyun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roh_Moo-hyun "Roh Moo-hyun") (2003–2008), [Lee Myung-bak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Myung-bak "Lee Myung-bak") (2008–2013), [Park Geun-hye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Geun-hye "Park Geun-hye") (2013–2017), [Moon Jae-in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_Jae-in "Moon Jae-in") (2017–2022), [Yoon Suk Yeol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoon_Suk_Yeol "Yoon Suk Yeol") (2022–2025) and [Lee Jae Myung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Jae_Myung "Lee Jae Myung"), (2025–) were elected as a president in order after 1987. In 1960, [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea "North Korea") was far wealthier than [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea "South Korea"). But in 1970, South Korea begins to outrun the North Korean economy. In 2018, South Korea is ranked #10 in world [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP "GDP") ranking.
