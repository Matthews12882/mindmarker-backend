# China

**China**, officially the **People's Republic of China** (**PRC**), is a country in [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia"). It is divided into 33 [province-level divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province-level_divisions_of_China "Province-level divisions of China"), including two [special administrative regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_administrative_regions_of_China "Special administrative regions of China"). [Beijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing "Beijing") is the capital, while [Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai "Shanghai") is the [most populous city by urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_China_by_population "List of cities in China by population"). Its geography features the [Central Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhongyuan "Zhongyuan"), major rivers such as the [Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze "Yangtze") and [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River "Yellow River"), deserts, [subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical "Subtropical") and temperate forests, plateaus, and [mountain ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_range "Mountain range") such as the [Himalayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayas "Himalayas"). It is the [world's second-most populous country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_(United_Nations) "List of countries by population (United Nations)") after [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India"), with [a population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_of_China "Population of China") exceeding 1.4 billion (17% of the world's population). Covering 9.6 million square kilometers (3,700,000 sq mi), it is the [third largest country by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area "List of countries and dependencies by area").

Humans first arrived in China during the [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic "Paleolithic"). During the 2nd millennium BCE, [dynastic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynasties_in_Chinese_history "Dynasties in Chinese history") emerged. In 221 BCE, China was unified under the [Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_dynasty "Qin dynasty"), followed by the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty "Han dynasty"), ushering in two millennia of imperial rule across periods of unity and division. The [imperial era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_era_of_Chinese_history "Imperial era of Chinese history") saw the expansion of the [Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road "Silk Road") and the invention of [gunpowder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invention_of_gunpowder "Invention of gunpowder"), [paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_paper#Paper_in_China "History of paper"), [printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing "Printing"), and the [compass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass "Compass"). After increased Western political, economic, and philosophical influence, the empire was succeeded by the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_(1912%E2%80%931949) "Republic of China (1912–1949)") (ROC) after the [1911 Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1911_Revolution "1911 Revolution"). The [Warlord Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warlord_Era "Warlord Era") and [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War "Chinese Civil War") followed, interrupted by the [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War "Second Sino-Japanese War"), which ended in a Chinese victory. In 1949, the [Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Communist_Party "Chinese Communist Party") (CCP) [proclaimed the PRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_the_People%27s_Republic_of_China "Proclamation of the People's Republic of China") and the [ROC retreated to Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retreat_of_the_government_of_the_Republic_of_China_to_Taiwan "Retreat of the government of the Republic of China to Taiwan"). Both sides still claim political legitimacy. Attempts by the CCP to advance [its political program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maoism "Maoism") faltered through [famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Chinese_Famine "Great Chinese Famine") and political turmoil. The [reform and opening-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_and_opening_up "Reform and opening up") that began in 1978 moved China towards a [socialist market economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_market_economy "Socialist market economy"), spurring economic growth.

China is a [unitary state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state "Unitary state") with the CCP as its [sole ruling party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-party_state "One-party state"). It is one of the five [permanent members](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_members_of_the_United_Nations_Security_Council "Permanent members of the United Nations Security Council") of the [UN Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Security_Council "UN Security Council") and a member of [many multilateral organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_organization_membership_of_China "International organization membership of China"). China is the world's [largest manufacturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_manufacturing_output "List of countries by manufacturing output") and has the [second-largest economy by nominal GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(nominal) "List of countries by GDP (nominal)"). International organizations have criticized China, alleging authoritarianism and [human rights abuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_China "Human rights in China"). Possessing [a large military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Liberation_Army "People's Liberation Army"), [space program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_space_program "Chinese space program"), and [nuclear stockpile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapons_of_China "Nuclear weapons of China"), China has been described as a [superpower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superpower "Superpower") due to its influence in geopolitics, [science and technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_technology_in_China "Science and technology in China"), [manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industry_in_China "Industry in China"), [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_China "Economy of China"), and [culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_culture "Chinese culture").

[Chinese art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_art "Chinese art") and culture have [influenced much of Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinosphere "Sinosphere"), with [a literary tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_literature "Chinese literature") dating back millennia to the [Chinese classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_classics "Chinese classics"). China is the birthplace of [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism "Confucianism") and [Daoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daoism "Daoism"), which form the traditional [three teachings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_teachings "Three teachings") of [Chinese philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_philosophy "Chinese philosophy") and [folk religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_folk_religion "Chinese folk religion") alongside [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"). [Chinese cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_cuisine "Chinese cuisine") is diverse and highly regional, with rice as a staple in the south and wheat in the north. It has [60 World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_China "List of World Heritage Sites in China"), including the [Great Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Wall_of_China "Great Wall of China") and [Grand Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Canal_(China) "Grand Canal (China)"). The [Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese "Han Chinese"), speakers of [Sinitic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinitic_languages "Sinitic languages"), are China's dominant ethnicity, although it is home to [55 recognized minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ethnic_groups_in_China "List of ethnic groups in China"), including the [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people "Hui people"), [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols "Mongols"), [Tibetans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetans "Tibetans"), [Uyghurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uyghurs "Uyghurs"), and [Zhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuang_people "Zhuang people").

## Etymology

The word "China" has been used in English since the 16th century; however, it was not used by the Chinese themselves during this period. Its origin has been traced through [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language "Portuguese language"), [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language "Malay language"), and [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language "Persian language") back to the [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit "Sanskrit") word *Cīna* (चिन), used in [ancient India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_India "Ancient India"). *Cīna* was first used in early [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism") scriptures dating from the 3rd century BCE to 4th century CE, including the *[Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata "Mahabharata")* and the *[Laws of Manu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manusmriti "Manusmriti")*. In 1655, the missionary [Martino Martini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martino_Martini "Martino Martini") suggested that the word China is derived ultimately from the ancient state of [Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_(state) "Qin (state)"). This remains a common etymology. Another possible source is the ancient [Guizhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guizhou "Guizhou") polity of [Yelang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yelang "Yelang"), known as *ʐina* in [Loloish languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loloish_languages "Loloish languages").

The official name of the modern state is the "People's Republic of China" (中华人民共和国; *Zhōnghuá Rénmín Gònghéguó*). The shorter form of this name is 中国; *Zhōngguó*, from *zhōng* ('central') and *guó* ('state'), a term first used for the [demesne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_land "Crown land") of the [Western Zhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Zhou "Western Zhou") dynasty. The names of ruling imperial dynasties were typically used to refer to the state and its territories. In the 1800s, *Zhongguo* was officially adopted as the name of the country by the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty "Qing dynasty").

## History

### Prehistory

Evidence of early humans such as *[Homo erectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_erectus "Homo erectus")* in China dates to the [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic "Paleolithic"), around 1.7 million years ago, with unconfirmed sites as old as 2 million years ago. Modern *[Homo sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_sapiens "Homo sapiens")* are attested from around 50,000 years ago. Following the end of the [Last Glacial Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Period "Last Glacial Period"), in about 8,000 BCE, pottery-making [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic "Neolithic") cultures emerged. By the 6000s BCE, sedentary agricultural societies had spread across the lower [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River "Yellow River") basin. These societies became increasingly complex, urbanized, and stratified, but many experienced a population collapse in the late 2000s BCE for unclear reasons.

### Ancient China

After 2000 BCE, a [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age "Bronze Age") culture emerged in the [Central Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhongyuan "Zhongyuan"), dubbed the [Erlitou culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erlitou_culture "Erlitou culture") after its [main site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erlitou "Erlitou"). Erlitou has been controversially identified with the [Xia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xia_dynasty "Xia dynasty"), the traditionally-accepted first [dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynasties_of_China "Dynasties of China"), but no written records exist to confirm this. The [Shang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang_dynasty "Shang dynasty") purportedly succeeded the Xia around 1600 BCE, with its early stages tentatively identified with an expansionist state known archaeologically as the [Erligang culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erligang_culture "Erligang culture"). The historicity of the [Late Shang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Shang "Late Shang") is attested through divinational writings in the [oracle bone script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle_bone_script "Oracle bone script"). These are the earliest known form of writing in China and the ancestor of modern [Chinese characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_characters "Chinese characters").

The Shang were overthrown by the [Zhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_dynasty "Zhou dynasty") c. 1046 BCE. The Zhou ruled over a vast and loose confederation of vassal states across central China, which was gradually weakened by regional lords. Centralized authority collapsed in 771 BCE, giving way to constant regional warfare. During the [Eastern Zhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Zhou "Eastern Zhou"), the multitude of small polities of the [Spring and Autumn period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_and_Autumn_period "Spring and Autumn period") evolved into seven territorial [Warring States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warring_States_period "Warring States period"). Literary and philosophical developments of this period include the emergence of various schools of thought, such as [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism "Confucianism"), [Daoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daoism "Daoism"), [Mohism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohism "Mohism"), and [Legalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legalism_(Chinese_philosophy) "Legalism (Chinese philosophy)"), alongside [Chinese classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_classics "Chinese classics") like the Confucian *[Analects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analects "Analects")* and the Daoist *[Dao De Jing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tao_Te_Ching "Tao Te Ching")*.

### Imperial China

#### Classical period

In the 220s BCE, [Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_(state) "Qin (state)") rapidly conquered the other warring states. In 221, its ruler [Qin Shi Huang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_Shi_Huang "Qin Shi Huang") proclaimed himself the first [emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_of_China "Emperor of China") (皇帝; *Huángdì*) and founded the [Qin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_dynasty "Qin dynasty"). He led an [autocratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocracy "Autocracy") Legalist state organized in a system of [commanderies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commandery_(China) "Commandery (China)") and [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_(China) "County (China)"). The dynasty lasted only fifteen years, falling soon after his death from [revolts and civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chu%E2%80%93Han_Contention "Chu–Han Contention").

After the collapse, the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty "Han dynasty") emerged to rule China between 206 BCE and 220 CE. The Han gradually reinstated centralized control, legitimizing their rule through Confucian scholarship and political institutions which would be maintained in some form into the 20th century. Military expeditions against the [Xiongnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiongnu "Xiongnu"), a confederation of nomadic [steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Steppe "Eurasian Steppe") tribes, expanded Han influence into parts of [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia") and helped to establish the [Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road "Silk Road"), allowing for trade connections between China and western Eurasia. Contemporaneously, merchants established [maritime trade routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Silk_Road "Maritime Silk Road") linking China, [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia"), and [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India"). The dynasty is the namesake of the [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese "Han Chinese"), China's main ethnic group.

The Han faced widespread uprisings and the emergence of local warlords in the 100s and 200s CE. By 220, the empire was split into the [Three Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms "Three Kingdoms"). These were briefly united by the [Jin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_dynasty_(266%E2%80%93420) "Jin dynasty (266–420)") in 280, which fell into civil war. [Sinicized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinicization "Sinicization") formerly-nomadic peoples who had settled in Northern China, such as the Xiongnu, rebelled and [founded new dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixteen_Kingdoms "Sixteen Kingdoms"). [Various Han Chinese dynasties in Southern China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_and_Southern_dynasties#Southern_dynasties "Northern and Southern dynasties") emerged in the 300s. During these conflicts, [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism") was introduced to China via the Silk Road.

#### Mid-imperial period

China was reunited under the [Sui dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_dynasty "Sui dynasty") in 589, which constructed the [Grand Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Canal_(China) "Grand Canal (China)") to link Northern and Southern China. The Sui collapsed in the 610s, and was succeeded by the [Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty "Tang dynasty"). The Tang dynasty centralized the state and dispatched military expeditions to pacify the surrounding regions. It promoted [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Buddhism "Chinese Buddhism"), but through expanding international trade built a heavily cosmopolitan society centered on its capital of [Chang'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang%27an "Chang'an"). It reformed the [civil service examinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_examination "Imperial examination") and oversaw a flourishing of art, poetry, architecture, and scholarship. The 755–763 [An Lushan rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Lushan_rebellion "An Lushan rebellion") weakened the Tang, which gradually fragmented before collapsing completely in 907, resulting in [another period of war and division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Dynasties_and_Ten_Kingdoms_period "Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms period").

The [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty "Song dynasty") rose to power in 960. It faced military crises, unable to subdue its Sinicized non-Han neighbors, the [Khitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khitan_people "Khitan people")-led [Liao dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liao_dynasty "Liao dynasty") and the [Tangut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangut_people "Tangut people")-led [Western Xia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Xia "Western Xia"). The Song's consistent focus on defense resulted in a heavily centralized state and military, which made the first military application of [gunpowder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunpowder "Gunpowder"). Books became widely available by the proliferation of [printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing "Printing") technology, while the elite class of [scholar-officials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholar-official "Scholar-official") grew increasingly powerful. Production, population, and trade expanded massively, alongside innovations such as industrial [metallurgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgy "Metallurgy") and [hydraulic machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_machinery "Hydraulic machinery"). The Song capital [Kaifeng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaifeng "Kaifeng") was overrun by the [Jurchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurchen_people "Jurchen people")-led [Jin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_dynasty_(1115%E2%80%931234) "Jin dynasty (1115–1234)") in 1127, forcing the Song to retreat to Southern China. During this period, the revivalist philosophical movement of [Neo-Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Confucianism "Neo-Confucianism") emerged.

#### Late imperial period

In 1206, [Genghis Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan "Genghis Khan") united the nomadic [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols "Mongols") to the north under the [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire "Mongol Empire"). Over the following fifty years, they [conquered the Western Xia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_conquest_of_Western_Xia "Mongol conquest of Western Xia") and [the Jin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_conquest_of_the_Jin_dynasty "Mongol conquest of the Jin dynasty"), in addition to [their other conquests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_conquests "Mongol conquests") as far west as Europe. [The empire split](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_the_Mongol_Empire "Division of the Mongol Empire") into separate khanates, with Mongol leader [Kublai Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kublai_Khan "Kublai Khan") establishing the [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty "Yuan dynasty") and [subjugating the Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_conquest_of_the_Song_dynasty "Mongol conquest of the Song dynasty") in the 1270s. Although Chinese culture remained largely unchanged, the Yuan maintained a Mongol elite culture and established a complex ethnic and occupational hierarchy. Foreign presence increased as China became more connected to Eurasian trade routes.

In the 1340s, central China was devastated by disease, famine, and severe flooding along the Yellow River. [Various rebellions erupted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Turban_Rebellions "Red Turban Rebellions"), leading to a peasant leader establishing the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty "Ming dynasty") in 1368 and declaring himself the [Hongwu Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongwu_Emperor "Hongwu Emperor"). The Ming later moved their capital from [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing "Nanjing") to [Beijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing "Beijing"), with the [Forbidden City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbidden_City "Forbidden City") as the imperial palace. However, the lower Yangtze remained the wealthiest region. Trade grew, and the [European colonization of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas "European colonization of the Americas") brought new crops and a massive influx of silver to China. The population doubled, and a growing publishing industry began producing works in [vernacular Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Written_vernacular_Chinese "Written vernacular Chinese"), including the [Four Classic Novels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_Chinese_Novels "Classic Chinese Novels").

The Ming state declined, and by the 1600s, became unable to contain [peasant rebellions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Ming_peasant_rebellions "Late Ming peasant rebellions"). The [Manchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_people "Manchu people") to the north declared the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty "Qing dynasty") in 1636 and [conquered the Ming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_from_Ming_to_Qing "Transition from Ming to Qing"), killing their last claimant emperor in 1662 and conquering a [Ming rump state on Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Tungning "Kingdom of Tungning") in 1683. From the late 1600s to the end of the 1700s, the [High Qing era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Qing_era "High Qing era") saw economic growth and territorial expansion westward, including the [conquest of Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_expedition_to_Tibet_(1720) "Chinese expedition to Tibet (1720)") and much of [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia").

European powers fought various wars against the Qing dynasty during the 19th century. These began with the United Kingdom's [First Opium War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Opium_War "First Opium War") in 1839–1842, which resulted in the first of the "[unequal treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unequal_treaties "Unequal treaties")" imposed on the dynasty. These opened [treaty ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_ports "Treaty ports"), allowed Christian missionary activity, and loosened trade restrictions. China faced an economic crisis and internal unrest, and uprisings such as the 1850–1864 [Taiping Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiping_Rebellion "Taiping Rebellion") resulted in the deaths of millions. Reformist factions of the Qing responded with the [Self-Strengthening Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-Strengthening_Movement "Self-Strengthening Movement"), seeking to adopt Western weapons and technologies, but this had a limited impact for much of the empire.

The [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan "Empire of Japan")'s defeat of the Qing in the [First Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Sino-Japanese_War "First Sino-Japanese War") (1894–1895) resulted in the loss of [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_Prefecture "Taiwan Prefecture") and the growth of both reformist and revolutionary political movements. An [imperial push for reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Days%27_Reform "Hundred Days' Reform") in 1898 ended after an internal coup, while the anti-foreign [Boxer Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxer_Rebellion "Boxer Rebellion") was defeated by a [coalition of foreign powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight-Nation_Alliance "Eight-Nation Alliance") in 1901. The late Qing dynasty again [advanced reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Qing_reforms "Late Qing reforms"), but growing revolutionary and anti-Manchu sentiment culminated in the [1911 Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1911_Revolution "1911 Revolution"). A coalition of revolutionaries led by [Sun Yat-sen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Yat-sen "Sun Yat-sen") overthrew the Qing and declared the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_(1912%E2%80%931949) "Republic of China (1912–1949)") (ROC) in 1912.

### Republic of China

President [Yuan Shikai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_Shikai "Yuan Shikai") rose to power in 1912 and crushed his main opposition, the [Kuomintang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuomintang "Kuomintang"), ruling China as a dictator until his death in 1916, following an abortive attempt to restore the monarchy. During the [Warlord Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warlord_Era "Warlord Era"), feuding regional warlords and governors took power across China, while Tibet and [Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogd_Khanate_of_Mongolia "Bogd Khanate of Mongolia") declared independence. The nominal republican government had little control outside Beijing. During this period, [New Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Culture_Movement "New Culture Movement") intellectuals and students criticized traditional society. A crackdown on a student protest in 1919 led to the [May Fourth Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Fourth_Movement "May Fourth Movement") and further agitation by intellectuals for a cultural and political upheaval, with many embracing Western political ideas such as [communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism "Communism").

In the mid-1920s, the Kuomintang [allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_United_Front "First United Front") with the nascent [Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Communist_Party "Chinese Communist Party") (CCP), and partially reunified China under [Chiang Kai-shek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Kai-shek "Chiang Kai-shek")'s leadership. Starting with the [Shanghai massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_massacre "Shanghai massacre") in 1927, the Kuomintang violently purged the CCP and gained the allegiance of the northern warlords, establishing a new government at Nanjing. The CCP was driven into the countryside and [repressed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Terror_(China) "White Terror (China)"), but subsequently [regrouped in the northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March "Long March").

Japan [occupied Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_invasion_of_Manchuria "Japanese invasion of Manchuria") in 1931, and launched an [invasion of the rest of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War "Second Sino-Japanese War") in 1937. A [renewed coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_United_Front "Second United Front") between the CCP and Kuomintang fought Japan in what became a theater of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), as Japanese forces committed numerous [war atrocities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_war_crimes "Japanese war crimes") against the civilian population and occupied most of China's major cities. After the [surrender of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrender_of_Japan "Surrender of Japan") in 1945, China became a founding member of the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations") and regained control over Manchuria and Taiwan. The [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War "Chinese Civil War") between the CCP and the Kuomintang resumed the following year.

### People's Republic of China

After a string of military victories, CCP chairman [Mao Zedong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao_Zedong "Mao Zedong") formally [proclaimed the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_the_People%27s_Republic_of_China "Proclamation of the People's Republic of China") (PRC) in 1949. The ROC government [retreated to Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retreat_of_the_government_of_the_Republic_of_China_to_Taiwan "Retreat of the government of the Republic of China to Taiwan") and continued to claim legitimacy. The following year, the PRC [occupied and annexed Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Tibet_by_the_People%27s_Republic_of_China "Annexation of Tibet by the People's Republic of China"). The PRC, which the CCP described as a "[people's democratic dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_democratic_dictatorship "People's democratic dictatorship")", enacted many reforms: it restructured the economy under state control, promoted literacy and gender equality, and expanded heavy industry. The [Land Reform Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_Reform_Movement "Land Reform Movement"), in which the state encouraged and organized peasant violence against landlords, leading to the death of upwards of a million people, had begun in northern China during the civil war and extended to most of the rest of China between 1950 and 1952. It distributed landlord land to the peasants as private property, but it was soon followed by collectivization of the land.

In 1958, Mao launched the [Great Leap Forward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Leap_Forward "Great Leap Forward"), a mass industrialization project which resulted in the [Great Chinese Famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Chinese_Famine "Great Chinese Famine") and millions of deaths. As the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War") deepened, China [grew politically isolated from its former ally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Soviet_split "Sino-Soviet split"), the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"), as well as the [Western Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Bloc "Western Bloc"). It [detonated its first atomic bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_596 "Project 596") in 1964. Attempting to reassert control over the CCP after the Great Leap, Mao and his allies launched the [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution "Cultural Revolution") in 1966, sparking a decade of political violence, crackdowns on perceived counterrevolutionaries, and social upheaval that lasted until [Mao's death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_state_funeral_of_Mao_Zedong "Death and state funeral of Mao Zedong") in 1976.

[Deng Xiaoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Xiaoping "Deng Xiaoping") served as [paramount leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_leader "Paramount leader") from 1978 to 1989. The [reform and opening-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_and_opening_up "Reform and opening up") policies of the 1980s and 1990s saw economic liberalization towards a [socialist market economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_market_economy "Socialist market economy"), rapid economic growth, and improved relations with the West. [Political dissent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protest_and_dissent_in_China "Protest and dissent in China") culminated in a mild crackdown early in 1987 and a much more severe and prolonged crackdown in 1989 that included the [Tiananmen Square massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Tiananmen_Square_protests_and_massacre "1989 Tiananmen Square protests and massacre"). China shifted away from an agricultural economy and rapidly urbanized, although this led to stark [economic inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_inequality_in_China "Economic inequality in China") between urban and rural areas, as well as environmental deterioration. Since the 1990s, market forces have become China's main economic driver. [Xi Jinping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi_Jinping "Xi Jinping") became [CCP general secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretary_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party "General Secretary of the Chinese Communist Party") in 2012; he has launched a far-reaching [anti-corruption campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-corruption_campaign_under_Xi_Jinping "Anti-corruption campaign under Xi Jinping"), expanded Chinese economic influence through the [Belt and Road Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belt_and_Road_Initiative "Belt and Road Initiative"), and abolished [term limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Term_limit "Term limit") for himself as [state president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_China "President of China").

## Geography

China's geography is highly varied, featuring a [dry and mountainous west](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_China "Western China"), alongside both mountains and wide river valleys in the east. Chinese civilization was traditionally centered in the lowlands around China's two longest rivers: the [Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze "Yangtze") and the [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River "Yellow River"), both originating on the [Tibetan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Plateau "Tibetan Plateau"). This temperate region is bordered by forest and [steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe "Steppe") to the north, as well as the mountainous and subtropical [south](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_and_southern_China "Northern and southern China"). China's shoreline spans the [Bohai Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohai_Sea "Bohai Sea"), [Yellow Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Sea "Yellow Sea"), [East China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_China_Sea "East China Sea"), and [South China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_Sea "South China Sea"), all part of the [Pacific Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean "Pacific Ocean"). Its largest island is [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Taiwan "Geography of Taiwan"), and the largest governed by the PRC is [Hainan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hainan "Hainan"). [Poyang Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poyang_Lake "Poyang Lake") is the largest of the country's many freshwater lakes.

Large mountain ranges in the west, including the [Himalayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayas "Himalayas") and [Tian Shan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tian_Shan "Tian Shan"), separate China from [South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia") and [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia"). China features both the [Turpan Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turpan_Depression "Turpan Depression"), among the deepest points on land, and the high Tibetan Plateau. The world's highest point, [Mount Everest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Everest "Mount Everest") (8,848 metres or 29,029 feet above [sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level "Sea level")), lies on the border with [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal "Nepal"). China has a wide variety of ore and mineral resources.

China's climate is dominated by [dry seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_season "Dry season") and wet [monsoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoons "Monsoons"), which lead to pronounced seasonal temperature differences. In the winter, northern winds coming from high-latitude areas are cold and dry; in summer, southern winds from coastal areas at lower latitudes are warm and moist. The northwestern deserts receive as little as 50 mm (2.0 in) of rainfall annually, while much of southern China exceeds 1,000 mm (39 in). Eastern China is well-suited for agriculture, with two or three harvests per year. The south is dominated by [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice "Rice") farming, while the north grows crops such as [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat "Wheat") and [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize "Maize").

China frequently [faces natural disasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_disasters_in_China "Natural disasters in China"). Its adjacency to both the [Pacific Ring of Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_Fire "Ring of Fire") and the [Alpide belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpide_belt "Alpide belt") (tectonic belts) results in frequent earthquakes, the cause of the majority of China's natural disaster deaths. Severe quakes such as the [1976 Tangshan earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Tangshan_earthquake "1976 Tangshan earthquake") cause hundreds of thousands of deaths. Rainstorms and flooding are common hazards, with the Yellow River's perennial floods devastating the surrounding plains throughout recorded history. [Typhoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon "Typhoon") frequently impact southeastern China.

### Environment

China is one of 17 [megadiverse countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megadiverse_countries "Megadiverse countries"), containing a wide variety of forests, [shrublands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrubland "Shrubland"), wetlands, [steppes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe "Steppe"), and deserts, which host (as of 2018) 92,300 known species and subspecies of plants, animals, and fungi. According to government surveys, China's forest coverage grew from 10% of the overall territory in 1949 to 25% in 2024. The far north contains [taiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiga "Taiga"), which transitions into [deciduous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deciduous "Deciduous") forest to the south. South of the Yangtze, mixed conifer and deciduous forest is common, with tropical and subtropical forests farther south.

In the 21st century, China has suffered from [environmental deterioration and pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_issues_in_China "Environmental issues in China") due to rapid development, alongside the broader effects of [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change "Climate change"). About 11% of its plant species and 21% of its [vertebrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebrate "Vertebrate") species are threatened or endangered due to [habitat loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat_loss "Habitat loss"), pollution, and [poaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poaching "Poaching"). "Flagship" endangered species such as the [giant panda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_panda "Giant panda") and [tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger "Tiger") have received additional funding and protection.

Although China has the world's highest [CO2 emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_carbon_dioxide_emissions "List of countries by carbon dioxide emissions"), it only emits 8 tons of [CO2 per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_carbon_dioxide_emissions_per_capita "List of countries by carbon dioxide emissions per capita") as of 2020, significantly lower than many developed countries. Total [greenhouse gas emissions by China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions_by_China "Greenhouse gas emissions by China") are the [world's largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_greenhouse_gas_emissions "List of countries by greenhouse gas emissions"). China has prioritized reducing pollution, announcing its aims to reach its peak emissions levels before 2030, and achieve [carbon neutrality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_neutrality "Carbon neutrality") by 2060. [Water quality in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_pollution_in_China "Water pollution in China") has been impacted by rapid development and high levels of [agricultural runoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_pollution "Agricultural pollution") and [heavy metal pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metal_pollution "Heavy metal pollution").

China is the world's leading investor in [renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy "Renewable energy") and [its commercialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_commercialization "Renewable energy commercialization"), investing CN¥3.6 trillion (US$546 billion) in 2022 alone. Traditionally reliant on non-renewable energy sources such as [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_in_China "Coal in China"), China's adoption of [renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_in_China "Renewable energy in China") has increased significantly in the 2010s and 2020s. In 2025, 54.4% of China's electricity came from coal, while 41.6% came from clean energy, with the remainder from other [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel "Fossil fuel"). Despite its emphasis on renewables, China remains dependent on global oil markets.

### Political geography

China is the [second-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area "List of countries and dependencies by area") country by land area after [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia "Russia"), and the third or fourth largest by total area. Total area figures are generally around 9,596,961 km2 (3,705,407 sq mi). China [borders 14 nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borders_of_China "Borders of China"), with a combined land border length of 22,117 km (13,743 mi). Its [coastline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastline_of_China "Coastline of China") spans approximately 14,500 km (9,000 mi).

The PRC has engaged in 23 border disputes since 1949, of which six remain unsettled. It actively [disputes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Indian_border_dispute "Sino-Indian border dispute") several portions of its Himalayan border with India, including [Aksai Chin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aksai_Chin "Aksai Chin") in [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir "Kashmir") and the Indian state of [Arunachal Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arunachal_Pradesh "Arunachal Pradesh"), which led to [military skirmishes in 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%932021_China%E2%80%93India_skirmishes "2020–2021 China–India skirmishes"). It maintains maritime disputes with Japan [over the Senkaku/Diaoyu Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senkaku_Islands_dispute "Senkaku Islands dispute") and with various countries over a group of [South China Sea Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_Sea_Islands "South China Sea Islands") within the "[Nine-dash line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine-dash_line "Nine-dash line")", including the [Spratlys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spratly_Islands "Spratly Islands") and [Paracels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracel_Islands "Paracel Islands"). This has led to [skirmishes with Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Vietnamese_conflicts_(1979%E2%80%931991) "Sino-Vietnamese conflicts (1979–1991)") and the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines").

Both the PRC and the ROC on Taiwan claim legitimacy as the government of China, leading to [cross-strait tensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-strait_relations "Cross-strait relations"). Taiwan is a [*de jure* province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_(People%27s_Republic_of_China_province) "Taiwan (People's Republic of China province)") of the PRC, with the ROC-controlled islands of [Kinmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinmen "Kinmen") and [Matsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsu_Islands "Matsu Islands") as a part of [Fujian Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujian "Fujian").

## Politics

China is a [unitary state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state "Unitary state") under the [absolute leadership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Party_leads_everything "The Party leads everything") of the Chinese Communist Party. Since the 1980s, the CCP has called its guiding theories and policies [socialism with Chinese characteristics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism_with_Chinese_characteristics "Socialism with Chinese characteristics"), which it considers an adapted form of [Marxism–Leninism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism%E2%80%93Leninism "Marxism–Leninism") that emphasizes a market economy as the [primary stage of socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_stage_of_socialism "Primary stage of socialism"). China describes itself as a [socialist state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_state_(communism) "Socialist state (communism)"), and a [people's democratic dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_democratic_dictatorship "People's democratic dictatorship"). Many academic sources describe it as an [authoritarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism "Authoritarianism") state, although others dispute this label as reductive, or as oversimplifying its methods of political control.

The [National People's Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_People%27s_Congress "National People's Congress") (NPC) holds the [unified powers of the state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_state_power "Unified state power") and oversight over all state organs, with its [Standing Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_Committee_of_the_National_People%27s_Congress "Standing Committee of the National People's Congress") elected to meet between the annual NPC meetings. NPC elections are [indirect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indirect_election "Indirect election") and the CCP controls nominations. The NPC elects the [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_China "President of China"), [vice president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_China "Vice President of China"), [military chairman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_of_the_Central_Military_Commission_(China) "Chairman of the Central Military Commission (China)"), and [chief justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Supreme_People%27s_Court "President of the Supreme People's Court"), and approves the president's nomination for [premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_China "Premier of China") (the head of government). In practice, the CCP leadership chooses candidates for these posts. The premier heads the [State Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Council_of_China "State Council of China"), which includes 26 ministers, such as the heads of ministries and commissions. The [Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_People%27s_Political_Consultative_Conference "Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference") is an advisory body that formally leads the CCP's [united front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_front_(China) "United front (China)") system, which aims to gain support from [non-CCP intellectuals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Party_intellectuals "Non-Party intellectuals"), eight [minor parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_parties_(China) "Democratic parties (China)"), and [people's organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_organization "People's organization").

[China's current constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_China "Constitution of China") dates to 1982, succeeding two earlier constitutions. Chinese governance is characterized by a high degree of political centralization but significant economic decentralization. Policy instruments and processes are often tested locally before being adopted more widely. Surveys have generally shown that the Chinese public has a high level of support for the government.

### Chinese Communist Party

The CCP is the founding and [sole ruling party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-party_state "One-party state") of China, organized along [Leninist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leninist "Leninist") principles as a [vanguard party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanguardism "Vanguardism"). With about 101 million members, it is among the [world's largest political parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_political_parties "List of largest political parties"), as well as the longest continuously ruling. The party's highest body is its [National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Congress_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party "National Congress of the Chinese Communist Party"), which mainly consists of members elected by party membership and meets every five years. The National Congress elects the [Central Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Committee_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party "Central Committee of the Chinese Communist Party"), which convenes yearly as the highest party organ between congresses. The Central Committee ostensibly elects the party's top leadership, the [Politburo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politburo_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party "Politburo of the Chinese Communist Party"), [Politburo Standing Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politburo_Standing_Committee_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party "Politburo Standing Committee of the Chinese Communist Party") (PSC) and the [general secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretary_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party "General Secretary of the Chinese Communist Party"), its top leader. In practice, the committee typically approves a slate of candidates created by existing party leadership.

The Politburo usually gathers once a month, while the smaller PSC is thought to meet weekly. The current general secretary is [Xi Jinping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi_Jinping "Xi Jinping"), who took office on 15 November 2012. Xi concurrently serves as president and the chair of the [Central Military Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Military_Commission_(China) "Central Military Commission (China)") (CMC); as such, he is referred to informally as the [paramount leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_leader "Paramount leader") of China. The National Congress also elects the [Central Commission for Discipline Inspection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Commission_for_Discipline_Inspection "Central Commission for Discipline Inspection"), the party's main disciplinary and anti-corruption organ.

As the CCP is closely intertwined with the government, disputes within the party represent the main form of political contention in China. The CCP controls government appointments, with most senior officials being CCP members. The appointments of [CCP cadres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadre_system_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party "Cadre system of the Chinese Communist Party") and the leadership of major [state-owned enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State-owned_enterprises_of_China "State-owned enterprises of China") and institutions are managed by the party's [Organization Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_Department_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party "Organization Department of the Chinese Communist Party"). The Leninist principle of [democratic centralism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_centralism "Democratic centralism") mandates that party decisions are binding on all members.

### Administrative divisions

China is constitutionally a [unitary state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state "Unitary state") divided into 23 [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_China "Provinces of China"), five [autonomous regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_regions_of_China "Autonomous regions of China") (each with a designated minority group), four [direct-administered municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct-administered_municipality "Direct-administered municipality"), as well as the autonomous [special administrative regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_administrative_regions_of_China "Special administrative regions of China") (SARs) of Hong Kong and Macau. Provinces and their equivalents are divided into [prefectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefecture_(China) "Prefecture (China)") and [prefecture-level cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefecture-level_city "Prefecture-level city"). These are divided into [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_(China) "County (China)"), [county-level cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County-level_city "County-level city"), and [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_(China) "District (China)"). Under these, [townships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Township_(China) "Township (China)") are the basic form of local government. [Direct elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_China "Elections in China") occur only at the township and county levels and below.

### Foreign relations

China is one of five permanent members of the [United Nations Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council "United Nations Security Council") and the second largest contributor to the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations"). China has the largest diplomatic network of any country: It has diplomatic relations with 179 [United Nations member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_states_of_the_United_Nations "Member states of the United Nations") and [embassies in 174](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_diplomatic_missions_of_China "List of diplomatic missions of China"). China is a member of the [G20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G20 "G20"), [BRICS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRICS "BRICS"), [East Asia Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia_Summit "East Asia Summit"), [APEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific_Economic_Cooperation "Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation"), and other intergovernmental organizations.

China is widely described as a [superpower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superpower "Superpower") due to its influence in geopolitics, technology, manufacturing, economics and culture. Although China moderated its relations with the West during the [reform era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_and_opening_up "Reform and opening up"), its rise in power has brought political tensions with many of its neighbors and [with the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93United_States_relations "China–United States relations"), the current dominant superpower. China is among the United States' largest trade partners, but their relationship is tempered by [a trade war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93United_States_trade_war "China–United States trade war") and disagreements over the [political status of Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_status_of_Taiwan "Political status of Taiwan").

China's foreign policy is based on the [Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Principles_of_Peaceful_Coexistence "Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence"), which include a principle of non-intervention in other countries' domestic affairs. Per its policy of non-alignment, China has no [defense pacts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_pact "Defense pact") except for [its 1961 treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_Friendship,_Cooperation_and_Mutual_Assistance_between_China_and_the_DPRK "Treaty on Friendship, Cooperation and Mutual Assistance between China and the DPRK") with [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea "North Korea"), although it maintains close relationships with neighbors such as [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan") and Russia. China has invested heavily in [developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_country "Developing country") across Asia, [Latin America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_America "Latin America"), and [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa "Africa"), creating a system of [development banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_bank "Development bank") and loans through the [Belt and Road Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belt_and_Road_Initiative "Belt and Road Initiative").

### Military

The [People's Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Liberation_Army "People's Liberation Army") (PLA) is the main armed forces of China, under the [direct control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Party_commands_the_gun "The Party commands the gun") of the CCP. It consists of four services: the [Ground Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Liberation_Army_Ground_Force "People's Liberation Army Ground Force"), the [Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Liberation_Army_Navy "People's Liberation Army Navy"), the [Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Liberation_Army_Air_Force "People's Liberation Army Air Force"), and the [Rocket Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Liberation_Army_Rocket_Force "People's Liberation Army Rocket Force"), and four arms: the [Aerospace Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Liberation_Army_Aerospace_Force "People's Liberation Army Aerospace Force"), the [Cyberspace Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Liberation_Army_Cyberspace_Force "People's Liberation Army Cyberspace Force"), the [Information Support Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Liberation_Army_Information_Support_Force "People's Liberation Army Information Support Force"), and the [Joint Logistics Support Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Liberation_Army_Joint_Logistics_Support_Force "People's Liberation Army Joint Logistics Support Force"). It has around 2 million active soldiers, and the world's [third largest stockpile of nuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapons_of_China "Nuclear weapons of China"), and the world's largest navy by ship count.

The PLA is considered one of the world's most powerful militaries and has [rapidly modernized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernization_of_the_People%27s_Liberation_Army "Modernization of the People's Liberation Army") in the 21st century. A 2026 study estimates that its 2025 expenditure was 2.27 trillion yuan (US$336 billion), 12% of global military spending and 1.7% of China's GDP. The PLA, the [People's Armed Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Armed_Police "People's Armed Police") and the [Militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militia_(China) "Militia (China)") are commanded by the Central Military Commission. Due to the [chairman responsibility system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_responsibility_system "Chairman responsibility system"), the [chairman of the CMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_of_the_Central_Military_Commission_(China) "Chairman of the Central Military Commission (China)"), currently Xi Jinping, is the [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_command_of_the_armed_forces_in_China "Supreme command of the armed forces in China") of the PLA.

### Law and policing

The [Chinese judicial system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_system_of_China "Judicial system of China") consists of [people's courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_people%27s_court "Local people's court") at provincial, regional, and local levels, alongside circuit courts for regional matters and specialized courts to handle professional disputes. The highest legal authority is the [Supreme People's Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_People%27s_Court "Supreme People's Court"). The courts are closely controlled by the CCP, supervised by corresponding [people's congresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_of_people%27s_congress "System of people's congress"), and led by CCP appointees. [PRC law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_the_People%27s_Republic_of_China "Law of the People's Republic of China") derives from a combination of [civil law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_(legal_system) "Civil law (legal system)"), [traditional Chinese law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_law "Chinese law"), and [socialist law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_law "Socialist law").

China's law enforcement system is managed by the [Ministry of Public Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Public_Security_(China) "Ministry of Public Security (China)"), which oversees [public security bureaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_security_bureau_(China) "Public security bureau (China)") (PSBs) in each province, themselves divided into local levels. Local sub-bureaus and stations handle most routine policing. The [People's Armed Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Armed_Police "People's Armed Police"), a [paramilitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramilitary "Paramilitary") force, is administered by the CMC and supports emergency response measures. The [social credit system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_credit_system "Social credit system") maintains a network of [blacklists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blacklisting "Blacklisting") to record businesses and individuals who violate regulations, court orders, or financial agreements. China has periodically mobilized anti-crime campaigns, using heavy penalties as a form of deterrence. Violent crimes are the highest priority for enforcement, although they occur at relatively low rates. [Capital punishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_China "Capital punishment in China") has declined since the Mao era, but remains common; China has the most executions per year of any country.

### Human rights

Various human rights organizations, United Nations assessments, and foreign governments have accused China of severe human rights violations. It has been accused of political repression, torture, [indefinite detention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indefinite_detention "Indefinite detention"), restrictions on free speech and religious practice, excessive use of capital punishment, and the persecution of ethnic minorities.

Although the Chinese constitution protects a wide variety of civil and political rights, these largely lack enforcement mechanisms. The [petitioning system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petitioning_(China) "Petitioning (China)") allows citizens to raise complaints of wrongdoing, although the concerns are often left unaddressed, and some petitioners have faced punitive arrest or incarceration. In the 21st century, China has participated extensively in the [United Nations Human Rights Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Human_Rights_Council "United Nations Human Rights Council"), but remains strongly critical of international scrutiny of its human rights, seeing it as interference in its internal affairs.

In [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang "Xinjiang"), China has persecuted the [Uyghur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uyghurs "Uyghurs") population through [widespread discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Uyghurs_in_China "Persecution of Uyghurs in China"), police crackdowns, and [restrictions on Islamic religious practice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamophobia_in_China "Islamophobia in China") and the use of the [Uyghur language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uyghur_language "Uyghur language"). China interred over a million Uyghurs in a [network of internment camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang_internment_camps "Xinjiang internment camps") in the late 2010s. These facilities have evolved into a system of prisons and [forced labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uyghur_forced_labour "Uyghur forced labour"). Several UN [OHCHR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_United_Nations_High_Commissioner_for_Human_Rights "Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights") experts have equated it to "[enslavement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_China "Slavery in China") as a [crime against humanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_against_humanity "Crime against humanity")". Alongside evidence of [compulsory sterilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compulsory_sterilization "Compulsory sterilization"), many [genocide scholars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide_scholar "Genocide scholar") have described these actions in Xinjiang as a [genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide "Genocide") or [cultural genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_genocide "Cultural genocide").

China has restricted the civil and political rights of [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong "Hong Kong") residents. Since the [2019–2020 protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%932020_Hong_Kong_protests "2019–2020 Hong Kong protests"), China has cracked down on the [pro-democracy movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-democracy_camp_(Hong_Kong) "Pro-democracy camp (Hong Kong)") in the SAR and removed much of its political autonomy. China has cracked down on political expression in Tibet and strengthened surveillance since the late 2000s, restricting residents from obtaining passports and most foreigners from entering the region.

China does not recognize [same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage "Same-sex marriage") or [same-sex adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_adoption "Same-sex adoption"), and regularly censors [LGBTQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_people "LGBTQ people")-related media. Many Chinese hospitals provide [sexual conversion therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversion_therapy "Conversion therapy"), viewing homosexuality as a mental disorder. [LGBTQ rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_rights_in_China "LGBTQ rights in China") advocates are surveilled and criminalized.

## Economy

The modern PRC is often described by analysts as an example of [state capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_capitalism "State capitalism") or [party-state capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party-state_capitalism "Party-state capitalism"), with both market forces and the state playing a major role in the economy. Various state organs manage the economy according to the State Council's [five-year plans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five-year_plans_of_China "Five-year plans of China"). China describes its economic system as a [socialist market economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_market_economy "Socialist market economy"), in which the market plays a decisive role in resource allocation under the control of a [socialist state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_state_(communism) "Socialist state (communism)"). The Chinese economy has expanded more than 20 times since 2000. It is now the [second largest economy by GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(nominal) "List of countries by GDP (nominal)") (135 trillion yuan) and [the largest when adjusted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(PPP) "List of countries by GDP (PPP)") for [purchasing power parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity "Purchasing power parity"). China has brought roughly 800 million people out of extreme [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_China "Poverty in China"), more than any other country in history.

China is the world's leading manufacturing power, accounting for 28% of global manufacturing as of 2024; it is the largest producer of [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_industry_in_China "Steel industry in China"), [rare earths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare-earth_industry_in_China "Rare-earth industry in China"), ships, and automobiles, as well as the largest exporter of electronics and [textiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile_industry_in_China "Textile industry in China").

The private sector constitutes the largest portion of the Chinese economy, with about 30–40% under state ownership. State-owned companies make less than 10% of exports, but are predominant in industries such as transportation, utilities, and financial services. Since the 1990s, China has decentralized its economic planning, with about 85% of government spending delegated to the provincial or local level, and local governments adapting national economic plans to suit local interests.

China's main [stock exchanges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_exchanges "Stock exchanges") are [Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_Stock_Exchange "Shanghai Stock Exchange") and [Shenzhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Stock_Exchange "Shenzhen Stock Exchange"). Its stock market is the second largest, behind the US. The [Chinese banking system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banking_in_China "Banking in China") makes up a much larger share of the economy than most other countries. Four state-owned banks hold about 40% of the sector's assets as of 2022. China has struggled to establish its currency, the [renminbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renminbi "Renminbi") (in units of yuan), as an international [reserve currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_currency "Reserve currency"); as of 2025, it is the seventh largest currency in foreign exchange reserves.

About a third of China's workforce consists of domestic migrant workers, the [floating population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating_population "Floating population"). The *[hukou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hukou "Hukou")* system restricts citizens' access to services and utilities outside of their registered home, leading to pronounced inequalities between urban and rural workers. Especially in larger cities, only those with local *hukou* may be able to buy property, though *hukou* restrictions have loosened significantly in the 21st century.

According to 2026 state data, China's urban unemployment rate was 5.0%. China has a high [debt-to-GDP ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debt-to-GDP_ratio "Debt-to-GDP ratio") (almost 300% in 2020), with [household debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Household_debt "Household debt") at about 65% of the GDP. China has faced a [property sector crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_property_sector_crisis_(2020%E2%80%93present) "Chinese property sector crisis (2020–present)") since 2020. By the late 2010s, Chinese real estate assets far exceed the combined total of stocks, [bonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_(finance) "Bond (finance)"), and [deposits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deposit_(finance) "Deposit (finance)").

### Science and technology

As the world's largest economy for much of its history, many inventions and scientific discoveries have been produced in China, including paper, printing, the compass, and gunpowder (referred to as the [Four Great Inventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Great_Inventions "Four Great Inventions")). China has pursued rapid technological development since the reform era. It has made great advances in the fields of [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence"), [robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotics "Robotics"), and [biotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotechnology "Biotechnology"), while the Xi administration has prioritized [green technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_technology "Green technology") and reducing Chinese dependence on foreign technology. It has emphasized [technocratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technocracy "Technocracy") leadership in the 2020s, promoting experts in scientific fields to national leadership positions, while bringing major private technology firms such as [Tencent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tencent "Tencent") and [Alibaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alibaba_Group "Alibaba Group") under state regulation. Geopolitical tensions have led Europe and the United States to restrict Chinese access to their technology, especially [semiconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor "Semiconductor"), and launch sanctions targeting the telecommunications and electronics firm [Huawei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huawei "Huawei").

Since launching [its first satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dong_Fang_Hong_1 "Dong Fang Hong 1") in 1970, China has maintained [an active space program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_space_program "Chinese space program"). After the United States and the Soviet Union, China became the third to independently put astronauts into orbit, with a [2003 mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_5 "Shenzhou 5"). The [Chinese Lunar Exploration Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Lunar_Exploration_Program "Chinese Lunar Exploration Program") has sent multiple robotic landers to the Moon, the [Planetary Exploration of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_Exploration_of_China "Planetary Exploration of China") program has sent [a lander and rover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianwen-1 "Tianwen-1") to [Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars "Mars"), and the [Tiangong space station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiangong_space_station "Tiangong space station") has hosted crewed operations in [Low Earth orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Earth_orbit "Low Earth orbit") since 2021.

### Infrastructure and transportation

Since the 1990s, automobiles have become a common form of transport in China. According to state data, the Chinese highway network exceeded 6,000,000 km (3,700,000 mi) by the end of 2021, having grown 30% over the past decade. In addition to local and provincial routes, this network includes the [national highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_highways_of_China "National highways of China"), which are divided between the ordinary highways (of various classes) and the [national expressway system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expressways_of_China "Expressways of China"). China has the largest highway and rail systems in the world by capacity, along with the [second longest rail network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_rail_transport_network_size "List of countries by rail transport network size") at roughly 165,000 km (102,526 mi). According to 2022 state data, over 95% of cities exceeding one million residents have access to [high-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail_in_China "High-speed rail in China"). Out of the [50 busiest container ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_container_ports "List of busiest container ports"), 18 are located in China, including the world's busiest, the [Port of Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Shanghai "Port of Shanghai"). China has the largest domestic aviation market by passenger capacity, and maintains 262 airports as of 2025.

Chinese cities are disparate in their level of infrastructure. [Tier-one cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_city_tier_system "Chinese city tier system") such as Beijing and Shanghai have elaborate and advanced infrastructure networks, while smaller cities and rural areas often have outdated and underfunded transit and public utilities. Water resources in China are unevenly distributed. With industrialization and rapid development, the drier north has faced endemic water shortages. To combat this, the [South–North Water Transfer Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South%E2%80%93North_Water_Transfer_Project "South–North Water Transfer Project") pumps water from the Yangtze basin to supply northern cities.

The [State Grid Corporation of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Grid_Corporation_of_China "State Grid Corporation of China"), the world's largest [utility company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_utility "Public utility"), manages the [electrical grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_grid "Electrical grid") for most of the country, with the [China Southern Power Grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Southern_Power_Grid "China Southern Power Grid") as the electric utility in some southern provinces. Power generation is primarily managed by five state-owned corporations, alongside local and provincial utilities. Most of China's renewable energy comes from [hydropower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectricity_in_China "Hydroelectricity in China"). The [Three Gorges Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Gorges_Dam "Three Gorges Dam") is the [world's largest power station by capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_power_stations "List of largest power stations").

China has the largest number of internet users and websites of any country. According to state data, it has 1.125 billion internet users as of 2025, about 80% of its population. It has an extensive [system of internet censorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_censorship_in_China "Internet censorship in China"). The [Great Firewall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Firewall "Great Firewall"), a system of internet controls and regulations, blocks access to many foreign websites. Users can circumvent it through [virtual private networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_private_network "Virtual private network"), but these face occasional crackdowns. The [Cyberspace Administration of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberspace_Administration_of_China "Cyberspace Administration of China") is the national internet regulator and censor. Most [telecommunications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_in_China "Telecommunications in China") are operated by state-owned companies.

## Demographics

The [2020 Chinese census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Chinese_census "2020 Chinese census") recorded the population as 1,411,778,724. About 17.95% were 14 years old or younger, 63.35% were between 15 and 59, and 18.7% were over 60. Between 2010 and 2020, the average population growth rate was 0.53%. Since 2022, deaths have outpaced births. China has one of the lowest [total fertility rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate "Total fertility rate") (births per woman) in the world, around 1.0 as of 2025. [China has a rapidly aging population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aging_of_China "Aging of China"): the proportion of Chinese over 65 is predicted to reach 26% by 2050, which would create a large economic and social burden from increased need for caretakers and medical assistance.

The [National Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bureau_of_Statistics_of_China "National Bureau of Statistics of China") estimated that the population fell 850,000 from 2021 to 2022, the first decline since 1961. In 2025, China recorded 5.6 births per 1000 people, the lowest recorded birthrate since at least 1949. China's population is concentrated in the eastern half of the country; although 64% of China's territory is west of the [Heihe–Tengchong Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heihe%E2%80%93Tengchong_Line "Heihe–Tengchong Line"), as of 2020 only 6.5% of its population lives west of the line.

Spurred by fears of [overpopulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overpopulation "Overpopulation"), China maintained a [one-child policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-child_policy "One-child policy") from 1979 to 2015, restricting most families to a single child. The policy was most strictly enforced in urban areas, while families from ethnic minorities and rural communities were often exempt from one-child limits. A two-child policy was introduced in 2015, and in July 2021, family size limits as well as penalties for exceeding them were removed. The policy resulted in a skewed [sex ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_ratio "Sex ratio"), as many families preferred to have sons, who were able to continue the patrilineal line and were perceived as better able to support the family. Through a combination of infanticide, [sex-selective abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex-selective_abortion "Sex-selective abortion"), and unreported female births, there were 120 boys born for every 100 girls by the late 1990s.

China maintains a restrictive [immigration policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_China "Immigration to China"), with permanent residence granted to only around 12,000 foreigners as of 2023, while an additional 845,697 [foreign nationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_national "Foreign national") lived in mainland China as of the 2020 census. The [Chinese diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_diaspora "Chinese diaspora") is among the largest on earth, comprising about 40 million people, mostly descended from [emigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_emigration "Chinese emigration") during the 19th and early 20th centuries. About two-thirds of this diaspora reside in [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Indonesians "Chinese Indonesians"), [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_Chinese "Thai Chinese"), [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Chinese "Malaysian Chinese"), and the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Americans "Chinese Americans").

### Cities and urbanization

China [has urbanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization_in_China "Urbanization in China") significantly since the 1980s. The percentage of the country's population living in urban areas increased from 20% in 1980 to over 68% in 2025, according to Chinese government figures, while the United Nations calculates the 2025 urbanization rate as 83.7%. Cities in China often include smaller towns and rural areas under their jurisdiction. [Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai "Shanghai") is its [most populous city by urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_China_by_population "List of cities in China by population"). Two [urban agglomerations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_agglomerations "Urban agglomerations"), the [Pearl Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River_Delta "Pearl River Delta") and [Yangtze Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_Delta "Yangtze Delta"), contain many of the largest cities and tens of millions of people.

### Languages and ethnic groups

China legally recognizes [56 distinct ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ethnic_groups_in_China "List of ethnic groups in China"). The largest of these are the [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese "Han Chinese"), who constitute more than 91% of the total population. The minority groups are generally found in the western and border regions of China. Social and wealth inequalities exist between many minority groups and the Han, especially in the rural [southwestern areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_China "Southwestern China"). The [Zhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuang_people "Zhuang people") of the southwest are the most populous minority group; other major groups include the [Uyghurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uyghurs "Uyghurs"), [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people "Hui people"), [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols_in_China "Mongols in China"), and [Tibetans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetans "Tibetans").

Most Han are speakers of [Mandarin Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese "Mandarin Chinese"). [Standard Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chinese "Standard Chinese"), largely based off the [Beijing dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing_dialect "Beijing dialect"), is the official language of China. Around 300 million people speak other [Chinese languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinitic_languages "Sinitic languages") (often described as 方言—local varieties or [dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialect "Dialect")—in Chinese sources). While also written with [Chinese characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_characters "Chinese characters"), these are not highly [mutually intelligible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_intelligibility "Mutual intelligibility") with Mandarin or each other. Major branches of Chinese include Mandarin, [Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Chinese "Wu Chinese"), [Yue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yue_Chinese "Yue Chinese") (Cantonese), [Min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_Chinese "Min Chinese"), [Gan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gan_Chinese "Gan Chinese"), [Hakka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_Chinese "Hakka Chinese"), and [Xiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiang_Chinese "Xiang Chinese"); the latter six are mainly spoken in Southern China. Since the 1950s, local dialects have steadily assimilated into Mandarin or one of the main regional registers.

[Simplified Chinese characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters "Simplified Chinese characters"), first standardized in the 1950s, are used to write Chinese throughout mainland China, while [traditional Chinese characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters "Traditional Chinese characters") remain in use in the [SARs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_administrative_regions_of_China "Special administrative regions of China") and Taiwan. [Hanyu Pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin "Pinyin") is the official [romanization system for Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Chinese "Romanization of Chinese") in the PRC.

Other language families are found throughout China. Distantly related to Chinese, [Tibeto-Burman languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibeto-Burman_languages "Tibeto-Burman languages") are found in the west and southwest. In southwestern China, many [Hmong–Mien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmong%E2%80%93Mien_languages "Hmong–Mien languages") and [Kra–Dai languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kra%E2%80%93Dai_languages "Kra–Dai languages") are spoken by minority groups. Offshoots of the [Austroasiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austroasiatic_languages "Austroasiatic languages") and [Austronesian language families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_languages "Austronesian languages") are also found in the south in smaller numbers. In the northwest, [Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_languages "Turkic languages") (including [Uyghur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uyghur_language "Uyghur language")) and [Mongolic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolic_languages "Mongolic languages") are found, as well as two [Indo-European languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_languages "Indo-European languages") ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language") and [Tajik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajik_language "Tajik language")). Several [Tungusic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungusic_languages "Tungusic languages") are found in northwestern and northeastern China. Since the 2010s, the Chinese government has intensified [linguistic assimilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_assimilation "Linguistic assimilation") policies, generally requiring schools to use Standard Chinese.

### Religion

The Chinese religious tradition is heavily syncretic, incorporating various [folk religious practices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_folk_religion "Chinese folk religion") alongside [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism "Confucianism"), [Taoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism "Taoism"), and [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"). These '[three teachings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_teachings "Three teachings")' were the three main institutionalized schools of Chinese religion. These exist alongside introduced minority religions such as [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity") and [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam"), as well as [various new religious movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_salvationist_religions "Chinese salvationist religions"). Statistics on affiliation are difficult to gather due to varying definitions of religion and the diffusive nature of local traditions. Many people follow religious teachings and practices without considering themselves adherents. [Local deities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudigong "Tudigong") are commonly worshiped alongside widely known gods, such as [Guan Yu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guan_Yu "Guan Yu") and [Mazu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazu "Mazu").

Confucianism is a philosophical and spiritual tradition that originated in the Eastern Zhou, emphasizing the study of the [classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_classics "Chinese classics") and the relations of the individual, society, and [heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tian "Tian"). It has sometimes been identified as a religion, following Western influences. Daoism emphasizes the *[Dao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dao "Dao")* ('way') as the world's origin and fundamental force, and seeks to harness it through spiritual cultivation. Initially a philosophical movement during the Eastern Zhou, the religious tradition of Daoism emerged during the 2nd century CE. [Mahayana Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahayana "Mahayana") was introduced to China during the Han dynasty and spread rapidly over the following centuries. Although often dismissed by Confucian elites, Buddhism greatly influenced other Chinese religious and philosophical traditions.

Although China and CCP are legally [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_atheism "State atheism"), [freedom of religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_religion_in_China "Freedom of religion in China") is formally guaranteed by the Chinese constitution. China [officially recognizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laws_regarding_religious_activities_in_China "Laws regarding religious activities in China") five religions: [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_China "Buddhism in China"), Taoism, [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_China_(1912%E2%80%93present) "Islam in China (1912–present)"), [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_China "Catholic Church in China"), and [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism_in_China "Protestantism in China"). Formal religious practice is managed by "patriotic associations" for each of the recognized faiths overseen by the CCP's [United Front Work Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Front_Work_Department "United Front Work Department"). However, the majority of religious activities take place outside of registered organizations. China has attempted to crack down on some forms of unregistered practice, such as Protestant [house churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_church_(China) "House church (China)"). Religious practices among minority groups, such as Islam among the Uyghur and [Tibetan Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism "Tibetan Buddhism"), have come under state scrutiny and regulation over fears of [separatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secession_in_China "Secession in China").

### Education

Following an optional two to three years of [preschool](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Preschool_in_China&action=edit&redlink=1 "Preschool in China (page does not exist)"), China has nine years of free [compulsory education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compulsory_education "Compulsory education"), generally with six years of elementary school and three years of junior high school. If they pass entrance examinations, students may then attend a senior high school, choosing between an academic or [vocational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocational_school "Vocational school") track. The *[gaokao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaokao "Gaokao")* exams allow access to [undergraduate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undergraduate_education "Undergraduate education") higher education, with additional examinations required to attend postgraduate programs. As of 2022, 95% of junior high students continue to high school, while higher education has a 59.6% enrollment rate.

As of 2022, China had 158 million students enrolled in compulsory school, and 46.5 million enrolled in higher education. There are over 200,000 elementary and junior high schools, over 22,000 secondary schools, and over 3,000 colleges and universities. Both private and public schools exist at all levels, with 10.6% of elementary and junior high students attending private schools. China became the world's [largest publisher of scientific papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_number_of_scientific_and_technical_journal_articles "List of countries by number of scientific and technical journal articles") in 2016. [Foreign language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_education "Language education") programs are mandatory from 3rd grade. Generally focused on English, other languages are increasingly being taught, especially at the senior high level.

### Health

The vast majority of [primary health care institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_health_care "Primary health care") in China offer both [allopathic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allopathic_medicine "Allopathic medicine") and [traditional Chinese medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_medicine "Traditional Chinese medicine") (TCM). In 2017, there were approximately 9,000 community health centers in urban areas—which oversaw roughly 25,000 health stations—and 37,000 township health centers in rural areas. There were about 639,000 village clinics, which operate as independent, for-profit enterprises subsidized by the state.

The [National Health Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Health_Commission "National Health Commission"), together with its local counterparts, oversees health policy. After the beginning of reform and opening-up in 1978, Chinese public health improved rapidly from better nutrition, although many free health services provided in the countryside disappeared. [Healthcare in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_China "Healthcare in China") became mostly privatized and experienced a significant quality increase. China is a major producer and exporter of [active pharmaceutical ingredients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_ingredient "Active ingredient").

According to 2025 state data, the [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy "Life expectancy") at birth exceeds 79 years, and the [infant mortality rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality "Infant mortality") is 4 per 1,000. Rates of [stunted growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stunted_growth "Stunted growth") declined from 33.1% in 1990 to 4.5% in 2024. Mental health services are underfunded and unevenly distributed. The [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic") was first identified in [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan "Wuhan"). China maintained a [zero-COVID policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-COVID "Zero-COVID"), using [lockdowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_lockdowns "COVID-19 lockdowns"), [quarantines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarantine "Quarantine"), and [contact tracing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_tracing "Contact tracing") to curb its transmission, until [protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_COVID-19_protests_in_China "2022 COVID-19 protests in China") in November–December 2022.

## Culture

Beginning in the Qin and Han dynasties and continuing over the following millennia, [an area of Chinese cultural influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinosphere "Sinosphere") grew to encompass much of East Asia. Japan, Korea, and Vietnam adopted various political, philosophical, religious, and aesthetic concepts from China, facilitated by their adoption of [Chinese characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_characters "Chinese characters") and participation in the [tributary system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary_system_of_China "Tributary system of China"). [China has 60 World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_China "List of World Heritage Sites in China"), including the [Great Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Wall_of_China "Great Wall of China") and Grand Canal, the second most of any country (behind [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy "Italy")).

Chinese culture has historically emphasized family connections, with [filial piety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filial_piety "Filial piety") and the [veneration of ancestors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veneration_of_the_dead "Veneration of the dead") seen as vital duties under Confucian ethics. Prior to the 20th century, family relations were highly [patriarchal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchy "Patriarchy"), and [arranged marriages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arranged_marriage "Arranged marriage") were commonplace. The CCP pushed for gender equality after coming to power, establishing the [All-China Women's Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-China_Women%27s_Federation "All-China Women's Federation") to manage women's issues. However, many patriarchal gender norms remain prevalent, and women face lower employment rates and [occupational gender segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupational_gender_segregation "Occupational gender segregation").

Prior to the adoption of the [Gregorian calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_calendar "Gregorian calendar") in 1912, China traditionally used a 12-month [lunisolar calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunisolar_calendar "Lunisolar calendar"). The 60-part [sexagenary cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexagenary_cycle "Sexagenary cycle") was often used to enumerate days as well as years, alongside the twelve-year [Chinese zodiac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_zodiac "Chinese zodiac"). [Major holidays and festivals in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_China "Public holidays in China") include the [Chinese New Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_New_Year "Chinese New Year") (or Spring Festival), the [Qingming Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingming_Festival "Qingming Festival"), the [Mid-Autumn Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Autumn_Festival "Mid-Autumn Festival"), and [National Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Day_of_the_People%27s_Republic_of_China "National Day of the People's Republic of China").

### Art

Traditional Chinese art makes use of a wide array of common motifs, metaphors, and symbols, often using nature as allegory. During the imperial era, skill in art was a marker of refinement and high social status. By the Song period, [landscape paintings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscape_painting "Landscape painting") became among the most prestigious genres. Renowned art pieces often bear inscriptions and [seals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seals_in_the_Sinosphere "Seals in the Sinosphere") from their previous owners, allowing for the tracing of [provenance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provenance "Provenance") over long periods of time.

Chinese paintings are traditionally made with [ink brushes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ink_brush "Ink brush") on paper or [silk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk "Silk"). Horizontal [handscrolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handscroll "Handscroll") and vertical [hanging scrolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanging_scroll "Hanging scroll") are common formats, alongside [wall paintings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mural "Mural"), [folding screens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folding_screen "Folding screen") and [hand fans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hand_fan "Hand fan"). [Calligraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_calligraphy "Chinese calligraphy"), emerging during the 3rd and 4th centuries CE, was traditionally regarded in China as the most prestigious form of art, and remains among the most widely practiced. Western painting styles gained popularity during the early 20th century, while the Mao government promoted art as a means to embody socialist ideals. The 21st century has seen a boom in the international influence of Chinese [contemporary art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_art "Contemporary art").

Pottery and metal crafts in China date to ancient times. [Porcelain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcelain "Porcelain"), also known as china, became a common form of ceramic during the 7th century. By the 14th century, it became popular to decorate porcelain vessels with elaborate [blue and white patterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_and_white_pottery "Blue and white pottery"), which became the most popular form of Chinese ceramics. [Jade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jade "Jade") crafts and [lacquerware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacquerware "Lacquerware") have been common decorative mediums since antiquity.

#### Architecture

Wood is the most commonly used material in traditional [Chinese architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_architecture "Chinese architecture"). Roofs of important buildings, generally ceramic tile, are often ornate and [hipped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip_roof "Hip roof"). Traditional buildings are often arranged around [cardinal directions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_direction "Cardinal direction"), with grandeur expressed through large complexes, often featuring [courtyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtyard "Courtyard") and large gates. The spiritual concept of [feng shui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feng_shui "Feng shui") influences the layout of houses and settlements, oriented around the flow of *[qi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qi "Qi")*, vital energy.

[Pagodas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagoda "Pagoda"), multi-story religious towers far taller than other forms of traditional architecture, were introduced to China alongside Buddhism, and comprise many of China's oldest buildings. Western-style architecture was introduced during the 1800s, while domestic architects began to implement Western design principles in the 1900s. The reform era has seen a boom in [contemporary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_architecture "Contemporary architecture") skyscrapers and offices, which have largely supplanted traditional architecture.

#### Performing arts

The [music of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_China "Music of China") is highly diverse and regional. Traditional Chinese music uses a wide variety of instruments, including the *[dizi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizi_(instrument) "Dizi (instrument)")* (a flute), *[erhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erhu "Erhu")* (a [bowed string instrument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowed_string_instrument "Bowed string instrument")), *[guqin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guqin "Guqin")* (a plucked string instrument), *[guzheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guzheng "Guzheng")* (a plucked [zither](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zither "Zither")), *[pipa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipa "Pipa")* (a [lute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lute "Lute")), *[sheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheng_(instrument) "Sheng (instrument)")* (a [mouth organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mouth_organ "Mouth organ")), *[suona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suona "Suona")* (a wind instrument), and various types of bells and drums. Western-style music spread during the early 20th century, leading the [Chinese orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_orchestra "Chinese orchestra") to develop as a fusion of Western orchestral music with Chinese instruments. Western [classical music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_music "Classical music") remains highly prestigious. Inspired by Western [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz") and boosted by the introduction of recording studios, [Mandarin pop music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_pop_music "Chinese pop music") emerged in Shanghai in the 1920s, while the [folk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_rock "Folk rock")-inspired [Cantopop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantopop "Cantopop") became popular around Hong Kong during the 1970s. After reform and opening-up, various forms of pop and rock music reached widespread popularity.

[Chinese opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_opera "Chinese opera") emerged from *[shuochang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuochang "Shuochang")*, folk narrative singing traditions. Influenced by various regional traditions and *[kunqu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunqu "Kunqu")*, a genre popular during the Ming dynasty, [Peking opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peking_opera "Peking opera") reached national popularity during the Qing dynasty, while various local and regional forms of opera emerged across the country. Maoist China promoted [revolutionary opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_opera "Revolutionary opera") and communist-oriented folk music.

By the Han period, [Chinese dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance_in_China "Dance in China") was a frequent subject of art and poetry, as well as an element of religious rites. Dancers often performed for the imperial courts, while Chinese opera dances led to increasing numbers of dance troupes across all levels of society during the Qing. Western dances gained popularity in the early 20th century, while the CCP promoted [folk dances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_dance "Folk dance") such as *[yangge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangge "Yangge")*.

### Cuisine

Chinese meals generally incorporate a grain (*fan*) alongside meat or vegetable-based dishes (*cai*). Flour—as [steamed buns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantou "Mantou"), [dumplings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumpling "Dumpling"), [pancakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bing_(bread) "Bing (bread)"), or [noodles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_noodles "Chinese noodles")—is the primary grain in the north, alongside [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize "Maize"), [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet "Millet"), [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice "Rice"), and in some regions, [sorghum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorghum "Sorghum"). Rice—steamed or in the form of [congee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congee "Congee"), [rice noodles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_noodles "Rice noodles") or [rice cakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_cake "Rice cake")—dominates in the south. Although Chinese cuisine is highly diverse and regional, pork, chicken, fish, and duck are the most common meats. Soybeans are a common staple nationally made into products such as [tofu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tofu "Tofu") and [soy sauce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soy_sauce "Soy sauce"). [Stir frying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stir_frying "Stir frying") in [woks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wok "Wok") is the most common cooking method, alongside deep-frying, steaming, and [hot pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_pot "Hot pot"), where dishes are cooked in simmering broth at the table.

People in China generally eat three meals a day, often with a simple breakfast and a more elaborate dinner. [Chopsticks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chopsticks "Chopsticks") are the main [eating utensil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eating_utensil "Eating utensil"). Dishes are usually served to be shared among multiple people, rather than served in individual portions. Hot tea almost always accompanies meals across the country, in lieu of water. Alcohol such as [rice wines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_wine "Rice wine") and the distilled liquor [baijiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baijiu "Baijiu") are commonly served with meals. Chinese cuisine was traditionally divided into four great traditions: [Lu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_cuisine "Shandong cuisine") (Shandong), [Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaiyang_cuisine "Huaiyang cuisine") (Jiangsu), [Chuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_cuisine "Sichuan cuisine") (Sichuan), and [Yue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese_cuisine "Cantonese cuisine") (Guangdong). Chinese Buddhists have developed a [unique cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_cuisine "Buddhist cuisine") that [avoids meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetarianism_in_China "Vegetarianism in China"), while [Chinese Islamic cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Islamic_cuisine "Chinese Islamic cuisine") excludes pork and alcohol.

### Fashion

Various forms of [robe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robe "Robe") served as the main form of clothing for men and women throughout Chinese history, adapting over the centuries. Silk, hemp, and later cotton were the main textiles. Both men and women generally bound their hair with caps or pins, except during periods where foreign hairstyles were enforced by the elite.

Chinese fashions changed greatly during the 20th century, as traditional designs largely gave way to Western clothing. The *[qipao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qipao "Qipao")* became a popular form of women's dress during the early 20th century, but are today rare outside of formal occasions. After a focus on simple, practical clothing during the Mao era, the reform era saw the emergence of a Western-style fashion industry. In the 21st century, the [Hanfu Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanfu_Movement "Hanfu Movement") has sought to promote a reconstructed form of traditional Chinese clothing.

### Literature

China has among the longest continuous literary traditions in the world. Late Shang-era [oracle bone inscriptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle_bone_script "Oracle bone script") are the oldest known form of Chinese writing, while the oldest surviving literary works—such as the *[Book of Documents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Documents "Book of Documents")—*date to the early Zhou. The Chinese literary canon was historically centered on the [Five Classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Classics "Five Classics") (ancient transmitted texts) and the [Four Books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Books_and_Five_Classics "Four Books and Five Classics") (central Confucian texts). [Writings on history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_historiography "Chinese historiography") were ubiquitous and closely connected to literature since ancient times, seen as a way to guide later generations.

The literati regarded literature as primarily [didactic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didacticism "Didacticism"), with fiction seen as useless and potentially corruptive. However, poetry was viewed highly for its ability to convey the nature of reality. The [poetry of the Tang era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_poetry "Tang poetry") was particularly prized in later dynasties. Before the early 20th century, fiction was generally written in [vernacular Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Written_vernacular_Chinese "Written vernacular Chinese"), with formal works generally in [Classical Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Chinese "Classical Chinese"). Popular vernacular literature spread with the introduction of printing, resulting in works such as the [Four Classic Novels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_Chinese_Novels "Classic Chinese Novels") of the Ming and Qing periods.

Modern Chinese literature has been heavily influenced by the political upheavals of the 19th and 20th centuries, seeking to explain the political climate or imagine its future. [New Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Culture_Movement "New Culture Movement") writers such as [Lu Xun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu_Xun "Lu Xun") promoted the importance of fiction and [vernacular Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Written_vernacular_Chinese "Written vernacular Chinese"), experimenting with new modernist forms, while writers of the Reform era sought to explain the trauma of the Mao era. [Chinese online literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_online_literature "Chinese online literature") has become popular alongside the growth of the internet, with about half of Chinese internet users regularly consuming it.

### Media

China's largest media company is [China Central Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Central_Television "China Central Television"), which competes with various provincial television networks. China is the largest online video market, with its major streaming platforms including [iQIYI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IQIYI "IQIYI"), [Youku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youku "Youku"), and [Sohu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sohu "Sohu"). As of 2025, the [Chinese film industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_China "Cinema of China") is the second largest by global box office earnings (behind the United States), although 98% of earnings are domestic. China limits the number of foreign films that can be shown domestically. According to state sources, it has over 90,000 cinema screens as of 2025, more than any other country.

As of 2023, China has over 2,600 radio stations, all state-owned, and over 1,900 newspapers. Cities generally have a newspaper published by the local government and another by the local CCP branch. Journalism is tightly controlled and monitored by the CCP, with content critical of the government censored. Foreign Chinese-language news outlets are blocked, while English-language outlets are occasionally available but often censored. Prominent newspapers in China include the *[People's Daily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Daily "People's Daily"),* *[Global Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Times "Global Times")*, *[China Daily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Daily "China Daily")*, and *[Reference News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reference_News "Reference News").* The most popular social media platform is [WeChat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WeChat "WeChat"). Other major platforms include the [microblogging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microblogging "Microblogging") service [Weibo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weibo "Weibo"), the social media platform [Xiaohongshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiaohongshu "Xiaohongshu"), the news and content aggregator [Toutiao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toutiao "Toutiao"), the video-sharing platform [Xigua Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xigua_Video "Xigua Video"), and the short-form video platform [Douyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douyin "Douyin"), available internationally as [TikTok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TikTok "TikTok").

### Sport and games

Traditional sports such as [martial arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_arts "Martial arts") and [dragon boat racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_boat "Dragon boat") have been practiced in China for over a millennium. The ball game *[cuju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuju "Cuju")* predates the Qin dynasty and remained popular for millennia, declining during the late [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty "Qing dynasty"). [Chuiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuiwan "Chuiwan") (similar to modern [golf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golf "Golf")) and [polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polo "Polo") were popular sports among elites throughout portions of the imperial period, while commoners played regional ball games. Evolving out of Chinese martial arts in the Qing period, [tai chi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_chi "Tai chi") incorporates Taoist practices into a form of [gymnastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnastics "Gymnastics").

Modern sporting was introduced to China from the West during the late 19th and early 20th centuries. [China first participated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_at_the_1932_Summer_Olympics "Republic of China at the 1932 Summer Olympics") in the [Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Games "Olympic Games") in 1932. Although [association football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football "Association football") draws large audiences, the [men's national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_national_football_team "China national football team") has only qualified once for the [FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup "FIFA World Cup"); the country hosted the [FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "FIFA Women's World Cup") twice and the [women's team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_women%27s_national_football_team "China women's national football team") finished as runners-up once.

China began to invest heavily in [elite sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-performance_sport "High-performance sport") during the 1990s, and in 2008 [Beijing hosted the Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Summer_Olympics "2008 Summer Olympics"). Elite sports in China are managed by the [General Administration of Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Administration_of_Sport_of_China "General Administration of Sport of China"), which oversees management centers for various sports, as well as provincial sports committees and teams. The vast majority of sports funding, especially for elite sports, comes from the government. China hosted the [Winter Olympics in 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Winter_Olympics "2022 Winter Olympics"), and the [Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Games "Asian Games") in [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Asian_Games "1990 Asian Games"), [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Asian_Games "2010 Asian Games"), and [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Asian_Games "2022 Asian Games"). China is dominant among international competition in [table tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_tennis_in_China "Table tennis in China") (often dubbed China's [national sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_sport "National sport")), [badminton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton "Badminton"), [diving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diving_(sport) "Diving (sport)"), and [weightlifting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weightlifting "Weightlifting"), and is among the top global competitors in [snooker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snooker "Snooker").

Various card, dice, and board games gained popularity in imperial China, most notably *[weiqi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_(game) "Go (game)")* (also known as Go) and *[xiangqi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiangqi "Xiangqi")* (a relative of [chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess "Chess")). [Mahjong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahjong "Mahjong") emerged during the 1800s, initially one of many popular [gambling games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambling "Gambling"). China is the world's largest video game market. Video games were introduced from Japan in the 1980s, and online video games grew to immense popularity alongside the spread of the internet.
