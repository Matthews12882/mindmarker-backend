# Arctic

The **Arctic** ([/ˈɑːr(k)tɪk/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"); from [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language "Ancient Greek language") [ἄρκτος](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E1%BC%84%CF%81%CE%BA%CF%84%CE%BF%CF%82#Ancient_Greek "wikt:ἄρκτος") *(*árktos*)* '[bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_bear "Polar bear")') is the [polar region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_regions_of_Earth "Polar regions of Earth") of [Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth "Earth") that surrounds the [North Pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pole "North Pole"), lying north of the [Arctic Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Circle "Arctic Circle"). The Arctic region, from the [IERS Reference Meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IERS_Reference_Meridian "IERS Reference Meridian") travelling east, consists of parts of northern Norway ([Nordland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordland "Nordland"), [Troms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troms "Troms"), [Finnmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnmark "Finnmark"), [Svalbard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svalbard "Svalbard") and [Jan Mayen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Mayen "Jan Mayen")), northernmost Sweden ([Västerbotten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A4sterbotten "Västerbotten"), [Norrbotten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norrbotten "Norrbotten") and [Lappland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapland_(Sweden) "Lapland (Sweden)")), northern Finland ([North Ostrobothnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Ostrobothnia "North Ostrobothnia"), [Kainuu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kainuu "Kainuu") and [Lappi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapland_(Finland) "Lapland (Finland)")), Russia ([Murmansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murmansk_Oblast "Murmansk Oblast"), [Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberia "Siberia"), [Nenets Okrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nenets_Autonomous_Okrug "Nenets Autonomous Okrug"), [Novaya Zemlya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novaya_Zemlya "Novaya Zemlya")), the United States ([Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska "Alaska")), Canada ([Yukon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukon "Yukon"), [Northwest Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Territories "Northwest Territories"), [Nunavut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nunavut "Nunavut")), [Danish Realm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Realm "Danish Realm") ([Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland "Greenland")), and northern Iceland ([Grímsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%ADmsey "Grímsey") and [Kolbeinsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolbeinsey "Kolbeinsey")), along with the [Arctic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Ocean "Arctic Ocean") and adjacent seas.

Land within the Arctic region has seasonally varying [snow and ice cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryosphere "Cryosphere"), with predominantly treeless [permafrost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permafrost "Permafrost") under the [tundra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra "Tundra"). Arctic seas contain seasonal [sea ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_ice "Sea ice") in many places.

The Arctic region is a unique area among Earth's ecosystems. The cultures in the region and the [Arctic indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumpolar_peoples "Circumpolar peoples") have adapted to its cold and extreme conditions. Life in the Arctic includes [zooplankton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zooplankton "Zooplankton") and [phytoplankton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytoplankton "Phytoplankton"), fish and [marine mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_mammal "Marine mammal"), birds, land animals, plants, and human societies. Arctic land is bordered by the [subarctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarctic "Subarctic").

## Definition and etymology

The word Arctic comes from the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language "Greek language") word [ἀρκτικός](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E1%BC%80%CF%81%CE%BA%CF%84%CE%B9%CE%BA%CF%8C%CF%82#Greek "wikt:ἀρκτικός") *arktikos* "near the Bear, northern" and from the word [ἄρκτος](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E1%BC%84%CF%81%CE%BA%CF%84%CE%BF%CF%82#Greek "wikt:ἄρκτος") *arktos* meaning "bear," named for either the [constellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constellation "Constellation") known as [Ursa Major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursa_Major "Ursa Major"), the "Great Bear", which is prominent in the northern portion of the [celestial sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_sphere "Celestial sphere"), or the constellation [Ursa Minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursa_Minor "Ursa Minor"), the "Little Bear", which contains the [celestial north pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_pole "Celestial pole") (currently very near [Polaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polaris "Polaris"), the current north Pole Star, or North Star).

There are several definitions of what area is contained within the Arctic. The area can be defined as north of the [Arctic Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Circle "Arctic Circle") (about 66° 34'N), the approximate southern limit of the [midnight sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnight_sun "Midnight sun") and the [polar night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_night "Polar night"). Another definition of the Arctic, which is popular with [ecologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecology "Ecology"), is the region in the [Northern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere "Northern Hemisphere") where the average temperature for the warmest month (July) is below 10 °C (50 °F); the northernmost [tree line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_line "Tree line") roughly follows the [isotherm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contour_line#Temperature_and_related_subjects "Contour line") at the boundary of this region.

## Climate

The [climate of the Arctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_the_Arctic "Climate of the Arctic") region is characterized by cold winters and cool summers. Its precipitation mostly comes in the form of [snow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow "Snow") and is low, with most of the area receiving less than 50 cm (20 in). High winds often stir up snow, creating the illusion of continuous snowfall. Average winter temperatures can go as low as −40 °C (−40 °F), and the coldest recorded temperature is approximately −68 °C (−90 °F). Coastal Arctic climates are moderated by oceanic influences, having generally warmer temperatures and heavier snowfalls than the colder and drier interior areas. The Arctic is affected by current [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change "Climate change"), leading to [climate change in the Arctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_the_Arctic "Climate change in the Arctic"), including [Arctic sea ice decline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_sea_ice_decline "Arctic sea ice decline"), diminished ice in the [Greenland ice sheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland_ice_sheet "Greenland ice sheet"), and [Arctic methane emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_methane_emissions "Arctic methane emissions") as the [permafrost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permafrost "Permafrost") thaws. The melting of Greenland's ice sheet is linked to [polar amplification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_amplification "Polar amplification").

Due to the poleward migration of the planet's isotherms (about 56 km (35 mi) per decade during the past 30 years as a consequence of global warming), the Arctic region (as defined by [tree line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_line "Tree line") and temperature) is currently shrinking. Perhaps the most alarming result of this is Arctic sea ice shrinkage. There is a large variance in predictions of Arctic [sea ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_ice "Sea ice") loss, with models showing near-complete to complete loss in September from 2035 to sometime around 2067.

## Flora and fauna

Arctic life is characterized by adaptation to short growing seasons with long periods of sunlight, and cold, dark, snow-covered winter conditions, with [soil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil "Soil") depth constrained by [permafrost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permafrost "Permafrost"). In the Arctic Circle, up to 24 hours of [daylight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daylight "Daylight") is experienced at the summer solstice in June and up to 24 hours of [darkness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darkness "Darkness") during the winter solstice in December which has an important impact on the ecology of the region.

### Plants

[Arctic vegetation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_vegetation "Arctic vegetation") is composed of plants such as [dwarf shrubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subshrub "Subshrub"), [graminoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graminoid "Graminoid"), [herbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herb "Herb"), [lichens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichen "Lichen"), and [mosses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moss "Moss"), which all grow relatively close to the ground, forming [tundra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra "Tundra"). An example of a dwarf shrub is the [bearberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bearberry "Bearberry"). As one moves northward, the amount of warmth available for plant growth decreases considerably. In the northernmost areas, plants are at their metabolic limits, and small differences in the total amount of summer warmth make large differences in the amount of energy available for maintenance, growth, and reproduction. Colder summer temperatures cause the size, abundance, productivity, and variety of plants to decrease. Trees cannot grow in the Arctic, but in its warmest parts, shrubs are common and can reach 2 m (6 ft 7 in) in height; [sedges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyperaceae "Cyperaceae"), mosses and lichens can form thick layers. In the coldest parts of the Arctic, much of the ground is bare; [non-vascular plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-vascular_plant "Non-vascular plant") such as lichens and mosses predominate, along with a few scattered grasses and [forbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forb "Forb") (like the [Arctic poppy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papaver_radicatum "Papaver radicatum")).

### Animals

Herbivores on the tundra include the [Arctic hare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_hare "Arctic hare"), [lemming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemming "Lemming"), [muskox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muskox "Muskox"), and [reindeer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reindeer "Reindeer") (caribou). They are preyed on by the [snowy owl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowy_owl "Snowy owl"), [Arctic fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_fox "Arctic fox"), [grizzly bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grizzly_bear "Grizzly bear"), and [Arctic wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_wolf "Arctic wolf"). The [polar bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_bear "Polar bear") is also a predator, though it prefers to hunt for marine life from the ice. There are also many [birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category:Birds_of_the_Arctic "Category:Birds of the Arctic") (some 200 species breed in the Arctic) and marine species endemic to the colder regions. Other terrestrial animals include [wolverines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolverine "Wolverine"), [moose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moose "Moose"), [Dall sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dall_sheep "Dall sheep"), [ermines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoat "Stoat"), and [Arctic ground squirrels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_ground_squirrel "Arctic ground squirrel"). Marine mammals include [seals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinniped "Pinniped"), [walruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walrus "Walrus"), and several species of [cetacean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cetacea "Cetacea")—[baleen whales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baleen_whale "Baleen whale") and also [narwhals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narwhal "Narwhal"), [orcas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orca "Orca"), and [belugas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beluga_whale "Beluga whale").

## Natural resources

There are copious [natural resources in the Arctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_resources_of_the_Arctic "Natural resources of the Arctic") (oil, gas, minerals, freshwater, fish, and, if the subarctic is included, forest) to which modern technology and the [economic opening up of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_resources_race "Arctic resources race") have given significant new opportunities. The interest in the tourism industry is also on the increase.

The Arctic contains some of the last and most extensive continuous [wilderness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilderness "Wilderness") areas in the world, and its significance in preserving [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity "Biodiversity") and [genotypes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genotype "Genotype") is considerable. The increasing presence of humans fragments vital habitats. The Arctic is particularly susceptible to the abrasion of [groundcover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundcover "Groundcover") and to the disturbance of the rare breeding grounds of the animals that are characteristic of the region. The Arctic also holds 1/5 of the Earth's water supply.

## Paleontology

During the [Cretaceous period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous "Cretaceous"), the Arctic still had seasonal snows, though only a light dusting and not enough to permanently hinder plant growth. Animals such as the *[Chasmosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chasmosaurus "Chasmosaurus")*, *[Hypacrosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypacrosaurus "Hypacrosaurus")*, *[Troodon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troodon "Troodon")*, and *[Edmontosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmontosaurus "Edmontosaurus")* may have all migrated north to take advantage of the summer growing season, and migrated south to warmer climes when winter came. A similar situation may also have been found amongst [dinosaurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinosaur "Dinosaur") that lived in [Antarctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic "Antarctic") regions, such as the *[Muttaburrasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muttaburrasaurus "Muttaburrasaurus")* of Australia.

However, others claim that dinosaurs lived year-round at very high latitudes, such as near the [Colville River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colville_River_(Alaska) "Colville River (Alaska)"), which is now at about 70°  N but at the time (70 million years ago) was 10° further north.

## Indigenous population

The earliest inhabitants of North America's central and eastern Arctic are referred to as the [Arctic small tool tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_small_tool_tradition "Arctic small tool tradition") (AST) and existed c. 2500 BCE. AST consisted of several [Paleo-Eskimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleo-Eskimo "Paleo-Eskimo") cultures, including the [Independence cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_I_culture "Independence I culture") and [Pre-Dorset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Dorset "Pre-Dorset") cultures. The [Dorset culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorset_culture "Dorset culture") ([Inuktitut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuktitut "Inuktitut"): *Tuniit* or *Tunit*) refers to the next inhabitants of central and eastern Arctic. The Dorset culture emerged across the North American Arctic through a gradual process of cultural and technological adaptation to changing environmental conditions and resource availability between 1050 and 500 BCE. With the exception of the [Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec "Quebec") / [Labrador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labrador "Labrador") peninsula, the Dorset culture vanished around 1500 CE. Supported by [genetic testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_testing "Genetic testing"), evidence shows that descendants of the Dorset culture, known as the [Sadlermiut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadlermiut "Sadlermiut"), survived in Aivilik, [Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Island "Southampton Island") and [Coats Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coats_Island "Coats Island"), until the beginning of the 20th century.

The Dorset / [Thule culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thule_people "Thule people") transition dates around the ninth–10th centuries CE. Scientists theorize that there may have been cross-contact between the two cultures with the sharing of technology, such as fashioning harpoon heads, or the Thule may have found Dorset remnants and adapted their ways with the predecessor culture. The evidence suggested that Inuit descend from the [Birnirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birnirk_culture "Birnirk culture") of Siberia, through the Thule culture [expanded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-modern_human_migration "Pre-modern human migration") into northern Canada and Greenland, where they genetically and culturally completely replaced the Indigenous [Dorset people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorset_people "Dorset people") sometime after 1300 CE. The question of why the Dorset disappeared so completely has led some to suggest that Thule invaders wiped out the Dorset people in "an example of prehistoric genocide."

By 1300 CE, the [Inuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuit "Inuit"), present-day Arctic inhabitants and descendants of Thule culture, had settled in west Greenland and moved into east Greenland over the following century ([Inughuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inughuit "Inughuit"), [Kalaallit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalaallit "Kalaallit") and [Tunumiit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunumiit "Tunumiit") are modern Greenlandic Inuit groups descended from Thule). Over time, the Inuit have migrated throughout the Arctic regions of Eastern Russia, the United States, Canada, and Greenland.

Other [Circumpolar North indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumpolar_peoples "Circumpolar peoples") include the [Chukchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chukchi_people "Chukchi people"), [Evenks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evenks "Evenks"), [Iñupiat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%C3%B1upiat "Iñupiat"), [Khanty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanty "Khanty"), [Koryaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koryaks "Koryaks"), [Nenets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nenets "Nenets"), [Sámi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A1mi_peoples "Sámi peoples"), [Yukaghir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukaghir_people "Yukaghir people"), [Gwichʼin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwich%CA%BCin "Gwichʼin"), and [Yupik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yupik_peoples "Yupik peoples").

## International cooperation and politics

The eight Arctic nations (Canada, Kingdom of Denmark [Greenland & The Faroe Islands], Finland, Iceland, Norway, Sweden, Russia, and US) are all members of the [Arctic Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Council "Arctic Council"), as are organizations representing six indigenous populations (The [Aleut International Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Council_Indigenous_Peoples_Secretariat#Aleut_International_Association "Arctic Council Indigenous Peoples Secretariat"), [Arctic Athabaskan Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Council_Indigenous_Peoples_Secretariat#Arctic_Athabaskan_Council "Arctic Council Indigenous Peoples Secretariat"), [Gwich'in Council International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Council_Indigenous_Peoples_Secretariat#Gwich'in_Council_International "Arctic Council Indigenous Peoples Secretariat"), [Inuit Circumpolar Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuit_Circumpolar_Council "Inuit Circumpolar Council"), [Russian Association of Indigenous Peoples of the North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Association_of_Indigenous_Peoples_of_the_North "Russian Association of Indigenous Peoples of the North"), and [Saami Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saami_Council "Saami Council")). The council operates on a consensus basis, mostly dealing with environmental treaties and not addressing boundary or resource disputes.

Though [Arctic policy priorities differ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_cooperation_and_politics "Arctic cooperation and politics"), every Arctic nation is concerned about sovereignty/defense, resource development, shipping routes, and environmental protection. Much work remains on regulatory agreements regarding shipping, tourism, and [resource development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_resources_race "Arctic resources race") in Arctic waters. Arctic shipping is subject to some regulatory control through the [International Code for Ships Operating in Polar Waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Code_for_Ships_Operating_in_Polar_Waters "International Code for Ships Operating in Polar Waters"), adopted by the [International Maritime Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Maritime_Organization "International Maritime Organization") on 1 January 2017 and applies to all ships in Arctic waters over 500 tonnes.

Research in the Arctic has long been a collaborative international effort, evidenced by the [International Polar Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Polar_Year "International Polar Year"). The [International Arctic Science Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Arctic_Science_Committee "International Arctic Science Committee"), hundreds of scientists and specialists of the [Arctic Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Council "Arctic Council"), and the Barents Euro-Arctic Council are more examples of collaborative international Arctic research.

### Territorial claims

While there are several ongoing [territorial claims in the Arctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_claims_in_the_Arctic "Territorial claims in the Arctic"), no country owns the geographic [North Pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pole "North Pole") or the region of the Arctic Ocean surrounding it. The surrounding six Arctic states that border the Arctic Ocean—Canada, the Kingdom of Denmark (with Greenland), Iceland, Norway, Russia, and the United States—are limited to a 200 [nautical miles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nautical_mile "Nautical mile") (370 km; 230 mi) [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone "Exclusive economic zone") (EEZ) off their coasts. Two Arctic states (Finland and Sweden) do not have direct access to the Arctic Ocean.

Upon ratification of the [United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Convention_on_the_Law_of_the_Sea "United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea"), a country has ten years to make claims to an [extended continental shelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_continental_shelf "Extended continental shelf") beyond its 200 nautical mile zone. Due to this, Norway (which ratified the convention in 1996), Russia (ratified in 1997), Canada (ratified in 2003) and the Kingdom of Denmark (ratified in 2004) launched projects to establish claims that certain sectors of the Arctic seabed should belong to their territories.

On 2 August 2007, two Russian [bathyscaphes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathyscaphe "Bathyscaphe"), [MIR-1 and MIR-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir_(submersible) "Mir (submersible)"), for the first time in history descended to the Arctic [seabed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seabed "Seabed") beneath the North Pole and placed there a [Russian flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Russia "Flag of Russia") made of rust-proof [titanium alloy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanium_alloys "Titanium alloys"). The flag-placing, during [Arktika 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arktika_2007 "Arktika 2007"), generated commentary on and concern for a race for control of the Arctic's vast hydrocarbon resources.

Foreign ministers and other officials representing Canada, the Kingdom of Denmark, Norway, Russia, and the United States met in [Ilulissat, Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilulissat "Ilulissat") on 28 May 2008 at the [Arctic Ocean Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Ocean_Conference "Arctic Ocean Conference") and announced the [Ilulissat Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilulissat_Declaration "Ilulissat Declaration"), blocking any "new comprehensive international legal regime to govern the Arctic Ocean," and pledging "the orderly settlement of any possible overlapping claims."

As of 2012, the Kingdom of Denmark is claiming the [continental shelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_shelf "Continental shelf") based on the [Lomonosov Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lomonosov_Ridge "Lomonosov Ridge") between Greenland and over the North Pole to the northern limit of the [exclusive economic zone of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone_of_Russia "Exclusive economic zone of Russia").

The Russian Federation is also [claiming a large swath of seabed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_shelf_of_Russia#2001_extension_claim "Continental shelf of Russia") along the Lomonosov Ridge but, unlike Denmark, confined its claim to its side of the Arctic region. In August 2015, Russia made a supplementary submission for the expansion of the external borders of its continental shelf in the [Arctic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Ocean "Arctic Ocean"), asserting that the eastern part of the Lomonosov Ridge and the [Mendeleyev Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendeleyev_Ridge "Mendeleyev Ridge") is an extension of the [Eurasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia "Eurasia") continent. In August 2016, the [UN Commission on the Limits of the Continental Shelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Convention_on_the_Law_of_the_Sea "United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea") began to consider Russia's submission.

Canada claims the [Northwest Passage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Passage "Northwest Passage") as part of its [internal waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Internal_Waters "Canadian Internal Waters") belonging to Canada, while the United States and most maritime nations regards it as an [international strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_waters "Territorial waters"), which means that foreign vessels have right of transit passage.

### Strategic competition

The Arctic is a site of strategic competition between [world powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_power "Great power"), particularly Russia and NATO. Russia's [Northern Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Fleet "Northern Fleet"), stationed in the [Kola Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kola_Peninsula "Kola Peninsula"), is a major element of its nuclear deterrence strategy. Russia also conducts nuclear weapons tests on [Novaya Zemlya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novaya_Zemlya "Novaya Zemlya"), and has sought to promote the use of the [Northern Sea Route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Sea_Route "Northern Sea Route") in international maritime trade. The Trump administration has focused US attention on the Arctic, with Trump threatening to invade Greenland in 2025 and 2026, seeing direct control over it as essential to US strategic interests. In response to Trump, the United States's NATO allies have sought to divert US attention toward Russian Arctic activities and bolster their own readiness. Two strategically important straits in the Arctic are the [GIUK Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GIUK_gap "GIUK gap") and the Bear Gap, between Svalbard and Norway.

### Exploration

Since 1937, the larger portion of the Asian-side Arctic region has been extensively explored by Soviet and Russian crewed [drifting ice stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drifting_ice_station "Drifting ice station"). Between 1937 and 1991, 88 international polar crews established and occupied scientific settlements on the [drift ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drift_ice "Drift ice") and were carried thousands of kilometers by the ice flow. Modern day scientists now rely more heavily on unmanned buoys and ice-tethered observatories than crewed stations for Arctic research and exploration

### Pollution

The Arctic is comparatively clean, although there are certain ecologically difficult [localized pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution_in_the_Arctic_Ocean "Pollution in the Arctic Ocean") problems that present a serious threat to people's health living around these pollution sources. Due to the prevailing worldwide sea and air currents, the Arctic area is the fallout region for long-range transport [pollutants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollutant "Pollutant"), and in some places, the concentrations exceed the levels of densely populated urban areas. An example of this is the phenomenon of [Arctic haze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_haze "Arctic haze"), which is commonly blamed on long-range pollutants. Another example is with the [bioaccumulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioaccumulation "Bioaccumulation") of PCBs ([polychlorinated biphenyls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polychlorinated_biphenyl "Polychlorinated biphenyl")) in Arctic wildlife and people.

### Preservation

There have been many proposals to preserve the Arctic over the years. Most recently a group of states[*[clarification needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")*] at the [United Nations Conference on Sustainable Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Conference_on_Sustainable_Development "United Nations Conference on Sustainable Development"), on 21 June 2012, proposed protecting the Arctic, similar to the [Antarctic Treaty System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic_Treaty_System "Antarctic Treaty System"). The initial focus of the campaign will be a UN resolution creating a global sanctuary around the pole, and a ban on oil drilling and unsustainable fishing in the Arctic.

The Arctic has climate change rates that are amongst the highest in the world. Due to the major impacts to the region from climate change the near climate future of the region will be extremely different under all scenarios of warming.

## Climate change

The [effects of climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change "Effects of climate change") in the Arctic include rising temperatures, loss of [sea ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_ice "Sea ice"), and melting of the [Greenland ice sheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland_ice_sheet "Greenland ice sheet"). The Arctic near-surface temperature is increasing at a rate that is 2–3 times faster than the global average, which is referred to as Arctic amplification. Potential [methane release](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_methane_emissions "Arctic methane emissions") from the region, especially through the thawing of [permafrost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permafrost "Permafrost") and [methane clathrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methane_clathrate "Methane clathrate"), is also a concern. Because of the [amplified response of the Arctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_amplification "Polar amplification") to global warming, it is often seen as a leading indicator of global warming. The melting of Greenland's ice sheet is linked to polar amplification.

The Arctic region is especially vulnerable to the effects of any [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change "Climate change"), as has become apparent with the reduction of sea ice in recent years. [Climate models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_model "Climate model") predict much greater [climate change in the Arctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_the_Arctic "Climate change in the Arctic") than the global average, resulting in significant international attention to the region. In particular, there are concerns that Arctic shrinkage, a consequence of melting glaciers and other ice in Greenland, could soon contribute to a substantial rise in sea levels worldwide.

The current Arctic warming is leading to ancient carbon being released from thawing [permafrost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permafrost "Permafrost"), leading to [methane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methane "Methane") and [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide "Carbon dioxide") production by micro-organisms. Release of methane and carbon dioxide stored in permafrost could cause abrupt and severe global warming, as they are potent [greenhouse gases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas "Greenhouse gas").

Climate change is also predicted to have a large impact on tundra vegetation, causing an increase of shrubs, and having a negative impact on bryophytes and lichens.

Apart from concerns regarding the detrimental effects of warming in the Arctic, some potential opportunities have gained attention. The melting of the ice is making the [Northwest Passage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Passage "Northwest Passage"), shipping routes through the northernmost latitudes, more navigable, raising the possibility that the Arctic region will become a prime [trade route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_route "Trade route"). One harbinger of the opening navigability of the Arctic took place in the summer of 2016 when the [Crystal Serenity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_Serenity "Crystal Serenity") successfully navigated the Northwest Passage, a first for a large cruise ship.

In addition, it is believed that the Arctic seabed may contain substantial [oil fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_reservoir#Oil_field "Petroleum reservoir") which may become accessible if the ice covering them melts. These factors have led to recent international debates as to which nations can claim sovereignty or ownership over the waters of the Arctic.
