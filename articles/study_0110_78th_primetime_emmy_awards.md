# 78th Primetime Emmy Awards

The **78th Primetime Emmy Awards** honored the best in American [prime time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_time "Prime time") television programming from June 1, 2025, until May 31, 2026, as chosen by the [Academy of Television Arts & Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Television_Arts_%26_Sciences "Academy of Television Arts & Sciences"). The awards ceremony was held live on September 14, 2026, at the [Peacock Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacock_Theater "Peacock Theater") in [Downtown Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Los_Angeles "Downtown Los Angeles"), California, and was preceded by the [78th Primetime Creative Arts Emmy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/78th_Primetime_Creative_Arts_Emmy_Awards "78th Primetime Creative Arts Emmy Awards") on September 5 and 6. The ceremony was produced by Jesse Collins Entertainment and broadcast in the United States by [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC "NBC") and [Peacock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacock_(streaming_service) "Peacock (streaming service)"). The ceremony was produced by Jesse Collins Entertainment, directed by Alex Rudzinski, and broadcast in the United States by [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC "NBC") and [Peacock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacock_(streaming_service) "Peacock (streaming service)"). [Mariska Hargitay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariska_Hargitay "Mariska Hargitay") hosted the event.

At the main ceremony, *[Widow's Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Widow%27s_Bay "Widow's Bay")* led all programs with six awards including [Outstanding Comedy Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Comedy_Series "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Comedy Series"), and *[The Pitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pitt "The Pitt")* won two awards including [Outstanding Drama Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Drama_Series "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Drama Series"). Other winning programs included *[Pluribus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pluribus_(TV_series) "Pluribus (TV series)")* with two awards, and *[The Beast in Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beast_in_Me_(TV_series) "The Beast in Me (TV series)")*, *[DTF St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DTF_St._Louis "DTF St. Louis")*, *[Hacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacks "Hacks")*, *[The Late Show with Stephen Colbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Late_Show_with_Stephen_Colbert "The Late Show with Stephen Colbert")*, *[Remarkably Bright Creatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remarkably_Bright_Creatures_(film) "Remarkably Bright Creatures (film)")*, *[Slow Horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slow_Horses "Slow Horses")*, and *[The Traitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Traitors_(American_TV_series) "The Traitors (American TV series)")* with one each. Including Creative Arts Emmys, *Widow's Bay* led all shows with 14 wins, while [Apple TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_TV_(streaming_service) "Apple TV (streaming service)") led all networks with 28 wins.

## Winners and nominees

The nominations for the 78th Primetime Emmy Awards were announced on July 8, 2026, at the Television Academy's Wolf Theatre in [North Hollywood, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Hollywood,_Los_Angeles "North Hollywood, Los Angeles"), hosted by actor [Jeff Hiller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Hiller "Jeff Hiller") and actress [Liza Colón-Zayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liza_Col%C3%B3n-Zayas "Liza Colón-Zayas"), along with Television Academy chair [Cris Abrego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cris_Abrego "Cris Abrego"). Including nominations at the [78th Primetime Creative Arts Emmy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/78th_Primetime_Creative_Arts_Emmy_Awards "78th Primetime Creative Arts Emmy Awards"), *[The Pitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pitt "The Pitt")* led all programs with 25 nominations, followed by *[Hacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacks "Hacks")* with 24 nominations. *Hacks* became the most-nominated comedy series for a single season in the awards' history with 24 nominations; the program surpassed the record jointly held by the [FX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FX_(TV_channel) "FX (TV channel)") comedy-drama series *[The Bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bear_(TV_series) "The Bear (TV series)")* and the [Apple TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_TV_(streaming_service) "Apple TV (streaming service)") comedy series *[The Studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Studio_(TV_series) "The Studio (TV series)")* which earned 23 nominations in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/76th_Primetime_Emmy_Awards "76th Primetime Emmy Awards") and [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/77th_Primetime_Emmy_Awards "77th Primetime Emmy Awards"), respectively. [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO "HBO") and its streaming service [HBO Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO_Max "HBO Max") led all networks and platforms with 123 nominations; [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") came in second place with 109 nominations.

Winners are listed first, highlighted in **boldface**, and indicated with a double dagger (‡). For simplicity, producers who received nominations for program awards have been omitted.

### Bob Hope Humanitarian Award

The [Bob Hope Humanitarian Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Hope_Humanitarian_Award "Bob Hope Humanitarian Award") was presented to actor and activist [Michael J. Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_J._Fox "Michael J. Fox"), in recognition of "his transformative contributions to [Parkinson's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinson%27s_disease "Parkinson's disease") research and advocacy".

## Ceremony information

The nominations for the 78th Primetime Emmy Awards were announced on July 8, 2026, at the Television Academy's Wolf Theatre in [North Hollywood, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Hollywood,_Los_Angeles "North Hollywood, Los Angeles"), hosted by actor [Jeff Hiller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Hiller "Jeff Hiller") and actress [Liza Colón-Zayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liza_Col%C3%B3n-Zayas "Liza Colón-Zayas"), along with Television Academy chair [Cris Abrego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cris_Abrego "Cris Abrego"). The nominations for [Outstanding Variety Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Variety_Series "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Variety Series") and [Outstanding Reality Competition Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Reality_Competition_Program "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Reality Competition Program") were announced three hours before the rest of the categories. On July 7, actress [Mariska Hargitay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariska_Hargitay "Mariska Hargitay") was announced as the ceremony's host. [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC "NBC") and the [Television Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Television_Arts_%26_Sciences "Academy of Television Arts & Sciences") considered talent both within and outside of [NBCUniversal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBCUniversal "NBCUniversal") for the role, but ultimately hired NBC's *[Law & Order: Special Victims Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_%26_Order:_Special_Victims_Unit "Law & Order: Special Victims Unit")*'s lead actress in honor of the network's 100th anniversary. This was the first Primetime Emmys ceremony that the host is not a comedian, comedy performer, reality host or television personality since [Angela Lansbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Lansbury "Angela Lansbury") in [1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/45th_Primetime_Emmy_Awards "45th Primetime Emmy Awards"), and the first time that the event was hosted by a woman since [Jane Lynch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Lynch "Jane Lynch") in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/63rd_Primetime_Emmy_Awards "63rd Primetime Emmy Awards").

Jesse Collins, Dionne Harmon, and Jeannae Rouzan-Clay of Jesse Collins Entertainment served as [executive producers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_producer "Executive producer") of the telecast. To avoid conflicts with the network's broadcast of *[NBC Sunday Night Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_Sunday_Night_Football "NBC Sunday Night Football")*, the event was scheduled on a Monday night, instead of a Sunday, which NBC had previously done when they aired the awards ceremony every four years since 2014.

### Category and rule changes

In January 2026, the Television Academy announced plans to present its first new major award in nearly twenty years with the creation of the Legacy Award, which will be presented annually to television programs that have made a "profound and lasting impact" on audiences, and remain relevant to culture, society and the industry. Additionally, [Outstanding Scripted Variety Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Scripted_Variety_Series "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Scripted Variety Series") and [Outstanding Talk Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Talk_Series "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Talk Series") were merged into Outstanding Variety Series. The new Variety Series category is tracked, meaning the number of nominees from each format is proportional to the number of submissions, and it became an area award where voters select any and all nominees they believe deserve an award, with the potential for multiple winners.

In July, the Television Academy voted to move five categories to the Creative Arts ceremonies on the preceding weekend: [Outstanding Supporting Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Supporting_Actor_in_a_Limited_or_Anthology_Series_or_Movie "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Supporting Actor in a Limited or Anthology Series or Movie"), [Supporting Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Supporting_Actress_in_a_Limited_or_Anthology_Series_or_Movie "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Supporting Actress in a Limited or Anthology Series or Movie"), [Directing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Directing_for_a_Limited_or_Anthology_Series_or_Movie "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Directing for a Limited or Anthology Series or Movie"), and [Writing for a Limited or Anthology Series or Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Writing_for_a_Limited_or_Anthology_Series_or_Movie "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Writing for a Limited or Anthology Series or Movie") and [Writing for a Variety Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Writing_for_a_Variety_Series "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Writing for a Variety Series"). Abrego justified the changes as a way to add other elements to the show such as musical performances or comedy segments, with the moves selected to "be as balanced and as equitable as possible". A joint statement from the [Directors Guild of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directors_Guild_of_America "Directors Guild of America"), the [Writers Guild of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writers_Guild_of_America "Writers Guild of America"), and [SAG-AFTRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAG-AFTRA "SAG-AFTRA") criticized the changes and commented, "The decision to eliminate these categories from the prime-time broadcast devalues the contributions of the talented people the Academy is meant to honor." "The move doesn't make much sense. All they've done is piss off a lot of the people and stars they are trying to court and attend their ceremony. I think this will blow up in their face", one outraged awards strategist told *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")*. Additionally, [Outstanding Variety Special (Live)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Variety_Special_(Live) "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Variety Special (Live)") was shifted back to the Creative Arts ceremonies, leaving 19 categories for the main ceremony (a decrease from last year's 26).
