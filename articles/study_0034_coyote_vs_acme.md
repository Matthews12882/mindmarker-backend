# Coyote vs. Acme

***Coyote vs. Acme*** is a 2026 American [comedy film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comedy_film "Comedy film") directed by [Dave Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Green_(director) "Dave Green (director)") and written by [Samy Burch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samy_Burch "Samy Burch"), who developed the story with [James Gunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gunn "James Gunn") and [Jeremy Slater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Slater "Jeremy Slater"). Loosely based on the 1990 *[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker "The New Yorker")* magazine article "Coyote v. Acme" by [Ian Frazier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Frazier "Ian Frazier") (itself using elements from the *[Looney Tunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Looney_Tunes "Looney Tunes")* franchise), it follows [Wile E. Coyote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wile_E._Coyote_and_the_Road_Runner "Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner") as he enlists a billboard attorney to sue the [Acme Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acme_Corporation "Acme Corporation") for repeatedly selling him faulty products. The film, combining [live action and animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live-action_animation "Live-action animation"), stars [Will Forte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Forte "Will Forte"), [Lana Condor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lana_Condor "Lana Condor"), [Tone Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tone_Bell "Tone Bell"), [Luis Guzmán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Guzm%C3%A1n "Luis Guzmán"), [Martha Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Kelly "Martha Kelly"), [P. J. Byrne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._J._Byrne "P. J. Byrne"), [Kenneth Choi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Choi "Kenneth Choi"), and [John Cena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cena "John Cena"), with [Eric Bauza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Bauza "Eric Bauza") leading the voice cast.

Development began in 2015 with James Gunn pitching to Warner Bros., which led to Jeremy Slater writing an early draft of the screenplay. In 2018, [Chris McKay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_McKay "Chris McKay") came on as producer and Jon and Josh Silberman as screenwriters. Dave Green was hired to direct in December 2019, and screenwriter Samy Burch joined the following year. Cena, Forte, and Condor were cast in early 2022. [Live-action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live-action "Live-action") filming took place in New Mexico from March to May 2022. The film was produced by [Warner Animation Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Animation_Group "Warner Animation Group"), Keylight Pictures, and Gunn's production company Troll Court Entertainment.

[Warner Bros. Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Discovery "Warner Bros. Discovery") initially shelved *Coyote vs. Acme* in November 2023 to obtain a tax [write-off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Write-off "Write-off") but later reversed its decision and allowed the filmmakers to seek other distributors following public backlash. After several unsuccessful negotiations with various distributors, [Ketchup Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketchup_Entertainment "Ketchup Entertainment") acquired the rights for $50 million in March 2025, after previously doing so for [Warner Bros. Animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Animation "Warner Bros. Animation")'s *Looney Tunes* film *[The Day the Earth Blew Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Day_the_Earth_Blew_Up "The Day the Earth Blew Up")* (2024).

*Coyote vs. Acme* was screened at [San Diego Comic-Con](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Comic-Con "San Diego Comic-Con") on July 25, 2026, and was theatrically released in the United States on August 28, 2026. It received positive reviews from critics, and grossed $102 million worldwide on a budget of $70–72 million.

## Plot

In 1992, in a world where cartoon characters co-exist amongst humans, [Acme Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acme_Corporation "Acme Corporation") scientist [Dr. Lorre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Looney_Tunes_and_Merrie_Melodies_characters#Dr._Lorre "List of Looney Tunes and Merrie Melodies characters") leaks a message to an unknown contact before being captured and imprisoned inside an Acme [portable hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portable_hole "Portable hole").

Thirty years later, [Wile E. Coyote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wile_E._Coyote_and_the_Road_Runner "Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner"), tired of failing to catch the [Road Runner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wile_E._Coyote_and_the_Road_Runner "Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner"), sees a television ad for Avery, Jones & Maltese, a personal injury law firm in [Albuquerque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albuquerque,_New_Mexico "Albuquerque, New Mexico") specializing in [cartoon violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartoon_violence "Cartoon violence") cases involving Acme products. Wile E. meets with the senior partner Kevin Avery, looking to sue Acme for the various defective devices used in chasing the Road Runner. While Kevin's niece and intern Paige is eager to take Acme to court, Kevin prefers to settle to avoid a risky trial. However, Wile E. rejects the settlement offered by Acme's lawyer Buddy Crane, forcing a trial.

The firm tries to subpoena the Road Runner to testify, but he evades them. Meanwhile, Acme CEO [Foghorn Leghorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foghorn_Leghorn "Foghorn Leghorn") warns Buddy to prevent the trial from revealing any corporate secrets. After a poor first trial session, Kevin receives a tip from an anonymous [whistleblower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whistleblower "Whistleblower") to meet with former Acme employee [Daffy Duck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daffy_Duck "Daffy Duck"), who conveys the warning Lorre sent him in 1992 about "Project [Sisyphus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisyphus "Sisyphus")". Kevin admits to Wile E. that he tries to settle all cases due to having lost multiple trials early in his career, but Wile E. encourages Kevin to believe he can beat Acme the same way Wile E. believes he will catch the Road Runner. Though this inspires Kevin, he soon discovers Wile E.'s motive for the lawsuit is to capture the Road Runner in court. Acme attempts to catch the Road Runner, believing he will testify against Wile E. given their past, but the Road Runner evades them. Meanwhile, Paige learns from Lorre's wife [Granny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granny_(Looney_Tunes) "Granny (Looney Tunes)") that, before his disappearance, Lorre did product development for Acme, who now force their pet [Tweety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tweety "Tweety") to serve as Acme's mercenary.

The team discovers the whistleblower is [Bugs Bunny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugs_Bunny "Bugs Bunny"), who reveals Project Sisyphus is a product testing program formerly headed by Dr. Lorre, using cartoon violence to improve Acme's products for humans. When the program drove its test subjects insane, Lorre tried to object, but was silenced, and Acme pivoted to testing in the wild on unsuspecting clients like Wile E. Off Bugs' suggestion, Kevin tries to get Project Sisyphus on the record by questioning Acme's Head of PR [Tasmanian Devil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasmanian_Devil_(Looney_Tunes) "Tasmanian Devil (Looney Tunes)") on the stand, but Acme's legal team sabotage him and the judge dismisses the case. However, Kevin reveals the conspiracy to the press, prompting the government to call a [congressional hearing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_congressional_hearing "United States congressional hearing") in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C.") Wile E. only agrees to join when Kevin claims the Road Runner will be there. The night before the hearing, Kevin admits the truth after being badgered about it and berates Wile E. for his obsession with the Road Runner, prompting Wile E. to storm out. While wandering the streets, Wile E. narrowly evades Tweety's attempt to kill him. Kevin tries to rescue him, but gets knocked out and separated when Wile E. abandons him once again, still offended.

Kevin rejects a last bribe attempt by Buddy and gives an impassioned speech at the hearing, admiring Wile E.'s determination despite repeated failures. Wile E. arrives, having overheard, and reconciles with Kevin. Kevin gets Foghorn to publicly admit to Project Sisyphus, but Foghorn argues that cartoon characters, like Wile E., are not important enough for anyone to care if they are harmed. However, the Road Runner arrives and testifies to the harm Acme products have caused Wile E. over the years and further explains as Wile E.'s friend that he cares for and misses him, moving Wile E. to spare him. Senator [Elmer Fudd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmer_Fudd "Elmer Fudd") declines to pursue federal charges due to Wile E. being the sole [plaintiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaintiff "Plaintiff"). As Buddy and Foghorn gloat, Wile E. activates the trap he had set for the Road Runner, caging and humiliating the Acme team.

Back in Albuquerque, the firm is inundated with cartoon characters also seeking to sue Acme for damages. Further leaks from Bugs launch a federal investigation that exposes the full extent of Project Sisyphus and leads to the arrests of Foghorn and Buddy. Dr. Lorre is freed and reunites with Granny and Tweety. Wile E. bids goodbye to Kevin and Paige, who is now a partner at the firm, before reuniting with the Road Runner and resuming their chase.

## Production

### Development and writing

The film is loosely based on [Ian Frazier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Frazier "Ian Frazier")'s 1990 *[New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker "The New Yorker")* legal piece parody "*Coyote v. Acme*", which imagines [Wile E. Coyote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wile_E._Coyote_and_the_Road_Runner "Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner") suing the [Acme Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acme_Corporation "Acme Corporation") for consistently selling him defective and inaccurately labeled products. A three-part series with the same premise, "Cliff-hanger Justice" by [Joey Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_Green "Joey Green"), had previously been published in *[National Lampoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Lampoon_(magazine) "National Lampoon (magazine)")* in 1982, though Frazier was unaware of it when writing "Coyote v. Acme". [Scott Rudin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Rudin "Scott Rudin") Productions bought the rights to Frazier's article in 1998 but could not get permission from [Warner Bros.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros. "Warner Bros.") to film it. The options finally lapsed in 2006 before [James Gunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gunn "James Gunn") successfully [pitched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitch_(filmmaking) "Pitch (filmmaking)") the film to Warner in 2015. Gunn asked [Jeremy Slater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Slater "Jeremy Slater") to write the film's first draft due to their friendship, which included growing up with *[Looney Tunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Looney_Tunes "Looney Tunes")*. The screenplay had some limitations due to other projects in development—[Bugs Bunny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugs_Bunny "Bugs Bunny") and [Daffy Duck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daffy_Duck "Daffy Duck") were off-limits, for example. The [Tasmanian Devil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasmanian_Devil_(Looney_Tunes) "Tasmanian Devil (Looney Tunes)") had a notable appearance in Slater's script. Gunn and Slater agreed that Wile E. should be a lovable loser who never speaks and is closer to a pet dog than a villain or the butt of a joke. Though Warner rejected Slater's screenplay, its [ethos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethos "Ethos") informed the final draft.

In August 2018, [Warner Bros. Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures "Warner Bros. Pictures") announced the development of a Wile E. Coyote project, titled *Coyote vs. Acme*, with *[The Lego Batman Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lego_Batman_Movie "The Lego Batman Movie")* (2017) director [Chris McKay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_McKay "Chris McKay") as producer and Jon and Josh Silberman writing the script. In mid-December 2019, [Warner Animation Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Animation_Group "Warner Animation Group") hired [Dave Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Green_(director) "Dave Green (director)") to direct the live-action/animated hybrid film, while Jon and Josh Silberman were replaced as screenwriters, but remained producers. In December 2020, McKay left the project, while the Silbermans left their producing roles and returned to screenwriting duties alongside [Samy Burch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samy_Burch "Samy Burch"), Slater and Gunn. In March 2023, the film's final writing credits were given: Burch received sole credit for the screenplay and shared credit for the story with Slater and Gunn, while off-screen additional literary credit was given to Alex Cuthbertson, Jacob Fleisher, Matt Fusfeld, [Dan Goor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Goor "Dan Goor"), [BenDavid Grabinski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BenDavid_Grabinski "BenDavid Grabinski"), [Craig Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Wright_(playwright) "Craig Wright (playwright)") and Jon & Josh Silberman. Gunn, alongside *[Tom & Jerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_and_Jerry_(2021_American_film) "Tom and Jerry (2021 American film)")* (2021) producer [Chris DeFaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_DeFaria "Chris DeFaria"), also received final credit as producers on the film, with Gunn under his production banner Two Monkeys, A Goat and Another, Dead, Monkey.

In November 2022, [Foghorn Leghorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foghorn_Leghorn "Foghorn Leghorn"), [Granny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granny_(Looney_Tunes) "Granny (Looney Tunes)"), [Sylvester the Cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_the_Cat "Sylvester the Cat") and [Tweety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tweety "Tweety") were confirmed to be in the film. In December, Bugs Bunny, Daffy Duck, [Elmer Fudd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmer_Fudd "Elmer Fudd") and [Yosemite Sam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yosemite_Sam "Yosemite Sam") were also confirmed to be in the film. In January 2023, [Porky Pig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porky_Pig "Porky Pig") was also confirmed to be in the film. Editor Carsten Kurpanek described the film's story as a "[David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David "David") vs. [Goliath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goliath "Goliath") story" that tackles Wile E. Coyote's persistence in the face of the Acme Corporation's "cynical and casual cruelness of [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism "Capitalism") and [corporate greed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criticism_of_capitalism "Criticism of capitalism")."

Green said his initial approach to the material was to combine a true-crime documentary with a legal drama. He cited *[The Insider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Insider_(film) "The Insider (film)")* (1999), *[Erin Brockovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erin_Brockovich_(film) "Erin Brockovich (film)")* (2000), and *[A Few Good Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Few_Good_Men "A Few Good Men")* (1992) as influences, with the human characters treating Wile E. Coyote's lawsuit and the surrounding cartoon logic as part of everyday life. Green also said that the tone of Frazier's original article—treating Acme's product liability in the language of a legal filing—was central to the adaptation, allowing the filmmakers to combine serious legal conventions with the physical comedy of the Looney Tunes.

### Casting

In February 2022, [John Cena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cena "John Cena") was cast as the film's secondary antagonist, described as the lawyer in defense of Acme and the former boss to Wile E.'s lawyer; he previously collaborated with Gunn on the [DC Extended Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Extended_Universe "DC Extended Universe") (DCEU) projects *[The Suicide Squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Suicide_Squad_(film) "The Suicide Squad (film)")* (2021) and *[Peacemaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacemaker_(TV_series) "Peacemaker (TV series)")* (2022–25). On March 9, [Will Forte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Forte "Will Forte") and [Lana Condor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lana_Condor "Lana Condor") were added to the cast, with the former starring as Wile E.'s lawyer.

### Filming and production design

[Principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography "Principal photography") took place from March to May 2022 in [Albuquerque, New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albuquerque,_New_Mexico "Albuquerque, New Mexico"), with [Brandon Trost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandon_Trost "Brandon Trost") serving as cinematographer. The creative team acknowledged *[Who Framed Roger Rabbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who_Framed_Roger_Rabbit "Who Framed Roger Rabbit")* (1988) as a reference for the film's live-action interaction with animated characters. New Mexico Film Office director Amber Dodson commented in a press release that the production would "showcase our beautiful city as an already iconic location and setting in the *Looney* [*Tunes*] cartoons." [David Barclay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Barclay_(puppeteer) "David Barclay (puppeteer)") built a life-size puppet of Wile E. Coyote for the actors to interact with. The most difficult sequence Dave Green had to film was on the second day of filming, where Wile E. tries to bring all kinds of weapons and gadgets through a metal detector. Green had to get roughly a few dozen background actors to locate the eyeline of an invisible character. Despite the difficulty, Green called the sequence his favorite.

### Visual effects and animation

Visual effects and [computer animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_animation "Computer animation") were provided by [DNEG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNEG_Animation "DNEG Animation") for Wile E. Coyote and other major *Looney Tunes* characters. According to Green, the 3D animators worked closely with a team of 2D artists, who guided them with drawn poses and expressions of the characters in order to "[find] the building blocks of performance." Editor Carsten Kurpanek explained that "artists would do [black-and-white] line drawings of key poses over a rough edit first," which were later used as reference for visual effects and animation; this process was dubbed "sketchviz" by the crew. [Traditional animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_animation "Traditional animation") services were also provided by Duncan Studio, for "a handful of shots" with the significant *Looney Tunes* characters in the film and for "certain [*Looney Tunes*] characters [who] were handled entirely in 2D." One of these characters was Bugs Bunny, which occurred because he was not originally planned to be in the film: the Acme whistleblower was scripted as an escaped Dr. Lorre. When this was changed in post-production, it was more productive to fully animate Bugs in 2D than to fit him into the cel-shaded 3D animation. Condor was unaware of this change and was starstruck at the "funeral screening" in 2023 when she learned her scene partner was Bugs Bunny.

### Music

Following the announcement of the film's initial cancellation, [Steven Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Price_(composer) "Steven Price (composer)") announced that he composed the film's score. He shared a choir rendition of the Road Runner's vocal effects set to [Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Ilyich_Tchaikovsky "Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky")'s "[1812 Overture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1812_Overture "1812 Overture")" titled the "Meep Meep Choir".

The soundtrack was released through [WaterTower Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WaterTower_Music "WaterTower Music") digitally on August 21, 2026, one week before the film's release. A physical [vinyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinyl_record "Vinyl record") release of the album is planned for the first quarter of 2027 through [iam8bit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iam8bit "Iam8bit") and WaterTower Music. ## Themes and analysis

Critics discussed the film's treatment of corporate power, consumer culture, and its parallels with the circumstances surrounding its attempted shelving. Catherine Wheatley of *[Sight and Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sight_and_Sound "Sight and Sound")* described Wile E. Coyote as a "high usage user" of Acme products who remains trapped in a cycle of consuming products that repeatedly fail him. She described Kevin Avery's struggle against Acme as "Capra-esque", comparing him to a modern-day [James Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Stewart "James Stewart") and noting parallels with *[Mr. Smith Goes to Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Smith_Goes_to_Washington "Mr. Smith Goes to Washington")* (1939) and *[It Happened One Night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It_Happened_One_Night "It Happened One Night")* (1934). Wheatley characterized the film as both a family movie and [political satire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_satire "Political satire"), with elements of the courtroom drama and 1970s conspiracy thriller.

[Justin Chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Chang "Justin Chang") of *[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker "The New Yorker")* noted a parallel between the film's story of an underdog confronting a powerful corporation and its own history at Warner Bros. Discovery, describing the premise as a metaphor for the circumstances surrounding its attempted shelving. Chang also emphasized Wile E.'s persistence in the face of repeated failure, noting that the screenplay refers to [Sisyphus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisyphus "Sisyphus") more than once.

British litigator [David Allen Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Allen_Green "David Allen Green") has called the movie "a good—indeed great—film about commercial law". He emphasised the realism in the attempts by lawyers to reach a settlement, the importance of evidence discovery and the motivation of the parties involved.

## Release

*Coyote vs. Acme* was initially planned to be theatrically released in the United States on July 21, 2023, by Warner Bros. Pictures. On April 26, 2022, the studio removed the film from its release schedule, replacing it with *[Barbie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbie_(film) "Barbie (film)")*. On March 31, 2025, it was announced that the film was set to be released in 2026, following the film's acquisition by [Ketchup Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketchup_Entertainment "Ketchup Entertainment"). In July 2025, it was given its specific release date at [San Diego Comic-Con](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Comic-Con "San Diego Comic-Con"). *Coyote vs. Acme* made its debut at San Diego Comic-Con on July 25, 2026. It was theatrically released in the United Kingdom and Ireland on August 21, 2026, and in the United States on August 28, 2026. [Beta Fiction Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_Fiction_Spain "Beta Fiction Spain") acquired Spanish distribution rights, programming a September 18 theatrical release.

### Marketing

[Trailer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trailer_(promotion) "Trailer (promotion)") marketing for the film began in April 2026 with two [teaser trailers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teaser_trailer "Teaser trailer"). The first, released on April 15, shows Wile E. Coyote hanging in the air, then holding up a sign that says "Happy [Tax Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_Day "Tax Day")" on one side and "Check Your Write-Offs" on the other, then falling off the cliff. The second, released on April 21, features [Will Forte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Forte "Will Forte") doing an intro. The first trailer was released on April 22. It was watched 25.6 million times within 24 hours, a new record for an independent family film. On July 23, the final trailer was released and ticket sales for the film opened. On July 29, a month before the premiere, the final poster was revealed.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] Reviewers noted that the film's marketing leaned into the tax write-off controversy, using the Acme Corporation as a placeholder for Warner Bros., such as with the slogan "The Movie Acme Doesn't Want You to See". Ketchup revealed that they hired 42West, their go-to public relations agency, and [Ryan Reynolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Reynolds "Ryan Reynolds")' [Maximum Effort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_Effort "Maximum Effort") for marketing the film, which suggested leaning into the controversy for promoting the film. 42West also was in charge of several promotional appearances involving the cast.

### Home media

On September 29, 2026, the film was made available for digital purchase on [Amazon Prime Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Prime_Video "Amazon Prime Video"). It will also be released by Ketchup Entertainment on [Ultra HD Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_HD_Blu-ray "Ultra HD Blu-ray"), [Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blu-ray "Blu-ray") and [DVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD "DVD") on December 1, 2026.

### Cancellation controversy

#### Initial cancellation and reversal

On November 9, 2023, [Warner Bros. Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Entertainment "Warner Bros. Entertainment") officials announced that the film was completed, but that they would not release it, because [Warner Bros. Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Discovery "Warner Bros. Discovery") preferred to claim a [tax loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Write-off "Write-off") of about $30 million. This would make it the third film to be canceled by Warner Bros. Discovery for the sake of a write-off, following *[Batgirl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batgirl_(film) "Batgirl (film)")* and *[Scoob! Holiday Haunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scoob!_Holiday_Haunt "Scoob! Holiday Haunt")*, neither of which finished post-production. The crew was not informed of the decision until after the film was completed. The move drew criticism from filmmakers, animation outlets, and talent representatives. Several filmmakers left angry phone calls with Warner Bros. expressing their frustration at the move, while others canceled meetings with the studio in protest. Many noted that the film had drawn praise at test screenings and interest from potential buyers. Director Dave Green voiced his disappointment over the film's cancellation, further emphasizing his love for the *Looney Tunes* and writing that "we were all determined to honor the legacies of these historic characters and actually get them right."

On November 13, 2023, *[Puck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puck_(media_company) "Puck (media company)")* reported Warner Bros. Discovery leaders had reversed their decision and allowed the filmmakers the option to shop the film to other distributors. *[Deadline Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadline_Hollywood "Deadline Hollywood")* reported that [Amazon MGM Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_MGM_Studios "Amazon MGM Studios"), [Apple Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Studios "Apple Studios"), and [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") were interested in buying the distribution rights to the film. *[TheWrap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TheWrap "TheWrap")* reported that despite interest from distributors to buy the film, there are "currently no hard offers, and that Green is crafting his own '[PR campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PR_campaign "PR campaign")'." On December 8, *Deadline Hollywood* further reported that the film had been screened for more studios, including [Paramount Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures "Paramount Pictures") and [Sony Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Pictures "Sony Pictures"). Of these, Netflix and Paramount had made bids, with the latter including a theatrical component; Amazon was still interested despite making no formal bids; and Sony and Apple were not planning to make bids.

On February 9, 2024, *TheWrap* reported that Warner Bros. Discovery had rejected the bids from Netflix, Amazon, and Paramount. The company wanted $75–$80 million to sell the film, but no distributors met their price, and Warner Bros. Discovery rejected counter bids. The company considered shelving and deleting the film (which remained in limbo at the time) and again claiming it as a [tax loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Write-off "Write-off"). Following their Q4 2023 earnings call on February 23, 2024, Warner Bros. Discovery took a $115 million write-down while not directly confirming the cancellation of the film.

On March 10, 2024, during the red carpet for the [96th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/96th_Academy_Awards "96th Academy Awards"), Burch said conversations were still ongoing within Warner Bros. and that "we hope it will somehow find its home and not end up stuck in a vault for the rest of time." In April, a Warner Bros. spokesperson told *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* that while the fate of the film is unclear, it "remains available for acquisition".

#### Responses

> Dedicated to everyone who posted, shared, held up signs and spoke out when it counted. This film made it to the big screen because of you.

— The dedication that appears at the end of the film's [credits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credits "Credits") sequence

As with the initial cancellation, *TheWrap*'s report on the film's possible final cancellation drew online criticism. The hashtags #ReleaseCoyoteVsAcme and #SaveCoyoteVsAcme, along with other hashtags relating to it, started trending on February 9, 2024 (the date of the article's release), and continued to trend for the following weeks. Eric Bauza addressed this in an improvised exchange between [Bugs Bunny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugs_Bunny "Bugs Bunny") and [Daffy Duck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daffy_Duck "Daffy Duck") during his speech at the [51st Annie Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/51st_Annie_Awards "51st Annie Awards"), with him saying "I hate to be political, but release *Coyote vs. Acme*!" in the latter's voice.

Industry figures who saw *Coyote vs. Acme* included [Phil Lord and Christopher Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Lord_and_Christopher_Miller "Phil Lord and Christopher Miller"), [Michael Chaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Chaves "Michael Chaves"), and [Daniel Scheinert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniels_(directors) "Daniels (directors)"). Lord called the film "wonderful" and "moving", and lamented that Warner Bros.' move was "anticompetitive", while Miller said he hoped "several studios go after it so the world can see all the good work people did." Writer-director [BenDavid Grabinski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BenDavid_Grabinski "BenDavid Grabinski") claimed it was "the best of its kind since *Who Framed Roger Rabbit*", while filmmaker [Brian Duffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Duffield "Brian Duffield"), who gave notes on the film, compared it to the studio's own *Barbie* film, feeling that both "[play] with iconography in a really fun, popcorn kind of way." In an interview with *[Screen Rant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Rant "Screen Rant")* on May 31, 2024, [Adil El Arbi and Bilall Fallah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adil_El_Arbi_and_Bilall_Fallah "Adil El Arbi and Bilall Fallah"), directors of the canceled *[Batgirl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batgirl_(film) "Batgirl (film)")* film, revealed that they are both friends with Green and said, "I think that movies must be seen by the audience, and the audience must judge them."

In February 2024, Forte released a public statement addressed to the cast and crew after seeing the final cut of the film: "I know that a lot of you haven't gotten a chance to see our movie. [...] Please know that all the years and years of hard work, dedication and love that you put into this movie shows in every frame." In January 2025, Condor revealed the film had a "funeral screening" when news of the initial cancellation was first broken.

#### Sale to Ketchup Entertainment

On March 19, 2025, *Deadline* reported that Ketchup Entertainment was in talks to acquire all rights to the film from Warner Bros. for approximately $50 million, following its acquisition of the North American distribution rights to *[The Day the Earth Blew Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Day_the_Earth_Blew_Up "The Day the Earth Blew Up")*; on March 31, *Deadline* confirmed that Warner Bros. completed the sale to Ketchup Entertainment. On May 8, *Deadline* reported that the film was set for international sales at the [Cannes Film Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes_Film_Market "Cannes Film Market") through Kinology, with footage available for buyers and a potential market screening.

## Reception

### Box office

As of October 4, 2026[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Coyote_vs._Acme&action=edit), *Coyote vs. Acme* has grossed $60 million in the United States and Canada, and $41 million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $101 million.

In the United States and Canada, *Coyote vs. Acme* was released alongside *[Buddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddy_(2026_film) "Buddy (2026 film)")*, *[The Dog Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dog_Stars_(film) "The Dog Stars (film)")*, and *[Idiots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idiots_(2026_film) "Idiots (2026 film)")*, and was projected to gross $12–18 million from 3,555 theaters in its opening weekend. The film made $5.3 million on its first day, including $1.7 million on its Thursday previews. It went on to debut to $15.9 million, finishing second behind holdover *[Spider-Man: Brand New Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man:_Brand_New_Day "Spider-Man: Brand New Day")*.

### Critical response

On the [review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator "Review aggregator") website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes"), 96% of 298 critics' reviews are positive, with an average rating of 7.9/10. The website's consensus reads: "A daffy satire that's equal parts heart and wily humor, *Coyote vs. Acme* makes a triumphant case for the Looney Tunes' modern relevance with a sense of good-natured mischief that'd make even Bugs Bunny bow." [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), which uses a [weighted average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_arithmetic_mean "Weighted arithmetic mean"), assigned the film a score of 77 out of 100, based on 46 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews. Audiences polled by [CinemaScore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CinemaScore "CinemaScore") gave the film an average grade of "A" on an A+ to F scale, while [PostTrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PostTrak "PostTrak") reported 93% of audience members gave the film a positive score, with 82% saying they would definitely recommend it.

Brian Tallerico of [RogerEbert.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RogerEbert.com "RogerEbert.com") gave the film three out of four stars, writing that it's constantly "clever and funny". [Frank Scheck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Scheck "Frank Scheck") of *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")* wrote that it's "a hilarious delight, one of the best [live-action animated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live-action_animated "Live-action animated") films, and will be treasured by *Looney Tunes* fans (and who isn't one?) who will be thrilled seeing their favorite cartoon characters return to the big screen". Alissa Wilkinson of *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* named the film a "Critic's Pick", calling it a "barrel of unhinged delights".
