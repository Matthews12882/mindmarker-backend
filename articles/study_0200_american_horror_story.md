# American Horror Story

***American Horror Story*** (***AHS***) is an American [horror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horror_film "Horror film") [anthology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthology_series "Anthology series") television series created by [Ryan Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Murphy_(producer) "Ryan Murphy (producer)") and [Brad Falchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Falchuk "Brad Falchuk") for the [cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_television "Cable television") network [FX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FX_(TV_channel) "FX (TV channel)"). The first installment in the *[American Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Story "American Story")* [media franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_franchise "Media franchise"), seasons of *AHS* are mostly conceived as self-contained [miniseries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miniseries "Miniseries"), following a different set of characters in a new setting within the same [fictional universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fictional_universe "Fictional universe") (which the show occasionally uses for [crossovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossover_(fiction) "Crossover (fiction)") between seasons, and shares with episodic spin-off *[American Horror Stories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Stories "American Horror Stories")*), and a storyline with its own beginning, middle, and end. Some plot elements of each season are loosely inspired by true events.

Many actors appear in more than one season, usually playing a new character though sometimes as a returning character. [Evan Peters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evan_Peters "Evan Peters") and [Sarah Paulson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Paulson "Sarah Paulson") appear in ten seasons; [Lily Rabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lily_Rabe "Lily Rabe") and [Frances Conroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Conroy "Frances Conroy") have starred in nine seasons; [Denis O'Hare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_O%27Hare "Denis O'Hare") appears in eight; [Emma Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Roberts "Emma Roberts"), [Billie Lourd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billie_Lourd "Billie Lourd") and [Leslie Grossman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Grossman "Leslie Grossman") appear in seven; [Jessica Lange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Lange "Jessica Lange"), [Kathy Bates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathy_Bates "Kathy Bates"), [Angela Bassett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Bassett "Angela Bassett") and [Jamie Brewer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Brewer "Jamie Brewer") appear in six; and [Adina Porter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adina_Porter "Adina Porter"), [Finn Wittrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finn_Wittrock "Finn Wittrock"), [Gabourey Sidibe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabourey_Sidibe "Gabourey Sidibe") and [John Carroll Lynch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Carroll_Lynch "John Carroll Lynch") appear in five seasons.

Although reception to individual seasons has varied, *American Horror Story* has largely been well received by television critics, with the majority of the praise going towards the cast, particularly [Jessica Lange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Lange "Jessica Lange"), who won two [Emmy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Award "Emmy Award"), a [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award "Golden Globe Award"), and a [Screen Actors Guild Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award "Screen Actors Guild Award") for her performances. [James Cromwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cromwell "James Cromwell") and [Kathy Bates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathy_Bates "Kathy Bates") each won an Emmy Award for their performances, while [Lady Gaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Gaga "Lady Gaga") won a Golden Globe Award. The series draws consistently high ratings for the FX network, with its first season being the most-viewed new cable series of 2011.

### *Murder House* (2011)

Set in 2011, the season follows the Harmon family, which consists of wife and mother Vivien ([Connie Britton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connie_Britton "Connie Britton")), her [psychiatrist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychiatrist "Psychiatrist") husband Ben ([Dylan McDermott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dylan_McDermott "Dylan McDermott")), and their teenage daughter Violet ([Taissa Farmiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taissa_Farmiga "Taissa Farmiga")), as they move from [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston "Boston") to [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles") to make a fresh start after Vivien has a [miscarriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscarriage "Miscarriage"). Soon after the miscarriage and before the move, Ben has an affair with one of his students, which almost tears the family apart. They move into a restored mansion and soon encounter the residence's [housekeeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Housekeeper_(domestic_worker) "Housekeeper (domestic worker)"), Moira O'Hara ([Frances Conroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Conroy "Frances Conroy") and [Alexandra Breckenridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandra_Breckenridge "Alexandra Breckenridge")), and their neighbors—the eccentric Langdon family, consisting of Constance ([Jessica Lange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Lange "Jessica Lange")) and her daughter Adelaide ([Jamie Brewer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Brewer "Jamie Brewer")), who has [Down syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Down_syndrome "Down syndrome").

The Harmons' lives are troubled by the meddling Langdons, the incompetent realtor Marcy ([Christine Estabrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christine_Estabrook "Christine Estabrook")), and the [disfigured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disfigured "Disfigured") Larry Harvey ([Denis O'Hare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_O%27Hare "Denis O'Hare")), a former resident of the mansion who is secretly in love with Constance, and the scorned Hayden McClaine ([Kate Mara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Mara "Kate Mara")), Ben's former student who follows him to Los Angeles. Ben and Vivien try to rekindle their relationship while Violet, experiencing depression, finds comfort in Tate ([Evan Peters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evan_Peters "Evan Peters")), one of Ben's patients.

The family soon discovers that the home is [haunted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haunted_house "Haunted house") by the [ghosts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost "Ghost") of anyone who has ever died on the property, including its creators Charles ([Matt Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Ross_(actor) "Matt Ross (actor)")) and Nora Montgomery ([Lily Rabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lily_Rabe "Lily Rabe")); and their [deformed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutation "Mutation") son Thaddeus ([Ben Woolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Woolf "Ben Woolf")) who is sometimes referred to as the 'Infantata'. Flashbacks depict the mansion's previous homeowners throughout the last century, dating back to its construction in the 1920s.

### *Asylum* (2012–2013)

Set in 1964, the season follows the patients and staff members of the church-owned [mental asylum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_asylum "Mental asylum") Briarcliff Manor, located in an undisclosed town in [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts "Massachusetts"), which was founded to treat and house the [criminally insane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminally_insane "Criminally insane"). Kit Walker ([Evan Peters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evan_Peters "Evan Peters")), accused of being a prolific serial killer named "Bloody Face" after the disappearance of his wife Alma ([Britne Oldford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britne_Oldford "Britne Oldford"))—though he claims she was [abducted by aliens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abducted_by_aliens "Abducted by aliens")—is incarcerated at Briarcliff. This piques the interest of ambitious lesbian [journalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journalist "Journalist") Lana Winters ([Sarah Paulson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Paulson "Sarah Paulson")), who is yearning to find a story for her big break.

At Briarcliff, Kit meets the other patients, many of whom claim to be unjustly institutionalized, including [microcephalic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microcephalic "Microcephalic") Pepper ([Naomi Grossman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Grossman "Naomi Grossman")), [nymphomaniac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nymphomania "Nymphomania") Shelley ([Chloë Sevigny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlo%C3%AB_Sevigny "Chloë Sevigny")), whose cheating husband hypocritically committed her after finding her in bed with two guys; and the unassuming Grace Bertrand ([Lizzie Brocheré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lizzie_Brocher%C3%A9 "Lizzie Brocheré")) from [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France"). Believed to be a violent serial killer, Kit becomes the subject of interest of pragmatic psychiatrist Dr. Oliver Thredson ([Zachary Quinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zachary_Quinto "Zachary Quinto")) and the sadistic Dr. Arthur Arden ([James Cromwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cromwell "James Cromwell")), with Arden routinely conducting scientific operations on patients.

The institution is run under the watchful eye of the stern Sister Jude ([Jessica Lange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Lange "Jessica Lange")), and her second-in-command, the naïve Sister Mary Eunice ([Lily Rabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lily_Rabe "Lily Rabe")), and the founder of the institution, [Monsignor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsignor "Monsignor") Timothy Howard ([Joseph Fiennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Fiennes "Joseph Fiennes")). Briarcliff's inhabitants are routinely subject to supernatural and scientific influences, including [demonic possession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demonic_possession "Demonic possession") and [extraterrestrial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extraterrestrial_life "Extraterrestrial life") abduction.

### *Coven* (2013–2014)

Set in 2013, the season follows the dwindling descendants of the witches who survived the [Salem witch trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_witch_trials "Salem witch trials") and their struggle to survive in the modern world since those who are discovered to be witches are often subjected to violent attacks from outside forces, such as [voodoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Voodoo "Louisiana Voodoo") practitioners. Zoe Benson ([Taissa Farmiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taissa_Farmiga "Taissa Farmiga")), a young teenager completely unaware of the existence of witches, discovers her identity after an accident that causes the death of her boyfriend. She is sent to an all-girls [boarding school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boarding_school "Boarding school") in [New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans "New Orleans") which aims to protect young witches and teach them how to control their powers.

### *Roanoke* (2016)

Set from 2014 to 2016, the season follows the supernatural events that occur in a renovated farmhouse in [North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina "North Carolina"), which is situated on the land where the [Roanoke Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roanoke_Colony "Roanoke Colony") moved after their infamous 1580s disappearance. In 2016, the success of the documentary leads to a sequel titled *Return to Roanoke: Three Days in Hell*, spearheaded by the producer of the original series, Sidney Aaron James ([Cheyenne Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheyenne_Jackson "Cheyenne Jackson")), who invites the Millers, as well as many of the reenactment actors, to return to the farmhouse for three days during the [blood moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_eclipse "Lunar eclipse"), where all their actions will be captured by hidden cameras. Although the Millers are aware of the entities that reside in the house, all three agree to return, each with their own agenda. ## Production

### Conception

> What you saw in the finale was the end of the Harmon house. The second season of the show will be a brand-new home or building to haunt. Just like this year, every season of this show will have a beginning, middle and end. [The second season] won't be in L.A. It will obviously be in America, but in a completely different locale.

— Murphy on the anthology format

Creators Murphy and Falchuk began working on *American Horror Story* before their Fox series *[Glee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glee_(TV_series) "Glee (TV series)")* began production. Murphy wanted to do the opposite of what he had done previously and thus began his work on the series. He stated: "I went from *[Nip/Tuck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nip/Tuck "Nip/Tuck")* to *Glee*, so it made sense that I wanted to do something challenging and dark. And I always had loved, as Brad had, the horror genre. So it just was a natural for me." Falchuk was intrigued by the idea of putting a different angle on the horror genre, stating that their main goal in creating the series was to scare viewers. "You want people to be a little bit off balance afterwards," he said.

In February 2011, FX officially announced that it had ordered a pilot for a possible series from Ryan Murphy and Brad Falchuk, with both Murphy and Falchuk writing and Murphy directing. [Dante Di Loreto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dante_Di_Loreto "Dante Di Loreto") was announced as executive producer. Production on the series began in April 2011. In July 2011, FX officially announced the project had been picked up to become a full series.

From the beginning, Murphy and Falchuk planned that each season of the series would tell a different story. After the first-season finale aired, Murphy spoke of his plans to change the cast and location for the second season. He said that some actors who starred in the first season would be returning. "The people that are coming back will be playing completely different characters, creatures, monsters, etc. [The Harmons'] stories are done. People who are coming back will be playing entirely new characters," he announced. In November 2012, FX chief executive, [John Landgraf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Landgraf "John Landgraf"), described the unique format of the series stating: "[T]he notion of doing an anthological series of miniseries with a repertory cast—has proven groundbreaking, wildly successful and will prove to be trendsetting."

At the 2013 [PaleyFest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PaleyFest "PaleyFest"), Falchuk compared the series to [horror films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horror_film "Horror film"): "It does demand a little bit of compassion at the end because you fall in love with these characters in a different way than you would in a movie," he said. "If you want to kill everybody in a movie except one person, you can kind of get away with that, but if you're looking to do a horror TV show, you have a different responsibility to the characters because the audience has a different affection for them."

Murphy then explained the process of planning a series' season takes about a year. "We come up with story first and then we come up with the characters," he said. "It is a repertory company, so we'll move people around and sometimes there won't yet be a role for somebody. Like when we started [the second season], I really had no idea that Dylan [McDermott] would be the person to play Sarah's son, but the deeper we got, I thought, that would work great."

In an August 2015 article for *Entertainment Weekly*, Murphy revealed that the show is producing two seasons a year, the first being broadcast late in the year and the second early in the next year. He explained, "We're doing something that we've never done before on the show where we're doing two different groups of writers rooms. Some of our writers will be bouncing around but a whole different group coming in late August. The next thing we're crafting up is very, very different than [*Hotel*]. Not smaller. But just not opulent. More rogue and more dark."

### Filming

Production and shooting for the first season began on 27 July 2011. The [pilot episode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot_(American_Horror_Story) "Pilot (American Horror Story)") was shot on location at the [Rosenheim Mansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosenheim_Mansion "Rosenheim Mansion") in [Country Club Park, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Club_Park,_Los_Angeles "Country Club Park, Los Angeles"), California, which serves as the [haunted house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haunted_house "Haunted house") and crime scene in the series. Designed and built in 1908 by [Alfred Rosenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Rosenheim "Alfred Rosenheim"), the president of the [American Institute of Architects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Institute_of_Architects "American Institute of Architects")' Los Angeles chapter, the [Tudor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudor_Revival_architecture "Tudor Revival architecture") or [Collegiate Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_Gothic_in_North_America "Collegiate Gothic in North America")-style single family home was previously used as a convent. The first season was filmed on sets which are an exact replica of the house. Details such as [Louis Comfort Tiffany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Comfort_Tiffany "Louis Comfort Tiffany") stained glass windows and hammered bronze light fixtures were recreated to preserve the look of the house.

Production and shooting for the second season began in late July 2012 for a scheduled premiere in mid October. The exteriors for the second season were filmed in [Hidden Valley, Ventura County, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidden_Valley,_Ventura_County,_California "Hidden Valley, Ventura County, California"), a rural area outside Los Angeles, although the season took place in Massachusetts.

Principal photography for the third season began on July 23, 2013, in [New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans "New Orleans"), [Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana "Louisiana"). It was first reported that the season would be filmed in multiple locations, but filming primarily took place in New Orleans.

Principal photography for the fourth season began on July 15, 2014, in New Orleans, though the story takes place in [Jupiter, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter,_Florida "Jupiter, Florida").

Principal photography for the fifth season began on July 14, 2015, in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles"), California, where the story also takes place. Murphy revealed a six-story hotel set was being built on the Fox lot. A dummy set of the hotel was built at the 2015 [San Diego Comic-Con](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Comic-Con "San Diego Comic-Con"), showing an [Art Deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Deco "Art Deco")-style building from the 1920s, inspired by the old [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_(film_industry) "Hollywood (film industry)") era.

Filming for the sixth season began on July 6, 2016, at [Santa Clarita, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Clarita,_California "Santa Clarita, California"). Set constructions include a colonial settler home to speculate the disappearance of the [Roanoke Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roanoke_Colony "Roanoke Colony").

Filming for the seventh season was originally planned to begin in [Orange, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange,_California "Orange, California"), in June 2017 before it was moved to May instead.

Filming for the eighth season began on June 16, 2018. It was filmed in multiple locations.

On July 11, 2019, Murphy confirmed that the ninth season had begun filming.

Season 10 was originally supposed to film in March 2020, but was delayed to the Fall due to [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19 "COVID-19"). On November 2, [Sarah Paulson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Paulson "Sarah Paulson") confirmed via [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram "Instagram") livestream that filming is set to begin either November 9, 10, or 11. On December 2, Lily Rabe confirmed that filming had begun. Filming began on December 2, 2020. The tenth season is split into two narrative parts that aired consecutively, each of which also has its own subtitle; *Red Tide* for the first six episodes, and *Death Valley* for the latter four.

Season 12 began filming on April 24, 2023. On May 4, 2023, it was reported that filming for the twelfth season had shut down due to the [WGA writers strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Writers_Guild_of_America_strike "2023 Writers Guild of America strike"). On the same day, it was reported that production resumed and that the cast and crew were using a back entrance to enter the studio and avoid crossing the picket line. On May 10, 2023, it was announced that the production of the season was halted due to the strike. Filming once again continued and wrapped in late October.

Season 13 began filming on April 6, 2026 and wrapped in July, 27, 2026.

## Promotion

As part of the promotion for the series, FX launched a "House Call" campaign, in which viewers at home could sign up and come face-to-face with a character from the series. Prior to the series premiere, FX released several clues to shine light on the series. They were offered on the show's official [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube") channel. Ten clues were released. In September 2011, FX launched a website which allows visitors to tour the Murder House throughout the decades and look for clues.

In August 2012, the first promo for the second season was released on the *American Horror Story* [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook") page entitled "Special Delivery", in which a nun carries a couple of buckets filled with body parts through a field. As a church bell rings, the nun empties one bucket's bloody contents, leaving the empty bucket behind, and resumes her trek. Over 20 subsequent teasers were released. Four photos were also released on EW.com.

Two televised teasers, titled "Meet the Residents", were released on August 31, 2012. They feature the patients and some staff (such as Dr. Thredson, played by Zachary Quinto, and Sister Mary Eunice, played by Lily Rabe) lying in twin beds and dealing with their individual issues as the heads of the asylum (Jessica Lange, Joseph Fiennes and James Cromwell) look on. The song "[Que Sera, Sera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Que_Sera,_Sera_(Whatever_Will_Be,_Will_Be) "Que Sera, Sera (Whatever Will Be, Will Be)")", mixed with the show's theme music, plays. To promote *Cult*, a competition was set up where fans who donated to the [Children's Hospital Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children%27s_Hospital_Los_Angeles "Children's Hospital Los Angeles") could get a chance to get a walk-on role in an episode, and lunch with Evan Peters.

Overall, premises and characters for each season are typically kept under wraps until soon before premiers and are alluded to with brief and vague teaser trailers.

In October 2020, the owners of the historic Alfred Rosenheim Mansion "The Murder House" hosted a virtual paranormal investigation. The house had been featured in several films, TV shows, and music videos including the first season of *American Horror Story*. A production crew set up 15 cameras throughout the home and featured Hollywood psychic [Patti Negri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patti_Negri "Patti Negri"), exorcist Bishop James Long, Michelle Belanger, and other notable guests.

### Universal's Halloween Horror Nights

On August 16, 2016, FX announced a deal had been struck to feature an *American Horror Story* [maze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haunted_attraction_(simulated) "Haunted attraction (simulated)") at [Universal Studios Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Studios_Hollywood "Universal Studios Hollywood") and [Universal Orlando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Orlando "Universal Orlando") for their [Halloween Horror Nights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halloween_Horror_Nights "Halloween Horror Nights") events. The maze featured sets and themes from *Murder House*, *Freak Show*, and *Hotel*. [Universal Parks & Resorts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Parks_%26_Resorts "Universal Parks & Resorts") said of the experience, "Twisted scenes from *Murder House* will unleash the evil spirits that possess the Harmon estate, spiraling guests through decades of the tortured dead who previously resided there.

In *Freak Show*, guests joined a troupe of biological misfits in a sinister sideshow where they were stalked by the murderous and deformed Twisty the Clown. Finally, guests succumbed to the warped desires of the Countess after checking into the haunted Hotel Cortez, conceived from the beginning as a torture chamber for its customers." In 2017, the show returned as haunted attractions to both parks, with Universal Orlando having an attraction based on *Asylum*, *Coven*, and *Roanoke*, and Universal Studios Hollywood basing their attraction solely on *Roanoke*.

In December 2017, [The Walt Disney Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company "The Walt Disney Company") announced it would [purchase 21st Century Fox which included the 20th Century Fox film and TV assets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acquisition_of_21st_Century_Fox_by_Disney "Acquisition of 21st Century Fox by Disney"). The deal was finalized in March 2019, making 20th Century Fox officially part of [The Walt Disney Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Studios_(division) "Walt Disney Studios (division)"). As Disney and Universal are bitter rivals in the [theme park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_park "Theme park") business, especially for their [Central Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Florida "Central Florida") properties, this would likely end the franchise's presence in Halloween Horror Nights at Universal parks.

### The Night Bites Bakery

On July 14, 2021, an *American Horror Story* themed bakery opened in [New York's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") [Meatpacking District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meatpacking_District,_Manhattan "Meatpacking District, Manhattan") in [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan "Manhattan") until July 24, 2021, to celebrate the release of spin-off *[American Horror Stories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Stories "American Horror Stories")* and the [tenth season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story:_Double_Feature "American Horror Story: Double Feature") titled *Double Feature*. Guests had to be over the age of 18 to enter the bakery and had to make reservations for 30 minute visits. It featured sweet treats inspired by the series, as well as unique designs and characters based on past installments of *American Horror Story* and *American Horror Stories*. After placing an order, the Rubber Woman, introduced in the first episode of *American Horror Stories*, delivered the order through a secret window. It opened in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles") from August 4, 2021, until August 14, 2021, in [Beverly Grove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beverly_Grove,_Los_Angeles "Beverly Grove, Los Angeles"). The bakery opened in [Provincetown, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincetown,_Massachusetts "Provincetown, Massachusetts"), where the first part of the tenth season were filmed, at Pat's Happy Park on September 1, 2021, until September 4, 2021.

The first season, *[American Horror Story: Murder House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story:_Murder_House "American Horror Story: Murder House")*, received generally positive reviews from critics. Review aggregator [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes") reported that 72% of 148 critics gave the first season a positive review. The site's consensus stated: "Convoluted yet effective, *American Horror Story* is strange, gory, and twisted enough to keep viewers hooked." The first season scored 62 out of 100 on [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic") based on 30 reviews, indicating generally favorable reviews. [Ken Tucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Tucker "Ken Tucker") from *[Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")* awarded the pilot episode a B+, stating: "*AHS* is pretty much all scare, all the time: a whole lotta screams, sex, jolts, mashed faces, psychotic behavior, and dead babies." [Hank Stuever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hank_Stuever "Hank Stuever") from *[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")* said in his review that: "Overdoing things is one of Murphy's trademark flaws, but this show has a captivating style and giddy gross-outs." Not all reviews were favorable: [Alan Sepinwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Sepinwall "Alan Sepinwall") of [HitFix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HitFix "HitFix") gave the series a D−, saying: "It is so far over the top that the top is a microscopic speck in its rearview mirror, and so full of strange sounds, sights and characters that you likely won't forget it—even though many of you will wish you could." Sepinwall would later go on to call it one of the worst TV shows of 2011. The *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")*' [Mary McNamara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_McNamara "Mary McNamara") gave it a mixed review, stating that it "collapses into [camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_(style) "Camp (style)")... upon more than one occasion" but also noting that it is "hard to look away."

The second season, *[American Horror Story: Asylum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story:_Asylum "American Horror Story: Asylum")*, received critical acclaim from critics. Review aggregator Rotten Tomatoes reported that 84% of 220 critics gave the season a positive review. The site's consensus is: "*American Horror Story: Asylum* crosses boundaries to shock and scare with sexy subplots and some innovative takes on current social issues." It scored 65 out of 100 on Metacritic based on 23 reviews, indicating generally favorable reviews. [James Poniewozik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Poniewozik "James Poniewozik") from *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")* stated: "*AHS: Asylum* feels like a more focused, if equally frenetic, screamfest. It's also gorgeously realized, with a vision of its '60s institution setting so detailed you can smell the stale air and incense." [Maureen Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maureen_Ryan "Maureen Ryan") of *[The Huffington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Huffington_Post "The Huffington Post")* said: "It's to the credit of *Asylum*'s writers, directors and cast that the emotional pain of the characters often feels as real as their uncertainty and terror." Verne Gay from *[Newsday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsday "Newsday")* gave the season a C grade, stating it "has some good special effects, just not much of a story to hang them on." Linda Stasi of the *[New York Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Post "New York Post")* thought the season was "over the top," adding: "I need to enter [an asylum] myself after two hours of this craziness."

The third season, *[American Horror Story: Coven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story:_Coven "American Horror Story: Coven")*, received critical acclaim from critics. Review aggregator Rotten Tomatoes reported that 85% of 221 critics gave the season a positive review. The site's consensus reads: "A noteworthy ensemble cast combined with creepy storytelling and campy, outrageous thrills make *American Horror Story: Coven* a potently structured fright-fest." It scored 71 out of 100 on Metacritic based on 24 reviews, the second-highest score for any season, after *Roanoke*, on that site. In their post-season reviews, *[The Michigan Daily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Michigan_Daily "The Michigan Daily")* gave *Coven* a B+, saying, "And while its conclusion, appropriately titled "The Seven Wonders", never quite matched the high standard it set for itself throughout the season, *Coven*'s reputation will nonetheless remain a solid one." Not all reviews were positive, however, with criticism focused on both the story and character arcs in the second half of the season. *[The A.V. Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_A.V._Club "The A.V. Club")* gave this season the low rating of a D+, with critic [Emily VanDerWerff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_St._James "Emily St. James") remarking: "It lurched drunkenly from idea to idea, never settling on one long enough to build anything of worth."

The fourth season, *[American Horror Story: Freak Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story:_Freak_Show "American Horror Story: Freak Show")*, received mostly positive reviews from critics. Review aggregator Rotten Tomatoes reports that 77% of 202 critics gave the season a positive review. The site's consensus is: "Though it may turn off new viewers unaccustomed to its unabashed weirdness, *Freak Show* still brings the thrills, thanks to its reliably stylish presentation and game cast." This season was Jessica Lange's final major performance on the show.

The fifth season, *[American Horror Story: Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story:_Hotel "American Horror Story: Hotel")*, received more mixed reviews from critics, in comparison to its predecessors. Rotten Tomatoes reports that 64% of 215 critics gave the season a positive review. The site's consensus is: "Favoring garish style over effective storytelling, the fifth *American Horror Story* strands a talented cast at Ryan Murphy's Hotel." *Hotel* scored a 60 out of 100 on Metacritic based on 24 reviews, indicating mixed or average reviews. Some praise the season for its increased amount of horror elements and experimentation, while many believe that Jessica Lange's departure negatively impacted the show's ratings and the overall character of the series moving forward. Dan Fienberg of *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")* gave a positive review, writing, "Early on, Hotel hasn't hooked me with its storytelling, but it's always fun to see what the series does with its repertory acting company and with new additions. Throw in the normal grotesquerie and visual panache, and that should keep me going for a while, even if all of the humor appears to have been funneled into *[Scream Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scream_Queens_(2015_TV_series) "Scream Queens (2015 TV series)")*." On the other hand, [Matt Zoller Seitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Zoller_Seitz "Matt Zoller Seitz") of *[New York Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Magazine "New York Magazine")* found the season "confusing, tedious, annoyingly precious, and often ostentatiously brutal", but also praised it for being "darkly beautiful, deeply weird, and (sometimes) exhilarating." Although Scott D. Pierce from *[The Salt Lake Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Salt_Lake_Tribune "The Salt Lake Tribune")* praised the production design and the cinematography, he said "the storytelling is derivative; the scares are non-existent; and it's all about style without much substance." Mike Hale from *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* complained that it "suffers from the absence of Jessica Lange". *[IGN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IGN "IGN")*'s Matt Fowler gave a rating of 5.9 out of 10, criticizing the season as "mediocre" and concluding "all weight and meaning is gone."

The sixth season, *[American Horror Story: Roanoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story:_Roanoke "American Horror Story: Roanoke")*, received mostly positive reviews from critics. Rotten Tomatoes reports that 74% of 160 critics gave the season a positive review. The site's consensus is: "*American Horror Story: My Roanoke Nightmare* takes a surprising turn away from prior *AHS* formats, revisiting the deliberate pace of earlier seasons on a spookier, smaller scale, even if the true-crime format feels overdone." It scored a 72 on Metacritic based on nine reviews, making it the highest-scored season on that site.

The seventh season, *[American Horror Story: Cult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story:_Cult "American Horror Story: Cult")*, received mostly positive reviews from critics. Rotten Tomatoes reports that 73% of 218 critics gave the season a positive review. The site's consensus is: "*American Horror Story: Cult* intrigues with timely, over-the-top creepiness – and lots of clowns – despite being hampered by broad political generalizations and occasional holes in the narrative's logic." It scored a 66 on Metacritic based on 24 reviews, indicating generally favorable reviews.

The eighth season, *[American Horror Story: Apocalypse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story:_Apocalypse "American Horror Story: Apocalypse")*, received positive reviews from critics, with some considering it as an improvement over the recent seasons. Rotten Tomatoes reports that 79% of 194 critics gave the season a positive review. The site's consensus is: "Ryan Murphy and his murderers' row of witchy performers literally save the world—and franchise—in Apocalypse, the series most ambitious crossover swing yet." It scored a 63 on Metacritic based on six reviews, indicating generally favorable reviews.

The ninth season, *[American Horror Story: 1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story:_1984 "American Horror Story: 1984")*, received critical acclaim. Rotten Tomatoes reports that 88% of 170 critics gave the season a positive review, making it the highest-scored season on that site. The site's consensus is: "A near-perfect blend of slasher tropes and *American Horror Story*'s trademark twists, *1984* is a bloody good time."

The tenth season, *[American Horror Story: Double Feature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story:_Double_Feature "American Horror Story: Double Feature")*, received positive reviews. The first part of the season, *Red Tide*, received very positive reviews from critics, while the finale was received negatively. The second part, *Death Valley*, received mixed-to-positive reviews. [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes") reports an 80% approval rating, based on 61 reviews for the season, with an average rating of 9.00/10. The site's consensus is: "Though its second tale is still a mystery, the first half of *Double Feature* proves a spine-tingling good time with terrific performances – especially from the spectacular Leslie Grossman." Liz Shannon Miller of *[Collider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collider_(website) "Collider (website)")* stated *Double Feature* manages to be one of the best *American Horror Story* seasons ever created, writing, "For 10 seasons now, *American Horror Story* has represented the wildest excesses of the Ryan Murphy brand — incredible casts, deliberately outlandish premises and plots, and experiments in form which explode the ideas of what's possible on television." Riley Runnells of *[The Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Post_(Ohio_student_newspaper) "The Post (Ohio student newspaper)")* praised the performances of the actors and complimented the writing, claiming, "The vibe of the show is almost more of a movie style and more professional-seeming than any other season so far. Not only are there incredible acting and technical moments, but the writing seems more polished and original than the more recent seasons of the show – especially the fresh take on a vampire plot." Andy Swift of *[TVLine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TVLine "TVLine")* applauded the performances of the cast members and stated the season succeeds to be terrifying, saying, "Chilling, compelling and wonderfully acted, *Double Feature* already feels like a return to form for the long-running franchise." Kayla Cobb of *[Decider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decider_(website) "Decider (website)")* said *Double Feature* succeeds to be frightening across its jumpscares and characters, while praising the performances of the actors, stating, "If you're into *American Horror Story* for the horror, you're going to have a blast. If not, then *Double Feature* has some killer performances from your favorites that you won't want to miss." Matt Fowler of *[IGN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IGN "IGN")* rated the season 7 out of 10, stating, "The first two episodes of the first half of *American Horror Story: Double Feature* start slow, but then amplify the intrigue with an interesting spin on vampires." Ron Hogan of *[Den of Geek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Den_of_Geek "Den of Geek")* rated the season 3 out of 5 stars, saying, "There are some very clever transitions between scenes, and the episode has good flow without getting slowed down during exposition dumps. The effects are used as appropriate punchlines, and *American Horror Story* is never shy about blood and gore when necessary. It (the half-season, not the episode) just felt a little too rushed, with too many ideas and not quite enough development."

The eleventh season, *[American Horror Story: NYC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story:_NYC "American Horror Story: NYC")*, received mostly positive reviews from critics. Rotten Tomatoes reports that 71% of seven critics gave the season a positive review.

The twelfth season, *[American Horror Story: Delicate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story:_Delicate "American Horror Story: Delicate")*, holds a Rotten Tomatoes approval rating of 77% with an average rating of 4.6/10, based on 13 critic reviews. The site's consensus reads: "*American Horror Story* reins in its Grand Guignol excess for a softer touch in *Delicate*, refreshing the series' formula with a slow-burn chiller about the anxieties of pregnancy. Metacritic gave it a score of 51 out of 100 based on reviews from 6 critics, indicating "mixed or average reviews".

Ryan Murphy and Brad Falchuk have been credited with creating a series that increases both the LGBT creators and storylines in the industry. Theresa L. Geller and Anna Marie Banker have stated that Murphy and Falchuk utilize "the formal structure of the serialized anthology...to present serial narratives made by and about 'queers,' openly subverting gender and sexual norms that define the majority of television." In the journal *Gender Forum*, Robert Sevenich further acknowledges that "*American Horror Story* is a unique and challenging text that confronts issues of queer visibility, provides queer performers and creators a vehicle to contribute to cultural conversations, and gives audiences a lens to glean meaning."

## Ratings

The pilot episode of *American Horror Story* was watched by 3.2 million viewers and averaged a 1.6 rating in the 18–49 years adult demographic, the most sought after by advertisers. These were the best numbers FX had ever received for a series premiere. The episode was seen by 3.2 million total viewers in 59 countries. Ratings increased as the season progressed, with the season finale being watched by 3.22 million viewers and receiving a 1.7 ratings share in the 18–49 years adult demographic.

The series premiere aired in November 2011 across Europe and Latin America on [Fox International Channels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_International_Channels "Fox International Channels"), and ranked as first or second most watched telecast in its timeslot among all [paid television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pay_television "Pay television") in most metered markets. "Numbers so strong, it's scary. *American Horror Story* has brought droves of new viewers to a killer global lineup", said Hernan Lopez, the president of Fox International Channels.

The second season's premiere gained a 2.2 18–49 ratings share and gathered 3.85 million viewers, marking the highest numbers for the series. By the season's sixth episode, the numbers dropped to a series-low 0.9 18–49 ratings share and 1.89 million viewers. They rebounded to above two million viewers for the subsequent episodes, and reached 2.29 million viewers for the season's finale. The premiere of the fifth season of the series became the second most-watched telecast in the network's history, only behind the premiere episode of the previous season, which was watched by 6.13 million viewers.

The show was renewed for another season. John Landgraf, the CEO of the network, commented on the show's success by saying that *American Horror Story*, the network's highest rated series, "has unquestionably joined the ranks of television's landmark series." In 2016, a study by *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* of the 50 TV shows with the most [Facebook Likes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook_Like "Facebook Like") found that "unlike a lot of shows about the supernatural", *American Horror Story* was "more popular in cities, but also throughout parts of the Southwest". [JustWatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JustWatch "JustWatch"), a guide to streaming content with access to data from more than 20 million users around the world, reported that *American Horror* Story was the eighth most-streamed horror show in Canada during the Halloween period between October 1, 2022, and October 13, 2025.

In September 2026, [Nielsen Media Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_Media_Research "Nielsen Media Research") reported that from May 2024 to August 2026 Americans spent more than 7 billion minutes streaming the show on [Hulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulu "Hulu"). *Coven* had the highest number of total minutes viewed (1.18 billion), while *Murder House* had the highest average minutes viewed per episode (95.6 million).

## Accolades

*American Horror Story* has won over 100 awards out of its 359 award nominations. The franchise has garnered 28 [Primetime Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award "Primetime Emmy Award") nominations, with [Jessica Lange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Lange "Jessica Lange") winning for [Outstanding Supporting Actress in a Miniseries or a Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Supporting_Actress_in_a_Miniseries_or_a_Movie "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Supporting Actress in a Miniseries or a Movie") and for [Outstanding Lead Actress in a Miniseries or a Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actress_in_a_Miniseries_or_a_Movie "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Miniseries or a Movie"), [James Cromwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cromwell "James Cromwell") winning for [Outstanding Supporting Actor in a Miniseries or a Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Supporting_Actor_in_a_Miniseries_or_a_Movie "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Supporting Actor in a Miniseries or a Movie"), and [Kathy Bates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathy_Bates "Kathy Bates") winning for Outstanding Supporting Actress in a Miniseries or a Movie. It received an additional 50 [Creative Arts Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Arts_Emmy_Award "Creative Arts Emmy Award") nominations, winning eleven times, including Outstanding Hairstyling for a Miniseries or a Movie, Outstanding Costumes for a Miniseries, Movie, or Special, Outstanding Sound Editing for a Miniseries, Movie, or Special, and Outstanding Prosthetic Makeup for a Series, Limited Series, Movie or a Special.

It has received nine [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award "Golden Globe Award") nominations, with Lange winning for [Best Supporting Actress in a Series, Miniseries or Television Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress_%E2%80%93_Series,_Miniseries_or_Television_Film "Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actress – Series, Miniseries or Television Film"), and [Lady Gaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Gaga "Lady Gaga") winning for [Best Actress in a Miniseries or Television Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_%E2%80%93_Miniseries_or_Television_Film "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress – Miniseries or Television Film"). The series has received three [Screen Actors Guild Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award "Screen Actors Guild Award") nominations, with Lange winning for [Outstanding Performance by a Female Actor in a Drama Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Female_Actor_in_a_Drama_Series "Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Female Actor in a Drama Series"). Additional accolades include eighteen [Critics' Choice Television Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Television_Award "Critics' Choice Television Award") nominations, with four wins, the [GLAAD Media Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GLAAD_Media_Award "GLAAD Media Award") for Outstanding TV Miniseries, four [Costume Designers Guild Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costume_Designers_Guild#Costume_Designers_Guild_Awards "Costume Designers Guild") nominations, winning three times, eight wins out of ten nominations at the [Make-Up Artists and Hair Stylists Guild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Make-Up_Artists_and_Hair_Stylists_Guild "Make-Up Artists and Hair Stylists Guild"), five [People's Choice Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Choice_Awards "People's Choice Awards") nominations, winning once, and eleven [Satellite Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_Awards "Satellite Awards") nomination, with three wins.

In April 2019, the Television Academy decided that *[American Horror Story: Apocalypse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Horror_Story:_Apocalypse "American Horror Story: Apocalypse")* would not qualify for the [Limited Series categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Limited_Series "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Limited Series"), and instead be moved to [Drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Drama_Series "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Drama Series"). *American Horror Story* was named the most in-demand horror TV show of 2018 by Guinness World Records, based on global TV demand data from Parrot Analytics. The series received the same accolade for 2019. It was honored again as the most in-demand horror TV show in 2023. In September 2021, *American Horror Story* was ranked fourth in a list of the top ten horror television series, based on a survey of over 2,500 fans conducted by [Fandango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fandango_Media "Fandango Media")'s transactional digital service [Vudu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vudu "Vudu").

## Companion and spin-off series

### *American Crime Story*

In October 2014, FX ordered a 10-episode companion series titled *[American Crime Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Crime_Story "American Crime Story")*, developed by [Scott Alexander and Larry Karaszewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Alexander_and_Larry_Karaszewski "Scott Alexander and Larry Karaszewski"). While each season of *American Horror Story* focuses on a new horror theme, each season of *American Crime Story* focuses on a new [true crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_crime "True crime") story. The series features *American Horror Story* cast members [Sarah Paulson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Paulson "Sarah Paulson"), [Connie Britton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connie_Britton "Connie Britton"), [Cuba Gooding Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba_Gooding_Jr. "Cuba Gooding Jr."), [Darren Criss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darren_Criss "Darren Criss"), [Finn Wittrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finn_Wittrock "Finn Wittrock"), [Max Greenfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Greenfield "Max Greenfield"), [Jon Jon Briones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Jon_Briones "Jon Jon Briones"), [Cody Fern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cody_Fern "Cody Fern") and [Billy Eichner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Eichner "Billy Eichner"). The first season premiered in February 2016, with the second season premiering in January 2018. A third season, titled *Impeachment*, focused on the [Clinton–Lewinsky scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton%E2%80%93Lewinsky_scandal "Clinton–Lewinsky scandal"), and aired in September 2021.

### *American Horror Stories*

On May 11, 2020, Murphy revealed that a [spin-off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin-off_(media) "Spin-off (media)") series named *American Horror Stories* was being developed; it would feature self-contained anthological episodes, instead of a season-long story arc as featured in *American Horror Story*. It was set to air on [FX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FX_(TV_channel) "FX (TV channel)"). On June 22, 2020, it was announced that *American Horror Stories* would stream on [FX on Hulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FX_on_Hulu "FX on Hulu") instead. On August 4, 2020, it was announced that [Sarah Paulson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Paulson "Sarah Paulson") was set to be a director on the series. The first season featured actors who have appeared in *American Horror Story*.

[Matt Bomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Bomer "Matt Bomer"), [Gavin Creel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavin_Creel "Gavin Creel"), [Sierra McCormick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_McCormick "Sierra McCormick"), [Paris Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Jackson "Paris Jackson"), [Belissa Escobedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belissa_Escobedo "Belissa Escobedo"), [Merrin Dungey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merrin_Dungey "Merrin Dungey"), Selena Sloan, Ashley Martin Carter, Valerie Loo, [Kaia Gerber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaia_Gerber "Kaia Gerber"), [Aaron Tveit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Tveit "Aaron Tveit") and Celia Finkelstein star in the first two-episode story.
Other *American Horror Story* alums that appear in the series include [Naomi Grossman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Grossman "Naomi Grossman"),
[John Carroll Lynch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Carroll_Lynch "John Carroll Lynch"), Nico Greetham, [Charles Melton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Melton "Charles Melton"), [Billie Lourd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billie_Lourd "Billie Lourd"), [Chad James Buchanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad_James_Buchanan "Chad James Buchanan"), [Cody Fern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cody_Fern "Cody Fern"), [Dylan McDermott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dylan_McDermott "Dylan McDermott"), [Jamie Brewer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Brewer "Jamie Brewer"), [Denis O'Hare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_O%27Hare "Denis O'Hare"), Matt Lasky, [Gabourey Sidibe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabourey_Sidibe "Gabourey Sidibe"), [Max Greenfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Greenfield "Max Greenfield"), Austin Woods, [Seth Gabel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Gabel "Seth Gabel"), [Rebecca Dayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_Dayan "Rebecca Dayan"), Cameron Cowperthwaite, Spencer Neville, [Teddy Sears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teddy_Sears "Teddy Sears") and [Jeff Hiller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Hiller "Jeff Hiller") along with many newcomers.

*American Horror Stories* premiered on July 15, 2021. Its first season consisted of seven episodes. A second season of eight episodes premiered on July 21, 2022. In April 2023, a third season was greenlit, which was released across October 26, 2023, and October 15, 2024.
