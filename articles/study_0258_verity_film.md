# Verity (film)

***Verity*** is a 2026 American erotic [psychological thriller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological_thriller "Psychological thriller") film directed by [Michael Showalter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Showalter "Michael Showalter"), written by [Nick Antosca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Antosca "Nick Antosca"), and based on [Colleen Hoover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colleen_Hoover "Colleen Hoover")'s [2018 novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verity_(novel) "Verity (novel)"). The film stars [Anne Hathaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hathaway "Anne Hathaway"), [Dakota Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_Johnson "Dakota Johnson"), and [Josh Hartnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Hartnett "Josh Hartnett").

*Verity* had its world premiere at the [Curzon Mayfair Cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curzon_Mayfair_Cinema "Curzon Mayfair Cinema") in London on September 24, 2026, and was released by [Amazon MGM Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_MGM_Studios "Amazon MGM Studios") in the United States and Canada on October 2, 2026. The film received mixed reviews from critics and has grossed $119.1 million against a production budget of $40 million.

## Plot

Lowen Ashleigh, a published author living in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"), grieves her mother's death from cancer while struggling to find a wider audience for her work. She is approached by a publisher looking for someone to help one of their bestselling authors [finish a popular novel series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuation_novel "Continuation novel"). The author, Verity Crawford, known for writing novels from the perspectives of immoral villains, has been left [catatonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catatonia "Catatonia") after a near-fatal car accident, a fact hidden from the media to prevent fans from thinking the series is over. Verity's husband, Jeremy, is mourning the recent drowning of their young daughter, Harper, from a canoeing accident. He assures Lowen she will be the best candidate for the job, as Verity has read her work and enjoyed it.

Lowen accepts the job and moves in with Jeremy, Verity, and Harper's twin brother Crew in their lakeside [Vermont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vermont "Vermont") home. Verity's office is in the house, allowing Lowen to immerse herself in Verity's copious notes to help her plan the next novel in the series. Lowen discovers an unfinished manuscript of Verity's autobiography, in which Verity describes her [infatuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infatuation "Infatuation") with Jeremy and eventual [resentment toward her children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postpartum_psychosis "Postpartum psychosis"), whom she viewed as competition for his love and attention. Disgusted and horrified, Lowen abandons the manuscript halfway through reading it. Despite Verity's inability to move or speak, Lowen starts to notice Verity looking at her when no one else can see, though she is unsure if these experiences are mere hallucinations.

Lowen successfully plans the next novel and prepares to begin writing it. Mutual affection grows between her and Jeremy, and they begin an affair. Jeremy confesses that though Verity never read Lowen's book, he did, and felt a deep connection to her, but lied because he thought the validation would mean more coming from Verity. Lowen revisits Verity's autobiography, and is horrified to discover a confession that Verity intentionally [capsized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capsizing "Capsizing") the canoe in an attempt to drown her children and thus reclaim Jeremy's devotion; their son Crew only survived because of his ability to swim. The manuscript ends with a declaration that Verity plans to commit [vehicular suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicular_suicide "Vehicular suicide") to escape legal consequences for her actions. Lowen confronts Verity, threatening to expose her to the police and take Verity's family and career for herself.

Lowen asks Crew about Harper's death; he refuses to answer, but implies that Verity speaks to him, despite her supposed catatonia. She confides her findings to Jeremy. He angrily confronts Verity, who fearfully admits to faking her catatonia. He begins to strangle her, but Lowen implores him to stop and suggests that he suffocate her instead to make her death look like an accident. After he kills her, they dump her body in the lake and convince police that she must have woken up from her catatonia, left the house in confusion, and accidentally drowned.

Seven months later, Lowen has published the next novel in Verity's series and is pregnant with Jeremy's daughter. As they prepare to move out of the house, Lowen discovers a handwritten letter from Verity, addressed to Jeremy in the event that she and Crew left him. The letter promises that the autobiography was merely a writing exercise to help her process her grief; she never resented her children, Harper's death was truly an accident, and she was not actually suicidal. It also reveals that Jeremy discovered the manuscript, believed it to be true, drugged Verity, and put her in her car with a brick on the accelerator in an attempt to kill her and make it look like a suicide. She survived, but faked catatonia for fear that he would report her to police or kill her if he thought she was capable of telling anyone what he did. Lowen decides not to tell Jeremy about the letter and continue her new life with Jeremy, Crew, and her daughter, no longer sure whether to believe Verity or Jeremy's account of what happened.

## Production

### Development and casting

A film adaptation of [Colleen Hoover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colleen_Hoover "Colleen Hoover")'s 2018 novel *[Verity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verity_(novel) "Verity (novel)")* was being developed by [Nick Antosca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Antosca "Nick Antosca"), Alex Hedlund, and Eat the Cat. They had hired April Maguire and Will Honley to write the screenplay before November 2020 for the then-named [Amazon MGM Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_MGM_Studios "Amazon MGM Studios"). In May 2024, Hilary Seitz was hired to rewrite the film's screenplay. After additional draft work from Angela LaManna, Antosca was finalizing the screenplay himself in November 2024 when [Michael Showalter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Showalter "Michael Showalter") had signed on to direct the film and [Anne Hathaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hathaway "Anne Hathaway") agreed to lead the film as Verity Crawford. The next month, [Josh Hartnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Hartnett "Josh Hartnett") and [Dakota Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_Johnson "Dakota Johnson") joined the cast for the film. In February 2025, [Ismael Cruz Córdova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismael_Cruz_C%C3%B3rdova "Ismael Cruz Córdova"), Brady Wagner, [Irina Dvorovenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irina_Dvorovenko "Irina Dvorovenko"), K. K. Moggie, and Michael Abbott Jr. were cast in undisclosed roles.

### Filming

[Principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography "Principal photography") began in February 2025, with Hathaway and Hartnett shooting on location in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"). Johnson began shooting her scenes by the following month. Production concluded in April 2025.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Ending

An alternate twist ending—the film's initial ending—was shot and included Johnson putting on shades, rolling down the window of the car and making waves with her hand (alluding to the audience that she is the master manipulator) before cutting to Hathaway's sinking body wherein she abruptly opens her eyes, indicating she is alive. However, test audiences were not satisfied by this ending, so it was cut.

## Release

*Verity* had its world premiere at the [Curzon Mayfair Cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curzon_Mayfair_Cinema "Curzon Mayfair Cinema") in London on September 24, 2026, and was released in the United States and Canada on October 2, 2026, by [Amazon MGM Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_MGM_Studios "Amazon MGM Studios"). and by [Sony Pictures Releasing International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Pictures_Releasing_International "Sony Pictures Releasing International") on that same date. It was originally set to be released on May 15, 2026. The film will be screened in the Gala section of the [39th Tokyo International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/39th_Tokyo_International_Film_Festival "39th Tokyo International Film Festival") on October 29, 2026.

## Reception

### Box office

As of October 14, 2026, *Verity* has grossed $58.9 million in the United States and Canada and $60.2 million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $119.1 million.

In the United States and Canada, *Verity* made $14.5 million on its first day, including $4.5 million from Thursday night previews. It went on to debut at $32.6 million, topping the box office. In its second weekend the film made $11.5 million, dropping 64% and finishing in second behind *[Other Mommy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Other_Mommy "Other Mommy")*.

### Critical response

*Verity* received mixed reviews from critics. On the [review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator "Review aggregator") website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes"), 31% of 180 critics' reviews are positive, with an average rating of 4.9/10. The website's consensus reads: "Possessing all the ingredients for a guilty pleasure, *Verity* leans more towards guilty than pleasurable due to plot twists that are more deflating than intriguing." [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), which uses a [weighted average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_arithmetic_mean "Weighted arithmetic mean"), assigned the film a score of 44 out of 100, based on 35 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews. Audiences polled by [CinemaScore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CinemaScore "CinemaScore") gave the film an average grade of "C+" on an A+ to F scale, while 50% of those surveyed by [PostTrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PostTrak "PostTrak") said they would definitely recommend the film.

[RogerEbert.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RogerEbert.com "RogerEbert.com")'s [Matt Zoller Seitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Zoller_Seitz "Matt Zoller Seitz") gave the film three out of four stars, and wrote, "it’s wickedly playful, and it gives its central performers plenty of chances to show off their technique and build their movie-star personas." Larushka Ivan-Zadeh of *[Time Out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Out_(magazine) "Time Out (magazine)")* gave the film three out of five stars, praising its combination of erotic thriller elements, dark humor, and deliberately excessive melodrama. Ivan-Zadeh highlighted Johnson's return to the sensual thriller style associated with *[Fifty Shades of Grey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifty_Shades_of_Grey_(film) "Fifty Shades of Grey (film)")*, as well as Showalter's ability to balance the film's darker subject matter with a playful, self-aware tone. She also praised the performances and described the film as a "wild and fun ride", while criticizing its excessive twists and lengthy reveal. Odie Henderson of *[The Boston Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Boston_Globe "The Boston Globe")* gave the film two and a half out of five stars, describing it as a "shockingly watchable" adaptation of Hoover's novel. He praised Hathaway's performance as the manipulative Verity, arguing that her scenes were more compelling than Johnson's, and noted Hartnett's humorous portrayal of Jeremy. Henderson also felt that the film would appeal to fans of the novel, although he criticized its slow pace and lengthy runtime.

[Peter Bradshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bradshaw "Peter Bradshaw") of *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* gave the film a negative review, praising its premise and opening sequence but arguing that the story gradually loses its effectiveness. Bradshaw noted the film's potentially entertaining combination of erotic mystery, gothic influences, and psychological suspense, but criticized the disappearance of supporting characters, the increasingly implausible plot, and the final act. He argued that the film's narrative ultimately "disintegrates" into an unsatisfying series of plot developments and described its resolution as unconvincing and inadequately ambiguous. Clarisse Loughrey of *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent "The Independent")* criticized the film as a derivative and less entertaining version of *[The Housemaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Housemaid_(2025_film) "The Housemaid (2025 film)")*, arguing that it closely resembles the earlier thriller while failing to generate the same level of excitement. Loughrey criticized the film's forced chemistry, glossy presentation, and reliance on familiar domestic-thriller conventions, while also arguing that Johnson and Hathaway were not given material strong enough to elevate the adaptation. She ultimately described the film as an example of a potentially compelling thriller being weakened by its execution and lack of originality.
