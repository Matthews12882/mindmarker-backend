# History of Europe

The **history of Europe** can be traced back to the appearance of the [first modern humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_European_modern_humans "Early European modern humans") identified as ancestors of contemporary Europeans about 37,000 years ago during the [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic_Europe "Paleolithic Europe"). [Early European Farmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_European_Farmers "Early European Farmers") migrating from [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia#Prehistoric_Anatolia "Anatolia") spread agriculture across the continent during the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_Europe "Neolithic Europe") (7000–2000 BC), a period that also included [early metallurgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgy_during_the_Copper_Age_in_Europe "Metallurgy during the Copper Age in Europe"). Starting around 4000 BC, westward migrations of [steppe pastoralists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Steppe_Herders "Western Steppe Herders") spread [Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-Europeans "Proto-Indo-Europeans") languages. The rise of the [city-states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polis "Polis") of [ancient Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece "Ancient Greece") defines the beginning of [classical antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_antiquity "Classical antiquity") (c. 800 BC-AD 500). The [Hellenistic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period "Hellenistic period") (323–30 BC) began with the death of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great "Alexander the Great") and ended with the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principate "Principate")'s control of the [entire Mediterranean basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mare_Nostrum "Mare Nostrum"). [Christianity emerged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Christianity "Early Christianity") under Roman rule in the 1st century AD. The [Migration Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_Period "Migration Period") of the [Germanic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_peoples "Germanic peoples") began in the late 4th century AD.

The [abdication of the last Western Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Western_Roman_Empire "Fall of the Western Roman Empire") in 476 traditionally marks the beginning of the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages "Middle Ages") (c. 500–1500). While the [Eastern Roman ("Byzantine") Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Empire "Eastern Roman Empire") with its capital in [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople "Constantinople") continued for another 1000 years, the former lands of the [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire "Western Roman Empire") fragmented into the [barbarian kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbarian_kingdoms "Barbarian kingdoms"). Beginning in the 7th century the [Mediterranean became a battleground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_conquests#Arab–Byzantine_naval_warfare "Early Muslim conquests") between the Byzantines and [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidun_Caliphate "Rashidun Caliphate"). The 8th-century [Umayyad conquest of Hispania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_conquest_of_Hispania "Umayyad conquest of Hispania") established [Al-Andalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus "Al-Andalus"). The Rome-based [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church#History "Latin Church") proclaimed the King of the [Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks#Carolingian_kingdom_(751–987) "Franks"), [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne "Charlemagne"), as ["Emperor of the Romans"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne#Coronation "Charlemagne") in the early 9th century and increasingly competitive [missionary efforts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization_of_the_Slavs "Christianization of the Slavs") by the [Latin West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_West "Latin West") and Byzantines resulted in the division of the [Slavic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_peoples "Slavic peoples") between [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_Latin "Medieval Latin") and [Cyrillic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrillic "Cyrillic") use. [Norsemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norsemen "Norsemen") [raided](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Paris_(885%E2%80%93886) "Siege of Paris (885–886)"), [invaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cnut%27s_invasion_of_England "Cnut's invasion of England"), and [trafficked slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_medieval_Europe#Vikings "Slavery in medieval Europe") across Europe during the [Viking Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_Age "Viking Age"). During the [High Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Middle_Ages "High Middle Ages") (c. 1000–1300), the Latin Church [split with the Eastern Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East%E2%80%93West_Schism "East–West Schism"), was [convulsed by reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_papacy_(1048%E2%80%931257)#Electoral_reform "History of the papacy (1048–1257)") and [entangled in conflict with secular monarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investiture_Controversy "Investiture Controversy"), called for the [Crusades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades "Crusades"), and provided key support for [northern Italian city-states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_League "Lombard League") achieving [independence from the Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Constance#Terms "Peace of Constance"). The [oldest university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_European_universities#The_first_European_universities "History of European universities") currently in continuous operation in the world was established in the 12th century amid rapid population and urban growth that continued until the end of the 13th century. Despite a [decline in population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_demography#Late_Middle_Ages "Medieval demography") caused by the [Great Famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Famine_of_1315%E2%80%931317 "Great Famine of 1315–1317") and the [Black Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Death "Black Death") in the 14th century, Europe experienced a cultural [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance "Renaissance") in the 15th century.

[Early modern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_Europe "Early modern Europe") began with 15th-century revolutions in both [printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing_press#Mass_production_and_spread_in_Europe "Printing press") and [seafaring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery#Technology:_Ship_design_and_the_compass "Age of Discovery"). Printing changed how [knowledge was created, preserved and disseminated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing_press#Science "Printing press") while the [Age of Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery "Age of Discovery") led to extensive [colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analysis_of_European_colonialism_and_colonization "Analysis of European colonialism and colonization"), the [Atlantic slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_slave_trade "Atlantic slave trade") and the [Columbian exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbian_exchange "Columbian exchange"). The 16th-century [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation "Reformation") challenged the hegemony of the [Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church#Age_of_Discovery_and_Counter-Reformation "Catholic Church") and precipitated [over a century of religious wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_wars_of_religion "European wars of religion"). With leadership from Italian [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer "Astronomer") [Galileo Galilei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo_Galilei "Galileo Galilei") and English [mathematical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_physicist "Mathematical physicist") [Isaac Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Newton "Isaac Newton"), modern science emerged during the [Scientific Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_Revolution "Scientific Revolution") of the 17th century.

The [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution "Industrial Revolution") and [waves of liberal and nationalist upheaval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Revolution "Age of Revolution") shaped the [long 19th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_nineteenth_century "Long nineteenth century") dominated by the five great powers of the [Concert of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concert_of_Europe "Concert of Europe"). [Two rival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Alliance_(1882) "Triple Alliance (1882)") [military alliances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Entente "Triple Entente") fought [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I") (1914–1918), which ended with multi-ethnic empires fragmented into [nation states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation_state "Nation state"). [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany")'s [invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland "Invasion of Poland") launched [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") (1939–1945), and as its armies overran Europe the regime systematically perpetrated [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust "The Holocaust"). During the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War") (1947–1991), Europe was divided between a [U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States#Cold_War_and_social_revolution_(1945–1991) "United States")-led [Western bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_bloc "Western bloc") of countries and the [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO") military alliance and the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union")-led [Eastern bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_bloc "Eastern bloc") and the [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact "Warsaw Pact"). The Western bloc's [colonial empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation "Decolonisation") and remaining military dictatorships in [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_transition_to_democracy "Portuguese transition to democracy"), [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_junta "Greek junta") and [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_transition_to_democracy "Spanish transition to democracy") were dismantled. After Soviet leader [Mikhail Gorbachev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gorbachev "Mikhail Gorbachev") refused to intervene militarily against [democratic reforms in Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Polish_parliamentary_election "1989 Polish parliamentary election") and [elsewhere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinatra_Doctrine "Sinatra Doctrine"), the [Revolutions of 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1989 "Revolutions of 1989") led to all European communist states [transitioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_economy "Transition economy") to [market economies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_economy "Market economy") and then the [Soviet Union itself dissolving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union "Dissolution of the Soviet Union"). The [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union") ("EU") was established in 1993. In the 21st century, most of the former communist states [joined the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expansion_of_the_European_Union "Expansion of the European Union") and Europe faced the [Euro area crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_area_crisis "Euro area crisis") (2009–2018) and the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine "2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine") (2022–).

## Prehistoric Europe

[Prehistoric Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_Europe "Prehistoric Europe") refers to the earliest era of human life in Europe predating any written records.

### Paleolithic and Mesolithic

The [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic_Europe "Paleolithic Europe") ("Old Stone Age") and [Mesolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesolithic#Europe "Mesolithic") ("Middle of Stone Age") eras in Europe were defined by a [hunter-gatherer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer "Hunter-gatherer") way of life and increasingly specialized stone tools. [Kostenki 14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostyonki%E2%80%93Borshchyovo#Kostenki-14 "Kostyonki–Borshchyovo"), who lived in the [Don River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_(river) "Don (river)") basin around 37,000 years ago, is among the earliest Europeans whose [genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_DNA "Ancient DNA") carries an ancestry signature recognizable in contemporary populations of the continent. The [Last Glacial Maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Maximum "Last Glacial Maximum"), roughly 27,000 to 19,000 years ago, pushed populations into [southern refugia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Maximum_refugia#Europe "Last Glacial Maximum refugia") and may have reduced the human population of Europe to as few as 20,000 people. The [Western Hunter-Gatherers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Hunter-Gatherers "Western Hunter-Gatherers"), who would later dominate the continent and form one of the three distinct ancestral components of modern Europeans, probably later [expanded from southeastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_hunter-gatherer#Origin_and_expansion_into_continental_Europe "Western hunter-gatherer"). By the late Mesolithic, the coastal foraging [Ertebølle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erteb%C3%B8lle "Ertebølle") of southern Scandinavia (c. 5,400–3,950 BC) were one of the most complex hunter-gatherer cultures in Europe.

### Neolithic and Copper Age

* [Neolithic expansion in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_Europe "Neolithic Europe"), 7000–4000 BC
* [Yamnaya culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamnaya_culture "Yamnaya culture"), 3300–2500 BC
* [Indo-European migrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_migrations "Indo-European migrations")

[Neolithic Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_Europe "Neolithic Europe") emerged in the 7th millennium BC as the first [permanent settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_settlement#Emergence_of_permanent_settlements "Human settlement") – with [houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_long_house "Neolithic long house"), [wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_water_supply_and_sanitation#Prehistory "History of water supply and sanitation"), granaries, and defensive structures – appeared in the [Balkans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkans#Antiquity "Balkans"). These settlements are linked to [Early European Farmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_European_Farmers "Early European Farmers") migrating from [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia "Anatolia"), the second main ancestral component (after the earlier Mesolithic [Western hunter-gatherers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_hunter-gatherers "Western hunter-gatherers")) of contemporary Europeans. Three Balkan archaeological sites hold, respectively, the world's [oldest securely dated evidence of high-temperature copper smelting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudnik_(mountain)#History "Rudnik (mountain)") (c. 5000 BC), the world's [oldest gold artifacts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varna_culture "Varna culture") (c. 4600 BC), and Europe's [oldest urban center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solnitsata "Solnitsata") (c. 4700–4200 BC). [Yamnaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamnaya_culture "Yamnaya culture") [pastoralists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Steppe_Herders "Western Steppe Herders") began expanding into Southeast Europe around 4200 BC but the main [Indo-European migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_migrations#Yamnaya-culture "Indo-European migrations") came c. 3300–2600 BC, after which [Indo-European languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_languages "Indo-European languages") spread across the continent.

* Neolithic settlement of [Sesklo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesklo "Sesklo"), Greece, c. 5900 BC
* [Varna necropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varna_culture "Varna culture") artefacts, Bulgaria, c. 4500 BC
* [Menga Dolmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolmen_of_Menga "Dolmen of Menga"), Spain, c. 3700 BC
* The city-like settlement of [Maidanetske](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maidanetske "Maidanetske"), Ukraine, c. 3700 BC, [Cucuteni-Trypillia culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cucuteni%E2%80%93Trypillia_culture "Cucuteni–Trypillia culture")
* The [Ljubljana Marshes Wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ljubljana_Marshes_Wheel "Ljubljana Marshes Wheel"), Slovenia, c. 3130 BC, the oldest transportation wheel with an axle ever discovered
* [Stonehenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonehenge "Stonehenge"), Britain, c. 2500 BC

## Protohistoric Europe

### Bronze Age

The first well-known literate civilization in Europe was the [Minoan civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoan_civilization "Minoan civilization") that arose on the island of [Crete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crete "Crete") and flourished from approximately the 27th century BC to the 15th century BC.

The Minoans were replaced by the [Mycenaean civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenaean_civilization "Mycenaean civilization") which flourished during the period roughly between 1600 BC, when [Helladic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helladic "Helladic") culture in [mainland Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Greece "Geography of Greece") was transformed under influences from Minoan Crete, and 1100 BC. The major Mycenaean cities were [Mycenae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenae "Mycenae") and [Tiryns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiryns "Tiryns") in Argolis, [Pylos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pylos "Pylos") in Messenia, [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens "Athens") in Attica, [Thebes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Thebes_(Boeotia) "Ancient Thebes (Boeotia)") and [Orchomenus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchomenus_(Boeotia) "Orchomenus (Boeotia)") in Boeotia, and [Iolkos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iolkos "Iolkos") in Thessaly. In [Crete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crete "Crete"), the Mycenaeans occupied [Knossos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knossos "Knossos"). Mycenaean settlement sites also appeared in [Epirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epirus "Epirus"), [Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonia_(region) "Macedonia (region)"), on islands in the [Aegean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegean_Sea "Aegean Sea"), on the coast of [Asia Minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia_Minor "Asia Minor"), the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant "Levant"), [Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus "Cyprus") and Italy. Mycenaean artefacts have been found well outside the limits of the Mycenaean world.

Quite unlike the Minoans, whose society benefited from trade, the Mycenaeans advanced through conquest. Mycenaean civilization was dominated by a warrior [aristocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristocracy "Aristocracy"). Around 1400 BC, the Mycenaeans extended their control to Crete, the centre of the Minoan civilization. The Mycenaean civilization perished with the [collapse of Bronze-Age civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age_collapse "Bronze Age collapse") on the eastern shores of the Mediterranean Sea. The collapse has been variously attributed to [Dorian invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorian_invasion "Dorian invasion"), natural disasters and/or climate change. Whatever the causes, the Mycenaean civilization had disappeared after [LH III C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LH_IIIC "LH IIIC"), when the sites of Mycenae and Tiryns were again destroyed. This end, during the last years of the 12th century BC, occurred after a slow decline of the Mycenaean civilization, which lasted many years before dying out. The beginning of the 11th century BC opened a new context, that of the protogeometric, the beginning of the geometric period, the *[Greek Dark Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Dark_Ages "Greek Dark Ages")* of traditional historiography.

The Bronze Age collapse may be seen in the context of technological history that saw the slow spread of [ironworking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironworking "Ironworking") technology from present-day [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria "Bulgaria") and [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania "Romania") in the 13th and the 12th centuries BC.

The [Tumulus culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumulus_culture "Tumulus culture") and the following [Urnfield culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urnfield_culture "Urnfield culture") of central Europe were part of the origin of the [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_ancient_Rome "Culture of ancient Rome") and [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Greece "Classical Greece") cultures.

## Classical antiquity

[Classical antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_antiquity "Classical antiquity"), also known as the classical era, classical period, classical age, or simply antiquity, is the period between the 8th century BC and the 5th century AD during which the interwoven civilizations of [ancient Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece "Ancient Greece") and [ancient Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome "Ancient Rome"), known together as the [Greco-Roman world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Roman_world "Greco-Roman world") and centered on the [Mediterranean Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Basin "Mediterranean Basin"), flourished and influenced Europe, [North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Africa "North Africa"), and [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia "West Asia").

### Ancient Greece

Ancient Greece (also "Hellenic civilisation") was a group of several hundred relatively independent but culturally and linguistically related city-states ("[poleis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poleis "Poleis")") – including [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Athens "Ancient Athens"), [Sparta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Sparta "History of Sparta"), [Thebes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thebes,_Greece#Archaic_and_classical_periods "Thebes, Greece"), [Corinth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Corinth "Ancient Corinth"), and [Syracuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_Sicily#Ancient_Syracuse "Syracuse, Sicily") – distinguished by remarkable accomplishments in [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_architecture "Ancient Greek architecture"), [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_philosophy "Ancient Greek philosophy"), [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_mathematics "Ancient Greek mathematics"), [sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Olympic_Games "Ancient Olympic Games"), [theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_of_ancient_Greece "Theatre of ancient Greece") and [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_ancient_Greece "Music of ancient Greece"). Athens governed itself with a form of [direct democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athenian_democracy "Athenian democracy") and by the late 4th century BC as many as half of the over one thousand existing Greek cities might have been democracies. Athens was the home of [Socrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socrates "Socrates") (c. 470-399 BC), [Plato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plato "Plato") (c. 428–347 BC), the [Platonic Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonic_Academy "Platonic Academy"), [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle "Aristotle") (384–322 BC) and the [Peripatetic school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peripatetic_school "Peripatetic school"). Hellenic city-states established colonies on the shores of the [Black Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea "Black Sea") ([Greek Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Crimea "Greek Crimea") and [Pontic Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontic_Greeks#Antiquity "Pontic Greeks")) and Mediterranean ([Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia#Early_Greek_presence "Anatolia"), [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Greek_Sicily "History of Greek Sicily"), and [southern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_Graecia "Magna Graecia")) and also fought [a half century of wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Persian_Wars "Greco-Persian Wars") with the [Persian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire "Achaemenid Empire").

Hellenic infighting left Greek city states vulnerable, and [Philip II of Macedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Macedon "Philip II of Macedon") (r. 359–336 BC) [united the Greek city states under his control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Corinth "League of Corinth"). His son, [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great "Alexander the Great") (r. 336–323 BC), conquered [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great#Egypt "Alexander the Great") and [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great#Persia "Alexander the Great") and going as far off as [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great#Indian_campaign "Alexander the Great"). During the [Hellenistic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period "Hellenistic period") (323–30 BC) following the [death of Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Alexander_the_Great "Death of Alexander the Great") and the [wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wars_of_the_Diadochi "Wars of the Diadochi") between [his generals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diadochi "Diadochi"), the resulting [Ptolemaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_Kingdom "Ptolemaic Kingdom"), [Seleucid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire "Seleucid Empire"), and [Macedonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonia_(ancient_kingdom) "Macedonia (ancient kingdom)") kingdoms spread Greek culture to regions as far away as [Bactria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bactria "Bactria"). In the [newly founded city of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Alexandria#Foundation "History of Alexandria"), [Euclid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid "Euclid")'s (fl. 300 BC) *[Elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid%27s_Elements "Euclid's Elements")* revolutionized [geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry#History "Geometry") and has been referred to as the most successful [textbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textbook "Textbook") ever written and second only to the [Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible "Bible") in the number of editions that have been published. The [Syracusan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_Sicily#Ancient_Syracuse "Syracuse, Sicily") mathematician, physicist, astronomer, engineer and inventor [Archimedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedes "Archimedes") (c. 287–c. 212 BC) is considered one of the greatest mathematicians of all time as well as the father of [mathematical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_physics "Mathematical physics").

### Ancient Rome

Beginning in the 3rd century BC, the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic "Roman Republic") began expanding outward from Italy, [defeating and absorbing Hellenistic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic#Roman_supremacy_in_the_Greek_East "Roman Republic") and assimilating Greek culture. The most serious challenge to Roman ascent came from the [Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia "Phoenicia") colony of [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage "Carthage"), and its defeats in the three [Punic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic_Wars "Punic Wars") marked the start of Roman [hegemony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegemony "Hegemony"). First governed by [kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_kings "Roman kings"), then as a senatorial republic, Rome was transformed into an empire at the end of the 1st century BC after [Julius Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Caesar "Julius Caesar")'s (r. 49–44 BC) military seizure of power [ended republican rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crisis_of_the_Roman_Republic "Crisis of the Roman Republic"), enabling [Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus "Augustus") (r. 27 BC-AD 14) and his authoritarian successors. During the reign of the early emperors, [Christianity emerged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Christianity "Early Christianity") in the [Roman province of Judaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaea_(Roman_province) "Judaea (Roman province)") as a movement centered on the 1st-century [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Temple_Judaism "Second Temple Judaism") preacher and religious leader [Jesus of Nazareth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_of_Nazareth "Jesus of Nazareth") (c. 6 BC-AD 33) and spread through the empire's cities over the following centuries despite [periodic persecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Christians_in_the_Roman_Empire "Persecution of Christians in the Roman Empire"). During the two-century *[Pax Romana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pax_Romana "Pax Romana")*, the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire "Roman Empire") became the only state in history to [encircle the entire Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mare_Nostrum "Mare Nostrum"), spanning three continents from the [Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Ocean "Atlantic Ocean") to the [Persian Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf "Persian Gulf").

* [Cicero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cicero "Cicero") addresses the [Roman Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Senate "Roman Senate") to denounce [Catiline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catiline "Catiline")'s conspiracy to overthrow the [Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic "Roman Republic"), by [Cesare Maccari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesare_Maccari "Cesare Maccari").
* The [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic "Roman Republic") and its neighbours in 58 BC
* The [Colosseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colosseum "Colosseum"), Rome, constructed between 72 and 80 AD.
* The [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire "Roman Empire") at its greatest extent in 117 AD.

In the 3rd century, [barbarian invasions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbarian_invasions_into_the_Roman_Empire_of_the_3rd_century "Barbarian invasions into the Roman Empire of the 3rd century") and a [series of civil wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crisis_of_the_Third_Century "Crisis of the Third Century") undermined the Empire. The emperors [Diocletian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocletian "Diocletian") (r. 284–305) and [Constantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_I_of_the_Roman_Empire "Constantine I of the Roman Empire") (r. 306–337) were able to slow down the process of decline by splitting the empire into a [Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire "Western Roman Empire") and an [Eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire "Byzantine Empire") part with the capital in the Greek city of [Byzantium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantium "Byzantium"), renamed [Nova Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Rome "New Rome") in 330 (marking the conventional start of the Byzantine Empire) and later as [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople "Constantinople") ("City of Constantine"). Constantine [declared an official end to state-sponsored persecution of Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edict_of_Milan "Edict of Milan") in 313 and convened [the council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Council_of_Nicaea "First Council of Nicaea") that standardized the [doctrines ("Nicene Christianity") still followed by most Christian churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicene_Christianity "Nicene Christianity") today. [Theodosius I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodosius_I "Theodosius I") (r. 379–395) made Nicene Christianity the [official religion of the Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_church_of_the_Roman_Empire "State church of the Roman Empire") in 381. Fifth-century emperors specifically [assigned authority over the territories of the Western Roman Empire to the bishop of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_the_West#History "Patriarch of the West").

The Roman Empire was repeatedly attacked by [Hunnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huns "Huns"), [Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronology_of_warfare_between_the_Romans_and_Germanic_peoples "Chronology of warfare between the Romans and Germanic peoples"), [Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_people "Slavic people") and other "barbarian" tribes (see: [Migration Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_Period "Migration Period")), and in 476 the Western part [fell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Western_Roman_Empire "Fall of the Western Roman Empire") when the [last emperor of the Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romulus_Augustus "Romulus Augustus") surrendered to a [barbarian king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odoacer "Odoacer").

## Post-classical and medieval Europe

### Early Middle Ages

* [Barbarian kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbarian_kingdoms "Barbarian kingdoms"), 476.
* "[T and O map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T_and_O_map "T and O map")", from [Isidore of Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isidore_of_Seville "Isidore of Seville")'s *[Etymologiae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymologiae "Etymologiae")* (c. 625) identifying the world's continents (Asia, Europe and Africa).
* Earliest European depiction of a horse collar, c. 800.

After the [fall of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Rome "Fall of Rome"), Western Europe fragmented into [warring kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbarian_kingdoms "Barbarian kingdoms") that continued to struggle with the destructive trends of [late antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_antiquity "Late antiquity"): [depopulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_demography#Late_Antiquity "Medieval demography"), [deurbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterurbanization "Counterurbanization"), and [barbarian invasions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_Period "Migration Period"). As the remnants of [Roman governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_institutions_of_ancient_Rome#State_offices "Political institutions of ancient Rome") disappeared, much of [Greco-Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Roman "Greco-Roman") culture was all but lost in the western part of the old empire. The gradual adoption of costly [parchment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parchment "Parchment"), followed by the end of imports of much cheaper [papyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papyrus#History "Papyrus"), deepened the Rome-based [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church "Latin Church")'s monopoly on literacy in the region – already entrenched by the collapse of the municipal schools and civic offices that rewarded literate civil officials – and left the Church as the primary source of [institutional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institution "Institution") continuity, [legal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law "Law") memory, and [administrative expertise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_administration#Medieval_to_19th-century "Public administration") for the turbulent post-Roman kingdoms of Western Europe. The [Visigoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Council_of_Toledo "Third Council of Toledo") and [Lombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perctarit#Return,_Catholicism,_Impact "Perctarit") abandoned [Arianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arianism "Arianism") for [Nicene Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicene_Christianity "Nicene Christianity") and the [pagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paganism "Paganism") [Anglo-Saxons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianisation_of_Anglo-Saxon_England "Christianisation of Anglo-Saxon England"), [Thuringians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thuringi#Ecclesiastical_history "Thuringi"), and [Bavarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavaria#Middle_Ages "Bavaria") converted to Christianity between the late 6th and mid-8th centuries but the [Umayyad conquest of the Visigothic Kingdom in Iberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_the_Iberian_Peninsula "Muslim conquest of the Iberian Peninsula") (711–720s) left the [Kingdom of the Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francia "Francia") ("Francia"), [Kingdom of the Lombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Lombards "Kingdom of the Lombards") (on the [Italian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Peninsula "Italian Peninsula")) and [petty Anglo-Saxon kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heptarchy "Heptarchy") (in the [British Isles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Isles "British Isles")) as the only significant Latin Christian realms. Within these three realms, however, the cultural influence of the Latin Church - including its [prohibitions on cousin marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Divergence#The_Catholic_Church's_Prohibition_of_Cousin_Marriage "Great Divergence"), [polygamy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygamy_in_Christianity#Middle_Ages "Polygamy in Christianity"), and remarriage within kin groups – ran longest and cut deepest, gradually eroding the extended [clans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clans "Clans") and [kinship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinship "Kinship") groups that had long organized Western European society.

The change in the character of the Constantinople-based [Eastern Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Empire "Eastern Roman Empire") ("Byzantine Empire") was gradual but by the 7th century [Latin titles and usages had been officially replaced with Greek versions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenization_in_the_Byzantine_Empire#Change_of_Imperial_Administrative_Language_from_Latin_to_Greek "Hellenization in the Byzantine Empire"). During most of its existence, the Empire was one of the most powerful economic, cultural, and military forces in Europe and Constantinople was Europe's largest and wealthiest city. During the period of the [Byzantine Papacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Papacy "Byzantine Papacy") (537–752), the [bishops of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome#Middle_Ages "Bishop of Rome") required Byzantine approval of their elevation, and many were selected from among the [apocrisiarii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_apocrisiarius "Papal apocrisiarius"), ambassadors from the Latin Church to the Byzantine court in Constantinople. The Empire [survived plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plague_of_Justinian "Plague of Justinian") (541–549), won the [Gothic War (535–554)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_War_(535%E2%80%93554) "Gothic War (535–554)"), lost [most of Italy to the Lombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alboin#March_to_Italy "Alboin") (568–c. 600), prevailed over a [Persian/Avar siege of its capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Constantinople_(626) "Siege of Constantinople (626)") (626), lost [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Syria "Muslim conquest of Syria") (639) and [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_conquest_of_Egypt "Arab conquest of Egypt") (642) to the [Rashidun Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidun_Caliphate "Rashidun Caliphate"), survived [another siege of Constantinople this time by the Umayyads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Constantinople_(674%E2%80%93678) "Siege of Constantinople (674–678)") (674–678), [lost the Balkans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ongal "Battle of Ongal") (681) to the [First Bulgarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire "First Bulgarian Empire"), [lost all of North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_the_Maghreb "Muslim conquest of the Maghreb") (by 709) to the Umayyads, and then survived a [*2nd* siege by the Umayyads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Constantinople_(717%E2%80%93718) "Siege of Constantinople (717–718)") (717 to 718). From the 650s onward, the Mediterranean became a [battleground between Arab and Byzantine naval forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_conquests#Arab–Byzantine_naval_warfare "Early Muslim conquests"), with both sides launching raids, counter raids and invasions against islands and coastal settlements and engaging in a naval arms race. In the latter part of the [Arab–Byzantine wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab%E2%80%93Byzantine_wars "Arab–Byzantine wars"), the [Republic of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice "Republic of Venice") emerged as an important ally of the Byzantines.

* The [Carolingian minuscule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_minuscule "Carolingian minuscule") alphabet was a significantly more legible calligraphic standard than previous scripts.
* [Early Cyrillic alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Cyrillic_alphabet "Early Cyrillic alphabet")

The Latin Church [pivoted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Succession_of_the_Roman_Empire#Papal_pivot "Succession of the Roman Empire") from the Byzantine Papacy to the [Frankish Papacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankish_Papacy "Frankish Papacy") (756–857) when [Pope Stephen II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Stephen_II "Pope Stephen II") (r. 752–757) became the [first bishop of Rome to ever cross the Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_travel#France "Papal travel") and appealed in person to [Frankish leadership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepin_the_Short#Expansion_of_the_Frankish_realm "Pepin the Short") for military aid against the [Lombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Lombards#Aistulf "Kingdom of the Lombards"), resulting in the [creation of the Papal States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donation_of_Pepin "Donation of Pepin") ruled by the bishops of Rome for the next 1000 years. In 800, [Pope Leo III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_III "Pope Leo III") (r. 795–816) crowned [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne "Charlemagne") (r. 768–814), the King of the Franks, as the [first western "Roman" emperor in over 300 years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne#Coronation "Charlemagne"), a direct provocation to the Byzantines who [considered themselves to be the Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantines#Self-perception "Byzantines"). After the [Christianization of the First Bulgarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization_of_Bulgaria "Christianization of Bulgaria") (c. 864) and with [key support from Byzantine scholars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyril_and_Methodius#Invention_of_the_Glagolitic_and_Cyrillic_alphabets "Cyril and Methodius"), the [early Cyrillic alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Cyrillic_alphabet "Early Cyrillic alphabet"), purpose-built for [Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs "Slavs") phonology, was [developed there](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preslav_Literary_School#History "Preslav Literary School"). An increasing rivalry between the [Greek East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_East_and_Latin_West "Greek East and Latin West") and [Latin West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_West "Latin West") was then expressed in an intense [missionary competition among the Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization_of_the_Slavs "Christianization of the Slavs") through the 9th and 10th centuries, in which [Cyrillic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrillic_script "Cyrillic script") played an important role. While [Moravia (c. 863)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization_of_Moravia "Christianization of Moravia"), [Bohemia (c. 884)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization_of_Bohemia "Christianization of Bohemia"), [Croatia (by 900)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Croatia#Conversion_of_the_Croats "Catholic Church in Croatia"), and [Poland (966)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization_of_Poland "Christianization of Poland") were drawn into the Latin West, the [Serbs (c. 870)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Orthodox_Church#Christianization_of_Serbs "Serbian Orthodox Church") and [Kievan Rus' (988)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization_of_Kievan_Rus%27 "Christianization of Kievan Rus'") embraced Cyrillic and linked themselves with the Greek East.

From the 8th until the 11th centuries, [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_conquests "Early Muslim conquests"), [Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_expansion "Viking expansion") and [Magyars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_invasions_of_Europe "Hungarian invasions of Europe") raided and invaded from the south, north, and east. [Charlemagne's empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_Empire "Carolingian Empire") was [partitioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Verdun "Treaty of Verdun") into [West Francia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Francia "West Francia") and [East Francia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Francia "East Francia") in 843. In West Francia, Vikings [occupied and plundered Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Paris_(845) "Siege of Paris (845)") in 845. Arab raiders [plundered the outskirts of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_raid_against_Rome "Arab raid against Rome") in 846. Vikings sacked Pisa in 860, [seized York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_York_(867) "Battle of York (867)") in 867 and [besieged Paris for over a year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Paris_(885%E2%80%93886) "Siege of Paris (885–886)") (885–886). The Muslim [Aghlabids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aghlabids "Aghlabids") completed their [conquest of Sicily by 902](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Sicily "Muslim conquest of Sicily"). In East Francia, Magyar horse archers [annihilated almost the entire secular and ecclesiastical leadership of southeastern Francia in a single battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Pressburg "Battle of Pressburg") in 907, clearing the way for the Magyars to raid unchecked across Western Europe for decades. In 911, however, [Robert I of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_I_of_France "Robert I of France") defeated an [attacking Viking force at Chartres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Chartres_(911) "Siege of Chartres (911)"). The [King of West Francia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_the_Simple "Charles the Simple") then signed a [treaty with the defeated Vikings that established the Duchy of Normandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Saint-Clair-sur-Epte "Treaty of Saint-Clair-sur-Epte") at the [mouth of the Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine#History "Seine"), turning former raiders into a defensive bulwark and helping to set in motion West Francia’s evolution into the [Kingdom of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_France "Kingdom of France"). East Francia also showed its resilience by winning decisive victories against the Magyars at [Riade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Riade "Battle of Riade") (933) and [Lechfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lechfeld "Battle of Lechfeld") (955) that directly led to it evolving into the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire#Formation_of_the_Holy_Roman_Empire "Holy Roman Empire"). By the turn of the millennium, the Magyars had ended their Western European raids, settled into a sedentary lifestyle, [converted to Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Christianity_in_Hungary#High_Middle_Ages "History of Christianity in Hungary"), and established the [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary_(1000%E2%80%931301) "Kingdom of Hungary (1000–1301)").

### High Middle Ages

In 1046 the Holy Roman Emperor [Henry III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_III,_Holy_Roman_Emperor "Henry III, Holy Roman Emperor") (r. 1046–1056) deposed [Pope Gregory VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VI "Pope Gregory VI") (r. 1045–1046) – who had purchased his office – along with two other claimants, beginning the process of freeing the leadership of the Latin Church from the [control of the local Roman nobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_papacy#Influence_of_powerful_Roman_families_(904–1048) "History of the papacy"). [Pope Leo IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_IX "Pope Leo IX") (r. 1049–1054), a German, recruited an international circle of radical reformers who helped him create an "itinerant papacy" that moved from city to city to hold church councils, consecrate cathedrals, and negotiate with secular rulers. After centuries of growing rivalry, Leo's dynamic papacy also precipitated the [East–West Schism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East%E2%80%93West_Schism "East–West Schism") (also "Great Schism") that split the Latin Church from the [Eastern Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church "Eastern Orthodox Church") of the Byzantine Empire in 1054. One of Leo's [reformist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_II#Election_reform "Pope Nicholas II") successors, [Pope Nicholas II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_II "Pope Nicholas II") (r. 1059–1061), and his influential advisor Cardinal Hildebrand of Sovana, issued the papal bull *[In nomine Domini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_nomine_Domini "In nomine Domini")* (1059), which stripped the Holy Roman Emperor of his traditional role in appointing popes and established the [College of Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Cardinals "College of Cardinals") as the sole electors. After Cardinal Hildebrand was himself [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1073_papal_election "1073 papal election") [Pope Gregory VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII "Pope Gregory VII") (r. 1073–1085), he worked both to [improve the moral integrity and independence of Church clergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_Reform "Gregorian Reform") and to establish the total freedom of the Church from secular interference (*"[libertas ecclesiae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertas_ecclesiae "Libertas ecclesiae")"*). By challenging the Holy Roman Emperor [Henry IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor "Henry IV, Holy Roman Emperor")'s (r. 1084–1105) right to appoint church officials, Gregory began the [Investiture Controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investiture_Controversy "Investiture Controversy"), a struggle for supremacy between the Church and the secular monarchies of Europe.

In 1095 the Byzantine emperor requested military support in his [Empire's conflict with the Seljuk Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine%E2%80%93Seljuk_wars "Byzantine–Seljuk wars"). In response, [Pope Urban II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Urban_II "Pope Urban II") (r. 1088–1099) spread a [message of holy war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades#Council_of_Clermont_and_its_aftermath "Crusades") across Western Europe. The ensuing [Crusades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades "Crusades") led to the foundation of [small Catholic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusader_states "Crusader states") in the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant "Levant"), which lasted for two centuries until [their final outpost fell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Acre_(1291) "Siege of Acre (1291)") to the [Mamluk Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Sultanate "Mamluk Sultanate"). Even as the later Crusades were failing, however, [Iberian Christian attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconquista#Kingdom_of_Castile_(1037–1230) "Reconquista") on Muslim-ruled [al-Andalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus "Al-Andalus") were reversing the 8th century [Muslim conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_the_Iberian_Peninsula "Muslim conquest of the Iberian Peninsula") of the [Visigothic Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigothic_Kingdom "Visigothic Kingdom"). Further expanding the [Latin West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_West "Latin West"), Scandinavians (Denmark, Norway and Sweden)  [converted to Latin Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization_of_Scandinavia "Christianization of Scandinavia").

* [Notre-Dame de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre-Dame_de_Paris "Notre-Dame de Paris"), constructed between 1163 and 1260. [Romanesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_architecture "Romanesque architecture") and [Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture "Gothic architecture") architecture flourished during the High Middle Ages.
* Member cities of the [Lombard League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_League "Lombard League").
* Holy Roman Emperor [Frederick Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Barbarossa "Frederick Barbarossa") kneels before [Pope Alexander III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Alexander_III "Pope Alexander III") before signing the [Treaty of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Venice "Treaty of Venice") (1177), fresco in [Palazzo Pubblico, Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Pubblico,_Siena "Palazzo Pubblico, Siena").

Along with the existing [Republic of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice "Republic of Venice"), new Italian [city-state maritime republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_republics "Maritime republics") like [Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa "Republic of Genoa") and [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Pisa "Republic of Pisa") (from c. 1000), and [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Florence "Republic of Florence") (from 1115), thrived on expanding trade. [Pope Alexander III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Alexander_III "Pope Alexander III") (r. 1159–1181) strongly backed the [1167–1168 alliance of these northern Italian cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_League "Lombard League"), which defeated the Holy Roman Empire at the [Battle of Legnano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Legnano "Battle of Legnano") (1176) and won their effective independence from the Empire in the [Peace of Constance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Constance "Peace of Constance") (1183). After capturing the [son of the Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzo_of_Sardinia "Enzo of Sardinia") at the [Battle of Fossalta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Fossalta "Battle of Fossalta") (1249), [Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna "Bologna")’s *[Liber Paradisus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liber_Paradisus "Liber Paradisus")* purchased the freedom of nearly 6,000 serfs in 1256, the first abolition of serfdom in Europe. Florence [passed laws in the 1290s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinances_of_Justice "Ordinances of Justice") to align itself [with the Catholic Church against the Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guelphs_and_Ghibellines "Guelphs and Ghibellines") and ensure that the [city's merchant and artisan guilds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilds_of_Florence "Guilds of Florence") controlled the government. Northern Italian cities led a [commercial revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_revolution#Maritime_republics_and_communes "Commercial revolution") and [made Italy the most productive region of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Maddison_GDP_per_capita_1500-1950.svg "File:Maddison GDP per capita 1500-1950.svg") in the Middle Ages.

As medieval Europe became perhaps the first society in human history to build an economy on nonhuman power, land under cultivation and the number of towns grew together in northern and central Europe. Central Europe alone saw some three thousand new towns founded between the eleventh and early fourteenth centuries. By 1250, robust population increase had brought the economy to levels it would not see again in some areas until the 19th century. Beginning in the 13th century, the [Hanseatic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League "Hanseatic League"), a commercial and defensive network of [merchant guilds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchant_guilds "Merchant guilds") and [market towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_town "Market town") in [Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Europe "Central Europe") and [Northern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Europe "Northern Europe"), grew to dominate [maritime trade in the Baltic and North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_maritime_trade_(c._1400%E2%80%931800)#Era_of_the_German_Hanse "Baltic maritime trade (c. 1400–1800)") seas in the following century. Northern European glassworks grew by producing less expensive [window glass from wood ashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_glass "Forest glass") while [Venetian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_glass#Late_Antiquity_and_Middle_Ages "Venetian glass") artisans specialized in the more expensive clear [soda-lime glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soda-lime_glass "Soda-lime glass") for luxury products that later enabled the 13th century invention of [spectacles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasses#Invention "Glasses"). Medieval engineers produced the first [weight-driven mechanical clocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clock#Fully_mechanical "Clock") around the same time and every city in Europe soon possessed at least one. Historical demographers estimate that [sustained growth during the High Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_demography#High_Middle_Ages "Medieval demography") – enabled by a thriving north-south "bipolar urban network" and major breakthroughs in technology - pushed Europe's population beyond the peak levels reached during the era of the Roman Empire.

In northern Italy at the turn of the 13th century, Leonardo da Pisa (c. 1170-c. 1240-50) - commonly known as [Fibonacci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci "Fibonacci") - popularized [Arabic numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_numerals "Arabic numerals") and [base-10 positional notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu%E2%80%93Arabic_numeral_system "Hindu–Arabic numeral system"), while the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna "University of Bologna"), the [oldest university currently in continuous operation in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oldest_universities_in_continuous_operation#Medieval_origins "List of oldest universities in continuous operation"), was being established. Two more universities were established in the early 13th century in the Kingdom of France (the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris "University of Paris")) and [Kingdom of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_England "Kingdom of England") (the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford")). Europe's universities became a foundation for many of its future achievements, especially in science. Initially, they were key participants in the [rediscovery of the works of Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_translations_of_the_12th_century "Latin translations of the 12th century"), which led the Italian priest [Thomas Aquinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Aquinas "Thomas Aquinas") (1225–1274) and other thinkers to develop the philosophy of [scholasticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholasticism "Scholasticism").

After [conquering the fractured principalities of the former Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Kievan_Rus%27 "Mongol invasion of Kievan Rus'") and the [Kipchak-Cuman Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kipchak-Cuman_Confederation "Kipchak-Cuman Confederation"), Mongol armies [invaded Central Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Europe#Invasion_of_Central_Europe "Mongol invasion of Europe") in 1241, defeating a [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Poland "Kingdom of Poland")-[Moravian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margraviate_of_Moravia "Margraviate of Moravia") force at [Legnica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Legnica "Battle of Legnica") and crushing the [Hungarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary "Kingdom of Hungary") at [Mohi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mohi "Battle of Mohi") before [withdrawing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Europe#End_of_the_Mongol_advance "Mongol invasion of Europe"). The adjacent [Russian principalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appanage_Russia "Appanage Russia") had a [vassalage relationship with Mongolian khanates for the next two centuries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appanage_Russia#13th–15th_centuries "Appanage Russia").

### Late Middle Ages

Centuries of European prosperity and growth unraveled during the [crisis of the late Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crisis_of_the_late_Middle_Ages "Crisis of the late Middle Ages"). The [Great Famine of 1315–1317](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Famine_of_1315%E2%80%931317 "Great Famine of 1315–1317") caused extreme levels of crime, disease, mass death, and even [cannibalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_cannibalism "Human cannibalism") and [infanticide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infanticide "Infanticide"). The [Black Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Death "Black Death") (1346–1353) killed people in a matter of days, reducing the population of some areas by half as many survivors fled:

:   The Black Death touched every aspect of life, hastening a process of social, economic, and cultural transformation already underway.... Fields were abandoned, workplaces stood idle, international trade was suspended. Traditional bonds of kinship, village, and even religion were broken amid the horrors of death, flight, and failed expectations. "People cared no more for dead men than we care for dead goats," wrote one survivor.

More than a [century of intermittent warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Years%27_War "Hundred Years' War") and popular uprisings, including the [Jacquerie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacquerie "Jacquerie") (1358) and the [Peasants' Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peasants%27_Revolt "Peasants' Revolt") (1381), staggered France and England, while the prestige and unity of the Catholic Church suffered long-term damage from the [Avignon Papacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avignon_Papacy "Avignon Papacy") (1309–1376) and the [Western Schism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Schism "Western Schism") (1378–1417).

Despite these crises, a great effort to rediscover and revive the cultural achievements of [classical antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_antiquity "Classical antiquity") began in 14th-century Florence, spread [across Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Renaissance "Italian Renaissance") and Europe, and transformed [art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_art "Renaissance art"), [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_architecture "Renaissance architecture"), and [literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_literature "Renaissance literature"). [Renaissance humanists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_humanism "Renaissance humanism"), including several 15th- and 16th-century popes, perceived their repossession of a lost past as a *[rebirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance "Renaissance")* of civilization.

The rising [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire "Ottoman Empire") conquered the [Second Bulgarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Bulgarian_Empire "Second Bulgarian Empire") by the end of the 14th century. The Byzantine Empire - the last remnant of the Roman Empire - [fell in 1453](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Constantinople "Fall of Constantinople") when the Ottomans' [massive cannons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Constantinople#Ottoman_dispositions_and_strategies "Fall of Constantinople") finally breached the [walls of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walls_of_Constantinople#Theodosian_walls "Walls of Constantinople"), ending eleven centuries of Byzantine civilization in Eastern Europe.

## Early modern Europe

Early modern Europe spans the centuries between the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages "Middle Ages") and the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution "Industrial Revolution"). The period includes the [rapid spread of mechanical movable-type printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing_press#Mass_production_and_spread_in_Europe "Printing press"), the [Age of Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery "Age of Discovery"), the Protestant [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation "Reformation"), the [European colonisation of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonisation_of_the_Americas "European colonisation of the Americas"), the [Scientific Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_Revolution "Scientific Revolution"), the disastrous [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years%27_War "Thirty Years' War") and the emergence of the [five great European powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concert_of_Europe "Concert of Europe").

### Printing

From a single location in the [Free Imperial City of Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_City_of_Mainz "Free City of Mainz") in the 1450s, [movable type printing had spread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing_press#Mass_production_and_spread_in_Europe "Printing press") to about 270 cities across Europe and produced more than 20 million volumes by the end of the 15th century. Just 25 miles away, the [Frankfurt Book Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Book_Fair#History "Frankfurt Book Fair") was established by 1462 and remained the primary marketplace for printers and [publishers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publishing#Europe "Publishing") and the center of Europe's book trade for the next two centuries. By the 16th century, the [manuscript culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuscript_culture "Manuscript culture") of the Middle Ages, where [facts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fact "Fact") were few and far between, had begun to give way to a [printing culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing_culture "Printing culture") where living scholars could compare their own observations with those of other (living) scholars, transforming how [knowledge was created, preserved and disseminated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing_press#Science "Printing press").

### Exploration, epidemics and colonies

* [Map of Juan de la Cosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Map_of_Juan_de_la_Cosa "Map of Juan de la Cosa") (1500), includes Europe, Africa, parts of Asia and the earliest known representation of the New World.
* *[Mare clausum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mare_clausum "Mare clausum")* ('closed sea') claims during the [Age of Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery "Age of Discovery").
* The title page of [Francis Bacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Bacon "Francis Bacon")'s *[Novum Organum Scientiarum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novum_Organum "Novum Organum")* (1620) depicts a [galleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galleon "Galleon") passing between the mythical [Pillars of Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillars_of_Hercules "Pillars of Hercules").

Following explorations of the coast of Africa using [new ship technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravel "Caravel") led by the [Kingdom of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Portugal "Kingdom of Portugal")’s [Prince Henry the Navigator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Henry_the_Navigator "Prince Henry the Navigator") (1394–1460) seeking an eastern [maritime route to India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_discovery_of_the_sea_route_to_India "Portuguese discovery of the sea route to India") and [Spanish-funded voyages to the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyages_of_Christopher_Columbus "Voyages of Christopher Columbus"), the two nations signed the [Treaty of Tordesillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Tordesillas "Treaty of Tordesillas") (1494), granting Africa and the East to Portugal while Spain claimed the Atlantic and everything beyond it. The Portuguese explorer [Vasco da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_da_Gama "Vasco da Gama") (c. 1460s-1524) completed the [first recorded trip directly from Europe to the Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_discovery_of_the_sea_route_to_India#The_voyage "Portuguese discovery of the sea route to India") (1497–1499). The Florentine [Amerigo Vespucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerigo_Vespucci "Amerigo Vespucci")'s [Mundus Novus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World#Mundus_Novus "New World") letter, published in 1503 and repeatedly reprinted, provided the first explicit articulation in print that the lands discovered by European navigators to the West were not the edges of Asia, but rather an [entirely different continent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americas "Americas") (a "New World"). Between 1519 and 1522, the [Magellan–Elcano expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magellan_expedition "Magellan expedition") achieved the first circumnavigation of Earth in history, including the first crossing of the Pacific by a European expedition, revealing the vast scale of that ocean. The encounter with continents that were completely unknown to the [ancients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_antiquity "Classical antiquity") had a profound impact on European intellectual life and specifically undermined the authority of [Claudius Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudius_Ptolemy "Claudius Ptolemy"), the 2nd-century [Alexandrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria#Ancient_era "Alexandria") scholar whose [geographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_(Ptolemy) "Geography (Ptolemy)") and [astronomical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geocentrism#Ptolemaic_model "Geocentrism") models had long been considered infallible.

Portugal forged the [first global empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire "Portuguese Empire"), but by the late 16th century, the [Hispanic Monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_Monarchy_(political_entity) "Hispanic Monarchy (political entity)") (incorporating Portugal from 1580 to 1640) had become the world's largest empire, with silver from its American colonies accounting for at least twenty percent of its total revenue. Epidemics of [smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_smallpox#Epidemics_in_the_Americas "History of smallpox") and other Eurasian diseases [swept the Americas subsequent to European contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_disease_and_epidemics "Native American disease and epidemics"), killing between 10 million and 100 million people, up to 95% of the indigenous population of the Americas. While Spain dominated the Americas and controlled the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_(1565%E2%80%931898) "History of the Philippines (1565–1898)"), in the 17th and 18th centuries Spanish dominance was challenged by the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_colonization_of_the_Americas "Dutch colonization of the Americas"), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonisation_of_the_Americas "French colonisation of the Americas") and [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_colonisation_of_the_Americas "British colonisation of the Americas"). With a [plantation economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Domingue#Plantation_economy "Saint-Domingue") fueled by [slave labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_Haiti "Slavery in Haiti"), Saint-Domingue – the "[Pearl of the Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Haiti#Pearl_of_the_Antilles_(1711–1789) "History of Haiti")" – was the richest colony in the 18th-century [French empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire "French colonial empire"). Europe's colonial plantation economies were sustained by the [Atlantic slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_slave_trade "Atlantic slave trade"), which forcibly transported 12 million enslaved Africans to the Americas between the 16th and 19th centuries.

The diffusion of [New World crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World_crops "New World crops") during the [Columbian exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbian_exchange "Columbian exchange") reshaped Europe. [Maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize#Columbian_exchange "Maize") arrived first and spread swiftly through the Mediterranean and Balkans in the 16th century while the [potato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potato#Spread "Potato") spread far more slowly in northern Europe. The higher and more reliable caloric yield per acre of both crops altered the [peasant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peasant#Medieval_European_peasants "Peasant") diet and contributed to the retreat of recurrent famine and to European [population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Europe#Past_populations_of_Europe_in_modern_national_borders,_AD_1–2020 "Demographics of Europe") and urbanization.

### Reformation

Sparked by the German priest [Martin Luther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther "Martin Luther")’s (1483–1546) *[Ninety-five Theses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninety-five_Theses "Ninety-five Theses")* (1517) and spread by the new [movable type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movable_type "Movable type") [printing press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing_press "Printing press") in [vernacular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernacular "Vernacular") languages (i.e. not Latin), the [Protestant Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformation "Protestant Reformation") was a religious and political challenge to the Catholic Church. In 1527, mutinous troops of the [Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Habsburg "House of Habsburg") Holy Roman Emperor [Charles V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor "Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor") (r. 1519-1556) – many of them fervent Protestant converts – [sacked Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sack_of_Rome_(1527) "Sack of Rome (1527)"), imprisoned [Pope Clement VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Clement_VII "Pope Clement VII") (r. 1523–1534), humiliated the Church, and decimated Rome’s population, which plummeted from over 55,000 to barely 10,000 through a combination of massacre, starvation, and plague. As the movement branched into [Lutheranism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheranism "Lutheranism"), [Calvinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvinism "Calvinism"), and [Anglicanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism "Anglicanism"), it triggered the Catholic [Counter-Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-Reformation "Counter-Reformation"), reshaped Europe’s political landscape and exploded into nearly [two centuries of religious wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_wars_of_religion "European wars of religion"). States were also torn apart internally by religious strife fostered by their external enemies, a fate suffered by France [between 1562 and 1598](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Wars_of_Religion "French Wars of Religion") and the [Habsburg Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_Netherlands "Habsburg Netherlands") from 1568 to 1648 (the "[Eighty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighty_Years%27_War "Eighty Years' War")").

* The [Second Defenestration of Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Defenestration_of_Prague "Second Defenestration of Prague") (1618) began the [Bohemian Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemian_Revolt "Bohemian Revolt") and the Thirty Years’ War (contemporary woodcut).
* A peasant begs for mercy in front of his burning farm. By the 1630s, being caught in the open by soldiers from either side was "tantamount to a death sentence".
* Europe after the [Peace of Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Westphalia "Peace of Westphalia") in 1648.

During the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years%27_War "Thirty Years' War") (1618–1648), entire regions of the German states and, to a lesser extent, the [Low Countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Countries "Low Countries"), the [Crown of Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Bohemia "Crown of Bohemia") and northern parts of Italy were scavenged bare by foraging armies, causing widespread famine, while bankrupting many of the states involved. Between one-fourth and one-third of the German population perished from direct military causes or from disease and starvation as [social order disintegrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years%27_War#Social_and_cultural_impact "Thirty Years' War"). Between 4 and 8 million people died due to the war, the vast majority being civilians, making it the most devastating European conflict until the 20th century. The [Peace of Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Westphalia "Peace of Westphalia") (1648) ending the war guaranteed the right to practice [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism "Catholicism"), [Lutheranism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheranism "Lutheranism") and [Calvinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvinism "Calvinism") as well as the independence of the [religiously tolerant Dutch Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic#Religious_tolerance_and_refugees "Dutch Republic"), which established a [safe haven for European Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Europe#Amsterdam_as_the_"Dutch_Jerusalem" "History of the Jews in Europe").

### Scientific Revolution

* [Galileo Galilei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo_Galilei "Galileo Galilei") reported in the *[Starry Messenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starry_Messenger "Starry Messenger")* (1610) that he saw at least ten times more stars through a telescope than are visible to unaided vision.
* [Newton's cannonball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton%27s_cannonball "Newton's cannonball"): Whether a cannonball is a terrestrial or celestial object depends only on how fast it is going, from *[A Treatise of the System of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophi%C3%A6_Naturalis_Principia_Mathematica#Book_3,_De_mundi_systemate "Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica")* (1728).

Italian [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer "Astronomer") [Galileo Galilei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo_Galilei "Galileo Galilei")’s (1564–1642) [early 17th-century telescopic observations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo_Galilei#Phases_of_Venus "Galileo Galilei") began the transformation of what had been a narrowly technical revision of [classical astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geocentrism#Ptolemaic_model "Geocentrism") into an increasingly aggressive challenge to [traditional cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_spheres#Philosophical_and_theological_discussions "Celestial spheres") and its long-standing synthesis with Christian theology. English [mathematical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_physicist "Mathematical physicist") [Isaac Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Newton "Isaac Newton")’s (1643–1727) *[Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophi%C3%A6_Naturalis_Principia_Mathematica "Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica")* (1687) dissolved the [ancient boundary between the celestial and the terrestrial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotelian_physics#Elements_and_spheres "Aristotelian physics") by showing how a single mathematical law – the [law of universal gravitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton%27s_law_of_universal_gravitation "Newton's law of universal gravitation") – ruled both realms, the first [great unification of physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_theories_in_physics#Unification_of_gravity_on_Earth_with_astronomical_behaviors "Unification of theories in physics").

The upheaval of the Scientific Revolution ended the medieval view of [natural philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_philosophy "Natural philosophy") as the servant (or "handmaiden") of theology. As natural philosophy continued to grow in power, self-confidence and independence during the 17th century, the intellectual attitude of educated people shifted from *[fides quaerens intellectum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fides_quaerens_intellectum "Fides quaerens intellectum")* toward a mode of understanding increasingly detached from religion. The "New Science" that emerged by the end of the century broke sharply with the natural philosophy that had preceded it, departed from previous Greek conceptions and traditions, was more mechanistic in its worldview and more integrated with mathematics, and was obsessed with the acquisition and interpretation of new evidence.

### Great powers

During the 1640s and 1650s, England endured [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Civil_War "English Civil War") (1642–1651), the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire "Spanish Empire") fractured from simultaneous revolts in [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Restoration_War "Portuguese Restoration War") (1640–1668) and [Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reapers%27_War "Reapers' War") (1640–1659), and the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth "Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth") faced near-extinction from a [Cossack rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmelnytsky_Uprising "Khmelnytsky Uprising") (1648–1657), the [Polish–Russian War (1654–1667)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Russian_War_(1654%E2%80%931667) "Polish–Russian War (1654–1667)") and [Swedish invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_War_of_1655%E2%80%931660 "Northern War of 1655–1660") (1655–1660). The failure of the Ottoman Empire's [second and last attempt to capture the Habsburg capital of Vienna (1683)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Vienna "Battle of Vienna") resulted in the [loss of Hungary to the Habsburgs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Karlowitz "Treaty of Karlowitz") (1699) and marked the end of [Ottoman expansion into Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_wars_in_Europe "Ottoman wars in Europe").

Largely spared from the devastation of the Thirty Years' War, [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_France "Kingdom of France") was the [leading European power at the end of the 17th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIV#France_as_the_pivot_of_warfare "Louis XIV"). England, however, had [surpassed Italy to have the most productive economy in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Maddison_GDP_per_capita_1500-1950.svg "File:Maddison GDP per capita 1500-1950.svg") and the [Glorious Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glorious_Revolution "Glorious Revolution") (1688) - which subjected the English crown to [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_England "Parliament of England") – sparked a [Financial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Revolution "Financial Revolution") that made the country into a more creditworthy borrower than its absolutist rivals on the Continent whose unchecked monarchs were considered poorer credit risks by lenders. These economic and financial advantages over France were tested in the [War of the Spanish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession "War of the Spanish Succession") (1701–1714), in which a pan-European coalition united to oppose France, Britain won the strategic ports of [Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar#Modern_era "Gibraltar") and [Minorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menorca#British_rule "Menorca"), and the country was set on a course towards "maritime, commercial, and financial supremacy."

The decline of the previously powerful Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, Ottoman Empire and [Kingdom of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sweden "Kingdom of Sweden") was mirrored by the ascent of three centralized, [absolutist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutism_(European_history) "Absolutism (European history)") monarchies: the [Habsburg monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy "Habsburg monarchy") ("Austria"), the [Tsardom of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsardom_of_Russia "Tsardom of Russia") and [Brandenburg–Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg%E2%80%93Prussia "Brandenburg–Prussia"). Following the [administrative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intendant_(Kingdom_of_France) "Intendant (Kingdom of France)") and military models of France's [Louis XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIV "Louis XIV") the "Sun King" (r. 1643–1715), these states [built the strong armies and large bureaucracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Revolution "Military Revolution") that the [escalating costs of gunpowder warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_warfare#Nature_of_war "Early modern warfare") demanded and expanded at the expense of their neighbors. Sweden [yielded territory to Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Nystad "Treaty of Nystad") and the Tsar of Russia became *Emperor* [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great "Peter the Great") (r. Russia 1682–1725) of the Russian Empire in 1721 while [Catherine the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_the_Great "Catherine the Great")'s (r. 1762–1796) reforms caused the Empire to develop into a major European power. [Frederick the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_the_Great "Frederick the Great") (r. Prussia 1740–1786) [improved the Prussian army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Army#Frederick_the_Great "Prussian Army") and [took Silesia from Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesian_Wars "Silesian Wars") (1740–1742). Finally, the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth [was carved up between the three rising powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitions_of_Poland "Partitions of Poland") (1772–1795).

The five great European powers that emerged in the 18th century – Austria, France, Prussia, Russia, and the United Kingdom – formed the [Concert of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concert_of_Europe "Concert of Europe") that dominated European politics in the 19th century and up to World War I.

### Enlightenment

The [Age of Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment "Age of Enlightenment") (also "Age of Reason" or simply "the Enlightenment") was a period of [intellectual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_movement "Intellectual movement") and [cultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_movement "Cultural movement") flourishing that emerged in the late 17th century in [Western Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Europe "Western Europe") and reached its peak in the 18th century as its ideas spread more widely across Europe. Characterized by an emphasis on [reason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reason "Reason"), [empirical evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empirical_evidence "Empirical evidence"), and the [scientific method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_method "Scientific method"), the Enlightenment promoted ideals of [individual liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_liberty "Individual liberty"), [religious tolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_tolerance "Religious tolerance"), [progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress "Progress"), and [natural rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_law "Natural law"). Its thinkers advocated for [constitutional government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_government "Constitutional government"), the [separation of church and state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_church_and_state "Separation of church and state"), and the application of rational principles to social and political reform. The movement was characterized by the widespread circulation of ideas through new institutions: [scientific academies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy "Academy"), [literary salons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salon_(gathering) "Salon (gathering)"), [coffeehouses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffeehouse "Coffeehouse"), [Masonic lodges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masonic_lodge "Masonic lodge"), and an expanding [print culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Print_culture "Print culture") of [books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book "Book"), [journals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_journal "Academic journal"), and [pamphlets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamphlet "Pamphlet"). The ideas of the Enlightenment undermined the authority of the [monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy "Monarchy") and religious officials and paved the way for the political [revolutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution "Revolution") of the 18th and 19th centuries. A variety of 19th-century movements, including [liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism "Liberalism"), [socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism "Socialism"), and [neoclassicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassicism "Neoclassicism"), trace their intellectual heritage to the Enlightenment.

## Industrial Revolution

The [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution "Industrial Revolution") was the most significant transition in human civilization since the [Agricultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Agricultural_Revolution "First Agricultural Revolution"). It was the first breakthrough from an agrarian, handicraft economy to one dominated by industry and machine manufacture, initiating unprecedented, cumulative and self-sustaining advances in technology and [economic productivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Maddison_GDP_per_capita_1500-1950.svg "File:Maddison GDP per capita 1500-1950.svg") during which Western Europe definitively emerged as the world's most powerful civilization, [eclipsing all of its Eurasian rivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Divergence "Great Divergence").

Originating in Britain in the mid-18th century with the [mechanisation of textile manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile_manufacture_during_the_British_Industrial_Revolution "Textile manufacture during the British Industrial Revolution"), advancements in iron-making and increased use of coal, it subsequently spread to Western Europe. Technological advancements, most notably the steam engine, accelerated and sustained the industrialisation process. Trade expansion was enabled by the introduction of [canals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal "Canal"), improved roads, and railways. The introduction of [steam power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_engine "Steam engine") (fuelled primarily by coal) and powered machinery (mainly in [textile manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile_manufacturing "Textile manufacturing")) underpinned the dramatic increases in production capacity.

The development of all-metal [machine tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_tool "Machine tool") in the first two decades of the 19th century facilitated the manufacture of more production machines for manufacturing in other industries. The effects spread throughout Western Europe and North America during the 19th century, eventually affecting most of the world.

The Industrial Revolution was the first time population and per-person income rose simultaneously. Europe's population increased from 100 million in 1700 to 400 million by 1900.

## Long 19th century

During the "[long 19th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_nineteenth_century "Long nineteenth century")" (1789–1914), Europe was transformed by the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution "Industrial Revolution") and by the political upheavals of the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution "French Revolution"), [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars "Napoleonic Wars"), the [rise of nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rise_of_nationalism_in_Europe "Rise of nationalism in Europe"), the [revolutions of 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848 "Revolutions of 1848") and [German unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Germany "Unification of Germany").

### French Revolution

The [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution "French Revolution") was a decade of political and social upheaval in France and Europe at the end of the 18th century. After France's [costly intervention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_in_the_American_Revolutionary_War#Financial_aspects "France in the American Revolutionary War") in the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War "American Revolutionary War") and [repeated failed attempts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XVI#Economic_policies "Louis XVI"), in 1789 [King Louis XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Louis_XVI "King Louis XVI") (r. 1774–1792) summoned the [Estates-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estates_General_of_1789 "Estates General of 1789") – a representative body comprising the medieval "estates" of [clergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estates_of_the_realm#First_Estate "Estates of the realm"), [nobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estates_of_the_realm#Second_Estate "Estates of the realm") and [commoners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estates_of_the_realm#Third_Estate "Estates of the realm") – to help solve France's fiscal crisis. The Third Estate, joined by some members of the other two, declared itself a [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_(French_Revolution) "National Assembly (French Revolution)"). After the [storming of the Bastille on 14 July](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storming_of_the_Bastille "Storming of the Bastille"), the Assembly passed the [Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_the_Rights_of_Man_and_of_the_Citizen "Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen"), [abolished feudalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolition_of_feudalism_in_France "Abolition of feudalism in France"), [sought complete control over the Catholic Church in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Constitution_of_the_Clergy "Civil Constitution of the Clergy"), [banned guilds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Chapelier_Law_1791 "Le Chapelier Law 1791") and approved France's [first written constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Constitution_of_1791 "French Constitution of 1791"), briefly turning France into a [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_France_(1791%E2%80%9392) "Kingdom of France (1791–92)"). The [French Revolutionary Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolutionary_Army "French Revolutionary Army")'s unexpected [victory over an Austro-Prussian invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Valmy "Battle of Valmy") emboldened the newly assembled [National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Convention "National Convention") to [abolish the monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_the_abolition_of_the_monarchy "Proclamation of the abolition of the monarchy") and make France a [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Republic "First French Republic") in 1792.

Following the French general [Napoleon Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_Bonaparte "Napoleon Bonaparte")’s [1796 invasion of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenotte_campaign "Montenotte campaign") and the [1798 French invasion of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_invasion_of_Switzerland "French invasion of Switzerland"), a network of [satellite states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_republic "Sister republic") replaced previous regimes. In 1799 during the [War of the Second Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Second_Coalition "War of the Second Coalition"), Napoleon abandoned a [French army in Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arm%C3%A9e_d%27Orient_(1798)_order_of_battle "Armée d'Orient (1798) order of battle"), returned to Paris, [overthrew the leadership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18_Brumaire "18 Brumaire") of the Republic and then [crowned himself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation_of_Napoleon#Ceremony "Coronation of Napoleon") [Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire "First French Empire") in 1804. Despite crushing victories at [Austerlitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Austerlitz "Battle of Austerlitz") (1805) and [Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Jena%E2%80%93Auerstedt "Battle of Jena–Auerstedt") (1806) ending both the [third](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Third_Coalition "War of the Third Coalition") and [fourth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Fourth_Coalition "War of the Fourth Coalition") coalitions, however, his empire later collapsed following an [invasion of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_invasion_of_Russia "French invasion of Russia"), a disastrous retreat from [Moscow (1812)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_of_Moscow_(1812) "Fire of Moscow (1812)") and a decisive defeat at [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Leipzig "Battle of Leipzig") (1813).

French politics were permanently polarized after the Revolution – new names were given, ["left" and "right"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left%E2%80%93right_political_spectrum#Origins_in_the_French_Revolution "Left–right political spectrum"), for the supporters and opponents of the principles of the Revolution. French-controlled areas abolished feudal privileges and [seigneurial dues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manorialism "Manorialism"), liberalised [property laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_law "Property law"), ended the [guilds of merchants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilds_of_merchants "Guilds of merchants") and [craftsmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craft_guilds "Craft guilds") to facilitate [entrepreneurship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entrepreneurship "Entrepreneurship"), reduced trade barriers, legalised divorce, closed [Jewish ghettos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_ghettos_in_Europe "Jewish ghettos in Europe") and [emancipated Jewish populations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_emancipation "Jewish emancipation"), and introduced the metric system. Italy and Switzerland both experienced radical change but Germany was also deeply impacted by the [dissolution of the thousand-year-old Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Holy_Roman_Empire "Dissolution of the Holy Roman Empire"). Its replacement by the [Confederation of the Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_of_the_Rhine "Confederation of the Rhine") fueled [German nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_nationalism "German nationalism"), with far-reaching consequences for Europe.

### Concert of Europe

At the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna "Congress of Vienna") (1814–1815), the great European powers agreed to massive territorial changes in Central and Eastern Europe and to [maintain a European balance of power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concert_of_Europe "Concert of Europe"), initially intended to be managed with regular meetings between them.

Beginning with uprisings in [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_revolution_of_1848 "Sicilian revolution of 1848") and [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution_of_1848 "French Revolution of 1848"), the [revolutions of 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848 "Revolutions of 1848") became the most widespread [revolutionary wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_wave "Revolutionary wave") in European history. In Paris, street fighting forced the [abdication of the king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Philippe_I#Abdication_and_death_(1848–1850) "Louis Philippe I") and the proclamation of the [French Second Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Second_Republic "French Second Republic") (1848–1852), a fast collapse of a great power monarchy that emboldened both liberals and nationalists and encouraged demands for both constitutions and [nation states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation_states "Nation states"). While [Denmark transitioned peacefully to a constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Denmark#Nationalism_and_liberalism "History of Denmark") and [Sardinia-Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sardinia_(1720%E2%80%931861) "Kingdom of Sardinia (1720–1861)") secured the [Statuto Albertino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statuto_Albertino "Statuto Albertino") (later the constitution of a united Italy), conservative reaction triumphed almost everywhere else. In France, the Second Republic curdled into authoritarian rule and then the [Second French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_French_Empire "Second French Empire") (1852–1870). Most consequentially, the [King of Prussia's rejection of the crown of a "German Empire"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_revolutions_of_1848%E2%80%931849#Backlash_in_Prussia "German revolutions of 1848–1849") offered by the [Frankfurt National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_National_Assembly "Frankfurt National Assembly") – the first freely elected parliament for all German states – suggested to Germans that parliamentary liberalism was incapable of achieving a German nation state, a lesson with far-reaching consequences for Europe.

Governments increasingly took control of both education and marriage away from churches, abolished taxes and tithes for the support of [established religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_religion "State religion"), and excluded bishops from legislative bodies. In France, [hostility to the Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Catholicism_in_France "Anti-Catholicism in France") became a major issue and in Germany in the 1870s there was a fierce *[Kulturkampf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulturkampf "Kulturkampf")* (culture war) against Catholics. Notwithstanding these conflicts and tensions, however, the last decades of the long 19th century – sometimes known as the [Belle Époque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belle_%C3%89poque "Belle Époque") (1871–1914) – were characterised by unusual political stability in [Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Europe "Western Europe") and [Central Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Europe "Central Europe"). With the notable exception of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire"), all of Europe's major powers had become [constitutional monarchies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchies "Constitutional monarchies") or republics. For many Europeans, transnational, class-based affiliations were as important as national identities, particularly among aristocrats.

The [Franco-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War "Franco-Prussian War") (1871) that ended a French empire and created a German one also left behind an [enmity between France and Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French%E2%80%93German_enmity#Nearly_looming_conflict_between_France_and_Germany "French–German enmity") because of the [loss of Alsace-Lorraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Frankfurt_(1871) "Treaty of Frankfurt (1871)"). The new [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire "German Empire") (1871–1918) industrialised rapidly and [sought to overtake Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire#Industrial_power "German Empire") to become Europe's leading industrial power. In 1882, [Otto von Bismarck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_von_Bismarck "Otto von Bismarck"), the architect of the [unification of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Germany "Unification of Germany") and chancellor of the Empire, drove the creation of the [Triple Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Alliance_(1882) "Triple Alliance (1882)"), a military alliance of Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy. In 1907, France, Russia and Britain formed the [Triple Entente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Entente "Triple Entente").

### Nationalism

The most revolutionary political force in 19th-century Europe was [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rise_of_nationalism_in_Europe "Rise of nationalism in Europe"), which emerged initially among the [South Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Slavs "South Slavs") and Greeks in the multi-ethnic Ottoman Empire. The success of the anti-Ottoman [Serbian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Revolution "Serbian Revolution") (1804–1817) was followed by the [Greek War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_War_of_Independence "Greek War of Independence") (1821–1829), which became a cause for European '[philhellenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philhellenes "Philhellenes")' who viewed the Greek conflict as a civilizational battle to free the Greek "cradle of Europe" from a foreign occupier. Closer to the heartland of the Empire, the [anti-Ottoman Bulgarian insurrection of 1876](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_Uprising_of_1876 "April Uprising of 1876"), and the [Russo-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_(1877%E2%80%931878) "Russo-Turkish War (1877–1878)") (1877–1878) that followed led to the [re-establishment of Bulgaria in 1878](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_Bulgaria "Liberation of Bulgaria").

The [Austro-Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary "Austria-Hungary") had the advantage of size and a large army but rising nationalism acted as a powerful centrifugal force that strained the multi-ethnic fabric of the Empire, fostering internal dissent and demands for self-determination that progressively undermined the stability of [Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hapsburg_empire "Hapsburg empire") rule. In the [Hungarian Revolution of 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Revolution_of_1848 "Hungarian Revolution of 1848"), Hungarian nationalists battled Austria only finally losing when [Russia sent some 200,000 troops to restore Habsburg rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Revolution_of_1848#Austra_seeks_Russian_help "Hungarian Revolution of 1848"). Russia also crushed large nationalist revolts in [Congress Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_Poland "Congress Poland") in [1830](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_Uprising "November Uprising") and [1863–64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_Uprising "January Uprising") and attempted to ["Russify"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russification_of_Poles_during_the_Partitions "Russification of Poles during the Partitions") Polish language, culture and religion.

French conquests of smaller German and Italian states during the Napoleonic Wars encouraged 19th century demands for national unity. [Italian unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_unification "Italian unification") (also known as the "Risorgimento") was the [consolidation of the seven states of the Italian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Italy#Second_Italian_Independence_War_of_1859_and_aftermath "Unification of Italy") into the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy "Kingdom of Italy") in the 1860s, culminating with the [end of the Papal States in 1870](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Rome "Capture of Rome"). Von Bismarck, the [Minister President of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_President_of_Prussia "Minister President of Prussia") in the 1860s leveraged German nationalism and a series of short, decisive wars – including the [Franco-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War "Franco-Prussian War"), which toppled the Second French Empire – to [consolidate dozens of German states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Germany#Constituent_states_of_the_Empire "Unification of Germany") into the powerful German Empire in 1871.

Under the influence of [Spanish nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_nationalism "Spanish nationalism"), Spain's 18th century Castilian-centric [assimilation policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Planta_decrees "Nueva Planta decrees") towards Catalan-speaking territories ([Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalonia "Catalonia"), [Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencian_Community "Valencian Community"), the [Balearic Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balearic_Islands "Balearic Islands"), part of [Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aragon "Aragon")) and other national minorities accelerated in the 19th century.

Nationalism stimulated the emergence of [national educational systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_school "State school") that prioritized the study of national heritage, [national language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_language "National language"), and patriotic identity over historical accuracy. [Compulsory elementary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compulsory_education "Compulsory education") became standard in Germany and then across Western Europe and then [was extended to girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_access_to_education "Universal access to education"). By the 1890s, strong movements emerged in some countries, including France and Germany, to extend compulsory education to the secondary level.

### Second Industrial Revolution

The [Second Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Industrial_Revolution "Second Industrial Revolution") was a phase of rapid [scientific discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_(observation) "Discovery (observation)"), [standardization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standardization "Standardization"), [mass production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_production "Mass production") and industrialisation that is generally dated between 1870 and 1914.

Although poverty remained pervasive, living standards for lower-class Europeans rose steadily as the century progressed. Wages increased, housing and diets improved, and working hours fell.

### New Imperialism

The [Scramble for Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scramble_for_Africa "Scramble for Africa") was the colonisation of Africa from the mid-1870s to World War I by seven Western European powers ([Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_colonial_empire "Belgian colonial empire"), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire "French colonial empire"), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_colonial_empire "German colonial empire"), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_colonial_empire "Italian colonial empire"), [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire "Portuguese Empire"), [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire "Spanish Empire") and the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire")) enabled by new technologies including steamships, railways and [telegraphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Red_Line "All Red Line"). The United Kingdom combined [naval supremacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Navy#Splendid_isolation "Royal Navy") and [economic and other influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informal_empire#United_Kingdom "Informal empire") to build a [global empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire#Britain's_imperial_century_(1815–1914) "British Empire") – the [largest empire in human history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_empires#Largest_empires_by_land_area "List of largest empires") (comprising more than 25% of all land on the planet in 1920) – on which ["the sun never set"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_empire_on_which_the_sun_never_sets#British_Empire "The empire on which the sun never sets").

## World wars

### World War I

At the beginning of the 20th century two rival alliances of European great powers – the [Triple Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Alliance_(1882) "Triple Alliance (1882)") of the German Empire, Austro-Hungarian Monarchy and Kingdom of Italy and the [Triple Entente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Entente "Triple Entente") of the French Republic, Russian Empire and United Kingdom (Great Britain) – opposed each other and were committed to come to the aid of their alliance partners. In 1914, Austria-Hungary threatened the [Kingdom of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Serbia "Kingdom of Serbia"). In response, Russia mobilized to threaten Austria. Germany then mobilized to threaten Russia and [preemptively strike France through Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schlieffen_Plan "Schlieffen Plan") while Britain [declared war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_entry_into_World_War_I "British entry into World War I") after Germany violated [Belgian neutrality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_London_(1839)#Belgian_neutrality "Treaty of London (1839)").

Modern armies with [machine guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_gun#Maxim_and_World_War_I "Machine gun") and [massive artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artillery_of_World_War_I "Artillery of World War I") fought indecisively for 3 years from [fortified and static trenches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trench_warfare "Trench warfare") on the [Western Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Front_(World_War_I) "Western Front (World War I)"). Single battles like [Verdun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Verdun "Battle of Verdun") and the [Somme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Somme "Battle of the Somme") killed hundreds of thousands. Russia collapsed in the [February Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_Revolution "February Revolution") of 1917 and Germany claimed victory on the [Eastern Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_(World_War_I) "Eastern Front (World War I)"). The [October Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution "October Revolution") brought the [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsheviks "Bolsheviks") to power, leading to the creation of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"). With [American entry into the war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_entry_into_World_War_I "American entry into World War I") in 1917, and the failure of [Germany's spring 1918 offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Days_Offensive "Hundred Days Offensive"), Germany had run out of manpower. Germany's allies, Austria-Hungary and the Ottoman Empire, surrendered and dissolved, followed by Germany. Over 65 million European soldiers were mobilised from 1914 to 1918; 20 million soldiers and civilians died.

The [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles "Treaty of Versailles") (1919) significantly [reduced Germany's military power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichswehr#Arms_limitations_under_the_Treaty_of_Versailles "Reichswehr") and required the nation to [accept responsibility for the conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Guilt_Clause "War Guilt Clause") and pay [extensive financial reparations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I_reparations "World War I reparations"). The conference also created the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations") and transferred [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_colonial_empire#Confiscation "German colonial empire") and [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_the_Ottoman_Empire#French_Mandates "Partition of the Ottoman Empire") territories to the control of Allied powers, chiefly Britain and France, as ["mandates"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations_mandate "League of Nations mandate"). The collapse of the Russian Empire in the First World War enabled the major powers to reestablish an independent [Second Polish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic "Second Polish Republic"), which survived until 1939, as well as an independent Finland, Latvia, Lithuania, Estonia and, briefly, independent Ukraine, Armenia, Georgia, and Azerbaijan. Independent nations of [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia "Czechoslovakia"), Hungary and [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Yugoslavia "Kingdom of Yugoslavia") were also created in central Europe from the defunct Austro-Hungarian and German empires. Multiple nations were required to sign [minority rights treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_Treaties "Minority Treaties").

### Interwar period

The Allied victory in the First World War seemed to mark the triumph of [liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Liberalism "History of Liberalism"). As early as 1917, however, the emerging liberal order was being challenged by the new [communist movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_International "Communist International"). Communist revolts were beaten back everywhere else, but succeeded in Russia. During the 1920s, democratic institutions were dismantled in [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy#Fascist_regime,_World_War_II,_and_Civil_War_(1922–1946) "Kingdom of Italy"), [Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_Kingdom_(1928%E2%80%931939) "Albanian Kingdom (1928–1939)"), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Coup_(Poland) "May Coup (Poland)"), [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/28_May_1926_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat "28 May 1926 coup d'état"), [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1926_Lithuanian_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat "1926 Lithuanian coup d'état") and [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6_January_Dictatorship "6 January Dictatorship").

After the [Wall Street crash of 1929](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street_crash_of_1929 "Wall Street crash of 1929"), most of the world sank into a Great Depression; prices and profits fell and unemployment soared. The worst hit sectors included heavy industry, export-oriented agriculture, mining and lumbering, and construction. World trade fell by two-thirds.

In the 1930s authoritarian regimes ended democracies in [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estado_Novo_(Portugal) "Estado Novo (Portugal)"), [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_State_of_Austria "Federal State of Austria") and [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_of_August_Regime "4th of August Regime"). A German [fascist party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Party "Nazi Party") began to [transform Germany into a totalitarian state in 1933](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enabling_Act_of_1933 "Enabling Act of 1933") and allied with the [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan "Empire of Japan") in the [Anti-Comintern Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Comintern_Pact "Anti-Comintern Pact") in 1936. The League of Nations failed to resolve any major crises and by 1937 it was largely ignored. Supported by [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany") and [Fascist Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_Italy "Fascist Italy"), [Nationalist rebels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_faction_(Spanish_Civil_War) "Nationalist faction (Spanish Civil War)") overthrew the [Second Spanish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic "Second Spanish Republic") in the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War "Spanish Civil War") (1936–1939). With [acquiescence from Britain and France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Agreement "Munich Agreement"), Nazi Germany annexed [part of Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudetenland "Sudetenland") in 1938, and then [occupied the rest of Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Czechoslovakia_(1938%E2%80%931945)#15–16_March "Occupation of Czechoslovakia (1938–1945)"), signed the "[Pact of Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pact_of_Steel "Pact of Steel")" alliance with Fascist Italy and made a [non-aggression treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molotov%E2%80%93Ribbentrop_Pact "Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact") with the Soviet Union in 1939.

### World War II

The German *[Führer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%BChrer "Führer")* [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler") launched [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") with invasions of [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland "Invasion of Poland") (1939), [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_invasion_of_Denmark_(1940) "German invasion of Denmark (1940)") (1940), [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_campaign#German_invasion "Norwegian campaign") (1940), the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_invasion_of_the_Netherlands "German invasion of the Netherlands") (1940), and [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_France "Battle of France") (1940). Britain defeated Germany's air attacks in the [Battle of Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Britain "Battle of Britain") (1940). The [invasion of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa "Operation Barbarossa") began in June 1941. The German [Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht "Wehrmacht") was [stopped close to Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Moscow "Battle of Moscow") in December 1941.

Over the next year the Germans started to suffer a series of defeats. War raged between the [Axis Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers "Axis powers") (Nazi Germany, Fascist Italy, and the Empire of Japan) and the [Allied Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II "Allies of World War II") (British Empire, Soviet Union, and the United States). The Allied Forces won in North Africa, [invaded Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_invasion_of_Italy "Allied invasion of Italy") in 1943, and [liberated France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_France "Liberation of France") in 1944. In 1945 Germany itself was [invaded from the east by the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Prussian_offensive "East Prussian offensive") and [from the west by the other Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Allied_invasion_of_Germany "Western Allied invasion of Germany"). The Soviet [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army "Red Army") conquered the [Reichstag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichstag_building "Reichstag building") in the [Battle of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Berlin "Battle of Berlin") and Germany surrendered.

World War II was the deadliest conflict in human history, causing [between 60 and 75 million deaths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_casualties "World War II casualties"), the majority of whom were civilians (approximately 38 to 55 million). The period was also marked by systematic genocide. In 1942–1945, separately from the war-related deaths, the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism "Nazism") killed over 11 million civilians [identified through IBM-enabled censuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_and_the_Holocaust "IBM and the Holocaust"), including the [majority of the Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust "The Holocaust") and [Gypsies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porajmos "Porajmos") of Europe, millions of [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_crimes_against_ethnic_Poles "Nazi crimes against ethnic Poles") and [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalplan_Ost "Generalplan Ost") Slavs, [homosexuals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_homosexual_men_in_Nazi_Germany "Persecution of homosexual men in Nazi Germany"), [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Jehovah%27s_Witnesses_in_Nazi_Germany "Persecution of Jehovah's Witnesses in Nazi Germany"), [disabled people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_eugenics "Nazi eugenics"), and political enemies. Meanwhile, in the 1930s the Soviet system of [forced labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulag "Gulag"), [expulsions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_transfer_in_the_Soviet_Union "Population transfer in the Soviet Union") and [allegedly engineered famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holodomor "Holodomor") had a similar death toll. Millions of civilians were affected by forced population transfers.

## Recent history

The world wars ended the pre-eminent position of Britain, France and Germany in Europe and the world.

### Cold War

The [victors of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Three_(World_War_II) "Big Three (World War II)") divided Europe into spheres of influence, with a [Western Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Bloc "Western Bloc") and an [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc "Eastern Bloc") of countries opposing each other in the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War"). The United States donated about $20 billion in [Marshall Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Plan "Marshall Plan") grants and other funding to Western Europe between 1945 and 1951. The Soviet Union, concentrating on its own recovery, seized and transferred most of Germany's industrial plants and exacted [war reparations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_reparations "World War II reparations") from, and imposed unfair trading arrangements on, eastern European countries under its control.

The United States and the majority of European liberal democracies established the [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO") military alliance in 1949. With the aim of uniting and defending Western Europe, first steps toward economic and political [integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_integration "European integration") began with the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe "Council of Europe") (1949–), the [European Coal and Steel Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Coal_and_Steel_Community "European Coal and Steel Community") (1952–2002) and [European Economic Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Economic_Community "European Economic Community") (1958–1993). The Soviet Union and its satellites established the [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact "Warsaw Pact") in 1955.

The peak period of European decolonization occurred in the two decades following World War II. During this window, European colonial powers began a [rapid withdrawal from their overseas territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonization#After_1945 "Decolonization"), starting with major Asian nations like [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_(India) "Independence Day (India)") (1947) and [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch%E2%80%93Indonesian_Round_Table_Conference#Aftermath "Dutch–Indonesian Round Table Conference") (1949). In the "[Year of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Year_of_Africa "Year of Africa")" (1960), 17 African nations gained independence in a single year.

After decades of economic growth and an increase in the standard of living of its working class, Western Europe experienced industrial decline, [oil-fueled inflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis#Impact_on_Western_Europe "1973 oil crisis") and [recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973%E2%80%931975_recession "1973–1975 recession") in the 1970s. Germany and Sweden sought to create a social consensus behind a gradual restructuring while Britain implemented aggressive austerity and privatization.

Western Europe's remaining military dictatorships all collapsed in quick succession in the 1970s. In Portugal's 1974 [Carnation Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnation_Revolution "Carnation Revolution"), military officers overthrew the decades-old *[Estado Novo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estado_Novo_(Portugal) "Estado Novo (Portugal)")* regime, beginning a [transition to democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_transition_to_democracy "Portuguese transition to democracy"). Later that year, [Greece's military junta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_junta "Greek junta") collapsed over its role in the [Cyprus crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Cypriot_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat "1974 Cypriot coup d'état"), restoring civilian rule in the period known as the [Metapolitefsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metapolitefsi "Metapolitefsi"). The death of Spain's *[caudillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caudillo "Caudillo")* in 1975 set off a top-down [transition in Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_transition_to_democracy "Spanish transition to democracy") led by reformist politicians and [King Juan Carlos I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Juan_Carlos_I "King Juan Carlos I") that replaced the old regime with a [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain#Governance "Spain").

### Post–Cold War era

Soviet leader [Mikhail Gorbachev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gorbachev "Mikhail Gorbachev") (1931-2022) repudiated the [Brezhnev Doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brezhnev_Doctrine "Brezhnev Doctrine") in the late 1980s and refused to intervene militarily against [Solidarność](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidarno%C5%9B%C4%87 "Solidarność")-led [democratic reforms in Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Polish_parliamentary_election "1989 Polish parliamentary election") (1989), which led rapidly to [fall of the Berlin Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Berlin_Wall "Fall of the Berlin Wall"), the [collapse of Communist governments outside the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1989 "Revolutions of 1989") and the end of the [Iron Curtain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Curtain "Iron Curtain") that had divided Europe since the end of World War II. In 1990 the [Federal Republic of Germany absorbed East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification "German reunification"). In 1991, the Soviet Union itself [split into fifteen independent states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union "Dissolution of the Soviet Union"). The most [violent dissolution occurred in Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Wars "Yugoslav Wars").

English [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist "Computer scientist") [Tim Berners-Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Berners-Lee "Tim Berners-Lee")’s (1955–) invention of the [World Wide Web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Web "World Wide Web") while working at the [European Organization for Nuclear Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Organization_for_Nuclear_Research "European Organization for Nuclear Research") in Switzerland in 1989 began the transformation of the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet "Internet") from a specialized research network for scholars into a global interconnected [information system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_system "Information system") used by billions of people every day, and the primary engine of the [Digital Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Revolution "Digital Revolution").

In 1993, the [Maastricht Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_Treaty "Maastricht Treaty") established the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union"), succeeding the [EEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Economic_Community "European Economic Community"). The neutral countries of Austria, Finland and Sweden [joined the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_enlargement_of_the_European_Union "1995 enlargement of the European Union") in 1995, and those that did not join were tied into the EU's economic market via the [European Economic Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Economic_Area "European Economic Area") and [Schengen Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schengen_Agreement "Schengen Agreement"), which lifted border controls between member states. The *[euro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro "Euro")* was created in 1999 and replaced all previous currencies in participating states in 2002, forming the *[eurozone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurozone "Eurozone")*. In 2004, the EU [gained 10 new members](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_enlargement_of_the_European_Union "2004 enlargement of the European Union") and [two more in 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_enlargement_of_the_European_Union "2007 enlargement of the European Union"). Public opinion in the EU turned against further enlargement. A [European Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_establishing_a_Constitution_for_Europe "Treaty establishing a Constitution for Europe") was [rejected by voters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Referendums_related_to_the_European_Union#European_Constitution,_2005 "Referendums related to the European Union") in 2005.

Russia [invaded Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Georgian_War "Russo-Georgian War") in 2008.

Touched off by the [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession "Great Recession") and the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis "2008 financial crisis"), which caused [government borrowing costs to soar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Long-term_interest_rates_(eurozone).png "File:Long-term interest rates (eurozone).png"), the decade-long [euro area crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_area_crisis "Euro area crisis") (2009–2018) was intensified by the structural inability of the most impacted countries to devalue their [shared currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro "Euro") and further aggravated by a continent-wide pivot toward [austerity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austerity "Austerity") resulting in widespread social unrest and the [anti-austerity movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-austerity_movement "Anti-austerity movement"). In a [referendum in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_Kingdom_European_Union_membership_referendum "2016 United Kingdom European Union membership referendum") on the [country's membership in the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union%E2%80%93United_Kingdom_relations "European Union–United Kingdom relations") in 2016, 52% of voters voted to leave the EU, leading to the complex [Brexit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brexit "Brexit") separation process and negotiations, which led to [political and economic changes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aftermath_of_the_2016_United_Kingdom_European_Union_membership_referendum "Aftermath of the 2016 United Kingdom European Union membership referendum") for both the UK and the remaining EU countries. Although multiple European countries currently outside of the EU remain interested in joining, the aspiration of a geographically and economically diminished EU to act as a third global superpower appeared increasingly unrealistic.

Beginning the [largest and deadliest war in Europe since World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Ukrainian_war_(2022%E2%80%93present) "Russo-Ukrainian war (2022–present)"), Russia [invaded Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine "2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine") on 24 February 2022, vastly escalating an ongoing conflict that had started with the [2014 Russian annexation of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Russian_annexation_of_Crimea "2014 Russian annexation of Crimea") and [separatist fighting in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas "War in Donbas"). In response, [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland "Finland") and [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden "Sweden") became members of NATO in 2023 and in 2024, respectively.

## Chronology

* 7000 BC: [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic "Neolithic") in Europe begins.
* 4600 – 4200 BC: First European proto-civilisation, first golden artefacts and first fortified stone town – the [Varna culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varna_culture "Varna culture").
* 5000 – 3500 BC: First European proto-script – the [Old European script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_European_script "Old European script") (Danubian script).
* 3850 – 3600 BC: [Malta's Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalithic_Temples_of_Malta "Megalithic Temples of Malta") period begins.
* 3500 BC: First European civilization, [Minoan civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoan_civilization "Minoan civilization"), begins on Crete.
* 3000 BC: [Indo-Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Europeans "Indo-Europeans") begin a large-scale settlement of the continent.
* 2500 BC: [Stonehenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonehenge "Stonehenge") is constructed.
* 2100 BC: First European script, [Cretan hieroglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretan_hieroglyphs "Cretan hieroglyphs"), is invented by Minoans.
* 1750 BC: [Mycenaean civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenaean_civilization "Mycenaean civilization") begins.
* 1600 BC: [Thera eruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thera_eruption "Thera eruption") occurs on the island of [Santorini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santorini "Santorini"), destructing the Minoan city of [Thera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thera "Thera").
* 1450 BC: [Crete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crete "Crete") is conquered by [Mycenaeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenaeans "Mycenaeans").
* 1200 BC: [Late Bronze Age collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Bronze_Age_collapse "Late Bronze Age collapse") begins, that may be seen in the context of a technological history that saw the slow spread of [ironworking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironworking "Ironworking") technology from present-day Bulgaria and [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania "Romania") in the 13th and the 12th centuries BC.
* 1100 BC: [Minoan civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoan_civilization "Minoan civilization") falls.
* 1050 BC: [Mycenaean civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenaean_civilization "Mycenaean civilization") falls after a period of palace destruction, marking the beginning of [Greek Dark Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Dark_Ages "Greek Dark Ages").
* 900 BC: [Etruscan civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruscan_civilization "Etruscan civilization") begins.
* 800 BC: [Greek Dark Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Dark_Ages "Greek Dark Ages") end, marking the beginning of [classical antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_antiquity "Classical antiquity").
* 753 BC: Traditional year of founding of Rome.
* 700 BC: [Homer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer "Homer") composes *[The Iliad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iliad "Iliad")*, an epic poem that represents the first extended work of European literature.
* 509 BC: [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic "Roman Republic") is created.
* 499 BC: [Greco-Persian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Persian_Wars "Greco-Persian Wars") begin.
* c. 480 BC: The [Thracian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thracians "Thracians") [Odrysian kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odrysian_kingdom "Odrysian kingdom") was founded as the most important [Daco-Thracian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classification_of_Thracian "Classification of Thracian") [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_(polity) "State (polity)") union.
* 449 BC: End of [Greco-Persian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Persian_Wars "Greco-Persian Wars") with Greeks defeating [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire "Achaemenid Empire").
* 440 BC: [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus "Herodotus") defends Athenian [political freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_freedom "Political freedom") in the *[Histories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histories_(Herodotus) "Histories (Herodotus)")*.
* 404 BC: [Sparta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparta "Sparta") wins the [Peloponnesian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnesian_War "Peloponnesian War").
* 323 BC: [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great "Alexander the Great") dies and his [Macedonian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonian_Empire "Macedonian Empire") (reaching far into Asia) fragments.
* 264 BC: [Punic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic_Wars "Punic Wars") begin.
* 212 BC: [Archimedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedes "Archimedes") killed after [Roman siege of Syracuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Syracuse_(213%E2%80%93212_BC) "Siege of Syracuse (213–212 BC)").
* 146 BC: [Punic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic_Wars "Punic Wars") end with destruction of [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage "Carthage").
* 48 BC: [Julius Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Caesar "Julius Caesar") crosses the Rubicon river, marking the beginning of a civil war.
* 44 BC: [Julius Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Caesar "Julius Caesar") [is murdered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Julius_Caesar "Assassination of Julius Caesar"). The [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic "Roman Republic") enters its terminal crisis.
* 27 BC: Establishment of the Roman Empire under [Octavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octavian "Octavian").

**AD**

* 14 AD: [Octavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octavian "Octavian") dies.
* 30 or 33 AD: [Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus "Jesus"), a popular [religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious "Religious") leader, is [crucified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crucifixion_of_Christ "Crucifixion of Christ").
* 45–55 (ca): First [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian "Christian") congregations in mainland Greece and in Rome.
* 68: First Roman imperial dynasty, [Julio-Claudian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio-Claudian "Julio-Claudian"), ends with suicide of [Nero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nero "Nero").
* 79: [Eruption of Vesuvius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eruption_of_Vesuvius "Eruption of Vesuvius") occurs, burying the cities of [Pompeii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeii "Pompeii"), [Herculaneum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herculaneum "Herculaneum") and [Stabiae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabiae "Stabiae") under the ashes.
* 117: Roman Empire reaches its territorial peak.
* 166: [Antonine Plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonine_Plague "Antonine Plague") begins.
* 293: [Diocletian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocletian "Diocletian") reorganizes the Empire by creating the [Tetrarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrarchy "Tetrarchy").
* 313: [Constantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_the_Great "Constantine the Great") officially recognises Christianity, marking the end of the persecution of Christians.
* 325: Constantine convenes the [First Council of Nicaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Council_of_Nicaea "First Council of Nicaea").
* 330: [Constantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_the_Great "Constantine the Great") makes [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople "Constantinople") into his capital, a [new Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Rome "New Rome").
* 370: [Huns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huns "Huns") first enter Europe.
* 395: Following the death of [Theodosius I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodosius_I "Theodosius I"), the Empire is permanently split into the [Eastern Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Empire "Eastern Roman Empire") (later Byzantium) and the [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire "Western Roman Empire").
* 476: [Odoacer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odoacer "Odoacer") captures [Ravenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravenna "Ravenna") and deposes the last Roman emperor in the west: traditionally seen as the end date of the [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire "Western Roman Empire").
* 527: [Justinian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_I "Justinian I") is [crowned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation "Coronation") emperor of [Byzantium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire "Byzantine Empire"). Orders the editing of *[Corpus Juris Civilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_Juris_Civilis "Corpus Juris Civilis")*, [Digest (Roman law)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digest_(Roman_law) "Digest (Roman law)").
* 597: Beginning of Roman Catholic [Christianization of Anglo-Saxon England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization_of_Anglo-Saxon_England "Christianization of Anglo-Saxon England") (missions and churches had been in existence well before this date, but their contacts with Rome had been loose or nonexistent)
* 600: [Saint Columbanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Columbanus "Saint Columbanus") uses the term "Europe" in a letter.
* 655: [Jus patronatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jus_patronatus "Jus patronatus").
* 681: [Khan Asparukh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_Asparukh "Khan Asparukh") leads the [Bulgars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgars "Bulgars") and in a union with the numerous [local](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Slavic_tribes "Seven Slavic tribes") [Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs "Slavs") invades the Byzantine Empire in the [Battle of Ongal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ongal "Battle of Ongal"), creating [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire "First Bulgarian Empire").
* 711: Beginning of the [Muslim conquest of the Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_the_Iberian_Peninsula "Muslim conquest of the Iberian Peninsula").
* 718: [Tervel of Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tervel_of_Bulgaria "Tervel of Bulgaria") helps the Byzantine Empire stop the Arabic invasion of Europe, and breaks the [siege of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Constantinople_(717%E2%80%93718) "Siege of Constantinople (717–718)").
* 722: [Battle of Covadonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Covadonga "Battle of Covadonga") in the Iberian Peninsula. [Pelayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelagius_of_Asturias "Pelagius of Asturias"), a noble Visigoth, defeats a Muslim army that tried to conquer the Cantabrian coast. This helps establish the Christian [Kingdom of Asturias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Asturias "Kingdom of Asturias"), and marks the beginning of the Reconquista.
* 732: At the [Battle of Tours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tours "Battle of Tours"), the Franks stop the advance of the Arabs into Europe.
* 752: [Pope Stephen II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Stephen_II "Pope Stephen II") is the first [bishop of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome "Bishop of Rome") to ever cross the [Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alps "Alps").
* 800: Coronation of [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne "Charlemagne") as [Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor "Holy Roman Emperor").
* 813: Third Council of Tours: Priests are ordered to preach in the [native language of the population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernacular "Vernacular").
* 828: [Legendary theft of the relics of St Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Mark%27s_relics#Legends "Saint Mark's relics") by Venetians.
* 843: [Treaty of Verdun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Verdun "Treaty of Verdun").
* 863: [Saints Cyril and Methodius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saints_Cyril_and_Methodius "Saints Cyril and Methodius") arrive in [Great Moravia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Moravia "Great Moravia"), initiating Christian mission among the Slav peoples.
* 864: [Boris I of Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_I_of_Bulgaria "Boris I of Bulgaria") [officially baptises the whole nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization_of_Bulgaria "Christianization of Bulgaria"), converting the non-Christian population from [Tengrism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tengrism "Tengrism"), [Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_paganism "Slavic paganism") and other paganism to [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity"), and officially founding the [Bulgarian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_Orthodox_Church "Bulgarian Orthodox Church")
* 872: [Unification of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Norway "Unification of Norway").
* 885–886: [Vikings siege Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Paris_(885%E2%80%93886) "Siege of Paris (885–886)").
* 886: [Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarians "Bulgarians") students of [Cyril and Methodius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyril_and_Methodius "Cyril and Methodius") – [Saint Sava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Sava_(disciple_of_Saints_Cyril_and_Methodius) "Saint Sava (disciple of Saints Cyril and Methodius)"), [Kliment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clement_of_Ohrid "Clement of Ohrid"), [Naum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Naum "Saint Naum"), [Gorazd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Gorazd "Saint Gorazd") and [Angelar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelar "Angelar")– arrive back to [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire "First Bulgarian Empire"), creating the [Preslav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preslav_Literary_School "Preslav Literary School") and [Ohrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohrid_Literary_School "Ohrid Literary School") Literary Schools.
* 893: The [Cyrillic alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrillic_alphabet "Cyrillic alphabet"), developed during the 9th century AD at the [Preslav Literary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preslav_Literary_School "Preslav Literary School") in the [First Bulgarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire "First Bulgarian Empire"), becomes the official [Bulgarian alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_alphabet "Bulgarian alphabet").
* 895: [Hungarian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_people "Hungarian people") led by [Árpád](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81rp%C3%A1d "Árpád") start to settle in the [Carpathian Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Basin "Carpathian Basin").
* 917: In the [Battle of Achelous (917)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Achelous_(917) "Battle of Achelous (917)") Bulgaria defeats the Byzantine Empire, and [Simeon I of Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simeon_I_of_Bulgaria "Simeon I of Bulgaria") is proclaimed as emperor, thus [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria "Bulgaria") becomes an [empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire "First Bulgarian Empire").
* 935: [Fatimid sack of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimid_sack_of_Genoa "Fatimid sack of Genoa").
* 962: [Otto I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor "Otto I, Holy Roman Emperor") of [East Francia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Francia "East Francia") is crowned as "Emperor" by the Pope, beginning the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire "Holy Roman Empire").
* 988 [Kievan Rus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus "Kievan Rus") adopts Christianity, often seen as the origin of the [Orthodox Church of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Church_of_Ukraine "Orthodox Church of Ukraine"), the [Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Greek_Catholic_Church "Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church"), and the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church "Russian Orthodox Church").
* 1049: Reformist [Pope Leo IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_IX#Papacy "Pope Leo IX") begins reign.
* 1054: Start of the [East–West Schism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East%E2%80%93West_Schism "East–West Schism"), which divides the Christian church for centuries.
* 1059: The [papal bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_bull "Papal bull") *[In nomine Domini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_nomine_Domini "In nomine Domini")* strips the [Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor "Holy Roman Emperor") of his traditional role in appointing popes.
* 1066: Successful [Norman invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_conquest_of_England "Norman conquest of England") of England by [William the Conqueror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_the_Conqueror "William the Conqueror").
* 1071: Decisive defeat of the [Byzantine army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_army "Byzantine army") by the [Seljuk Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Empire "Seljuk Empire") at the [Battle of Manzikert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Manzikert "Battle of Manzikert").
* 1076: [Investiture Controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investiture_Controversy "Investiture Controversy") begins.
* 1095: [Pope Urban II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Urban_II "Pope Urban II") calls for the [First Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Crusade "First Crusade").
* 12th century: The [12th century in literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th_century_in_literature "12th century in literature") saw an increase in the number of texts. The [Renaissance of the 12th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_of_the_12th_century "Renaissance of the 12th century") occurs.
* 1128: [Battle of São Mamede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_S%C3%A3o_Mamede "Battle of São Mamede"), formation of Portuguese sovereignty.
* 1131: Birth of the [Kingdom of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sicily "Kingdom of Sicily")
* 1176: [Lombard League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_League "Lombard League") defeats [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire "Holy Roman Empire") at the [Battle of Legnano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Legnano "Battle of Legnano")
* 1183: [Northern Italian city states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_League "Lombard League") win autonomy from [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire "Holy Roman Empire") in the [Peace of Constance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Constance "Peace of Constance")
* 1180s: The [oldest university currently in continuous operation in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oldest_universities_in_continuous_operation "List of oldest universities in continuous operation") is founded in [Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna "Bologna").
* 1185: [Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Bulgarian_Empire "Second Bulgarian Empire") sovereignty was reestablished with the [anti-Byzantine uprising of the Bulgarians and Vlachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uprising_of_Asen_and_Peter "Uprising of Asen and Peter")
* 1202: [Fibonacci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci "Fibonacci") (Leonardo Bonacci of [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Pisa "Republic of Pisa")) produces *[Liber Abaci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liber_Abaci "Liber Abaci")*, introducing both [base-10 positional notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu%E2%80%93Arabic_numeral_system "Hindu–Arabic numeral system") and the symbols known as [Arabic numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_numerals "Arabic numerals") in Europe.
* 1241: Mongol armies [invade Central Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Europe#Invasion_of_Central_Europe "Mongol invasion of Europe") and defeat a [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Poland "Kingdom of Poland")-[Moravian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margraviate_of_Moravia "Margraviate of Moravia") force at the [Battle of Legnica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Legnica "Battle of Legnica") and crush the [Hungarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary "Kingdom of Hungary") at the [Battle of Mohi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mohi "Battle of Mohi") before [withdrawing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Europe#End_of_the_Mongol_advance "Mongol invasion of Europe").
* 1250: Death of [emperor Frederick II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor "Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor"); end of effective ability of emperors to exercise control in Italy.
* 1256: Bologna's *[Liber Paradisus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liber_Paradisus "Liber Paradisus")* purchases the freedom of nearly 6,000 serfs, the first abolition of serfdom in Europe.
* 1295: Florence's [Ordinances of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinances_of_Justice "Ordinances of Justice") aligns [the city with the Catholic Church against the Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guelphs_and_Ghibellines "Guelphs and Ghibellines") and ensures that the [city's merchant and artisan guilds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilds_of_Florence "Guilds of Florence") control its government.
* 1303: The period of the [Crusades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades "Crusades") is over.
* 1309–1378: The [Avignon Papacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avignon_Papacy "Avignon Papacy")
* 1315–1317: The [Great Famine of 1315–1317](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Famine_of_1315%E2%80%931317 "Great Famine of 1315–1317") in Northern Europe
* 1341: [Petrarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrarch "Petrarch"), the "Father of [Humanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_Humanism "Renaissance Humanism")", becomes the first [poet laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poet_laureate "Poet laureate") since antiquity.
* 1337–1453: The [Hundred Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Years%27_War "Hundred Years' War") between England and France.
* 1348–1351: [Black Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Death "Black Death") kills about one-third of Europe's population.
* 1439: [Johannes Gutenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Gutenberg "Johannes Gutenberg") invents first [movable type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movable_type "Movable type") and the first [printing press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing_press "Printing press") for books, starting the [Printing Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing_Revolution "Printing Revolution").
* 1453: [Fall of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Constantinople "Fall of Constantinople") to the [Ottoman Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turks "Ottoman Turks").
* 1487: The [Wars of the Roses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wars_of_the_Roses "Wars of the Roses") end.
* 1492: The [Reconquista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconquista "Reconquista") ends in the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula "Iberian Peninsula"). A Spanish expeditionary group, commanded by [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus "Christopher Columbus"), lands in the [New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World "New World").
* 1494: [Treaty of Tordesillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Tordesillas "Treaty of Tordesillas") grants Africa and the East to Portugal and the Atlantic and everything beyond it to Spain.
* 1497: [Vasco da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_da_Gama "Vasco da Gama") departs to India starting direct trade with Asia.
* 1498: [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci "Leonardo da Vinci") paints *[The Last Supper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Supper_(Leonardo_da_Vinci) "The Last Supper (Leonardo da Vinci)")* in Milan as the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance "Renaissance") flourishes.
* 1503: [Amerigo Vespucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerigo_Vespucci "Amerigo Vespucci")'s [Mundus Novus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World#Mundus_Novus "New World") letter is published.
* 1508: [Maximilian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilian_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor "Maximilian I, Holy Roman Emperor") the last ruling "[King of the Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_the_Romans "King of the Romans")" and the first "elected Emperor of the Romans".
* 1517: [Martin Luther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther "Martin Luther") nails his [95 theses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/95_theses "95 theses") on indulgences to the door of the [church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Saints%27_Church,_Wittenberg "All Saints' Church, Wittenberg") in [Wittenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wittenberg "Wittenberg"), triggering discussions which would soon lead to the [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation "Reformation").
* 1519: [Ferdinand Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Magellan "Ferdinand Magellan") and [Juan Sebastián Elcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Sebasti%C3%A1n_Elcano "Juan Sebastián Elcano") begin first global circumnavigation. Their expedition returns in 1522.
* 1519: [Hernán Cortés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hern%C3%A1n_Cort%C3%A9s "Hernán Cortés") begins conquest of Mexico for Spain.
* 1527: [Sack of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sack_of_Rome_(1527) "Sack of Rome (1527)") by the mutinous troops of the [Holy Roman Emperor Charles V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor "Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor").
* 1529: Failure of the Ottoman Empire's [first attempt to capture the Habsburg capital of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Vienna_(1529) "Siege of Vienna (1529)").
* 1532: [Francisco Pizarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Pizarro "Francisco Pizarro") begins the conquest of Peru (the [Inca Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_Empire "Inca Empire")) for Spain.
* 1532: [Niccolò Machiavelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niccol%C3%B2_Machiavelli "Niccolò Machiavelli")'s *[The Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prince "The Prince")* is published.
* 1543: [Nicolaus Copernicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus "Nicolaus Copernicus") publishes *[De revolutionibus orbium coelestium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_revolutionibus_orbium_coelestium "De revolutionibus orbium coelestium") (On the Revolutions of the Celestial Spheres)*.
* 1547: The [Grand Duchy of Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Moscow "Grand Duchy of Moscow") becomes the [Tsardom of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsardom_of_Russia "Tsardom of Russia").
* 1568–1570: The [first](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_(1568%E2%80%931570) "Russo-Turkish War (1568–1570)") of twelve [Russo-Turkish wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_wars "Russo-Turkish wars").
* 1572: [Tycho Brahe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tycho_Brahe "Tycho Brahe") witnesses a [supernova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SN_1572#Historic_description "SN 1572").
* 1582: The introduction of the [Gregorian calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_calendar "Gregorian calendar"); Russia refuses to adopt it until 1918.
* 1609: [Johannes Kepler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Kepler "Johannes Kepler")'s *[Astronomia nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomia_nova "Astronomia nova")* includes the first mention of elliptical planetary orbits.
* 1610: [Galileo Galilei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo_Galilei "Galileo Galilei") uses his telescope to discover the moons of [Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter "Jupiter") and publishes the [Starry Messenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidereus_Nuncius "Sidereus Nuncius").
* 1618: The [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years%27_War "Thirty Years' War") brings massive devastation to central Europe.
* 1648: The [Peace of Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Westphalia "Peace of Westphalia") ends the Thirty Years' War, and introduces the principle of the integrity of the nation state.
* 1683: Failure of the Ottoman Empire's [second and last attempt to capture the Habsburg capital of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Vienna "Battle of Vienna").
* 1687: [Isaac Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Newton "Isaac Newton") publishes *[Principia Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophi%C3%A6_Naturalis_Principia_Mathematica "Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica")*, having a profound impact on [The Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Enlightenment "The Enlightenment").
* 1688: [Glorious Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glorious_Revolution "Glorious Revolution") in England.
* 1699: [Treaty of Karlowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Karlowitz "Treaty of Karlowitz") concludes the [Austro-Ottoman War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Turkish_War "Great Turkish War"). This marks the end of Ottoman control of Central Europe and the beginning of Ottoman stagnation, establishing the Habsburg monarchy as the dominant power in Central and Southeastern Europe.
* 1700: Outbreak of the [War of the Spanish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession "War of the Spanish Succession") and the [Great Northern War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_War "Great Northern War"). The first would check the aspirations of [Louis XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIV "Louis XIV"), king of France to dominate European affairs; the second would lead to Russia's emergence as a great power and a recognizably European state.
* 18th century: [Age of Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment "Age of Enlightenment") spurs an intellectual renaissance across Europe.
* 1707: The [Kingdom of Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain "Kingdom of Great Britain") is formed by the union of the [Kingdom of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_England "Kingdom of England") and the [Kingdom of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Scotland "Kingdom of Scotland").
* 1712: [Thomas Newcomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Newcomen "Thomas Newcomen") invents first practical steam engine which begins Industrial Revolution in Britain.
* 1721: [Treaty of Nystad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Nystad "Treaty of Nystad") and foundation of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire").
* 1772–1795: [Three partitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitions_of_Poland "Partitions of Poland") of the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth "Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth")
* 1775: [James Watt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Watt "James Watt") invents a new efficient steam engine accelerating the Industrial Revolution in Britain.
* 1776: [Adam Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Smith "Adam Smith") publishes [The Wealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wealth_of_Nations "The Wealth of Nations").
* 1784: [Immanuel Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant "Immanuel Kant") publishes *[Answering the Question: What Is Enlightenment?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Answering_the_Question:_What_Is_Enlightenment%3F "Answering the Question: What Is Enlightenment?")*.
* 1789: Beginning of the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution "French Revolution").
* 1793–1794: [Reign of Terror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reign_of_Terror "Reign of Terror")
* 1799: [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon "Napoleon")'s [coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup_of_18_Brumaire "Coup of 18 Brumaire")
* 1806: [Dissolution of the Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Holy_Roman_Empire "Dissolution of the Holy Roman Empire")
* 1812: [Fire of Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_of_Moscow_(1812) "Fire of Moscow (1812)")
* 1815: [Battle of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Waterloo "Battle of Waterloo") and permanent exile of Napoleon
* 1814–1815: [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna "Congress of Vienna"); Treaty of Vienna; France is reduced to 1789 boundaries; Reactionary forces dominate across Europe.
* 1825: [George Stephenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Stephenson "George Stephenson") opens the [Stockton and Darlington Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_and_Darlington_Railway "Stockton and Darlington Railway") the first steam train railway for passenger traffic in the world.
* 1830: The southern provinces secede from the [United Kingdom of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_the_Netherlands "United Kingdom of the Netherlands") in the [Belgian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_Revolution "Belgian Revolution").
* 1836: [Louis Daguerre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Daguerre "Louis Daguerre") invents first practical photographic method, in effect the first camera.
* 1838: [SS *Great Western*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Great_Western "SS Great Western"), the first steamship built for regularly scheduled transatlantic crossings enters service.
* 1848: [Revolutions of 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848 "Revolutions of 1848") and publication of *[The Communist Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Communist_Manifesto "The Communist Manifesto")*
* 1853: Start of the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War "Crimean War"), which ends in 1856 in a defeat for Russia.
* 1859: [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin "Charles Darwin") publishes *On the Origin of Species*.
* 1861: [Unification of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Italy "Unification of Italy") after victories by [Giuseppe Garibaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Garibaldi "Giuseppe Garibaldi").
* 1866: First commercially successful [transatlantic telegraph cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transatlantic_telegraph_cable "Transatlantic telegraph cable") is completed.
* 1860s: Russia [emancipates its serfs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emancipation_reform_of_1861 "Emancipation reform of 1861") and [Karl Marx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Marx "Karl Marx") completes the first volume of *[Das Kapital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Das_Kapital "Das Kapital")*.
* 1870: [Franco-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War "Franco-Prussian War") and the fall of the [Second French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_French_Empire "Second French Empire").
* 1871: [Unification of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Germany "Unification of Germany") under the direction of [Otto von Bismarck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_von_Bismarck "Otto von Bismarck").
* 1873: [Panic of 1873](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panic_of_1873 "Panic of 1873") occurs. The [Long Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Depression "Long Depression") begins.
* 1877–1878: [Russo-Turkish War (1877–1878)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_(1877%E2%80%931878) "Russo-Turkish War (1877–1878)") and re-establishment of [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Bulgaria "Principality of Bulgaria"), independence of [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Serbia "Kingdom of Serbia"), [Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Montenegro "Principality of Montenegro") and [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania "Kingdom of Romania")
* 1882: [Triple Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Alliance_(1882) "Triple Alliance (1882)") formed between [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire "German Empire"), [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary "Austria-Hungary") and [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy "Kingdom of Italy").
* 1884: First permanent citywide [electrical tram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tram "Tram") system in Europe (in [Brighton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton "Brighton")).
* 1885: [Karl Benz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Benz "Karl Benz") invents [Benz Patent-Motorwagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benz_Patent-Motorwagen "Benz Patent-Motorwagen"), the world's first automobile.
* 1895: [Auguste and Louis Lumière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auguste_and_Louis_Lumi%C3%A8re "Auguste and Louis Lumière") begin exhibitions of projected films before the paying public with their [cinematograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinematograph "Cinematograph"), a portable camera, printer, and projector.
* 1902: [Guglielmo Marconi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guglielmo_Marconi "Guglielmo Marconi") sends first transatlantic radio transmission.
* 1905: [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein")'s *[annus mirabilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annus_mirabilis_papers "Annus mirabilis papers")*.
* 1907: [Triple Entente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Entente "Triple Entente") formed between [French Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Third_Republic "French Third Republic"), [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire") and [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland "United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland").
* 1914: [Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archduke_Franz_Ferdinand_of_Austria "Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria") is [assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Archduke_Franz_Ferdinand "Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand") in [Sarajevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarajevo "Sarajevo"); [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I") begins a month later.
* 1917: [Vladimir Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Lenin "Vladimir Lenin") and the [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik "Bolshevik") seize power in the [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution "Russian Revolution"). The ensuing [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War "Russian Civil War") lasts until 1922.
* 1918: World War I ends with the defeat of Germany and the Central Powers. Ten million soldiers killed; collapse of Russian, German, Austrian, and Ottoman empires.
* 1918: Collapse of the German Empire and monarchic system; founding of [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic "Weimar Republic").
* 1918: Worldwide [Spanish flu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_flu "Spanish flu") epidemic kills millions in Europe.
* 1918: Austro-Hungarian Empire dissolves.
* 1919: [Versailles Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versailles_Treaty "Versailles Treaty") strips Germany of its colonies, several provinces and its navy and air force; limits army; Allies occupy western areas; reparations ordered.
* 1920: [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations") begins operations; largely ineffective; defunct by 1939.
* 1921–22: Ireland divided; Irish Free State becomes independent and civil war erupts.
* 1922: [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini "Benito Mussolini") and the [Fascists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Fascist_Party "National Fascist Party") take power in Italy.
* 1929: Worldwide [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression "Great Depression") begins with [stock market crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street_crash_of_1929 "Wall Street crash of 1929") in New York City.
* 1933: [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler") and the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazis "Nazis") take power in Germany.
* 1935: Italy conquers Ethiopia; League sanctions are ineffective.
* 1936: Start of the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War "Spanish Civil War"); ends in 1939 with victory of Nationalists who are aided by Germany and Italy.
* 1938: Germany escalates the persecution of Jews with [Kristallnacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristallnacht "Kristallnacht").
* 1938: [Appeasement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appeasement "Appeasement") of Germany by Britain and France; [Munich Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Agreement "Munich Agreement") splits [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia "Czechoslovakia"); Germany seized the remainder in 1939.
* 1939: Britain and France hurriedly rearm; failed to arrange treaty with USSR.
* 1939: [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler") and [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin "Joseph Stalin") agree partition of Eastern Europe in [Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molotov%E2%80%93Ribbentrop_Pact "Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact").
* 1939: [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany") [invades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland "Invasion of Poland") [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic "Second Polish Republic"), starting the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War "Second World War").
* 1940: Great Britain under [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill "Winston Churchill") becomes the last nation to hold out against the Nazis after winning the [Battle of Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Britain "Battle of Britain").
* 1941: U.S. begins large-scale [lend-lease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lend-lease "Lend-lease") aid to Britain, Free France, the USSR and other [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II "Allies of World War II"); Canada also provides financial aid.
* 1941: Germany invades the Soviet Union in [Operation Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa "Operation Barbarossa"); fails to capture Moscow or Leningrad.
* 1941: [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler") and [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany") begin the systematic murder of Jews with [mass shootings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust#Mass_shooting "The Holocaust") during the [invasion of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa "Operation Barbarossa").
* 1943: After [Stalingrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Stalingrad "Battle of Stalingrad") and [Kursk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kursk "Battle of Kursk"), Soviet forces begin recapturing Nazi-occupied territory in the East.
* 1944: U.S., British and Canadian armed forces [invade Nazi-occupied France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normandy_landings "Normandy landings") at [Normandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normandy "Normandy").
* 1945: [Hitler commits suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Adolf_Hitler "Death of Adolf Hitler"), Mussolini is executed. World War II ends with Europe in ruins and Germany defeated.
* 1945: [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations") formed.
* 1947: The [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire") begins a process of voluntarily dismantling with the granting of independence to India and Pakistan.
* 1947: [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War") begins as Europe is polarized East versus West.
* 1948–1951: U.S. provides large sums to rebuild Western Europe through the [Marshall Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Plan "Marshall Plan"); stimulates large-scale modernization of European industries and reduction of trade restrictions.
* 1949: The [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO") alliance and the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe "Council of Europe") are founded.
* 1950: The [Schuman Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schuman_Declaration "Schuman Declaration") begins the process of [European integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_integration "European integration").
* 1954: The [French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire "French colonial empire") begins to be dismantled; Withdraws from Vietnam.
* 1955: USSR creates a rival military coalition to the [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO"), the [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact "Warsaw Pact").
* 1956: [Suez Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Crisis "Suez Crisis") signals the end of the effective power of the British Empire.
* 1956: [Hungarian Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Revolution_of_1956 "Hungarian Revolution of 1956") defeated by Soviet military forces.
* 1957: [Treaties of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaties_of_Rome "Treaties of Rome") establish the [European Economic Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Economic_Community "European Economic Community") from 1958.
* 1962: The [Second Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council "Second Vatican Council") opens and begins a period of reform in the [Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church "Catholic Church")
* 1968: The [May 1968 events in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_1968_events_in_France "May 1968 events in France") lead France to the brink of revolution.
* 1968: The [Prague Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Spring "Prague Spring") is defeated by [Warsaw Pact military forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact_invasion_of_Czechoslovakia "Warsaw Pact invasion of Czechoslovakia"). The [Club of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_of_Rome "Club of Rome") is founded.
* 1973: Denmark, Ireland and the United Kingdom [join the European Communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_enlargement_of_the_European_Communities "1973 enlargement of the European Communities").
* 1980: The [Solidarność](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidarity_(Polish_trade_union) "Solidarity (Polish trade union)") movement under [Lech Wałęsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lech_Wa%C5%82%C4%99sa "Lech Wałęsa") begins open, overground opposition to the Communist rule in Poland.
* 1981: Greece [joins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_enlargement_of_the_European_Communities "1981 enlargement of the European Communities") the European Communities.
* 1985: [Mikhail Gorbachev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gorbachev "Mikhail Gorbachev") becomes leader of the Soviet Union and [begins reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perestroika "Perestroika") which inadvertently leads to the fall of Communism and the Soviet Union.
* 1986: Portugal and Spain [join the European Communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_enlargement_of_the_European_Communities "1986 enlargement of the European Communities").
* 1986: [Chernobyl disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_disaster "Chernobyl disaster") occurs, the worst nuclear disaster in history.
* 1989: Soviet leader [Mikhail Gorbachev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gorbachev "Mikhail Gorbachev") refuses to use military force when a [free Polish election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Polish_parliamentary_election "1989 Polish parliamentary election") is overwhelmingly won by non-Communist candidates. [Communism overthrown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1989 "Revolutions of 1989") in all the [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact "Warsaw Pact") countries except the Soviet Union. [Fall of the Berlin Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Berlin_Wall "Fall of the Berlin Wall") (opening of unrestrained border crossings between east and west, which effectively deprived the wall of any relevance).
* 1990: [Reunification of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification "German reunification").
* 1991: [Breakup of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakup_of_Yugoslavia "Breakup of Yugoslavia") and the beginning of the [Yugoslav Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Wars "Yugoslav Wars").
* 1991: [Dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union "Dissolution of the Soviet Union") and the creation of the [Commonwealth of Independent States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Independent_States "Commonwealth of Independent States").
* 1993: [Maastricht Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_Treaty "Maastricht Treaty") establishes the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union").
* 1995: Austria, Finland and Sweden [join the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_enlargement_of_the_European_Union "1995 enlargement of the European Union").
* 1997–99: End of European [colonial empires in Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_imperialism_in_Asia "Western imperialism in Asia") with the handover of [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Hong_Kong "British Hong Kong") and [Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Macau "Portuguese Macau") to China.
* 2004: Slovenia, Hungary, the Czech Republic, Slovakia, Poland, Lithuania, Latvia, Estonia, Cyprus and Malta [join the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_enlargement_of_the_European_Union "2004 enlargement of the European Union").
* 2007: Bulgaria and Romania [join the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_enlargement_of_the_European_Union "2007 enlargement of the European Union").
* 2008: The [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession#Effects_on_Europe "Great Recession") begins. Unemployment rises in some parts of Europe.
* 2009–2018: [Euro area crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_area_crisis "Euro area crisis")
* 2013: Croatia [joins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_enlargement_of_the_European_Union "2013 enlargement of the European Union") the European Union.
* 2014: [Revolution of Dignity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_of_Dignity "Revolution of Dignity") in Ukraine and the beginning of the [Russo-Ukrainian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Ukrainian_War "Russo-Ukrainian War").
* 2015: [European migrant crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_migrant_crisis "European migrant crisis") starts.
* 2020: The United Kingdom [leaves the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brexit "Brexit").
* 2020–2023: [COVID-19 pandemic in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Europe "COVID-19 pandemic in Europe"), countries with the most cases are Russia, the United Kingdom, France, Spain, and Italy.
* 2022: [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine "Russian invasion of Ukraine") opens with some of the most intense combat operations in Europe since the end of the Cold War.
* 2023: Finland [joins NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlargement_of_NATO "Enlargement of NATO").
* 2024: Sweden joins [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO").
