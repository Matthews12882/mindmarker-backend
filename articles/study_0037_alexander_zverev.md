# Alexander Zverev

**Alexander** "**Sascha**" **Zverev** (born 20 April 1997) is a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany") professional [tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis "Tennis") player. Zverev has won 26 [ATP Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Tour "ATP Tour") singles titles, including two [majors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis) "Grand Slam (tennis)") at the [2026 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2026 French Open – Men's singles") and [2026 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2026 US Open – Men's singles"), a gold medal at the [2020 Tokyo Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "Tennis at the 2020 Summer Olympics – Men's singles"), seven [ATP 1000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Masters_1000_tournaments "ATP Masters 1000 tournaments") events, and the [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_ATP_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2018 ATP Finals – Singles") and [2021 ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_ATP_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 ATP Finals – Singles").

Zverev is a former junior world No. 1, and won a junior major singles title at the [2014 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Boys%27_singles "2014 Australian Open – Boys' singles"). He had an early breakthrough on the professional tour, becoming one of the youngest [ATP Challenger Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Challenger_Tour "ATP Challenger Tour") title winners in history at the age of 17. As a teenager, Zverev won two ATP titles and upset then–world No. 3 [Roger Federer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Federer "Roger Federer") on grass. At 20 years old, he became the youngest player to debut in the top 20 since [Novak Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic"). At the [Laver Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laver_Cup "Laver Cup"), Zverev played an instrumental role in Team Europe's early success, winning the clinching matches in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Laver_Cup "2018 Laver Cup"), [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Laver_Cup "2019 Laver Cup"), and [2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Laver_Cup "2026 Laver Cup").

After reaching his first major final at the [2020 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2020 US Open – Men's singles"), he became an Olympic gold medalist in 2021. He suffered an ankle injury at the [2022 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 French Open – Men's singles"), which required an extended recovery period. He rebounded in the following years to re-enter the top 10, where he has since remained a permanent fixture and reached five more major finals. In 2026, Zverev enjoyed his most successful [season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Alexander_Zverev_tennis_season "2026 Alexander Zverev tennis season") winning the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2026 French Open – Men's singles") and [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2026 US Open – Men's singles"), and is guaranteed to rise to number 1 in the [world rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Singles_Rankings "ATP Singles Rankings") in November 2026.

## Early life and background

Alexander Zverev was born on 20 April 1997 in [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg "Hamburg"), Germany, to [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians "Russians") parents Irina Zvereva and [Alexander Zverev Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Zverev_Sr. "Alexander Zverev Sr.") His older brother, [Mischa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mischa_Zverev "Mischa Zverev"), born nearly a decade earlier, was also a professional tennis player. Both of his parents were professional tennis players for the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"). His father, who ranked as high as No. 175 in the world, became the top-ranked men's player nationally, while his mother was the fourth-highest-ranked women's player in the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"). They both moved from [Sochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sochi "Sochi") to the capital to train at the [CSKA Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSKA_Moscow "CSKA Moscow") military-run tennis club. The Soviet government often restricted their players from competing outside the country, an impediment that limited how high either of Alexander's parents could rise in the world rankings. With the [collapse of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union "Dissolution of the Soviet Union") imminent, Irina went to Germany to compete at a tournament in 1990, with her husband accompanying as her coach. While in Germany, they were offered jobs as tennis instructors. After initially declining, they accepted an offer to work at the [Uhlenhorster Hockey Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uhlenhorster_HC "Uhlenhorster HC") in Hamburg the following year and ended up settling in the country.

Zverev, known in his family as Sascha (the Russian-language diminutive for Alexander),[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] started playing tennis at the age of three. Since he began playing tennis at a very young age, he has said, "One day, when I was, I think, one year and five months old, I just picked up a little racket and I was starting to push the ball all over our apartment, and since then, they took me out on the court. I enjoy it still, I enjoyed it back then." When he was five years old, he started to play tennis at least half an hour each day. He was extremely competitive as a child. His brother, Mischa, said, "He would not understand or accept that he was losing," when the two would play against each other. He would never want to leave the court unless he won the match. He also played hockey and football as a child but decided to focus only on tennis around the age of twelve after an early-round loss at a high-level international junior tournament in Florida.

When Alexander was young, his mother was his primary coach while his father was focused on coaching his brother. He has said, "I think I have pretty good technique, which my mum did at a young age, so credit to her for that. My backhand, in particular, is 100 percent down to my mum." While his mother had a more relaxed teaching style, his father "had a very Soviet way of doing physical training sessions" that involved doing timed drills for fixed numbers of repetitions. Alexander's coaches aimed for him to have a riskier, aggressive playing style built around hitting the ball with pace and finishing points quickly. This was a big contrast from how he played around age twelve when his style focused on being an "unbelievable fighter" from the baseline in part because he was too slow to go to the net. Initially, Alexander struggled to change his playing style. He "made a lot of errors" and lost to opponents who excelled at keeping points alive. However, his father stuck with this strategy, saying, "We must practice fast tennis, aggressive tennis. If you lose today it's no big deal. You must think about the future."

## Junior career

Zverev played his first junior match in January 2011 at the age of 13 at a grade 4 tournament in Poland.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] Near the beginning of 2012, Zverev won his first ITF title at the Fujairah Junior Championships, a low-level Grade 4 tournament in the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates "United Arab Emirates"). He would pick up a lower level Grade 5 title at the Oman International Junior 2 a few weeks later, which led him to begin competing in higher-level events shortly before his 15th birthday. He did not have much success at tournaments that were Grade 2 and above until the following year when he reached back-to-back doubles finals with [Spencer Papa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spencer_Papa "Spencer Papa") at the Grade A [Copa Gerdau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Gerdau "Copa Gerdau") and the Grade 1 USTA International Spring Championships.

Zverev's early-season success in doubles proved to be the precursor of a major improvement in singles as well. During the European clay court season, he won his first Grade 1 title over [Andrey Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Rublev "Andrey Rublev") at the Open International Junior de Beaulieu-sur-Mer. He followed up that performance with his first Grade A title at the [Trofeo Bonfiglio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trofeo_Bonfiglio "Trofeo Bonfiglio") a month later, becoming the youngest boys' singles champion in the tournament's history. He also finished runner-up at the [2013 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Boys%27_singles "2013 French Open – Boys' singles") to [Cristian Garín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristian_Gar%C3%ADn "Cristian Garín"). Zverev had some grass court success as well, finishing runner-up to [Nick Kyrgios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Kyrgios "Nick Kyrgios") at the Junior International Roehampton. However, he needed to retire at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Boys%27_singles "2013 Wimbledon Championships – Boys' singles") due to a shoulder injury. Zverev came close to reaching another major boys' singles final at the [2013 Junior US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Boys%27_singles "2013 US Open – Boys' singles"), but was defeated by the eventual champion [Borna Ćorić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borna_%C4%86ori%C4%87 "Borna Ćorić") in the semifinals. This success was enough for him to take over the No. 1 ranking in late October. Before the end of the season, Zverev also represented Germany in the Junior Fed Cup, leading them to a fourth-place finish. His last tournament of the year was the Grade A [Orange Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Bowl_(tennis) "Orange Bowl (tennis)"), where he was defeated by [Stefan Kozlov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Kozlov "Stefan Kozlov") in the semifinals. As the top-ranked junior at the end of the season, he was named the [ITF Junior World Champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITF_World_Champion "ITF World Champion"), becoming the youngest boys' champion since [Donald Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Young_(tennis) "Donald Young (tennis)") in 2005.

Zverev played just two tournaments in 2014, both in Australia in January. He won the singles events at both tournaments, the first of which came against Australian [Omar Jasika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Jasika "Omar Jasika") at the Traralgon Junior International. At the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Boys%27_singles "2014 Australian Open – Boys' singles"), he was able to defeat Kozlov, who was seeded second, to finish his junior career with a first major title.

## Professional career

### 2011–14: ATP semifinal at 17

At the age of 14, Zverev entered qualifying at three different tournaments, including the [2011 Moselle Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Moselle_Open "2011 Moselle Open") on the ATP Tour, but lost all of his matches. He won his professional main draw debut against compatriot Christian Lichtenegger at a Futures event in Germany in August 2012. At the end of the year, he made his first professional final, finishing runner-up to Florian Reynet at an ITF $10K event in Florida. Zverev continued to focus on the juniors in 2013 and did not reach another pro-level final that year, but he did make his main draw debut on the ATP Tour in July, losing to [Roberto Bautista Agut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Bautista_Agut "Roberto Bautista Agut") at his hometown tournament, the [International German Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_International_German_Open "2013 International German Open"). He also made his [ATP Challenger Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Challenger_Tour "ATP Challenger Tour") debut, losing to [Máximo González](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1ximo_Gonz%C3%A1lez "Máximo González") at the [Meerbusch Challenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Maserati_Challenger "2013 Maserati Challenger") in August.

After winning the boys' singles title at the [2014 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Australian_Open "2014 Australian Open"), Zverev shifted his focus to his professional career, only playing in pro events the rest of the year. Initially, he struggled on the pro tour, failing to qualify for the main draw at his first five events of the season. He did not win a main draw match until he recorded a single victory at the [Heilbronner Neckarcup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Heilbronner_Neckarcup "2014 Heilbronner Neckarcup") Challenger, his tenth event of the year. One of his losses was a retirement against his brother Mischa. Zverev made his first professional breakthrough in July when he won the [Braunschweig Challenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Sparkassen_Open "2014 Sparkassen Open") for his first professional title, despite entering the tournament with just one career Challenger-level match win and no top 100 victories. Three of the players he defeated were in the top 100, including his first-round opponent No. 87 [Tobias Kamke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobias_Kamke "Tobias Kamke"), his semifinal opponent No. 56 [Andrey Golubev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Golubev "Andrey Golubev"), and his final opponent No. 89 [Paul-Henri Mathieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul-Henri_Mathieu "Paul-Henri Mathieu").

At the age of 17 years and 2 months, he became the youngest player to win a Challenger title since [Bernard Tomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Tomic "Bernard Tomic") in 2009 and the twelfth youngest in history. Zverev followed up this title with a breakthrough at the ATP Tour level. He entered the [International German Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_International_German_Open "2014 International German Open") having never won an ATP match but managed to reach the semifinals. He recorded four match wins at the event, including his first career victory against [Robin Haase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Haase "Robin Haase") and his first top 20 victory over No. 16 [Mikhail Youzhny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Youzhny "Mikhail Youzhny") before losing to No. 7 [David Ferrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ferrer "David Ferrer"). He became the first 17-year old to defeat a top 20 opponent since [Richard Gasquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Gasquet "Richard Gasquet") in 2004 and the first to make a semifinal since [Marin Čilić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marin_%C4%8Cili%C4%87 "Marin Čilić") in 2006. Zverev had risen from No. 665 to No. 285 after his Challenger title, and his [ATP 500 Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_500_Series "ATP 500 Series") semifinal appearance took him to No. 161 in the world. He finished the season ranked No. 136.

### 2015: ATP doubles final, top 100

At the beginning of the season, Zverev was still ranked too low to receive direct entry into the main draws of ATP Tour events, which forced him to continue playing Challenger events through July. He also needed to enter qualifying for ATP events throughout the year. Zverev did not qualify for either of the first two major singles events of the season. He was able to qualify for his first Masters event at the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Miami_Open "2015 Miami Open") and reached the second round. During the clay-court season, Zverev partnered with his brother Mischa to reach his first career ATP final in doubles at the [Bavarian International Tennis Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_BMW_Open "2015 BMW Open"). The Zverev brothers finished runner-up to top-seeded [Alexander Peya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Peya "Alexander Peya") and [Bruno Soares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Soares "Bruno Soares"). Later that month, he won his second career Challenger title at the [Heilbronn Neckarcup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Heilbronner_Neckarcup_%E2%80%93_Singles "2015 Heilbronner Neckarcup – Singles") to crack the top 100 of the ATP rankings.

With a higher ranking, Zverev was directly accepted into [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2015 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles"). A week before the tournament, he took part in the [Boodles Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Boodles_Challenge "2015 Boodles Challenge") exhibition and surprisingly upset world No. 1 [Novak Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic") in straight sets. Zverev backed up this performance in his Grand Slam tournament main draw debut, defeating [Teymuraz Gabashvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teymuraz_Gabashvili "Teymuraz Gabashvili") in a tight match that lasted until 9–7 in the fifth and final set. He lost in the next round to [Denis Kudla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Kudla "Denis Kudla"). Zverev opted to play on clay after Wimbledon and reached another ATP semifinal at the [Swedish Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Swedish_Open "2015 Swedish Open") to put him back in the top 100 after one week out. In August, Zverev returned to the United States and competed in two US Open Series events. He notably upset No. 14 [Kevin Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Anderson_(tennis) "Kevin Anderson (tennis)") at the [Citi Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Citi_Open "2015 Citi Open") en route to the quarterfinals. Zverev's brief rankings drop in July meant that he needed to qualify for the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2015 US Open – Men's singles"). He succeeded, but lost his opening round match to compatriot [Philipp Kohlschreiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philipp_Kohlschreiber "Philipp Kohlschreiber") in five sets. Zverev won just one more main draw match the rest of the season and ended the year ranked No. 83 in the world. As the youngest player in the top 100, he was named the [ATP Newcomer of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Newcomer_of_the_Year "ATP Newcomer of the Year").

### 2016: First ATP title, Federer upset, top 20

Zverev's first ATP event of the year was the [2016 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2016 Australian Open – Men's singles"), where he had a difficult draw against world No. 2 [Andy Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Murray "Andy Murray") and was only able to win six games in his opening match. He bounced back at the [Open Sud de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Open_Sud_de_France "2016 Open Sud de France"), reaching the semifinals in the singles event and his second career final with his brother in the doubles event. During the singles event, he upset No. 13 Marin Čilić, the highest-ranked player he had defeated in an official match at the time. Zverev then produced a strong result at the [Indian Wells Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_BNP_Paribas_Open "2016 BNP Paribas Open"), where he defeated two top 30 players. In the fourth round, he nearly upset No. 5 [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal "Rafael Nadal"). Zverev had a match point while he was serving for the final set, but missed a routine forehand volley and then proceeded to lose 14 of the remaining 15 points in the match. He commented on the match point that, "I missed probably the easiest shot I had the whole match." Back in Europe, Zverev was able to recover as he reached his first ATP singles final at the [Open de Nice Côte d'Azur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Open_de_Nice_C%C3%B4te_d%27Azur "2016 Open de Nice Côte d'Azur"), finishing runner-up to No. 15 [Dominic Thiem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominic_Thiem "Dominic Thiem"), the top seed and defending champion. Thiem also defeated him in the third round of the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2016 French Open – Men's singles").

> "I haven't really grasped it yet. That I would be standing here as a winner, I couldn't have imagined it yesterday... It's unbelievable to get a win against Roger, especially on grass."

— Zverev on his victory against Federer at the Halle Open.

Zverev's next breakthrough came during the grass court season at the [Halle Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Gerry_Weber_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2016 Gerry Weber Open – Singles"), where he upset world No. 3 [Roger Federer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Federer "Roger Federer") in the semifinals, ending Federer's streak of ten consecutive appearances in the final while competing at the tournament. He was also the first teenager to defeat Federer since Murray nearly a decade earlier. Nonetheless, he finished runner-up at the event to veteran compatriot [Florian Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florian_Mayer "Florian Mayer"). After this final, he entered the top 30 for the first time and stayed ranked in the 20s for the rest of the season. Despite being seeded at a major tournament for the first time, Zverev could then only match his best major singles result at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2016 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles"), again falling to Berdych. During the US Open Series, he reached the semifinals of the [Washington Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Citi_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2016 Citi Open – Men's singles"), but lost his opening round matches at both Masters events. He was then upset in the second round of the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2016 US Open – Men's singles") by [Dan Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Evans_(tennis) "Dan Evans (tennis)").

After the US Open, Zverev returned to Europe and won his first career ATP title at the [St. Petersburg Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_St._Petersburg_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2016 St. Petersburg Open – Singles"). He recorded his first win over No. 9 Berdych in the semifinals, and No. 3 [Stan Wawrinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stan_Wawrinka "Stan Wawrinka") in the final, coming back from 0–3 down in the third set. At his next event, he then defeated No. 10 Thiem in the first round of the [2016 China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_China_Open_(tennis) "2016 China Open (tennis)") for the first time in four tries this year. In doing so, Zverev became the first teenager to record three consecutive victories against top ten opponents since [Boris Becker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Becker "Boris Becker") in 1986. Zverev's third round appearance at the [Shanghai Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Shanghai_Rolex_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2016 Shanghai Rolex Masters – Singles") then helped him rise to No. 20 in the ATP rankings, making him the youngest player to debut in the top 20 since Novak Djokovic in 2006.

### 2017: Two Masters titles, world No. 3

During the 2017 season, Zverev greatly improved his results at the higher level tournaments on the ATP Tour except for the four major events. It was at this time Zverev, who had been using his nickname Sascha, stated in a postmatch interview with Pam Shriver, that he would no longer be referred to as Sascha. Zverev stated that only his close family can refer to him as Sascha, as he was born in Germany and identifies as a German, and does not want a Russian nickname to be used for his professional career. At the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Australian_Open "2017 Australian Open"), Zverev again pushed Rafael Nadal to the brink, but ultimately lost in five sets. His next ATP tournament was the [Open Sud de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Open_Sud_de_France "2017 Open Sud de France"), where he won both the singles and doubles events. He and his brother defeated [Fabrice Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabrice_Martin "Fabrice Martin") and [Daniel Nestor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Nestor "Daniel Nestor") for Alexander's first doubles title. The following month, he made his first Masters quarterfinal at the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2017 Miami Open – Men's singles"), upsetting No. 3 Stan Wawrinka along the way. Nick Kyrgios defeated him at both Masters events that month.

After a slow start to the clay court season, Zverev won two more titles in May, the first of which came at home in Germany at the [Bavarian International Tennis Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_BMW_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2017 BMW Open – Singles"). He then followed up a second Masters quarterfinal at the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Mutua_Madrid_Open "2017 Mutua Madrid Open") with his first Masters title at the [Rome Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Italian_Open_(tennis) "2017 Italian Open (tennis)"). He defeated Novak Djokovic in the final to become the youngest Masters champion since Djokovic in 2007 and the first such champion born in the 1990s. With the title, he also entered the top 10 for the first time. Despite his triumph in Rome, he lost his opening round match at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2017 French Open – Men's singles"). During the grass court season, Zverev made another singles final and again faced Federer at the [Halle Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Gerry_Weber_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2017 Gerry Weber Open – Singles"), but could not defeat him this time. He and his brother also finished runner-up in the doubles event to [Łukasz Kubot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%81ukasz_Kubot "Łukasz Kubot") and [Marcelo Melo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcelo_Melo "Marcelo Melo"). At [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2017 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles"), he achieved his best result at a major event to date, ultimately losing in the fourth round to the previous year's runner-up [Milos Raonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milos_Raonic "Milos Raonic") in a tight five-set match.

Back on hard courts, Zverev won his last two titles of the season in August. He won the [Washington Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Open_(tennis) "Washington Open (tennis)") as well as a second consecutive Masters title at the [Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Rogers_Cup "2017 Rogers Cup"), only dropping a single set at each tournament in each of his opening matches. Notably, he needed to save three match points in his first match in Canada against [Richard Gasquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Gasquet "Richard Gasquet"), including a 49 shot rally. He then defeated Roger Federer in the final to become the first player outside of the [Big Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Four_(tennis) "Big Four (tennis)") to win multiple Masters titles in the same season since [David Nalbandian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Nalbandian "David Nalbandian") in 2007. Despite this success, he was upset in the second round of the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Open_(tennis) "US Open (tennis)") by fellow Next Gen player [Borna Ćorić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borna_%C4%86ori%C4%87 "Borna Ćorić"). At the end of the season, Zverev qualified for both the inaugural [Next Generation Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Next_Generation_ATP_Finals "2017 Next Generation ATP Finals") as one of the top seven 21-and-under players, and the [ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_ATP_Finals "2017 ATP Finals") as one of the top eight players in the world. He opted to skip the former event to focus on the latter. At the ATP Finals, Zverev was grouped with Roger Federer, Marin Čilić, and [Jack Sock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sock "Jack Sock"). He defeated Čilić in his first match, but lost his final two matches and did not advance out of his round robin group. Zverev finished the year ranked No. 4, peaking at No. 3 right before the ATP Finals, and accumulated five ATP titles from just six finals.

### 2018: ATP Finals champion

Zverev maintained his top 5 ranking throughout the year. However, he still did not deliver any high-calibre results at the major tournaments. He was upset at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Australian_Open "2018 Australian Open") by reigning Next Gen Finals champion and No. 59 [Chung Hyeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chung_Hyeon "Chung Hyeon") in five sets in the third round. Zverev stated that issues at majors were "definitely not physical" when asked if his problems were physical or mental, and also attributed this lack of success to the extra pressure he was putting on himself at these events. Zverev did not reach his first final of the year until early April at the [Miami Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Miami_Open "2018 Miami Open"). Despite taking the first set of the final, he finished runner-up to American [John Isner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Isner "John Isner"), who had never previously won a Masters title.

Zverev continued his Masters success into the clay-court season, reaching the semifinals at the [Monte Carlo Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Monte-Carlo_Masters "2018 Monte-Carlo Masters"), winning his third career Masters title at the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Mutua_Madrid_Open "2018 Mutua Madrid Open"), and making the final at the [Rome Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Italian_Open_(tennis) "2018 Italian Open (tennis)"). In Madrid, he defeated Dominic Thiem in the final to become the only active player outside of the Big Four to have won three Masters titles. He came close to winning back-to-back Masters events, going up a break in the third set against Nadal at the Italian Open. However, Nadal was able to recover and win the final five games of the match after a rain delay. Zverev had also defended his title at the [Bavarian International Tennis Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_BMW_Open "2018 BMW Open") in his only other French Open tune-up to help build up a 13 match win streak that lasted until the Italian Open final. He capped off his excellent clay court season by reaching his first major quarterfinal at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_French_Open "2018 French Open"). He needed to win three five-set matches to get that far before Thiem ended his run while he was faced with a hamstring injury.

Up until the year-end championships, Zverev struggled to build on his early season success. He lost in the third round at both [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Wimbledon_Championships "2018 Wimbledon Championships") and the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_US_Open_(tennis) "2018 US Open (tennis)"), and his best result at the four remaining Masters events was a semifinal at the [Shanghai Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Shanghai_Rolex_Masters "2018 Shanghai Rolex Masters"). He was able to defend his title at the [Washington Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Citi_Open "2018 Citi Open"), his only title during this period. Zverev also reached two more doubles finals with his brother, but did not win either of them. At the end of the season, Zverev qualified for both the [Next Generation Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Next_Generation_ATP_Finals "2018 Next Generation ATP Finals") and the [ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_ATP_Finals "2018 ATP Finals") for the second consecutive year, again choosing to only compete at the latter event. He was placed in a group with Novak Djokovic, Marin Čilić, and John Isner. This year, Zverev was able to advance out of the group, only losing to No. 1 Djokovic in the round robin. He faced Federer in the semifinals and defeated him in straight sets to set up a rematch with Djokovic. Despite being a heavy underdog and having just lost to Djokovic earlier in the week, Zverev won the final in straight sets for the tenth and biggest title of his career. He became the youngest tour champion since Djokovic a decade earlier and the first German to win the season-ending championships since Boris Becker in 1995. This was also Zverev's first victory over a current world No. 1 player.

### 2019: Sixth Masters final

Zverev opened his [2019 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_ATP_Tour "2019 ATP Tour") with a fourth round appearance at the [2019 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2019 Australian Open – Men's singles"), defeating [Jérémy Chardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%A9r%C3%A9my_Chardy "Jérémy Chardy") in five sets in the second round before losing to [Milos Raonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milos_Raonic "Milos Raonic") in straight sets. Zverev next played at the [Mexican Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Abierto_Mexicano_Telcel "2019 Abierto Mexicano Telcel") and finished runner-up to [Nick Kyrgios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Kyrgios "Nick Kyrgios"). Following this tournament, he did not win more than one match at any of his next six events, a streak that ended with two match wins at the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Mutua_Madrid_Open "2019 Mutua Madrid Open"). Nonetheless, Zverev finished the clay court season strong. The week before the French Open, he won his only title of the year at the [Geneva Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Geneva_Open "2019 Geneva Open"), defeating [Nicolás Jarry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Jarry "Nicolás Jarry") in the final after saving two match points in the third set tiebreak. He then went on to defeat No. 12 [Fabio Fognini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabio_Fognini "Fabio Fognini") and reach his second consecutive quarterfinal at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2019 French Open – Men's singles"), where he lost to [Novak Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic"). Zverev did not follow through on that success into the grass court season, where his best result was a quarterfinal at the [Halle Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Halle_Open "2019 Halle Open"). He was upset in the first round at both the [Stuttgart Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_MercedesCup "2019 MercedesCup") and [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2019 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles"), the latter of which to qualifier [Jiří Veselý](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ji%C5%99%C3%AD_Vesel%C3%BD "Jiří Veselý"). Zverev attributed his early season struggles to being distracted by a legal dispute with his former agent [Patricio Apey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patricio_Apey "Patricio Apey"), with whom he had split in the offseason.

Zverev began to turn his season around following Wimbledon. He reached the semifinals of the [German Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Hamburg_European_Open "2019 Hamburg European Open") and the quarterfinals at the [Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Rogers_Cup "2019 Rogers Cup"). He then reached the last 16 at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2019 US Open – Men's singles") for the first time, where he lost to seed No. 20 [Diego Schwartzman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Schwartzman "Diego Schwartzman") to continue his lack of success at the major tournaments. He had also fallen out of the top 5 in early August for the first time in nearly two years. Zverev's best result of the season came at the [Shanghai Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Shanghai_Masters "2019 Shanghai Masters"). He upset No. 3 [Roger Federer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Federer "Roger Federer") in the quarterfinals before finishing runner-up to No. 4 [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev "Daniil Medvedev"), who was playing in his sixth consecutive final. This performance helped him qualify for the [ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_ATP_Finals "2019 ATP Finals") at the end of the year. At the event, Zverev was drawn into a round-robin group with [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal "Rafael Nadal"), [Stefanos Tsitsipas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefanos_Tsitsipas "Stefanos Tsitsipas"), and Daniil Medvedev. He defeated Nadal for the first time in his opening match before losing to Tsitsipas. He then won his match against Medvedev, which he had needed to win to advance via the tiebreak criteria. Nonetheless, he could not defend his title, losing to [Dominic Thiem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominic_Thiem "Dominic Thiem") in the semifinals. Zverev finished the season at No. 7 in the world.

### 2020: US Open final

Zverev began his [2020 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_ATP_Tour "2020 ATP Tour") at the inaugural [ATP Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_ATP_Cup "2020 ATP Cup"). Germany was in Group F alongside Australia, Greece, and Canada. Against Australia, he lost to [Alex de Minaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_de_Minaur "Alex de Minaur") in three sets. Australia won the tie over Germany 3–0. Against Greece, he was defeated by [Stefanos Tsitsipas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefanos_Tsitsipas "Stefanos Tsitsipas"). Germany won the tie over Greece 2–1. Against Canada, he was beaten by [Denis Shapovalov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Shapovalov "Denis Shapovalov"). Canada won the tie over Germany 2–1. Germany ended third in Group F. Seeded seventh at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2020 Australian Open – Men's singles"), he first defeated [Marco Cecchinato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Cecchinato "Marco Cecchinato") in the first round in straight sets. After the match, he pledged to donate all his prize money from the tournament to relief efforts for the [ongoing bushfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Australian_bushfire_season "2019–20 Australian bushfire season") if he won the title, a total of A$4.12 million. He then defeated [Egor Gerasimov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egor_Gerasimov "Egor Gerasimov"), [Fernando Verdasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Verdasco "Fernando Verdasco"), and 17th seed [Andrey Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Rublev "Andrey Rublev") to reach the quarterfinals without dropping a set. He defeated 15th seed and 2014 champion, [Stan Wawrinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stan_Wawrinka "Stan Wawrinka"), in the quarterfinals in four sets to reach his first major singles semifinal. He fell in his semifinal match to fifth seed, [Dominic Thiem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominic_Thiem "Dominic Thiem"), in four sets.

During the week of 24 February, Zverev played at the [Mexican Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Abierto_Mexicano_Telcel_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2020 Abierto Mexicano Telcel – Men's singles"). Seeded second, he was upset in his second-round match by qualifier [Tommy Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Paul_(tennis) "Tommy Paul (tennis)"). Starting from 12 March through early August, the ATP suspended tournament play due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"). At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2020 US Open – Men's singles"), Zverev was seeded fifth. He defeated [Kevin Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Anderson_(tennis) "Kevin Anderson (tennis)") in four sets, then beat 19-year old Brandon Nakashima in four sets. In the third round, he beat [Adrian Mannarino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Mannarino "Adrian Mannarino") in four sets before beating [Alejandro Davidovich Fokina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Davidovich_Fokina "Alejandro Davidovich Fokina") in straight sets. In the quarterfinals, he beat [Borna Ćorić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borna_%C4%86ori%C4%87 "Borna Ćorić") in four sets to reach his second major semifinal. There, he lost the first two sets to [Pablo Carreño Busta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Carre%C3%B1o_Busta "Pablo Carreño Busta"), but came back to win the match in five; this was his first-ever match win from two sets down. He thus advanced to his first major final, where he faced [Dominic Thiem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominic_Thiem "Dominic Thiem"). He became the runner-up, losing the final in a final-set tiebreaker despite leading by two sets and twice coming within two points of the title in the final set.

Zverev was seeded 6th at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2020 French Open – Men's singles"). He beat [Dennis Novak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Novak "Dennis Novak"), [Pierre-Hugues Herbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-Hugues_Herbert "Pierre-Hugues Herbert"), and [Marco Cecchinato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Cecchinato "Marco Cecchinato"), before losing in four sets to [Jannik Sinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jannik_Sinner "Jannik Sinner"). In October, he won two consecutive ATP 250 events in Cologne, which took place in the [2020 ATP Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_ATP_Tour "2020 ATP Tour") because of the cancellation of several tournaments due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"). In the finals, he defeated [Félix Auger-Aliassime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Auger-Aliassime "Félix Auger-Aliassime") and [Diego Schwartzman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Schwartzman "Diego Schwartzman"), both in straight sets. Zverev then reached the final of the [Paris Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Rolex_Paris_Masters "2020 Rolex Paris Masters"), beating Nadal in straight sets in the semifinals, where he lost to [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev "Daniil Medvedev") in three sets. In the [ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_ATP_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2020 ATP Finals – Singles"), Zverev was eliminated in the group stage after a three-set win over [Diego Schwartzman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Schwartzman "Diego Schwartzman"), and straight-set losses to [Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic") and the eventual champion [Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev "Daniil Medvedev").

### 2021: Olympic gold, second ATP Finals title

Zverev began his season in February with the [ATP Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_ATP_Cup "2021 ATP Cup"), where he represented Germany with [Jan-Lennard Struff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan-Lennard_Struff "Jan-Lennard Struff"), [Kevin Krawietz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Krawietz "Kevin Krawietz") and [Andreas Mies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Mies "Andreas Mies") to reach the semi-finals. In his first Grand Slam tournament appearance at the 2021 [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2021 Australian Open – Men's singles"), he defeated [Marcos Giron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcos_Giron "Marcos Giron"), [Maxime Cressy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxime_Cressy "Maxime Cressy"), [Adrian Mannarino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Mannarino "Adrian Mannarino") and [Dušan Lajović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du%C5%A1an_Lajovi%C4%87 "Dušan Lajović"). His run ended in the quarterfinals, where he lost to the eventual champion and world No. 1 Novak Djokovic. In March his first tournament was in [Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_ABN_AMRO_World_Tennis_Tournament_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 ABN AMRO World Tennis Tournament – Singles"), where he lost in the first round to Kazakh [Alexander Bublik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Bublik "Alexander Bublik"). He continued his performances in [Acapulco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Abierto_Mexicano_Telcel_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 Abierto Mexicano Telcel – Singles"), where he won his 14th ATP title by defeating Stefanos Tsitsipas in the final. At the tournament in [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2021 Miami Open – Men's singles"), he lost in the second round to Finland's [Emil Ruusuvuori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Ruusuvuori "Emil Ruusuvuori").

At his first tournament in 2021 on clay in [Monte Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Monte-Carlo_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 Monte-Carlo Masters – Singles"), Zverev finished in the third round, losing to [David Goffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Goffin "David Goffin"). In Munich, he reached the quarterfinals, losing to the 107th ranked tennis player in the world, [Ilya Ivashka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_Ivashka "Ilya Ivashka"). At the Madrid Open, Zverev defeated Rafael Nadal, Dominic Thiem, and [Matteo Berrettini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Berrettini "Matteo Berrettini") en route to his fourth Masters 1000 title and 15th career title. At the same tournament, he also reached his first semifinal in doubles at a Masters 1000, partnering with compatriot [Tim Pütz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_P%C3%BCtz "Tim Pütz"), but withdrew from the match. He reached the quarterfinals of the Masters 1000 in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2021 Italian Open – Men's singles"), losing to eventual tournament winner Rafael Nadal. In his second Grand Slam tournament of the year – in [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2021 French Open – Men's singles"), where he was seeded No. 6 – he won against [Oscar Otte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Otte "Oscar Otte"), [Roman Safiullin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Safiullin "Roman Safiullin"), [Laslo Đere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laslo_%C4%90ere "Laslo Đere"), [Kei Nishikori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kei_Nishikori "Kei Nishikori") and [Alejandro Davidovich Fokina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Davidovich_Fokina "Alejandro Davidovich Fokina"). In the semifinal, he played Stefanos Tsitsipas, to whom he lost after a 5-set battle.

Zverev's first appearance on grass started with a tournament at home in [Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Halle_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 Halle Open – Singles") – he lost in the second round to the eventual tournament winner [Ugo Humbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugo_Humbert "Ugo Humbert"). At [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2021 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles"), seeded No. 4, he equaled his best achievement at the tournament to date – reaching the fourth round for a second time. Along the way he defeated [Tallon Griekspoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallon_Griekspoor "Tallon Griekspoor"), [Tennys Sandgren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennys_Sandgren "Tennys Sandgren") and [Taylor Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Fritz "Taylor Fritz"). He was defeated in the fourth round by [Félix Auger-Aliassime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Auger-Aliassime "Félix Auger-Aliassime") in five sets. Following this run he returned to the top 5 after 2 years on 12 July 2021. At the [Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "Tennis at the 2020 Summer Olympics – Men's singles"), Zverev beat [Yen-hsun Lu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yen-hsun_Lu "Yen-hsun Lu"), [Daniel Elahi Galán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Elahi_Gal%C3%A1n "Daniel Elahi Galán"), [Nikoloz Basilashvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikoloz_Basilashvili "Nikoloz Basilashvili") and [Jérémy Chardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%A9r%C3%A9my_Chardy "Jérémy Chardy") to reach the semifinals. In the semifinals, despite being a set and a break down against world No. 1 [Novak Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic"), Zverev rallied to break back and won eight games in a row on his way to win in three sets. He defeated [Karen Khachanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Khachanov "Karen Khachanov") in straight sets in the final to claim the Olympic gold medal. Zverev became the first German man to win a gold medal in singles and the first to win a medal since [Tommy Haas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Haas "Tommy Haas") won his silver medal at the 2000 Sydney Olympics.

Following the Olympics, Zverev reached his second Masters 1000 final of the year at the [2021 Western & Southern Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Western_%26_Southern_Open "2021 Western & Southern Open") after defeating [Lloyd Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Harris_(tennis) "Lloyd Harris (tennis)"), [Guido Pella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guido_Pella "Guido Pella"), [Casper Ruud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casper_Ruud "Casper Ruud") and [Stefanos Tsitsipas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefanos_Tsitsipas "Stefanos Tsitsipas") after a close three-set battle in the semifinal. Zverev had not won a match at the tournament in six prior appearances. He beat [Andrey Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Rublev "Andrey Rublev") for the 17th title of his career and the fourth of the season, in 59 minutes, the shortest match in the tournament history. At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2021 US Open – Men's singles"), Zverev sought to claim his first major title following his run to the final the previous year. He reached the semifinals following wins over [Jack Sock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sock "Jack Sock") and [Lloyd Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Harris_(tennis) "Lloyd Harris (tennis)"). There, he lost to Novak Djokovic in five sets despite taking the first set, ending his career-high 16-match win streak stretching back to the Olympics.

Zverev was seeded 3rd at the [2021 BNP Paribas Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2021 BNP Paribas Open – Men's singles"), where he reached the quarterfinals. On the way, he defeated [Jenson Brooksby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenson_Brooksby "Jenson Brooksby"), [Andy Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Murray "Andy Murray") and [Gaël Monfils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ga%C3%ABl_Monfils "Gaël Monfils"). He lost to [Taylor Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Fritz "Taylor Fritz"), despite having two match points. At the [Erste Bank Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erste_Bank_Open "Erste Bank Open") in Vienna, seeded second, he defeated [Filip Krajinović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filip_Krajinovi%C4%87 "Filip Krajinović") and [Alex de Minaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_de_Minaur "Alex de Minaur") to reach the quarterfinals. The victory over De Minaur gave Zverev his 300th ATP tour match win. He then beat [Félix Auger-Aliassime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Auger-Aliassime "Félix Auger-Aliassime") and [Carlos Alcaraz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Alcaraz "Carlos Alcaraz") to reach the final where he won his fifth title of the year and 18th overall, defeating [Frances Tiafoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Tiafoe "Frances Tiafoe") in straight sets.

Zverev was the fourth seed at the [2021 Rolex Paris Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Rolex_Paris_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 Rolex Paris Masters – Singles"). He received a bye into the second round, where he defeated [Dušan Lajović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du%C5%A1an_Lajovi%C4%87 "Dušan Lajović"). He defeated next sixteenth seed [Grigor Dimitrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigor_Dimitrov "Grigor Dimitrov") in the third round and sixth seed [Casper Ruud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casper_Ruud "Casper Ruud") in the quarterfinals. He lost in the semifinals to second seed [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev "Daniil Medvedev") in straight sets. As a result of this run he equaled his singles career-high ranking of World No. 3 on 8 November 2021. For a fifth successive season, Zverev qualified for the [2021 ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_ATP_Finals "2021 ATP Finals") in Turin, as the third seed. He began his campaign by defeating home favourite [Matteo Berrettini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Berrettini "Matteo Berrettini") who retired with an injury. On his way to the title he defeated the top two seeds [Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic") and [Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev "Daniil Medvedev") in the semifinals and finals respectively. Due to this win, he became the only player active to have won multiple [ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Finals "ATP Finals") titles (2) other than Djokovic and Roger Federer, with 7 and 6, respectively.

### 2022: World No. 2, ankle injury

Zverev started his 2022 season by representing Germany at the [ATP Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_ATP_Cup "2022 ATP Cup"). Germany was in Group C alongside Great Britain, the US, and Canada. Against Great Britain, he beat [Cameron Norrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Norrie "Cameron Norrie"). Great Britain ended up winning the tie over Germany 2–1. Against the US, he defeated [Taylor Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Fritz "Taylor Fritz"). Germany won the tie 2–1 over the USA. Against Canada, he lost to [Félix Auger-Aliassime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Auger-Aliassime "Félix Auger-Aliassime") in three sets. Canada won the tie over Germany 2–1. Germany not only failed to qualify for the semi-finals, but they also ended third in Group C. Seeded third at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 Australian Open – Men's singles"), he reached the fourth round where he was defeated by 14th seed, [Denis Shapovalov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Shapovalov "Denis Shapovalov"), in straight sets.

After the Australian Open, Zverev played at the [Open Sud de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Open_Sud_de_France_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 Open Sud de France – Singles"). As the top seed, he made it to the final where he lost to sixth seed [Alexander Bublik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Bublik "Alexander Bublik"). Seeded second at the [Mexican Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Abierto_Mexicano_Telcel_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 Abierto Mexicano Telcel – Singles"), Zverev played in what was the record latest match ever played, defeating [Jenson Brooksby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenson_Brooksby "Jenson Brooksby") at 4:55 A.M. He was then defaulted from the tournament ahead of his following match against compatriot, [Peter Gojowczyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Gojowczyk "Peter Gojowczyk"), for violently and repeatedly hitting the umpire's chair with his racket after his loss in doubles. Representing Germany in the [Davis Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Davis_Cup_qualifying_round "2022 Davis Cup qualifying round") tie against Brazil, he won both of his matches by beating [Thiago Seyboth Wild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiago_Seyboth_Wild "Thiago Seyboth Wild") and [Thiago Monteiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiago_Monteiro_(tennis) "Thiago Monteiro (tennis)"). Germany ended up winning the tie over Brazil 3–1 to qualify for the Davis Cup Finals. Seeded third at the [Indian Wells Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 BNP Paribas Open – Men's singles"), he was stunned in his second-round match by [Tommy Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Paul_(tennis) "Tommy Paul (tennis)"). However, in [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_doubles "2022 BNP Paribas Open – Men's doubles"), he and partner, [Andrey Golubev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Golubev "Andrey Golubev"), reached the semifinals where they lost to eventual champions [John Isner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Isner "John Isner") and [Jack Sock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sock "Jack Sock"). Seeded second at the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 Miami Open – Men's singles"), he reached the quarterfinals where he lost to sixth seed, world No. 8, and eventual finalist, [Casper Ruud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casper_Ruud "Casper Ruud"), in three sets.

Zverev started his clay-court season at the [Monte-Carlo Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Monte-Carlo_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 Monte-Carlo Masters – Singles"). Seeded second, he reached the semifinals after beating ninth seed and world No. 12, Jannik Sinner, in the quarterfinals in a three-set thriller. He lost in the semifinals to third seed, world No. 5, defending champion, and eventual champion, [Stefanos Tsitsipas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefanos_Tsitsipas "Stefanos Tsitsipas"), in straight sets. As the top seed at the [BMW Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_BMW_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 BMW Open – Singles") in Munich, he was upset in the second round by world No. 70 and eventual champion, [Holger Rune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holger_Rune "Holger Rune"). Seeded second at the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 Mutua Madrid Open – Men's singles"), he returned to defend his title. He reached the semifinals with a straight set win over eighth seed and world No. 10, [Félix Auger-Aliassime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Auger-Aliassime "Félix Auger-Aliassime"), in the quarterfinals, after a tough three sets match win over [Marin Čilić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marin_%C4%8Cili%C4%87 "Marin Čilić") in the second round and [Lorenzo Musetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Musetti "Lorenzo Musetti")'s retirement in the third round due to a leg injury. In the semifinals, he took revenge on fourth seed, world No. 5, two-time Monte-Carlo champion, and 2019 finalist, Stefanos Tsitsipas, to reach his third final at this Masters 1000 event. He improved his ATP Head2Head record to 4–7 against Tsitsipas, earning his first clay-court win against him in the process. In the final, he was defeated by seventh seed and world No. 9, [Carlos Alcaraz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Alcaraz "Carlos Alcaraz"). Seeded second at the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 Italian Open – Men's singles"), he reached the semifinals defeating [Cristian Garín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristian_Gar%C3%ADn "Cristian Garín") in the quarterfinals. In the semifinals, he faced fourth seed, Stefanos Tsitsipas, again for the third consecutive time at this Masters level in the clay court season. He lost his semifinal match to Tsitsipas in three sets.

Seeded third at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 French Open – Men's singles"), Zverev matched his semifinal result from the previous year, defeating sixth seed, Carlos Alcaraz, in the quarterfinals in what was his first top-10 victory at a major after 12 attempts. In his semifinal match against fifth seed, former world No. 1, and 13 time Roland Garros champion, [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal "Rafael Nadal"), he retired 3 hours and 13 minutes into the match after [rolling his right ankle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sprained_ankle "Sprained ankle") and tearing all three lateral ligaments. He was rolled off the court in a wheelchair. Zverev announced that the injury would cause him to miss the [2022 Wimbledon Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Wimbledon_Championships "2022 Wimbledon Championships") and on 8 June 2022 underwent surgery to repair the torn ligaments in his ankle. Despite his exit at Roland Garros, he reached a career-high ranking of world No. 2 on 13 June 2022. In September, he was due to return for the [Davis Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Davis_Cup "2022 Davis Cup") play but suffered a new injury, a bone edema, and withdrew from competition for the rest of the season. Zverev ended the year ranked 12, his lowest in five years, when he debuted in the top 10 and remained a fixture there.

### 2023: French Open semifinals, Hamburg title

Zverev returned to the tour for the first time since his ankle injury by participating at the inaugural [United Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_United_Cup "2023 United Cup") where he lost in straight sets to [Jiří Lehečka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ji%C5%99%C3%AD_Lehe%C4%8Dka "Jiří Lehečka") and [Taylor Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Fritz "Taylor Fritz"). Seeded 12th at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 Australian Open – Men's singles"), he won his first match since June 2022 by beating lucky loser, [Juan Pablo Varillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Pablo_Varillas "Juan Pablo Varillas"), in the first round in five sets. He was defeated in the second round by American and lucky loser, [Michael Mmoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Mmoh "Michael Mmoh"), in four sets.

After the Australian Open, Zverev represented Germany in the [Davis Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Davis_Cup_qualifying_round "2023 Davis Cup qualifying round") tie against Switzerland. He won his first match over [Stan Wawrinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stan_Wawrinka "Stan Wawrinka"). He lost his second match to [Marc-Andrea Hüsler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc-Andrea_H%C3%BCsler "Marc-Andrea Hüsler"). Switzerland ended up winning the tie over Germany 3–2. Seeded eighth at the [Rotterdam Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_ABN_AMRO_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 ABN AMRO Open – Singles"), he was eliminated from the tournament in the second round by Dutch wildcard, [Tallon Griekspoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallon_Griekspoor "Tallon Griekspoor"), in three sets. Seeded fourth at the [Qatar ExxonMobil Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Qatar_ExxonMobil_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 Qatar ExxonMobil Open – Singles"), he fell in the second round to wildcard, former world No. 1, two-time champion, and eventual finalist, [Andy Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Murray "Andy Murray"). In March, he played at the [Dubai Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Dubai_Tennis_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 Dubai Tennis Championships – Men's singles"). Seeded seventh, he reached the semifinals where he lost to second seed, defending champion, and eventual finalist, [Andrey Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Rublev "Andrey Rublev"). Zverev began his early U.S. hard court campaign at the [Indian Wells Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 BNP Paribas Open – Men's singles"). Seeded 12th, he reached the fourth round but lost to fifth seed, world No. 6, and eventual finalist, [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev "Daniil Medvedev"), in three close sets. Seeded 13th at the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 Miami Open – Men's singles"), he was beaten in the second round by wildcard [Taro Daniel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taro_Daniel "Taro Daniel").

Zverev began his clay-court season at the [Monte-Carlo Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Monte-Carlo_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 Monte-Carlo Masters – Singles"). Seeded 13th, he earned his first victory over [Alexander Bublik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Bublik "Alexander Bublik") by beating him in the first round in three sets. In the second round, he defeated world No. 29, [Roberto Bautista Agut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Bautista_Agut "Roberto Bautista Agut"), in the second round. In the third round, he lost to third seed, Daniil Medvedev, despite serving for the match twice and having two match points in the third-set tie-break. This defeat was his seventh out of the last eight matches played with Medvedev. Seeded third at the [BMW Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_BMW_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 BMW Open – Singles") in Munich, he was defeated in the second round by [Christopher O'Connell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_O%27Connell "Christopher O'Connell"). Seeded 13th at the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 Mutua Madrid Open – Men's singles"), he recorded his 350th career win by defeating [Roberto Carballés Baena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Carball%C3%A9s_Baena "Roberto Carballés Baena") in the second round, becoming the only player under 30 to reach this milestone. In the third round, he overpowered qualifier, [Hugo Grenier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Grenier "Hugo Grenier"), in 55 minutes. In the fourth round, he lost to top seed, world No. 2, defending champion, and eventual champion, [Carlos Alcaraz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Alcaraz "Carlos Alcaraz"), in a rematch of the [previous year's final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 Mutua Madrid Open – Men's singles"). Failing to defend his finalist points from last year, his ranking fell from 16 to 22. Seeded 19th at the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 Italian Open – Men's singles"), he made it to the fourth round where he lost to third seed and eventual champion, Daniil Medvedev. Having made the semifinals last year, his ranking again fell, this time from 22 to 27. Seeded third at the [Geneva Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Geneva_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 Geneva Open – Singles"), he reached the semifinal where he lost to [Nicolás Jarry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Jarry "Nicolás Jarry"), who would end up winning the tournament. Seeded 22nd at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 French Open – Men's singles"), he defeated [Lloyd Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Harris_(tennis) "Lloyd Harris (tennis)"), [Alex Molčan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Mol%C4%8Dan "Alex Molčan"), 12th seed [Frances Tiafoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Tiafoe "Frances Tiafoe"), and 28th seed [Grigor Dimitrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigor_Dimitrov "Grigor Dimitrov") in his first four rounds. In the quarterfinals, he faced [Tomás Martín Etcheverry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1s_Mart%C3%ADn_Etcheverry "Tomás Martín Etcheverry"). He prevailed over Etcheverry in four sets to reach his third consecutive semi-final at this tournament. He fell in his semi-final match to world No. 4 and last year finalist, [Casper Ruud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casper_Ruud "Casper Ruud"), in straight sets.

Zverev began his grass season by withdrawing from the [BOSS Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_BOSS_Open "2023 BOSS Open") in Stuttgart due to a thigh injury that he picked up during his semifinal match at Roland Garros. Zverev then defeated [Dominic Thiem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominic_Thiem "Dominic Thiem"), [Denis Shapovalov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Shapovalov "Denis Shapovalov"), and [Nicolas Jarry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Jarry "Nicolás Jarry") in [Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Halle_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 Halle Open – Singles") to reach the semifinals where he lost to eventual champion [Alexander Bublik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Bublik "Alexander Bublik"). At [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Wimbledon_Championships "2023 Wimbledon Championships"), Zverev lost in third round to [Matteo Berrettini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Berrettini "Matteo Berrettini"). In [Båstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Swedish_Open "2023 Swedish Open"), Zverev lost in the quarterfinals to Andrey Rublev. Zverev won the [Hamburg European Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Hamburg_European_Open "2023 Hamburg European Open"), defeating [Laslo Djere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laslo_Djere "Laslo Djere") in the final. His triumph marked his 20th career ATP Title.

During his [2023 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_US_Open_(tennis) "2023 US Open (tennis)") match against Jannik Sinner, Zverev halted play and complained to officials in response to a fan shouting the opening stanza to "Deutschlandlied", a song which has served as the national anthem of Germany; the use of the first and second stanza are presently commonly discouraged because of history. Alexander Zverev interpreted this as carrying Nazi sentiment and told officials that the fan had shouted, "the most famous Hitler phrase". Zverev lost the quarterfinals match against Carlos Alcaraz after winning a 4-hour 40 minutes five-set match against Jannik Sinner. This match, in combination with the Roland Garros 2023 semi-final, cemented Zverev's return to top form.

His performance in the Asian swing was marked by his 21st title in [Chengdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Chengdu_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 Chengdu Open – Singles"), defeating [Roman Safiullin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Safiullin "Roman Safiullin") in three sets. He followed this result with a semifinals showing in [Beijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_China_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 China Open – Men's singles"), losing to [Danill Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev "Daniil Medvedev") in their 5th meeting of the year. Unable to maintain his form, he lost in the second round of the [Shanghai Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Rolex_Shanghai_Masters "2023 Rolex Shanghai Masters") against [Roman Safiullin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Safiullin "Roman Safiullin"), where he was the defending finalist from [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Rolex_Shanghai_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2019 Rolex Shanghai Masters – Singles"), and lost to [Jordan Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Thompson_(tennis) "Jordan Thompson (tennis)") in [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Japan_Open_Tennis_Championships_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 Japan Open Tennis Championships – Singles"). During the European indoor hardcourt season, Zverev made the quarterfinals in [Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Erste_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 Erste Bank Open – Singles") before losing to [Andrey Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Rublev "Andrey Rublev") in three sets and reached the third round in [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Rolex_Paris_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 Rolex Paris Masters – Singles"), losing to [Stefanos Tsitsipas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefanos_Tsitsipas "Stefanos Tsitsipas"). Despite this, he qualified for the [ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_ATP_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 ATP Finals – Singles") for the first time since [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_ATP_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 ATP Finals – Singles"). At the event, he was drawn in the red group and defeated [Carlos Alcaraz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Alcaraz "Carlos Alcaraz") in three sets and [Andrey Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Rublev "Andrey Rublev") in straight sets whilst losing to Medvedev. He failed to qualify for the semifinals after Alcaraz and Medvedev were more successful in terms of win-loss percentage in sets, ending his 2023 season.

### 2024: United Cup champion, two Masters titles and French Open final

Alongside Team Germany, Zverev competed and won the United Cup, saving two championship points in his singles match against [Hubert Hurkacz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Hurkacz "Hubert Hurkacz") and defeating Team Poland in the final. In [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 Australian Open – Men's singles"), he reached the quarterfinals of the [2024 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 Australian Open – Men's singles"), defeating [Dominik Koepfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominik_Koepfer "Dominik Koepfer"), [Lukas Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luk%C3%A1%C5%A1_Klein "Lukáš Klein") for his 400th career win, [Alex Michelsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Michelsen "Alex Michelsen"), and [Cameron Norrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Norrie "Cameron Norrie"), with two matches requiring deciding set tiebreakers. He then achieved his first-ever win against a top-5 player at a Grand Slam, defeating [Carlos Alcaraz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Alcaraz "Carlos Alcaraz") in four sets in the quarterfinals, to make his first Australian Open semifinal appearance since [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2020 Australian Open – Men's singles"). He subsequently lost to [Danill Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev "Daniil Medvedev"), despite being two sets to love up. His defeat marked only his second loss from being up two sets to love in a 5-set match, his first being against [Dominic Thiem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominic_Thiem "Dominic Thiem") in the final of the [2020 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2020 US Open – Men's singles").

In Mexico, Zverev reached the semifinals in [Los Cabos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Los_Cabos_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2024 Los Cabos Open – Singles") with straight sets wins against [Yoshihito Nishoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshihito_Nishioka "Yoshihito Nishioka") and [Thanasi Kokkinakis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanasi_Kokkinakis "Thanasi Kokkinakis") but lost to [Jordan Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Thompson_(tennis) "Jordan Thompson (tennis)") in a deciding set tiebreaker. In [Acapulco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Abierto_Mexicano_Telcel_%E2%80%93_Singles "2024 Abierto Mexicano Telcel – Singles"), Zverev lost to [Daniel Altmaier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Altmaier "Daniel Altmaier") in the first round. Seeded sixth at the [BNP Paribas Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_BNP_Paribas_Open "2024 BNP Paribas Open"), Zverev reached the quarterfinals for the first time since [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_BNP_Paribas_Open "2021 BNP Paribas Open"), defeating [Christopher O'Connell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_O%27Connell "Christopher O'Connell"), [Tallon Griekspoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallon_Griekspoor "Tallon Griekspoor"), and [Alex de Minaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_de_Minaur "Alex de Minaur") before losing to eventual champion [Carlos Alcaraz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Alcaraz "Carlos Alcaraz"). Seeded fourth at the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Miami_Open "2024 Miami Open"), Zverev reached the semifinals, defeating [Félix Auger-Aliassime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Auger-Aliassime "Félix Auger-Aliassime"), [Christopher Eubanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Eubanks "Christopher Eubanks"), [Karen Khachanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Khachanov "Karen Khachanov"), and [Fábián Marozsán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A1bi%C3%A1n_Marozs%C3%A1n "Fábián Marozsán") before losing to [Grigor Dimitrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigor_Dimitrov "Grigor Dimitrov"). This was his best result in Miami since reaching the final in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Miami_Open "2018 Miami Open").

Zverev began his 2024 clay season seeded fifth at the [Monte-Carlo Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Monte-Carlo_Masters "2024 Monte-Carlo Masters"), receiving a bye into the second round and defeating [Sebastian Ofner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Ofner "Sebastian Ofner") before losing to eventual champion [Stefanos Tsitsipas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefanos_Tsitsipas "Stefanos Tsitsipas") in the third round. As the top seed at the [BMW Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_BMW_Open "2024 BMW Open"), Zverev received a bye into the second round and beat [Jurij Rodionov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurij_Rodionov "Jurij Rodionov") before being upset by world No. 106 [Cristian Garín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristian_Gar%C3%ADn "Cristian Garín") in the quarterfinals. Seeded fourth in [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Mutua_Madrid_Open "2024 Mutua Madrid Open"), Zverev reached the fourth round, beating [Borna Ćorić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borna_%C4%86ori%C4%87 "Borna Ćorić") and [Denis Shapovalov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Shapovalov "Denis Shapovalov") before being upset by 21st seed [Francisco Cerúndolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Cer%C3%BAndolo "Francisco Cerúndolo"). Seeded third in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Italian_Open_(tennis) "2024 Italian Open (tennis)"), Zverev won his sixth Masters title, defeating [Aleksandar Vukic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandar_Vukic "Aleksandar Vukic"), [Nuno Borges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuno_Borges_(tennis) "Nuno Borges (tennis)"), [Taylor Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Fritz "Taylor Fritz"), [Alejandro Tabilo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Tabilo "Alejandro Tabilo"), and [Nicolás Jarry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Jarry "Nicolás Jarry") in the final. This was his best result in Rome since winning his first Masters 1000 title at the [2017 Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Italian_Open_(tennis) "2017 Italian Open (tennis)"), and his first Masters title since 2021.

At the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 French Open – Men's singles"), Zverev contested Rafael Nadal's final match at the French Open, defeating the 14-time champion in straight sets to record Nadal's fourth ever defeat at the event. He went on to reach the semifinals, winning over [David Goffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Goffin "David Goffin"), [Tallon Griekspoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallon_Griekspoor "Tallon Griekspoor"), [Holger Rune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holger_Rune "Holger Rune"), and [Alex de Minaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_de_Minaur "Alex de Minaur"). He then defeated two-time finalist [Casper Ruud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casper_Ruud "Casper Ruud") to reach his first Major final since the [2020 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2020 US Open – Men's singles") and his first on clay. In the deciding set of the final match, Alcaraz hit a second serve which was called out by the line judges. However, the chair umpire overruled the call, declaring the ball in. While electronic review later indicated the serve was out, Roland Garros does not use Hawk-Eye as an official officiating tool; instead, the technology is used exclusively for television broadcasts. It is noted that the broadcast version of Hawk-Eye operates with significantly lower precision than the official Electronic Line Calling (ELC) systems installed at other tournaments, leaving open the possibility that the umpire's ruling on the clay mark was accurate. However, the overrule generated controversy. Had the call not been overturned, Zverev would have broken back. Tennis commentator [Robbie Koenig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Koenig "Robbie Koenig") and former world No. 1 [Andy Roddick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Roddick "Andy Roddick") disagreed with the overrule. Zverev would go on to lose the fifth set, giving up his two-sets-to-one lead and finishing runner-up to [Carlos Alcaraz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Alcaraz "Carlos Alcaraz"), who collected his maiden French Open title.

In [Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Halle_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2024 Halle Open – Singles"), Zverev reached the semifinals after collecting wins against [Oscar Otte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Otte "Oscar Otte"), [Lorenzo Sonego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Sonego "Lorenzo Sonego"), and [Arthur Fils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Fils "Arthur Fils"), losing to [Hubert Hurkacz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Hurkacz "Hubert Hurkacz") in straight sets. At [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Wimbledon_Championships "2024 Wimbledon Championships"), Zverev beat [Roberto Carballés Baena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Carball%C3%A9s_Baena "Roberto Carballés Baena"), [Marcos Giron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcos_Giron "Marcos Giron"), and [Cameron Norrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Norrie "Cameron Norrie") before losing to [Taylor Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Fritz "Taylor Fritz") in the fourth round. His third-set tiebreak with Norrie was noted as one of the longest tiebreaks of the tournament, with Zverev prevailing 17–15 after 26 minutes. Back on clay, Zverev reached the final in [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Hamburg_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 Hamburg Open – Men's singles"), losing to [Arthur Fils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Fils "Arthur Fils") in three sets. At the [Olympic Games in Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "Tennis at the 2024 Summer Olympics – Men's singles"), Zverev represented [Team Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics "Germany at the 2024 Summer Olympics") as defending champion. He progressed to the quarterfinals after winning over [Jaume Munar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaume_Munar "Jaume Munar") of Spain, [Tomáš Macháč](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomas_Machac "Tomas Machac") of Czech Republic, and [Alexei Popyrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Popyrin "Alexei Popyrin") of Australia, all in straight sets. He was defeated in the quarterfinals by eventual bronze medalist [Lorenzo Musetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Musetti "Lorenzo Musetti") of Italy in straight sets.

In the American hard court swing, Zverev reached the quarterfinals in [Montréal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 National Bank Open – Men's singles"), losing to [Sebastian Korda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Korda "Sebastian Korda") in three sets. He went on to defend his semifinal points in [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Cincinnati_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 Cincinnati Open – Men's singles"), losing to Jannik Sinner in an attritive three-set match. At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 US Open – Men's singles"), he reached the quarterfinals without facing a seeded opponent, with wins over lucky loser [Maximilian Marterer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilian_Marterer "Maximilian Marterer"), wildcard [Alexandre Müller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_M%C3%BCller "Alexandre Müller"), [Tomás Martín Etcheverry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1s_Mart%C3%ADn_Etcheverry "Tomás Martín Etcheverry") and [Brandon Nakashima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandon_Nakashima "Brandon Nakashima") — his 450th career win. With that milestone, he became the second player born in the 1990s, after [Grigor Dimitrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigor_Dimitrov "Grigor Dimitrov"), to reach 450 ATP wins. With the win, he qualified for his seventh appearance at the [2024 ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_ATP_Finals "2024 ATP Finals"). Zverev lost in the quarterfinals to [Taylor Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Fritz "Taylor Fritz").

At the [Paris Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Rolex_Paris_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2024 Rolex Paris Masters – Singles"), Zverev defeated [Tallon Griekspoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallon_Griekspoor "Tallon Griekspoor"), [Arthur Fils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Fils "Arthur Fils"), and [Stefanos Tsitsipas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefanos_Tsitsipas "Stefanos Tsitsipas") to set up a semifinal clash with former champion [Holger Rune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holger_Rune "Holger Rune"). He won the match in straight sets to reach the final for the second time at this event. Zverev defeated home crowd favorite [Ugo Humbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugo_Humbert "Ugo Humbert") in the final to clinch his second Masters title of the season and the world No. 2 ranking. Zverev won all three of his group matches at the [ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_ATP_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2024 ATP Finals – Singles") to reach the semifinals, where he lost to [Taylor Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Fritz "Taylor Fritz") in a deciding set tie-break.

### 2025: Australian Open final, 500th win

Alongside Team Germany, Zverev failed to defend the United Cup after withdrawing from the event due to injury. Entering the [2025 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 Australian Open – Men's singles") as the second seed for the first time in his career, Zverev began his tournament with a dominant victory over 2019 semifinalist [Lucas Pouille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_Pouille "Lucas Pouille") in the first round. He reached his second consecutive and third overall semifinal at the Australian Open defeating [Pedro Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Mart%C3%ADnez_(tennis) "Pedro Martínez (tennis)"), [Jacob Fearnley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Fearnley "Jacob Fearnley"), [Ugo Humbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugo_Humbert "Ugo Humbert"), and [Tommy Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Paul_(tennis) "Tommy Paul (tennis)"). Against the 24-time major champion, Zverev won the 81-minute first set against Djokovic before the latter's retirement due to injury, advancing to his third major final. In the final, Zverev lost to defending champion Jannik Sinner in straight sets. Following his defeat to Sinner, a visibly distraught Zverev delivered an emotional speech during the trophy ceremony, stating, "I want to thank my team... I'm just not good enough. It's as simple as that." In the post-match press conference, he reflected on his career trajectory and the pressure of being a perennial contender without a Major title, reportedly remarking that he "didn't want to be the best player in history without a Grand Slam", expressing his desperation to finally break the barrier after losing his third major final.

Over the next two months, Zverev made quarterfinals at the South American events he played in, [Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Argentina_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 Argentina Open – Singles") and [Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Rio_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 Rio Open – Singles"), losing to Argentines [Francisco Cerundolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Cer%C3%BAndolo "Francisco Cerúndolo") and [Francisco Comesana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Comesa%C3%B1a "Francisco Comesaña"), respectively. Zverev then made only modest achievements in the succeeding weeks, losing to [Learner Tien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learner_Tien "Learner Tien") in the second round of the [Mexican Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Abierto_Mexicano_Telcel_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 Abierto Mexicano Telcel – Singles"), to [Tallon Griekspoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallon_Griekspoor "Tallon Griekspoor") in the second round of [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 BNP Paribas Open – Men's singles"), to [Arthur Fils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Fils "Arthur Fils") in the fourth round of the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 Miami Open – Men's singles"), and to [Matteo Berrettini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Berrettini "Matteo Berrettini") in [Monte-Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Monte-Carlo_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 Monte-Carlo Masters – Singles").

In April, Zverev won his 3rd title in [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_BMW_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 BMW Open – Singles") and 24th overall on his birthday, winning in straight sets over Ben Shelton. Zverev was presented his BMW trophy by [Dominic Thiem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominic_Thiem "Dominic Thiem"), and reclaimed the world No. 2 ranking from Alcaraz. The next week, he reached the round of 16 at the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 Mutua Madrid Open – Men's singles"). During his third round match, Zverev was given a code violation for taking a photo of the ball mark of a shot which Zverev considered to have been erroneously called in. Later, social media posts from the ATP Tour suggested that the Hawk-Eye call was correct and that Zverev likely took a picture of a different mark. Following this, Zverev failed to defend either his titles in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 Italian Open – Men's singles") and [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Hamburg_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 Hamburg Open – Men's singles") with a straight sets loss against an in-form [Lorenzo Musetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Musetti "Lorenzo Musetti") and a three-set loss against [Alexandre Müller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_M%C3%BCller "Alexandre Müller"), respectively. At the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 French Open – Men's singles") where he was defending finalist points, Zverev advanced to the quarterfinals, where he was defeated by [Novak Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic") in a four-set match.

On the grass, Zverev reached his third final of the year in [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_BOSS_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 BOSS Open – Singles"), losing to [Taylor Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Fritz "Taylor Fritz") in straight sets in his fifth consecutive loss against the American. He then reached the semifinals against [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev "Daniil Medvedev") in [Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Halle_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 Halle Open – Singles"), losing in three sets. Zverev then lost in the first round of [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles") to [Arthur Rinderknech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Rinderknech "Arthur Rinderknech") in five sets, which marked the first time he lost in the first round of a major since [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2019 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles"). Zverev reached the semifinals for the first time in [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 National Bank Open – Men's singles") since [winning the title in 2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Rogers_Cup_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2017 Rogers Cup – Men's singles") after defeating the defending champion [Alexei Popyrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Popyrin "Alexei Popyrin") in the quarterfinals. He lost to the eventual finalist [Karen Khachanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Khachanov "Karen Khachanov"), despite holding a match point. He rebounded in [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Cincinnati_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 Cincinnati Open – Men's singles") by advancing to his 3rd consecutive semifinal in the American Midwest, which included wins against the recent finalists from Toronto the week before, Khachanov and [Ben Shelton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Shelton "Ben Shelton"), both of whom were notably exhausted from their Canadian campaigns. Zverev, also struggling and exhausted in his semifinal against Carlos Alcaraz, went on to lose in straight sets. In [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 US Open – Men's singles"), he was defeated in four sets by a resurgent [Félix Auger-Aliassime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Auger-Aliassime "Félix Auger-Aliassime"). Before leaving the United States, Zverev participated in the [Laver Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Laver_Cup "2025 Laver Cup") with Team Europe in San Francisco but lost to Team World.

In the Asian swing, Zverev was defeated by frequent rival [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev "Daniil Medvedev") in the quarterfinals in [Beijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_China_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 China Open – Men's singles"), and by eventual finalist [Arthur Rinderknech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Rinderknech "Arthur Rinderknech") in the third round in [Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Rolex_Shanghai_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 Rolex Shanghai Masters – Singles"). Zverev returned to form in the European indoor swing, but found himself obstructed by Jannik Sinner for the remainder of the season, losing to him in the final in [Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Erste_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 Erste Bank Open – Singles"), the semifinals of [Nanterre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Rolex_Paris_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 Rolex Paris Masters – Singles"), and a round-robin match in [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_ATP_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 ATP Finals – Singles"). At the [Davis Cup Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Davis_Cup_Finals "2025 Davis Cup Finals"), Zverev won both of his singles matches, but [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_Davis_Cup_team "Germany Davis Cup team") was defeated in the semifinal tie against Spain. He ended his season ranked world No. 3 behind Sinner and Alcaraz.

### 2026: French Open and US Open champion, first year-end No. 1

For the second consecutive year, Zverev and Team Germany failed to progress at the [United Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_Cup "2026 United Cup"). At the [2026 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2026 Australian Open – Men's singles"), Zverev progressed to his third consecutive semifinal, defeating [Cameron Norrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Norrie "Cameron Norrie"), [Francisco Cerúndolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Cer%C3%BAndolo "Francisco Cerúndolo"), and [Learner Tien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learner_Tien "Learner Tien") en route. In a rematch of their quarterfinal match in 2024, Zverev lost to eventual champion [Carlos Alcaraz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Alcaraz "Carlos Alcaraz") in a five-set epic semifinal lasting five hours and 27 minutes, ranking as the third longest match in Australian Open history. During the semifinal, Zverev dropped the first two sets, but rebounded to win the next two in consecutive tiebreaks to push the match to a deciding set, the first deciding set of the men's tournament on [Rod Laver Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_Laver_Arena "Rod Laver Arena"). In the fifth set, Zverev had an opportunity to serve for the match, but was broken twice to lose the match. Failing to defend his finalist points from [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 Australian Open – Men's singles"), he dropped to world No. 4 in the rankings, behind [Novak Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic").

In [Acapulco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Abierto_Mexicano_Telcel_%E2%80%93_Singles "2026 Abierto Mexicano Telcel – Singles"), Zverev lost in the second round of singles to [Miomir Kecmanovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miomir_Kecmanovi%C4%87 "Miomir Kecmanović"). However, in doubles, partnered with [Marcelo Melo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcelo_Melo "Marcelo Melo"), the pair took the doubles title, defeating [Alexander Erler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Erler "Alexander Erler") & [Robert Galloway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Galloway_(tennis) "Robert Galloway (tennis)") in the final. Zverev then made consecutive Masters 1000 semifinals at [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2026 BNP Paribas Open – Men's singles"), [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2026 Miami Open – Men's singles"), and [Monte-Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Monte-Carlo_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2026 Monte-Carlo Masters – Singles"), improving significantly on his results from the previous year. By reaching his first Indian Wells semifinal, he completed the semifinal set of all nine Masters 1000 tournaments, the first person outside of the [Big Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Three_(tennis) "Big Three (tennis)") to do so. He went on to lose all three semifinals to the eventual champion of all three events, Jannik Sinner, failing to win a set in any of the three matches and extending his ongoing losing streak to eight consecutive losses against the Italian. Zverev then failed to defend the title in [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_BMW_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2026 BMW Open – Singles"), being defeated by [Flavio Cobolli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flavio_Cobolli "Flavio Cobolli") in the semifinals. In [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2026 Mutua Madrid Open – Men's singles"), Zverev advanced to his fourth final in the [Magic Box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caja_M%C3%A1gica "Caja Mágica"), a first Masters 1000 final of the season for a chance at a third Madrid Open title. He went on to lose to Sinner again in straight sets. The following week, he was upset by home favourite [Luciano Darderi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luciano_Darderi "Luciano Darderi") at the round of 16 of the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2026 Italian Open – Men's singles"), ending his run of five successive Masters 1000 semifinals dating back to the [Paris Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Rolex_Paris_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 Rolex Paris Masters – Singles").

At the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_French_Open "2026 French Open"), Zverev won his maiden major title. He entered the tournament as the second seed, after two-time defending champion [Carlos Alcaraz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Alcaraz "Carlos Alcaraz") withdrew due to an ongoing wrist injury. Following Jannik Sinner's abrupt loss to [Juan Manuel Cerundolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Manuel_Cer%C3%BAndolo "Juan Manuel Cerúndolo") and three-time French Open champion [Novak Djokovic's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic") loss to [Joāo Fonseca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_Fonseca_(tennis) "João Fonseca (tennis)"), Zverev was widely labelled the tournament favourite, a label he distanced himself from as the tournament progressed. By reaching his sixth consecutive quarterfinal appearance in Paris, Zverev joined [Roger Federer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Federer "Roger Federer"), [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal "Rafael Nadal"), and Djokovic as the only men's singles players to achieve this feat. He then advanced to his second French Open final and fourth major final following wins against [Rafael Jódar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_J%C3%B3dar "Rafael Jódar") in the quarterfinals and [Jakub Menšík](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakub_Men%C5%A1%C3%ADk "Jakub Menšík") in the semifinal. In the final, Zverev defeated [Flavio Cobolli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flavio_Cobolli "Flavio Cobolli") in a five-set contest, his longest match of the tournament by duration and sets played. With his win, Zverev became the first German men's singles champion since [Henner Henkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henner_Henkel "Henner Henkel") in [1937](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1937_French_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "1937 French Championships – Men's singles"), ending an 89 year drought. He became the first German male to win a major since [Boris Becker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Becker "Boris Becker") in [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "1996 Australian Open – Men's singles") and joined [Michael Stich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Stich "Michael Stich") and Becker to become the third German men's singles player to win a Major. In addition, he became the first player, male or female, to win a Major with [Type 1 diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_1_diabetes "Type 1 diabetes").

Zverev rejoined the tour in [Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Halle_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2026 Halle Open – Singles"), reaching the semifinals just dropping one set to [Vit Kopřiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%ADt_Kop%C5%99iva "Vít Kopřiva") in the second round. He lost the semifinal match to eventual finalist [Taylor Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Fritz "Taylor Fritz") despite winning the first set, extending his ongoing losing streak against the American since [Wimbledon 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles") to seven consecutive losses. During the [2026 Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Wimbledon_Championships "2026 Wimbledon Championships"), Zverev reached back-to-back second weeks of a major, and after defeating this time Taylor Fritz in the quarterfinal, and [Arthur Fery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Fery "Arthur Fery") in the semifinal, he made it to his second consecutive appearance in a Grand Slam final.. In the final, he lost to Sinner in four sets.

Zverev lost his second round match to [Tallon Griekspoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallon_Griekspoor "Tallon Griekspoor") at the [National Bank Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bank_Open "National Bank Open") in 3 sets, then lost his 4th round match at the [Cincinnati Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati_Masters "Cincinnati Masters") to [Tommy Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Paul_(tennis) "Tommy Paul (tennis)") in 3 sets. During the [2026 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2026 US Open – Men's singles"), Zverev entered as the first seed following Jannik Sinner's withdrawal due to a knee injury. He nearly suffered a first-round defeat, being 2 points away from losing to [Lorenzo Sonego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Sonego "Lorenzo Sonego") in the 4th set while down two sets to one, and serving at 4-5, 30-30. He came back to win the match in 5 sets, and won his second round match against [Quentin Halys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quentin_Halys "Quentin Halys") in five sets. Zverev then earned victories against [Alejandro Tabilo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Tabilo "Alejandro Tabilo"), [Luciano Darderi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luciano_Darderi "Luciano Darderi"), [Botic van de Zandshulp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botic_van_de_Zandschulp "Botic van de Zandschulp"), and [Karen Khachanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Khachanov "Karen Khachanov") without dropping a single set. He defeated [Ben Shelton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Shelton "Ben Shelton") 6–3, 7–6(7–2), 5–7, 6–2 in the final, winning his second major title of the season and becoming the first German man to win the US Open since [Boris Becker in 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "1989 US Open – Men's singles").

In October, with Sinner withdrawing from the rest of his season, it was announced that Zverev will attain the year-end No. 1 ranking.

## Team competitions

### Laver Cup

Zverev took part in the inaugural [Laver Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Laver_Cup "2017 Laver Cup") in Prague in 2017. He accrued four points by winning both of his singles matches for Team Europe as they defeated Team World 15–9. He played a more crucial role in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Laver_Cup "2018 Laver Cup") and [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Laver_Cup "2019 Laver Cup"), winning the clinching matches in both editions against Kevin Anderson and [Milos Raonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milos_Raonic "Milos Raonic") respectively. In the [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Laver_Cup "2021 Laver Cup") edition in Boston, he defeated [John Isner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Isner "John Isner") in three sets in his only match in Team Europe's 14–1 win. In [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Laver_Cup "2024 Laver Cup"), Zverev returned to the Laver Cup after a two-year absence in which Team World had won both editions. He lost his first match to [Taylor Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Fritz "Taylor Fritz"), but rebounded on the third day to win against [Frances Tiafoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Tiafoe "Frances Tiafoe") in a deciding match tie-break. In [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Laver_Cup "2025 Laver Cup"), Zverev failed to win any matches against Team World, losing in straight sets to [Alex de Minaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_de_Minaur "Alex de Minaur") and [Taylor Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Fritz "Taylor Fritz"), the latter contributing his sixth consecutive loss against the American and allowing Team World to secure their third Laver Cup title. In [2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Laver_Cup "2026 Laver Cup"), Zverev fell again to de Minaur, after being a set and a break up, but won the doubles with [Casper Ruud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casper_Ruud "Casper Ruud") later that day, and triumphed over [Learner Tien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learner_Tien "Learner Tien") on the final day to return the Laver Cup to Team Europe, winning his first singles match at the event since 2024, and clinching the event for the fourth time.

Notably, Zverev has played in a record seven Laver Cups, missing only the 2022 and 2023 editions. Additionally, of the seven he has played, Team Europe won the tournament on six of those occasions, meaning Zverev has been present every year Team Europe has won. The 2025 Laver Cup was the only edition where Zverev played but Team Europe was not victorious.

Zverev made his Davis Cup debut for [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_Davis_Cup_team "Germany Davis Cup team") against the [Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Republic_Davis_Cup_team "Czech Republic Davis Cup team") in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Davis_Cup "2016 Davis Cup"). He faced No. 7 [Tomáš Berdych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Berdych "Tomáš Berdych") in his debut match and took a two sets to one lead before ultimately losing in five sets. After the Czech Republic won the doubles rubber while Kohlschreiber won both singles matches, Zverev faced [Lukáš Rosol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luk%C3%A1%C5%A1_Rosol "Lukáš Rosol") in a decisive fifth rubber. Rosol won the match easily to send the Czech Republic into the next round. In the [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Davis_Cup "2017 Davis Cup") against [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium_Davis_Cup_team "Belgium Davis Cup team"), Zverev recorded his first career match win in the competition against [Arthur De Greef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_De_Greef_(tennis) "Arthur De Greef (tennis)"), but lost the doubles rubber with his brother as well as his second singles match to [Steve Darcis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Darcis "Steve Darcis"). Belgium won the tie 4–1. Zverev won his first [Davis Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Davis_Cup "2018 Davis Cup") tie in 2018, winning both of his singles matches against [Alex de Minaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_de_Minaur "Alex de Minaur") and Kyrgios to lead Germany to a 3–1 victory over [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_Davis_Cup_team "Australia Davis Cup team"). In the quarterfinals, Germany took a 2–1 lead against [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_Davis_Cup_team "Spain Davis Cup team") behind Zverev's win over [David Ferrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ferrer "David Ferrer") and a victory in doubles. However, Spain ultimately won the tie on the final day after Zverev was unable to defeat Nadal and Kohlschreiber lost a tight five-set match to Ferrer.

In [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Davis_Cup "2019 Davis Cup"), the format of the Davis Cup was changed to have eighteen countries competing in the finals over a single week in November, all but six of which were decided through a qualifying round in February. Germany was placed in the qualifying round and drawn against [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_Davis_Cup_team "Hungary Davis Cup team"). Zverev participated and won both of his singles matches as Germany won the tie 5–0. He did not participate in the finals in November, in which Germany lost in the quarterfinals.

### Hopman Cup

Zverev represented Germany at the Hopman Cup for four consecutive years from 2016 through 2019 with three different partners. In [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Hopman_Cup "2016 Hopman Cup"), he competed with [Sabine Lisicki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabine_Lisicki "Sabine Lisicki"). The duo won their tie against the French team, with Zverev winning both his singles and mixed doubles matches. However, they were shut out against Great Britain and Australia Green and did not advance to the final. The [following year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Hopman_Cup "2017 Hopman Cup"), he entered the competition with [Andrea Petkovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Petkovic "Andrea Petkovic"). While the pair only won their tie against Great Britain, Zverev also defeated Federer in singles in his first tournament back from injury.

Zverev enjoyed more success in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Hopman_Cup "2018 Hopman Cup") and [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Hopman_Cup "2019 Hopman Cup"), pairing with [Angelique Kerber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelique_Kerber "Angelique Kerber"). In their first year together, they advanced to the final, primarily on the strength of the pair winning all three of their mixed doubles matches. In the final against Switzerland, Zverev lost his singles match to Federer. Although Kerber won her singles match against [Belinda Bencic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belinda_Bencic "Belinda Bencic"), they lost the decisive mixed doubles rubber. They returned in 2019 and again reached the final to set up a rematch of the previous year's final with Federer and Bencic. In a round-robin group with Australia, France, and Spain, the two of them won all six of their singles matches, but lost two of their three mixed doubles matches against Australia and France. Like the previous year, the final was decided by the mixed doubles match after Federer defeated Zverev and Kerber defeated Bencic. With the [Fast4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast4_Tennis "Fast4 Tennis") format, the match went to three sets. In the third set tiebreak, both teams had a match point at 4–4. Switzerland won the point with Federer serving to win the title.

## Playing style

Zverev is a baseline counterpuncher who relies on his massive serve and powerful backhand. He is able to hit strong groundstrokes to open the court, and finish points effectively at the net. Although his height slows him down on the court, it also adds to his reach and gives him the ability to get more balls back in play. [Novak Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic") has commented, "He moves well for his height." His backhand, in particular, is regarded as his strength and one of the best in the game. He can generate a lot of power off it with high accuracy, and his ability to absorb pace makes it one of the best shots in the modern game for both attack and defense. He often uses a semi-open stance and employs a western grip on his forehand. He has a continental-eastern grip on his backhand. Zverev can also slice his backhands that require a low centre of gravity, despite his height. He is often criticized for being too passive in the big moments.

With Zverev's height of 1.98 metres (6 ft 6 in), he can generate big serves at 220 kilometres per hour (140 mph) or faster at sharper angles than shorter players. The year Zverev first broke into the top 20, he was still not one of the better servers on tour, ranking just 38th in serve rating. This was well behind him ranking 18th in return rating, showing that his return game was much stronger than his serving. By 2018, he had improved in both categories, ranking 19th in serve rating and 8th in return rating. Zverev excels in particular at hitting a high percentage of first serves in, landing 64.2% in 2018. In his return game, he had the fifth highest percentage of first serve points won that year at 32.4%. His second serve has been a weakness for him in his earlier years, yet he has managed to turn it into a consistent strength. Zverev hit the most double faults on tour in 2016 and 2020. While it has improved since, it can still cause trouble for the German in big points.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Zverev is an all-court player and does not aim to be better on any surface in particular. He has said, "I feel like I can play on all surfaces. I've been to two finals or won tournaments on every single surface. I don't feel like I have to focus on one... I feel like I have good chances at all of them." [Toni Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toni_Nadal "Toni Nadal"), the uncle and coach of [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal "Rafael Nadal"), has praised his ability on hard courts in particular, saying, "His best surface is, in my eyes, the hard court, because he moves better than he does on clay." Six of Zverev's first ten titles came on hard courts, while the other four were on clay. As of 2024, he has yet to win a title on grass, but has reached two finals and defeated Federer on that surface. His best grass tournament remains [Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halle_Open "Halle Open"), in his home country.

Zverev has the ability to [serve-and-volley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serve-and-volley "Serve-and-volley") as well. His older brother Mischa is regarded as the biggest proponent of this playing style on the modern tour. Although Zverev does not employ this technique as often as his brother, he has shown it can be effective in big matches such as the Madrid Masters final against [Dominic Thiem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominic_Thiem "Dominic Thiem"), a player who rarely comes to the net. Zverev's volley and overhead technique are regarded as some of his biggest weaknesses.

Zverev was tall but skinny and not very muscular when growing up. His fitness trainer Jez Green has focused on making him stronger and set a goal for him to add 4 kilograms (8.8 lb) of muscle each year. Initially, Zverev had difficulty adjusting to this training style on the court, saying, "I had no idea what was going on in my body", and needed to adapt his shot technique as he became more muscular. Eventually, he improved on the court. Green remarked, "He started to become less wobbly. He started to absorb the power, so when someone hit hard he could hit the ball back and everything locked into place."

## Coaching team

Zverev has been coached by his parents since he was very young. His mother was initially his primary coach before his father took over at some point. Zverev made the decision to hire former world No. 1 [Juan Carlos Ferrero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Carlos_Ferrero "Juan Carlos Ferrero") at the [Washington Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Citi_Open "2017 Citi Open") in the summer of 2017. He fired Ferrero following the [2018 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Australian_Open "2018 Australian Open") after Ferrero criticized the rest of Zverev's coaching team. [Ivan Lendl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Lendl "Ivan Lendl"), another former world No. 1, joined Zverev's team in August 2018. They split up in July 2019 due to disappointing results and personal differences. Zverev has stated that Lendl was more interested in his dog or his golf game than in professional coaching. In 2020, Zverev started to work with former World Number 3 and 2013 French Open Finalist [David Ferrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ferrer "David Ferrer"). They announced their split in January 2021. Zverev's coaching team also includes physio Hugo Gravil. Fitness trainer Jez Green, who previously worked with [Andy Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Murray "Andy Murray"), also worked with Zverev beginning 2013. Green departed Zverev's team in 2021 after working with him for around seven years.

## Controversies

### COVID-19 pandemic

In June 2020, following Novak Djokovic's [Adria Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adria_Tour "Adria Tour"), several players tested positive for COVID-19. Due to his possible exposure, Zverev announced on Twitter that he would be following self-isolation procedures after testing negative for the virus. However, one week later, Zverev was captured on video at a party in [Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monaco "Monaco"), prompting criticism from fellow tennis players [Nick Kyrgios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Kyrgios "Nick Kyrgios") and [Katie Boulter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Boulter "Katie Boulter").

### First allegation of domestic abuse

In October 2020, Zverev's ex-girlfriend Olga Sharypova, in an article in *Racquet* magazine by journalist Ben Rothenberg, accused Zverev of physically and emotionally abusing her over the course of their relationship. Sharypova named multiple instances where Zverev allegedly became violent towards her, including punching her in the face during an argument that took place in the pair's hotel room while Zverev was competing at the [2019 Laver Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Laver_Cup "2019 Laver Cup"). Zverev denied the allegations and issued a statement after Sharypova's initial accusations: "I very much regret that she makes such statements. Because the accusations are simply not true." A second article by Rothenberg about the allegations was released in August 2021, this time in *[Slate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine) "Slate (magazine)")* magazine. The second article picked up Sharypova's story where the first left off. It includes this description of the alleged violence escalating in October 2019:

> Sharypova said she went to take a shower, during which Zverev continued berating her from outside the bathroom door. "When I got out of the shower, I was starting to take a towel and he came and said, 'Pack your stuff right now and leave,'" Sharypova recalled. "I'm just like, 'OK, can you wait a few minutes please? I'm naked here.'" From there, Sharypova said, Zverev attacked her more violently than he ever had before. She said that he grabbed her by the throat and pushed her up against the hard tile wall of the bathroom. "He started to punch me, and this time I understand that I can't be dough for punching," she said.

Rothenberg reports that "Sharypova has repeatedly said that she is not interested in pursuing criminal or civil action against Zverev." She told Rothenberg she wanted to be open and honest to help other women who tend to stay silent in such situations due to fear of not being believed. Zverev secured an injunction from a Berlin court against *Slate* later in August 2021, barring it from publishing the assault allegations without stronger evidence. In response, *Slate* stated that it stands by the reporting in the article and has not removed the article from its website. They protested that the injunction was obtained without the organization having an opportunity to present evidence, and they appealed the decision.

Commentator [Mary Carillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Carillo "Mary Carillo") stepped down from her presenting role at the [2021 Laver Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Laver_Cup "2021 Laver Cup") in response to the ATP's handling of the allegations. Following a 15-month ATP-commissioned investigation conducted by The Lake Forest Group (a third-party consultant working with the ATP's outside legal counsel, Smith Hulsey & Busey), the ATP announced in late January 2023 that they would not punish Zverev due to insufficient evidence to substantiate the allegations.

### 2022 Mexican Open default

After a first-round [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Abierto_Mexicano_Telcel_%E2%80%93_Doubles "2022 Abierto Mexicano Telcel – Doubles") loss at the [2022 Mexican Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Abierto_Mexicano_Telcel "2022 Abierto Mexicano Telcel"), Zverev repeatedly and violently struck umpire Alessandro Germani's chair with his racket, believing that Germani had incorrectly called a ball out during a tiebreak. Zverev was subsequently defaulted from the tournament, forfeiting all points and prize money that he had won thus far. He received the maximum fine of $40,000 by the ATP, an additional $25,000 fine, and a suspended eight-week ban. He was also placed on probation for one year. Zverev later issued an apology on social media.

### Second allegation of domestic abuse

About six months after the ATP declined to take action against Zverev, in July 2023, allegations from a second woman became public through a court filing. The public prosecution office requested a penalty order against Zverev in a Berlin criminal court over alleged bodily harm against Brenda Patea, Zverev's ex-girlfriend and the mother of his daughter. On 2 October 2023, the court issued a summary penalty order against Zverev, which included a fine of €450,000. In Germany, summary orders can be issued, typically without a prior hearing, when a judge considers the evidence sufficient to support the accusation and a trial is not deemed necessary. Zverev lodged an objection against the order, which under German law resulted in a public trial. Patea spoke publicly for the first time about the alleged abuse on 1 November 2023 in an interview with the *[Süddeutsche Zeitung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%BCddeutsche_Zeitung "Süddeutsche Zeitung")*. She said that during a dispute in May 2020, Zverev pushed her into a wall and choked her.

In January 2024, Zverev was appointed to serve on the ATP Players Advisory Council. The ATP has been criticized for allowing him to serve on the council while the domestic abuse charge is pending in a criminal court. Also in January 2024, Zverev was featured on the second season of the [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") series *[Break Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Break_Point_(2023_TV_series) "Break Point (2023 TV series)")* in an episode focused on his comeback from injury. Netflix was criticized for ignoring both allegations of domestic abuse. One columnist argued that "*Break Point* promised to take viewers behind the curtain of professional tennis and reveal the personalities behind it. That promise is null and void if Zverev – and the media – isn't willing to be honest."

At the beginning of the [2024 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Australian_Open "2024 Australian Open"), a court official announced that Zverev was scheduled to stand trial before the Tiergarten District Court in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin "Berlin"). The timing of the announcement, combined with Zverev's semifinal run at the tournament, brought heightened interest in and scrutiny of the allegations from both the press and the public. The trial began on 31 May 2024. On 7 June 2024, the third day of the trial, the judge agreed to a request by the public prosecution office, endorsed by the alleged victim, to provisionally terminate the proceedings subject to the condition that Zverev pay €200,000, of which €150,000 would go to the German State Treasury and €50,000 to charitable organizations. The trial therefore concluded without a finding of guilt. The prosecutor's request to terminate the proceedings followed an out-of-court agreement between Zverev and the alleged victim, the terms of which were not disclosed. According to his counsel, Zverev entered into the agreement "to put a quick end to the proceedings – above all in the interest of their child", emphasizing that the agreement "includes no determination or admission of guilt".

## Personal life

Zverev lives in [Monte Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo "Monte Carlo"), Monaco. He grew up in Hamburg and also has spent his winters living in Florida at the [Saddlebrook Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddlebrook_Academies "Saddlebrook Academies"). He can speak German, Russian, and English. Zverev has a daughter, Mayla, who was born in 2021 to Brenda Patea, an ex-girlfriend. Both parents reached an out-of-court financial settlement after a lawsuit over alleged physical assault was filed against Zverev by the ex-partner in 2023. Since 2021, Zverev has been dating German actress [Sophia Thomalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophia_Thomalla "Sophia Thomalla").

In 2017, Zverev said that his tennis idol is [Roger Federer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Federer "Roger Federer"). He is a fan of the [Miami Heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Heat "Miami Heat") and [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich "FC Bayern Munich") and wore the jersey of Bayern player [Michael Olise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Olise "Michael Olise") during the [2026 BMW Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_BMW_Open "2026 BMW Open"). In his spare time, he enjoys playing basketball and golf. His best friend on the tennis tour is Brazilian doubles specialist [Marcelo Melo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcelo_Melo "Marcelo Melo"). He is also close friends with [Dominic Thiem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominic_Thiem "Dominic Thiem") and [Andrey Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Rublev "Andrey Rublev"), and has known the latter since childhood. Zverev filed a lawsuit against the ACE Group International and its CEO, [Patricio Apey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patricio_Apey "Patricio Apey"), accusing them of "unlawful restraint of trade". He argued that the contract between them was oppressive, but eventually agreed to settle the dispute out of court before the trial began.

### Health

In August 2022, Zverev announced that he has [Type 1 diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_1_diabetes "Type 1 diabetes"), having been diagnosed at the age of three. That year, he launched the Alexander Zverev Foundation, a charity to support people with diabetes. During a match at the [2023 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_French_Open "2023 French Open"), Zverev was not permitted to inject [insulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insulin "Insulin") on-court, prompting criticism from the [International Diabetes Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Diabetes_Federation "International Diabetes Federation") and the [JDRF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JDRF "JDRF"). Tournament organizers later clarified that Zverev would be allowed to inject insulin on-court.
