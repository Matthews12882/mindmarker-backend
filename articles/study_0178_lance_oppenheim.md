# Lance Oppenheim

**Lance Oppenheim** (born January 26, 1996) is an American director and producer. His work blends nonfiction storytelling with heightened, cinematic formalism. Oppenheim's documentaries include the films *[Some Kind of Heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Some_Kind_of_Heaven "Some Kind of Heaven")* (2020) about [The Villages, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Villages,_Florida "The Villages, Florida"), *[Spermworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spermworld "Spermworld")* (2024), about unregulated sperm donation, and *[You Can See Everything](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_Can_See_Everything "You Can See Everything")* (2026), about [Elizabeth Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Holmes "Elizabeth Holmes"), which he co-directed with [Nathan Fielder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Fielder "Nathan Fielder"). He also directed the [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO "HBO") documentary series *[Ren Faire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren_Faire "Ren Faire")* (2024), about the [Texas Renaissance Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Renaissance_Festival "Texas Renaissance Festival"). He made his narrative film debut in 2026 with *[Primetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_(film) "Primetime (film)")*.

## Early life and education

Oppenheim was born on January 26, 1996, into a Jewish family in [Hollywood, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood,_Florida "Hollywood, Florida"), and grew up in [Weston, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weston,_Florida "Weston, Florida"). He attended [Pine Crest School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_Crest_School "Pine Crest School") from 2010 to 2014. He graduated from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") in 2019 with a bachelor's degree in visual and environmental studies. At Harvard, Oppenheim studied with filmmakers [Ross McElwee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_McElwee "Ross McElwee"), [Robb Moss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robb_Moss "Robb Moss"), and [Guy Maddin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Maddin "Guy Maddin"), and lived in [Adams House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adams_House_(Harvard_College) "Adams House (Harvard College)").

## Career

In high school, Oppenheim directed several short documentaries, one of which PBS distributed nationwide. He sent documentary pitches to the *[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times "New York Times")* Op-Docs' open submission portal, documenting "crazy things happening in [his] backyard", which the *Times* would "politely reject." While in college, Oppenheim directed three short documentaries acquired and distributed by [The New York Times Op-Docs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times"). His short *The Happiest Guy in the World*, about long-term cruise passenger [Mario Salcedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Salcedo "Mario Salcedo"), premiered at the [Tribeca Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribeca_Film_Festival "Tribeca Film Festival") in 2018.

Oppenheim directed and produced his debut feature, *[Some Kind of Heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Some_Kind_of_Heaven "Some Kind of Heaven")*, a documentary exploring life inside [The Villages, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Villages,_Florida "The Villages, Florida"), as part of his undergraduate senior thesis. In it, he follows four people living in The Villages and how they cope with later adult life. The film was produced by filmmaker [Darren Aronofsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darren_Aronofsky "Darren Aronofsky") and *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* as one of the paper's first feature-length productions. It premiered at the [2020 Sundance Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Sundance_Film_Festival "2020 Sundance Film Festival") and [International Film Festival Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Film_Festival_Rotterdam "International Film Festival Rotterdam") to critical acclaim and was later acquired by [Magnolia Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnolia_Pictures "Magnolia Pictures"). In 2023, Oppenheim appeared on the Forbes 30 Under 30 list in Hollywood & Entertainment. His second film, *[Spermworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spermworld "Spermworld")*, premiered at the 2024 [True/False Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True/False_Film_Festival "True/False Film Festival") to critical acclaim, and is available on [FX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FX_(TV_channel) "FX (TV channel)"), [Disney +](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney_%2B "Disney +"), and [Hulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulu "Hulu"). In 2024, Oppenheim completed his first television series, *[Ren Faire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren_Faire "Ren Faire")*. The series, produced by [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO "HBO") and [Elara Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elara_Pictures "Elara Pictures"), premiered at the 2024 [South by Southwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_by_Southwest "South by Southwest") Film Festival. In 2025, Oppenheim served as a producer on *[The Python Hunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Python_Hunt "The Python Hunt")*, directed by Xander Robin and produced by [Artists Equity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artists_Equity "Artists Equity").

Oppenheim made his narrative feature film debut with *[Primetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_(film) "Primetime (film)")* (2026), starring [Robert Pattinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pattinson "Robert Pattinson") as *[To Catch a Predator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Catch_a_Predator "To Catch a Predator")* host [Chris Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Hansen "Chris Hansen").
