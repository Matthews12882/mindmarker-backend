# Primetime (film)

***Primetime*** is a 2026 American [psychological thriller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological_thriller "Psychological thriller") film written by Ajon Singh and directed by [Lance Oppenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Oppenheim "Lance Oppenheim"). It dramatizes the production of the [reality television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reality_television "Reality television") series *[To Catch a Predator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Catch_a_Predator "To Catch a Predator")* (2004–2007); [Robert Pattinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pattinson "Robert Pattinson") portrays its host [Chris Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Hansen "Chris Hansen"), an [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC "NBC") journalist who works with the vigilante group [Perverted-Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perverted-Justice "Perverted-Justice") to catch [child predators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_predator "Child predator") in [sting operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sting_operation "Sting operation"). [Merritt Wever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merritt_Wever "Merritt Wever"), [Skyler Gisondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyler_Gisondo "Skyler Gisondo"), and [Phoebe Bridgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoebe_Bridgers "Phoebe Bridgers") appear in supporting roles.

Oppenheim, who had previously directed [documentaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Documentary_film "Documentary film"), conceived *Primetime* as his first narrative feature film after his wife hired Hansen to record a [Cameo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_(website) "Cameo (website)") video for him in 2024. Singh based the script on Luke Dittrich's 2007 *[Esquire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esquire_(magazine) "Esquire (magazine)")* article "Tonight on *Dateline* This Man Will Die", which details the botched *To Catch a Predator* sting operation that culminated in the 2006 [suicide of Bill Conradt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_of_Bill_Conradt "Suicide of Bill Conradt"), and sought to make a stylized retelling rather than a [biographical film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biographical_film "Biographical film"). [Principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography "Principal photography") took place from February to March 2025; cinematographer [David Bolen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bolen_(cinematographer) "David Bolen (cinematographer)") used 12 camera formats, seeking to replicate *To Catch a Predator* sting operations and [digital recordings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_video_recorder "Digital video recorder").

*Primetime* premiered at the [83rd Venice International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/83rd_Venice_International_Film_Festival "83rd Venice International Film Festival") on September 5, 2026, and was released theatrically by [A24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A24 "A24") in the United States on September 25. It has grossed $45.5 million against a $14.5 million budget and received positive reviews. Hansen, who was not consulted during the production, criticized it as "detached from reality".

## Plot

In 2006, journalist [Chris Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Hansen "Chris Hansen") hosts *[To Catch a Predator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Catch_a_Predator "To Catch a Predator")*, a *[Dateline NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dateline_NBC "Dateline NBC")* series where he conducts [sting operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sting_operations "Sting operations") with the anti-pedophilia vigilante group [Perverted-Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perverted-Justice "Perverted-Justice"). They lure would-be [child predators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_predator "Child predator"), expecting to engage in sex with minors (played by decoy actors of legal age), to houses where Hansen and his camera crew confront them before they are arrested. *To Catch a Predator* is a ratings success, and [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC "NBC")'s head [Jeff Zucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Zucker "Jeff Zucker") places it in the [primetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime "Primetime") slot to compete with [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company "American Broadcasting Company")'s hit show *[Lost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_(TV_series) "Lost (TV series)")*.

Dan Plum, an aspiring actor seeking credits for membership in the [Screen Actors Guild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild "Screen Actors Guild") (SAG), is cast as one of *To Catch a Predator*'s decoys. Although he is initially disturbed by the shooting process, he acclimates and comes to admire Hansen. In [Fort Myers, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Myers,_Florida "Fort Myers, Florida"), Hansen breaks off an extramarital affair with a younger woman at a restaurant, unaware he is being photographed by a reporter for the *[National Enquirer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Enquirer "National Enquirer")* who proceeds to make threatening calls to Hansen.

Disturbed by the threat of his affair going public and under pressure to maintain the show's ratings, Hansen becomes consumed in making *To Catch a Predator* a commercial success, growing increasingly erratic as producer Andie Bender considers leaving the program for a rival [reality television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reality_television "Reality television") series called *[What Would You Do?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_Would_You_Do%3F_(2008_TV_program) "What Would You Do? (2008 TV program)")* that seeks to replicate *To Catch a Predator*'s success. At the same time, Dan worries he is not advancing his acting career on the show, confiding in Hansen who tells Dan they serve a greater purpose of "cleansing the world of an ancient evil", recounting his prior work in India investigating the disappearances of children, where he was told of this "ancient evil" retreating into a cave after the villagers resorted to killing one another out of desperation.

After receiving a letter from a fan in [Murphy, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murphy,_Texas "Murphy, Texas"), Perverted-Justice founder Xavier Von Erck begins searching for pedophiles in the area. He finds a prospective predator who covers their tracks diligently, but Xavier discovers they are attending a [wake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_(ceremony) "Wake (ceremony)") for a Texas state senator in nearby [Denton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denton,_Texas "Denton, Texas") and surmises they may be a government official. Convinced catching a high-profile "whale" will solve his and the show's problems, Hansen cancels an upcoming shoot in [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey "New Jersey") over Andie's objections and takes the *To Catch a Predator* team to Texas.

The team chat with the "whale" online for some time, eventually getting them to give their phone number, exposing their identity as local assistant district attorney [Bill Conradt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_of_Bill_Conradt "Suicide of Bill Conradt"). Forgoing usual protocol, Hansen and the Murphy police decide to confront Conradt at his house. Reinvigorated by the events, Andie declines the rival show's job offer, and Hansen, just prior to the raid on Conradt's home, receives a call from his wife confirming the *National Enquirer* plans to go public with his affair.

As Hansen and the police prepare to move in, Conradt glares through a window at Hansen, who sees Conradt as the manifestation of the "ancient evil" from his trip to India, before Conradt shoots himself. Andie is disappointed they could not interview Conradt and plans to interview the police in an attempt to salvage the episode. Hansen cries while repeating incantations as he sees the "ancient evil" escape back into the woods. A shaken Dan walks away from the scene; a year later, living in [Mesa, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesa,_Arizona "Mesa, Arizona"), he receives his SAG membership card in the mail.

## Production

### Development

Documentarian [Lance Oppenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Oppenheim "Lance Oppenheim") first became aware of [Chris Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Hansen "Chris Hansen") and his show *[To Catch a Predator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Catch_a_Predator "To Catch a Predator")* through parodies in other shows such as *[South Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Park "South Park")*. In March 2024, Oppenheim's wife, Abbey Rowe, hired Hansen to film a [Cameo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_(website) "Cameo (website)") video to congratulate Oppenheim on his 2024 documentary film *[Spermworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spermworld "Spermworld")*, which is when Oppenheim began to believe Hansen had developed into a "strange, performative version of himself". Hansen promoted his own content in the video to Oppenheim, prompting Oppenheim to view Hansen's other Cameo videos, saying "[in] one, he called somebody a certified 'flex offender' instead of a certified sex offender. I was like, this is so weird—you can pay him $30, and he’s going to pretend you’re a child predator and then wish you happy birthday. What happened to the Chris Hansen I grew up with? Or was this always the Chris Hansen who existed?" Oppenheim retrospectively called *Primetime* an "adaptation" of that video.

Two weeks after Oppenheim received the Cameo that Hansen recorded for him, Oppenheim, while meeting with [Robert Pattinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pattinson "Robert Pattinson") and producer Brighton McCloskey for an unrelated project, showed them the Cameo as spurred by what Oppenheim described as an "[intrusive thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intrusive_thought "Intrusive thought")"; a month later, Pattinson and McCloskey came across a draft of a *To Catch a Predator* screenplay written by Ajon Singh while looking for scripts to produce for their production company Icki Eneo Arlo. It compelled Pattinson to join the project as producer and approach Oppenheim to direct, calling Oppenheim's directorial style "perfect" for it. Oppenheim considered making a film about Hansen ever since receiving his Cameo, but dropped the idea when he heard [David Osit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Osit "David Osit") was already producing a documentary about Hansen called *[Predators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predators_(2025_film) "Predators (2025 film)")*. Singh's screenplay, however, changed Oppenheim's mind, with him reflecting that "[Singh] tapped into something that was so up my alley, that felt closer to a fever dream than it did like a conventional biopic ... I [had] to drop everything. This is destiny."

### Writing

Over three years prior to *Primetime*, the 2007 *[Esquire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esquire_(magazine) "Esquire (magazine)")* article "Tonight on *Dateline* This Man Will Die" by Luke Dittrich, which details the circumstances behind Conradt's suicide and the botched *To Catch a Predator* sting operation that attempted to confront him, was [optioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Option_(filmmaking) "Option (filmmaking)") by Dittrich to William Iannaccone, a producer at Range Media Partners. In 2023, Ajon Singh, a freshman screenwriter, got into contact with Iannaccone through a mutual friend at the [Creative Artists Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Artists_Agency "Creative Artists Agency"), who introduced Singh to Dittrich's article.

Singh, intrigued by the concept, agreed to write a screenplay based off Dittrich's article, completing the script in two weeks after researching the show and Hansen for months prior. His screenplay was originally titled *To Catch a Predator*, directly off the show itself. Singh said he was "not really interested in a biopic" and instead "wanted to create something heightened, stylized and subjective, dancing on the line between reality and spectacle". According to Singh, his initial first draft was met with a "'sobering' marketplace" due to its controversial subject matter.

After months of uncertainty regarding the viability of the project, Pattinson got possession of the script and expressed an interest in producing it to Singh, reviving it; Singh, unsure of how the script ever reached Pattinson, named Tyler Falbo, a member of the sketch group Almost Friday, as potentially responsible for passing it along to Pattinson or his producing partner McCloskey at some point.

For the segment in the film where Hansen recounts investigating a string of child disappearances in India, Singh cited a 1990s article by *The New York Times* as inspiration. As is depicted in the film, one child each was stolen from villages in [Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttar_Pradesh "Uttar Pradesh"); due to nobody being able to solve why it was happening or who was the perpetrator, [mob justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungle_justice "Jungle justice") took hold. Singh said he "thought that was a really strange story from a reputable news source" and "felt so compelled to do something with it" and chose to combine it with Hansen's real-life investigations of child exploitation in India from the same time period, to give Hansen's "mythical hero" a "mythical villain" to contend with.

### Pre-production

In October 2024, it was officially announced that Oppenheim would make his narrative feature film debut using Singh's script, with [A24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A24 "A24") financing and distributing and Pattinson and [Ari Aster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ari_Aster "Ari Aster") producing; Pattinson was rumored to star as the film's lead, but went unconfirmed. The project's plot was given as "a journalist who takes on an underworld of crime and changes television forever", with speculation that it was based on Hansen and *To Catch a Predator*.

Pattinson resisted starring in the film at first but was convinced by the "compelling" version of Hansen Singh and Oppenheim crafted. Singh reflected "we had this fateful dinner at Rob's house ... [Rob] talked about how he would run around the [Silver Lake Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Lake_Reservoir "Silver Lake Reservoir") listening to Chris Hansen's podcast and he was reciting lines from the script ... so I rewrote the character in five days and we sent the script back to Rob and we were like, 'Here's the version. If you don't like it, we'll go fuck ourselves. But this is the version we think that you could act in.' And he took the weekend and he got back to us and he said, 'Let's do it.'" Singh credited Pattinson's inclusion for the film's greater emphasis on including a "mystical angels and demons sort of element".

In December 2024, Singh met with Pattinson and Oppenheim over [Zoom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoom_(software) "Zoom (software)") to further refine the film's screenplay ahead of filming the next month. In October 2025, Singh called *Primetime* the most personally impactful project he had ever worked on.

### Filming

> [*To Catch a Predator*] doesn’t exist on [DVDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD "DVD"), [Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blu-ray "Blu-ray"), all that we really have of it are these 360p and 480p versions that have seemingly been digitized by somebody else. So we’re watching something that’s been essentially reprinted twice. The audience is interacting with an object that had been scanned, that had been corrupted in some way. I liked conceptually that it rhymed with what we were doing with this sort of operatic, heightened portrait of Chris Hansen, the internet’s version of Chris Hansen.

— Oppenheim explaining the purpose for printing *Primetime* onto [16 mm film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16_mm_film "16 mm film") before having it digitized

[Principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography "Principal photography") began in [New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans "New Orleans") under the production title *Bluefin Tuna* on February 10, 2025, and concluded by March 28. Oppenheim wanted to shoot *Primetime* on-location in his native Florida, but due to Louisiana's tax incentives it was chosen instead. Filming also took place in New York and [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka"). The production spent $14.5 million in Louisiana.

*Primetime* was printed entirely onto [16 mm film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16_mm_film "16 mm film") which was then digitized, creating a "layer of removal the scan supplied between the audience and the original image" as an attempt to capture the way *To Catch a Predator* is commonly viewed in the modern day. For the film's color palette, Oppenheim wished to avoid the "[grayscale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grayscale "Grayscale")" of modern cinema and have *Primetime* harken back to the "saturated, almost pastel-like" look of 2000s television as overseen by colorist Damien Vandercruyssen, citing *[The O.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_O.C. "The O.C.")* and *[Smallville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallville "Smallville")* as visual templates he hoped to emulate.

Seeking to authentically capture the sting operations for *Primetime*, Oppenheim consulted with Mitchell Wagenberg, a veteran camera operator within the television industry who worked on the original *To Catch a Predator* series, who recommended use of a [Sony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony "Sony") Plumbicon, a "novelty" [video camera tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_camera_tube "Video camera tube") from the 1980s that had long since been discontinued. The Plumbicon the film crew were able to find had deteriorated in quality, but to an extent Oppenheim approved of due to its "dreamlike" quality originating from its "tubes [having] been burnt to a crisp, so every time we turned the camera on it would distort the images. It almost looked like a nuclear explosion had been set off right in front of us, and I loved that about it".

Daniel Garber, the film's editor, was brought on in the early stages of development for *Primetime*, prior to the commencement of principal photography. Garber described the process of editing *Primetime* as "very complicated" due to the film's varied stylistic choices that combine several methods of filming, including "degraded, grungy-looking" [DV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DV_(video_format) "DV (video format)") footage, surveillance video meant to replicate the show itself, traditional [digital cinematography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_cinematography "Digital cinematography") which added a "fantastical and hallucinatory" aura, among others. Oppenheim prescribed different "psychological formats" to the various camera types used when shooting *Primetime*, positing that when the film was shot in DV footage it was "objective" or when it was shot with the Plumbicon or a digital camera it respectively became a "full expression of [Chris Hansen's] [id](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Id,_ego_and_superego#Id "Id, ego and superego")" or "[superego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Id,_ego_and_superego#Superego "Id, ego and superego")". Altogether, *Primetime'*s cinematographer [David Bolen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bolen_(cinematographer) "David Bolen (cinematographer)") used 12 different camera formats during filming. Garber cited [Oliver Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Stone "Oliver Stone")'s *[JFK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JFK_(film) "JFK (film)")* as a point of inspiration for both Oppenheim and him, specifically "[the] kinds of rhythms they were able to find—even within really disparate pieces of footage—and [how they were able to draw] something more fictional out of real-world events."

For the moment in the film when Hansen and his crew discover the identity of their "whale", the filmmakers used a tiny camera made for [colonoscopies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonoscopy "Colonoscopy") and shot it traveling through a "mini-LED tunnel on a rotating gimbal that had spinning lights" which landed on Pattinson's face at the end to visualize the effect of "the camera [moving] through [a] computer". Bolen was worried if A24 would approve the use of his footage due to the colonoscopy camera's native resolution being in 480p, but they were ultimately given full permission to incorporate it, which Bolen was thankful for, calling it his favorite part of the movie.

According to Garber, *Primetime* originally ended during the immediate aftermath of the raid on Conradt's home, with Pattinson's Hansen "stuck in this loop doing these incantations, repeating the chat logs". Gisondo suggested they add a follow-up with Dan one year later to Oppenheim, showing Dan getting his SAG card, to add "a sense of closure" for his character. Oppenheim agreed and convinced Singh to write an additional scene to bookend the film.

Oppenheim said that just as his documentary films *[Some Kind of Heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Some_Kind_of_Heaven "Some Kind of Heaven")* and *[Ren Faire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren_Faire "Ren Faire")* explore themes of power in [The Villages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Villages,_Florida "The Villages, Florida") and [Renaissance fairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_fair "Renaissance fair"), respectively, *Primetime* explores power dynamics in the realm of [broadcast television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrestrial_television "Terrestrial television"):

> I think, thematically, a lot of the ideas of power, and how power is obtained, and how power is maintained, and what happens when you have it, and what happens when you don't have it—I think these are kind of constant. They're perennially fascinating subjects, but I think I like looking at that lens and kind of applying it to—whether it's the Renaissance Fair or The Villages or broadcast television with the film I just made—I think there's a lot of overlap with a lot of the subjects.

Hansen was not involved with the production, with Oppenheim saying "I felt like I had everything I needed with the Cameo." [Nathan Fielder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Fielder "Nathan Fielder"), described as a "great creative mentor" of Oppenheim's by Singh, watched cuts of the film and gave advice during production, which earned him a "special thanks" inclusion within the film's end credits.

### Music

Ari Balouzian composed the film's score, while Bridgers contributed the single "On Video", which plays over the end credits for *Primetime*. Having composed for Oppenheim's previous projects, such as *Some Kind of Heaven* and *Ren Faire*, Balouzian would create 24 individual score pieces for the film's soundtrack, with his track "Savior" showcasing alongside "On Video" as an end credits feature. Pattinson listened to the soundtrack while filming to help get into character. Speaking to *[The Line of Best Fit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Line_of_Best_Fit "The Line of Best Fit")* in September 2026, Balouzian stated that he "started working on *Primetime* after reading the script, and had access to the dailies while they were shooting, so the process started super early".

As described by *[NME](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NME "NME")*, Bridgers's "On Video" works to "draw on elements of the original score for the film" featuring "a softer opening [building] into heavier, distorted guitar textures before settling back down." She performed the single as part of [The Lost Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lost_Tour "The Lost Tour"), which coincided with the release of *Primetime*. She also performed "On Video" for A24's *Sound Check at [Cherry Lane Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry_Lane_Theatre "Cherry Lane Theatre")* series as a live performance directed by Oppenheim, featuring [Ethan Gruska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Belle_Brigade "The Belle Brigade") on piano and Bridgers in front of a backdrop of TV sets, which had been filmed in September and released on October 9.

The soundtrack released alongside the film on September 25, 2026, published by A24 Music. The track "Savior" dropped prior to the soundtrack on September 11.

All music is composed by Ari Balouzian.

## Release

The film's connection to Hansen and *To Catch a Predator* was revealed in May 2026, when A24 released the film's teaser trailer and first poster with Pattinson playing Hansen. The film's accompanying logline said the film was set in 2006, which some publications recognized as the year [Bill Conradt committed suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_of_Bill_Conradt "Suicide of Bill Conradt"), which contributed to the show's cancellation.

*Primetime* premiered in the main competition of the [83rd Venice International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/83rd_Venice_International_Film_Festival "83rd Venice International Film Festival") on September 5, 2026. It was theatrically released in the US on September 25. In April 2026, Indian streaming service [Lionsgate Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionsgate_Play "Lionsgate Play") announced that *Primetime* would be part of its initial batch of theatrical-first releases in September 2026. The film released in France and Germany on September 23 and 24, respectively. *Primetime* is scheduled to be released in Australia and New Zealand on October 29, and the following day in the United Kingdom on October 30. Beginning on November 3, *Primetime* will be made available for [digital streaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_media "Streaming media").

*Primetime* was banned in [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia") by the country's [Film Censorship Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_Censorship_Board_of_Malaysia "Film Censorship Board of Malaysia"), previously having had its release delayed before being cancelled outright.

## Reception

### Box office

In the United States and Canada, *Primetime* was released alongside *[Heart of the Beast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_of_the_Beast "Heart of the Beast")*, *[Forgotten Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forgotten_Island "Forgotten Island")*, and the re-release of *[Avengers: Endgame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers:_Endgame "Avengers: Endgame")*, and was projected to gross $17–20 million in its opening weekend. The film went on to debut to $19.2 million from 2,863 theaters, becoming the third-best opening weekend for A24, behind *[Backrooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backrooms_(film) "Backrooms (film)")* ($81.4 million) and *[Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_War_(film) "Civil War (film)")* ($25.5 million). In its sophomore weekend the film made $7.7 million (a drop of 59%) and then $4 million in its third weekend.

### Critical response

On the [review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator "Review aggregator") website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes"), 86% of 239 critics' reviews are positive, with an average rating of 7.3/10. The website's consensus reads: "Turning the camera's scrutinizing eye back onto *To Catch a Predator'*s journalistic legacy, *Primetime* interrogates the slippery boundary between justice and lurid spectacle with crackling style and an outstanding Robert Pattinson." [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), which uses a [weighted average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_arithmetic_mean "Weighted arithmetic mean"), assigned the film a score of 67 out of 100, based on 49 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews. Audiences polled by [CinemaScore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CinemaScore "CinemaScore") gave the film an average grade of "B" on an A+ to F scale, while 56% of those surveyed by [PostTrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PostTrak "PostTrak") said they would definitely recommend it.

[Manohla Dargis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manohla_Dargis "Manohla Dargis") for *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* praised [Skyler Gisondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyler_Gisondo "Skyler Gisondo") and [Alex Winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Winter "Alex Winter") for their respective roles, writing that they helped to give the film "emotion" and "push the movie into depths it doesn’t remain in" but otherwise felt that *Primetime* "looks good and moves well, and the dark, saturated colors and visual grittiness add some fine [Expressionistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expressionism "Expressionism") texture. Yet it’s hard to know to what end, or to care." Nicholas Barber of the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") gave the film 5 out of 5 stars, calling Pattinson's performance "extraordinary" and "the best performance yet from the most intriguing actor of his generation". He described Pattinson as "completely immersed in the character's strangeness" and his approach to the role as "exaggerated and theatrical without ever being arch." While finding "nothing radical" about the film's message, Barber praised Oppenheim's direction for "[dropping] us into the middle of a fever dream in which everything is wholly believable yet unsettlingly weird", calling the film "not a straightforward drama, but a heightened, hallucinatory black comedy."

David Rooney of *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")* called the film "a morbidly fascinating inquiry into performative justice, moral superiority and public shaming as crude fuel for reality television". He called Pattinson "riveting" and praised the supporting performances of [Phoebe Bridgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoebe_Bridgers "Phoebe Bridgers"), Gisondo, and [Merritt Wever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merritt_Wever "Merritt Wever"), but found the film's screenplay "a tad heavy-handed in making Hansen a borderline-unhinged egomaniac from the start".

Jessica Kiang of *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* praised the "dazzling" cast for delivering "revelatory" and "career-best" work, writing, "there are really no weak links in the chain of performances here". She called Pattinson's impression of Hansen's voice and physical mannerisms "uncanny" and also praised Wever ("absolutely blistering"), Gisondo ("moving"), and Bridgers, whose performance she considered "the second of the film's most impressive transformations". Kiang also commended the film's craft, including the cinematography, which she described as "greasy, saturated images, so livid with grain that it feels like they leave dirt under your fingernails", as well as the "throbbing, queasy score" and the "inspired, [...] often very witty editing".

Ryan Lattanzio of *[IndieWire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndieWire "IndieWire")* gave the film a B; he considered the "exuberance" of Oppenheim's direction to be the film's "primary allure", noting its "tweaked-out style and sleazy/douchey vibe of early-2000s videotape and closed-circuit four-quadrant TV." Lattanzio wrote that the film begins with "Hansen's sordid personality already baked in", feeling Pattinson played the character as "someone visibly diseased in the head from the very top". Nick Schager for *[The Daily Beast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Beast "The Daily Beast")* wrote that "*Primetime* has nowhere to go but pure luridness" and criticized Pattinson's performance, saying "[there] isn’t a single moment when Pattinson’s Hansen resembles a real-life human being; though his accent is spot-on, his mannered speech, deliberate movements, unhinged glares, and affected smiles turn him into a cross between *[Henry: Portrait of a Serial Killer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry:_Portrait_of_a_Serial_Killer "Henry: Portrait of a Serial Killer")* and [Heath Ledger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heath_Ledger "Heath Ledger")’s [Joker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joker_(The_Dark_Knight) "Joker (The Dark Knight)")."

Some critics unfavorably compared the film to [David Osit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Osit "David Osit")'s 2025 documentary *[Predators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predators_(2025_film) "Predators (2025 film)")*, which also chronicles the production of *To Catch a Predator.* Rooney wrote, "While Osit’s doc contemplated what the collective thirst for justice and appetite for televised humiliation might say about the American public, *Primetime* never digs that deep, which made me question what this flashy but shallow movie hopes to achieve." Matt Brennan of the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")* found the film "remarkably incurious about *To Catch a Predator* and its consequences", writing that Oppenheim's "lurid" style caused the film to "[stumble] into some of the very same gutter sleaze tactics as the original series." He criticized the film's focus on Hansen himself as thematically limiting, writing: "*Primetime* isn't a Chris Hansen hit piece. In fact, it's so in love with its flawed protagonist that it can't see he's the least compelling character in the movie, an ambitious, arrogant, grasping, unscrupulous man we've met countless times before." Both Brennan and Lattanzio felt the film offered "little insight" into Hansen and *To Catch a Predator* that was not already covered in *Predators*. Osit was asked to view a cut of *Primetime* prior to its release, calling it "probably one of the most interesting professional experiences of my life, sitting and watching that film" and saying it was "fascinating to watch."

### Hansen's response

The *Primetime* crew did not consult Hansen during production. Hansen said he learned about *Primetime* in late 2025, and that he was informed that Oppenheim intended it as a "love letter to [him]"; Oppenheim disputed this in an interview with the *[Columbia Journalism Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Journalism_Review "Columbia Journalism Review")*. When asked about it in May 2026, Hansen said, "they reached out to me this week, and I've been asked not to talk about it a great deal at this point." Of the trailer, Hansen said: "It looks very dramatic. I know [Pattinson] is a popular actor, and I know the director is a popular guy. I also have some questions: What's Hollywood's motive here?" He added that Pattinson's performance looked "almost vampirish", and that Pattison's imitation of his voice was "pretty good", if overly dramatic.

On *[Jesse Watters Primetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Watters_Primetime "Jesse Watters Primetime")*, Hansen said a lawyer from A24 asked him before a private screening to sign a contract that would "take away his name, image, and brand, essentially, as well as any potential legal rights that would come out of the movie". Hansen refused to sign it after speaking with his lawyers. *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* reported that the agreement was a standard [non-disclosure agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-disclosure_agreement "Non-disclosure agreement") (NDA) signed by preview audiences to prevent spoilers and protect A24's [intellectual property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_property "Intellectual property"). Hansen attested that Osit did not have to sign an NDA before he saw *Primetime*, and that the NDA included an agreement not to take legal action against A24.

Hansen purchased pre-show advertising for every theatrical screening of *Primetime*. The ads show Hansen in a kitchen like the ones seen in his sting operations, asking the audience to "take a seat" before directing the audience to his [streaming service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_media "Streaming media") TruBlu. Hansen wanted to end the advertisement with the disclaimer that *Primetime* is "a Hollywood fantasy version" of true events, but was advised against it to avoid potential legal action by A24. Hansen told the *Columbia Journalism Review* that he rejected involvement in a pitched promotional campaign: "[A24 seemed] eager to whore me out in a promotional campaign ... [all] this bullshit about Robert this and Robert that and 'We'll do a podcast, you can fly to London.' I don't care, honestly. I'm really fucking busy being the real Chris Hansen. It’s a seven-day-a-week job." In response to Hansen's criticisms, Oppenheim said he was "excited for him to have a seat and watch the movie in a movie theater, if that's where he chooses to watch it ... I'm excited to hear what he has to say about it."

Hansen was critical of *Primetime* after seeing it: "Obviously I'm not objective because the character is, you know, me, but it was so detached from reality or anything I've experienced then or now doing the investigations. I'm not sure what the point of it was." He felt *Primetime* portrayed him in a "twisted, dark, [and] dishonest" manner and said the filmmakers were "just as big of predators as the people who get caught in these investigations". Oppenheim responded that Hansen was a "complicated hero", that he "wanted to show what the effects were on someone who does this work", and that *Primetime* "[isn't meant] to be representational of reality in any way." Hansen subsequently invited Oppenheim and Pattinson to appear on his podcast, *Have A Seat with Chris Hansen*.
