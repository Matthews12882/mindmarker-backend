# Labor Day

**Labour Day** (or **Labor Day**) is an official public holiday in many countries. In most countries, Labour Day is synonymous with, or linked with, [International Workers' Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Workers%27_Day "International Workers' Day"), which happens on 1 May. It was originally chosen to commemorate the 1886 general strike which culminated in the [Haymarket affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haymarket_affair "Haymarket affair"). In the United States and Canada, it is a public holiday on [the first Monday in September](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Day "Labor Day").

Labour Day is a [public / statutory holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holiday "Public holiday"). It is an annual day of celebration of the [labour movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_movement "Labour movement") and the institution of [achievements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_rights "Labor rights"). It has its origins in the [labour union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_union "Trade union") movement, specifically the [eight-hour day movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight-hour_day_movement "Eight-hour day movement"), which advocated eight hours for work, eight hours for recreation, and eight hours for rest.

## International Workers' Day

For some countries, "Labour Day" is synonymous with, or linked to, [International Workers' Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Workers%27_Day "International Workers' Day"), which occurs on 1 May. International Workers' Day commemorates the 1886 [Haymarket affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haymarket_affair "Haymarket affair") in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago"), when there was a general strike for the eight-hour workday.

Many countries have a holiday at or around this date, but it is not a Labour Day celebration. Others celebrate Labour Day on the first Monday after 1 September or on dates commemorating significant national labour events in their histories.

## Labour Day dates by nation

### Australia

Labour Day is a [public holiday in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Australia "Public holidays in Australia") on dates which vary between [states and territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_and_territories_of_Australia "States and territories of Australia"). In some states the date commemorates the Eight Hours Day march (see below). It is the first Monday in October in the [Australian Capital Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Capital_Territory "Australian Capital Territory"), [New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_South_Wales "New South Wales") and [South Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Australia "South Australia"). In [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_(state) "Victoria (state)") and [Tasmania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasmania "Tasmania"), it is the second Monday in March (though the latter calls it the [Eight Hours Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight_Hours_Day "Eight Hours Day")). In [Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Australia "Western Australia"), Labour Day is the first Monday in March. In [Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensland "Queensland") and the [Northern Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Territory "Northern Territory"), Labour Day occurs on the first Monday in May (though the latter calls it May Day). It is on the fourth Monday of March in the territory of [Christmas Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_Island "Christmas Island").

#### Victoria

The first march for an [eight-hour day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight-hour_day_movement "Eight-hour day movement") by the [labour movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_labour_movement#Early_history "Australian labour movement") occurred in [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne "Melbourne") on 21 April 1856. On this day, stonemasons and building workers on building sites around Melbourne stopped work and marched from the [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne "University of Melbourne") to [Parliament House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_House,_Melbourne "Parliament House, Melbourne") to achieve an eight-hour day. Their direct action protest was a success, and they are noted as being among the first organised workers in the world to achieve an eight-hour day, with no loss of pay.

#### Queensland

Labour Day was first celebrated with a public holiday in [Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensland "Queensland") in 1865 as Eight Hours Celebration Day. It occurred on 1 March ([Saint David's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_David%27s_Day "Saint David's Day")) and celebrated the winning of an eight-hour work day by [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane "Brisbane") workers in 1858. The date was moved to May Day around 1896, in solidarity with the attack on United States workers on the first May Day parade in the [Haymarket affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haymarket_affair "Haymarket affair"). In 1901, the holiday was moved to the first Monday in May, to ensure a long weekend.

### Austria

1 May is a state holiday (with no specific official purpose) in Austria. Labour organizations and the [social democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SP%C3%96 "SPÖ") organize demonstration marches on this day.

### Bangladesh

Bangladesh Garment Sramik Sanghati, an organisation working for the welfare of garment workers, has requested that 24 April be declared Labour Safety Day in [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh"), in memory of the victims of the 2013 [Rana Plaza collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rana_Plaza_collapse "Rana Plaza collapse"). However, Bangladesh does observe May Day on 1 May.

### The Bahamas

Labour Day is a [national holiday in The Bahamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_the_Bahamas "Public holidays in the Bahamas"), celebrated on the first Friday in June in order to create a long weekend for workers. The traditional date of Labour Day in [The Bahamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bahamas "The Bahamas"), however, is 7 June, in commemoration of a significant workers' strike that began on that day in 1942.

Labour Day is meant to honour and celebrate workers and the importance of their contributions to the nation and society. In the capital city, [Nassau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nassau,_The_Bahamas "Nassau, The Bahamas"), thousands of people come to watch a parade through the streets, which begins at mid-morning. Bands in colourful uniforms, traditional African [Junkanoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junkanoo "Junkanoo") performers, and members of various labour unions and political parties are all part of the procession, which ends at the Southern Recreation Grounds, where government officials make speeches for the occasion.

### Canada

[Labour Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Day_(Canada) "Labour Day (Canada)") ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language "French language"): *Fête du Travail*) has been marked as a statutory [public holiday in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Canada "Public holidays in Canada") on the first Monday in September since 1894. Its origins can be traced back to numerous local demonstrations and celebrations in earlier decades. Such events assumed political significance when a labour demonstration in [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto "Toronto") in April 1872, in support of striking printers, led directly to the enactment of the Trade Union Act, a law that confirmed the legality of unions. On 22 July 1882, a labour celebration in Toronto attracted the attention of American labour leader [Peter J. McGuire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_J._McGuire "Peter J. McGuire"), who organised a similar parade in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") on 5 September that year. Labour parades were held in several Canadian cities that day as well.

Unions associated with the [Knights of Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_of_Labor "Knights of Labor") and the [American Federation of Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Federation_of_Labor "American Federation of Labor") in both Canada and the United States subsequently promoted parades and festivals on the first Monday in September. In Canada, local celebrations took place in [Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_Ontario "Hamilton, Ontario"), [Oshawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oshawa "Oshawa"), [Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal "Montreal"), [St. Catharines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Catharines "St. Catharines"), [Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_Nova_Scotia "Halifax, Nova Scotia"), [Ottawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa "Ottawa"), [Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver "Vancouver") and [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London,_Ontario "London, Ontario") during these years. Montreal declared a civic holiday in 1889. In [Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Scotia "Nova Scotia"), coal miners had been holding picnics and parades since 1880 to celebrate the anniversary of their union, the Provincial Workmen's Association, first organised in 1879.

In addition, in 1889, the [Royal Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_commission "Royal commission") on the Relations of Labour and Capital in Canada, chaired by [James Sherrard Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Sherrard_Armstrong "James Sherrard Armstrong") (1886–88) and Augustus Toplady Freed (1888–89), recommended recognition of an official "labour day" by the [federal government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Canada "Government of Canada"). In March and April 1894, unions lobbied Parliament to recognise Labour Day as a public holiday. Legislation was introduced in May by prime minister [Sir John Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Sparrow_David_Thompson "John Sparrow David Thompson") and received [royal assent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_assent "Royal assent") in July 1894.

### China

In the People's Republic of China, International Labour Day is a public holiday. As of 2025, the statutory Labour Day holiday is two days, 1 and 2 May. This is an increase from the one statutory day observed from 2008 to 2024. Labour Day had been a three-day statutory holiday from 1999 until 2007.

In practice, the Labour Day break is often longer than the statutory holiday itself. The State Council commonly adjusts adjacent weekends to create a longer holiday period, often a three- to five-day "short holiday", with one or more nearby weekend days designated as working days to make up for the additional days off. For example, in 2025 the Labour Day holiday ran from 1 May to 5 May, while 27 April was designated as a working day.

### Hong Kong S.A.R.

Labour Day, observed 1 May, has been considered a [public holiday in Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Hong_Kong "Public holidays in Hong Kong") since 1999.

### India

The first Labour Day was celebrated in India on 1 May 1923, in [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai "Chennai") (then known as Madras), organised by the [Labour Kisan Party of Hindustan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Kisan_Party_of_Hindustan "Labour Kisan Party of Hindustan").

### Jamaica

Labour Day in Jamaica is historically tied to the working class and the country's shift away from colonial traditions. Originally, 24 May was observed as Empire Day, celebrating Queen Victoria's birthday. In British history, she is credited with granting freedom to enslaved people in the colonies. Empire Day was widely observed across the Commonwealth, including Jamaica, but as the country moved toward independence, there was growing sentiment to replace it with something more reflective of Jamaica's own labour struggles.

Premier Norman Manley led this change in 1960 by introducing a bill to abolish Empire Day and replace it with Labour Day to commemorate the 1938 labour strikes. These strikes were a turning point in Jamaican history, as grossly underpaid workers across the island organized in protest. On 23 May 1938, public sector and transport workers in Kingston had joined the movement, halting activity in the capital. The unrest led to 46 deaths, 429 injuries, and numerous arrests, marking a major push for labour rights in the country. Manley's proposal to commemorate these events gained unanimous support in Parliament, officially establishing Labour Day on 23 May.

In the 1960s, Labour Day celebrations were heavily tied to politics, with the two major trade unions—the Bustamante Industrial Trade Union (JLP) and the National Workers Union (PNP)—leading marches and rallies in Kingston. However, these events often turned violent. In 1962, a politically motivated clash during a Labour Day march resulted in the death of a woman and injuries to several police officers, who resorted to using tear gas to control the crowd. Additional conflicts in 1966 led authorities to ban Labour Day marches in Kingston, marking a shift in how the holiday was observed.

Prime Minister Michael Manley redefined Labour Day in 1972 with the theme “Put Work into Labour Day.” Rather than focusing on rallies and marches, Manley encouraged Jamaicans to actively participate in community projects. His vision emphasized the role of labour in nation-building, inspiring voluntary efforts across the island. The first national project focused on beautifying the Palisadoes Road, with Manley himself participating in tree planting. The response was overwhelming, with 600 projects completed that year, cementing Labour Day as a day of national pride and community service. This momentum slowed under Prime Minister Edward Seaga in 1980, when large-scale Labour Day activities were halted, leading to an eight-year absence of organized projects. It was not until 1989, when Michael Manley returned to office, that Labour Day was fully revived. The Jamaican government consistently introduced themes to guide national activities, ensuring that community development remained central to the celebrations. Since then, Labour Day has continued as a tradition that blends historical remembrance with civic responsibility.

### Japan

A [public holiday in Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Japan "Public holidays in Japan"), Labour Day is officially conflated with [Thanksgiving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanksgiving "Thanksgiving") on 23 November, as [Labor Thanksgiving Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Thanksgiving_Day "Labor Thanksgiving Day").

### Kazakhstan

Labour Day, a [public holiday in Kazakhstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Kazakhstan "Public holidays in Kazakhstan"), is celebrated on the last Sunday in September. The holiday was officially established in late 2013. In 1995, the government of [Kazakhstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakhstan "Kazakhstan") replaced [International Workers' Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Workers%27_Day "International Workers' Day") with [Kazakhstan People's Unity Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakhstan_People%27s_Unity_Day "Kazakhstan People's Unity Day"). Kazakh president [Nursultan Nazarbayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nursultan_Nazarbayev "Nursultan Nazarbayev") also instituted a special medal that is awarded to veterans of labour on the occasion of the holiday.

Labour Day is widely celebrated across the country with official speeches, award ceremonies, cultural events, etc. It is a non-working holiday for most citizens of Kazakhstan because it always falls on a weekend.

### Kenya

A [public holiday in Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Kenya "Public holidays in Kenya"), Labour Day has been commemorated annually on May 1st since 1964, after the country gained independence in December 1963. This celebration is usually marked with song and dance as [Kenyans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya "Kenya") gather at designated locations to celebrate and listen to speeches from the leadership of the country. [Francis Atwoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Atwoli "Francis Atwoli"), Secretary General of the [Central Organization of Trade Unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Organization_of_Trade_Unions_(Kenya) "Central Organization of Trade Unions (Kenya)") (COTU) has been vocal in spearheading the celebrations in the country while advocating for favourable working conditions for the Kenyan people.

### Macau S.A.R.

In [Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macau "Macau"), 1 May is [a public holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Macau "Public holidays in Macau") and is officially known as *Dia do Trabalhador* ([Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language "Portuguese language") for 'Workers' Day').

### Malaysia

On 1 May, people in [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia") take the time to remember the economic and social accomplishments of the labour movement.

Also known as May Day, [this public holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Malaysia "Public holidays in Malaysia") is sometimes celebrated with groups organising parades, rallies or both, to promote and protect workers' rights.

### New Zealand

Labour Day ([Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_language "Māori language"): *Te Rā Whakanui i ngā Kaimahi*) is a [public holiday in New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_New_Zealand "Public holidays in New Zealand") and is held on the fourth Monday in October. Its origins are traced to the eight-hour working day movement that arose in the newly founded [Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington "Wellington") colony in 1840, primarily because of carpenter [Samuel Duncan Parnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Duncan_Parnell "Samuel Duncan Parnell")'s refusal to work more than eight hours a day. That year, Parnell reportedly told a prospective employer: "There are twenty-four hours per day given us; eight of these should be for work, eight for sleep, and the remaining eight for recreation".

The first Labour Day in New Zealand was celebrated on 28 October 1890, which marked the first anniversary of the Maritime Council, an organisation of transport and mining unions. Several thousand trade union members and supporters attended parades in the main city centres. Government employees were given the day off to attend, and many businesses closed for at least part of the day. Initially, the day was variously called Labour Day or Labour Demonstration Day.

The government legislated that the day be a public holiday through the Labour Day Act of 1899. The day was set as the second Wednesday in October and first celebrated the following year, in 1900. In 1910, the holiday was moved to the fourth Monday in October.

### Pakistan

Labour Day, or *Youm-e-Mazdoor*, is observed in [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan") on 1 May as a public holiday to recognize the contributions and rights of workers. Officially declared a holiday in 1972 under [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Pakistan "Prime Minister of Pakistan") [Zulfikar Ali Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulfikar_Ali_Bhutto "Zulfikar Ali Bhutto"), it was part of the country's first labour policy, which also introduced welfare initiatives for workers. The day is marked by rallies, seminars, and demonstrations organized by trade unions to highlight labour issues such as fair wages and safe working conditions. Government offices, banks, and many businesses remain closed in observance.

### Poland

Labour Day is a [public holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Poland "Public holidays in Poland") in Poland, celebrated on 1 May. Together with [Polish National Flag Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_National_Flag_Day "Polish National Flag Day") on 2 May and [Constitution Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3_May_Constitution_Day "3 May Constitution Day") on 3 May – the latter also being a public holiday, coinciding with the [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church "Catholic Church") holiday of [The Most Holy Virgin Mary, Queen of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Most_Holy_Virgin_Mary,_Queen_of_Poland "The Most Holy Virgin Mary, Queen of Poland") – it typically forms a long May weekend, referred to as *majówka*, widely regarded as the informal start of the spring outdoor leisure season. It is marked with picnics, barbecues, local events and travel.

### Philippines

In the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines"), Labor Day is a [public holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_the_Philippines "Public holidays in the Philippines") commemorated nationwide on 1 May. Initially observed in 1903 through a protest by the [Unión Obrera Democrática Filipina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uni%C3%B3n_Obrera_Democr%C3%A1tica_Filipina "Unión Obrera Democrática Filipina") in [Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila "Manila") during the [American colonial era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_(1898%E2%80%931946) "History of the Philippines (1898–1946)"), it was officially recognised as a holiday in 1908, with the first official commemoration taking place in 1913.

### Switzerland

In Switzerland, Labour Day on 1 May is not a [federal holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Switzerland "Public holidays in Switzerland") across the entire country, but several of the [Swiss cantons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantons_of_Switzerland "Cantons of Switzerland") have made it one of their cantonal holidays. In the [Canton of Fribourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Fribourg "Canton of Fribourg"), it is traditional for children to sing at people's doors in exchange for sweets and money.

### Taiwan

Labour Day, observed 1 May, in [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan"), is an [official holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Taiwan "Public holidays in Taiwan"), though not everybody gets a day off. [Public servants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_service "Civil service"), teachers, and students do not have this day off.

### Tajikistan

Labour Day was celebrated on 1 May in the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"), and the tradition lives on in [Tajikistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajikistan "Tajikistan") as [International Labour Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Workers%27_Day "International Workers' Day") Although Labour Day is [a working day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Tajikistan "Public holidays in Tajikistan"), folk festivals, performances and fairs organised throughout the country create a holiday atmosphere.

### Trinidad and Tobago

In [Trinidad and Tobago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinidad_and_Tobago "Trinidad and Tobago"), Labour Day is celebrated every 19 June. This [public holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Trinidad_and_Tobago "Public holidays in Trinidad and Tobago") was proposed in 1973 to be commemorated on the anniversary of the labour riots led by [Tubal Uriah Butler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tubal_Uriah_Butler "Tubal Uriah Butler") in 1937, part of the [British West Indian labour unrest of 1934–1939](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_West_Indian_labour_unrest_of_1934%E2%80%931939 "British West Indian labour unrest of 1934–1939").

### Turkey

In [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey "Turkey"), "Labour and Solidarity Day" ([Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language "Turkish language"): *Emek ve Dayanışma Günü*) became an [official holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Turkey "Public holidays in Turkey") in 2009. Prior to that, the day had been observed by workers and unions but had been banned for many years after the [1980 military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Turkish_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat "1980 Turkish coup d'état"). A turning point came in 2009, when the [Turkish government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Turkey "Government of Turkey"), led by Prime Minister [Recep Tayyip Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdo%C4%9Fan "Recep Tayyip Erdoğan"), officially recognized 1 May as a public holiday again. Demonstrations at [Taksim Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taksim_Square "Taksim Square") carry historical significance due to the [Taksim Square Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taksim_Square_massacre "Taksim Square massacre") on 1 May 1977.

### United States

In the United States, [Labor Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Day "Labor Day") is a [federal holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_holidays_in_the_United_States "Federal holidays in the United States") and [public holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_the_United_States "Public holidays in the United States"), observed on the first Monday of September. All banks, schools, and most places of business are closed in celebration of Labor Day. The holiday is viewed as the informal end of summer. Many schools open for the year on the day or week after Labor Day.
