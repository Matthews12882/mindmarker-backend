# Charles III

**Charles III** (Charles Philip Arthur George; born 14 November 1948) is [King of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_the_United_Kingdom "King of the United Kingdom") and 14 other [Commonwealth realms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_realm "Commonwealth realm").

Charles is the eldest son of Queen [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II "Elizabeth II") and [Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Philip,_Duke_of_Edinburgh "Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh"). He was born during the reign of his maternal grandfather, King [George VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_VI "George VI"), and became [heir apparent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heir_apparent "Heir apparent") following his mother's accession in 1952. Charles was created [Prince of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Wales "Prince of Wales") in 1958 and [his investiture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investiture_of_Charles,_Prince_of_Wales "Investiture of Charles, Prince of Wales") was held in 1969. He was educated at [Cheam School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheam_School "Cheam School") and [Gordonstoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordonstoun "Gordonstoun"), and later spent six months at the [Timbertop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbertop "Timbertop") campus of [Geelong Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geelong_Grammar_School "Geelong Grammar School") in Victoria, Australia. After completing a history degree at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge"), he served in the [Royal Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Air_Force "Royal Air Force") and the [Royal Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Navy "Royal Navy") from 1971 to 1976. [He married](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedding_of_Prince_Charles_and_Lady_Diana_Spencer "Wedding of Prince Charles and Lady Diana Spencer") [Lady Diana Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Diana_Spencer "Lady Diana Spencer") in 1981 and they had two sons, [William](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William,_Prince_of_Wales "William, Prince of Wales") in 1982 and [Harry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Harry,_Duke_of_Sussex "Prince Harry, Duke of Sussex") in 1984. Charles and Diana divorced in August 1996 after years of estrangement and well-publicised extramarital affairs. [Diana died](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Diana,_Princess_of_Wales "Death of Diana, Princess of Wales") the following year from injuries sustained in a car crash. In 2005, [Charles married](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedding_of_Prince_Charles_and_Camilla_Parker_Bowles "Wedding of Prince Charles and Camilla Parker Bowles") his long-time partner, [Camilla Parker Bowles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camilla_Parker_Bowles "Camilla Parker Bowles").

As heir apparent, Charles undertook official duties and engagements on behalf of his mother and represented the United Kingdom on visits abroad. He founded the [Prince's Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince%27s_Trust "Prince's Trust") in 1976, sponsored [the Prince's Charities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prince%27s_Charities "The Prince's Charities") and became patron or president of more than 800 other charities and organisations. He advocated for the conservation of historic buildings and the importance of traditional architecture in society, and in that vein generated the experimental new town of [Poundbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poundbury "Poundbury"). An [environmentalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentalist "Environmentalist"), Charles supported [organic farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_farming "Organic farming") and action to address [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change "Climate change") during his time as manager of the [Duchy of Cornwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Cornwall "Duchy of Cornwall") estates, [earning him awards and recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_received_by_Charles_III "List of awards received by Charles III"). He has also been a prominent critic of [genetically modified food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetically_modified_food "Genetically modified food"), and has drawn scrutiny for his support of [alternative medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_medicine "Alternative medicine"). He has [authored or co-authored 17 books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliography_of_Charles_III "Bibliography of Charles III").

Charles became king upon [his mother's death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_state_funeral_of_Elizabeth_II "Death and state funeral of Elizabeth II") in 2022. At the age of 73, he was the oldest person to accede to the British throne, after having been the longest-serving heir apparent and Prince of Wales in British history. Significant events of his reign have included [his coronation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation_of_Charles_III_and_Camilla "Coronation of Charles III and Camilla") in 2023; his cancer diagnosis in 2024, which temporarily suspended planned public engagements; and the removal of his brother [Andrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Mountbatten-Windsor "Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor")'s remaining titles, styles, and honours in 2025.

## Early life, family, and education

Charles was born at 9:14 pm on 14 November 1948 by [caesarean section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesarean_section "Caesarean section") at [Buckingham Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckingham_Palace "Buckingham Palace"), during the reign of his maternal grandfather, King [George VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_VI "George VI"). He was the first child of Princess Elizabeth, Duchess of Edinburgh (later Queen [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II "Elizabeth II")), and [Philip, Duke of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip,_Duke_of_Edinburgh "Philip, Duke of Edinburgh"). He was [christened](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_baptism "Infant baptism") Charles Philip Arthur George on 15 December in the Music Room at Buckingham Palace by the [archbishop of Canterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury "Archbishop of Canterbury"), [Geoffrey Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Fisher "Geoffrey Fisher"). He has three younger siblings: [Anne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne,_Princess_Royal "Anne, Princess Royal") (born 1950), [Andrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Mountbatten-Windsor "Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor") (born 1960), and [Edward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Edward,_Duke_of_Edinburgh "Prince Edward, Duke of Edinburgh") (born 1964).

[George VI died](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_VI_died "George VI died") on 6 February 1952, after which Charles's mother acceded to the throne as Elizabeth II and he became [heir apparent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heir_apparent "Heir apparent"). Under a charter issued by [Edward III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_III "Edward III") in 1337, and as the monarch's eldest son, he automatically assumed the titles of [Duke of Cornwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_of_Cornwall "Duke of Cornwall") and, in the Scottish peerage, [Duke of Rothesay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_of_Rothesay "Duke of Rothesay"), [Earl of Carrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_of_Carrick "Earl of Carrick"), [Baron of Renfrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baron_of_Renfrew_(title) "Baron of Renfrew (title)"), [Lord of the Isles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_of_the_Isles "Lord of the Isles"), and [Prince and Great Steward of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_and_Great_Steward_of_Scotland "Prince and Great Steward of Scotland"). He attended [his mother's coronation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation_of_Elizabeth_II "Coronation of Elizabeth II") at [Westminster Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_Abbey "Westminster Abbey") on 2 June 1953. He was later created [Prince of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Wales "Prince of Wales") in 1958.

When Charles was five, Catherine Peebles was appointed as his [governess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governess "Governess") to oversee his education at Buckingham Palace. He began attending [Hill House School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_House_School "Hill House School") in [West London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_London "West London") in November 1956, becoming the first heir apparent to be educated at a school rather than by private tutors. He did not receive preferential treatment from the school's founder and headmaster, [Stuart Townend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Townend_(headmaster) "Stuart Townend (headmaster)"), who encouraged the Queen to have Charles train in [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football "Association football"), noting that boys on the pitch were never deferential to anyone. Charles later attended two of his father's former schools: [Cheam School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheam_School "Cheam School") in [Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampshire "Hampshire") from 1958, followed by [Gordonstoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordonstoun "Gordonstoun") in [Moray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moray "Moray") in Scotland, where he began classes in April 1962. He became patron of Gordonstoun in May 2024.

[Jonathan Dimbleby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Dimbleby "Jonathan Dimbleby")'s [authorised 1994 biography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prince_of_Wales:_A_Biography "The Prince of Wales: A Biography") described Elizabeth and Philip as physically and emotionally distant parents, and criticised Philip for disregarding Charles's sensitive nature, including insisting that Charles attend Gordonstoun, where he was bullied. Although Charles reportedly referred to the school as "[Colditz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oflag_IV-C "Oflag IV-C") in [kilts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilt "Kilt")", he later praised Gordonstoun for teaching him "a great deal about myself and my own abilities and disabilities". In a 1975 interview Charles said he was "glad" to have attended the school and that its "toughness" had been "much exaggerated".

In 1966, Charles spent two terms at the [Timbertop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbertop "Timbertop") campus of [Geelong Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geelong_Grammar_School "Geelong Grammar School") in Victoria, Australia, during which he visited Papua New Guinea on a school trip with his history tutor, Michael Collins Persse. Whilst based at Timbertop, he frequently travelled to the main [Geelong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geelong "Geelong") campus as part of his schooling arrangements, residing in Cuthbertson House, one of the school's boarding houses. Charles later described his time at Timbertop as the most enjoyable part of his education. On returning to Gordonstoun, he became [head boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_boy "Head boy"), and left in 1967 with six [GCE O-levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GCE_O-levels "GCE O-levels") and two [A-levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-level "A-level") in history and French, at grades B and C respectively. Reflecting on his schooling, Charles later remarked that he "didn't enjoy school as much as I might have", adding that he was "happier at home than anywhere else".

Breaking royal tradition, Charles proceeded directly to university after completing his A‑levels rather than joining the [British Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Armed_Forces "British Armed Forces"). In October 1967, he was admitted to the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") to study [archaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology "Archaeology") and [anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology "Anthropology") for the first part of the [Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripos "Tripos") at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge"), later switching to [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faculty_of_History,_University_of_Cambridge "Faculty of History, University of Cambridge"). During his second year he spent one term at the [University College of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_of_Wales "University College of Wales") in [Aberystwyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberystwyth "Aberystwyth"), studying [Welsh history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_history "Welsh history") and [Welsh language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language "Welsh language"). Charles became the first British heir apparent to earn a university degree, graduating from Cambridge in June 1970 with a Bachelor of Arts (BA) degree with [lower second-class honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_second-class_honours "Lower second-class honours") (2:2). Following standard practice, his BA was promoted to a [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts_(Oxbridge_and_Dublin) "Master of Arts (Oxbridge and Dublin)") (MA Cantab) in August 1975.

## Prince of Wales

Charles was created [Prince of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Wales "Prince of Wales") and [Earl of Chester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_of_Chester "Earl of Chester") on 26 July 1958, although [his investiture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investiture_of_Charles,_Prince_of_Wales "Investiture of Charles, Prince of Wales") did not take place until 1 July 1969, when he was crowned by his mother in a televised ceremony at [Caernarfon Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caernarfon_Castle "Caernarfon Castle"). The [event was controversial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controversy_of_the_Prince_of_Wales_title "Controversy of the Prince of Wales title") in Wales amid rising [Welsh nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_nationalist "Welsh nationalist") sentiment. He [took his seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introduction_(House_of_Lords) "Introduction (House of Lords)") in the [House of Lords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Lords "House of Lords") the following year, delivering his [maiden speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_speech "Maiden speech") on 13 June 1974, the first royal to speak from the floor since the future [Edward VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_VII "Edward VII") in 1884. He addressed the House again in 1975.

Charles increasingly undertook public duties, founding [the Prince's Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prince%27s_Trust "The Prince's Trust") in 1976 and travelling to the United States in 1981. In the mid-1970s he expressed interest in serving as [Governor-General of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_Australia "Governor-General of Australia"), following a suggestion by the [Australian prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_prime_minister "Australian prime minister"), [Malcolm Fraser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Fraser "Malcolm Fraser"). The proposal was ultimately abandoned owing to a lack of public enthusiasm. Charles later remarked, "so, what are you supposed to think when you are prepared to do something to help and you are just told you're not wanted?"

### Military training and career

Charles served in the [Royal Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Air_Force "Royal Air Force") (RAF) and the [Royal Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Navy "Royal Navy") during the 1970s. His military training began in 1969, in his second year at Cambridge, when he joined the [Cambridge University Air Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University_Air_Squadron "Cambridge University Air Squadron") and learned to fly the [Chipmunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Canada_DHC-1_Chipmunk "De Havilland Canada DHC-1 Chipmunk"). He was presented with his [RAF wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircrew_brevet "Aircrew brevet") in August 1971.

After the [passing-out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passing_out_(military) "Passing out (military)") parade that September, Charles embarked on a naval career and undertook a six-week course at the [Royal Naval College, Dartmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Naval_College,_Dartmouth "Royal Naval College, Dartmouth"). He subsequently served from 1971 to 1972 on the guided-missile destroyer [HMS *Norfolk*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Norfolk_(D21) "HMS Norfolk (D21)"), and on the frigates [HMS *Minerva*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Minerva_(F45) "HMS Minerva (F45)") from 1972 to 1973 and [HMS *Jupiter*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Jupiter_(F60) "HMS Jupiter (F60)") in 1974. That same year he qualified as a helicopter pilot at [RNAS Yeovilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNAS_Yeovilton "RNAS Yeovilton"), and during his helicopter training completed commando instruction at the [Commando Training Centre Royal Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commando_Training_Centre_Royal_Marines "Commando Training Centre Royal Marines") at Lympstone. Charles then joined [845 Naval Air Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/845_Naval_Air_Squadron "845 Naval Air Squadron"), a [Royal Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Marines "Royal Marines") air support unit of the Fleet Air Arm, serving as a pilot aboard [HMS *Hermes*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Hermes_(R12) "HMS Hermes (R12)") and flying the Royal Marines commando variant of the [Westland Wessex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westland_Wessex#Variants "Westland Wessex") helicopter.

Charles spent his final ten months of active naval service commanding the coastal minehunter [HMS *Bronington*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Bronington "HMS Bronington"), beginning on 9 February 1976. He retired from active service later that year with the rank of [Commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_(Royal_Navy) "Commander (Royal Navy)"). Two years later he undertook the parachute training course at [RAF Brize Norton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Brize_Norton "RAF Brize Norton"), having been appointed colonel-in-chief of the [Parachute Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parachute_Regiment_(United_Kingdom) "Parachute Regiment (United Kingdom)") in 1977, and was a member of Parachute Course 841a. Charles gave up flying after crash-landing a [BAe 146](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAe_146 "BAe 146") in [Islay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islay "Islay") in 1994, when, as a passenger invited to fly the aircraft, he was at the controls; a board of inquiry found the crew negligent.

### Relationships and marriages

#### Bachelorhood

In his youth, Charles was romantically linked to several women. His girlfriends included Georgiana Russell, the daughter of [Sir John Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_John_Russell "Sir John Russell"), who was the [British ambassador to Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_ambassador_to_Spain "British ambassador to Spain"); [Lady Jane Wellesley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Jane_Wellesley "Lady Jane Wellesley"); Davina Sheffield; [Lady Sarah Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Sarah_Spencer "Lady Sarah Spencer"); and [Camilla Shand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camilla_Shand "Camilla Shand"), who later became his second wife.

Charles's great-uncle [Lord Mountbatten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Mountbatten "Lord Mountbatten") advised him to "sow his wild oats and have as many affairs as he can before settling down", but, for a wife, he "should choose a suitable, attractive, and sweet-charactered girl before she has met anyone else she might fall for ... It is disturbing for women to have experiences if they have to remain on a pedestal after marriage". Early in 1974, Mountbatten began corresponding with 25-year-old Charles about a potential marriage to his granddaughter, [Amanda Knatchbull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Knatchbull "Amanda Knatchbull"). Charles wrote to Amanda's mother, [Lady Brabourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patricia_Knatchbull,_2nd_Countess_Mountbatten_of_Burma "Patricia Knatchbull, 2nd Countess Mountbatten of Burma"), who was also his godmother, expressing interest in her daughter. Lady Brabourne replied approvingly, but suggested that a courtship with a 16-year-old was premature.

Four years later, Mountbatten arranged for Amanda and himself to accompany Charles on his 1980 visit to India. Both fathers, however, objected: Prince Philip feared that his famous uncle would eclipse Charles, while [Lord Brabourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Knatchbull,_7th_Baron_Brabourne "John Knatchbull, 7th Baron Brabourne") warned that a joint visit would concentrate media attention on the cousins before they could decide whether to become a couple.

In August 1979, before Charles was due to depart alone for India, [Mountbatten was assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Lord_Mountbatten "Assassination of Lord Mountbatten") by the [Provisional Irish Republican Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Irish_Republican_Army "Provisional Irish Republican Army"). When Charles returned, he proposed to Amanda. But in addition to her grandfather, she had lost her [paternal grandmother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doreen_Knatchbull,_Baroness_Brabourne "Doreen Knatchbull, Baroness Brabourne") and [younger brother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Knatchbull "Nicholas Knatchbull") in the bomb attack, and was now reluctant to join the royal family.

#### Lady Diana Spencer

Charles first met [Lady Diana Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Diana_Spencer "Lady Diana Spencer") in 1977, while he was visiting her home, [Althorp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Althorp "Althorp"). He was then the companion of her elder sister Sarah and did not consider Diana romantically until mid-1980. While Charles and Diana were sitting together on a bale of hay at a friend's barbecue in July, she mentioned that he had looked forlorn and in need of care at the [funeral of Lord Mountbatten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funeral_of_Lord_Mountbatten "Funeral of Lord Mountbatten"). Soon, according to Dimbleby, "without any apparent surge in feeling, he began to think seriously of her as a potential bride" and she accompanied him on visits to [Balmoral Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balmoral_Castle "Balmoral Castle") and [Sandringham House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandringham_House "Sandringham House").

Charles's cousin [Norton Knatchbull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norton_Knatchbull,_8th_Baron_Brabourne "Norton Knatchbull, 8th Baron Brabourne") and his wife told Charles that Diana appeared awestruck by his position and that he did not seem to be in love with her. Meanwhile, the couple's continuing courtship attracted intense attention from the press and paparazzi. When Charles's father told him that the media speculation would injure Diana's reputation if Charles did not come to a decision about marrying her soon, and realising that she was a suitable royal bride (according to Mountbatten's criteria), Charles construed his father's advice as a warning to proceed without further delay. He proposed to Diana in February 1981, with their engagement becoming official on 24 February; the wedding took place at [St Paul's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Paul%27s_Cathedral "St Paul's Cathedral") on 29 July. Upon his marriage, Charles reduced his voluntary tax contribution from the profits of the Duchy of Cornwall from 50 per cent to 25 per cent. The couple lived at [Kensington Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kensington_Palace "Kensington Palace") and [Highgrove House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highgrove_House "Highgrove House"), near [Tetbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetbury "Tetbury"), and had two children: [William](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William,_Prince_of_Wales "William, Prince of Wales"), in 1982, and [Harry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Harry,_Duke_of_Sussex "Prince Harry, Duke of Sussex"), in 1984. As of 2025, Charles has an estranged relationship with his son Harry, who relinquished his royal family obligations and moved to the United States in 2020.

Within five years, the marriage was in trouble due to the couple's incompatibility and near 13-year age difference. In 1986 Charles had fully resumed his affair with former girlfriend, Camilla Parker Bowles. In a video tape recorded by [Peter Settelen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Settelen "Peter Settelen") in 1992, Diana admitted that, from 1985 to 1986, she had been "deeply in love with someone who worked in this environment." It was assumed that she was referring to [Barry Mannakee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Mannakee "Barry Mannakee"), who had been transferred to the Diplomatic Protection Squad in 1986, after his managers determined his relationship with Diana had been inappropriate. Diana later commenced a relationship with Major [James Hewitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hewitt "James Hewitt"), the family's former riding instructor.

Charles and Diana's evident discomfort in each other's company led to them being dubbed "[The Glums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Take_It_from_Here#The_Glums "Take It from Here")" by the press. Diana exposed Charles's affair with Parker Bowles in a book by Andrew Morton, *[Diana: Her True Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diana:_Her_True_Story_(book) "Diana: Her True Story (book)")*. Audio tapes of [her own extramarital flirtations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squidgygate "Squidgygate") also surfaced, as did persistent suggestions that Hewitt is Prince Harry's father, based on a physical similarity between Hewitt and Harry. However, Harry had already been born by the time Diana's affair with Hewitt began.

In December 1992, the [British prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_prime_minister "British prime minister"), [John Major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Major "John Major"), announced the couple's legal separation in the [House of Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_the_United_Kingdom "House of Commons of the United Kingdom"). Early the following year, the British press published transcripts of a passionate, bugged telephone conversation between Charles and Parker Bowles that had taken place in 1989, which was dubbed "[Tampongate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampongate "Tampongate")" and "Camillagate". Charles subsequently sought public understanding in a television film with Dimbleby, *[Charles: The Private Man, the Public Role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles:_The_Private_Man,_the_Public_Role "Charles: The Private Man, the Public Role")*, broadcast in June 1994. In an interview in the film, Charles confirmed his own extramarital affair with Parker Bowles, saying that he had rekindled their association in 1986, only after his marriage to Diana had "irretrievably broken down". This was followed by Diana's own admission of marital troubles in [an interview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Interview_with_HRH_The_Princess_of_Wales "An Interview with HRH The Princess of Wales") with journalist [Martin Bashir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Bashir "Martin Bashir") on the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") current affairs programme *[Panorama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panorama_(British_TV_programme) "Panorama (British TV programme)")*, broadcast in November 1995. Referring to Charles's relationship with Parker Bowles, she said, "well, there were three of us in this marriage. So, it was a bit crowded." She also expressed doubt about her husband's suitability for kingship. Charles and Diana divorced on 28 August 1996, after being advised by the Queen in December 1995 to end the marriage. The couple shared custody of their children.

Diana [died following a car crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Diana,_Princess_of_Wales "Death of Diana, Princess of Wales") in Paris on 31 August 1997. Charles flew to Paris with Diana's sisters to accompany her body back to Britain. In 2003, Diana's butler [Paul Burrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Burrell "Paul Burrell") published a note that he alleged had been written by Diana in 1995, in which there were allegations that Charles was "planning 'an accident' in [Diana's] car, brake failure and serious head injury", so that he could remarry. She had allegedly expressed similar concerns in October 1995 to [Lord Mishcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Mishcon "Lord Mishcon"), her solicitor, that "reliable sources" had told her "that she and Camilla would be put aside" for Charles to marry [Tiggy Legge-Bourke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiggy_Legge-Bourke "Tiggy Legge-Bourke"). When questioned by the [Metropolitan Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Police "Metropolitan Police") inquiry team as a part of [Operation Paget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Paget "Operation Paget"), Charles told the authorities that he did not know about his former wife's note from 1995 and could not understand why she had those feelings. The allegations were later revealed to have been among the smears spread by Bashir to secure the interview with Diana for the BBC.

#### Camilla Parker Bowles

Because Charles and Parker Bowles were romantically involved periodically, both before and during their respective first marriages, their relationship received criticism from the public and the media. Following both of their divorces, Charles declared his relationship with Parker Bowles was "non-negotiable" and appointed [Mark Bolland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Bolland "Mark Bolland") to enhance Parker Bowles's public profile.

In 1999 Charles and Parker Bowles made their first public appearance as a couple at the [Ritz London Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritz_London_Hotel "Ritz London Hotel") and the following year, she met Queen Elizabeth II at the 60th birthday party of former [King Constantine II of Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Constantine_II_of_Greece "King Constantine II of Greece"), which was seen as an apparent seal of approval by the Queen on the relationship. In 2003, Parker Bowles moved into Charles's official residence, [Clarence House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarence_House "Clarence House") and she accompanied Charles on almost all of his official events, including the annual [Highland Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Games "Highland Games") in Scotland. Former Archbishop of Canterbury, [George Carey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Carey "George Carey"), told *The Times* that Charles and Parker Bowles should marry, "He is heir to the throne and he loves her. The natural thing is that they should get married." Their engagement was announced on 10 February 2005. The Queen's consent to the marriage – as required by the [Royal Marriages Act 1772](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Marriages_Act_1772 "Royal Marriages Act 1772") – was recorded in a [Privy Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_Majesty%27s_Most_Honourable_Privy_Council "Her Majesty's Most Honourable Privy Council") meeting on 2 March. In Canada, the [Department of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Justice_(Canada) "Department of Justice (Canada)") determined the consent of the [Queen's Privy Council for Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen%27s_Privy_Council_for_Canada "Queen's Privy Council for Canada") was not required, as the union would not produce any heirs to the [Canadian throne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_throne "Canadian throne").

Charles was the only member of the royal family to have a [civil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_marriage "Civil marriage"), rather than a church, wedding in England. British government documents from the 1950s and 1960s, published by the BBC, stated that such a marriage was illegal; these claims were dismissed by Charles's spokesman and explained by the sitting government to have been repealed by the Registration Service Act 1953.

The union was scheduled to take place in a civil ceremony at [Windsor Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windsor_Castle "Windsor Castle"), with a subsequent religious blessing at the castle's [St George's Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_George%27s_Chapel "St George's Chapel"). The wedding venue was changed to [Windsor Guildhall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windsor_Guildhall "Windsor Guildhall") after it was realised a civil marriage at Windsor Castle would oblige the venue to be available to anyone who wished to be married there. Four days before the event, it was postponed from the originally scheduled date of 8 April until the following day in order to allow Charles and some of the invited dignitaries to attend the [funeral of Pope John Paul II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funeral_of_Pope_John_Paul_II "Funeral of Pope John Paul II").

Charles's parents did not attend the marriage ceremony; the Queen's reluctance to attend possibly arose from her position as [Supreme Governor of the Church of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Governor_of_the_Church_of_England "Supreme Governor of the Church of England"). However, his parents did attend the [service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_service "Church service") of [blessing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blessing "Blessing") and held a reception for the newlyweds at Windsor Castle. The blessing by Archbishop of Canterbury [Rowan Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowan_Williams "Rowan Williams") was televised.

Since their marriage in 2005, Charles has paid tribute to Camilla repeatedly in public letters, speeches and interviews, thanking her for "steadfast support" and referring to her as "my darling wife".

### Official duties

In 1965, Charles undertook his first public engagement by attending a student garden party at the [Palace of Holyroodhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Holyroodhouse "Palace of Holyroodhouse"). During his time as Prince of Wales, he undertook official duties on behalf of the Queen, completing 10,934 engagements between 2002 and 2022. He officiated at [investitures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investiture "Investiture") and attended the funerals of foreign dignitaries.

Charles made regular tours of Wales, fulfilling a week of engagements each summer, and attending important national occasions, such as opening the [Senedd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senedd_building "Senedd building"). The six trustees of the [Royal Collection Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Collection_Trust "Royal Collection Trust") met three times a year under his chairmanship. Charles also represented his mother at the independence celebrations in Fiji in 1970, The Bahamas in 1973, Papua New Guinea in 1975, Zimbabwe in 1980 and Brunei in 1984.
In 1983, a man named [Christopher John Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_John_Lewis "Christopher John Lewis"), who had fired a shot with a [.22 rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.22_rifle ".22 rifle") at the Queen in 1981, attempted to escape from a psychiatric hospital in order to assassinate Charles, who was visiting New Zealand with Diana and William. While Charles was visiting Australia on [Australia Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_Day "Australia Day") in January 1994, [David Kang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Kang "David Kang") fired two shots at him from a [starting pistol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starting_pistol "Starting pistol") in protest against the treatment of several hundred Cambodian asylum-seekers held in detention camps. In 1995 Charles became the first member of the royal family to visit the Republic of Ireland in an official capacity. In 1997 he represented the Queen at the [Hong Kong handover ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_handover_ceremony "Hong Kong handover ceremony").

At the funeral of Pope John Paul II in 2005, Charles caused controversy when he shook hands with Zimbabwean president [Robert Mugabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Mugabe "Robert Mugabe"), who had been seated next to him. Charles's office subsequently released a statement saying that he could not avoid shaking Mugabe's hand and that he "finds the current Zimbabwean regime abhorrent".

Charles represented the Queen at [the opening ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Commonwealth_Games_opening_ceremony "2010 Commonwealth Games opening ceremony") of the [2010 Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Commonwealth_Games "2010 Commonwealth Games") in Delhi, India. In November 2010 he and Camilla were indirectly involved in [student protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_Kingdom_student_protests "2010 United Kingdom student protests") when their car was attacked by protesters. In November 2013 he represented the Queen for the first time at a [Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Heads_of_Government_Meeting "Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting"), in [Colombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo "Colombo"), Sri Lanka.

Charles and Camilla made their first joint trip to the Republic of Ireland in May 2015. The British Embassy called the trip an important step in "promoting peace and reconciliation". During the trip, he shook hands in [Galway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galway "Galway") with [Gerry Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerry_Adams "Gerry Adams"), leader of [Sinn Féin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinn_F%C3%A9in "Sinn Féin") and widely believed to be the leader of the [IRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Republican_Army "Irish Republican Army"), the militant group that had assassinated Lord Mountbatten in 1979. The event was described by the media as a "historic handshake" and a "significant moment for [Anglo-Irish relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Irish_relations "Anglo-Irish relations")".

Commonwealth heads of government decided at their [2018 meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Commonwealth_Heads_of_Government_Meeting "2018 Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting") that Charles would be the next Head of the Commonwealth after the Queen. The head is chosen and therefore not hereditary. In March 2019, at the request of the British government, Charles and Camilla went on an official tour of Cuba, making them the first British royals to visit the country. The tour was seen as an effort to form a closer relationship between Cuba and the United Kingdom.

Charles contracted [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19 "COVID-19") during the [pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_United_Kingdom "COVID-19 pandemic in the United Kingdom") in March 2020. Several newspapers were critical that Charles and Camilla were tested promptly at a time when many [National Health Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Health_Service "National Health Service") doctors, nurses and patients had been unable to be tested expeditiously. He tested positive for COVID-19 for a second time in February 2022. He and Camilla, who also tested positive, had received doses of a [COVID-19 vaccine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_vaccine "COVID-19 vaccine") in February 2021.

Charles attended the November 2021 ceremonies to mark [Barbados's transition into a parliamentary republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republicanism_in_Barbados#2021_Constitutional_amendment "Republicanism in Barbados"), abolishing the position of [monarch of Barbados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarch_of_Barbados "Monarch of Barbados"). He was invited by Prime Minister [Mia Mottley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mia_Mottley "Mia Mottley") as the future Head of the Commonwealth; it was the first time that a member of the royal family attended the transition of a realm to a republic. In May of the following year, Charles attended the [State Opening of the British Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_State_Opening_of_Parliament "2022 State Opening of Parliament"), delivering the Queen's Speech on behalf of his mother, as a [counsellor of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counsellor_of_state "Counsellor of state").

## Reign

Charles acceded to the British throne on [his mother's death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_state_funeral_of_Elizabeth_II "Death and state funeral of Elizabeth II") on 8 September 2022. He was the longest-serving British heir apparent, having surpassed Edward VII's record of 59 years on 20 April 2011. Charles was the oldest person to succeed to the British throne, at the age of 73, surpassing the previous record holder, [William IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_IV "William IV"), who was 64 when he became king in 1830.

Charles gave his first speech to the nation as monarch at 6 pm on 9 September, in which he paid tribute to his mother and announced the appointment of his elder son, William, as Prince of Wales. The following day, the [Accession Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_Council "Accession Council") publicly [proclaimed Charles as king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_accession_of_Charles_III "Proclamation of accession of Charles III"), the ceremony being televised for the first time. Attendees included Queen Camilla, Prince William and the British prime minister, [Liz Truss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liz_Truss "Liz Truss"), along with her six living predecessors. The proclamation was also read out by local authorities around the United Kingdom. Other realms signed and read their own proclamations, as did Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland, the [British Overseas Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Overseas_Territories "British Overseas Territories"), the [Crown Dependencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Dependencies "Crown Dependencies"), [Canadian provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_provinces "Canadian provinces") and [Australian states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_states "Australian states").

In November 2022, the King and Queen hosted the [South African president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_president "South African president"), [Cyril Ramaphosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyril_Ramaphosa "Cyril Ramaphosa"), during the first official state visit to the United Kingdom of Charles III's reign. The following March, they undertook a state visit to Germany, where Charles became the first British monarch to address the [Bundestag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundestag "Bundestag"). On 6 May 2023, [Charles and Camilla's coronation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_and_Camilla%27s_coronation "Charles and Camilla's coronation") took place at Westminster Abbey, in a ceremony planned for many years under the code name *Operation Golden Orb.* Prior to Charles's accession, reports suggested that the coronation would be "shorter, smaller, less expensive, and more representative of different faiths and community groups than that of his mother in 1953, reflecting the King's wish to acknowledge the ethnic diversity of modern Britain". Nonetheless, the coronation remained a [Church of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_England "Church of England") rite, including the [coronation oath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation_oath "Coronation oath"), the [anointing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anointing "Anointing"), crowning, investing with the [regalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regalia "Regalia"), and [enthronement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enthronement "Enthronement").

In July 2023, the royal couple attended a [national service of thanksgiving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presentation_of_the_Honours_of_Scotland_to_Charles_III "Presentation of the Honours of Scotland to Charles III") at [St Giles' Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Giles%27_Cathedral "St Giles' Cathedral"), where Charles was presented with the [Honours of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honours_of_Scotland "Honours of Scotland"). That same month, the King asked for the profits from Britain's growing fleet of offshore wind-farms to be used for the "wider public good" rather than as extra funding for the monarchy, and it was announced that the monarchy's share of [Crown Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Estate "Crown Estate") net profits would be reduced to 12 per cent. In September, Charles became the first British monarch to give a speech from the [French Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Senate "French Senate") chamber during a [state visit to France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_visit_by_Charles_III_to_France "State visit by Charles III to France"). The following month [he visited Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_visit_by_Charles_III_to_Kenya "State visit by Charles III to Kenya"), where he faced calls to apologise for colonial abuses; at a state banquet he acknowledged "abhorrent and unjustifiable acts of violence" but did not issue a formal apology.

In May 2024, the British prime minister, [Rishi Sunak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rishi_Sunak "Rishi Sunak"), asked the King to call [a general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Kingdom_general_election "2024 United Kingdom general election"); subsequently, royal engagements that could divert attention from the election campaign were postponed. In June 2024, Charles and Camilla travelled to [Normandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normandy "Normandy") to attend the 80th anniversary commemorations of [D-Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-Day "D-Day"). The same month, the King received [Emperor Naruhito of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Naruhito_of_Japan "Emperor Naruhito of Japan") during his state visit to the United Kingdom. In July, the annual Holyrood Week, which is usually spent in Scotland, was shortened so that Charles could return to London and appoint a new prime minister following the general election. After Sunak's [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK) "Conservative Party (UK)") lost the election to the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_(UK) "Labour Party (UK)") led by Sir [Keir Starmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keir_Starmer "Keir Starmer"), the King appointed Starmer as prime minister.

In October 2024, [the King and Queen toured Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_royal_tour_of_Australia "2024 royal tour of Australia") and [Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samoa "Samoa"); Australia was the first Commonwealth realm Charles visited since his accession. In Samoa, he attended the [Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Commonwealth_Heads_of_Government_Meeting "2024 Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting") for the first time as Head of the Commonwealth. The tour was significantly scaled back owing to his cancer diagnosis, with a planned visit to New Zealand among the cancelled events. It was reported that Charles temporarily paused his cancer treatment during the tour.

In March 2025, the King received Ukrainian president [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volodymyr_Zelenskyy "Volodymyr Zelenskyy") at [Sandringham House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandringham_House "Sandringham House"), just days after the [Trump–Zelenskyy Oval Office meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump%E2%80%93Zelenskyy_Oval_Office_meeting "Trump–Zelenskyy Oval Office meeting"). Zelenskyy later told *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* that Charles played a quiet but influential role in altering US president [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump")'s attitude toward Ukraine.

The King and Queen initially cancelled a planned visit to [Vatican City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatican_City "Vatican City") during [their state visit to Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_visit_by_Charles_III_to_Italy "State visit by Charles III to Italy"), but on 9 April 2025 – their 20th wedding anniversary – they visited [Pope Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Francis "Pope Francis") at [Domus Sanctae Marthae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domus_Sanctae_Marthae "Domus Sanctae Marthae") as he was recovering from pneumonia. Francis died 12 days later.

The King, accompanied by the Queen, made [his first visit to Canada as monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_royal_visit_to_Canada "2025 royal visit to Canada") in May 2025 at the invitation of Prime Minister [Mark Carney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Carney "Mark Carney"), a trip that took place during a period of tension with the United States after President Trump made comments questioning Canada's sovereignty. During the visit, [he opened](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Speech_from_the_Throne "2025 Speech from the Throne") the [45th Canadian Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/45th_Canadian_Parliament "45th Canadian Parliament") and delivered the Speech from the Throne, the first time a Canadian monarch had done so in person since 1977.

In June 2025, the King approved the decommissioning of the [British Royal Train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Royal_Train "British Royal Train") ahead of its maintenance contract ending in 2027. Described by the [Keeper of the Privy Purse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keeper_of_the_Privy_Purse "Keeper of the Privy Purse") as part of a commitment to "fiscal discipline", the decision marked the end of 180 years of the royal family's use of a dedicated royal train.

In October 2025, during a [state visit to the Holy See](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_visit_by_Charles_III_to_the_Holy_See "State visit by Charles III to the Holy See"), Charles became the first British monarch to pray alongside a pope since the [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation "Reformation"), joining [Pope Leo XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIV "Pope Leo XIV") for a church service in the [Sistine Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sistine_Chapel "Sistine Chapel") of the [Apostolic Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_Palace "Apostolic Palace") in Vatican City. Later that month, Charles undertook his first official engagement in support of the [LGBT+ community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT%2B_community "LGBT+ community"), unveiling "[An Opened Letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Opened_Letter "An Opened Letter")", the UK's first national memorial honouring LGBT armed forces veterans, at the [National Memorial Arboretum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Memorial_Arboretum "National Memorial Arboretum") in [Staffordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staffordshire "Staffordshire"). On 30 October, amid continuing controversy surrounding [his brother Andrew's association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relationship_of_Prince_Andrew_and_Jeffrey_Epstein "Relationship of Prince Andrew and Jeffrey Epstein") with the American financier and child sex offender [Jeffrey Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein "Jeffrey Epstein"), the King initiated a formal process to remove his style, titles and honours. Following Andrew's arrest on 19 February 2026 on suspicion of [misconduct in public office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misconduct_in_public_office "Misconduct in public office"), the King expressed his "deepest concern" and stated that "the law must take its course".

In April 2026, during [a state visit to the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_visit_by_Charles_III_to_the_United_States "State visit by Charles III to the United States"), Charles became the second British monarch to address a [Joint Meeting of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Meeting_of_Congress "Joint Meeting of Congress"), after his mother [Elizabeth II in 1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_state_visit_by_Elizabeth_II_to_the_United_States "1991 state visit by Elizabeth II to the United States"). His speech received around a dozen standing ovations. In May, Charles visited [Golders Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golders_Green "Golders Green") after the [previous month's stabbings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Golders_Green_attack "2026 Golders Green attack"), meeting victims at a Jewish Care charity centre, the [Chief Rabbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Rabbi "Chief Rabbi"), and members of the community's medical and security response teams. The next month, Buckingham Palace announced that the King would publish his personal tax bills, saying the move was intended to enhance "clarity and accessibility", making him the first UK head of state to do so. In July, the King, acting on the advice of Deputy Prime Minister [David Lammy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lammy "David Lammy"), granted a [conditional pardon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_prerogative_of_mercy#Conditional_pardons "Royal prerogative of mercy") to [Ruth Ellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Ellis "Ruth Ellis"), the [last woman to be executed in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_the_United_Kingdom "Capital punishment in the United Kingdom"). In September, the King and Queen, along with French president [Emmanuel Macron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Macron "Emmanuel Macron"), viewed the [Bayeux Tapestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayeux_Tapestry "Bayeux Tapestry") exhibition at the [British Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Museum "British Museum"), marking the first time in more than 900 years that the tapestry had returned to England from France.

## Health

In March 1998, Charles had laser [keyhole surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keyhole_surgery "Keyhole surgery") on his right knee and in March 2003, he underwent surgery at [King Edward VII's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Edward_VII%27s_Hospital "King Edward VII's Hospital") to treat a [hernia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hernia "Hernia") injury. In 2008, a non-cancerous growth was removed from his [nasal bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasal_bridge "Nasal bridge").

In January 2024, Charles underwent a "corrective procedure" at [the London Clinic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_London_Clinic "The London Clinic") to treat benign [prostate enlargement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostate_enlargement "Prostate enlargement"), which resulted in the postponement of some of his public engagements. In February, Buckingham Palace announced that cancer had been discovered during the treatment, but that it was not [prostate cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostate_cancer "Prostate cancer"). Although his public duties were postponed, it was reported Charles would continue to fulfill his constitutional functions during his [outpatient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outpatient "Outpatient") treatment. He released a statement espousing his support for cancer charities and that he "remain[ed] positive" on making a full recovery. In March, Camilla deputised for him in his absence at the [Commonwealth Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Day "Commonwealth Day") service at Westminster Abbey and at the [Royal Maundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Maundy "Royal Maundy") at [Worcester Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcester_Cathedral "Worcester Cathedral"). He made his first major public appearance since his cancer diagnosis at the Easter service held at [St George's Chapel, Windsor Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_George%27s_Chapel,_Windsor_Castle "St George's Chapel, Windsor Castle"), on 31 March. In April 2024, it was announced that he would resume public-facing duties after making progress in his cancer treatment. In March 2025, Charles was briefly admitted to hospital after experiencing temporary side effects from his cancer treatment. He subsequently postponed several planned engagements. In December, as part of the [Stand Up to Cancer UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand_Up_to_Cancer_UK "Stand Up to Cancer UK") campaign hosted by [Cancer Research UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_Research_UK "Cancer Research UK") and [Channel 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_4 "Channel 4"), he announced that his treatment schedule would be reduced the following year. The King's openness about his illness, contrasting with the traditionally guarded approach of earlier monarchs, was described by the media as unprecedented and as bringing him closer to the public.

### Diet

As early as 1985, Charles was questioning meat consumption. In the *1985 Royal Special* television programme*,* he told the host, [Alastair Burnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alastair_Burnet "Alastair Burnet"), that "I actually now don't eat as much meat as I used to. I eat more fish." He also pointed out the societal double standard whereby eating meat is not questioned but eating less meat means "all hell seems to break loose". In 2021, Charles spoke to the BBC about the environment and revealed that, two days per week, he eats no meat nor fish and, one day per week, he eats no dairy products. In 2022, it was reported that he eats a breakfast of fruit salad, seeds, and tea. He does not eat lunch, but takes a break for tea at 5:00 pm and eats dinner at 8:30 pm, returning to work until midnight or later. Ahead of [Christmas dinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_dinner#United_Kingdom "Christmas dinner") in 2022, Charles confirmed to the animal rights group [People for the Ethical Treatment of Animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_for_the_Ethical_Treatment_of_Animals "People for the Ethical Treatment of Animals") that [foie gras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foie_gras "Foie gras") would not be served at any royal residences; he had stopped the use of foie gras at his own properties for more than a decade before becoming king. During a September 2023 state banquet at the [Palace of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Versailles "Palace of Versailles"), it was reported that he did not want foie gras or out-of-season asparagus on the menu. Instead, he was served lobster.

## Charity work

Since founding the [Prince's Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince%27s_Trust "Prince's Trust") in 1976, using his £7,500 of [severance pay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severance_pay "Severance pay") from the Royal Navy, Charles has established 16 more charitable organisations and now serves as president of each. Together they form a loose alliance, [the Prince's Charities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prince%27s_Charities "The Prince's Charities"), which describes itself as "the largest multi-cause charitable enterprise in the United Kingdom, raising more than £100 million annually ... [and is] active across a broad range of areas including education and young people, environmental sustainability, the built environment, responsible business and enterprise, and international". [King Charles III Charitable Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Charles_III_Charitable_Fund "King Charles III Charitable Fund") has donated over £73 million to various causes since 1979. By September 2020 the Prince's Trust had supported over 1,000,000 young people and created 125,000 entrepreneurs. As Prince of Wales, Charles became [patron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patron "Patron") or president of more than 800 other charities and organisations. He received a Gold [Blue Peter badge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Peter_badge "Blue Peter badge") in 2023 for his environmental work and for his support of young people through the Prince's Trust.

[The Prince's Charities Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prince%27s_Charities_Canada "The Prince's Charities Canada") was established in 2010, in a similar fashion to its namesake in Britain. Charles uses [his tours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_tours_of_Canada "Royal tours of Canada") of Canada as a way to help to draw attention to youth, the disabled, the environment, the arts, medicine, the elderly, heritage conservation and education. He has also set up [the Prince's Charities Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prince%27s_Charities_Australia "The Prince's Charities Australia"), based in [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne "Melbourne"), to provide a coordinating presence for his Australian and international charitable endeavours.

Charles is a long-time supporter of humanitarian projects. In 1980s, he donated his blood to raise awareness for health causes, including [AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS "HIV/AIDS"). Charles and his sons took part in ceremonies that marked the 1998 [International Day for the Elimination of Racial Discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Day_for_the_Elimination_of_Racial_Discrimination "International Day for the Elimination of Racial Discrimination"). Charles expressed strong concerns about the human rights record of the Romanian dictator [Nicolae Ceaușescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Ceau%C8%99escu "Nicolae Ceaușescu"). After Ceaușescu was deposed, he supported the FARA Foundation, a charity for Romanian orphans and abandoned children. Charles has also supported humanitarian causes in Israel, including a private donation to the [Peres Center for Peace and Innovation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peres_Center_for_Peace "Peres Center for Peace") in 2020.

### Investigations of donations

Two of Charles's charities, [the Prince's Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prince%27s_Foundation "The Prince's Foundation") and [the Prince of Wales's Charitable Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prince_of_Wales%27s_Charitable_Fund "The Prince of Wales's Charitable Fund") (later renamed the King's Foundation and King Charles III Charitable Fund), came under scrutiny in 2021 and 2022 for accepting donations many in the media deemed inappropriate. In August 2021 it was announced that the Prince's Foundation was launching an investigation into the reports, with Charles's support. The [Charity Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charity_Commission "Charity Commission") also launched an investigation into allegations that the donations meant for the Prince's Foundation had been instead sent to the [Mahfouz Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Mahfouz_Foundation&action=edit&redlink=1 "Mahfouz Foundation (page does not exist)"). In February 2022, the Metropolitan Police launched an investigation into the cash-for-honours allegations linked to the foundation, passing their evidence to the [Crown Prosecution Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Prosecution_Service "Crown Prosecution Service") for deliberation in October. In August 2023 the Metropolitan Police announced that they had concluded their investigations and no further action would be taken.

*[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times "The Times")* reported in June 2022 that, between 2011 and 2015, Charles accepted €3 million in cash from the [Prime Minister of Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Qatar "Prime Minister of Qatar"), [Hamad bin Jassim bin Jaber Al Thani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamad_bin_Jassim_bin_Jaber_Al_Thani "Hamad bin Jassim bin Jaber Al Thani"). There was no evidence that the payments were illegal or that the money was not intended to go to the charity, although the Charity Commission stated it would review the information and announced in July 2022 that there would be no further investigation. In the same month, *The Times* reported that the Prince of Wales's Charitable Fund received a donation of £1 million from [Bakr bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakr_bin_Laden "Bakr bin Laden") and Shafiq bin Laden – both half-brothers of [Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden "Osama bin Laden") – during a private meeting in 2013. The Charity Commission described the decision to accept donations as a "matter for trustees" and added that no investigation was required.

## Personal interests

From young adulthood, Charles encouraged the understanding of [Indigenous voices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples "Indigenous peoples"), saying they held crucial messages about preservation of the land, respecting community and shared values, resolving conflict, and recognising and making good on past iniquities. He dovetailed this view with his efforts against climate change, as well as [reconciliation between Indigenous and non-Indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truth_and_Reconciliation_Commission_of_Canada "Truth and Reconciliation Commission of Canada") and his charitable work in Canada. At the [2022 Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Commonwealth_Heads_of_Government_Meeting "2022 Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting") Charles, who was representing his mother, raised that reconciliation process as an example for dealing with the history of [slavery in the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_Britain#Enslaved_Africans "Slavery in Britain"), for which he expressed his sorrow.

Letters sent by Charles to British government ministers in 2004 and 2005 expressing his concerns over various policy issues – known as the [black spider memos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_spider_memos "Black spider memos") – presented potential embarrassment following a challenge by *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* to release the letters under the [Freedom of Information Act 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_Information_Act_2000 "Freedom of Information Act 2000"). In March 2015 the [Supreme Court of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_United_Kingdom "Supreme Court of the United Kingdom") decided that Charles's letters must be released. The [Cabinet Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Office "Cabinet Office") published the letters in May that year. The reaction was largely supportive of Charles, with little criticism of him; the press variously described the memos as "underwhelming" and "harmless", and concluded that their release had "backfired on those who seek to belittle him". It was revealed in the same year that Charles had access to confidential Cabinet papers.

In October 2020, a letter sent by Charles to the Governor-General of Australia, [Sir John Kerr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_John_Kerr "Sir John Kerr"), after [Kerr's dismissal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Australian_constitutional_crisis "1975 Australian constitutional crisis") of Prime Minister [Gough Whitlam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gough_Whitlam "Gough Whitlam") in 1975, was released as part of the collection of [palace letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_letters "Palace letters") regarding the Australian constitutional crisis. In the letter, Charles was supportive of Kerr's decision, writing that what Kerr "did last year was right and the courageous thing to do".

*The Times* reported in June 2022 that Charles had privately described the British government's [Rwanda asylum plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwanda_asylum_plan "Rwanda asylum plan") as "appalling" and he feared that it would overshadow the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting in Rwanda that same month. It was later claimed that Cabinet ministers had warned Charles to avoid making political comments, as they feared a constitutional crisis could arise if he continued to make such statements once he became King.

### Built environment

Charles has openly expressed his views on architecture and [urban planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning "Urban planning"); he fostered the advancement of [New Classical architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Classical_architecture "New Classical architecture") and asserted that he "care[s] deeply about issues such as the environment, architecture, inner-city renewal, and the quality of life." In a speech given for the 150th anniversary of the [Royal Institute of British Architects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institute_of_British_Architects "Royal Institute of British Architects") in May 1984, he described a proposed extension to the [National Gallery in London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Gallery_in_London "National Gallery in London") as a "monstrous carbuncle on the face of a much-loved friend" and deplored the "glass stumps and concrete towers" of modern architecture. Charles called for local community involvement in architectural choices and asked, "why has everything got to be vertical, straight, unbending, only at right angles – and functional?" Charles has "a deep understanding of [Islamic art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_art "Islamic art") and [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_architecture "Islamic architecture")", according to the Oxford professor Mohammed Talib, and has been involved in the construction of a building and garden at the [Oxford Centre for Islamic Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_Centre_for_Islamic_Studies "Oxford Centre for Islamic Studies") that combine Islamic and Oxford architectural styles.

In Charles's 1989 book *[A Vision of Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Vision_of_Britain:_A_Personal_View_of_Architecture "A Vision of Britain: A Personal View of Architecture")*, and in speeches and essays, he has been critical of [modern architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_architecture "Modern architecture"), arguing that traditional designs and methods should guide contemporary ones. He has continued to campaign for traditional urbanism, human scale, restoration of historic buildings, and sustainable design despite criticism in the press. Two of his charities – [the Prince's Regeneration Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prince%27s_Regeneration_Trust "The Prince's Regeneration Trust") and [the Prince's Foundation for Building Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prince%27s_Foundation_for_Building_Community "The Prince's Foundation for Building Community"), which were later merged into one charity – promote his views. The village of [Poundbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poundbury "Poundbury") was built on land owned by the [Duchy of Cornwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Cornwall "Duchy of Cornwall") to a master plan by [Léon Krier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9on_Krier "Léon Krier"), under the guidance of Charles and in line with his philosophy. In 2011, Charles saved the historic [Middleport Pottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middleport_Pottery "Middleport Pottery") from permanent closure by leading a £9 million rescue and regeneration project through The Prince's Regeneration Trust. In 2013 developments for the suburb of [Nansledan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nansledan "Nansledan") began on the estate of the Duchy of Cornwall with Charles's endorsement. Charles helped purchase [Dumfries House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumfries_House "Dumfries House") and its complete collection of 18th century furnishings in 2007, taking a £20 million loan from his charitable trust to contribute towards the £45 million cost. The house and gardens remain property of the Prince's Foundation and serve as a museum and community and skills training centre. This led to the development of [Knockroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knockroon "Knockroon"), called the "Scottish Poundbury". In 2025, it was reported that the design of a new generation of British towns would incorporate Charles's architectural ideas.

After lamenting in 1996 the unbridled destruction of many of Canada's historic urban cores, Charles offered his assistance to the [Department of Canadian Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Canadian_Heritage "Department of Canadian Heritage") in creating a trust modelled on Britain's [National Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Trust "National Trust"), a plan that was implemented with the passage of the [federal budget in 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Canadian_federal_budget "2007 Canadian federal budget"). In 1999 Charles agreed to the use of his title for the [Prince of Wales Prize for Municipal Heritage Leadership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Wales_Prize_for_Municipal_Heritage_Leadership "Prince of Wales Prize for Municipal Heritage Leadership"), awarded by the [National Trust for Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Trust_for_Canada "National Trust for Canada") to municipal governments that have committed to the conservation of historic places.

Whilst visiting the US and surveying the damage caused by [Hurricane Katrina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Katrina "Hurricane Katrina"), Charles received the [National Building Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Building_Museum "National Building Museum")'s [Vincent Scully Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Scully_Prize "Vincent Scully Prize") in 2005 for his efforts in regard to architecture; he donated US$25,000 of the prize money towards restoring storm-damaged communities. For his work as patron of New Classical architecture, Charles was awarded the 2012 [Driehaus Architecture Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driehaus_Architecture_Prize "Driehaus Architecture Prize") from the [University of Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame "University of Notre Dame"). The [Worshipful Company of Carpenters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worshipful_Company_of_Carpenters "Worshipful Company of Carpenters") installed Charles as an Honorary Liveryman "in recognition of his interest in London's architecture".

Charles has occasionally intervened in projects that employ architectural styles such as [modernism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernism "Modernism") and [functionalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functionalism_(architecture) "Functionalism (architecture)"). In 2009 he wrote to the [Qatari royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatari_royal_family "Qatari royal family") – the financier of the redevelopment of the [Chelsea Barracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_Barracks "Chelsea Barracks") site – labelling [Lord Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Rogers "Lord Rogers")'s design for the site "unsuitable". Rogers claimed that Charles had also intervened to block his designs for the [Royal Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Opera_House "Royal Opera House") and [Paternoster Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paternoster_Square "Paternoster Square"). [CPC Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPC_Group "CPC Group"), the project developer, took a case against [Qatari Diar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatari_Diar "Qatari Diar") to the [High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_Justice "High Court of Justice"). After the suit was settled, the CPC Group apologised to Charles "for any offence caused ... during the course of the proceedings".

### Natural environment

Since the 1970s, Charles has promoted environmental awareness. At the age of 21 he delivered his first speech on [environmental issues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_issues_in_the_United_Kingdom "Environmental issues in the United Kingdom") in his capacity as the chairman of the Welsh Countryside Committee. Charles was named one of [Time Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Magazine "Time Magazine")'s 100 most influential climate leaders of 2025. An avid gardener, Charles has also emphasised the importance of [talking to plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talking_to_plants "Talking to plants"), stating that "I happily talk to the plants and trees, and listen to them. I think it's absolutely crucial". His interest in gardening began in 1980 when he took over the Highgrove estate. His "healing garden", based on sacred geometry and ancient religious symbolism, went on display at the [Chelsea Flower Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_Flower_Show "Chelsea Flower Show") in 2002.

Upon moving into Highgrove House, Charles developed an interest in [organic farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_farming "Organic farming"), which culminated in the 1990 launch of his own organic brand, [Duchy Originals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_Originals "Duchy Originals"), which sells more than 200 different sustainably produced products; the profits (more than £6 million by 2010) are donated to the Prince's Charities. Charles became involved with farming and various industries within it, regularly meeting with farmers to discuss their trade. A prominent critic of the practice, Charles has also spoken against the use of [genetically modified crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetically_modified_crops "Genetically modified crops"), and in a letter to [Tony Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Blair "Tony Blair") in 1998 he criticised the development of [genetically modified foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetically_modified_food "Genetically modified food").

The Sustainable Markets Initiative – a project that encourages putting [sustainability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainability "Sustainability") at the centre of all activities – was launched by Charles at the [World Economic Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Economic_Forum "World Economic Forum")'s annual meeting in [Davos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davos "Davos") in January 2020. In May of the same year, the initiative and the World Economic Forum initiated the [Great Reset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Reset "Great Reset") project, a five-point plan concerned with enhancing sustainable economic growth following the global recession caused by the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic").

The holy [chrism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrism "Chrism") oil used at Charles's coronation was vegan, made from oils of olive, sesame, rose, jasmine, cinnamon, neroli and benzoin, along with amber and orange blossom. His mother's chrism oil contained animal-based oils.

Charles delivered a speech at the [2021 G20 Rome summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_G20_Rome_summit "2021 G20 Rome summit"), describing [COP26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COP26 "COP26") as "the last chance saloon" for preventing climate change and asking for actions that would lead to a green-led, sustainable economy. In his speech at the opening ceremony for COP26, he repeated his sentiments from the previous year, stating that "a vast military-style campaign" was needed "to marshal the strength of the global private sector" for tackling climate change. In 2022, the media alleged that Liz Truss had advised Charles against attending [COP27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COP27 "COP27"), to which advice he agreed. Charles delivered the opening speech at [COP28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COP28 "COP28"), saying among others he prayed "with all my heart that COP28 will be a critical turning point towards genuine transformational action." He received a Green Blue Peter badge on [Earth Day 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_Day_2026 "Earth Day 2026") in recognition of his long‑standing support for environmental protection and sustainability.

Charles, who is patron of the [Cambridge Institute for Sustainability Leadership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Institute_for_Sustainability_Leadership "Cambridge Institute for Sustainability Leadership"), introduced the Climate Action Scholarships for students from small island nations in partnership with the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge"), the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto "University of Toronto"), the [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne "University of Melbourne"), [McMaster University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMaster_University "McMaster University") and the [University of Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Montreal "University of Montreal") in March 2022. In 2010, he funded the Prince's Countryside Fund (renamed [the Royal Countryside Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Royal_Countryside_Fund "The Royal Countryside Fund") in 2023), a charity that aims for a "confident, robust and sustainable agricultural and rural community".

### Alternative medicine

Charles has controversially championed [alternative medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_medicine "Alternative medicine"), including [homeopathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeopathy "Homeopathy"). He first publicly expressed his interest in the topic in December 1982, in an address to the [British Medical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Medical_Association "British Medical Association"). This speech was seen as "combative" and "critical" of modern medicine and was met with anger by some medical professionals. Similarly, [the Prince's Foundation for Integrated Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prince%27s_Foundation_for_Integrated_Health "The Prince's Foundation for Integrated Health") (FIH) attracted opposition from the scientific and medical community over its campaign encouraging [general practitioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_practitioner "General practitioner") to offer [herbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbal_medicine "Herbal medicine") and other alternative treatments to NHS patients.

In April 2008, *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times "The Times")* published a letter from [Edzard Ernst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edzard_Ernst "Edzard Ernst"), professor of [complementary medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complementary_medicine "Complementary medicine") at the [University of Exeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Exeter "University of Exeter"), which asked the FIH to recall two guides promoting alternative medicine. That year, Ernst published a book with [Simon Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Singh "Simon Singh") called *[Trick or Treatment: Alternative Medicine on Trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trick_or_Treatment:_Alternative_Medicine_on_Trial "Trick or Treatment: Alternative Medicine on Trial")* and mockingly dedicated to "HRH the Prince of Wales". The last chapter is highly critical of Charles's advocacy of complementary and alternative treatments.

Charles's Duchy Originals produced a variety of complementary medicinal products, including a "Detox Tincture" that Ernst denounced as "financially exploiting the vulnerable" and "outright [quackery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quackery "Quackery")". Charles personally wrote at least seven letters to the [Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicines_and_Healthcare_products_Regulatory_Agency "Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency") shortly before it relaxed the rules governing labelling of such herbal products, a move that was widely condemned by scientists and medical bodies. It was reported in October 2009 that Charles had lobbied the health secretary, [Andy Burnham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Burnham "Andy Burnham"), regarding greater provision of alternative treatments in the NHS.

Following accounting irregularities, the FIH announced its closure in April 2010. The FIH was re-branded and re-launched later in 2010 as [the College of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_College_of_Medicine "The College of Medicine"), of which Charles became a patron in 2019.

### Sports

From his youth until 2005, Charles was an avid player of competitive [polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polo "Polo"). Charles also frequently took part in [fox hunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_hunting "Fox hunting") until [the sport was banned in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunting_Act_2004 "Hunting Act 2004"), also in 2005. By the late 1990s opposition to the activity was growing when Charles's participation was viewed as a "political statement" by those who were opposed to it. Charles suffered several polo- and hunting-related injuries throughout the years, including a two-inch scar on his left cheek in 1980, a broken arm in 1990, a torn cartilage in his left knee in 1992, a broken rib in 1998, and a fractured shoulder in 2001.

Charles has been a keen salmon angler since youth and supported [Orri Vigfússon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orri_Vigf%C3%BAsson "Orri Vigfússon")'s efforts to protect the North Atlantic salmon. He frequently fishes the [River Dee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Dee,_Aberdeenshire "River Dee, Aberdeenshire") in [Aberdeenshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeenshire "Aberdeenshire"), and claims his most special angling memories are from his time spent in [Vopnafjörður](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vopnafj%C3%B6r%C3%B0ur "Vopnafjörður"), Iceland. Charles is a supporter of [Burnley F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnley_F.C. "Burnley F.C.")

Apart from hunting, Charles has also participated in target rifle competitions, representing the House of Lords in the Vizianagram Match (Lords vs. Commons) at [Bisley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisley_Camp "Bisley Camp"). He became President of the British [National Rifle Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Rifle_Association_(United_Kingdom) "National Rifle Association (United Kingdom)") in 1977.

### Visual, performing, and literary arts

Charles has been involved in performance since his youth, and appeared in [sketches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sketch_comedy "Sketch comedy") and [revues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revue "Revue") while studying at Cambridge.

Charles is president or patron of more than 20 performing arts organisations, including the [Royal College of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Music "Royal College of Music"), [Royal Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Royal_Opera "The Royal Opera"), [English Chamber Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Chamber_Orchestra "English Chamber Orchestra"), [Philharmonia Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philharmonia_Orchestra "Philharmonia Orchestra"), [Welsh National Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_National_Opera "Welsh National Opera"), [Royal Shakespeare Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Shakespeare_Company "Royal Shakespeare Company") (attending performances in [Stratford-Upon-Avon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratford-Upon-Avon "Stratford-Upon-Avon"), supporting fundraising events, and attending the company's annual general meeting), [British Film Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Film_Institute "British Film Institute"), and [Purcell School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purcell_School "Purcell School"). In 2000 he revived the tradition of appointing [an official harpist to the Prince of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_Harpist_to_the_Prince_of_Wales "Official Harpist to the Prince of Wales"), in order to foster Welsh talent at playing the national instrument of Wales. The role, now referred to as King's Harpist, is currently held by [Mared Pugh Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mared_Pugh_Evans "Mared Pugh Evans").

Charles is a keen [watercolourist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watercolour "Watercolour"), having published books on the subject and exhibited and sold a number of his works to raise money for charity; in 2016 it was estimated that he had sold lithographs of his watercolours for a total of £2 million from a shop at his Highgrove House residence. For his 50th birthday 50 of his watercolours were exhibited at [Hampton Court Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton_Court_Palace "Hampton Court Palace") and, for his 70th birthday, his works were exhibited at the [National Gallery of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Gallery_of_Australia "National Gallery of Australia"). Charles founded the [Royal Drawing School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Drawing_School "Royal Drawing School") in 2000, which offers drawing classes to children and the public. In 2001, 20 [lithographs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithographs "Lithographs") of his watercolour paintings illustrating his country estates were exhibited at the [Florence International Biennale of Contemporary Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Biennale "Florence Biennale") and 79 of his paintings were put on display in London in 2022. To mark the 25th anniversary of his investiture as Prince of Wales in 1994, the [Royal Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Mail "Royal Mail") issued a series of postage stamps that featured his paintings. Charles is Honorary President of the [Royal Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Academy "Royal Academy") of Arts Development Trust and, in 2015, 2022 and 2023, commissioned paintings of 12 D-Day veterans, [seven Holocaust survivors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Portraits:_Surviving_the_Holocaust "Seven Portraits: Surviving the Holocaust"), and ten members of the [Windrush generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windrush_generation "Windrush generation"), respectively, which went on display at the Queen's Gallery in Buckingham Palace.

Charles [is the author of several books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliography_of_Charles_III "Bibliography of Charles III") and has contributed a [foreword](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreword "Foreword") or [preface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preface "Preface") to numerous books by others. He has also been featured in a variety of documentary films.

### Religion and philosophy

Shortly after his accession to the throne, Charles publicly described himself as "a committed [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican "Anglican") Christian"; at age 16, during Easter 1965, he had been [confirmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confirmed "Confirmed") into the [Anglican Communion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Communion "Anglican Communion") by Archbishop of Canterbury [Michael Ramsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Ramsey "Michael Ramsey") in St George's Chapel, Windsor Castle. The King is the Supreme Governor of the Church of England and a member of the [Church of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Scotland "Church of Scotland"); he swore an oath to uphold that church immediately after he was proclaimed King. He attends services at various Anglican churches close to Highgrove and attends the Church of Scotland's [Crathie Kirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crathie_Kirk "Crathie Kirk") with the rest of the royal family when staying at Balmoral Castle.

[Laurens van der Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurens_van_der_Post "Laurens van der Post") became a friend of Charles in 1977; he was dubbed Charles's "spiritual guru" and was godfather to Prince William. From van der Post, Charles developed a focus on philosophy and an interest in other religions. Charles expressed his philosophical views in his 2010 book, *[Harmony: A New Way of Looking at Our World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmony:_A_New_Way_of_Looking_at_Our_World "Harmony: A New Way of Looking at Our World")*, which won a Nautilus Book Award. He has also visited [Eastern Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox "Eastern Orthodox") monasteries on [Mount Athos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Athos "Mount Athos"), in Romania, and in Serbia, and met with Eastern Church leaders in Jerusalem in 2020, during a visit that culminated in an ecumenical service in the [Church of the Nativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Nativity "Church of the Nativity") in Bethlehem and a walk through the city accompanied by Christian and Muslim dignitaries. Charles also attended the consecration of Britain's first [Syriac Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Orthodox "Syriac Orthodox") cathedral, [St Thomas Cathedral, Acton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Thomas_Cathedral,_Acton "St Thomas Cathedral, Acton"). Charles is patron of the [Oxford Centre for Islamic Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_Centre_for_Islamic_Studies "Oxford Centre for Islamic Studies") at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford") and attended the inauguration of the [Markfield Institute of Higher Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markfield_Institute_of_Higher_Education "Markfield Institute of Higher Education"), which is dedicated to Islamic studies in a multicultural context.

In his 1994 documentary with Dimbleby, Charles said that, when king, he wished to be seen as a "defender of faith", rather than the British monarch's traditional title of *[Defender of the Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defender_of_the_Faith "Defender of the Faith")*, "prefer[ring] to embrace all religious traditions and 'the pattern of the divine, which I think is in all of us.'" This attracted controversy at the time, as well as speculation that the [coronation oath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation_oath_of_the_British_monarch "Coronation oath of the British monarch") might be altered. He stated in 2015 that he would retain the title of *Defender of the Faith*, whilst "ensuring that other people's faiths can also be practised", which he sees as a duty of the Church of England. Charles reaffirmed this theme shortly after his accession and declared that his duties as sovereign included "the duty to protect the diversity of our country, including by protecting the space for faith itself and its practice through the religions, cultures, traditions, and beliefs to which our hearts and minds direct us as individuals." Although the text of the coronation oath was not altered, because the process of passing new legislation through Parliament would be lengthy and liable to raise divisive issues, a [preamble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preamble "Preamble") spoken by the [Archbishop of Canterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury "Archbishop of Canterbury") was added to the text:

> Your Majesty, the Church established by law, whose settlement you will swear to maintain, is committed to the true profession of the Gospel, and, in so doing, will seek to foster an environment in which people of all faiths and beliefs may live freely.

His inclusive, multi-faith approach and his own Christian beliefs were expressed in his first [Christmas message](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_message "Christmas message") as king. In line with his multi-faith approach, Charles has served as a patron of the [Council of Christians and Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Christians_and_Jews "Council of Christians and Jews") and of the [Jewish Museum London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Museum_London "Jewish Museum London").

## Media image and public opinion

Since his birth, Charles has received close media attention, which increased as he matured. It has been an ambivalent relationship, largely impacted by his marriages to Diana and Camilla and their aftermath, but also centred on his future conduct as king.

Described as the "[world's most eligible bachelor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eligible_bachelor "Eligible bachelor")" in the late 1970s, Charles was subsequently overshadowed by Diana. After her death the media regularly breached Charles's privacy and printed exposés. Known for expressing his opinions, when asked during an interview to mark his 70th birthday whether this would continue in the same way once he is King, he responded "No. It won't. I'm not that stupid. I do realise that it is a separate exercise being sovereign. So, of course, you know, I understand entirely how that should operate." In September 2025, *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist "The Economist")* analysed all Charles's public speeches over the past 30 years and concluded that he has mellowed over the past three decades.

In 2009, Charles was named the world's best-dressed man by *[Esquire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esquire_(magazine) "Esquire (magazine)")* magazine. Fashion magazine *[British GQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_GQ "British GQ")* described Charles as "the true guardian of traditional British menswear" in 2012 and named him one of the most stylish people of 2025. Italian designer [Donatella Versace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donatella_Versace "Donatella Versace") once said Charles "is an archetype of style". In 2023 the *[New Statesman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Statesman "New Statesman")* named Charles as the fourth-most-powerful right-wing figure of the year, describing him as a "[romantic traditionalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditionalism_(perennialism) "Traditionalism (perennialism)")" and "the very last [reactionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reactionary "Reactionary") in public life" for his support of various traditionalist [think tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Think_tank "Think tank") and previous writings. He was also named one of the [100 most influential people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100 "Time 100") of 2023 by *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")* magazine. Charles and Camilla topped *Tatler*'s Social Power Index for 2022 and 2023.

A 2018 BMG Research poll found that 46 per cent of Britons wanted Charles to abdicate immediately on his mother's death, in favour of William. However, a 2021 opinion poll reported that 60 percent of the British public had a favourable opinion of him. On his accession to the throne, *[The Statesman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Statesman_(India) "The Statesman (India)")* reported an opinion poll that put Charles's popularity with the British people at 42 per cent. More recent polling suggested that his popularity increased sharply after he became King. On the first anniversary of his reign, a [YouGov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouGov "YouGov") poll found that 59 per cent of Britons said he was doing a good job. *[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")* wrote that his first year as king had proved "unexpectedly popular". As of July 2026, Charles continued to hold majority support, with an approval rating of 62 per cent, and 68 percent of respondents saying "he is doing a good job", according to YouGov.

### Reaction to press treatment

In 1994, the German tabloid *[Bild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bild "Bild")* published nude photos of Charles that were taken while he was vacationing in [Le Barroux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Barroux "Le Barroux"); they had reportedly been put up for sale for £30,000. Buckingham Palace reacted by stating that it was "unjustifiable for anybody to suffer this sort of intrusion".

Charles, "so often a target of the press, got his chance to return fire" in 2002, when addressing "scores of editors, publishers, and other media executives" gathered at [St Bride's Fleet Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Bride%27s_Fleet_Street "St Bride's Fleet Street") to celebrate 300 years of journalism. Defending public servants from "the corrosive drip of constant criticism", he noted that the press had been "awkward, cantankerous, cynical, bloody-minded, at times intrusive, at times inaccurate, and at times deeply unfair and harmful to individuals and to institutions". However, he concluded, regarding his own relations with the press, "from time to time we are probably both a bit hard on each other, exaggerating the downsides and ignoring the good points in each".

In 2006, Charles [filed a court case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Wales_v_Associated_Newspapers_Ltd "Prince of Wales v Associated Newspapers Ltd") against *[The Mail on Sunday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mail_on_Sunday "The Mail on Sunday")*, after excerpts of his personal journals were published, revealing his opinions on matters such as the [transfer of sovereignty over Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_of_sovereignty_over_Hong_Kong "Transfer of sovereignty over Hong Kong") to China in 1997, in which Charles described the Chinese government officials as "appalling old waxworks". Charles and Camilla were named in 2011 as [individuals whose confidential information was reportedly targeted or actually acquired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_news_media_phone_hacking_scandal_victims "List of news media phone hacking scandal victims") in conjunction with the [news media phone hacking scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_media_phone_hacking_scandal "News media phone hacking scandal").

*[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent "The Independent")* noted in 2015 that Charles would only speak to broadcasters "on the condition they have signed a 15-page contract, demanding that Clarence House attends both the 'rough cut' and 'fine cut' edits of films and, if it is unhappy with the final product, can 'remove the contribution in its entirety from the programme'". This contract stipulated that all questions directed at Charles must be pre-approved and [vetted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vetted "Vetted") by his representatives.

## Residences and finance

In 2023, *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* estimated Charles's personal wealth at £1.8 billion. This estimate includes the assets of the [Duchy of Lancaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Lancaster "Duchy of Lancaster") worth £653 million (and paying Charles an annual income of £20 million), jewels worth £533 million, real estate worth £330 million, shares and investments worth £142 million, [a stamp collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Philatelic_Collection "Royal Philatelic Collection") worth at least £100 million, racehorses worth £27 million, artworks worth £24 million and cars worth £6.3 million. Most of this wealth, which he inherited from his mother, [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II "Elizabeth II"), was exempt from inheritance tax.

Clarence House, previously the residence of the Queen Mother, has been Charles's official London residence from 2003, after being renovated at a cost of £6.1 million. He previously shared apartments eight and nine at [Kensington Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kensington_Palace "Kensington Palace") with Diana before moving to [York House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_House,_St_James%27s_Palace "York House, St James's Palace") at [St James's Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_James%27s_Palace "St James's Palace"), which remained his principal residence until 2003. [Highgrove House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highgrove_House "Highgrove House") in [Gloucestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloucestershire "Gloucestershire") is owned by the [Duchy of Cornwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Cornwall "Duchy of Cornwall"), having been purchased for Charles's use in 1980, and which he rented for £336,000 per annum. Since [William](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William,_Prince_of_Wales "William, Prince of Wales") became [Duke of Cornwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_of_Cornwall "Duke of Cornwall"), Charles is expected to pay £700,000 per annum for use of the property. Charles also owns a property near the village of [Viscri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viscri "Viscri") in Romania.

As [Prince of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Wales "Prince of Wales"), Charles's primary source of income was generated from the Duchy of Cornwall, which owns 133,658 acres of land (around 54,090 hectares), including farming, residential, and commercial properties, as well as an investment portfolio. Since 1993, he has paid tax voluntarily under the *Memorandum of Understanding on Royal Taxation*, updated in 2013. [Her Majesty's Revenue and Customs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_Majesty%27s_Revenue_and_Customs "Her Majesty's Revenue and Customs") were asked in December 2012 to investigate alleged [tax avoidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_avoidance "Tax avoidance") by the Duchy of Cornwall. The Duchy was named in the [Paradise Papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise_Papers "Paradise Papers"), a set of confidential electronic documents relating to [offshore investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offshore_investment "Offshore investment") that were leaked to the German newspaper *[Süddeutsche Zeitung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%BCddeutsche_Zeitung "Süddeutsche Zeitung")* in 2017. In the 2021–22 financial year, Charles received £23 million from the Duchy of Cornwall and paid a voluntary income tax of £5.9 million.

In June 2026, Charles revealed that he had paid £12.9 million in tax for the 2024–25 financial year, and it was also announced that, once the renovations at [Buckingham Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckingham_Palace "Buckingham Palace") are completed, he and Camilla will not move into the palace but will instead continue living at Clarence House.

## Titles, styles, honours, and arms

### Titles and styles

Charles has held many titles and honorary military positions throughout the Commonwealth, is sovereign of many orders in his own countries and has received honours and awards from around the world. In each of his realms he has a distinct title that follows a similar formula: *King of Saint Lucia and of His other Realms and Territories* in [Saint Lucia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_Saint_Lucia "Monarchy of Saint Lucia"), *King of Australia and His other Realms and Territories* in Australia, etc. In the [Isle of Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Man "Isle of Man"), which is a [Crown Dependency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Dependency "Crown Dependency") rather than a separate realm, he is known as [Lord of Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_of_Mann "Lord of Mann"). Charles is also styled *Defender of the Faith*.

There had been speculation throughout Elizabeth II's reign as to what [regnal name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regnal_name "Regnal name") Charles would choose upon his accession; instead of *Charles III*, he could have chosen to reign as *George VII* or used one of his other given names. It was reported that he might use George in honour of his grandfather George VI and to avoid associations with previous controversial kings named Charles. Charles's office asserted in 2005 that no decision had yet been made. Speculation continued for a few hours following his mother's death, until Prime Minister Liz Truss announced and Clarence House confirmed that Charles had chosen the regnal name *Charles III*.

Charles, who left active military service in 1976, was awarded the highest rank in all three armed services in 2012 by his mother: [Admiral of the Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_of_the_Fleet_(Royal_Navy) "Admiral of the Fleet (Royal Navy)"), [Field Marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_marshal_(United_Kingdom) "Field marshal (United Kingdom)"), and [Marshal of the Royal Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshal_of_the_Royal_Air_Force "Marshal of the Royal Air Force").

### Arms

As Prince of Wales, Charles's coat of arms was based on the arms of the United Kingdom, differenced with a white label and an [inescutcheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inescutcheon "Inescutcheon") of the Principality of Wales, surmounted by the heir apparent's crown, and with the motto *Ich dien* (German: [[ɪç ˈdiːn]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German"), "I serve") instead of *[Dieu et mon droit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieu_et_mon_droit "Dieu et mon droit")*.

When Charles became king, he inherited the royal coats of arms of the United Kingdom and of Canada. The design of his royal cypher, featuring a depiction of the [Tudor Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudor_Crown "Tudor Crown") instead of [St Edward's Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Edward%27s_Crown "St Edward's Crown"), was revealed on 27 September 2022. The College of Arms envisaged that the Tudor crown will be used in new arms, uniforms and crown badges as they are replaced. On 10 October 2024, designed by artist Timothy Noad, the new [Royal Arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Arms "Royal Arms"), alongside the new Lesser Arms, used by the UK government were officially unveiled, with both arms reflecting the stylistic changes made after Charles's accession.

### Banners, flags, and standards

#### As heir apparent

The banners used by Charles as Prince of Wales varied depending upon location. His personal standard for the United Kingdom was the [Royal Standard of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Standard_of_the_United_Kingdom "Royal Standard of the United Kingdom") differenced as in his arms, with a label of three points *argent* and the escutcheon of the arms of the Principality of Wales in the centre. It was used outside Wales, Scotland, Cornwall and Canada, and throughout the entire United Kingdom when Charles was acting in an official capacity associated with the British Armed Forces.

The personal flag for use in Wales was based upon the [Royal Badge of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Badge_of_Wales "Royal Badge of Wales"). In Scotland the personal banner used between 1974 and 2022 was based upon three ancient Scottish titles: [Duke of Rothesay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_of_Rothesay "Duke of Rothesay") (heir apparent to the [King of Scots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Scots "King of Scots")), [High Steward of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Steward_of_Scotland "High Steward of Scotland"), and Lord of the Isles. In Cornwall, the banner was the arms of the Duke of Cornwall.

In 2011, the [Canadian Heraldic Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Heraldic_Authority "Canadian Heraldic Authority") introduced a [personal heraldic banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_standards_of_Canada#Other_members_of_the_royal_family "Royal standards of Canada") for the [Prince of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Wales "Prince of Wales") for Canada, consisting of the shield of the [Royal Coat of Arms of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Coat_of_Arms_of_Canada "Royal Coat of Arms of Canada") defaced with both a blue [roundel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roundel_(heraldry) "Roundel (heraldry)") of the [Prince of Wales's feathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Wales%27s_feathers "Prince of Wales's feathers") surrounded by a wreath of gold maple leaves and a white label of three points.

#### As sovereign

The royal standard of the United Kingdom is used to represent the King in the United Kingdom and on official visits overseas, except in Australia and Canada. It is the royal arms in banner form undifferentiated, having been used by successive British monarchs since 1702. The royal standard of Canada is used by the King in Canada and while acting on behalf of Canada overseas. It is the *[escutcheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escutcheon_(heraldry) "Escutcheon (heraldry)")* of the Royal Coat of Arms of Canada in banner form undifferentiated.
