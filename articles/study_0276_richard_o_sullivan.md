# Richard O'Sullivan

**Richard Thomas O'Sullivan** (7 May 1944 – 4 September 2026) was an English comedy actor.

O'Sullivan started his career as a [child actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_actor "Child actor"). He became best known for his roles as Robin Tripp in the television sitcoms *[Man About the House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_About_the_House "Man About the House")* (1973–1976) and its spin-off, *[Robin's Nest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin%27s_Nest_(TV_series) "Robin's Nest (TV series)")* (1977–1981), and also as the title character in the period adventure series *[Dick Turpin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Turpin_(TV_series) "Dick Turpin (TV series)")* (1979–1982).

Other television credits include *[Doctor at Large](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_at_Large_(TV_series) "Doctor at Large (TV series)")* (1971), *[Doctor in Charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_in_Charge "Doctor in Charge")* (1972–1973), *[Alcock and Gander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcock_and_Gander "Alcock and Gander")* (1972), *[Me and My Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Me_and_My_Girl_(TV_series) "Me and My Girl (TV series)")* (1984–1988), and *[Trouble In Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trouble_in_Mind_(TV_series) "Trouble in Mind (TV series)")* (1991).

## Early life

O'Sullivan was born to John and Ellen O'Sullivan (née Fleming) on 7 May 1944, in [Chiswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiswick "Chiswick"), England, where he grew up with his younger brother. His early education was at St John the Evangelist's RC Primary School in [Brentford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brentford "Brentford"), Middlesex. After a family holiday in Ireland as a boy, he returned with a strong Irish accent and was sent to the [Corona Theatre School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corona_Theatre_School "Corona Theatre School") to soften it.

## Career as child actor

O'Sullivan appeared in his first film at the age of eight as an extra as one of the children singing in the [Sunday school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunday_school "Sunday school") sequence of *[The Yellow Balloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Yellow_Balloon_(film) "The Yellow Balloon (film)")*, filmed in 1952 and released in 1953. He then played the main character in *[The Stranger's Hand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stranger%27s_Hand "The Stranger's Hand")*, starring [Alida Valli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alida_Valli "Alida Valli") and [Trevor Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trevor_Howard "Trevor Howard"), in 1953.

Possibly his earliest television work was his part in a *[Sherlock Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes_(1954_TV_series) "Sherlock Holmes (1954 TV series)")* episode, "The Case of the Unlucky Gambler", broadcast on 18 July 1955. He appeared in the [Children's Film Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children%27s_Film_Foundation "Children's Film Foundation")'s first serial, *Raiders of the River*, also produced in 1955. He also acted the part of Don Armado's pageboy Moth in Shakespeare's *[Love's Labour's Lost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love%27s_Labour%27s_Lost "Love's Labour's Lost")* directed by [Peter Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Hall_(director) "Peter Hall (director)") at the [Royal Shakespeare Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Shakespeare_Theatre "Royal Shakespeare Theatre"), Stratford, in 1956. In the film *[It's Great to Be Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%27s_Great_to_Be_Young_(1956_film) "It's Great to Be Young (1956 film)")* (1956), he appeared alongside [John Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Mills "John Mills").

In 1957, he played the title character in the BBC Television five part Sunday serial *[Little Lord Fauntleroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Lord_Fauntleroy "Little Lord Fauntleroy")*, and then with [Keith Michell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Michell "Keith Michell") and [Belinda Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belinda_Lee "Belinda Lee") in the opulent and swashbuckling *[Dangerous Exile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dangerous_Exile "Dangerous Exile")* playing [Louis XVII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XVII "Louis XVII"), the ten-year-old son of [Louis XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XVI "Louis XVI") and [Marie Antoinette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Antoinette "Marie Antoinette"). During this period he also featured in two episodes of [Sapphire Films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapphire_Films "Sapphire Films")' *[The Adventures of Robin Hood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Adventures_of_Robin_Hood_(TV_series) "The Adventures of Robin Hood (TV series)")* (1957) alongside [Richard Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Greene "Richard Greene"), one role being that of Will Dale in the episode "The Challenge of the Black Knight". In the *[Sword of Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sword_of_Freedom "Sword of Freedom")* series (1957), also made by Sapphire, he played Alberto in the episode "Chart of Gold". In an early *[Carry On](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carry_On_films "Carry On films")* film, *[Carry On Teacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carry_On_Teacher "Carry On Teacher")* (1959), he had the small role of pupil Robin Stevens. Around the same time, he was cast in the role of Pierre van der Mal in an early scene of *[The Nun's Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nun%27s_Story_(film) "The Nun's Story (film)")* (also 1959), playing the younger brother of Gabrielle ([Audrey Hepburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audrey_Hepburn "Audrey Hepburn")). He had a leading role in an episode of the Sapphire/[ITC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITC_Entertainment "ITC Entertainment") series *[The Four Just Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Four_Just_Men_(TV_series) "The Four Just Men (TV series)")* ("The Man with the Golden Touch") (1959) as the [Neapolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples "Naples") street urchin Pietro who foils a robbery.

In the 1960s, O'Sullivan appeared in two [Cliff Richard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cliff_Richard "Cliff Richard") films: *[The Young Ones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Young_Ones_(1961_film) "The Young Ones (1961 film)")* (1961) and *[Wonderful Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonderful_Life_(1964_film) "Wonderful Life (1964 film)")* (1964).

## Adult acting career

O'Sullivan featured in the 1963 blockbuster film *[Cleopatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleopatra_(1963_film) "Cleopatra (1963 film)")* as [Pharaoh Ptolemy XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy_XIII "Ptolemy XIII"), the younger brother of the title character played by [Elizabeth Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Taylor "Elizabeth Taylor").

For the remainder of the 1960s, O'Sullivan was a jobbing actor appearing in such TV series as *[Dr. Syn, Alias the Scarecrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Syn#The_Scarecrow_of_Romney_Marsh_(1963) "Doctor Syn")*, *[Emergency Ward 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_Ward_10 "Emergency Ward 10")*, *[Redcap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redcap_(TV_series) "Redcap (TV series)")*, *[Danger Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danger_Man "Danger Man")*, *[No Hiding Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Hiding_Place "No Hiding Place")*, *[Dixon of Dock Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dixon_of_Dock_Green "Dixon of Dock Green")*, and *[Strange Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strange_Report "Strange Report")* among others, until he was offered the role of Lawrence Bingham in the [LWT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LWT "LWT") sitcom *[Doctor at Large](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_at_Large_(TV_series) "Doctor at Large (TV series)")* (1971), a role which continued in the later *[Doctor in Charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_in_Charge "Doctor in Charge")* (1972–73). Meanwhile, he also had a main role in the [Thames Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thames_Television "Thames Television") comedy *[Alcock and Gander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcock_and_Gander "Alcock and Gander")* (1972) with [Beryl Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beryl_Reid "Beryl Reid").

By then a regular in TV [sitcoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitcom "Sitcom"), he starred as Robin Tripp, a trainee chef, in the flatshare sitcom *[Man About the House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_About_the_House "Man About the House")* written by [Johnnie Mortimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnnie_Mortimer "Johnnie Mortimer") and [Brian Cooke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Cooke "Brian Cooke"), launched in 1973. The series was a huge success and was considered risqué due to Tripp sharing a flat with two young women, played by [Paula Wilcox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paula_Wilcox "Paula Wilcox") and [Sally Thomsett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sally_Thomsett "Sally Thomsett"), whilst under the watchful eye of their landlords, played by [Yootha Joyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yootha_Joyce "Yootha Joyce") and [Brian Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Murphy_(actor) "Brian Murphy (actor)"). A [feature film version](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_About_the_House_(film) "Man About the House (film)") of the series was released in 1974. In 1975, O'Sullivan starred in the stage comedy [*Boeing Boeing*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing-Boeing_(play) "Boeing-Boeing (play)"), undertaking two record-breaking national tours, alongside three of his *Man About the House* co-stars, Joyce, Thomsett and [Doug Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Fisher_(actor) "Doug Fisher (actor)").

When *Man About the House* ended in 1976, O'Sullivan continued playing Robin Tripp in the spin-off sitcom *[Robin's Nest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin%27s_Nest_(TV_series) "Robin's Nest (TV series)")*, in which Robin sets up a [bistro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bistro "Bistro") with funding from his girlfriend Vicky's father, James Nicholls ([Tony Britton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Britton "Tony Britton")).

*Robin's Nest* was a ratings success, and was the first UK sitcom to feature an unmarried couple cohabiting. To tie in with the series, O'Sullivan wrote a recipe book called *Man About the Kitchen*, and a sequel *Roastin' with Richard*, which were published in 1980. He also wrote the *Robin's Nest* theme tune, which was arranged by [Brian Bennett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Bennett_(musician) "Brian Bennett (musician)"). During that period, O'Sullivan also appeared in adverts for [British Gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Gas_plc "British Gas plc").

In 1979, O'Sullivan starred in the title role of [LWT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LWT "LWT")'s [drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama "Drama") series *[Dick Turpin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Turpin_(TV_series) "Dick Turpin (TV series)")*, which ran until 1982. He then played the [widower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Widower "Widower") Simon Harrup in the sitcom *[Me and My Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Me_and_My_Girl_(TV_series) "Me and My Girl (TV series)")*, broadcast from 1984 to 1988, co-starring [Tim Brooke-Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Brooke-Taylor "Tim Brooke-Taylor") and [Joan Sanderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Sanderson "Joan Sanderson"), also produced by LWT. He also appeared in a one-off comedy-drama *The Giftie*, shown on Channel Four in 1988, in which he and a friend ([John Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wells_(satirist) "John Wells (satirist)")) discovered a photocopier at work that could duplicate living copies of themselves, unwisely doing so, and predictably leading to mistaken identities and chaos. In the 1990s, his profile diminished, although he was never short of work. His final acting role was in a 1996 one-off satire titled *Holed*, with [Tony Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Robinson "Tony Robinson"), about a suburban golf club.

O'Sullivan largely retired from public life in 1996. His last appearance on television was as a guest on a 1999 edition of *[This Is Your Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Is_Your_Life_(British_TV_series) "This Is Your Life (British TV series)")* held in honour of his *Doctor...* co-star [George Layton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Layton "George Layton"). O'Sullivan had himself been the subject of the show in 1974. In 2006, O'Sullivan recorded a commentary for the DVD release of *[Carry On Teacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carry_On_Teacher "Carry On Teacher")*.

## Personal life and death

O'Sullivan married the model Diana Terry in 1971; they divorced later that year. During his run as Robin, O'Sullivan had relationships with two of his co-stars, [Sally Thomsett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sally_Thomsett "Sally Thomsett") from *Man About the House*, and [Tessa Wyatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tessa_Wyatt "Tessa Wyatt") who played Vicky in *Robin's Nest*. She divorced [Tony Blackburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Blackburn "Tony Blackburn") during their seven-year relationship. They had one son together. O'Sullivan was married to Christine Smart from 1988 until their divorce in 1994.

A fan of [Chelsea F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_F.C. "Chelsea F.C."), O'Sullivan also played for a number of charity football teams until a broken leg forced retirement.

During the 1990s, O'Sullivan suffered from a period of ill health. Following a stroke in 2003, O'Sullivan lived in [Brinsworth House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brinsworth_House "Brinsworth House"), a [Royal Variety Charity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Variety_Charity "Royal Variety Charity") retirement home for entertainers in [Twickenham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twickenham "Twickenham").

O'Sullivan died at Brinsworth House on 4 September 2026, aged 82.
