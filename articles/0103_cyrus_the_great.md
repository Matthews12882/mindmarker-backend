# Cyrus the Great

**Cyrus II of Persia** (c. 600 – 530 BC), commonly known as **Cyrus the Great**, was the founder of the [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire "Achaemenid Empire"). Hailing from [Persis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persis "Persis"), he brought the [Achaemenid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_dynasty "Achaemenid dynasty") to power by defeating the [Median Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_Empire "Median Empire") and embracing all of the previous civilized states of the [ancient Near East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Near_East "Ancient Near East"), expanding vastly across most of [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia "West Asia") and much of [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia") to create what would soon become the [largest empire in history at the time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_empires#Timeline_of_largest_empires_at_the_time "List of largest empires"). The Achaemenid Empire's greatest territorial extent was achieved under [Darius the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_the_Great "Darius the Great"), whose rule stretched from [Southeast Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Europe "Southeast Europe") and [Northeast Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Africa "Northeast Africa") in the west to the [Indus Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley "Indus Valley") in the east.

After absorbing the Median Empire, Cyrus conquered [Lydia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia "Lydia") and eventually the [Neo-Babylonian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Babylonian_Empire "Neo-Babylonian Empire"), granting him control of [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia "Anatolia") and the [Fertile Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertile_Crescent "Fertile Crescent"), respectively. He also led a major expedition into Central Asia, where his army brought "into subjection every nation without exception" before he allegedly died in battle with the [Massagetae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massagetae "Massagetae"), a nomadic [Eastern Iranian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_peoples#Eastern_Iranian_peoples "Iranian peoples"), along the [Syr Darya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syr_Darya "Syr Darya") in December 530 BC. However, according to [Xenophon of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenophon "Xenophon"), Cyrus did not die fighting and had instead returned to the capital city of [Pasargadae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persepolis "Persepolis"). He was succeeded by his son [Cambyses II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambyses_II "Cambyses II"), whose campaigns led to the conquests of [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt "Ancient Egypt"), [Nubia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubia "Nubia"), and [Cyrenaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrenaica "Cyrenaica") during his short reign.

To the [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece "Ancient Greece"), he was known as *Cyrus the Elder* and was particularly renowned among contemporary scholars because of his habitual policy of tolerance for peoples' customs and religions in the lands that he conquered. Similarly, he is exalted in [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism "Judaism") for his role in freeing the [Jewish people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews") from the [Babylonian captivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonian_captivity "Babylonian captivity") by issuing the [Edict of Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edict_of_Restoration "Edict of Restoration") following the [Persian conquest of Babylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Babylon "Fall of Babylon"). This event is described in the [Hebrew Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible "Hebrew Bible") as the [return to Zion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_to_Zion "Return to Zion"), whereby displaced Jews were repatriated to what had been the [Kingdom of Judah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Judah "Kingdom of Judah"), thus enabling the resurgence of [Jewish sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_and_Judaism_in_the_Land_of_Israel "History of the Jews and Judaism in the Land of Israel") in the [Land of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_of_Israel "Land of Israel"). Cyrus also facilitated Jewish aspirations for a new [Temple in Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_in_Jerusalem "Temple in Jerusalem") in the Achaemenid Empire's province of [Yehud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yehud_(Persian_province) "Yehud (Persian province)"), where the original [Solomon's Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon%27s_Temple "Solomon's Temple") had once stood before being destroyed during the [Babylonian siege of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Jerusalem_(587_BC) "Siege of Jerusalem (587 BC)"). His efforts resulted in the completion of the [Second Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Temple "Second Temple"), which marked the beginning of the [Second Temple period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Temple_period "Second Temple period") and [Second Temple Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Temple_Judaism "Second Temple Judaism"). According to the [Book of Isaiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Isaiah "Book of Isaiah"), he was anointed by [Yahweh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_in_Judaism "God in Judaism") and explicitly designated "[messiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messiah_in_Judaism "Messiah in Judaism")" for this task; Cyrus is the only non-Jewish figure to be revered in this capacity.

In addition to his influence on traditions in both [the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_world "Eastern world") and [the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_world "Western world"), Cyrus is recognized for his achievements in politics and military strategy. He was influential in developing the system of a central administration at his capital city to govern the Achaemenid Empire's [satraps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satrap "Satrap"), who worked for the profit of both rulers and subjects. His realm's prestige in the ancient world would gradually reach as far west as [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Athens "History of Athens"), where upper-class Greeks adopted aspects of the culture of the ruling Persian class as their own. Likewise, Cyrus's reign played a crucial role in defining the [history of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Iran "History of Iran") for well over a millennium, as future [Persian empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_monarchs_of_Iran "List of monarchs of Iran") often viewed the Achaemenid era with deference and as the ideal example to emulate. His dynasty was also instrumental in allowing [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism "Zoroastrianism") to develop and spread as far east as [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_China "History of China"). To this end, he remains a cult figure in [modern Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran"), with his [Pasargadae tomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Cyrus_the_Great "Tomb of Cyrus the Great") serving as a spot of reverence for millions of the country's citizens.

## Etymology

The name *Cyrus* is a [Latinized form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latinisation_of_names "Latinisation of names") derived from the [Greek-language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Greek "History of Greek") name [Κῦρος](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%CE%9A%E1%BF%A6%CF%81%CE%BF%CF%82 "wikt:Κῦρος") (*Kỹros*), which itself was derived from the [Old Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Persian "Old Persian") name *Kūruš*. The name and its meaning have been recorded within ancient inscriptions in different languages. The [ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece "Ancient Greece") historians [Ctesias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ctesias "Ctesias") and [Plutarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutarch "Plutarch") stated that Cyrus was named from the [Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun "Sun") (*Kuros*), a concept which has been interpreted as meaning "like the Sun" (*Khurvash*) by noting its relation to the Persian noun for Sun, *khor*, while using *-vash* as a suffix of likeness. [Karl Hoffmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Hoffmann_(linguist) "Karl Hoffmann (linguist)") has suggested a translation based on the meaning of an [Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European_language "Proto-Indo-European language") root "to humiliate", and accordingly, the name "Cyrus" means "humiliator of the enemy in verbal contest". Another possible Iranian derivation would mean "the young one, child", similar to [Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_language "Kurdish language") *kur* ("son, little boy") or [Ossetian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ossetian_language "Ossetian language") *i-gur-un* ("to be born") and *kur* (young bull). In the [Persian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language "Persian language") and especially in [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran"), Cyrus's name is spelled as [کوروش](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%DA%A9%D9%88%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B4 "wikt:کوروش") (*Kūroš*, [[kuːˈɾoʃ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Persian "Help:IPA/Persian")). In the [Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible "Bible"), he is referred to in the [Hebrew language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language "Hebrew language") as *Koresh* (כורש). Some pieces of evidence suggest that Cyrus is [Kay Khosrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kay_Khosrow "Kay Khosrow"), a legendary Persian king of the [Kayanian dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayanian_dynasty "Kayanian dynasty") and a character in *[Shahnameh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahnameh "Shahnameh")*, a Persian [epic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_poetry "Epic poetry").

Some scholars, however, believe that neither Cyrus nor Cambyses were [Iranian names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_name "Persian name"), proposing that Cyrus was [Elamite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elam "Elam") in origin and that the name meant "he who bestows care" in the extinct [Elamite language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elamite_language "Elamite language"). One reason is that, while Elamite names may end in *-uš*, no Elamite texts spell the name this way – only *Kuraš*. Meanwhile, Old Persian did not allow names to end in *-aš*, so it would make sense for Persian speakers to change an original *Kuraš* into the more grammatically correct form *Kuruš*. Elamite scribes, on the other hand, would not have had a reason to change an original *Kuraš* into *Kuruš*, since both forms were acceptable. Therefore, *Kuraš* probably represents the original form. Another scholarly opinion is that *Kuruš* was a name of [Indo-Aryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_languages "Indo-Aryan languages") origin, in honour of the Indo-Aryan [Kuru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuru_kingdom "Kuru kingdom") and [Kamboja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kambojas "Kambojas") mercenaries from eastern Afghanistan and Northwest India that helped in the conquest of the Middle East.

## Dynastic history

The Persian domination and kingdom in the Iranian plateau started as an extension of the Achaemenid dynasty, who expanded their earlier dominion possibly from the 9th century BC onward. The eponymous founder of the dynasty was [Achaemenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenes "Achaemenes") (from Old Persian *Haxāmaniš*). Achaemenids are "descendants of Achaemenes", as [Darius the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_the_Great "Darius the Great"), the ninth king of the dynasty, traced his ancestry to him, declaring "for this reason, we are called Achaemenids". Achaemenes built the state of [Parsumash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parsua "Parsua") in the southwest of [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran") and was succeeded by [Teispes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teispes "Teispes"), who took the title "King of [Anshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anshan_(Persia) "Anshan (Persia)")" after seizing the city Anshan and enlarging his kingdom further to include [Pars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fars_province "Fars province") proper. Ancient documents mention that Teispes had a son called [Cyrus I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_I "Cyrus I"), who also succeeded his father as "king of Anshan". Cyrus I had a full brother whose name is recorded as [Ariaramnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariaramnes "Ariaramnes").

In 600 BC, Cyrus I was succeeded by his son, [Cambyses I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambyses_I "Cambyses I"), who reigned until 559 BC. Cyrus II "the Great" was a son of Cambyses I, who had named his son after his father, Cyrus I. There are several inscriptions of Cyrus the Great and later kings that refer to Cambyses I as the "great king" and "king of Anshan". Among these are some passages in the Cyrus cylinder where Cyrus calls himself "son of Cambyses, great king, king of Anshan". Another inscription (from CM's) mentions Cambyses I as a "mighty king" and "an Achaemenian", which according to the bulk of scholarly opinion was engraved under Darius and considered as a later forgery by Darius. However, Cambyses II's maternal grandfather Pharnaspes is named by historian [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus "Herodotus") as "an Achaemenian". [Xenophon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenophon "Xenophon")'s account in his Cyropædia names Cambyses's wife as Mandane and mentions Cambyses as king of Iran (ancient Persia). These agree with Cyrus's own inscriptions, as Anshan and [Parsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persepolis "Persepolis") were different names for the same land. These also agree with other non-Iranian accounts, except on one point from Herodotus which states that Cambyses was not a king but a "Persian of good family". However, in some other passages, Herodotus's account is wrong also on the name of the son of [Chishpish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teispes "Teispes"), which he mentions as Cambyses but according to modern scholars, should be [Cyrus I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_I "Cyrus I").

The traditional view based on archaeological research and the genealogy given in the [Behistun Inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behistun_Inscription "Behistun Inscription") and by Herodotus holds that Cyrus the Great was an Achaemenid. However, M. Waters has suggested that Cyrus is unrelated to the Achaemenids or Darius the Great, and that his family was of [Teispid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teispid "Teispid") and Anshanite origin instead of Achaemenid.

### Titles

His regal titles in full were The Great King, King of Persia, King of Anshan, King of Media, King of Babylon, King of Sumer and Akkad, and [King of the Four Corners of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_the_Four_Corners_of_the_World "King of the Four Corners of the World"). The [Nabonidus Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabonidus_Chronicle "Nabonidus Chronicle") notes the change in his title from "King of Anshan" to "King of Persia". Assyriologist François Vallat wrote that "When Astyages marched against Cyrus, Cyrus is called 'King of Anshan", but when Cyrus crosses the [Tigris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigris "Tigris") on his way to Lydia, he is 'King of Persia.' The coup therefore took place between these two events."

## Early and personal life

Cyrus was born to [Cambyses I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambyses_I "Cambyses I"), King of Anshan, and Mandane, daughter of [Astyages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astyages "Astyages"), King of Media, during the period of 600–599 BC.

By his own account, generally believed now to be accurate, Cyrus was preceded as king by his father Cambyses I, grandfather Cyrus I, and great-grandfather Teispes. Cyrus married [Cassandane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassandane "Cassandane") who was an Achaemenian and the daughter of Pharnaspes. Cassandane bore him two sons, [Cambyses II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambyses_II "Cambyses II") and [Bardiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bardiya "Bardiya") along with three daughters, [Atossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atossa "Atossa"), [Artystone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artystone "Artystone"), and Roxane.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] Cyrus and Cassandane were known to love each other very much – Cassandane said that she found it more bitter to leave Cyrus than to depart her life. After her death, Cyrus insisted on public mourning throughout the kingdom. The [Nabonidus Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabonidus_Chronicle "Nabonidus Chronicle") states that Babylonia mourned Cassandane for six days (identified as 21–26 March 538 BC). After his father's death, Cyrus inherited the Persian throne at [Pasargadae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasargadae "Pasargadae"), which was a vassal of Astyages. The Greek historian [Strabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabo "Strabo") has said that Cyrus was originally named Agradates by his step-parents. It is possible that, when reuniting with his original family, following the naming customs, Cyrus's father, [Cambyses I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambyses_I "Cambyses I"), named him Cyrus after his grandfather, who was [Cyrus I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_I "Cyrus I"). There is also an account by Strabo that claimed Agradates adopted the name Cyrus after the Cyrus river near Pasargadae.

### Mythology

[Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus "Herodotus") gave a mythological account of Cyrus's early life. In this account, Astyages had two prophetic dreams in which a flood, and then a series of fruit-bearing vines, emerged from his daughter Mandane's pelvis, and covered the entire kingdom. These were interpreted by his advisers as a foretelling that his grandson would one day rebel and supplant him as king. Astyages summoned Mandane, at the time pregnant with Cyrus, back to [Ecbatana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecbatana "Ecbatana") to have the child killed. His general [Harpagus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpagus "Harpagus") delegated the task to Mithradates, one of the shepherds of Astyages, who raised the child and passed off his stillborn son to Harpagus as the dead infant Cyrus.

Cyrus lived in secrecy, but when he reached the age of 10, during a childhood game, he had the son of a nobleman beaten when he refused to obey Cyrus's commands. As it was unheard of for the son of a shepherd to commit such an act, Astyages had the boy brought to his court, and interviewed him and his adoptive father. Upon the shepherd's confession, Astyages sent Cyrus back to Persia to live with his biological parents. However, Astyages summoned the son of Harpagus, and in retribution, chopped him to pieces, roasted some portions while boiling others, and tricked his adviser into [eating his child](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_cannibalism "Child cannibalism") during a large banquet. Following the meal, Astyages's servants brought Harpagus the head, hands and feet of his son on platters, so he could realize his inadvertent cannibalism.

## Rise to power

### Succession to Media

Cyrus the Great succeeded to the throne in 559 BC following his father's death; however, Cyrus was not yet an independent ruler. Like his predecessors, Cyrus had to recognize [Median](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medes "Medes") overlordship. [Astyages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astyages "Astyages"), last king of the [Median Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_Empire "Median Empire") and Cyrus's grandfather, may have ruled over the majority of the [Ancient Near East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Near_East "Ancient Near East"), from the [Lydian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia "Lydia") frontier in the west to the lands of the [Parthians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthia "Parthia") and Persians in the east.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

According to the [Nabonidus Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabonidus_Chronicle "Nabonidus Chronicle"), [Astyages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astyages "Astyages") launched an attack against Cyrus, "king of Ansan". According to the historian Herodotus, it is known that Astyages placed [Harpagus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpagus "Harpagus") in command of the Median army to conquer Cyrus. However, Harpagus contacted Cyrus and encouraged his revolt against Media, before eventually defecting along with several of the nobility and a portion of the army. This mutiny is confirmed by the Nabonidus Chronicle. The Chronicle suggests that the hostilities lasted for at least three years (553–550 BC), and the final battle resulted in the capture of Ecbatana. This was described in the paragraph that preceded the entry for Nabonidus's year 7, which detailed Cyrus's victory and the capture of his grandfather. According to the historians Herodotus and Ctesias, Cyrus spared the life of Astyages and married his daughter, Amytis. This marriage pacified several vassals, including the [Bactrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bactria "Bactria"), [Parthians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthia "Parthia"), and [Saka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saka "Saka"). Cyrus also subdued and incorporated [Sogdia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sogdia "Sogdia") into the empire during his military campaigns of 546–539 BC.

With Astyages out of power, all of his vassals (including many of Cyrus's relatives) were now under his command. His father's cousin [Arsames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsames "Arsames"), who had been the king of the city-state of Parsa under the [Medes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medes "Medes"), therefore would have had to give up his throne. However, this transfer of power within the family seems to have been smooth, and it is likely that Arsames was still the nominal governor of Parsa under Cyrus's authority—more a Prince or a Grand Duke than a King. His son, [Hystaspes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hystaspes_(father_of_Darius_I) "Hystaspes (father of Darius I)"), who was also Cyrus's second cousin, was then made [satrap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satrap "Satrap") of Parthia and [Phrygia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phrygia "Phrygia"). Cyrus the Great thus united the twin Achaemenid kingdoms of Parsa and Anshan into Persia proper. Arsames lived to see his grandson become Darius the Great, [Shahanshah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahanshah "Shahanshah") of Persia, after the deaths of both of Cyrus's sons. Cyrus's conquest of Media was merely the start of his wars.

### Conquest of Lydia and Asia Minor

The exact dates of the Lydian conquest are unknown, but it must have taken place between Cyrus's overthrow of the Median kingdom (550 BC) and his conquest of Babylon (539 BC). It was common in the past to give 547 BC as the year of the conquest due to some interpretations of the [Nabonidus Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabonidus_Chronicle "Nabonidus Chronicle"), but this position is currently not much held. The Lydians first attacked the Achaemenid Empire's city of [Pteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pteria_(Turkey) "Pteria (Turkey)") in [Cappadocia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cappadocia "Cappadocia"). The king of Lydia [Croesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croesus "Croesus") besieged and captured the city enslaving its inhabitants. Meanwhile, the Persians invited the citizens of [Ionia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionia "Ionia") who were part of the Lydian kingdom to revolt against their ruler. The offer was rebuffed, and thus Cyrus levied an army and marched against the Lydians, increasing his numbers while passing through nations in his way. The [Battle of Pteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Pteria "Battle of Pteria") was effectively a stalemate, with both sides suffering heavy casualties by nightfall. Croesus retreated to [Sardis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardis "Sardis") the following morning.

While in Sardis, Croesus sent out requests for his allies to send aid to Lydia. However, near the end of the winter, before the allies could unite, Cyrus the Great pushed the war into Lydian territory and besieged Croesus in his capital, Sardis. Shortly before the final [Battle of Thymbra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Thymbra "Battle of Thymbra") between the two rulers, Harpagus advised Cyrus the Great to place his [dromedaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dromedary "Dromedary") in front of his warriors; the Lydian horses, not used to the dromedaries' smell, would be very afraid. The strategy worked; the Lydian cavalry was routed. Cyrus defeated and captured Croesus. Cyrus occupied the capital at Sardis, conquering the Lydian kingdom in 546 BC. According to Herodotus, Cyrus the Great spared Croesus's life and kept him as an advisor, but this account conflicts with some translations of the contemporary Nabonidus Chronicle which interpret that the king of Lydia was slain.

Before Cyrus returned to the capital, [Commagene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commagene "Commagene") was incorporated into Persia in 546 BC. Later, a Lydian named [Pactyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pactyas "Pactyas") was entrusted by Cyrus to send Croesus's treasury to Persia. However, soon after Cyrus's departure, Pactyas hired mercenaries and caused an uprising in Sardis, revolting against the Persian [satrap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satrap "Satrap") of Lydia, Tabalus. Cyrus sent [Mazares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazares "Mazares"), one of his commanders, to subdue the insurrection but demanded that Pactyas be returned alive. Upon Mazares's arrival, Pactyas fled to [Ionia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionia "Ionia"), where he had hired more mercenaries. Mazares marched his troops into the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece "Greece") country and subdued the cities of [Magnesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnesia_on_the_Maeander "Magnesia on the Maeander") and [Priene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priene "Priene"). The fate of Pactyas is unknown, but after capture, he was probably sent to Cyrus and put to death after being tortured.

[Mazares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazares "Mazares") continued the conquest of Asia Minor but died of unknown causes during his campaign in Ionia. Cyrus sent Harpagus to complete Mazares's conquest of Asia Minor. Harpagus captured [Lycia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycia "Lycia"), [Aeolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeolis "Aeolis") and [Caria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caria "Caria"), using the technique of building earthworks to breach the walls of besieged cities, a method unknown to the Greeks. He ended his conquest of the area in 542 BC and returned to Persia.

### Conquests in Central and South Asia

After the conquest of Lydia, Cyrus campaigned in the east between around 545 BC to 540 BC. Cyrus first tried to [conquer Gedrosia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gedrosian_campaign "Gedrosian campaign"), however he was decisively defeated and departed Gedrosia. Gedrosia was most likely conquered during the reign of Darius I. After the failed attempt to conquer Gedrosia, Cyrus attacked the regions of [Bactria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bactria "Bactria"), [Arachosia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arachosia "Arachosia"), [Sogdia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sogdia "Sogdia"), [Saka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saka "Saka"), [Chorasmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chorasmia "Chorasmia"), [Margiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margiana "Margiana") and other provinces in the east. In 533 BC, Cyrus the Great crossed the Hindu Kush mountains and collected tribute from the Indus cities. Thus, Cyrus probably had established vassal states in western India. Cyrus then returned with his army to Babylon due to the unrest taking place in and around Babylon.

### Conquest of the Neo-Babylonian Empire

By the year 540 BC, Cyrus captured [Elam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elam "Elam") and its capital, [Susa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susa "Susa"). The Nabonidus Chronicle records that, prior to the battle(s), the king of the Neo-Babylonian Empire, [Nabonidus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabonidus "Nabonidus"), had ordered cult statues from outlying Babylonian cities to be brought into the capital, suggesting that the conflict had begun possibly in the winter of 540 BC. Just before October 539 BC, Cyrus fought the [Battle of Opis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Opis "Battle of Opis") in or near the strategic riverside city of [Opis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opis "Opis") on the Tigris, north of Babylon. The Babylonian army was routed, and on 10 October, Sippar was seized without a battle, with little to no resistance from the populace. It is probable that Cyrus engaged in negotiations with the Babylonian generals to obtain a compromise on their part and therefore avoid an armed confrontation. Nabonidus, who had retreated to Sippar following his defeat at Opis, fled to Borsippa.

Around 12 October, Persian general [Gubaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gobryas_(general) "Gobryas (general)")'s troops entered Babylon, again without any resistance from the Babylonian armies, and detained Nabonidus. [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus "Herodotus") explains that to accomplish this feat, the Persians, using a basin dug earlier by the Babylonian queen [Nitokris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitocris_of_Babylon "Nitocris of Babylon") to protect Babylon against Median attacks, diverted the [Euphrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates "Euphrates") river into a canal so that the water level dropped "to the height of the middle of a man's thigh", which allowed the invading forces to march directly through the river bed to enter at night. Shortly thereafter, Nabonidus returned from Borsippa and surrendered to Cyrus. On 29 October, Cyrus entered the city of Babylon.

Prior to Cyrus's invasion of Babylon, the [Neo-Babylonian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Babylonian_Empire "Neo-Babylonian Empire") had conquered many kingdoms. In addition to Babylonia, Cyrus probably incorporated its sub-national entities into his Empire, including [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria "Syria"), [Judea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judea "Judea"), and [Arabia Petraea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tayma "Tayma"), although there is no direct evidence to support this assumption.

After taking Babylon, Cyrus the Great proclaimed himself "king of Babylon, king of [Sumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumer "Sumer") and [Akkad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_Empire "Akkadian Empire"), king of the four corners of the world" in the famous [Cyrus Cylinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_Cylinder "Cyrus Cylinder"), an inscription on a cylinder that was deposited in the foundations of the [Esagila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esagila "Esagila") temple dedicated to the chief Babylonian god, Marduk. The text of the cylinder denounces Nabonidus as impious and portrays the victorious Cyrus as pleasing the god Marduk. It describes how Cyrus had improved the lives of the citizens of Babylonia, repatriated displaced peoples, and restored temples and cult sanctuaries. Although some have asserted that the cylinder represents a form of human rights charter, historians generally portray it in the context of a long-standing Mesopotamian tradition of new rulers beginning their reigns with declarations of reforms.

Cyrus the Great's dominions composed the largest empire the world had ever seen to that point. At the end of Cyrus's rule, the Achaemenid Empire stretched from [Asia Minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia "Anatolia") in the west to the [Indus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River "Indus River") in the east.

## Death

### Conflicting narratives

The details of Cyrus's death vary by account. [Ctesias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ctesias "Ctesias"), in his *Persica*, has the longest account, which says Cyrus met his death while putting down resistance from the [Derbices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massagetae "Massagetae") infantry, aided by other Scythian archers and cavalry, plus [Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_India#Iron_Age_(c._1800_–_200_BCE) "History of India") and their [war elephants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_elephant "War elephant"). According to him, this event took place northeast of the headwaters of the Syr Darya.

The account of [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus "Herodotus") from his *Histories* provides the second-longest detail, in which Cyrus met his fate in a fierce battle with the Massagetae, a Scythian tribal confederation from the southern deserts of [Khwarezm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khwarezm "Khwarezm") and [Kyzyl Kum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyzyl_Kum "Kyzyl Kum") in the southernmost portion of the [Eurasian Steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Steppe "Eurasian Steppe") regions of modern-day [Kazakhstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakhstan "Kazakhstan") and [Uzbekistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbekistan "Uzbekistan"), following the advice of [Croesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croesus "Croesus") to attack them in their own territory. The Massagetae were related to the [Scythians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythians "Scythians") in their dress and mode of living; they fought on horseback and on foot. In order to acquire her realm, Cyrus first sent an offer of marriage to their ruler, the queen [Tomyris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomyris "Tomyris"), a proposal she rejected.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

He then commenced his attempt to take Massagetae territory by force (c. 529 BC), beginning by building bridges and towered war boats along his side of the river [Oxus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxus "Oxus"), or [Amu Darya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amu_Darya "Amu Darya"), which separated them. Sending him a warning to cease his encroachment (a warning which she stated she expected he would disregard anyway), Tomyris challenged him to meet her forces in honorable warfare, inviting him to a location in her country a day's march from the river, where their two armies would formally engage each other. He accepted her offer, but, learning that the Massagetae were unfamiliar with wine and its intoxicating effects, he set up and then left camp with plenty of it behind, taking his best soldiers with him and leaving the least capable ones.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The general of Tomyris's army, [Spargapises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spargapises "Spargapises"), who was also her son, and a third of the Massagetian troops, killed the group Cyrus had left there and, finding the camp well stocked with food and the wine, unwittingly drank themselves into inebriation, diminishing their capability to defend themselves when they were then overtaken by a surprise attack. They were successfully defeated, and, although he was taken prisoner, Spargapises committed [suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide "Suicide") once he regained sobriety. Upon learning of what had transpired, Tomyris denounced Cyrus's tactics as underhanded and swore vengeance, leading a second wave of troops into battle herself. Cyrus the Great was ultimately killed, and his forces suffered massive casualties in what Herodotus referred to as the fiercest battle of his career and the ancient world. When it was over, Tomyris ordered the body of Cyrus brought to her, then decapitated him and dipped his head in a vessel of blood in a symbolic gesture of revenge for his bloodlust and the death of her son. However, some scholars question this version, mostly because even Herodotus admits this event was one of many versions of Cyrus's death that he heard from a supposedly reliable source who told him no one was there to see the aftermath.

[Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus "Herodotus") also recounts that Cyrus saw in his sleep the oldest son of Hystaspes ([Darius I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_I "Darius I")) with wings upon his shoulders, shadowing with the one wing Asia, and with the other wing Europe. Archaeologist [Sir Max Mallowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Mallowan "Max Mallowan") explains this statement by Herodotus and its connection with the four winged bas-relief figure of Cyrus the Great in the following way:

> Herodotus therefore, as I surmise, may have known of the close connection between this type of winged figure and the image of Iranian majesty, which he associated with a dream prognosticating the king's death before his last, fatal campaign across the Oxus.

[Muhammad Dandamayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Dandamayev "Muhammad Dandamayev") says that Persians may have taken Cyrus's body back from the Massagetae, unlike what Herodotus claimed.

According to the [Chronicle of Michael the Syrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronicle_of_Michael_the_Syrian "Chronicle of Michael the Syrian") (AD 1166–1199) Cyrus was killed by his wife Tomyris, queen of the Massagetae (Maksata), in the 60th year of Jewish captivity.

An alternative account from [Xenophon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenophon "Xenophon")'s *Cyropaedia* contradicts the others, claiming that Cyrus died peacefully at his capital. The final version of Cyrus's death comes from [Berossus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berossus "Berossus"), who only reports that Cyrus met his death while warring against the [Dahae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahae "Dahae") archers northwest of the headwaters of the Syr Darya.

### Resting place

Cyrus the Great's remains may have been interred in his capital city of [Pasargadae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasargadae "Pasargadae"), where today a [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone "Limestone") tomb (built around 540–530 BC) still exists, which many believe to be his. [Strabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabo "Strabo") and [Arrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrian "Arrian") give nearly identical descriptions of the tomb, based on the eyewitness report of [Aristobulus of Cassandreia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristobulus_of_Cassandreia "Aristobulus of Cassandreia"), who at the request of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great "Alexander the Great") visited the tomb twice. Though the city itself is now in ruins, the burial place of Cyrus the Great has remained largely intact, and the tomb has been partially restored to counter its natural deterioration over the centuries. According to [Plutarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutarch "Plutarch"), his epitaph read:

> O man, whoever you are and wherever you come from, for I know you will come, I am Cyrus who won the Persians their empire. Do not therefore begrudge me this bit of earth that covers my bones.

Cuneiform evidence from Babylon proves that Cyrus died around December 530 BC, and that his son [Cambyses II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambyses_II "Cambyses II") had become king. Cambyses continued his father's policy of expansion, and captured [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt "Ancient Egypt") for the Empire, but soon died after only seven years of rule. He was succeeded either by Cyrus's other son [Bardiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bardiya "Bardiya") or an impostor posing as Bardiya, who became the sole ruler of Persia for seven months, until he was killed by [Darius the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_I_of_Persia "Darius I of Persia").

The translated ancient Roman and Greek accounts give a vivid description of the tomb both geometrically and aesthetically; the tomb's geometric shape has changed little over the years, still maintaining a large stone of quadrangular form at the base, followed by a pyramidal succession of smaller rectangular stones, until after a few slabs, the structure is curtailed by an edifice, with an arched roof composed of a pyramidal shaped stone, and a small opening or window on the side, where the slenderest man could barely squeeze through.

Within this edifice was a golden [coffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffin "Coffin"), resting on a table with golden supports, inside of which the body of Cyrus the Great was interred. Upon his resting place, was a covering of tapestry and drapes made from the best available Babylonian materials, utilizing fine Median worksmanship; below his bed was a fine red carpet, covering the narrow rectangular area of his tomb. Translated Greek accounts describe the tomb as having been placed in the fertile Pasargadae gardens, surrounded by trees and ornamental shrubs, with a group of Achaemenian protectors called the "Magi", stationed nearby to protect the edifice from theft or damage.

Years later, in the chaos created by [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great "Alexander the Great")'s invasion of Persia and after the defeat of [Darius III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_III "Darius III"), Cyrus the Great's tomb was broken into and most of its luxuries were looted. When Alexander reached the tomb, he was horrified by the manner in which the tomb was treated, and questioned the Magi and put them to court. On some accounts, Alexander's decision to put the Magi on trial was more about his attempt to undermine their influence and his show of power in his newly conquered empire, than a concern for Cyrus's tomb. However, Alexander admired Cyrus, from an early age reading Xenophon's *[Cyropaedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyropaedia "Cyropaedia")*, which described Cyrus's heroism in battle and governance as a king and legislator. In any case, Alexander ordered Aristobulus to improve the tomb's condition and restore its interior. Despite his admiration for Cyrus the Great, and his attempts at renovation of his tomb, Alexander had, six years previously (330 BC), sacked [Persepolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persepolis "Persepolis"), the opulent city that Cyrus may have chosen the site for, and either ordered its burning as an act of pro-Greek propaganda or set it on fire during drunken revels.

The edifice has survived the test of time, through invasions, internal divisions, successive empires, regime changes, and revolutions. The last prominent Persian figure to bring attention to the tomb was [Mohammad Reza Pahlavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Reza_Pahlavi "Mohammad Reza Pahlavi") (Shah of Iran) the last official monarch of Persia, during his [celebrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2,500_year_celebration_of_the_Persian_Empire "2,500 year celebration of the Persian Empire") of 2,500 years of monarchy. Just as Alexander the Great before him, the Shah of Iran wanted to appeal to Cyrus's legacy to legitimize his own rule by extension. The United Nations recognizes the tomb of Cyrus the Great and Pasargadae as a UNESCO [World Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage "World Heritage") site.

## Legacy

### Influence on Eastern and Western civilizations

British historian [Charles Freeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Freeman_(historian) "Charles Freeman (historian)") suggests that "In scope and extent his achievements [Cyrus] ranked far above that of the Macedonian king, Alexander, who was to demolish the [Achaemenid] empire in the 320s but fail to provide any stable alternative." Cyrus has been a personal hero to many people, including [Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson "Thomas Jefferson"), [Mohammad Reza Pahlavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Reza_Pahlavi "Mohammad Reza Pahlavi"), and [David Ben-Gurion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ben-Gurion "David Ben-Gurion").

The achievements of Cyrus the Great throughout antiquity are reflected in the way he is remembered today. His own nation, the Iranians, have regarded him as "The Father", the very title that had been used during the time of Cyrus himself, by the many nations that he conquered, as according to [Xenophon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenophon "Xenophon"): Cyrus's standard, described as a golden eagle mounted upon a "lofty shaft", remained the official banner of the Achaemenids.

> And those who were subject to him, he treated with esteem and regard, as if they were his own children, while his subjects themselves respected Cyrus as their "Father" ... What other man but 'Cyrus', after having overturned an empire, ever died with the title of "The Father" from the people whom he had brought under his power? For it is plain fact that this is a name for one that bestows, rather than for one that takes away!

The historian [Plutarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutarch "Plutarch") (c. 46 – c. 119 AD) tells that "the Persians, because Cyrus was hook-nosed, even to this day love hook-nosed men and consider them the most handsome".

The Babylonians regarded him as "The Liberator", as they were offended by their previous ruler, Nabonidus, for committing sacrilege. The rise of Persia under Cyrus's rule had a profound impact on the course of world history, including in forms of [Iranian philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_philosophy "Iranian philosophy"), [literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_literature "Persian literature") and [religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranic_religions "Iranic religions").
Many of the Iranian dynasties following the Achaemenid Empire and their kings saw themselves as the heirs to Cyrus the Great and have claimed to continue the line begun by Cyrus. However, there are different opinions among scholars whether this is also the case for the [Sassanid Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassanid_Dynasty "Sassanid Dynasty").

[Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great "Alexander the Great") was himself infatuated with and admired Cyrus the Great, from an early age reading Xenophon's *[Cyropaedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyropaedia "Cyropaedia")*, which described Cyrus's heroism in battle and governance and his abilities as a king and a legislator. During his visit to Pasargadae he ordered Aristobulus to decorate the interior of the sepulchral chamber of Cyrus's tomb.

Cyrus's legacy has been felt even as far away as [Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceland "Iceland") and colonial America. Many of the thinkers and rulers of [Classical Antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Antiquity "Classical Antiquity") as well as the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance "Renaissance") and [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment "Age of Enlightenment") era, and the forefathers of the United States of America sought inspiration from Cyrus the Great through works such as *Cyropaedia*. Thomas Jefferson, for example, owned two copies of *Cyropaedia*, one with parallel Greek and Latin translations on facing pages showing substantial Jefferson markings that signify the amount of influence the book has had on drafting the [United States Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Declaration_of_Independence "United States Declaration of Independence").

According to Professor [Richard Nelson Frye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nelson_Frye "Richard Nelson Frye"), Cyrus—whose abilities as conqueror and administrator Frye says are attested by the longevity and vigor of the Achaemenid Empire—held an almost mythic role among the Persian people "similar to that of [Romulus and Remus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romulus_and_Remus "Romulus and Remus") in Rome or [Moses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses "Moses") for the Israelites", with a story that "follows in many details the stories of hero and conquerors from elsewhere in the ancient world." Frye writes, "He became the epitome of the great qualities expected of a ruler in antiquity, and he assumed heroic features as a conqueror who was tolerant and magnanimous as well as brave and daring. His personality as seen by the Greeks influenced them and Alexander the Great, and, as the tradition was transmitted by the Romans, may be considered to influence our thinking even now."

His rule was studied and admired by many of the great leaders, such as [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great "Alexander the Great"), [Julius Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Caesar "Julius Caesar") and [Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson "Thomas Jefferson").

### Administration and politics

#### Satrapy system

Cyrus founded the empire as a multi-[state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_state "Sovereign state") empire governed by four capital states; [Pasargadae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasargadae "Pasargadae"), [Babylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylon "Babylon"), [Susa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susa "Susa") and [Ecbatana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecbatana "Ecbatana"). He allowed a certain amount of regional autonomy in each state, in the form of a [satrapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satrap "Satrap") system. A satrapy was an administrative unit, usually organized on a geographical basis. A 'satrap' (governor) was the [vassal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassal "Vassal") king, who administered the region, a 'general' supervised military recruitment and ensured order, and a 'state secretary' kept the official records. The general and the state secretary reported directly to the satrap as well as the central government.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

During his reign, Cyrus maintained control over a vast region of conquered kingdoms, achieved through retaining and expanding the satrapies. Further organization of newly conquered territories into provinces ruled by satraps was continued by Cyrus's successor, [Darius the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_I_of_Persia "Darius I of Persia").

#### Military strategy

Cyrus's empire was based on [tribute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribute "Tribute") and [conscripts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription "Conscription") from the many parts of his realm. Through his military savvy, Cyrus created an organized army including the [Immortals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Immortals "Persian Immortals") unit, consisting of 10,000 highly trained soldiers.

Cyrus's conquests began a new era in the age of empire building, where a vast [superstate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superstate "Superstate"), comprising many dozens of countries, races, religions, and languages, were ruled under a single administration headed by a central government. This system lasted for centuries, and was retained both by the invading [Seleucid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire "Seleucid Empire") during their control of Persia, and later Iranian dynasties including the [Parthians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthian_Empire "Parthian Empire") and [Sasanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire "Sasanian Empire").

#### Infrastructure projects

Cyrus has been known for his innovations in building projects; he further developed the technologies that he found in the conquered cultures and applied them in building the palaces of Pasargadae. He was also famous for his love of gardens; the recent excavations in his capital city has revealed the existence of the Pasargadae [Persian Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_gardens "Persian gardens") and a network of irrigation [canals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal "Canal"). Pasargadae was a place for two magnificent palaces surrounded by a majestic royal park and vast formal gardens; among them was the four-quartered wall gardens of "[Paradisia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise "Paradise")" with over 1000 meters of channels made out of carved [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone "Limestone"), designed to fill small basins at every 16 meters and water various types of wild and domestic [flora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora "Flora"). The design and concept of Paradisia were exceptional and have been used as a model for many ancient and modern parks, ever since.

He also formed an innovative [postal system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postal_system "Postal system") throughout the empire, based on several relay stations called [Chapar Khaneh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapar_Khaneh "Chapar Khaneh").

In 1658, while the [Protectorate of Cromwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Protectorate "The Protectorate") ruled Britain, the English physician and philosopher Sir [Thomas Browne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Browne "Thomas Browne") penned a discourse entitled [The Garden of Cyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Garden_of_Cyrus "The Garden of Cyrus"), in which Cyrus is depicted as an archetypal wise ruler. However, despite being featured in the title, Cyrus was not a main focus of Browne's work.

"Cyrus the elder, brought up in Woods and Mountains, when time and power enabled, pursued the dictate of his education, and brought the treasures of the field into rule and circum-scription. So nobly beautifying the hanging Gardens of Babylon, that he was also thought to be the author thereof."

#### Currency denomination

The use of the name *[Kuruş](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuru%C5%9F "Kuruş")* as a currency denomination for coinage goes back to the 6th century BC, dating to the time of the [Croeseid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croeseid "Croeseid"), the world's first gold coin, originally minted by King [Croesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croesus "Croesus") of [Lydia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia "Lydia"). The Croeseid was later [continued to be minted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_coinage "Achaemenid coinage") and spread in a wide geographical area by Cyrus the Great ([Old Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Persian_language "Old Persian language"): [𐎤𐎢𐎽𐎢𐏁](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%F0%90%8E%A4%F0%90%8E%A2%F0%90%8E%BD%F0%90%8E%A2%F0%90%8F%81 "wikt:𐎤𐎢𐎽𐎢𐏁") *Kūruš*), the founder of the [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire "Achaemenid Empire"), who defeated King Croesus and conquered Lydia with the [Battle of Thymbra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Thymbra "Battle of Thymbra") in 547 BC. Cyrus (Kūruš) made the Croeseid the standard gold coin of his vast empire, using the same *lion and bull* design, but with a reduced weight (8.06 grams, instead of the standard 10.7 grams of the original version issued by King Croesus) due to the need for larger amounts of these coins, for a much larger population.

### Human rights and multiculturalism

#### Cyrus Cylinder

One of the few surviving sources of information that can be dated directly to Cyrus's time is the Cyrus Cylinder ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language "Persian language"): استوانه کوروش), a document in the form of a clay cylinder inscribed in [Akkadian cuneiform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_language "Akkadian language"). It had been placed in the foundations of the [Esagila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esagila "Esagila") (the temple of [Marduk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marduk "Marduk") in [Babylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylon "Babylon")) as a [foundation deposit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundation_deposit "Foundation deposit") following the Persian conquest in 539 BC. It was discovered in 1879 and is kept today in the [British Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Museum "British Museum") in London.

The text of the cylinder denounces the deposed Babylonian king [Nabonidus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabonidus "Nabonidus") as impious and portrays Cyrus as pleasing to the chief god [Marduk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marduk "Marduk"). It describes how Cyrus had improved the lives of the citizens of Babylonia, [repatriated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repatriation "Repatriation") displaced peoples and restored temples and cult sanctuaries. Although not mentioned specifically in the text, the repatriation of the [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews") from their "[Babylonian captivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonian_captivity "Babylonian captivity")" has been interpreted as part of this general policy.

In the 1970s, the [Shah of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Reza_Pahlavi "Mohammad Reza Pahlavi") adopted the Cyrus cylinder as a political symbol, using it "as a central image in his celebration of 2500 years of Iranian monarchy", and asserting that it was "the first human rights charter in history". This view has been disputed by some as "rather anachronistic" and tendentious, as the modern concept of human rights would have been quite alien to Cyrus's contemporaries and is not mentioned by the cylinder. The cylinder has, nonetheless, become seen as part of Iran's cultural identity.

The United Nations has declared the relic to be an "ancient declaration of human rights" since 1971, approved by then Secretary General Sithu [U Thant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Thant "U Thant"), after he "was given a replica by [the sister of the Shah of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashraf_Pahlavi "Ashraf Pahlavi")". The British Museum describes the cylinder as "an instrument of ancient Mesopotamian propaganda" that "reflects a long tradition in Mesopotamia where, from as early as the third millennium BC, kings began their reigns with declarations of reforms." The cylinder emphasizes Cyrus's continuity with previous Babylonian rulers, asserting his virtue as a traditional Babylonian king while denigrating his predecessor.

[Neil MacGregor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_MacGregor "Neil MacGregor"), Director of the British Museum, has stated that the cylinder was "the first attempt we know about running a society, a state with different nationalities and faiths – a new kind of statecraft." He explained that "It has even been described as the first declaration of human rights, and while this was never the intention of the document – the modern concept of human rights scarcely existed in the ancient world – it has come to embody the hopes and aspirations of many."

### Religion and philosophy

#### Zoroastrian presence in Achaemenid state affairs

French Iranologist [Pierre Briant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Briant "Pierre Briant") wrote that, given the poor information we have, "it seems quite reckless to try to reconstruct what the religion of Cyrus might have been." It is also debated whether he was a [Zoroastrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism "Zoroastrianism") or whether Zoroastrianism only becomes involved as the Achaemenid state religion after his lifetime. The evidence in favour of it comes from some of the names of members of Cyrus's family, and similarities between the description of [Yahweh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahweh "Yahweh") in [Isaiah 40–48](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Isaiah "Book of Isaiah") (which was after [Isaiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaiah "Isaiah") came into contact with a Persian propagandist under Cyrus's administration who was Zoroastrian) and that of [Ahura Mazda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahura_Mazda "Ahura Mazda") in the [Gathas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatha_(Zoroaster) "Gatha (Zoroaster)"). Against the thesis is how Cyrus permitted total freedom of religion and provided funding for the establishment of their temples and other holy sites, as well as a possible late-date for the activity of the Iranian prophet [Zoroaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroaster "Zoroaster"), who founded Zoroastrianism.

#### Tolerance and freedom of religion

The policies of Cyrus with respect to treatment of minority religions are documented in many historical accounts, particularly in Babylonian and Jewish sources. Cyrus had a general policy of religious tolerance throughout his vast empire. Whether this was a new policy or the continuation of policies followed by the [Babylonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonia "Babylonia") and [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyria "Assyria") (as American historian [Lester Grabbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_Grabbe "Lester Grabbe") maintains) is disputed. He brought peace to the Babylonians and is said to have kept his army away from the temples and restored the statues of the [Babylonian gods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonian_gods "Babylonian gods") to their sanctuaries.

#### Personal faith and practices of Cyrus

Information about religion and ritual during the reign of Cyrus is also available from the [Cyropaedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyropaedia "Cyropaedia") of [Xenophon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenophon "Xenophon"), the [Histories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histories_(Herodotus) "Histories (Herodotus)") of [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus "Herodotus"), and inscriptions, though these were written in later periods and so must be used carefully.

The [Cyrus Cylinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_Cylinder "Cyrus Cylinder") was composed in the name of Cyrus with him as the first-person speaker. The Cylinder is highly religious and is framed around the interventions of the Babylonian national god [Marduk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marduk "Marduk"). It is Marduk who is praised in the outset of the text and whose direct intervention is thought to be responsible for what happened in recent history, and it is Marduk who summons Cyrus for the purpose of righting the wrongs of his predecessor [Nabonidus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabonidus "Nabonidus"). Furthermore, Cyrus offers respect not only to the cult of Marduk, but also to local cults. One inscription from [Uruk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruk "Uruk") states "(Cyrus) loves [Esangil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esangil "Esangil") and [Ezida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezida "Ezida")" while another from [Ur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ur "Ur") mentions that he "returned the gods to their shrines." Another from [Babylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylon "Babylon") or [Sippar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sippar "Sippar") says that unlike Nabonidus, Cyrus provided proper and even increased the sacrifices for the gods. Additional reliable information may come from the funerary customs around the [tomb of Cyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Cyrus_the_Great "Tomb of Cyrus the Great"), which indicates a privileged cult honouring the Iranian god [Mithra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mithra "Mithra"). Some have also identified the figure depicted in the bas-relief from [Pasargadae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasargadae "Pasargadae") as Mithra, further indicating the reverence of Cyrus for this deity. Additional evidence further indicates the connection between Cyrus and Mithra.

#### Liberation of the Jewish people

The treatment of the [Jewish people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_people "Jewish people") by Cyrus during their [exile in Babylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonian_captivity "Babylonian captivity") after the Babylonian king [Nebuchadnezzar II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebuchadnezzar_II "Nebuchadnezzar II") destroyed the [Kingdom of Judah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Judah "Kingdom of Judah") is recorded in the [Hebrew Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible "Hebrew Bible"). Cyrus is represented positively and as an [agent of Yahweh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahwism "Yahwism"), even though he is said to "not know" Yahweh (Isaiah 45:4–5).

The [Ketuvim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketuvim "Ketuvim") ends in [Second Chronicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Chronicles "Second Chronicles") with the decree of Cyrus, which returned the exiles to the Land of Israel from Babylon along with a commission to rebuild the [Temple in Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_in_Jerusalem "Temple in Jerusalem") in light of [Solomon's Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon%27s_Temple "Solomon's Temple") having been destroyed by Babylonian armies.

The [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israelites "Israelites") honored him as a dignified and righteous king. In one Biblical passage, [Isaiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaiah "Isaiah") refers to him as [Messiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messiah "Messiah"), making him the only [gentile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentile "Gentile") to be so referred. Elsewhere in [Isaiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Isaiah "Book of Isaiah"), God is described as saying, "I will raise up Cyrus in my righteousness: I will make all his ways straight. He will rebuild my city and set my exiles free, but not for a price or reward, says God Almighty." These particular passages (often referred to as *[Deutero-Isaiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutero-Isaiah "Deutero-Isaiah")*) are believed by most modern [critical scholars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_criticism "Higher criticism") to have been added by another author toward the end of the Babylonian exile (*c.* 536 BC).

[Josephus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephus "Josephus"), the first-century Jewish historian, relates the traditional view of the Jews regarding the prediction of Cyrus in Isaiah in his *[Antiquities of the Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiquities_of_the_Jews "Antiquities of the Jews")*, book 11, chapter 1:

> In the first year of the reign of Cyrus, which was the seventieth from the day that our people were removed out of their own land into Babylon, God commiserated the captivity and calamity of these poor people, according as he had foretold to them by Jeremiah the prophet, before the destruction of the city, that after they had served Nebuchadnezzar and his posterity, and after they had undergone that servitude seventy years, he would restore them again to the land of their fathers, and they should build their temple, and enjoy their ancient prosperity. And these things God did afford them; for he stirred up the mind of Cyrus, and made him write this throughout all Asia: "Thus saith Cyrus the king: Since God Almighty hath appointed me to be king of the habitable earth, I believe that he is that God which the nation of the Israelites worship; for indeed he foretold my name by the prophets, and that I should build him a house at Jerusalem, in the country of Judea." This was known to Cyrus by his reading the book which Isaiah left behind him of his prophecies; for this prophet said that God had spoken thus to him in a secret vision: "My will is, that Cyrus, whom I have appointed to be king over many and great nations, send back my people to their own land, and build my temple." This was foretold by Isaiah one hundred and forty years before the temple was demolished. Accordingly, when Cyrus read this, and admired the Divine power, an earnest desire and ambition seized upon him to fulfill what was so written; so he called for the most eminent Jews that were in Babylon, and said to them, that he gave them leave to go back to their own country, and to rebuild their city Jerusalem, and the temple of God, for that he would be their assistant, and that he would write to the rulers and governors that were in the neighborhood of their country of Judea, that they should contribute to them gold and silver for the building of the temple, and besides that, beasts for their sacrifices.

There was Jewish criticism of Cyrus after he was lied to by the [Cuthites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuthites "Cuthites"), who wanted to halt the building of the [Second Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Temple "Second Temple"). They accused the Jews of conspiring to rebel, so Cyrus in turn stopped the construction, which would not be completed until 515 BC, during the reign of [Darius I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_I "Darius I"). According to the Bible, it was King [Artaxerxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artaxerxes_I_of_Persia "Artaxerxes I of Persia") who was convinced to stop the construction of the temple in Jerusalem. The historical nature of this decree has been challenged. Professor Lester L Grabbe argues that there was no decree but that there was a policy that allowed exiles to return to their homelands and rebuild their temples. He also argues that the archaeology suggests that the return was a "trickle", taking place over perhaps decades, resulting in a maximum population of perhaps 30,000. [Philip R. Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_R._Davies "Philip R. Davies") called the authenticity of the decree "dubious", citing Grabbe and adding that arguing against "the authenticity of Ezra 1.1–4 is J. Briend, in a paper given at the Institut Catholique de Paris on 15 December 1993, who denies that it resembles the form of an official document but reflects rather biblical prophetic idiom."

Mary Joan Winn Leith believes that the decree in Ezra might be authentic and along with the Cylinder that Cyrus, like earlier rulers, was through these decrees trying to gain support from those who might be strategically important, particularly those close to Egypt which he wished to conquer. She also wrote that "appeals to Marduk in the cylinder and to Yahweh in the biblical decree demonstrate the Persian tendency to co-opt local religious and political traditions in the interest of imperial control."

#### Theorized allusion in Islamic scripture

In the 19th century, a number of prominent [Indian Muslim scholars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_South_Asia "Islam in South Asia") began theorizing that Cyrus the Great is the individual mentioned in the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran "Quran") by the name [Dhu al-Qarnayn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhu_al-Qarnayn "Dhu al-Qarnayn"), who is described as travelling west and east (towards the "[setting and rising places of the Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_path "Sun path")") and erecting a large barrier to separate a troubled people from "[Ya'juj and Ma'juj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ya%27juj_and_Ma%27juj "Ya'juj and Ma'juj")" in exchange for tribute, with this barrier destined to keep them sealed until their release signals the [end of the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_eschatology "Islamic eschatology") and the [Day of Judgement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judgement_Day_in_Islam "Judgement Day in Islam"). It was first promoted by [Syed Ahmad Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syed_Ahmad_Khan "Syed Ahmad Khan") (1817–1898) and then by [Maulana Abul Kalam Azad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maulana_Abul_Kalam_Azad "Maulana Abul Kalam Azad") (1888–1958), subsequently generating wider acceptance among some Muslim communities over the years.

This theory was proposed in 1855 by the German philologist [Gustav Moritz Redslob](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gustav_Moritz_Redslob&action=edit&redlink=1 "Gustav Moritz Redslob (page does not exist)") [[de](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Moritz_Redslob "de:Gustav Moritz Redslob")], but it was largely dismissed in the [Western world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_world "Western world"). Most Western scholars of religion have instead lent credence to more popular theories in traditional [Islamic mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_mythology "Islamic mythology") that identify Dhu al-Qarnayn as either the Greek king [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great "Alexander the Great") or a certain [pre-Islamic Arabian king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Islamic_Arabia "Pre-Islamic Arabia").
