# Tom Cruise

**Thomas Cruise Mapother IV** ([/ˈmeɪˌpɒθər/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:En-us-Mapother.ogg "File:En-us-Mapother.ogg"); born July 3, 1962) is an American actor and film producer. [His accolades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Tom_Cruise "List of awards and nominations received by Tom Cruise") include an [Honorary Palme d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_Palme_d%27Or "Honorary Palme d'Or"), an [Academy Honorary Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Honorary_Award "Academy Honorary Award"), and three [Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards "Golden Globe Awards"), as well as nominations for four competitive [Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards"). Considered to be an [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood,_Los_Angeles "Hollywood, Los Angeles") icon and one of its most [bankable stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bankable_star "Bankable star"), [Cruise's films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cruise_filmography "Tom Cruise filmography") have grossed over $13.3 billion worldwide, making him one of the [highest-grossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_actors "List of highest-grossing actors") and [highest-paid actors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-paid_film_actors "List of highest-paid film actors") of all time.

Cruise began acting in the early 1980s and made his breakthrough with leading roles in *[Risky Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risky_Business "Risky Business")* (1983) and *[Top Gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Gun "Top Gun")* (1986), the latter earning him a reputation as a [sex symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_symbol "Sex symbol"). Critical acclaim came with his roles in the dramas *[The Color of Money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Color_of_Money "The Color of Money")* (1986), *[Rain Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_Man "Rain Man")* (1988), and *[Born on the Fourth of July](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Born_on_the_Fourth_of_July_(film) "Born on the Fourth of July (film)")* (1989). For his portrayal of [Ron Kovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Kovic "Ron Kovic") in the latter, he won a [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actor_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Drama "Golden Globe Award for Best Actor – Motion Picture Drama") and was nominated for the [Academy Award for Best Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor "Academy Award for Best Actor"). As a leading Hollywood star in the 1990s, he starred in commercially successful films, including the drama *[A Few Good Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Few_Good_Men "A Few Good Men")* (1992), the thriller *[The Firm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Firm_(1993_film) "The Firm (1993 film)")* (1993), the horror film *[Interview with the Vampire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interview_with_the_Vampire_(film) "Interview with the Vampire (film)")* (1994), and the sports comedy-drama *[Jerry Maguire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Maguire "Jerry Maguire")* (1996); for the latter, he won a [Golden Globe Award for Best Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actor_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Musical_or_Comedy "Golden Globe Award for Best Actor – Motion Picture Musical or Comedy") and his second nomination for the Academy Award for Best Actor. Cruise's performance in the drama *[Magnolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnolia_(film) "Magnolia (film)")* (1999) earned him another [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture "Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actor – Motion Picture") and nomination for the [Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor "Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor").

Cruise subsequently established himself as a star of [science fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction_film "Science fiction film") and [action films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_film "Action film"), often performing his own risky stunts. He played fictional agent [Ethan Hunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethan_Hunt "Ethan Hunt") in the [*Mission: Impossible* film series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission:_Impossible_(film_series) "Mission: Impossible (film series)") (1996–2025). His other films in the genre include *[Vanilla Sky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanilla_Sky "Vanilla Sky")* (2001), *[Minority Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_Report "Minority Report")* (2002), *[The Last Samurai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Samurai "The Last Samurai")* (2003), *[Collateral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collateral_(film) "Collateral (film)")* (2004), *[War of the Worlds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Worlds_(2005_film) "War of the Worlds (2005 film)")* (2005), *[Knight and Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_and_Day "Knight and Day")* (2010), *[Jack Reacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Reacher_(film) "Jack Reacher (film)")* (2012), *[Oblivion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblivion_(2013_film) "Oblivion (2013 film)")* (2013), *[Edge of Tomorrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_of_Tomorrow "Edge of Tomorrow")* (2014), and *[Top Gun: Maverick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Gun:_Maverick "Top Gun: Maverick")* (2022).

Cruise holds the [Guinness World Record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Records "Guinness World Records") for the most consecutive $100 million-grossing movies, with eleven such films released between 2012 and 2025. In December 2024, he was awarded the [U.S. Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy "United States Navy")'s highest civilian honor, the [Distinguished Public Service Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy_Distinguished_Public_Service_Award "Navy Distinguished Public Service Award"), in recognition of his "outstanding contributions" to the military with his screen roles. In March 2025, he was named the recipient of the [British Film Institute Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Film_Institute_Fellowship "British Film Institute Fellowship"), the BFI's highest honor, for his contributions to cinema. *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes "Forbes")* ranked him the world's most powerful celebrity in 2006. He was named *People*'s [Sexiest Man Alive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexiest_Man_Alive "Sexiest Man Alive") in 1990, and received the top honor of "Most Beautiful People" in 1997.
Cruise has been an outspoken advocate for the [Church of Scientology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Scientology "Church of Scientology"), which has resulted in controversy and scrutiny of his involvement in the organization. An aviation enthusiast, he has held a [pilot certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot_certification_in_the_United_States "Pilot certification in the United States") since 1994.

## Early life and education

Cruise was born on July 3, 1962, in [Syracuse, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_New_York "Syracuse, New York"), to electrical engineer Thomas Cruise Mapother III (1934–1984) and [special education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_education "Special education") teacher Mary Lee (née Pfeiffer; 1936–2017). His parents were both from [Louisville, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisville,_Kentucky "Louisville, Kentucky"), and had English, German, and Irish ancestry. Cruise has two older sisters, Lee Anne and Marian, and a younger sister, Cass. One of his cousins, [William Mapother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Mapother "William Mapother"), is also an actor and has appeared alongside Cruise in five films.

Cruise grew up in near [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty "Poverty") and had a [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church "Catholic Church") upbringing. He later called his father "a merchant of chaos", a "bully", and a "coward" who beat his children: "[My father] was the kind of person where, if something goes wrong, they kick you. It was a great lesson in my life—how he'd lull you in, make you feel safe and then, bang! For me, it was like, 'There's something wrong with this guy. Don't trust him. Be careful around him.'" Cruise's father died of cancer in 1984.

In total, Cruise attended fifteen schools in fourteen years. He spent part of his childhood in Canada; when his father took a job as a defense consultant with the [Canadian Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Armed_Forces "Canadian Armed Forces"), his family moved in late 1971 to [Beacon Hill, Ottawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beacon_Hill,_Ottawa "Beacon Hill, Ottawa"). He attended the new [Robert Hopkins Public School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hopkins_Public_School "Robert Hopkins Public School") for his fourth and fifth grade education. He first became involved in drama in fourth grade, under drama teacher George Steinburg. He and six other boys put on an improvised play to music called *IT* at the Carleton Elementary School drama festival. Drama organizer Val Wright was in the audience and later said that "the movement and improvisation were excellent ... a classic ensemble piece."

In sixth grade, Cruise went to [Henry Munro Middle School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Munro_Middle_School "Henry Munro Middle School") in Ottawa. That year, his mother left his father, taking Cruise and his sisters back to the United States. In 1978 she married Jack South. Cruise briefly took a Catholic church scholarship and attended [St. Francis Seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Francis_Seminary_(Ohio) "St. Francis Seminary (Ohio)") in [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati "Cincinnati"). He aspired to become a [priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priesthood_in_the_Catholic_Church "Priesthood in the Catholic Church") in the [Franciscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscans "Franciscans") order but left after a year. Priests at the seminary have said Cruise chose to leave the school when his family relocated again, but a former classmate said that they were both asked to leave after getting caught taking liquor. In his senior year of high school, he played [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_football "American football") for the varsity team as a [linebacker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linebacker "Linebacker"), but was cut from the squad after getting caught drinking beer before a game. He went on to star in the school's production of *[Guys and Dolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guys_and_Dolls "Guys and Dolls")*. In 1980, he graduated from [Glen Ridge High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glen_Ridge_High_School "Glen Ridge High School") in [Glen Ridge, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glen_Ridge,_New_Jersey "Glen Ridge, New Jersey").

## Acting career

### 1980s: Breakthrough and stardom

At age 18, with the blessing of his mother and stepfather, Cruise moved to New York City to pursue an acting career. After working as a [busboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busboy "Busboy") in New York, he went to [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles") to try out for television roles. He signed with [CAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Artists_Agency "Creative Artists Agency") and began acting in films. He made his film debut in a [bit part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit_part "Bit part") in the 1981 film *[Endless Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endless_Love_(1981_film) "Endless Love (1981 film)")*, followed by a major supporting role as a crazed military academy student in *[Taps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taps_(film) "Taps (film)")* later that year. Cruise was originally supposed to appear as a [background actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extra_(acting) "Extra (acting)") but his role was expanded after he impressed director [Harold Becker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Becker "Harold Becker"). He next won the role of Steve Randle in [Francis Ford Coppola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Ford_Coppola "Francis Ford Coppola")'s 1983 adaptation of [S. E. Hinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._E._Hinton "S. E. Hinton")'s [novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Outsiders_(novel) "The Outsiders (novel)") *[The Outsiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Outsiders_(film) "The Outsiders (film)")*, and shared the screen with an [ensemble cast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensemble_cast "Ensemble cast") that included [Matt Dillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Dillon "Matt Dillon"), [Emilio Estevez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilio_Estevez "Emilio Estevez"), [Leif Garrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leif_Garrett "Leif Garrett"), [C. Thomas Howell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._Thomas_Howell "C. Thomas Howell"), [Diane Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diane_Lane "Diane Lane"), [Rob Lowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Lowe "Rob Lowe"), [Ralph Macchio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Macchio "Ralph Macchio"), and [Patrick Swayze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Swayze "Patrick Swayze"). Also in 1983, he appeared in *[All the Right Moves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_the_Right_Moves_(film) "All the Right Moves (film)")* and *[Risky Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risky_Business "Risky Business")*, which has been called "a Generation X classic, and a career maker for Tom Cruise." He played the male lead in the [Ridley Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridley_Scott "Ridley Scott") film *[Legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legend_(1985_film) "Legend (1985 film)")*, released in 1985. By 1986's *[Top Gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Gun "Top Gun")*, his status as a [superstar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superstar "Superstar") had been cemented.

Cruise followed up *Top Gun* with [Martin Scorsese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Scorsese "Martin Scorsese")'s *[The Color of Money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Color_of_Money "The Color of Money")* (1986), which came out the same year, and which paired him with [Paul Newman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Newman "Paul Newman"). Their chemistry won praise among critics, with *[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")* writing, "One of the subtle achievements of both Cruise's and Newman's performances is that you feel that both of them are genuinely top-notch pool hustlers". In 1988, Cruise starred in *[Cocktail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocktail_(1988_film) "Cocktail (1988 film)")*, a film that was a box-office success but panned by critics. His performance earned him a nomination for the [Razzie Award for Worst Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Razzie_Award_for_Worst_Actor "Razzie Award for Worst Actor"). Later that year he starred with [Dustin Hoffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dustin_Hoffman "Dustin Hoffman") in [Barry Levinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Levinson "Barry Levinson")'s *[Rain Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_Man "Rain Man")*, which won the [Academy Award for Best Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture").

In 1989, Cruise portrayed paralyzed [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War") veteran [Ron Kovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Kovic "Ron Kovic") in [Oliver Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Stone "Oliver Stone")'s war epic *[Born on the Fourth of July](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Born_on_the_Fourth_of_July_(film) "Born on the Fourth of July (film)")*. Film critic [Roger Ebert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ebert "Roger Ebert") of the *[Chicago Sun-Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Sun-Times "Chicago Sun-Times")* wrote: "Nothing Cruise has done will prepare you for what he does in *Born on the Fourth of July* ... His performance is so good that the movie lives through it. Stone is able to make his statement with Cruise's face and voice and doesn't need to put everything into the dialogue." The performance earned him a [Golden Globe Award for Best Actor – Motion Picture Drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actor_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Drama "Golden Globe Award for Best Actor – Motion Picture Drama"), the [Chicago Film Critics Association Award for Best Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Film_Critics_Association_Award_for_Best_Actor "Chicago Film Critics Association Award for Best Actor"), the [People's Choice Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Choice_Award "People's Choice Award") for Favorite Motion Picture Actor, a nomination for [BAFTA Award for Best Actor in a Leading Role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role "BAFTA Award for Best Actor in a Leading Role"), and Cruise's first [Best Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor "Academy Award for Best Actor") [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards") nomination.

### 1990s: Dramatic roles

Cruise's next films were *[Days of Thunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Days_of_Thunder "Days of Thunder")* (1990) and *[Far and Away](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_and_Away "Far and Away")* (1992), both of which co-starred then-wife [Nicole Kidman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Kidman "Nicole Kidman") as his love interest, followed by the [legal thriller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_thriller "Legal thriller") *[The Firm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Firm_(1993_film) "The Firm (1993 film)")*, a critical and commercial success. In 1994, Cruise starred with [Brad Pitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Pitt "Brad Pitt"), [Antonio Banderas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Banderas "Antonio Banderas"), and [Christian Slater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Slater "Christian Slater") in [Neil Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Jordan "Neil Jordan")'s *[Interview with the Vampire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interview_with_the_Vampire_(film) "Interview with the Vampire (film)")*, a gothic drama/horror film based on [Anne Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Rice "Anne Rice")'s [best-selling novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interview_with_the_Vampire "Interview with the Vampire"). The film was well-received, although Rice initially criticized the casting of Cruise, as [Julian Sands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Sands "Julian Sands") was her first choice. Upon seeing the film, she paid $7,740 (equivalent to $16,813 in 2025) for a two-page ad in *[Daily Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* praising his performance and apologizing for her doubts about him.

In 1996, Cruise starred as superspy [Ethan Hunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethan_Hunt "Ethan Hunt") in the reboot of *[Mission: Impossible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission:_Impossible_(film) "Mission: Impossible (film)")*, which he also produced. The film was directed by [Brian De Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_De_Palma "Brian De Palma") and was a box-office success. Film critic Stephen Holden of *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* praised Cruise's performance, writing, "Tom Cruise has found the perfect superhero character on which to graft his breathlessly gung-ho screen personality." The same year, Cruise had the title role in [Cameron Crowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Crowe "Cameron Crowe")'s [sports drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_drama "Sports drama") *[Jerry Maguire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Maguire "Jerry Maguire")*, playing a [sports agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_agent "Sports agent") in search of love. The film was a massive financial success, grossing more than $273 million worldwide against its $50 million budget.

In 1999, Cruise co-starred with Kidman in [Stanley Kubrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Kubrick "Stanley Kubrick")'s [erotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erotic "Erotic") and [psychological drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological_drama "Psychological drama") film *[Eyes Wide Shut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyes_Wide_Shut "Eyes Wide Shut")*. Peter Bradshaw of *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian_(website) "The Guardian (website)")* praised both of their performances, writing, "Cruise in particular lays himself open in that fiercely committed way that he tries everything as an actor". Also in 1999, Cruise took a rare [supporting role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supporting_actor "Supporting actor"), as motivational speaker Frank T. J. Mackey in [Paul Thomas Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Thomas_Anderson "Paul Thomas Anderson")'s *[Magnolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnolia_(film) "Magnolia (film)")*. *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone_(magazine) "Rolling Stone (magazine)")* film critic Peter Travers heaped praise on Cruise, writing, "Cruise is a revelation, fully deserving of the shower of superlatives coming his way ... Cruise seethes with the chaotic energy of a wounded animal—he's devastating." For his performance he received another [Golden Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture "Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actor – Motion Picture") and nomination for an [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor "Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor").

### 2000s: Established career

In 2000, Cruise returned as Ethan Hunt in the second installment of the *Mission: Impossible* films, *[Mission: Impossible 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission:_Impossible_2 "Mission: Impossible 2")*. The film was helmed by Hong Kong director [John Woo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Woo "John Woo") and branded with his [gun fu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_fu "Gun fu") style; it continued the series' success at the box office, taking in $547 million worldwide. Unlike its predecessor, it was the highest-grossing film of the year but had a mixed critical reception. Cruise received an [MTV Movie Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Movie_Award "MTV Movie Award") for Best Male Performance for the film. His next five films were major critical and commercial successes. The next year, Cruise starred in the romantic thriller *[Vanilla Sky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanilla_Sky "Vanilla Sky")* with [Cameron Diaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Diaz "Cameron Diaz") and [Penélope Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pen%C3%A9lope_Cruz "Penélope Cruz"). In 2002, he starred in the [dystopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dystopia "Dystopia") science fiction action film *[Minority Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_Report "Minority Report")*, directed by [Steven Spielberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Spielberg "Steven Spielberg") and based on the short story by [Philip K. Dick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_K._Dick "Philip K. Dick"). It has been included on lists of the greatest science fiction films of all time.

In 2003, he starred in [Edward Zwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Zwick "Edward Zwick")'s period action drama *[The Last Samurai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Samurai "The Last Samurai")*, for which he received a [Golden Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globes "Golden Globes") nomination for [Best Actor in a Motion Picture – Drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Drama "Golden Globe Award for Best Actor in a Motion Picture – Drama"). In 2004, Cruise received critical acclaim for his performance as Vincent in *[Collateral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collateral_(film) "Collateral (film)")*. The critical consensus reads, "Driven by director [Michael Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Mann "Michael Mann")'s trademark visuals and a lean, villainous performance from Tom Cruise, *Collateral* is a stylish and compelling noir thriller." In 2005, Cruise worked with Spielberg again in *[War of the Worlds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Worlds_(2005_film) "War of the Worlds (2005 film)")*, a loose adaptation of [H. G. Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._G._Wells "H. G. Wells")'s [novel of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_War_of_the_Worlds "The War of the Worlds"), which was the year's fourth highest-grossing film, at $591.4 million worldwide. Also in 2005, he was a nominee for the People's Choice Award for Favorite Male Movie Star and won the [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV "MTV") Generation Award. Cruise was nominated for seven [Saturn Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn_Awards "Saturn Awards") between 2002 and 2009, winning once. Nine of the ten films he starred in during the decade made more than $100 million at the box office.

In 2006, he returned to his role as Ethan Hunt in *[Mission: Impossible III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission:_Impossible_III "Mission: Impossible III")*. The film was more positively received by critics than the previous films in the series and grossed nearly $400 million at the box office. In 2007, Cruise took a supporting role for the second time in *[Lions for Lambs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lions_for_Lambs "Lions for Lambs")*, a commercial disappointment. This was followed by an appearance as Les Grossman in the 2008 comedy *[Tropic Thunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropic_Thunder "Tropic Thunder")* with [Ben Stiller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Stiller "Ben Stiller"), [Jack Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Black "Jack Black"), and [Robert Downey Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Downey_Jr. "Robert Downey Jr.") The performance earned Cruise a [Golden Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globes "Golden Globes") nomination. Cruise played the central role in the historical thriller *[Valkyrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valkyrie_(film) "Valkyrie (film)")*, released on December 25, 2008, to box-office success.

### 2010s: Action star

In March 2010, Cruise completed filming the action-comedy *[Knight and Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_and_Day "Knight and Day")*, in which he re-teamed with Cameron Diaz; the film was released on June 23, 2010. On February 9, 2010, Cruise confirmed that he would star in *[Mission: Impossible – Ghost Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission:_Impossible_%E2%80%93_Ghost_Protocol "Mission: Impossible – Ghost Protocol")*, the fourth installment in the series. It was released in December 2011 to high critical acclaim and box-office success. Unadjusted for ticket price inflation, it was Cruise's biggest commercial success to that date.

On May 6, 2011, Cruise received a humanitarian award from the [Simon Wiesenthal Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Wiesenthal_Center "Simon Wiesenthal Center") and its [Museum of Tolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Tolerance "Museum of Tolerance") for his work as a dedicated philanthropist. In mid-2011, Cruise started shooting *[Rock of Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_of_Ages_(2012_film) "Rock of Ages (2012 film)")*, in which he plays the fictional character [Stacee Jaxx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stacee_Jaxx "Stacee Jaxx"). The film was released in June 2012 and was a rare box-office misstep for Cruise, but he received positive reviews, with *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")*'s film critic Justin Chang writing, "Channeling the likes of [Axl Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axl_Rose "Axl Rose") and [Keith Richards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Richards "Keith Richards") with his tattoos, heavy furs and even heavier eyeshadow, Cruise clearly relishes the opportunity to play against type even as he sends up his world's-biggest-movie-star identity, displaying a cock-of-the-rock strut that viewers haven't seen since his turn in *Magnolia*."

Cruise starred as [Jack Reacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Reacher "Jack Reacher") in the [film adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Reacher_(film) "Jack Reacher (film)") of British author [Lee Child](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Child "Lee Child")'s 2005 novel *[One Shot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Shot_(novel) "One Shot (novel)")*. The film was released on December 21, 2012. It met with positive reviews from critics and was a box-office success, grossing $217 million worldwide. In 2013, he starred in the science fiction film *[Oblivion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblivion_(2013_film) "Oblivion (2013 film)")* based on director [Joseph Kosinski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Kosinski "Joseph Kosinski")'s graphic novel of the same name. The film met with mixed reviews and grossed $286 million worldwide. It also starred [Morgan Freeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Freeman "Morgan Freeman") and [Olga Kurylenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_Kurylenko "Olga Kurylenko"). In 2014, Cruise starred in the science fiction-action film *[Edge of Tomorrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_of_Tomorrow "Edge of Tomorrow")*, which received positive reviews and grossed more than $370 million.

In 2015, Cruise returned as Ethan Hunt in the fifth installment of the *Mission: Impossible* series, *[Mission: Impossible – Rogue Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission:_Impossible_%E2%80%93_Rogue_Nation "Mission: Impossible – Rogue Nation")*, which he also produced. Returning cast members included [Simon Pegg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Pegg "Simon Pegg") as Benji and [Jeremy Renner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Renner "Jeremy Renner") as William Brandt. [Christopher McQuarrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_McQuarrie "Christopher McQuarrie") directed. The film earned high critical acclaim and was a commercial success. Cruise starred in the 2017 reboot of [Boris Karloff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Karloff "Boris Karloff")'s 1932 horror movie *[The Mummy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mummy_(1932_film) "The Mummy (1932 film)")*. The new film, also titled *[The Mummy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mummy_(2017_film) "The Mummy (2017 film)")*, received poor reviews and disappointed at the box office, though still grossing more than $400 million. In 2018, Cruise again reprised Ethan Hunt, in the sixth film in his franchise, *[Mission: Impossible – Fallout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission:_Impossible_%E2%80%93_Fallout "Mission: Impossible – Fallout")*. The film was more positively received by critics than the previous films in the series and grossed more than $791 million at the box office. Unadjusted for ticket price inflation, it was Cruise's biggest commercial success to date.

In May 2020, it was reported that Cruise will star in and produce a movie shot in [outer space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_space "Outer space"). ## Production

Cruise partnered with his former talent agent [Paula Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paula_Wagner "Paula Wagner") to form [Cruise/Wagner Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise/Wagner_Productions "Cruise/Wagner Productions") in 1993, and the company has since co-produced several of Cruise's films, the first being *[Mission: Impossible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission:_Impossible_(film) "Mission: Impossible (film)")* in 1996 which was also Cruise's first project as a producer. In addition, Cruise has produced films in which he does not appear, namely *[The Others](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Others_(2001_film) "The Others (2001 film)")*, *[Shattered Glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shattered_Glass_(film) "Shattered Glass (film)")*, *[Elizabethtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabethtown_(film) "Elizabethtown (film)")*, *[Narc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narc_(film) "Narc (film)")*, *[Ask the Dust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ask_the_Dust_(film) "Ask the Dust (film)")*, and *[Without Limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Without_Limits "Without Limits")*.

Cruise is noted as having negotiated some of the most lucrative film deals in Hollywood and was described in 2005 by Hollywood economist [Edward Jay Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Jay_Epstein "Edward Jay Epstein") as "one of the most powerful—and richest—forces in Hollywood." Epstein argues that Cruise is one of the few producers (the others being [George Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Lucas "George Lucas"), [Steven Spielberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Spielberg "Steven Spielberg") and [Jerry Bruckheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Bruckheimer "Jerry Bruckheimer")) who are regarded as able to guarantee the success of a billion-dollar film franchise. Epstein also contends that the public obsession with Cruise's tabloid controversies obscures full appreciation of Cruise's exceptional commercial prowess.

### Break with Paramount

On August 22, 2006, [Paramount Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures "Paramount Pictures") announced it was ending its 14-year relationship with Cruise. In *[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")*, chairman of [Viacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_(2005%E2%80%93present) "Viacom (2005–present)") (Paramount's parent company) [Sumner Redstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumner_Redstone "Sumner Redstone") cited the economic damage to Cruise's value as an actor and producer from his controversial public behavior and views. Cruise/Wagner Productions responded that Paramount's announcement was a face-saving move after the production company had successfully sought alternative financing from [private equity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_equity "Private equity") firms. Industry analysts such as Edward Jay Epstein commented that the real reason for the split was most likely Paramount's discontent over Cruise/Wagner's exceptionally large share of DVD sales from the *Mission: Impossible* franchise.

### Management of United Artists

In November 2006, Cruise and Paula Wagner announced that they had taken over the film studio [United Artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Artists "United Artists") (UA). Cruise acted as a producer and starred in films for UA, while Wagner served as UA's chief executive. Production began in 2007 of *[Valkyrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valkyrie_(film) "Valkyrie (film)")*, a thriller based on the July 20, 1944, assassination attempt against [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler"). The film was acquired in March 2007 by UA. On March 21, 2007, Cruise signed to play [Claus von Stauffenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claus_von_Stauffenberg "Claus von Stauffenberg"), the [protagonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protagonist "Protagonist"). This project marked the second production to be greenlighted since Cruise and Wagner took control of UA. The first was its inaugural film, *[Lions for Lambs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lions_for_Lambs "Lions for Lambs")*, directed by [Robert Redford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Redford "Robert Redford") and starring Redford, [Meryl Streep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meryl_Streep "Meryl Streep") and Cruise. In August 2008, Wagner stepped down from her position at UA; she retained her stake in UA, which combined with Cruise's share amounted to 30% of the studio.

### Return to Paramount

Cruise began working with Paramount again as a producer and star with *[Mission: Impossible – Ghost Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission:_Impossible_%E2%80%93_Ghost_Protocol "Mission: Impossible – Ghost Protocol")*, without Wagner, which was a critical and commercial success. He and Wagner collaborated for the last time on the modestly successful *[Jack Reacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Reacher_(film) "Jack Reacher (film)")* series, also for Paramount.

### Move to Warner Bros. Discovery

In January 2024, it was announced that TC Productions was forming a new strategic partnership with [Warner Bros. Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Discovery "Warner Bros. Discovery") to develop and produce original and franchise films.

## Personal life

### Marriages and relationships

Cruise splits his time between homes in [Beverly Hills, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beverly_Hills,_California "Beverly Hills, California"); [Clearwater, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clearwater,_Florida "Clearwater, Florida"); and the south of England, where he has lived in [Central London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_London "Central London"), [Dulwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulwich "Dulwich"), [East Grinstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Grinstead "East Grinstead"), and [Biggin Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biggin_Hill "Biggin Hill"). In the early to mid-1980s, Cruise had relationships with [Melissa Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melissa_Gilbert "Melissa Gilbert"), [Rebecca De Mornay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_De_Mornay "Rebecca De Mornay"), [Patti Scialfa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patti_Scialfa "Patti Scialfa"), and [Cher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cher "Cher").

Cruise married actress [Mimi Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimi_Rogers "Mimi Rogers") on May 9, 1987. They divorced on February 4, 1990. Rogers had grown up in [Scientology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientology "Scientology") and was one of its "[auditors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auditing_(Scientology) "Auditing (Scientology)")"; they met when Cruise became one of her clients. In a 1993 *[Playboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playboy "Playboy")* interview, Rogers discussed her split from Cruise and said he had been considering becoming a [monk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monk "Monk"), which affected their intimacy. Rogers later retracted the comments and said she had been misinterpreted. According to a friend of Rogers, "Tom, his agents and certain studios were furious about what Mimi said in *Playboy*. She was told in no uncertain terms that her career would be over unless she put things right." Rogers received a $4 million divorce settlement.

Cruise met his second wife, actress [Nicole Kidman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Kidman "Nicole Kidman"), on the set of their film *[Days of Thunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Days_of_Thunder "Days of Thunder")* (1990). The couple married on December 24, 1990. They adopted two children: Isabella Jane (born 1992) and Connor Antony (born 1995). On February 5, 2001, the couple's spokesperson announced their separation. Cruise filed for divorce two days later, and their marriage was dissolved later that year, with Cruise citing irreconcilable differences. Kidman said their marriage failed because at the time, she "was a child" who "needed to grow up". According to former church spokesperson and board member Mike Rinder, the Church of Scientology used various means to push the couple to break up, including pressuring Cruise into more auditing and tapping Kidman's phone. In a 2007 interview with *[Marie Claire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Claire "Marie Claire")*, Kidman noted the incorrect reporting of a miscarriage early in her marriage: "It was wrongly reported as miscarriage by everyone who picked up the story. So it's huge news, and it didn't happen. I had a miscarriage at the end of my marriage, but I had an [ectopic pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ectopic_pregnancy "Ectopic pregnancy") at the beginning of my marriage."

Cruise was next romantically linked with [Penélope Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pen%C3%A9lope_Cruz "Penélope Cruz"), his co-star in *[Vanilla Sky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanilla_Sky "Vanilla Sky")* (2001). Their three-year relationship ended in 2004. The October 2012 issue of *[Vanity Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanity_Fair_(magazine) "Vanity Fair (magazine)")* reported that several sources said that after he broke up with Cruz, Scientologist leaders launched a secret project to find Cruise a new girlfriend. According to those sources, a series of "auditions" of Scientologist actresses resulted in a short-lived relationship with Iranian-British actress [Nazanin Boniadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazanin_Boniadi "Nazanin Boniadi"), who subsequently left Scientology. Scientology and Cruise's lawyers issued strongly worded denials and threatened to sue, accusing *Vanity Fair* of "shoddy journalism" and "religious bigotry". Journalist [Roger Friedman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Friedman "Roger Friedman") later reported that he received an email from director and ex-Scientologist [Paul Haggis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Haggis "Paul Haggis") confirming the story.

In April 2005, Cruise began dating actress [Katie Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Holmes "Katie Holmes"). On April 27 that year, Cruise and Holmes—the [supercouple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercouple#Celebrity "Supercouple") was dubbed *TomKat* by the media—made their first public appearance together in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome "Rome"). A month later, Cruise publicly declared his love for Holmes on *[The Oprah Winfrey Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Oprah_Winfrey_Show "The Oprah Winfrey Show")*; he jumped on Winfrey's yellow couch and stood there to make the announcement. On October 6, 2005, Cruise and Holmes announced they were expecting a child. In April 2006, their daughter, Suri Noelle, was born.

Their publicists said the couple had "officialized" their marriage in Los Angeles the day before the Italian ceremony. It has been widely speculated that the Church of Scientology arranged the marriage. [David Miscavige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Miscavige "David Miscavige"), the head of Scientology, served as Cruise's best man. On June 29, 2012, Holmes filed for divorce from Cruise. On July 9, the couple signed a divorce settlement. New York law requires that all divorce documents remain sealed, so the settlement's terms are not publicly available. Cruise said that Holmes divorced him in part to protect the couple's daughter from Scientology and that Suri is no longer a practicing member of the organization. In 2026, it was reported that Suri had legally changed her surname from Cruise to Noelle, having used the latter for her 2024 high school graduation and voter registration.

### Litigation

In 1998, Cruise sued the *[Daily Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Express "Daily Express")*, a British tabloid that alleged that his marriage to Kidman was a [sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sham_marriage "Sham marriage") designed to cover up his homosexuality. In May 2001, Cruise sued [gay porn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_pornography "Gay pornography") actor Chad Slater, who had told the celebrity magazine *[Actustar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actustar "Actustar")* that he had been involved in an affair with Cruise. Cruise strongly denied that, and Slater was ordered to pay $10 million to Cruise in damages after declaring he could not afford to defend himself in the suit and would therefore default. Cruise requested a default judgment, and in January 2003, a Los Angeles judge decided against Slater after he admitted his claims were false.

Cruise sued *Bold Magazine* publisher Michael Davis for $100 million after Davis said that he had video proving Cruise was gay. The suit was dropped in exchange for a public statement by Davis that the video was not of Cruise and that Cruise was heterosexual. In 2006, Cruise sued [cybersquatter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cybersquatting "Cybersquatting") Jeff Burgar to obtain control of the domain name TomCruise.com. When owned by Burgar, the domain redirected to information about Cruise on Celebrity1000.com. The decision to turn TomCruise.com over to Cruise was handed down by the [World Intellectual Property Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Intellectual_Property_Organization "World Intellectual Property Organization") (WIPO) on July 5, 2006.

In 2009, magazine editor Michael Davis Sapir filed a suit charging that his phone had been wiretapped at Cruise's behest. That suit was dismissed by a Central Civil West court judge in Los Angeles on the grounds that the [statute of limitations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_limitations "Statute of limitations") had expired on Sapir's claim. In October 2012, Cruise sued *[In Touch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Touch_Weekly "In Touch Weekly")* and *[Life & Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_%26_Style_(magazine) "Life & Style (magazine)")* magazines for [defamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defamation "Defamation") after they claimed Cruise had "abandoned" his six-year-old daughter. During deposition, Cruise said he hadn't seen his daughter for 110 days. The suit was settled the next year.

### Scientology advocacy

Cruise was converted to [Scientology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientology "Scientology") by his first wife, [Mimi Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimi_Rogers "Mimi Rogers"), in 1986, and became an outspoken advocate for it in the 2000s. His involvement in the organization was leaked by the tabloid *[Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_(magazine) "Star (magazine)")* in 1990, and he publicly announced he followed Scientology in a 1992 interview with [Barbara Walters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Walters "Barbara Walters"). Cruise has said that Scientology, through its teaching method [Study Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Study_Technology "Study Technology"), helped him overcome his [dyslexia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyslexia "Dyslexia"). He has been a close friend of Scientology leader [David Miscavige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Miscavige "David Miscavige") since the 1980s.

Several years after Cruise started studying Scientology, the organization's leaders promised to share some Scientology secrets with him, including information about the extraterrestrial ruler [Xenu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenu "Xenu"). According to Janet Reitman's book *[Inside Scientology: The Story of America's Most Secretive Religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inside_Scientology:_The_Story_of_America%27s_Most_Secretive_Religion "Inside Scientology: The Story of America's Most Secretive Religion")* (2011), Cruise "freaked out" and took a step back from the Church to work on the 1999 film *Eyes Wide Shut.* In 1999, Miscavige sent [Marty Rathbun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Rathbun "Mark Rathbun") to convince Cruise to return to the Church and continue his studies. Cruise sparked controversy in the 2000s with his [efforts to promote Scientology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Chanology#Tom_Cruise_video "Project Chanology").

In the aftermath of the [September 11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_attacks "September 11 attacks"), Cruise co-founded and raised donations for [Downtown Medical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rescue_Workers_Detoxification_Project "New York Rescue Workers Detoxification Project") to offer New York City rescue workers "[detoxification therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purification_Rundown "Purification Rundown")", drawing criticism from medical professionals and firefighters. In late 2004, Miscavige created the Scientology [Freedom Medal of Valor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Association_of_Scientologists#Freedom_Medal_of_Valor "International Association of Scientologists") and awarded it to Cruise for this work. Former Scientologist [Paul Haggis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Haggis "Paul Haggis") has said that Cruise attempted to convert several celebrities to Scientology, including [James Packer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Packer "James Packer"), [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Beckham "Victoria Beckham") and [David Beckham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Beckham "David Beckham"), [Jada Pinkett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jada_Pinkett_Smith "Jada Pinkett Smith"), [Will Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Smith "Will Smith"), and [Steven Spielberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Spielberg "Steven Spielberg"). Since 2008, Cruise has restricted interviewers from asking him about Scientology.

In the 2020s, Cruise dated [Ana de Armas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ana_de_Armas "Ana de Armas"). They broke up in 2025, reportedly due to religious differences, primarily his desire to recruit her into Scientology. He reportedly severed contact with his daughter, a practice known as [disconnection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disconnection_(Scientology) "Disconnection (Scientology)"), when her mother, Katie Holmes, refused to raise her in the Church. He did not attend her high school graduation, and Suri dropped her father's surname at age 18.

#### Political lobbying

In addition to promoting various programs to introduce people to Scientology, Cruise campaigned for Scientology to be recognized as a religion in Europe. In 2005, the [Council of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Paris "Council of Paris") revealed that Cruise had lobbied French Interior Minister [Nicolas Sarkozy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Sarkozy "Nicolas Sarkozy") and Senate President [Jean-Claude Gaudin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Claude_Gaudin "Jean-Claude Gaudin"); they described Cruise as a militant spokesman for Scientology and barred any further dealings with him.

### Politics

Cruise donated to [Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)") candidate [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton "Hillary Clinton") during her [2000 Senate campaign in New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_United_States_Senate_election_in_New_York "2000 United States Senate election in New York"). In October 2000, he attended and spoke at a birthday [fundraising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_finance "Campaign finance") event for Clinton, alongside other major entertainment figures.

While promoting *[Valkyrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valkyrie_(film) "Valkyrie (film)")* in January 2009, Cruise said the recent [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election "2008 United States presidential election") of [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") as president brought "hope, absolute hope" to the United States and urged Americans to support the new administration.

In 2025, Cruise declined an offer from President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") to be honored by the [Kennedy Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Center_Honors "Kennedy Center Honors"). Later that year, it was reported that Cruise avoided seeking assistance from Trump for an outer-space film project involving [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA"), which led to its cancellation.

### Controversies

#### Criticism of psychiatry

In January 2004, Cruise said, "I think [psychiatry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychiatry "Psychiatry") should be outlawed." In 2005, he criticized actress [Brooke Shields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooke_Shields "Brooke Shields") for using the [antidepressant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antidepressant "Antidepressant") [Paxil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paxil "Paxil") to treat [postpartum depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postpartum_depression "Postpartum depression") following the birth of her first daughter. Cruise denied the existence of chemical imbalances in the brain and described psychiatry as a form of [pseudoscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoscience "Pseudoscience"). In response, Shields argued that Cruise "should stick to saving the world from aliens and let women who are experiencing postpartum depression decide what treatment options are best for them," and further called Cruise's comments "a disservice to mothers everywhere". In a June 2005 episode of NBC's *[Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Today_(NBC_program) "Today (NBC program)")*, Cruise reiterated his views, leading to a heated exchange with interviewer [Matt Lauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Lauer "Matt Lauer"). In August 2006, Cruise apologized in person to Shields for his comments.

Medical authorities viewed Cruise's comments as contributing to the social stigma surrounding mental illness. According to *[The Lancet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lancet "The Lancet")*, Cruise "may be right that psychotropic drugs are overused, sometimes misused; and that lifestyle changes (and exercise for depression) can be helpful. But he is wrong, as a celebrity, to add to the burden of those with a mental illness, who often fear seeking or continuing treatment because of the stigma still attached to their condition."

Scientology is well known for its [opposition to mainstream psychiatry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-psychiatry "Anti-psychiatry") and to [psychoactive drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychoactive_drug "Psychoactive drug") that are routinely prescribed for treatment. It was reported that Cruise's anti-psychiatry actions caused a rift with director [Steven Spielberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Spielberg "Steven Spielberg"). Spielberg had reportedly mentioned in Cruise's presence the name of a doctor friend who prescribed psychiatric medication. Shortly thereafter, the doctor's office was [picketed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picketing "Picketing") by Scientologists, reportedly angering Spielberg.

#### YouTube video removal

On January 15, 2008, a video produced by Church of Scientology featuring an interview with Cruise was posted on YouTube by the [Anonymous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anonymous_(hacker_group) "Anonymous (hacker group)")-linked group [Project Chanology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Chanology "Project Chanology"), showing Cruise discussing what being a Scientologist means to him. Church of Scientology said the video had been "pirated and edited", and was taken from a three-hour video produced for members of Scientology. YouTube removed the Cruise video from their site under threat of litigation. It was subsequently reinstated on the site, and as of June 2020, the video has achieved more than 15 million views.

#### Purported influence

In March 2004, Cruise's publicist of 14 years, [Pat Kingsley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Kingsley "Pat Kingsley"), resigned. Cruise's next publicist was Lee Anne DeVette, his sister, who was herself a Scientologist. She served in that role until November 2005. DeVette was replaced with Paul Bloch from the publicity firm [Rogers and Cowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogers_%26_Cowan "Rogers & Cowan"). Such restructuring was seen as a move to curtail publicity of his views on Scientology, as well as the controversy surrounding his relationship with Katie Holmes. [Lawrence Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Wright "Lawrence Wright")'s 2013 book *[Going Clear: Scientology and the Prison of Belief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Going_Clear_(book) "Going Clear (book)")* and [Alex Gibney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Gibney "Alex Gibney")'s 2015 [documentary adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Going_Clear_(film) "Going Clear (film)") of the book cast a spotlight on Cruise's role in Scientology. The book and the film both state that the Scientology organization groomed romantic partners for Cruise and that Cruise used [Sea Org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Org "Sea Org") and [Rehabilitation Project Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehabilitation_Project_Force "Rehabilitation Project Force") workers as a source of free labor. In the film, Cruise's former auditor [Mark Rathbun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Rathbun "Mark Rathbun") says that Cruise's then-wife [Nicole Kidman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Kidman "Nicole Kidman") was wiretapped on Cruise's suggestion, which Cruise's lawyer denies. Cruise's ex-girlfriend [Nazanin Boniadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazanin_Boniadi "Nazanin Boniadi") compared the Scientology organization's auditioning of women to date Cruise and experiences with him to "[white slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_trafficking "Sex trafficking")".
