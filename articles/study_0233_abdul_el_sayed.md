# Abdul El-Sayed

**Abdulrahman Mohamed El-Sayed** (born October 31, 1984) is an American [epidemiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemiology "Epidemiology") and politician who is the [Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)") nominee in the [2026 United States Senate election in Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Michigan "2026 United States Senate election in Michigan").

Born and raised in [southeast Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Michigan "Southeast Michigan"), El-Sayed graduated from the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan "University of Michigan") and was a [Rhodes Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes_Scholarship "Rhodes Scholarship") at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford"), where he earned a doctorate in [public health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health "Public health"). He then earned his medical degree from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"). He then became an assistant professor of epidemiology at the [Columbia University Mailman School of Public Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University_Mailman_School_of_Public_Health "Columbia University Mailman School of Public Health"). He was the [executive director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_director "Executive director") of the [Detroit Health Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Health_Department "Detroit Health Department") from 2015 to 2017. From 2023 to 2025, he served as director of the [Wayne County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_County,_Michigan "Wayne County, Michigan") Department of Health, Human & Veterans Services.

A [progressive Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States)#Progressives "Democratic Party (United States)"), El-Sayed supports [Medicare for All](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicare_for_All "Medicare for All"), ending [US military funding to Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_support_for_Israel_in_the_Gaza_war#Military_support "United States support for Israel in the Gaza war"), and [abolishing ICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolish_ICE "Abolish ICE"). In the 2026 Michigan Democratic Senate primary, El-Sayed defeated [U.S. representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives "United States House of Representatives") [Haley Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haley_Stevens "Haley Stevens"). He faces Republican nominee [Mike Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Rogers_(Michigan_politician) "Mike Rogers (Michigan politician)") in the 2026 United States Senate election in Michigan. If elected, El-Sayed would be the first [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims "Muslims") member of the [U.S. Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Senate "U.S. Senate").

## Early life, family, and education

Abdulrahman Mohamed El-Sayed was born on October 31, 1984, in [Rochester Hills, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester_Hills,_Michigan "Rochester Hills, Michigan"), a suburb of [Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit "Detroit"), to [Egyptian immigrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Egyptian_Americans "List of Egyptian Americans") parents. His father, Mohamed El-Sayed, was raised in [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria "Alexandria"), Egypt, and immigrated to the United States to pursue graduate studies at [Wayne State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_State_University "Wayne State University"). His mother, Fatten El-Komy, is a nurse practitioner in [Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri "Missouri"). El-Sayed was raised in the Detroit area by his father and stepmother, Jacqueline Johnson, a native of [Gratiot County, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gratiot_County,_Michigan "Gratiot County, Michigan"). Both his father and stepmother are [mechanical engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering "Mechanical engineering").

In 2003, El-Sayed graduated from [Bloomfield Hills Andover High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andover_High_School_(Michigan) "Andover High School (Michigan)"), where he was a three-sport athlete and [captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_(sports) "Captain (sports)") of the [wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrestling "Wrestling"), [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_football "American football"), and [lacrosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacrosse "Lacrosse") teams. He then attended the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan "University of Michigan"), majoring in biology and political science and playing on the men's club lacrosse team. He graduated [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa "Phi Beta Kappa") with highest distinction in 2007 with a bachelor's degree and gave that year's student [commencement speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commencement_speech "Commencement speech"). According to El-Sayed, after the ceremony, former U.S. president [Bill Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Clinton "Bill Clinton") (who gave the keynote speech) encouraged him to consider public service.

El-Sayed received a full-tuition scholarship to the [University of Michigan Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan_Medical_School "University of Michigan Medical School"), where he completed the first two years of a combined M.D./Ph.D. program and initially considered becoming a [neurosurgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurosurgery "Neurosurgery"). There he led a student medical mission to [Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru "Peru") and founded a student organization that raised money and coordinated community service for a local free clinic. He later wrote in his [Rhodes Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes_Scholarship "Rhodes Scholarship") application, "My religion teaches that if one saves a life, she has saved all of humanity", recounting a childhood conversation with his father about the importance of saving lives while teaching others to do the same.

As a second-year medical student, El-Sayed received a Rhodes Scholarship to attend [Oriel College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriel_College,_Oxford "Oriel College, Oxford"), where he completed a [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in public health in 2011. At Oxford, he earned a [full blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_blue "Oxford blue") as captain of the men's [lacrosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacrosse "Lacrosse") team. In 2014, he completed a [Doctor of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Medicine "Doctor of Medicine") degree at the [Columbia University College of Physicians and Surgeons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University_College_of_Physicians_and_Surgeons "Columbia University College of Physicians and Surgeons"), supported by a [Paul & Daisy Soros Fellowships for New Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_%26_Daisy_Soros_Fellowships_for_New_Americans "Paul & Daisy Soros Fellowships for New Americans") and the [Medical Scientist Training Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Scientist_Training_Program "Medical Scientist Training Program"). He later worked as an assistant professor of epidemiology at Columbia but did not pursue [residency training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residency_(medicine) "Residency (medicine)"), and therefore did not become licensed to practice medicine.

## Career in public health

### Public health professor

In 2014, El-Sayed joined the faculty at the [Columbia University Mailman School of Public Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University_Mailman_School_of_Public_Health "Columbia University Mailman School of Public Health") as an assistant professor in the epidemiology department. He served as director of Columbia's Systems Science Program and Global Research Analytics for Population Health. He created and taught the Mailman School's first course on systems science and population health. El-Sayed and [Sandro Galea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandro_Galea "Sandro Galea") co-edited a textbook on the topic *Systems Science and Population Health*, published in 2017 by [Oxford University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University_Press "Oxford University Press").

### Health director of Detroit

In August 2015, Mayor [Mike Duggan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Duggan "Mike Duggan") appointed El-Sayed Health Officer and Executive Director of the [Detroit Health Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Health_Department "Detroit Health Department"), making him, at 30 years old, the youngest health officer in a major U.S. city at the time. In that role, he was charged with rebuilding the Detroit Health Department after government public health activities were provided by a nonprofit when [the city went bankrupt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_bankruptcy "Detroit bankruptcy") in 2012.

In his first year as director, he led efforts to oppose increases in sulfur dioxide emissions by [Marathon Petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathon_Petroleum "Marathon Petroleum")'s Southwest Refinery, which resulted in reductions in overall emissions. He also led efforts to test Detroit schools for lead in the wake of the [Flint water crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flint_water_crisis "Flint water crisis") and to increase funding for the city's Animal Control department.

El-Sayed was appointed to the governor's statewide Childhood Lead Elimination Board. He also served on the State of Michigan's Public Health Advisory Commission and the advisory committee to the US Secretary of [Health and Human Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Health_and_Human_Services "United States Department of Health and Human Services") for the [Healthy People program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthy_People_program "Healthy People program").

In 2016, El-Sayed was named one of *[Crain's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crain_Communications "Crain Communications") Detroit's* "40 under 40" and the Michigan [League of Conservation Voters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Conservation_Voters "League of Conservation Voters")' "Public Official of the Year". In 2017, the University of Michigan awarded him a Bicentennial Alumni Award.

Since 2019, El-Sayed has hosted *America Dissected with Dr. Abdul El-Sayed*, a podcast about politics and public health produced by [Crooked Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crooked_Media "Crooked Media").

### Health director of Wayne County

In December 2022, El-Sayed was named director of Wayne County's Department of Health, Human, and Veterans Services, assuming the role in March 2023.

During his tenure, El-Sayed oversaw the installation of air quality monitors across Wayne County to identify pollution hotspots and helped eliminate medical debt for about 300,000 county residents. He helped the county restructure the Juvenile Youth Services and relocate the Criminal Justice Complex, and started a program for the county to provide eyeglasses to children in lower-income schools.

In April 2025, El-Sayed resigned to run for the U.S. Senate.

## 2018 gubernatorial campaign

On February 9, 2017, the *Detroit News* reported that El-Sayed would resign as health director to run for governor of Michigan in the 2018 Democratic Party primary. He officially announced his candidacy on February 25, 2017, saying he was inspired to run for governor after the [Flint water crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flint_water_crisis "Flint water crisis").

El-Sayed pledged to accept no campaign donations from corporations, and raised over $5 million from individual donations.

El-Sayed was endorsed by U.S. senator [Bernie Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Sanders "Bernie Sanders"), Democratic congressional nominee [Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria_Ocasio-Cortez "Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez"), and [2017 Women's March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Women%27s_March "2017 Women's March") organizer [Linda Sarsour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Sarsour "Linda Sarsour"). He was also endorsed by the organization [Justice Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_Democrats "Justice Democrats") and *[The Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nation "The Nation")* magazine. In the August 7 primary, he received 340,560 votes, or about 30% of the vote, losing to [Gretchen Whitmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gretchen_Whitmer "Gretchen Whitmer").

In September 2018, El-Sayed founded Southpaw Michigan, a [political action committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_action_committee "Political action committee"), to help elect progressive candidates and support ballot initiatives in Michigan. In 2020, he served on the [Joe Biden–Bernie Sanders Unity Task Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden_2020_presidential_campaign#Biden–Sanders_Unity_Task_Forces "Joe Biden 2020 presidential campaign") on [healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care_in_the_United_States "Health care in the United States").

## 2026 U.S. Senate campaign

In April 2025, after incumbent [Gary Peters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Peters "Gary Peters") announced he would not run for reelection, El-Sayed launched his campaign in the [2026 United States Senate election in Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Michigan "2026 United States Senate election in Michigan").

In April 2026, left-wing political commentator [Hasan Piker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasan_Piker "Hasan Piker") appeared with El-Sayed at two campus rallies and a primary-night party in Detroit. Centrist Democrats and El-Sayed's primary opponents criticized Piker's participation over his past comments about the [September 11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_attacks "September 11 attacks"), [Hamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamas "Hamas"), and [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel"). El-Sayed defended his appearances with Piker as an effort to engage with his audience and said he does not agree with everything Piker has said.

After Michigan state Senator [Mallory McMorrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallory_McMorrow "Mallory McMorrow") withdrew from the Democratic primary in July, news outlets called the race a choice between the party's moderate, establishment-backed wing, represented by [Haley Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haley_Stevens "Haley Stevens"), and its progressive wing, represented by El-Sayed. Outside spending became a significant feature of the contest, with about $62 million spent by outside groups in support of Stevens, of which [AIPAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIPAC "AIPAC") and its United Democracy Project affiliate spent $33 million.

On August 4, the day of the primary, as polling showed El-Sayed roughly two points ahead of Stevens, McMorrow endorsed El-Sayed. On August 5, the *[Associated Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_Press "Associated Press")* called the race for El-Sayed, who finished with 48.4% to Stevens's 47.6%. He will face Republican nominee [Mike Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Rogers_(Michigan_politician) "Mike Rogers (Michigan politician)") in the general election on November 3, 2026.

After El-Sayed won the primary, AIPAC initially planned to spend millions more in favor of Rogers in the general election, but Rogers asked it not to advertise openly, fearing that its brand is too toxic. In October, AIPAC started spending money on the race with a $95,000 purchase of mailers.

In August 2026, El-Sayed apologized for remarks he made after the [2026 Temple Israel attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Temple_Israel_attack "2026 Temple Israel attack") in [West Bloomfield Township, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bloomfield_Township,_Michigan "West Bloomfield Township, Michigan"). After that attack, El-Sayed released a video statement that included the phrase "Hurt people hurt people", a reference to the perpetrator's relatives having been killed in an Israeli airstrike. In his apology, El-Sayed said, "I should have just issued a statement, of course condemning it, and it should have been the full stop."

Also in August 2026, El-Sayed said he had no plans to campaign with Piker again. This came after Piker said during a Twitch stream that if pro-Israel Jewish leaders continue to conflate Judaism and Jewish identity with support for the state of Israel, that could fuel antisemitism and endanger American Jews due to declining public support for and anger toward Israel. In a statement El-Sayed said: "Antisemitism is a scourge, and we need to address it in all its forms. And I stand against any rhetoric that puts the community at risk. [...] While politicians and pundits try to make this race about a Twitch streamer, we're going to be talking about gas, groceries, good schools, and healthcare."

In September, [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris "Kamala Harris") endorsed and campaigned with El-Sayed. In October, [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") endorsed and campaigned with El-Sayed.

El-Sayed's win yielded a wave of [anti-Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Muslim "Anti-Muslim") rhetoric online. In September, without evidence, Mike Rogers said El-Sayed was funded by Muslim "terrorist groups", which he did not retract. [JD Vance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JD_Vance "JD Vance") falsely claimed that El-Sayed supports "[sharia law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharia_law "Sharia law")". While campaigning for Rogers, [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") has called El-Sayed "Mohammed", repeatedly called him a "[jihadist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihadist "Jihadist")", and a "terrorist sympathizer". In response, El-Sayed said Trump "thinks he can use my name and my faith to distract us from the suffering his policies are inflicting on us", and asked "Can you afford your gas? Can you afford your groceries?"

If elected, El-Sayed would be the first Muslim and first Egyptian-American elected to the US Senate.

## Political positions

El-Sayed is a [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism_in_the_United_States "Progressivism in the United States") [economic populist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-wing_populism "Left-wing populism"). He supports [Medicare for All](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicare_for_All "Medicare for All") and co-authored a book on the topic.

He has vowed not to accept [PAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_action_committee "Political action committee") donations, accepting donations from individuals only. He served as a national [campaign surrogate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_campaign_staff "Political campaign staff") for [Bernie Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Sanders "Bernie Sanders")'s [2020 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Sanders_2020_presidential_campaign "Bernie Sanders 2020 presidential campaign"), speaking on Sanders's behalf at events and in the media.

### Artificial intelligence

In June 2026, El-Sayed released a plan calling for more [regulation of artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulation_of_artificial_intelligence "Regulation of artificial intelligence"). In August 2026, he criticized [data centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_centers "Data centers"), saying, "I stand with local and state elected officials saying that we cannot approve any more of these until we have federal-level guardrails." He has called for guarantees that the operation of data centers will not result in rate increases passed on to customers.

### Economy

El-Sayed supports [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism "Capitalism"), saying, "When it comes to the question of [socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism "Socialism"), I'm not a socialist. I'm just a capitalist who understands how capitalism works." He has said that regulating billionaires to fund public infrastructure is "not that radical", while advocating for stronger enforcement of [anti-monopoly laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competition_law "Competition law") and the elimination of corporate tax breaks.

### Environment

El-Sayed has called for tougher enforcement of environmental laws and for merging Michigan's [Department of Natural Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Department_of_Natural_Resources "Michigan Department of Natural Resources") and the [Department of Environmental Quality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Department_of_Environmental_Quality "Michigan Department of Environmental Quality") into a single "Department of the Environment", and achieving "[100% renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100%25_renewable_energy "100% renewable energy")" in Michigan by 2030.

### Foreign policy

El-Sayed generally opposes [United States military aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_military_aid "United States military aid") to foreign countries, but has cited [US actions during Russia's invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_and_the_Russo-Ukrainian_war "United States and the Russo-Ukrainian war") and [in Serbia during the 1990s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia%E2%80%93United_States_relations#Deteriorating_relations_and_Yugoslav_Wars "Serbia–United States relations") as examples of beneficial US intervention.

In 2026, El-Sayed said the US should not fund foreign militaries at all, and in particular he identified Israel, Egypt, [Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan "Jordan"), and [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia "Saudi Arabia").

#### Israel

The *New York Times* called El-Sayed a "fierce critic of Israel". He has criticized Israel over the [war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_war "Gaza war") and [genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_genocide "Gaza genocide") in Gaza. He has also criticized [US military funding to Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_support_for_Israel_in_the_Gaza_war#Military_support "United States support for Israel in the Gaza war"), the [American Israel Public Affairs Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIPAC "AIPAC") (AIPAC), and [Israeli influence in US politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_lobby_in_the_United_States "Israel lobby in the United States").

In a January 2024 podcast, he called the war in the [Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Strip "Gaza Strip") a [public health catastrophe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_humanitarian_crisis_(2023%E2%80%93present) "Gaza humanitarian crisis (2023–present)") and called for a ceasefire. El-Sayed has called the Gaza genocide a morality test, asking: "If you can't call it what it is, which is a genocide, then how are you going to stand up to Donald Trump? How are you going to stand up to pharma CEOs?"

In April 2026, El-Sayed said in a [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN "CNN") interview: "Killing tens of thousands of people makes you pretty damn evil. It's not how evil is this one versus that one. Hamas: evil. Israeli government: evil. We can say both."

El-Sayed opposes Israel's right to exist as a [Jewish state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_state "Jewish state"). In April 2026, when asked by *[The Free Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Free_Press_(CBS_News) "The Free Press (CBS News)")* writer [Olivia Reingold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_Reingold "Olivia Reingold") whether Israel has a right to exist as a Jewish state, he replied, "What do you mean by Jewish state?" After Reingold failed to answer, he said, "If you can't define the question, I'm not going to answer your question."

El-Sayed believes Israel is an [ethno-state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethno-state "Ethno-state") and has said, "I don't believe in ethno-states, and I certainly don't believe that our country is responsible to sustain ethno-states." He has said his rejection of [ethno-nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethno-nationalism "Ethno-nationalism") has nothing to do with whether a country has a "right to exist", saying he "just [disputes] the idea that you should have an ethno-state" and objects to Egyptian and [Saudi Arabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudis "Saudis") ethno-nationalism in the same way he objects to [Zionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism "Zionism").

El-Sayed attributed the [2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war "2026 Iran war") to AIPAC's influence in US politics. He called Israel a "[rogue state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogue_state "Rogue state")" that is committing "human rights abuses, genocide, and [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_apartheid "Israeli apartheid")" and said the U.S. should "stop funding the Israeli military unilateral blank checks".

#### Russia

In March 2022, El-Sayed called the [Russo-Ukrainian war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Ukrainian_war_(2022%E2%80%93present) "Russo-Ukrainian war (2022–present)") "a war between democracy and autocracy, between self-government and fascism", condemning Russian president [Vladimir Putin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Putin "Vladimir Putin") as an "authoritarian dictator".

In April 2025, El-Sayed said that [U.S. military aid to Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_military_aid_to_Ukraine_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_war "List of military aid to Ukraine during the Russo-Ukrainian war") is different from aid to Israel, as the former is in accordance with several multinational organizations. In September 2025, he said that military aid to [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine "Ukraine") was about "enforcing the rules of the rules-based international order in accordance with our allies".

### Local authority

In 2018, El-Sayed supported repealing Michigan state laws that [block local governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_preemption "State preemption") from levying a [municipal sales tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sales_taxes_in_the_United_States "Sales taxes in the United States") or enacting [rent control laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rent_control_in_the_United_States "Rent control in the United States").

### Immigrations and Customs Enforcement (ICE)

El-Sayed supports [abolishing ICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolish_ICE "Abolish ICE"), arguing that it no longer functions as a legitimate law enforcement agency. While he has expressed support for securing the [Mexico–United States border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_border "Mexico–United States border") and for "certain kinds" of border law enforcement, he has called ICE "corrupted at its soul".

### Unionization

El-Sayed supports [unionization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unionization "Unionization"), having been a member of the [SEIU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEIU "SEIU"), [AFT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Federation_of_Teachers "American Federation of Teachers"), [UAW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAW "UAW"), and [National Writers Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Writers_Union "National Writers Union"). He has said union expansion is necessary to protect "worker dignity" in response to the growth of [AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence"). He has expressed support for the [Protecting the Right to Organize Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protecting_the_Right_to_Organize_Act "Protecting the Right to Organize Act") alongside unionization efforts among further professions, such as physicians.

## Personal life

El-Sayed was born and raised in [southeast Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Michigan "Southeast Michigan"), and lives in [Ann Arbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Arbor "Ann Arbor") with his wife, Sarah Jukaku, whom he married in 2006, and their two daughters. He is Muslim.
