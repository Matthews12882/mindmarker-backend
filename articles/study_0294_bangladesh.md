# Bangladesh

**Bangladesh**, officially the **People's Republic of Bangladesh**, is a country in [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia"). It is the [eighth-most populous country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population "List of countries and dependencies by population") in the world with a population of almost 175.4 million within an area of 148,461 square kilometres (57,321 sq mi). Bangladesh shares land borders with [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") to the north, west, and east, and [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar") to the southeast. It has a coastline along the [Bay of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Bengal "Bay of Bengal") to its south and is separated from [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan "Bhutan") and [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal "Nepal") by the [Siliguri Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siliguri_Corridor "Siliguri Corridor"), and from [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") by the [Indian states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_states "List of Indian states") of [West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bengal "West Bengal") and [Sikkim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikkim "Sikkim") to its north. [Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka "Dhaka"), the capital and [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_Bangladesh "List of cities and towns in Bangladesh"), is the nation's political, financial, and cultural centre. [Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong "Chittagong") is the second-largest city and [the busiest port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Chittagong "Port of Chittagong") of the country.

The territory of present-day Bangladesh was ruled by successive [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Hindu_empires_and_dynasties "List of Hindu empires and dynasties") and [Buddhist dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Buddhist_kingdoms_and_empires "List of Buddhist kingdoms and empires") in antiquity. Following the [Muslim conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghurid_conquest_of_Bengal "Ghurid conquest of Bengal") in 1204, the region came under [Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Sultanate "Bengal Sultanate") and [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire "Mughal Empire") rule. As the [largest subdivision of the Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Subah "Bengal Subah"), [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal "Bengal") became a major commercial centre, known for its [textile industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslin_trade_in_Bengal "Muslin trade in Bengal") and agricultural output. The [Battle of Plassey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Plassey "Battle of Plassey") in 1757 began [British colonial rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Presidency "Bengal Presidency"), which lasted nearly two centuries; colonial policies redirected wealth from Bengal to Britain, while the region itself suffered repeated deadly famines. After the [Partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_British_India "Partition of British India") in 1947, [East Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bengal "East Bengal") became the eastern wing of the newly formed [Dominion of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Pakistan "Dominion of Pakistan") and was later renamed [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan "East Pakistan").

During more than two decades of political and economic marginalisation under the government based in [West Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pakistan "West Pakistan"), East Pakistan saw growing civil unrest that culminated in the [1948–52 language movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Language_Movement "Bengali Language Movement"), [1969 mass uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_East_Pakistan_mass_uprising "1969 East Pakistan mass uprising") and the [1971 non-cooperation movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-cooperation_movement_(1971) "Non-cooperation movement (1971)"). Following the Pakistani military's [Operation Searchlight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Searchlight "Operation Searchlight"), the [Bangladesh Liberation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War "Bangladesh Liberation War") began. The [Mukti Bahini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukti_Bahini "Mukti Bahini"), aided by Indian forces, fought a war of independence, during which Pakistani forces carried out the [Bangladesh genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_genocide "Bangladesh genocide"); Bangladesh became a sovereign nation on 16 December 1971. After independence, [Sheikh Mujibur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Mujibur_Rahman "Sheikh Mujibur Rahman") led the country until his [assassination in 1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Sheikh_Mujibur_Rahman "Assassination of Sheikh Mujibur Rahman"); [Ziaur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziaur_Rahman "Ziaur Rahman"), who later came to power, was himself [assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Ziaur_Rahman "Assassination of Ziaur Rahman") in 1981. The 1980s were dominated by the dictatorship of [Hussain Muhammad Ershad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussain_Muhammad_Ershad "Hussain Muhammad Ershad"), who was overthrown in a [mass uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Bangladesh_mass_uprising "1990 Bangladesh mass uprising") in 1990. Since the restoration to parliamentary democracy in 1991, national politics has largely dominated between the [Awami League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awami_League "Awami League"), led by [Sheikh Hasina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Hasina "Sheikh Hasina"), and the [Bangladesh Nationalist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Nationalist_Party "Bangladesh Nationalist Party"), led by [Khaleda Zia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaleda_Zia "Khaleda Zia"). After more than 15 years of autocratic rule, Hasina was ousted in another [mass uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_Uprising "July Uprising") in 2024.

Bangladesh is a unitary [parliamentary republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_republic "Parliamentary republic") based on the [Westminster system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_system "Westminster system"). It has South Asia's second-largest economy and is one of the largest contributors to [United Nations peacekeeping operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_peacekeeping "United Nations peacekeeping"). Bangladesh consists of [eight divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisions_of_Bangladesh "Divisions of Bangladesh"), [64 districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Bangladesh "Districts of Bangladesh"), [497 upazilas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upazila "Upazila"), and [4,599 union councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_councils_of_Bangladesh "Union councils of Bangladesh"). It is home to the [largest mangrove forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundarbans "Sundarbans") in the world and hosts one of the world's largest [refugee populations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya_refugees_in_Bangladesh "Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh"). Bangladesh continues to face challenges in [corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_Bangladesh "Corruption in Bangladesh"), [human rights concerns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Bangladesh "Human rights in Bangladesh"), political instability, and the [effects of climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change "Effects of climate change"). Bangladesh is a member state of [SAARC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Association_for_Regional_Cooperation "South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation") and other regional and international organisations.

## Etymology

The etymology of *Bangladesh* ("Bengali country") can be traced to the early 20th century, when Bengali patriotic songs, such as *Aaji Bangladesher Hridoy* by [Rabindranath Tagore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabindranath_Tagore "Rabindranath Tagore") and *Namo Namo Namo Bangladesh Momo* by [Kazi Nazrul Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazi_Nazrul_Islam "Kazi Nazrul Islam"), used the term in 1905 and 1932 respectively. Starting in the 1950s, Bengali nationalists used the term in political rallies in [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan "East Pakistan").

The term *Bangla* can refer to both the [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal "Bengal") region and the [Bengali language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language "Bengali language"). The origins of the term *Bangla* are unclear, with theories pointing to a Bronze Age [proto-Dravidian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Dravidian_language "Proto-Dravidian language") tribe, and the Iron Age [Vanga Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanga_Kingdom "Vanga Kingdom"). The earliest known usage of the term is the [Nesari plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nesari "Nesari") in 805 AD. The term *Vangala Desa* is found in 11th-century South Indian records. The term gained official status during the [Sultanate of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Bengal "Sultanate of Bengal") in the 14th century. [Shamsuddin Ilyas Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamsuddin_Ilyas_Shah "Shamsuddin Ilyas Shah") proclaimed himself as the first "[Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah "Shah") of Bangala" in 1342. The word *Bangāl* became the most common name for the region during the Islamic period. Sixteenth century historian [Abu'l-Fazl ibn Mubarak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu%27l-Fazl_ibn_Mubarak "Abu'l-Fazl ibn Mubarak") mentions in his *[Ain-i-Akbari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ain-i-Akbari "Ain-i-Akbari")* that the addition of the suffix *"al"* came from the fact that the ancient rajahs of the land raised mounds of earth in lowlands at the foot of the hills which were called "al". This is also mentioned in [Ghulam Husain Salim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghulam_Husain_Salim "Ghulam Husain Salim")'s [Riyaz-us-Salatin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riyaz-us-Salatin "Riyaz-us-Salatin").

The Indo-Aryan suffix *[Desh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/-desh "-desh")* is derived from the Sanskrit word *deśha*, which means "land" or "country". Hence, the name *Bangladesh* means "Land of Bengal" or "Country of Bengal".

## History

### Early history

The first great indigenous empire to cover the territory was the [Maurya Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire "Maurya Empire") (c. 320–185 BC). Following its decline, the kingdom of [Samatata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samatata "Samatata") arose, which was a tributary state of the [Gupta Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire "Gupta Empire") (ca. 319–ca. 540 AD). [Harsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harsha "Harsha") (606–47 AD) drew Samatata into its loosely administered political structure. The Buddhist [Pala Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pala_Empire "Pala Empire") ruled the region from 750 to 1150 AD. It was overthrown by the Hindu [Sena dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sena_dynasty "Sena dynasty"), which ruled the territory until the [Muslim conquests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquests_in_the_Indian_subcontinent "Muslim conquests in the Indian subcontinent") led by [Muhammad Bakhtiyar Khalji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Bakhtiyar_Khalji "Muhammad Bakhtiyar Khalji") of the [Ghurid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghurid_dynasty "Ghurid dynasty") in 1204.

### Medieval period

Bengal was then incorporated into the [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate "Delhi Sultanate") (1206–1526 AD). In 1341, the independent [Bengal Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Sultanate "Bengal Sultanate") was established by [Fakhruddin Mubarak Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fakhruddin_Mubarak_Shah "Fakhruddin Mubarak Shah"). Two dynasties ruled the independent Sultanate of Bengal consecutively, the [Hussain Shahi dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussain_Shahi_dynasty "Hussain Shahi dynasty") and the [Ilyas Shahi dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilyas_Shahi_dynasty "Ilyas Shahi dynasty"), with a brief reign of the [House of Ganesha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Ganesha "House of Ganesha"). Amid geographic expansion and economic prosperity, it was regarded by European and Chinese visitors as the "richest country to trade with". The [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire "Mughal Empire") conquered Bengal in 1576. By the 18th century, the [Bengal Subah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Subah "Bengal Subah") emerged as one of the wealthiest provinces of the empire and was described as the "Paradise of Countries" and the "breadbasket of India". The region was a major global exporter and producer of cotton textiles ([muslin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslin_trade_in_Bengal "Muslin trade in Bengal") in particular), silk and [shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipbuilding_in_Bangladesh "Shipbuilding in Bangladesh"). Following the decline of the Mughal Empire in the early 1700s, the region became a semi-independent state under the [Nawabs of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawabs_of_Bengal "Nawabs of Bengal"), founded by [Murshid Quli Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murshid_Quli_Khan "Murshid Quli Khan") in 1717.

### British colonial rule

On 23 June 1757, the state led by [Siraj-ud-Daulah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siraj-ud-Daulah "Siraj-ud-Daulah") was defeated by the British [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company "East India Company") in the [Battle of Plassey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Plassey "Battle of Plassey")—which helped establish colonial [British rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj") over Bengal and the wider Indian subcontinent. Bengal's economy became connected to the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution "Industrial Revolution") at the expense of an extraordinary [capital flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_flight "Capital flight") and deindustrialisation following colonial extraction and the collapse of the Bengali textile industry. Estimates of deaths during the [Great Bengal famine of 1770](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Bengal_famine_of_1770 "Great Bengal famine of 1770") range from one to ten million, killing up to one-third of the total population of the Bengal Presidency.

### As a part of Pakistan

In the aftermath of [direct British rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Presidency "Bengal Presidency") for nearly two centuries, the borders of modern Bangladesh were established with the [partition of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_Bengal_(1947) "Partition of Bengal (1947)") between India and Pakistan by the [Radcliffe Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_Line "Radcliffe Line") during the [partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India "Partition of India") on 15 August 1947, when the region became [East Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bengal "East Bengal") as the eastern and most populous wing of the newly formed [Dominion of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Pakistan "Dominion of Pakistan")—alongside [West Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pakistan "West Pakistan"). The western and eastern wings of the newly formed Pakistan were geographically separated by a distance of over 1,000 miles, which became the root cause of deep [economic inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_inequality "Economic inequality"). Khawaja Nazimuddin was East Bengal's first [chief minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_East_Bengal "Chief Minister of East Bengal") with [Frederick Chalmers Bourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Chalmers_Bourne "Frederick Chalmers Bourne") its governor. The [All Pakistan Awami Muslim League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Pakistan_Awami_Muslim_League "All Pakistan Awami Muslim League") was formed in 1949. In 1950, the [East Bengal Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bengal_Legislative_Assembly "East Bengal Legislative Assembly") enacted [land reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reform "Land reform"), abolishing the Permanent Settlement and the zamindari system. The Awami Muslim League was renamed as a more "secular" [Awami League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awami_League "Awami League") in 1953. The first constituent assembly was dissolved in 1954. The [United Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Front_(East_Pakistan) "United Front (East Pakistan)") coalition led by [A. K. Fazlul Huq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._K._Fazlul_Huq "A. K. Fazlul Huq") swept aside the Muslim League in a landslide victory in the [1954 East Bengali legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_East_Bengali_legislative_election "1954 East Bengali legislative election"). East Bengal was renamed [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan "East Pakistan") in 1956 as part of the [One Unit Scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Unit_Scheme "One Unit Scheme") under the [new constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_Constitution_of_Pakistan "1956 Constitution of Pakistan"), and the province became a vital part of the [Southeast Asia Treaty Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia_Treaty_Organization "Southeast Asia Treaty Organization").

Amidst rising cultural and societal differences, the brutal government crackdown on the 1952 [Bengali language movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language_movement "Bengali language movement") to establish [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language "Bengali language") as the official language of Pakistan spurred [Bengali nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_nationalism "Bengali nationalism") and [pro-democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-democracy "Pro-democracy") movements. Pakistan adopted a new constitution in 1956. The [Pakistan Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Armed_Forces "Pakistan Armed Forces") imposed [martial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law "Martial law") in 1958, following a [coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_Pakistani_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat "1958 Pakistani coup d'état"), with [Ayub Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayub_Khan_(general) "Ayub Khan (general)") establishing a dictatorship for over a decade. A new constitution was introduced in 1962, replacing the [parliamentary system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system "Parliamentary system") with a presidential and gubernatorial system (based on [electoral college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_college "Electoral college") selection) known as "Basic Democracy". In 1962, Dhaka became the seat of the [National Assembly of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Pakistan "National Assembly of Pakistan"), a move seen as appeasing increased Bengali nationalism. In 1966, Awami League leader [Sheikh Mujibur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Mujibur_Rahman "Sheikh Mujibur Rahman") announced a [six-point movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_point_movement "Six point movement") for a federal parliamentary democracy.

Ethnic, linguistic, and cultural discrimination was common in Pakistan's civil and military services, in which Bengalis were under-represented; leading to East Pakistan forging a distinct political identity. Authorities banned Bengali literature and music in the state media. The Pakistani government practised extensive [economic discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_discrimination "Economic discrimination") against East Pakistan, including the refusal for foreign aid allocation. Despite generating 70% of Pakistan's export revenue with jute and tea, East Pakistan received much less government spending. Notable economists from East Pakistan, including [Rehman Sobhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehman_Sobhan "Rehman Sobhan") and [Nurul Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nurul_Islam_(economist) "Nurul Islam (economist)"), demanded a separate foreign exchange account for the eastern wing, also pointing to the existence of two different economies within Pakistan itself, dubbed the Two-Economies Theory. The populist leader Sheikh Mujibur Rahman was arrested for treason in the [Agartala Conspiracy Case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agartala_Conspiracy_Case "Agartala Conspiracy Case") and was released during the [1969 uprising in East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_uprising_in_East_Pakistan "1969 uprising in East Pakistan") which resulted in Ayub Khan's resignation. General [Yahya Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahya_Khan "Yahya Khan") assumed power, reintroducing martial law.

After the Pakistan Government's poor response to the [1970 Bhola Cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_Bhola_Cyclone "1970 Bhola Cyclone") which left over 500,000 people dead, [Abdul Hamid Khan Bhashani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Hamid_Khan_Bhashani "Abdul Hamid Khan Bhashani"), a prominent political figure who had previously led multiple rebellion against the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj"), was the first ever Bengali to declare independence of East Pakistan in a massive public rally on 23 November 1970 but it was not officially recognized. After the December 1970 elections, the Bengali-nationalist Awami League won 167 of 169 East Pakistani seats in the National Assembly. The League claimed the right to form a government and develop a new constitution but was strongly opposed by the Pakistani military and the [Pakistan Peoples Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Peoples_Party "Pakistan Peoples Party") led by [Zulfikar Ali Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulfikar_Ali_Bhutto "Zulfikar Ali Bhutto").

The [7 March Speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7_March_Speech_of_Sheikh_Mujibur_Rahman "7 March Speech of Sheikh Mujibur Rahman") of Mujib led to a [non-cooperation movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-cooperation_movement_(1971) "Non-cooperation movement (1971)"). The autocratic Pakistani government then initiated [Operation Searchlight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Searchlight "Operation Searchlight") on 25 March 1971 in response. While Mujib was arrested by the Pakistani Army on 26 March 1971, freedom fighter Major [Ziaur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziaur_Rahman "Ziaur Rahman") publicly broadcast the independence of Bangladesh on behalf of Mujib. A nine-month-long bloody [liberation war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War "Bangladesh Liberation War") ensued, during which [a genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_genocide "Bangladesh genocide") of ethnic Bengalis occurred. The culmination of the war established Bangladesh as a sovereign nation following [Pakistani surrender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_Instrument_of_Surrender "Pakistani Instrument of Surrender") on 16 December 1971.

### Independent Bangladesh

The [Constitution of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Bangladesh "Constitution of Bangladesh") was enacted on 4 November 1972. Following independence, the Mujib-led government faced allegations of corruption and mismanagement during a period of lawlessness and economic crisis. Efforts to establish [One-party socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAKSAL "BAKSAL") and a [large famine in 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_famine_of_1974 "Bangladesh famine of 1974") led to [Mujib's assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Sheikh_Mujibur_Rahman "Assassination of Sheikh Mujibur Rahman") in 1975 following a significant decline in his popularity. The presidency was then transferred to [Ziaur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziaur_Rahman "Ziaur Rahman"), who re-established public order, industrialised agriculture, founded the [Bangladesh Nationalist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Nationalist_Party "Bangladesh Nationalist Party") and initiated the creation of the [South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Association_for_Regional_Cooperation "South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation"). Ziaur Rahman founded the Bangladesh Nationalist Party on 1 September 1978. Following [Rahman's assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Ziaur_Rahman "Assassination of Ziaur Rahman") in 1981, the ensuing decade was a [military dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_dictatorship "Military dictatorship") under [Hussain Muhammad Ershad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussain_Muhammad_Ershad "Hussain Muhammad Ershad") that saw infrastructural development, [devolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution "Devolution") reforms, privatisation of nationalised industries and the declaration of Islam as the [state religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_religion "State religion") in 1988.

After the [restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Bangladesh_mass_uprising "1990 Bangladesh mass uprising") of [parliamentary democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_democracy "Parliamentary democracy") in [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Bangladeshi_general_election "1991 Bangladeshi general election"), power alternated between [Khaleda Zia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaleda_Zia "Khaleda Zia") of the BNP and [Sheikh Hasina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Hasina "Sheikh Hasina") of the Awami League, an era dubbed the [Battle of Begums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Begums "Battle of Begums")—which shaped national politics until 2024. The country under Sheikh Hasina's leadership saw economic progress alongside criticism over [democratic backsliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_backsliding "Democratic backsliding"), [corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_Bangladesh "Corruption in Bangladesh"), and [human rights abuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Bangladesh "Human rights in Bangladesh"). Hasina won her second, third and fourth consecutive terms in the [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Bangladeshi_general_election "2014 Bangladeshi general election"), [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Bangladeshi_general_election "2018 Bangladeshi general election") and the [2024 general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Bangladeshi_general_election "2024 Bangladeshi general election"); the 2014, 2018 and 2024 elections were criticized by opposition parties and outside observers over boycotts, violence, and restrictions on political competition. Following a [July Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_Uprising "July Uprising") against the authoritarian government, Hasina was [forced to resign and flee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-cooperation_movement_(2024) "Non-cooperation movement (2024)") to India on [5 August 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_36 "July 36"). An interim government was formed in August 2024, with Nobel laureate [Muhammad Yunus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Yunus "Muhammad Yunus") as the [Chief Adviser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Adviser_of_Bangladesh "Chief Adviser of Bangladesh"). After the [2026 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Bangladeshi_general_election "2026 Bangladeshi general election") the [Bangladesh Nationalist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Nationalist_Party "Bangladesh Nationalist Party") (BNP) returned to power with Chairman [Tarique Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarique_Rahman "Tarique Rahman") being inaugurated as the Prime Minister of Bangladesh on 17 February 2026. [Bangladesh Jamaat-e-Islami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Jamaat-e-Islami "Bangladesh Jamaat-e-Islami") became the main opposition party.

Since the early 1990s, driven by [free market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_market "Free market") policies and [economic liberalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalisation "Economic liberalisation") measures, Bangladesh's economy has grown rapidly, driven in part by its large [textile industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile_industry_in_Bangladesh "Textile industry in Bangladesh"), which is the second-largest in the world. It has emerged as the [second-largest economy in South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(nominal) "List of countries by GDP (nominal)"), surpassing the nominal GDP per capita of neighboring India since 2018. Bangladesh has reduced its [poverty rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_Bangladesh "Poverty in Bangladesh"), which has gone down from 80% in 1971, to 44.2% in 1991, and all the way down to 18.7% in 2022. Its [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_Human_Development_Index "List of countries by Human Development Index") growth during the 21st century was surpassed only by China. As part of the [green transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_transition "Green transition"), Bangladesh's industrial sector emerged as a leader in building [green factories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_factory "Green factory"), with the country having the largest number of certified green factories in the world. It has also given shelter to [over a million Rohingya refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya_refugees_in_Bangladesh "Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh") fleeing the [Rohingya genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya_genocide "Rohingya genocide") since 2017, which has strained its resources and highlighted its humanitarian commitments.

## Geography

Bangladesh is in South Asia on the [Bay of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Bengal "Bay of Bengal"). It is surrounded almost entirely by neighbouring India, and shares a small border with [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar") to its southeast, though it lies very close to [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal "Nepal"), [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan "Bhutan"), and China. The country is divided into three regions. Most of the country is dominated by the fertile [Ganges Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges_Delta "Ganges Delta"), the largest river delta in the world. The northwest and central parts of the country are formed by the [Madhupur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhupur_tract "Madhupur tract") and the [Barind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barind_Tract "Barind Tract") plateaus. The northeast and southeast are home to [evergreen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evergreen "Evergreen") hill ranges.

The Ganges delta is formed by the confluence of the Ganges (local name [Padma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_River "Padma River") or *Pôdda*), [Brahmaputra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra_River "Brahmaputra River") ([Jamuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamuna_River_(Bangladesh) "Jamuna River (Bangladesh)") or *Jomuna*), and [Meghna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meghna_River "Meghna River") rivers and their tributaries. The Ganges unites with the Jamuna (main channel of the Brahmaputra) and later joins the Meghna, finally flowing into the Bay of Bengal. Bangladesh is called the "Land of Rivers", as it is home to over 57 [trans-boundary rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-boundary_river "Trans-boundary river"), the most of any nation-state. Water issues are politically complicated since Bangladesh is downstream of India.

Bangladesh is predominantly rich fertile flat land. Most of it is less than 12 m (39 ft) above sea level, and it is estimated that about 10% of its land would be flooded if the sea level were to rise by 1 m (3.3 ft). 12% of the country is covered by hill systems. The country's [haor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haor "Haor") wetlands are of significance to global environmental science. The [highest point in Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mountains_of_Bangladesh "List of mountains of Bangladesh") is the [Saka Haphong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saka_Haphong "Saka Haphong"), located near the border with Myanmar, with an elevation of 1,064 m (3,491 ft). Previously, either [Keokradong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keokradong "Keokradong") or [Tazing Dong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tazing_Dong "Tazing Dong") were considered the highest.

In Bangladesh [forest cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_cover "Forest cover") is around 14% of the total land area, equivalent to 1,883,400 hectares (ha) of forest in 2020, down from 1,920,330 hectares (ha) in 1990. In 2020, naturally regenerating forest covered 1,725,330 hectares (ha) and planted forest covered 158,070 hectares (ha). Of the naturally regenerating forest 0% was reported to be [primary forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_forest "Primary forest") (consisting of native tree species with no clearly visible indications of human activity) and around 33% of the forest area was found within protected areas. For the year 2015, 100% of the forest area was reported to be under [public ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_ownership "State ownership").

### Climate

Straddling the [Tropic of Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropic_of_Cancer "Tropic of Cancer"), Bangladesh's climate is tropical, with a mild winter from October to March and a hot, humid summer from March to June. The country has never recorded an air temperature below 0 °C (32 °F), with a record low of 1.1 °C (34.0 °F) in the northwest city of [Dinajpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinajpur "Dinajpur") on 3 February 1905. A warm and humid [monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon "Monsoon") season lasts from June to October and supplies most of the country's rainfall. Natural calamities, such as [floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floods_in_Bangladesh "Floods in Bangladesh"), [tropical cyclones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclone "Tropical cyclone"), [tornadoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado "Tornado"), and [tidal bores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_bore "Tidal bore") occur almost every year, combined with the effects of [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation "Deforestation"), [soil degradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soils_retrogression_and_degradation "Soils retrogression and degradation") and [erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion "Erosion"). The [cyclones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Bangladesh_tropical_cyclones "List of Bangladesh tropical cyclones") of 1970 and 1991 were particularly devastating, the [latter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Bangladesh_cyclone "1991 Bangladesh cyclone") killing approximately 140,000 people.

In September 1998, Bangladesh saw the [most severe flooding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Bangladesh_floods "1998 Bangladesh floods") in modern history, after which two-thirds of the country went underwater, along with a death toll of 1,000. As a result of various international and national level initiatives in disaster risk reduction, the human toll and economic damage from floods and cyclones have come down over the years. The [2007 South Asian floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_South_Asian_floods "2007 South Asian floods") ravaged areas across the country, leaving five million people displaced, with a death toll around 500.

#### Climate change

Bangladesh is recognised to be one of the countries most [vulnerable to climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_vulnerability "Climate change vulnerability"). Over the course of a century, 508 cyclones have affected the Bay of Bengal region, 17% of which are believed to have made landfall in Bangladesh. [Natural hazards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_hazards "Natural hazards") that come from increased rainfall, rising sea levels, and tropical cyclones are expected to increase as the climate changes, each seriously affecting agriculture, water and food security, human health, and shelter. It is estimated that by 2050, a three-foot rise in sea levels will inundate some 20% of the land and displace more than 30 million people. To address the [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise "Sea level rise") threat in Bangladesh, the [Bangladesh Delta Plan 2100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Delta_Plan_2100 "Bangladesh Delta Plan 2100") has been launched.

### Biodiversity

Bangladesh is located in the [Indomalayan realm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indomalayan_realm "Indomalayan realm"), and lies within four terrestrial ecoregions: [Lower Gangetic Plains moist deciduous forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Gangetic_Plains_moist_deciduous_forests "Lower Gangetic Plains moist deciduous forests"), [Mizoram–Manipur–Kachin rain forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizoram%E2%80%93Manipur%E2%80%93Kachin_rain_forests "Mizoram–Manipur–Kachin rain forests"), [Sundarbans freshwater swamp forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundarbans_freshwater_swamp_forests "Sundarbans freshwater swamp forests"), and [Sundarbans mangroves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundarbans_mangroves "Sundarbans mangroves"). Its ecology includes a long sea coastline, numerous [rivers and tributaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rivers_in_Bangladesh "List of rivers in Bangladesh"), lakes, [wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetland "Wetland"), evergreen forests, semi evergreen forests, hill forests, moist [deciduous forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deciduous_forest "Deciduous forest"), freshwater swamp forests and flat land with tall grass. The Bangladesh Plain is famous for its fertile [alluvial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alluvial "Alluvial") soil which supports extensive cultivation. The country is dominated by lush vegetation, with villages often buried in groves of [mango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mango "Mango"), [jackfruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackfruit "Jackfruit"), [bamboo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo "Bamboo"), [betel nut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betel_nut "Betel nut"), [coconut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut "Coconut"), and [date palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm "Date palm"). The country has up to 6000 species of plant life, including 5000 flowering plants. Water bodies and wetland systems provide a habitat for many aquatic plants. [Water lilies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nymphaeaceae "Nymphaeaceae") and [lotuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelumbo_nucifera "Nelumbo nucifera") grow vividly during the monsoon season. The country has [60 wildlife sanctuaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_protected_areas_of_Bangladesh "List of protected areas of Bangladesh").

Bangladesh is home to most of the [Sundarbans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundarbans "Sundarbans"), the world's largest [mangrove forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove_forest "Mangrove forest"), covering an area of 6,000 square kilometres (2,300 sq mi) in the southwest littoral region. It is divided into three protected sanctuaries: the [South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundarbans_South_Wildlife_Sanctuary "Sundarbans South Wildlife Sanctuary"), [East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundarbans_East_Wildlife_Sanctuary "Sundarbans East Wildlife Sanctuary"), and [West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundarbans_West_Wildlife_Sanctuary "Sundarbans West Wildlife Sanctuary") zones. The forest is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The northeastern Sylhet region is home to haor wetlands, a distinctive ecosystem. It also includes [tropical and subtropical coniferous forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_and_subtropical_coniferous_forests "Tropical and subtropical coniferous forests"), a [freshwater swamp forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater_swamp_forest "Freshwater swamp forest"), and mixed deciduous forests. The southeastern Chittagong region covers evergreen and semi-evergreen hilly jungles. Central Bangladesh includes the plainland Sal forest running along with the districts of Gazipur, [Tangail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangail "Tangail"), and [Mymensingh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mymensingh "Mymensingh"). [St. Martin's Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Martin%27s_Island "St. Martin's Island") is the only [coral reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_reef "Coral reef") in the country.

Bangladesh has an abundance of [wildlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_in_Bangladesh "Wildlife in Bangladesh") in its forests, marshes, woodlands, and hills. The vast majority of animals dwell within a habitat of 150,000 square kilometres (58,000 sq mi). The [Bengal tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_tiger "Bengal tiger"), [clouded leopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clouded_leopard "Clouded leopard"), [saltwater crocodile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltwater_crocodile "Saltwater crocodile"), [black panther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_panther "Black panther") and [fishing cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_cat "Fishing cat") are among the chief predators in the Sundarbans. Northern and eastern Bangladesh is home to the [Asian elephant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_elephant "Asian elephant"), [hoolock gibbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoolock_gibbon "Hoolock gibbon"), [Asian black bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_black_bear "Asian black bear") and [oriental pied hornbill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_pied_hornbill "Oriental pied hornbill"). The [chital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chital "Chital") deer are widely seen in southwestern woodlands. Other animals include the [black giant squirrel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_giant_squirrel "Black giant squirrel"), [capped langur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capped_langur "Capped langur"), [Bengal fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_fox "Bengal fox"), [sambar deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambar_deer "Sambar deer"), [jungle cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungle_cat "Jungle cat"), [king cobra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_cobra "King cobra"), [wild boar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_boar "Wild boar"), [mongooses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongoose "Mongoose"), [pangolins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangolin "Pangolin"), [pythons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_(genus) "Python (genus)") and [water monitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_water_monitor "Asian water monitor"). Bangladesh has one of the largest populations of [Irrawaddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrawaddy_dolphins "Irrawaddy dolphins") and [Ganges dolphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_river_dolphin "South Asian river dolphin"). The country has numerous species of [amphibians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibian "Amphibian") (53), reptiles (139), [marine reptiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_reptile "Marine reptile") (19) and [marine mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_mammal "Marine mammal") (5). It also has [628 species of birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_birds_of_Bangladesh "List of birds of Bangladesh").

Several animals became extinct in Bangladesh during the last century, including the one-horned and two-horned [rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinoceros "Rhinoceros") and common [peafowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peafowl "Peafowl"). The human population is concentrated in urban areas, limiting deforestation to a certain extent. Rapid urban growth has threatened natural habitats. The country has widespread environmental issues; pollution of the [Dhaleshwari River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaleshwari_River "Dhaleshwari River") by the textile industry and shrimp cultivation in Chakaria Sundarbans have both been described by academics as [ecocides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecocide "Ecocide"). Although many areas are protected under law, some Bangladeshi wildlife is threatened by this growth. The [Bangladesh Environment Conservation Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Environment_Conservation_Act "Bangladesh Environment Conservation Act") was enacted in 1995. The government has designated several regions as [Ecologically Critical Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecologically_Critical_Area "Ecologically Critical Area"), including wetlands, forests, and rivers. The [Sundarbans tiger project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundarbans_tiger_project "Sundarbans tiger project") and the Bangladesh Bear Project are among the key initiatives to strengthen conservation. It ratified the Rio [Convention on Biological Diversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_on_Biological_Diversity "Convention on Biological Diversity") on 3 May 1994. As of 2014[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bangladesh&action=edit), the country was set to revise its [National Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_action_plan "Biodiversity action plan").

## Government and politics

Bangladesh, by [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Bangladesh "Constitution of Bangladesh"), is a [unitary state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state "Unitary state") and a *[de jure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_jure "De jure")* [representative democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy "Representative democracy") with a [Westminster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_system "Westminster system")-style [parliamentary system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_republic "Parliamentary republic") that has [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage "Universal suffrage"). The [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Bangladesh "Government of Bangladesh") can be divided into three pillars: the executive, the legislative and the [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_of_Bangladesh "Judiciary of Bangladesh"). All function to ensure accountability, transparency and [checks and balances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers "Separation of powers"). Since its independence, the [Awami League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awami_League "Awami League") (AL) and the [Bangladesh Nationalist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Nationalist_Party "Bangladesh Nationalist Party") (BNP) have remained two of the most powerful [political parties in Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_Bangladesh "List of political parties in Bangladesh").

* The first pillar of the government is the executive organ, which is entrusted with the administration of the country. Executive powers are largely vested in the [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Bangladesh "Prime Minister of Bangladesh"), who is the [head of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_government "Head of government") and oversees the [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Bangladesh "Cabinet of Bangladesh"). The tenure of a parliamentary government is five years. Various ministers form the bulk of the executive organ, overseeing government departments and forming policies. The [Civil Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Civil_Service "Bangladesh Civil Service") assists the ministers in implementing policy. All authorities unite to formulate policies, manage public services, and implement national development plans. The [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Bangladesh "President of Bangladesh") is the ceremonial [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state "Head of state"), whose powers include signing bills passed by parliament into law, maintaining the government's stability and continuity, and fulfilling their duties as the commander-in-chief of the [Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Armed_Forces "Bangladesh Armed Forces") and the [chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor "Chancellor") of all universities.
* The second pillar of the government is the legislative organ, also known as the [Jatiya Sangsad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jatiya_Sangsad "Jatiya Sangsad") (House of the Nation). Citizens elect the members of parliament (MPs). The [unicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicameral "Unicameral") parliament has 350 MPs, including 300 elected on the [first past the post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_past_the_post "First past the post") system and 50 appointed to reserved seats for [women's empowerment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_empowerment "Women's empowerment"). [Article 70 of the Constitution of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_70_of_the_Constitution_of_Bangladesh "Article 70 of the Constitution of Bangladesh") forbids MPs from voting against their party. The parliament is presided over by the [Speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_Jatiya_Sangsad "Speaker of the Jatiya Sangsad"), who is second in line to the president.
* The third pillar of the government is the judiciary organ, which is in charge of interpreting the law, resolving conflicts, and maintaining justice. The [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Bangladesh "Supreme Court of Bangladesh") is the highest court, separated into the [Appellate Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appellate_Division,_Supreme_Court_of_Bangladesh "Appellate Division, Supreme Court of Bangladesh") and the [High Court Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_Division,_Supreme_Court_of_Bangladesh "High Court Division, Supreme Court of Bangladesh"). It is led by the [Chief Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_Bangladesh "Chief Justice of Bangladesh"). The judiciary has the power to assess a law's constitutionality and offer legal remedies. It protects citizens' rights, ensures the law is applied fairly, and preserves the balance of power within the government. The courts have wide latitude in [judicial review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_review_in_Bangladesh "Judicial review in Bangladesh"), and judicial [precedent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precedent "Precedent") is supported by Article 111 of the constitution. The judiciary includes district and metropolitan courts divided into civil and criminal courts. Due to a shortage of judges, the judiciary has a large backlog.

According to [International IDEA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_for_Democracy_and_Electoral_Assistance "International Institute for Democracy and Electoral Assistance")'s Global State of Democracy (GSoD) Indices and Democracy Tracker, Bangladesh performs in the low range on overall democratic measures, with particular weaknesses in political representation, including credible elections, inclusive suffrage and elected government.

### Administrative divisions

Bangladesh is divided into eight administrative divisions, each named after its headquarters: [Barisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barisal_Division "Barisal Division") (officially *Barishal*), [Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Division "Chittagong Division") (officially *Chattogram*), [Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka_Division "Dhaka Division"), [Khulna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khulna_Division "Khulna Division"), [Mymensingh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mymensingh_Division "Mymensingh Division"), [Rajshahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajshahi_Division "Rajshahi Division"), [Rangpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangpur_Division "Rangpur Division"), and [Sylhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet_Division "Sylhet Division").

Divisions are subdivided into districts (*zila*). Bangladesh has 64 districts, each subdivided into *upazila* (subdistricts) or *thana*. The area within each police station, except for those in metropolitan areas, is divided into *[unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Councils_of_Bangladesh "Union Councils of Bangladesh")*, with each union comprising multiple villages. In the metropolitan areas, police stations are divided into wards, further divided into *mahallas*.

There are no elected officials at the divisional or district levels, and the administration comprises only government officials. Direct elections are held in each union (or ward) for a chairperson and several members. In 1997, Parliament passed legislation reserving three seats (out of 12) in every union for women.

### Foreign relations

Bangladesh is considered a [middle power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_power "Middle power") in [global politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_politics "Global politics"). It plays an important role in the geopolitical affairs of the [Indo-Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pacific "Indo-Pacific"), due to its strategic location between South and Southeast Asia. Bangladesh joined the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations "Commonwealth of Nations") in 1972 and the United Nations in 1974. It relies on multilateral diplomacy on issues like climate change, nuclear non-proliferation, trade policy and non-traditional security issues. Bangladesh pioneered the creation of [SAARC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Association_for_Regional_Cooperation "South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation"), the preeminent forum for regional diplomacy on the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent"). It joined the [OIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_Islamic_Cooperation "Organisation of Islamic Cooperation") in 1974, and is a founding member of the [Developing-8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-8_Organization_for_Economic_Cooperation "D-8 Organization for Economic Cooperation"). Recently, Bangladesh has focused on promoting regional trade and transport links with support from the [World Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank "World Bank"). It is also [attempting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Bangladesh_to_ASEAN "Accession of Bangladesh to ASEAN") to join [ASEAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN "ASEAN") as one of its top foreign policy goals. Dhaka hosts the headquarters of [BIMSTEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BIMSTEC "BIMSTEC"), an organisation that brings together [countries dependent on the Bay of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countries_dependent_on_the_Bay_of_Bengal "Countries dependent on the Bay of Bengal").

[Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh%E2%80%93Myanmar_relations "Bangladesh–Myanmar relations") with neighbouring Myanmar have been severely strained since 2016–2017, after over 700,000 [Rohingya refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya_refugees_in_Bangladesh "Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh") fled into Bangladesh. Bangladesh's parliament, government, and civil society have been at the forefront of [international criticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_reaction_to_the_2016%E2%80%9317_Rohingya_exodus "International reaction to the 2016–17 Rohingya exodus") against Myanmar for military operations against the Rohingya, and have demanded their [right of return](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_of_return "Right of return") to [Arakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakan "Arakan").

Bangladesh shares an important [bilateral and economic relationship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh%E2%80%93India_relations "Bangladesh–India relations") with its largest neighbour, India, which is often strained by [water politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_politics_in_India "Water politics in India") of the [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges_water_dispute "Ganges water dispute") and the [Teesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teesta_River#Water_sharing_challenge "Teesta River"), and the [border killings of Bangladeshi civilians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deaths_along_the_Bangladesh%E2%80%93India_border "Deaths along the Bangladesh–India border"). Bangladesh's relationship with Pakistan is problematic, mainly because Pakistan denies the 1971 [Bangladesh genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_genocide "Bangladesh genocide"). It maintains a [warm relationship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh-China_relations "Bangladesh-China relations") with China, its largest trading partner and largest arms supplier. Japan is Bangladesh's largest economic aid provider, and the two have a [strategic and economic partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh%E2%80%93Japan_relations "Bangladesh–Japan relations"). Political relations with Middle Eastern countries are robust. Bangladesh receives 59% of its remittances from the Middle East, despite poor working conditions affecting over four million [Bangladeshi workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshis_in_the_Middle_East "Bangladeshis in the Middle East"). Bangladesh plays a major role in [global climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change "Climate change") diplomacy as a leader of the [Climate Vulnerable Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_Vulnerable_Forum "Climate Vulnerable Forum").

### Military

The [Bangladesh Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Armed_Forces "Bangladesh Armed Forces") have inherited the institutional framework of the [British military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_military "British military") and the [British Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Army "British Indian Army"). In 2024, the active personnel strength of the Bangladesh Armed Forces was around 230,000, including the Air Force (21,000) and the Navy (27,000). In addition to traditional defence roles, the military has supported civil authorities in disaster relief and provided internal security during periods of political unrest. For many years, Bangladesh has been the world's largest contributor to [UN peacekeeping forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_peacekeeping "United Nations peacekeeping"). The [military budget of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_budget_of_Bangladesh "Military budget of Bangladesh") accounts for 1.3% of GDP, amounting to US$4.3 billion in 2021.

The [Bangladesh Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Navy "Bangladesh Navy"), one of the largest in the Bay of Bengal, includes a [fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_active_ships_of_the_Bangladesh_Navy "List of active ships of the Bangladesh Navy") of frigates, submarines, corvettes, and other vessels. The [Bangladesh Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Air_Force "Bangladesh Air Force") has a [small fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_active_Bangladesh_military_aircraft "List of active Bangladesh military aircraft") of multi-role combat aircraft. Most of Bangladesh's military equipment comes from China. In recent years, Bangladesh and India have increased joint military exercises, high-level visits of military leaders, [counter-terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-terrorism "Counter-terrorism") cooperation and intelligence sharing. Bangladesh is vital to ensuring stability and security in [northeast India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_India "Northeast India").

Bangladesh's strategic importance in the eastern subcontinent hinges on its proximity to China, its frontier with Burma, the separation of mainland and northeast India, and its maritime territory in the Bay of Bengal. In 2002, Bangladesh and China signed a Defense Cooperation Agreement. The United States has pursued negotiations with Bangladesh on a [Status of forces agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_of_forces_agreement "Status of forces agreement"), an [Acquisition and Cross-Servicing Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acquisition_and_Cross-Servicing_Agreement "Acquisition and Cross-Servicing Agreement") and a General Security of Military Information Agreement. In 2019, Bangladesh ratified the UN [Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Prohibition_of_Nuclear_Weapons "Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons").

### Civil society

Since the colonial period, Bangladesh has had a prominent [civil society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_society "Civil society"). There are various special interest groups, including [non-governmental organisations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-governmental_organisations "Non-governmental organisations"), human rights organisations, professional associations, [chambers of commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_commerce "Chamber of commerce"), employers' associations, and trade unions. The [National Human Rights Commission of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Human_Rights_Commission_of_Bangladesh "National Human Rights Commission of Bangladesh") was set up in 2007. Notable human rights organisations and initiatives include the [Centre for Law and Mediation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Law_and_Mediation_(Bangladesh) "Centre for Law and Mediation (Bangladesh)"), [Odhikar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odhikar "Odhikar"), the [Alliance for Bangladesh Worker Safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_for_Bangladesh_Worker_Safety "Alliance for Bangladesh Worker Safety"), the [Bangladesh Environmental Lawyers Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Environmental_Lawyers_Association "Bangladesh Environmental Lawyers Association"), the [Bangladesh Hindu Buddhist Christian Unity Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Hindu_Buddhist_Christian_Unity_Council "Bangladesh Hindu Buddhist Christian Unity Council") and the [War Crimes Fact Finding Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Crimes_Fact_Finding_Committee "War Crimes Fact Finding Committee"). The world's largest international NGO [BRAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRAC_(organisation) "BRAC (organisation)") is based in Bangladesh. There has been concern about the shrinking space for independent civil society in recent years.

### Human rights

[Torture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torture "Torture") is banned by the [Constitution of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Bangladesh "Constitution of Bangladesh"), but is rampantly used by Bangladesh's security forces. Bangladesh joined the [Convention against Torture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_against_Torture "Convention against Torture") in 1998 and enacted its first anti-torture law, the [Torture and Custodial Death (Prevention) Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torture_and_Custodial_Death_(Prevention)_Act "Torture and Custodial Death (Prevention) Act"), in 2013. The first conviction under this law was announced in 2020. Amnesty International [Prisoners of Conscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner_of_conscience "Prisoner of conscience") from Bangladesh have included [Saber Hossain Chowdhury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saber_Hossain_Chowdhury "Saber Hossain Chowdhury") and [Shahidul Alam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahidul_Alam "Shahidul Alam"). The widely criticised [Digital Security Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Security_Act "Digital Security Act") was repealed and replaced by the Cyber Security Act in 2023. The repeal was welcomed by the [International Press Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Press_Institute "International Press Institute").

On [International Human Rights Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Human_Rights_Day "International Human Rights Day") in December 2021, the [United States Department of the Treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Treasury "United States Department of the Treasury") announced [sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_sanctions "Economic sanctions") on commanders of the [Rapid Action Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_Action_Battalion "Rapid Action Battalion") for extrajudicial killings, torture, and other human rights abuses. [Freedom House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_House "Freedom House") has criticised the government for human rights abuses, the crackdown on the opposition, mass media, and civil society through politicised enforcement. Bangladesh is ranked "partly free" in Freedom House's *[Freedom in the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_in_the_World "Freedom in the World")* report, but its [press freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Press_freedom "Press freedom") has deteriorated from "free" to "not free" in recent years due to increasing government pressure. According to the British [Economist Intelligence Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist_Intelligence_Unit "Economist Intelligence Unit"), the country has a [hybrid regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_regime "Hybrid regime"): the third of four rankings in its [Democracy Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_Index "Democracy Index"). Bangladesh ranked 96th among 163 countries in the 2022 [Global Peace Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Peace_Index "Global Peace Index"). According to National Human Rights Commission, 70% of alleged human-rights violations are committed by law-enforcement agencies.

[LGBT rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_Bangladesh "LGBT rights in Bangladesh") are frowned upon among social conservatives. Homosexuality is affected by [Section 377](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_377 "Section 377") of the [Penal Code of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penal_Code_of_Bangladesh "Penal Code of Bangladesh"), originally enacted by the British colonial government. The government only recognises the [transgender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender "Transgender") and [intersex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersex "Intersex") community known as the [Hijra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hijra_(South_Asia) "Hijra (South Asia)"). According to the 2023 Global Slavery Index, an estimated 1.2 million people were enslaved in Bangladesh as of 2021[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bangladesh&action=edit), which is among the highest in the world.

### Corruption

Like many developing countries, [institutional corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutional_corruption "Institutional corruption") is an issue of concern for Bangladesh. Bangladesh ranked 146th among 180 countries on [Transparency International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transparency_International "Transparency International")'s 2018 [Corruption Perceptions Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_Perceptions_Index "Corruption Perceptions Index"). Land administration was the sector with the most bribery in 2015, followed by education, police and water supply. The [Anti Corruption Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti_Corruption_Commission_Bangladesh "Anti Corruption Commission Bangladesh") was formed in 2004, and was active during the [2006–2008 Bangladeshi political crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006%E2%80%932008_Bangladeshi_political_crisis "2006–2008 Bangladeshi political crisis"), indicting many leading politicians, bureaucrats and businessmen for [graft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graft_(politics) "Graft (politics)").

## Economy

Bangladesh is a [lower-middle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GNI_(nominal)_per_capita#Lower-middle-income_group "List of countries by GNI (nominal) per capita") income [mixed-market economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_economy "Mixed economy"). It has the world's [36th-largest economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(nominal) "List of countries by GDP (nominal)") by nominal terms, and the [25th-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(PPP) "List of countries by GDP (PPP)") by [PPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity "Purchasing power parity"). Bangladesh has a [labour force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_force "Labour force") of 71.4 million, which is the world's [seventh-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_labour_force "List of countries by labour force"), with an unemployment rate of 3.6% as of 2024[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bangladesh&action=edit). Its [foreign exchange reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_exchange_reserves "Foreign exchange reserves"), although depleting, remain the [second-highest in South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_foreign_exchange_reserves "List of countries by foreign exchange reserves"), after India. Bangladesh's [large diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshi_diaspora "Bangladeshi diaspora") contributed roughly $27 billion in [remittances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remittance_to_Bangladesh "Remittance to Bangladesh") in 2024. The [Bangladeshi taka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshi_taka "Bangladeshi taka") is the national currency.

As of 2023[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bangladesh&action=edit), the large service sector accounts for about 51.5% of total GDP, followed by the industrial sector (34.6%), while the [agriculture sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Bangladesh "Agriculture in Bangladesh") is by far the smallest, making up only 11% of total GDP. Although it is the largest employment sector, it provides roughly half of the total workforce. Over 84% of the export earnings come from the [textile industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile_industry_in_Bangladesh "Textile industry in Bangladesh"). Bangladesh is the second-leading garments exporter in the world, and supplies the global [fast fashion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_fashion "Fast fashion") industry, exporting to various Western fashion brands. It is also a major producer of [jute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jute_industry_of_Bangladesh "Jute industry of Bangladesh"), [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_production_in_Bangladesh "Rice production in Bangladesh"), [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fishes_in_Bangladesh "List of fishes in Bangladesh"), [tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_production_in_Bangladesh "Tea production in Bangladesh"), and flowers. Other major industries include [shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipbuilding_in_Bangladesh "Shipbuilding in Bangladesh"), [pharmaceuticals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmaceutical_industry_in_Bangladesh "Pharmaceutical industry in Bangladesh"), [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_industry_in_Bangladesh "Steel industry in Bangladesh"), [electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics_industry_in_Bangladesh "Electronics industry in Bangladesh") and [leather goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leather_industry_in_Bangladesh "Leather industry in Bangladesh"). China is the [largest trading partner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_largest_trading_partners_of_Bangladesh "List of the largest trading partners of Bangladesh") of Bangladesh, accounting for 15% of the total trade, followed by India, which accounts for 8% of the total trade.

The private sector accounts for 80% of GDP compared to the dwindling role of state-owned companies. Bangladesh's economy is dominated by family-owned [conglomerates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_companies_of_Bangladesh "List of companies of Bangladesh") and small and medium-sized businesses. Some of the largest publicly traded companies in Bangladesh include [BEXIMCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BEXIMCO "BEXIMCO"), [BRAC Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRAC_Bank "BRAC Bank"), [BSRM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSRM "BSRM"), [GPH Ispat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPH_Ispat "GPH Ispat"), [Grameenphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grameenphone "Grameenphone"), [Summit Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summit_Group "Summit Group"), and [Square Pharmaceuticals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_Pharmaceuticals "Square Pharmaceuticals"). The [Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka_Stock_Exchange "Dhaka Stock Exchange") and [Chittagong Stock Exchanges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Stock_Exchange "Chittagong Stock Exchange") are the country's twin [capital markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_market "Capital market"). Its [telecommunications industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_in_Bangladesh "Telecommunications in Bangladesh") is one of the world's fastest growing, with 188.78 million cellphone subscribers at the end of November 2024. Political instability, high inflation, [endemic corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_Bangladesh "Corruption in Bangladesh"), insufficient power supplies, and slow implementation of reforms are major challenges to economic growth.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Bangladesh was ranked 106th in the [Global Innovation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Innovation_Index "Global Innovation Index") in 2024 and in 2025.

### Energy

Bangladesh, a country experiencing daily blackouts several times a day in 2009, achieved 100% electrification by 2022. It is gradually transitioning to a [green economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_economy "Green economy") and has the largest off-grid solar power programme in the world, benefiting 20 million people. An [electric car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_car "Electric car") called the *Palki* is being developed for production in the country. [Biogas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biogas "Biogas") is being used to produce organic fertiliser. The under-construction [Rooppur Nuclear Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rooppur_Nuclear_Power_Plant "Rooppur Nuclear Power Plant"), under-construction with assistance from the Russian company [Rosatom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosatom "Rosatom"), will be the first operational [nuclear power plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_plant "Nuclear power plant") in the country. Its first unit, out of the two total units, is expected to go into operation in 2025.

Bangladesh continues to have huge untapped reserves of natural gas, particularly in its maritime territory. A lack of exploration and decreasing proven reserves have forced Bangladesh to import [LNG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LNG "LNG") from abroad. Gas shortages were further exacerbated by the [Russo-Ukrainian war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Ukrainian_war_(2022%E2%80%93present) "Russo-Ukrainian war (2022–present)"). Bangladesh stopped buying [spot price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spot_price "Spot price") LNG temporarily in July 2022, despite constant load-shedding, due to a steep price hike in the global market. It restarted buying spot price LNG once again in February 2023 as prices eased.

While [government-owned companies in Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_government-owned_companies_of_Bangladesh "List of government-owned companies of Bangladesh") generate nearly half of Bangladesh's electricity, privately owned companies like the Summit Group and [Orion Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orion_Group_(Bangladesh) "Orion Group (Bangladesh)") are playing an increasingly important role in both generating electricity, and supplying machinery, reactors, and equipment. Bangladesh increased electricity production capacity from 5 gigawatts in 2009 to 25.5 gigawatts in 2022. It plans to further increase it to 50 [gigawatts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigawatts "Gigawatts") by 2041. US companies like [Chevron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevron_Corporation "Chevron Corporation") and [General Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric "General Electric") supply around 55% of Bangladesh's domestic natural gas production and are among the largest investors in power projects. 80% of Bangladesh's installed gas-fired power generation capacity comes from turbines manufactured in the United States.

### Tourism

The [tourism industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Bangladesh "Tourism in Bangladesh") is expanding, contributing some 3.02% of total GDP. Bangladesh's international tourism receipts in 2019 amounted to $391 million. The country has [three UNESCO World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_Bangladesh "List of World Heritage Sites in Bangladesh") ([the Mosque City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Mosque_City_of_Bagerhat "Historic Mosque City of Bagerhat"), [the Buddhist Vihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruins_of_the_Buddhist_Vihara_at_Paharpur "Ruins of the Buddhist Vihara at Paharpur") and the [Sundarbans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundarbans "Sundarbans")) and seven [tentative-list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Site#Nominating_process "World Heritage Site") sites. The [World Travel and Tourism Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Travel_and_Tourism_Council "World Travel and Tourism Council") (WTTC) reported in 2019 that the travel and tourism industry in Bangladesh directly generated 1,180,500 jobs in 2018 or 1.9% of the country's total employment. According to the same report, Bangladesh experiences around 125,000 international tourist arrivals per year. Domestic spending generated 97.7% of direct travel and tourism gross domestic product (GDP) in 2012.

Bangladesh had a recorded population of 169.8 million in the [2022 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Bangladeshi_census "2022 Bangladeshi census"), which rose to 175.4 million as of 2026[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bangladesh&action=edit). Bangladesh's [total fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate "Total fertility rate") (TFR), once among the highest in the world, has experienced a dramatic decline, from 5.5 in 1985 to 3.7 in 1995, down to 1.9 in 2022, which is below the [sub-replacement fertility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-replacement_fertility "Sub-replacement fertility") of 2.1. Most of the population lives in rural areas, with only 40% of the population living in urban areas as of 2023[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bangladesh&action=edit). Bangladesh has a [median age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_age "Median age") of roughly 28 years, with 26% of the total population aged 14 or younger, and 6% aged 65 and above as of 2023[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bangladesh&action=edit).

Bangladesh is an [ethnically and culturally homogeneous society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_ranked_by_ethnic_and_cultural_diversity_level "List of countries ranked by ethnic and cultural diversity level"), as [Bengalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_people "Bengali people") form 99% of the population. The [Adivasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adivasi "Adivasi") population includes the [Chakmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakma_people "Chakma people"), [Marmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marma_people "Marma people"), [Santhals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santhal_people "Santhal people"), [Mros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mru_people_(Mrucha) "Mru people (Mrucha)"), [Tanchangyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanchangya_people "Tanchangya people"), [Bawms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bawm_people "Bawm people"), [Tripuris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripuri_people "Tripuri people"), [Khasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khasi_people "Khasi people"), [Khumis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khumi_people "Khumi people"), [Kukis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuki_people "Kuki people"), [Garos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garo_people "Garo people"), and [Bisnupriya Manipuris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisnupriya_Manipuri_people "Bisnupriya Manipuri people"). The Chittagong Hill Tracts region experienced unrest and an [insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Hill_Tracts_conflict "Chittagong Hill Tracts conflict") from 1975 to 1997 in an autonomy movement by its indigenous people. Although a peace accord was signed in 1997, the region [remains militarised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Uttaran "Operation Uttaran"). [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu "Urdu")-speaking [stranded Pakistanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stranded_Pakistanis "Stranded Pakistanis") were given citizenship by the Supreme Court in 2008. Bangladesh also hosts over 700,000 Rohingya refugees since 2017, giving it one of the largest refugee populations in the world.

### Language

The official and predominant language of Bangladesh is [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language "Bengali language"), which is spoken by more than 99% of the population as their [native language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_language "First language"). Bengali is described as a [dialect continuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialect_continuum "Dialect continuum") where there are various [dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_dialects "Bengali dialects") spoken throughout the country. There is a [diglossia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diglossia "Diglossia") in which much of the population can understand or speak in Standard Colloquial Bengali, and in their regional dialect or language variety. These include [Chittagonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagonian_language "Chittagonian language") which is spoken in the southeastern region of Chittagong, [Noakhali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noakhali_language "Noakhali language") spoken in the southern district of [Noakhali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noakhali_District "Noakhali District") and [Sylheti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylheti_language "Sylheti language") spoken in the northeastern region of [Sylhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet_Division "Sylhet Division").

English plays an important role in Bangladesh's judicial and educational affairs, due to the country's history as part of the British Empire. It is widely spoken and commonly understood, and is taught as a compulsory subject in all [schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_schools_in_Bangladesh "List of schools in Bangladesh"), [colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_colleges_in_Bangladesh "List of colleges in Bangladesh") and [universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Bangladesh "List of universities in Bangladesh"), while the English-medium educational system is widely attended.

Tribal languages, although increasingly endangered, include the [Chakma language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakma_language "Chakma language"), another native Eastern Indo-Aryan language, spoken by the Chakma people. Others are [Garo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garo_language "Garo language"), [Meitei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_language "Meitei language"), [Kokborok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokborok "Kokborok") and [Rakhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhine_language "Rakhine language"). Among the [Austroasiatic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austroasiatic_languages "Austroasiatic languages"), the most spoken is the [Santali language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santali_language "Santali language"), native to the [Santal people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santal_people "Santal people"). The [stranded Pakistanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stranded_Pakistanis_in_Bangladesh "Stranded Pakistanis in Bangladesh") and some sections of the [Old Dhakaites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhakaiyas "Dhakaiyas") often use [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu "Urdu") as their native tongue. Still, the usage of the latter remains highly reproached.

### Religion

[Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Bangladesh "Islam in Bangladesh") is the [state religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_religion "State religion") of Bangladesh. However, the [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Bangladesh "Constitution of Bangladesh") also upholds secularism and ensures equal rights for all religions. Every citizen has the freedom to practice any religion.

Islam is the largest religion in the country, followed by about 91% of the population. The vast majority of Bangladeshi citizens are [Bengali Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Muslims "Bengali Muslims"), adhering to [Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam "Sunni Islam"). The country is the third-most populous Muslim-majority state in the world and has the fourth-largest overall Muslim population.

[Bengali Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Hindus "Bengali Hindus") form the country's [second-largest religious minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Bangladesh "Hinduism in Bangladesh") and the [third-largest Hindu community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus#Demographics "Hindus") in the world. According to the [2022 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Bangladeshi_census "2022 Bangladeshi census") Hindus form 7.95% of the total population. In the [2011 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Bangladeshi_census "2011 Bangladeshi census"), Hindus formed 8.54% of the population.

[Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_Bangladesh "Buddhism in Bangladesh") is the third-most followed religion, adhered to by 0.6% of the population. Bangladeshi Buddhists are concentrated among the tribal ethnic groups in the [Chittagong Hill Tracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Hill_Tracts "Chittagong Hill Tracts") and by the [Bengali Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Buddhists "Bengali Buddhists") minority across coastal Chittagong, who mostly follow the [Theravada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theravada "Theravada") school.

[Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Bangladesh "Christianity in Bangladesh") is the fourth-largest religion at 0.3%, followed mainly by a small [Bengali Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Christians "Bengali Christians") minority. 0.1% of the population practices other religions such as [Animism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animism "Animism") or is [irreligious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion "Irreligion").

### Education

The constitution states that all children shall receive free and compulsory education. Education in Bangladesh is overseen by the [Ministry of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_(Bangladesh) "Ministry of Education (Bangladesh)"). The [Ministry of Primary and Mass Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Primary_and_Mass_Education "Ministry of Primary and Mass Education") is responsible for implementing policy for primary education and state-funded schools at a local level. Primary and secondary education is [compulsory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compulsory_education "Compulsory education"), and is financed by the state and free of charge in public schools. Bangladesh has a [literacy rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy_rate "Literacy rate") of 76% as of 2021: 79% for males and 71.9% for females. Its educational system is three-tiered and heavily subsidised, with the government operating many schools at the primary, secondary and higher secondary levels and subsidising many private schools. However, government expenditure in education remains among the lowest in the world, at only 1.8% of the total GDP.

The education system is divided into five levels: primary (first to fifth grade), junior secondary (sixth to eighth grade), secondary (ninth and tenth grade), higher secondary (11th and 12th grade), and tertiary which is university level. Primary level students have to pass the [Primary Education Completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_Education_Completion "Primary Education Completion") (PEC) exam to proceed to junior secondary. The junior secondary students then give the [Junior School Certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_School_Certificate "Junior School Certificate") (JSC) exam to get enrolled in ninth grade, while tenth-grade students have to pass the [Secondary School Certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_School_Certificate_(Bangladesh) "Secondary School Certificate (Bangladesh)") (SSC) exam to proceed to eleventh grade. Lastly, students have to pass the [Higher Secondary Certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_Secondary_Certificate "Higher Secondary Certificate") (HSC) exam at grade twelve to apply for higher education or universities.

[Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universities_in_Bangladesh "Universities in Bangladesh") are three general types: public (government-owned and funded by the [University Grants Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Grants_Commission_(Bangladesh) "University Grants Commission (Bangladesh)")), private (privately owned universities) and international (operated and funded by international organisations). The country has 55 public, 115 private and 2 international universities. [National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University,_Bangladesh "National University, Bangladesh") is the [second-largest university in the world by enrolment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_universities_and_university_networks_by_enrollment "List of largest universities and university networks by enrollment"). The [University of Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dhaka "University of Dhaka"), established in 1921, is the oldest public university. [BUET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_University_of_Engineering_and_Technology "Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology") is the premiere university for [engineering education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_education "Engineering education"). The [University of Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chittagong "University of Chittagong"), established in 1966, has the largest campus. [Dhaka College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka_College "Dhaka College"), established in 1841, is among the oldest educational institutes in the Indian subcontinent. Medical education is provided by 39 government, 6 armed force and 68 private [medical colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_medical_colleges_in_Bangladesh "List of medical colleges in Bangladesh"). All medical colleges are affiliated with the [Ministry of Health and Family Welfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Health_and_Family_Welfare_(Bangladesh) "Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (Bangladesh)").

### Health

Bangladesh, by the constitution, guarantees healthcare services as a fundamental right to all of its citizens. The [Ministry of Health and Family Welfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Health_and_Family_Welfare_(Bangladesh) "Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (Bangladesh)") is the largest institutional healthcare provider in Bangladesh, and contains two divisions: [Health Service Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_Service_Division "Health Service Division") and [Medical Education And Family Welfare Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Education_And_Family_Welfare_Division "Medical Education And Family Welfare Division"). However, healthcare facilities in Bangladesh are considered less than adequate, although they have improved as the economy has grown and poverty levels have decreased significantly. Bangladesh faces a severe health workforce crisis, as formally trained providers make up a small percentage of the total health workforce. Significant deficiencies in the treatment practices of village doctors persist, with widespread harmful and inappropriate drug prescribing.

Bangladesh's poor healthcare system suffers from severe underfunding from the government. As of 2021[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bangladesh&action=edit), some 2.36% of total GDP was attributed to healthcare, and domestic general government spending on healthcare was 16.88% of the total budget, while out-of-pocket expenditures made up the vast majority of the total budget, totalling roughly 73%. Domestic private health expenditure was about 75.48% of the total healthcare expenditure. There were only 5.3 doctors per 10,000 people, and about six physicians and six nurses per 1,000 people, while the number of hospital beds is 9 per 1,000. The specialist surgical workforce was only 3 per 100,000 people, and there were about 5 community health workers per 1,000 people.

Roughly 60% of the population had access to [drinking water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drinking_water "Drinking water") in 2022. In 2002, it was estimated that half of the drinking water was polluted with [arsenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenic "Arsenic"), exceeding levels of 10 micrograms per litre. Bangladesh is crippled with one of the worst air qualities in the world, mostly concentrated in the densely populated urban areas, especially the capital [Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka "Dhaka") and its [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Dhaka "Greater Dhaka"). The World Bank estimated that roughly 80,000–90,000 deaths occurred in Bangladesh due to the drastic effects of air pollution in 2019. It was second-leading cause of death and disability, costing the country roughly 4–4.4% of its total GDP.

As of 2022[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bangladesh&action=edit), the overall life expectancy in Bangladesh at birth was 74 years (72 years for males and 76 years for females). It has a comparably high [infant mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality "Infant mortality") rate (24 per 1,000 live births) and [child mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_mortality "Child mortality") rate (29 per 1,000 live births). As of 2020[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bangladesh&action=edit), [maternal mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternal_death "Maternal death") remains high, clocking at 123 per 100,000 live births. Bangladesh is a key source market for [medical tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_tourism "Medical tourism") for various countries, mainly [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_tourism_in_India "Medical tourism in India"), due to its citizens dissatisfaction and distrust over their own healthcare system.

The main causes of death are [coronary artery disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronary_artery_disease "Coronary artery disease"), stroke, and chronic [respiratory disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_disease "Respiratory disease"); comprising 62% and 60% of all adult male and female deaths, respectively. [Malnutrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malnutrition "Malnutrition") is a major and persistent problem in Bangladesh, mainly affecting the rural regions, more than half of the population suffers from it. Severe acute malnutrition affects 450,000 children, while nearly 2 million children have moderate acute malnutrition. For children under the age of five, 52% are affected by [anemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anemia "Anemia"), 41% are [stunted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stunted_growth "Stunted growth"), 16% are [wasted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasting "Wasting"), and 36% are [underweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underweight "Underweight"). A quarter of women are underweight and around 15% have short stature, while over half also suffer from anemia. Bangladesh was ranked 84th out of the 127 countries listed in the 2024 [Global Hunger Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Hunger_Index "Global Hunger Index").

## Culture

### Holidays and festivals

Traditional festivals include [Pahela Baishakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahela_Baishakh "Pahela Baishakh") (Bengali New Year), which is the major festival of Bengali culture, with widespread festivities. [Pohela Falgun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pohela_Falgun "Pohela Falgun") coincides with [Valentine's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentine%27s_Day_in_Bangladesh "Valentine's Day in Bangladesh"), and is celebrated with a display of music, dance and other cultural activities. Other festivals include [Nabonno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabanna "Nabanna") and Poush Parbon, which celebrate new harvests of crops. [Shakrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakrain "Shakrain") is an annual celebration, observed by flying [kites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kite "Kite"), occurring at the end of [Poush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poush "Poush"), the ninth month of the [Bengali calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_calendar "Bengali calendar"). The festival coincides with [Makar Sankranti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makar_Sankranti "Makar Sankranti") celebrated in India and Nepal.

Among religious festivals, the two biggest festivals of the Muslim majority are [Eid al-Fitr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eid_al-Fitr "Eid al-Fitr"), which marks the end of the month of [Ramadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan "Ramadan")—and [Eid al-Adha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eid_al-Adha "Eid al-Adha"), which is the festival of [sacrifice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacrifice#Islam "Sacrifice"). Both Eids are associated with the longest periods of [national holidays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Bangladesh "Public holidays in Bangladesh"). Other Muslim festivals include [Mawlid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mawlid "Mawlid") (Eid-e-Milad Un Nabi), [Ashura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashura "Ashura") on the tenth day of [Muharram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muharram "Muharram"), [Chaand Raat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaand_Raat "Chaand Raat"), and [Shab-e-Barat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shab-e-Barat "Shab-e-Barat"), [Shab-e-Meraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shab-e-Meraj "Shab-e-Meraj"), [Shab-e-Qadr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_of_Power "Night of Power") during Ramadan and [Bishwa Ijtema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishwa_Ijtema "Bishwa Ijtema"). The most celebrated Hindu festival is [Durga Puja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Puja "Durga Puja"). Other major Hindu festivals include [Krishna Janmashtami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna_Janmashtami "Krishna Janmashtami"), [Kali Puja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kali_Puja "Kali Puja"), [Saraswati Puja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraswati_Puja "Saraswati Puja"), and [Ratha Yatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratha_Yatra "Ratha Yatra"). The biggest festival of the Buddhists across the country is [Buddha Purnima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesak "Vesak"), which marks the birth of [Gautama Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautama_Buddha "Gautama Buddha"). Among Christians, Christmas, known as [Boro Din](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boro_Din "Boro Din"), and [Easter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter "Easter") are the most widely celebrated.

Patriotic national festivals include the [Language Movement Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_Movement_Day "Language Movement Day"), which is celebrated on 21 February in remembrance of the martyrs of the 1952 [Bengali language movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language_movement "Bengali language movement"). It was declared as [International Mother Language Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mother_Language_Day "International Mother Language Day") by [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO") in 1999. [Independence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_(Bangladesh) "Independence Day (Bangladesh)") is celebrated on 26 March to commemorate the [proclamation of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_Bangladeshi_Independence "Proclamation of Bangladeshi Independence") from Pakistan. [Victory Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_Day_(Bangladesh) "Victory Day (Bangladesh)") is celebrated on 16 December to celebrate the victory in the [Bangladesh Liberation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War "Bangladesh Liberation War"). Public gatherings are observed at the [Shaheed Minar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaheed_Minar,_Dhaka "Shaheed Minar, Dhaka") and [National Martyrs' Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Martyrs%27_Memorial "National Martyrs' Memorial") during the three latter festivals to pay homage to the fallen martyrs.

### Literature

Bengali literature forms an important part of Bengali culture. The [Charyapada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charyapada "Charyapada") poems dating back to the 10th to 12th centuries are the oldest extant examples of the Bengali language. During the [Bengal Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Sultanate "Bengal Sultanate"), medieval Bengali writers were influenced by Arabic and [Persian literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_literature "Persian literature"). Milestones of the medieval age include the [Mangal-Kāvyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangal-K%C4%81vya "Mangal-Kāvya"). The [Vaishnava Padavali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishnava_Padavali "Vaishnava Padavali") movement was led by writers such as [Vidyapati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidyapati "Vidyapati"), [Chandidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandidas "Chandidas"), [Govindadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Govindadas "Govindadas") and [Balarama Dasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balarama_Dasa "Balarama Dasa"). *[Shreekrishna Kirtana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreekrishna_Kirtana "Shreekrishna Kirtana")* written by Chandidas marked a particular height of poetic achievement since the Charyapadas. Other important works include [Krittibas Ojha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krittibas_Ojha "Krittibas Ojha")'s translation of the *[Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana "Ramayana")*, [Kashiram Das](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashiram_Das "Kashiram Das")' translation of the *[Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata "Mahabharata")*, and [Maladhar Basu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maladhar_Basu "Maladhar Basu")'s translation of the *[Bhagavata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhagavata "Bhagavata")*. Writers such as [Bipradas Pipilai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipradas_Pipilai "Bipradas Pipilai"), [Vijay Gupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijay_Gupta_(poet) "Vijay Gupta (poet)"), [Shah Muhammad Sagir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Muhammad_Sagir "Shah Muhammad Sagir"), [Zainuddin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zainuddin_(poet) "Zainuddin (poet)") and [Abdul Hakim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Hakim_(poet) "Abdul Hakim (poet)") were important figures. [Alaol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaol "Alaol"), considered a [bard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bard "Bard"), is a prolific poet of medieval period.

The [Bengal Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Renaissance "Bengal Renaissance") from the late 18th century to the early 20th century had a profound effect on modern Bengali literature. [Michael Madhusudan Dutt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Madhusudan_Dutt "Michael Madhusudan Dutt") invented the [blank verse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blank_verse "Blank verse") in Bengali literature. [Mir Mosharraf Hossain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir_Mosharraf_Hossain "Mir Mosharraf Hossain") was the first prominent Bengali Muslim writer. [Lalon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalon "Lalon"), a [fakir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fakir "Fakir") practising [Sufism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism "Sufism") and [sādhanā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C4%81dhan%C4%81 "Sādhanā") influenced the [bauls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baul "Baul"). [Sarat Chandra Chattopadhyay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarat_Chandra_Chattopadhyay "Sarat Chandra Chattopadhyay") wrote about the characteristics of the Bengali society.

[Rabindranath Tagore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabindranath_Tagore "Rabindranath Tagore") was the first Asian and non-European laureate of the [Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "Nobel Prize in Literature"). [Kazi Nazrul Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazi_Nazrul_Islam "Kazi Nazrul Islam") was a revolutionary poet who espoused political rebellion against colonialism and fascism. [Jibanananda Das](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jibanananda_Das "Jibanananda Das") was the most recognised Bengali poet after Tagore and Nazrul. [Begum Rokeya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Begum_Rokeya "Begum Rokeya") is regarded as the pioneer feminist writer of Bangladesh. [Syed Mujtaba Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syed_Mujtaba_Ali "Syed Mujtaba Ali") is noted for his [cosmopolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmopolitanism "Cosmopolitanism") views. [Jasimuddin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasimuddin "Jasimuddin") was a renowned pastoral poet, popularly called *Palli Kabi* (folk poet). [Farrukh Ahmad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farrukh_Ahmad "Farrukh Ahmad") is considered the poet of the "Islamic Renaissance". [Syed Waliullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syed_Waliullah "Syed Waliullah") was a notable novelist.

[Shamsur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamsur_Rahman_(poet) "Shamsur Rahman (poet)") and [Al Mahmud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Mahmud "Al Mahmud") are considered two of the greatest Bangladeshi poets to have emerged in the late 20th century. [Ahmed Sofa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Sofa "Ahmed Sofa") is regarded as the most important intellectual in the post-independence era. [Sufia Kamal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufia_Kamal "Sufia Kamal") was a major feminist writer. [Humayun Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humayun_Ahmed "Humayun Ahmed") was the most popular author in post-Independence Bangladesh. [Shahidul Zahir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahidul_Zahir "Shahidul Zahir") was widely acclaimed for his usage of [magical realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magical_realism "Magical realism"). Other major writers include [Akhteruzzaman Elias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhteruzzaman_Elias "Akhteruzzaman Elias"), [Shawkat Osman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawkat_Osman "Shawkat Osman") and [Syed Shamsul Haq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syed_Shamsul_Haq "Syed Shamsul Haq"). [Selina Hossain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selina_Hossain "Selina Hossain") is a prolific female author in the modern era. [Muhammad Zafar Iqbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Zafar_Iqbal "Muhammad Zafar Iqbal") is a pioneer science fiction writer. [Anisul Hoque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anisul_Hoque "Anisul Hoque") is a popular contemporary literary figure. The annual [Ekushey Book Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekushey_Book_Fair "Ekushey Book Fair") and [Dhaka Lit Fest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka_Lit_Fest "Dhaka Lit Fest"), organised by the [Bangla Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangla_Academy "Bangla Academy"), are among the largest literary festivals in South Asia.

### Architecture

The architecture of Bangladesh is intertwined with that of the [Bengal region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Bengal "Architecture of Bengal") and the broader [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_India "Architecture of India"). It is influenced by the country's [culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Bangladesh "Culture of Bangladesh"), [religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Bangladesh "Religion in Bangladesh") and [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Bangladesh "History of Bangladesh"). Hindu and Buddhist architectural remnants have been found in [Mahasthangarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahasthangarh "Mahasthangarh"), which dates back to the 3rd century BCE. [Nandipada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nandipada "Nandipada") and [Swastika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swastika "Swastika") symbols have been found on stone querns in the [Wari-Bateshwar ruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wari-Bateshwar_ruins "Wari-Bateshwar ruins"), which indicate the presence of [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism") in the area during the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age "Iron Age")—from 400 to 100 BCE. The [Somapura Mahavihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somapura_Mahavihara "Somapura Mahavihara") built under the rule of the Buddhist [Pala Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pala_Empire "Pala Empire") in the 8th century is a notable example of the pre-Islamic era. Other Buddhist [vihāras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vih%C4%81ra "Vihāra") include [Shalban Bihar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalban_Bihar "Shalban Bihar") in [Mainamati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainamati "Mainamati") and [Bikrampur Vihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bikrampur_Vihara "Bikrampur Vihara") in [Bikrampur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bikrampur "Bikrampur"). Recent excavations have also uncovered new evidence of pre-Islamic smaller temples which served the Hindu, Buddhist and [Jain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism "Jainism") populations of the area. [Indo-Islamic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Islamic_architecture "Indo-Islamic architecture") can be seen from the 13th century, especially in the distinctive mosque architecture of the [Bengal Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Sultanate "Bengal Sultanate"), an example being the [Sixty Dome Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixty_Dome_Mosque "Sixty Dome Mosque") among others in the [Mosque City of Bagerhat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosque_City_of_Bagerhat "Mosque City of Bagerhat")—which is a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_Bangladesh "List of World Heritage Sites in Bangladesh").

[Mughal Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Bengal "Mughal Bengal") saw the spread of [Mughal architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_architecture "Mughal architecture") in the region. Examples in Dhaka include the [Bara Katra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bara_Katra "Bara Katra") and [Choto Katra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choto_Katra "Choto Katra") in Old Dhaka, the [Sat Gambuj Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sat_Gambuj_Mosque "Sat Gambuj Mosque") in [Mohammadpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammadpur_Thana "Mohammadpur Thana") and the [Musa Khan Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musa_Khan_Mosque "Musa Khan Mosque") in [Curzon Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curzon_Hall "Curzon Hall"). Notable Mughal-era forts include the [Lalbagh Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalbagh_Fort "Lalbagh Fort") in [Old Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Dhaka "Old Dhaka"), the [Idrakpur Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idrakpur_Fort "Idrakpur Fort") in [Munshiganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munshiganj "Munshiganj")—and the [Hajiganj Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajiganj_Fort "Hajiganj Fort") and the [Sonakanda Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonakanda_Fort "Sonakanda Fort") in [Narayanganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narayanganj "Narayanganj"), respectively. The [Kantajew Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kantajew_Temple "Kantajew Temple") and [Dhakeshwari Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhakeshwari_Temple "Dhakeshwari Temple") are examples of late medieval [Hindu temple architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_temple_architecture "Hindu temple architecture").
Bengali [vernacular architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernacular_architecture "Vernacular architecture") is noted for pioneering the [bungalow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bungalow "Bungalow"). [Panam Nagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panam_Nagar "Panam Nagar") in [Sonargaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonargaon "Sonargaon") exhibits architectural influence from the Sultanate, Mughal, British and hybrid colonial traditions. [Indo-Saracenic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Saracenic_architecture "Indo-Saracenic architecture") flourished during the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj"), examples include the [Curzon Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curzon_Hall "Curzon Hall") of the [University of Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dhaka "University of Dhaka"), the [Chittagong Court Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Court_Building "Chittagong Court Building"), [Rangpur Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangpur_Town_Hall "Rangpur Town Hall") and [Rajshahi College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajshahi_College "Rajshahi College"). The [zamindar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamindar "Zamindar") gentry built many palaces in the latter style, including the [Ahsan Manzil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahsan_Manzil "Ahsan Manzil"), the [Baliati Zamnidar Bari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baliati_Zamindari "Baliati Zamindari"), the [Tajhat Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajhat_Palace "Tajhat Palace"), the [Rose Garden Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Garden_Palace "Rose Garden Palace"), the [Dighapatia Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttara_Gonobhaban "Uttara Gonobhaban"), the [Puthia Rajbari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puthia_Rajbari "Puthia Rajbari"), [Natore Rajbari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natore_Rajbari "Natore Rajbari") and the [Mohera Zamindar Bari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohera_Zamindar_Bari "Mohera Zamindar Bari"). [Muzharul Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzharul_Islam "Muzharul Islam") is considered to be a pioneer of [modernist movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_architecture "Modern architecture") in Bangladesh and South Asia. [Louis Kahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Kahn "Louis Kahn") is a notable foreign architect who designed the [National Parliament Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jatiya_Sangsad_Bhaban "Jatiya Sangsad Bhaban") in [Sher-e-Bangla Nagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sher-e-Bangla_Nagar "Sher-e-Bangla Nagar").

### Visual arts, crafts and clothing

The recorded history of art in Bangladesh can be traced to the 3rd century BCE, when [terracotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terracotta "Terracotta") sculptures were made in the region. In classical antiquity, notable sculptural [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_art "Hindu art"), [Jain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain_art "Jain art"), and [Buddhist art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_art "Buddhist art") developed in the [Pala Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pala_Empire "Pala Empire") and the [Sena dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sena_dynasty "Sena dynasty"). The [Bengal Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Sultanate "Bengal Sultanate") saw [Islamic art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_art "Islamic art") evolve since the 14th century. During the Mughal rule, [Jamdani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamdani "Jamdani"), a fine-muslin textile, was woven with Persian [motifs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motif_(textile_arts) "Motif (textile arts)") in Dhaka. It was classified by UNESCO as an [Intangible cultural heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intangible_cultural_heritage "Intangible cultural heritage") in 2013. Bangladesh also produces the [Rajshahi silk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajshahi_silk "Rajshahi silk"), a fine silk noted for its softness and use in patterned textiles. [Ivory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory "Ivory"), [brass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brassware_industry_in_Bangladesh "Brassware industry in Bangladesh") and [pottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pottery_of_Bangladesh "Pottery of Bangladesh") have deep roots in Bangladeshi culture. The [Nakshi Kantha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakshi_Kantha "Nakshi Kantha"), a centuries-old [embroidery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embroidery "Embroidery") tradition for [quilts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quilt "Quilt") in Bengal, is made throughout Bangladesh.
The [modern art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_art "Modern art") movement in Bangladesh took shape in post-independence [East Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bengal "East Bengal"), especially with the pioneering works of [Zainul Abedin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zainul_Abedin "Zainul Abedin"). Other leading painters include [SM Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SM_Sultan "SM Sultan"), [Mohammad Kibria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Kibria "Mohammad Kibria"), [Safiuddin Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safiuddin_Ahmed "Safiuddin Ahmed"), [Shahabuddin Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahabuddin_Ahmed_(artist) "Shahabuddin Ahmed (artist)"), [Kanak Chanpa Chakma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanak_Chanpa_Chakma "Kanak Chanpa Chakma"), [Qayyum Chowdhury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qayyum_Chowdhury "Qayyum Chowdhury"), [Rashid Choudhury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashid_Choudhury "Rashid Choudhury"), [Quamrul Hassan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quamrul_Hassan "Quamrul Hassan"), [Rafiqun Nabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafiqun_Nabi "Rafiqun Nabi") and [Syed Jahangir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syed_Jahangir "Syed Jahangir").

[Novera Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novera_Ahmed "Novera Ahmed") is the pioneer of modernist sculpture in Bangladesh. Other eminent sculptors include [Nitun Kundu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitun_Kundu "Nitun Kundu"), [Syed Abdullah Khalid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syed_Abdullah_Khalid "Syed Abdullah Khalid"), [Hamiduzzaman Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiduzzaman_Khan "Hamiduzzaman Khan"), [Shamim Sikder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamim_Sikder "Shamim Sikder"), [Ferdousi Priyabhashini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdousi_Priyabhashini "Ferdousi Priyabhashini") and [Abdur Razzaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdur_Razzaque_(artist) "Abdur Razzaque (artist)"). The annual [Mangal Shobhajatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangal_Shobhajatra "Mangal Shobhajatra") (Bengali New Year parade) organised by the [Faculty of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faculty_of_Fine_Arts,_University_of_Dhaka "Faculty of Fine Arts, University of Dhaka") of the University of Dhaka on [Pohela Boishakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pohela_Boishakh "Pohela Boishakh") was enlisted as an Intangible cultural heritage by UNESCO in 2016. Photography as a form of art has seen rapid growth in the 21st century. [Chobi Mela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chobi_Mela_International_Photography_Festival "Chobi Mela International Photography Festival"), held biennially, is considered the largest photography festival in Asia.

[Lungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lungi "Lungi") is the most common informal clothing for men, while [kurta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurta "Kurta") (*panjabi*) and [pajama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pajama "Pajama") are worn by men on festivals and holidays. Domestically tailored [suits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suit_(clothing) "Suit (clothing)"), [neckties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neckties "Neckties") and [pants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trouser "Trouser") are customarily worn by men at formal events, and the traditional [sherwani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherwani "Sherwani") and [churidar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churidar "Churidar") are worn along with the [turban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turban "Turban") in weddings. Women commonly wear the [shalwar kameez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalwar_kameez "Shalwar kameez") accompanied by [orna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dupatta "Dupatta"); while [sari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sari "Sari") is worn on more formal events. Some women follow [Islamic clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_clothing "Islamic clothing").

### Performing arts

[Theatre in Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_in_Bangladesh "Theatre in Bangladesh") includes various forms with a history dating back to the 4th century CE. It includes narrative forms, song and dance forms, supra-personae forms, performances with scroll paintings, puppet theatre and processional forms. The [Jatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jatra_(theatre) "Jatra (theatre)") is the most popular form of Bengali folk theatre. Apart from the various forms of [Indian classical dances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_classical_dance "Indian classical dance"), including the *[Kathakali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathakali "Kathakali")*, *[Bharatanatyam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatanatyam "Bharatanatyam")*, [Odissi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odissi "Odissi") and [Manipuri dances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipuri_dance "Manipuri dance")—native dance traditions that developed across the country.

[Music of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Bangladesh "Music of Bangladesh") can be classed into classical, light-classical, [devotional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devotional_music "Devotional music"), and [popular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_music "Popular music"). Classical music in Bangladesh is represented by the common forms of devotional music across the Indian subcontinent, such as the [Hindustani classical music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustani_classical_music "Hindustani classical music") genre [dhrupad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhrupad "Dhrupad") and [khayal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khayal "Khayal"). Other major forms include [qawwali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qawwali "Qawwali") and [kirtan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirtan "Kirtan"). [Rabindra Sangeet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabindra_Sangeet "Rabindra Sangeet") and [Nazrul Sangeet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazrul_geeti "Nazrul geeti") retain their popularity. Native [folk music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") features the [baul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baul "Baul") mystical tradition, which was popularised by [Lalon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalon "Lalon") in the 18th century, and is listed by UNESCO as a [Masterpiece of Intangible Cultural Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masterpiece_of_the_Oral_and_Intangible_Heritage_of_Humanity "Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity"). Other native lyric-based forms of folk music include [bhatiali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhatiali "Bhatiali"), [bhawaiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhawaiya "Bhawaiya"), [dhamail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhamail "Dhamail"), [kavigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavigan "Kavigan"), [jarigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarigan "Jarigan"), [sari gan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sari_gan "Sari gan"), marfati, and [gombhira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gombhira "Gombhira").

Folk music is accompanied by instruments such as the [ektara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ektara "Ektara"), [dotara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dotara "Dotara"), [dhol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhol "Dhol"), [bansuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bansuri "Bansuri") (a type of flute), mandira, [khanjani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanjani "Khanjani"), [sarinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarinda_(instrument) "Sarinda (instrument)"), [khamak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khamak "Khamak"), [dugdugi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duggi_(drum) "Duggi (drum)"), [juri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jori_(instrument) "Jori (instrument)"), jhunjhuni and [majira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taal_(instrument) "Taal (instrument)") (a type of [cymbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cymbal "Cymbal")). Bangladesh has a rich tradition of [Indian classical music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_classical_music "Indian classical music"), which uses instruments like the [sitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitar "Sitar"), tabla, [sarod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarod "Sarod"), and [santoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santoor "Santoor"). Musical organisations and schools such as the [Bangladesh Shilpakala Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Shilpakala_Academy "Bangladesh Shilpakala Academy") and [Chhayanaut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhayanaut "Chhayanaut") have helped preserve the traditions of Bengali folk music.

[Sabina Yasmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabina_Yasmin "Sabina Yasmin") and [Runa Laila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runa_Laila "Runa Laila") are considered two of the greatest female playback singers in the country. [Andrew Kishore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Kishore "Andrew Kishore"), another leading playback singer, is considered the "King of Playback". [Azam Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azam_Khan_(singer) "Azam Khan (singer)"), nicknamed the "Pop Samrat" and the "Rock Guru", is a founding figure of [Bangladeshi rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshi_rock "Bangladeshi rock"). Musicians such as [Ayub Bachchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayub_Bachchu "Ayub Bachchu") and [James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_(musician) "James (musician)") have also gained nationwide popularity. [Shayan Chowdhury Arnob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shayan_Chowdhury_Arnob "Shayan Chowdhury Arnob") has been an influential figure in [indie rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_rock "Indie rock"). Popular pop singers in the 21st century include [Habib Wahid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habib_Wahid "Habib Wahid") and [Tahsan Rahman Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahsan_Rahman_Khan "Tahsan Rahman Khan"). Influential [heavy metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metal_music "Heavy metal music") include [Artcell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artcell "Artcell") and [Warfaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warfaze "Warfaze").

### Media and cinema

The history of press in Bangladesh dates back to 1860, when the first printing press was established in Dhaka. Bangladesh has a diverse media landscape, including commercial and state-owned outlets. Prominent news agencies in Bangladesh include [Bangladesh Sangbad Sangstha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Sangbad_Sangstha "Bangladesh Sangbad Sangstha") (BSS) and [Bdnews24.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bdnews24.com "Bdnews24.com"). Television is the most popular form of media consumption. [Bangladesh Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Television "Bangladesh Television") (BTV) is the sole state-owned television network with nationwide coverage. Private television networks include [ATN Bangla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATN_Bangla "ATN Bangla"), [Channel I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_I "Channel I"), [NTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NTV_(Bangladeshi_TV_channel) "NTV (Bangladeshi TV channel)"), [RTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTV_(Bangladeshi_TV_channel) "RTV (Bangladeshi TV channel)"), [Ekushey TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekushey_TV "Ekushey TV"), [Ekattor TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekattor_TV "Ekattor TV"), [Jamuna TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamuna_TV "Jamuna TV") and [Somoy TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somoy_TV "Somoy TV"). Print media is the second-most widely consumed, and newspapers are privately owned and outspoken, including [The Daily Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Star_(Bangladesh) "The Daily Star (Bangladesh)"), [Dhaka Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka_Tribune "Dhaka Tribune"), [The Financial Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Financial_Express_(Bangladesh) "The Financial Express (Bangladesh)"), [Bangladesh Pratidin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Pratidin "Bangladesh Pratidin"), [Kaler Kantho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaler_Kantho "Kaler Kantho"), [Prothom Alo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prothom_Alo "Prothom Alo"), [The Daily Ittefaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Ittefaq "The Daily Ittefaq") and [Jugantor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jugantor "Jugantor").

[Bangladesh Betar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Betar "Bangladesh Betar") is the lone state-run radio service. [Radio Foorti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Foorti "Radio Foorti"), [Radio Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Today "Radio Today"), [Radio Aamar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Aamar "Radio Aamar") and ABC Radio were popular privately owned radio stations; popularity of radio has declined significantly. Popular foreign media include [BBC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_News "BBC News") ([BBC Bangla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Bangla "BBC Bangla")), [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN "CNN"), [VOA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VOA "VOA") and [Al Jazeera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera "Al Jazeera"). [Indian television drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_television_drama "Indian television drama") has a substantial presence on Bangladeshi satellite television. [Freedom of the press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_the_press_in_Bangladesh "Freedom of the press in Bangladesh") remains a major concern due to government attempts at censorship and the harassment of journalists. Bangladesh ranked 152nd out of the 180 countries listed in the 2026 [World Press Freedom Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Press_Freedom_Index "World Press Freedom Index"), among the lowest rankings in the world.
The [cinema of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_Bangladesh "Cinema of Bangladesh") dates back to a screening of a [bioscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movie_camera "Movie camera") in 1898. The [Nawabs of Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawab_of_Dhaka "Nawab of Dhaka") patronised the production of several [silent films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_film "Silent film") from the 1900s. Picture House, the first permanent cinema in Dhaka, began its operation during the year between 1913 and 1914. *[Sukumari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukumari_(film) "Sukumari (film)")* (The Good Girl), released in 1929, was the first film produced in Bangladesh. *[The Last Kiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Kiss_(1931_film) "The Last Kiss (1931 film)")*, the first full-length feature film, was released in 1931. By 1947, a total of 80 cinemas were listed. The first Bengali-language film in East Pakistan, *[Mukh O Mukhosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukh_O_Mukhosh "Mukh O Mukhosh")* (Face and Mask), was released in 1956. *Akash ar Mati* (Sky and Earth), released in 1959, was the second film as such.

The [Bangladesh Film Development Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Film_Development_Corporation "Bangladesh Film Development Corporation") was founded in Dhaka as the East Pakistan Film Development Corporation in 1957–1958, as a full-service film production studio. [Zahir Raihan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zahir_Raihan "Zahir Raihan") made various influential films throughout the period, notably *[Kokhono Asheni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokhono_Asheni "Kokhono Asheni")* in 1961, *Shangam* in 1964 (the first [colour film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_print_film "Color print film") in Pakistan), and *Jibon Theke Neowa* in 1970. The film industry in Dhaka positioned itself as the base for a Bengali Muslim cinema from the 1960s onward. The first movie post-Independence, *[Ora Egaro Jon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ora_Egaro_Jon "Ora Egaro Jon")*, was directed by [Chashi Nazrul Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chashi_Nazrul_Islam "Chashi Nazrul Islam") and released in 1972. At the industry's peak, about 80 movies were produced each year between 1996 and 2003, a number which has constantly declined thereafter. Prominent directors include [Khan Ataur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_Ataur_Rahman "Khan Ataur Rahman"), [Alamgir Kabir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamgir_Kabir_(film_maker) "Alamgir Kabir (film maker)"), [Amjad Hossain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amjad_Hossain "Amjad Hossain"), [Humayun Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humayun_Ahmed "Humayun Ahmed"), [Morshedul Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morshedul_Islam "Morshedul Islam"), [Tanvir Mokammel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanvir_Mokammel "Tanvir Mokammel"), [Tareque Masud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tareque_Masud "Tareque Masud"), [Salahuddin Lavlu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salahuddin_Lavlu "Salahuddin Lavlu") and [Enamul Karim Nirjhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enamul_Karim_Nirjhar "Enamul Karim Nirjhar"). Tareque Masud was honoured by [FIPRESCI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIPRESCI "FIPRESCI") at the 2002 [Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes_Film_Festival "Cannes Film Festival") for his film *[Matir Moina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matir_Moina "Matir Moina")* (The Clay Bird). [Film societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_society "Film society") have contributed to the development of cinema in Bangladesh.

### Cuisine

Bangladeshi cuisine has been shaped by the nation's geographic location, climate, and history. The staple dish is [white rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_rice "White rice"), which along with fish, forms the culinary base. Varieties of [leaf vegetables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaf_vegetable "Leaf vegetable"), potatoes, [gourds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gourd "Gourd") and [lentils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lentil "Lentil") ([dal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dal "Dal")) also play an important role. [Curries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curry "Curry") of beef, [mutton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutton "Mutton"), chicken and duck are commonly consumed, along with multiple types of [bhortas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhurta "Bhurta") (mashed vegetables), *bhajis* ([stir fried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stir_frying "Stir frying") vegetables) and [tarkaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarkari "Tarkari") (curried vegetables). Mughal-influenced dishes include [kormas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korma "Korma"), kalias, [biryanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biryani "Biryani"), [pulaos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilaf "Pilaf"), [teharis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahri_(dish) "Tahri (dish)") and [khichuris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khichuri "Khichuri").
Among the various used spices, [turmeric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turmeric "Turmeric"), [fenugreek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenugreek "Fenugreek"), [nigella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigella "Nigella"), [coriander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coriander "Coriander"), [anise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anise "Anise"), [cardamom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardamom "Cardamom") and [chili powder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chili_powder "Chili powder") are widely used; a famous spice mix is the [panch phoron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panch_phoron "Panch phoron"). Condiments and herbs used include [red onions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_onion "Red onion"), [green chillies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chili_pepper "Chili pepper"), garlic, [ginger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginger "Ginger"), [cilantro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilantro "Cilantro"), and [mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mentha "Mentha"). [Coconut milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut_milk "Coconut milk"), [mustard paste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustard_(condiment) "Mustard (condiment)"), [mustard seeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustard_seeds "Mustard seeds"), [mustard oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustard_oil "Mustard oil"), [ghee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghee "Ghee"), [achars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_pickle "South Asian pickle") and [chutneys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chutney "Chutney") are also widely used in the cuisine.

Fish is the main source of protein, owing to the country's riverine geography, and it is often eaten with its [roe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roe "Roe"). The [hilsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilsa "Hilsa") is the national fish and is widely consumed. Other highly consumed fish include [rohu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohu "Rohu"), [pangas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasius_pangasius "Pangasius pangasius"), and [tilapia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilapia "Tilapia"). [Lobsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobster "Lobster"), [shrimps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrimp "Shrimp") and [dried fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dried_fish "Dried fish") (*shutki*) also play an important role in local cuisine. In Chittagong, dishes involving beef include [kala bhuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kala_bhuna "Kala bhuna") and [mezban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mezban "Mezban"), the latter being a traditionally popular feast, featuring the serving of *mezbani gosht*, a hot and spicy beef curry. In Sylhet, the *[shatkora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shatkora "Shatkora")* lemons are used to marinate dishes, a notable example is [beef hatkora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beef_Hatkhora "Beef Hatkhora"). Among the tribal communities in the [Chittagong Hill Tracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Hill_Tracts "Chittagong Hill Tracts"), cooking with [bamboo shoots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_shoot "Bamboo shoot") is popular. Khulna is known for using *chui jhal* ([piper chaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piper_chaba "Piper chaba")) in its meat-based dishes. *Kalai ruti*, a type of rustic [flatbread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flatbread "Flatbread"), is widely consumed in [Chapai Nawabganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapai_Nawabganj "Chapai Nawabganj") of Rajshahi.

Bangladesh has a wide range of desserts, including [sweets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweets "Sweets") such as the *[roshogolla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasgulla "Rasgulla")*, *[roshmalai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasmalai "Rasmalai")*, *[chomchom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chomchom "Chomchom")*, *[sondesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandesh_(confectionery) "Sandesh (confectionery)")*, *[mishti doi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mishti_doi "Mishti doi")* and *[kalojaam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulab_jamun "Gulab jamun")*, and *[jilapi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalebi "Jalebi")*. [Pithas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitha "Pitha") are traditional boiled desserts made with rice or fruits. [Halwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halwa "Halwa"), [shemai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shemai "Shemai") and [falooda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falooda "Falooda"), the latter two variations of [vermicelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vermicelli "Vermicelli"), are popular desserts during religious festivities. [Ruti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roti "Roti"), [naan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naan "Naan"), [paratha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paratha "Paratha"), [luchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luchi "Luchi") and [bakarkhani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakarkhani "Bakarkhani") are the main local breads. Hot [milk tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk_tea "Milk tea") is the most commonly consumed beverage in the country, being at the centre of [group conversations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adda_(South_Asian) "Adda (South Asian)"). [Borhani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borhani "Borhani"), [mattha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattha "Mattha") and [lassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lassi "Lassi") are popular traditionally consumed beverages. [Kebabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kebab "Kebab") are widely popular, particularly [seekh kebab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seekh_kebab "Seekh kebab"), [chapli kebab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapli_kebab "Chapli kebab"), [shami kebab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shami_kebab "Shami kebab"), [chicken tikka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_tikka "Chicken tikka") and [shashlik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashlik "Shashlik"), along with various types of *chaaps*. Popular street foods include [chotpoti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chotpoti "Chotpoti"), [jhal muri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhal_muri "Jhal muri"), *shingara*, [samosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samosa "Samosa") and [fuchka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panipuri "Panipuri").

### Sports

In rural Bangladesh, several [traditional indigenous sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_traditional_games "Bengali traditional games") such as [Kabaddi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabaddi "Kabaddi"), [Boli Khela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boli_Khela "Boli Khela"), [Lathi Khela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lathi_Khela "Lathi Khela") and [Nouka Baich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nouka_Baich "Nouka Baich") remain fairly popular. While Kabaddi is the national sport, [Cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket "Cricket") is widely described as the most popular sport in the country. The [national cricket team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_national_cricket_team "Bangladesh national cricket team") played their first [Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_World_Cup "Cricket World Cup") in 1999 and were granted [Test cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_cricket "Test cricket") status the following year. Bangladesh reached the quarter-final of the [2015 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Cricket_World_Cup "2015 Cricket World Cup"), the semi-final of the [2017 ICC Champions Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_ICC_Champions_Trophy "2017 ICC Champions Trophy") and reached the final of the [Asia Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia_Cup "Asia Cup") 3 times – in 2012, 2016, and 2018. [Shakib Al Hasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakib_Al_Hasan "Shakib Al Hasan") is widely regarded as one of the greatest [all-rounders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-rounder "All-rounder") in the history of the sport. In 2020, the [Bangladesh national under-19 cricket team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_national_under-19_cricket_team "Bangladesh national under-19 cricket team") won the men's [Under-19 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Under-19_Cricket_World_Cup "2020 Under-19 Cricket World Cup"). The Bangladesh national under-19 cricket team also won the [U-19 Asia cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under-19_Men%27s_Asia_Cup "Under-19 Men's Asia Cup") in 2023 and 2024 consecutively. In 2018, the [Bangladesh women's national cricket team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_women%27s_national_cricket_team "Bangladesh women's national cricket team") won the [2018 Women's Twenty20 Asia Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Women%27s_Twenty20_Asia_Cup "2018 Women's Twenty20 Asia Cup").

[Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football "Association football") is the second-most popular sport in Bangladesh, following cricket. The first instance of a national football team was the emergence of the [Shadhin Bangla football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadhin_Bangla_football_team "Shadhin Bangla football team") during the liberation war of 1971. On 25 July 1971, the team's captain, [Zakaria Pintoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakaria_Pintoo "Zakaria Pintoo"), became the first person to hoist the Bangladesh flag on foreign land before their match in neighbouring India. Following independence, the [national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_national_football_team "Bangladesh national football team") made its debut in 1973 and eventually qualified for the [AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Asian_Cup "AFC Asian Cup") (1980), becoming only the second South Asian team to do so. Notable achievements in Bangladeshi football include the [2003 SAFF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_SAFF_Gold_Cup "2003 SAFF Gold Cup") and [1999 South Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1999_South_Asian_Games "Football at the 1999 South Asian Games"). The [Bangladesh women's national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_women%27s_national_football_team "Bangladesh women's national football team") won the [SAFF Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAFF_Women%27s_Championship "SAFF Women's Championship") consecutively in 2022 and 2024.
Bangladesh archers Ety Khatun and Roman Sana won all 10 gold medals in the [archery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archery "Archery") events (individual and team) in the [2019 South Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_South_Asian_Games "2019 South Asian Games"). The [National Sports Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Sports_Council "National Sports Council") regulates 42 sporting federations. [Chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess "Chess") is very popular in Bangladesh. Bangladesh has five grandmasters in chess. Among them, [Niaz Murshed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niaz_Murshed "Niaz Murshed") was the first grandmaster in South Asia. In 2010, mountain climber [Musa Ibrahim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musa_Ibrahim "Musa Ibrahim") became the first Bangladeshi climber to conquer [Mount Everest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Everest "Mount Everest"). [Wasfia Nazreen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasfia_Nazreen "Wasfia Nazreen") is the first Bangladeshi climber to climb the [Seven Summits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Summits "Seven Summits") and the [K2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K2 "K2"). Babar Ali became the first Bangladeshi to climb [Mount Makalu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Makalu "Mount Makalu") in May 2026.
