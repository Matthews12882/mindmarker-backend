# Duncan Sheik

**Duncan Scott Sheik** (November 18, 1969 – September 17, 2026) was an American singer-songwriter and composer. He is known for his 1996 debut single "[Barely Breathing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barely_Breathing "Barely Breathing")", which earned him a [Grammy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award "Grammy Award") nomination for [Best Male Pop Vocal Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Male_Pop_Vocal_Performance "Best Male Pop Vocal Performance"). Sheik composed music for motion pictures and Broadway musicals, winning the 2007 [Tony Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Awards "Tony Awards") for Best Original Score and Best Orchestrations for his work on the musical *[Spring Awakening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_Awakening_(musical) "Spring Awakening (musical)")*.

## Early life

Sheik was born on November 18, 1969, in [Glen Ridge, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glen_Ridge,_New_Jersey "Glen Ridge, New Jersey"). He grew up in [Montclair, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montclair,_New_Jersey "Montclair, New Jersey"). Following his parents' divorce, he split time between his father's house in New Jersey and his mother's home in South Carolina. He was the half-brother of Broadway actress Kacie Sheik. Sheik's [Juilliard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juilliard "Juilliard")-trained grandmother introduced him to the piano, and he later took up the electric guitar. By age 12, he was playing guitar with high school students in a cover band. After graduating from [Phillips Academy, Andover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_Academy "Phillips Academy") in 1988, Sheik studied [semiotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiotics "Semiotics") at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University "Brown University"); while at Brown, he played guitar in a band with fellow Brown student [Lisa Loeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Loeb "Lisa Loeb"). Following his graduation from Brown in 1992, he moved to Los Angeles.

## Career

Early in his musical career, Sheik played guitar for other artists, including [Liz and Lisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liz_and_Lisa "Liz and Lisa") (with [Elizabeth Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Mitchell_(musician) "Elizabeth Mitchell (musician)") and [Lisa Loeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Loeb "Lisa Loeb")). Sheik also played on [His Boy Elroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/His_Boy_Elroy "His Boy Elroy")'s 1993 album through his connections from a fellow Brown alum, [Tracee Ellis Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracee_Ellis_Ross "Tracee Ellis Ross").

### Singer-songwriter

In 1996, Sheik released his self-titled [debut album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duncan_Sheik_(album) "Duncan Sheik (album)"), which was certified gold. The album featured the hit single "[Barely Breathing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barely_Breathing "Barely Breathing")", which peaked at number 16 and remained on the [*Billboard* Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100 "Billboard Hot 100") for 55 consecutive weeks. "Barely Breathing" also enjoyed Top 20 success on [Adult Contemporary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_Contemporary "Adult Contemporary") radio, reached No. 2 on the Adult Top 40 charts, and garnered Sheik a [Grammy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award "Grammy Award") nomination for [Best Male Pop Vocal Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Male_Pop_Vocal_Performance "Best Male Pop Vocal Performance").

In 1998, Sheik recorded "Embraceable You" for *[Red Hot + Rhapsody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Hot_%2B_Rhapsody "Red Hot + Rhapsody")*, a [George Gershwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gershwin "George Gershwin") tribute, and also recorded "Songbird" for another tribute, *Legacy: A Tribute to Fleetwood Mac's Rumours*. Also in 1998, Sheik released *[Humming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humming_(album) "Humming (album)")*, which featured orchestral and string arrangements.

Sheik sang a duet with singer [Howard Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Jones_(British_musician) "Howard Jones (British musician)") on Jones' 2000 single entitled *Someone You Need*.

He released *[Phantom Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_Moon "Phantom Moon")*, a [Nick Drake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Drake "Nick Drake")-influenced album created in collaboration with poet and playwright [Steven Sater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Sater "Steven Sater"), in 2001. The following year, Sheik released *[Daylight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daylight_(Duncan_Sheik_album) "Daylight (Duncan Sheik album)")*, which included the singles "[On a High](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_a_High "On a High")" and "Half-Life". After a four-year recording break, Sheik released *[White Limousine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Limousine "White Limousine")* (2006), which included a DVD allowing listeners to remix and reinterpret songs from the album.

In 2009, he released *[Whisper House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whisper_House "Whisper House")*, a concept album which provided the score for the musical of the same name. The stage musical premiered at [The Old Globe Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Globe_Theatre "Old Globe Theatre") in San Diego in January 2010, after a workshop at [Vassar College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassar_College "Vassar College"), produced by [New York Stage and Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Stage_and_Film "New York Stage and Film") in 2009.

In 2011, Sheik released *[Covers 80s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covers_80s "Covers 80s")*, an album including covers of popular 1980s songs. Concert dates in support of the album were later canceled due to Sheik seeking treatment for alcohol addiction. A remixed version of the album was released the following year. Sheik released the studio album *[Legerdemain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legerdemain_(album) "Legerdemain (album)")* in October 2015.

### Composer

In addition to being a singer-songwriter, Sheik had also composed music for plays, musicals, and movie soundtracks. He composed original music for the 2002 [New York Shakespeare Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Shakespeare_Festival "New York Shakespeare Festival") production of *[Twelfth Night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelfth_Night "Twelfth Night")*.

In 2004, he composed the score for the film *[A Home at the End of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Home_at_the_End_of_the_World_(film) "A Home at the End of the World (film)")*. The *[AllMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllMusic "AllMusic")* reviewer wrote: "...takes the sensitivity and tension of the film's plot and crafts incidental music and new songs that complement the movie well." He also composed the score for the 2005 film *[Through the Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Through_the_Fire_(2005_film) "Through the Fire (2005 film)")* with Pete Miser.

Sheik wrote the music for *[Spring Awakening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_Awakening_(Musical) "Spring Awakening (Musical)")* (2006) in collaboration with [Steven Sater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Sater "Steven Sater"). Written over a period of eight years, the musical, which premiered [off-Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-Broadway "Off-Broadway") during the summer, opened on [Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_(theatre) "Broadway (theatre)") to critical acclaim later in the fall. The musical was based on the controversial German [expressionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expressionism "Expressionism") play *[The Awakening of Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_Awakening_(play) "Spring Awakening (play)")*, written by [Frank Wedekind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Wedekind "Frank Wedekind"). Sheik won the 2006 [Tony Award for Best Orchestrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Orchestrations "Tony Award for Best Orchestrations") for his work on *Spring Awakening*, and he and Sater won a [Tony Award for Best Original Score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Original_Score "Tony Award for Best Original Score"). In addition, *Spring Awakening* won the [Tony Award for Best Musical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Musical "Tony Award for Best Musical") and a [Grammy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award "Grammy Award") for Best Musical Show Album. The guitar that Sheik used to compose songs for *Spring Awakening* was displayed at the [New York Public Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Public_Library "New York Public Library") for the Performing Arts at [Lincoln Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Center "Lincoln Center"). As of 2012, Sheik was composing music for a feature-film adaptation of *Spring Awakening*, an adaptation almost a decade in the making.

In 2012, Sheik wrote *[Alice By Heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_by_Heart "Alice by Heart")*, an adaptation of [Lewis Carroll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Carroll "Lewis Carroll")'s *[Alice's Adventures in Wonderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice%27s_Adventures_in_Wonderland "Alice's Adventures in Wonderland")*, along with collaborator Sater. Directed by [Jessie Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessie_Nelson_(filmmaker) "Jessie Nelson (filmmaker)") with musical direction by Lance Horne, the musical was workshopped at the [Royal National Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_National_Theatre "Royal National Theatre") and commissioned by the [National Theatre Connections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Theatre_Connections "National Theatre Connections").

He wrote the music and lyrics to the 2013 musical adaptation of *[American Psycho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Psycho_(musical) "American Psycho (musical)")*, which opened at the [Almeida Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almeida_Theatre "Almeida Theatre") in London, and was later staged on Broadway in 2016.

In 2013, Sheik wrote the music for the musical adaptation of the novel *[Because of Winn-Dixie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Because_of_Winn-Dixie "Because of Winn-Dixie")*, which premiered at the [Arkansas Repertory Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Repertory_Theatre "Arkansas Repertory Theatre"). *Dixie* was a collaboration with then-director [John Tartaglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tartaglia "John Tartaglia") and [Nell Benjamin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nell_Benjamin "Nell Benjamin"), who wrote the book and lyrics. The musical ran at the [Goodspeed Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodspeed_Opera_House "Goodspeed Opera House") in [East Haddam, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Haddam,_Connecticut "East Haddam, Connecticut") from July 2019 to September 2019, directed by [John Rando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rando "John Rando").

In 2015, Sheik wrote the musical thriller *Noir* with [Kyle Jarrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Jarrow "Kyle Jarrow"). It premiered as part of New York Stage and Film's season in July to August 2015 at the [Powerhouse Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powerhouse_Theater "Powerhouse Theater") at [Vassar College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassar_College "Vassar College"). Inspired by live radio plays and classic film noir, the musical was directed by [Rachel Chavkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Chavkin "Rachel Chavkin").

In 2016, he prepared the music for the [Shakespeare Theatre Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakespeare_Theatre_Company "Shakespeare Theatre Company") (Washington, D.C.) production of *[The Taming of the Shrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Taming_of_the_Shrew "The Taming of the Shrew")*, described in *[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")* as "an assortment of preexisting songs by singer-songwriter Duncan Sheik".

### Other work

Sheik produced singer-songwriter Micah Green's 2000 debut album[*[better source needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")*] as well as his 2012 follow-up.

In 2000, Sheik wrote the foreword to *The Way of Youth: Buddhist Common Sense for Handling Life's Questions*, by [Soka Gakkai International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soka_Gakkai_International "Soka Gakkai International") leader [Daisaku Ikeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisaku_Ikeda "Daisaku Ikeda").

In 2006, Sheik recorded the song "A Purple Trail" for *Other Songs and Dances, Vol. 1*. In 2008, Sheik participated in *[Songs for Tibet: The Art of Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songs_for_Tibet:_The_Art_of_Peace "Songs for Tibet: The Art of Peace")*, an initiative to support [Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet "Tibet") and the 14th [Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalai_Lama "Dalai Lama"),[Tenzin Gyatso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenzin_Gyatso "Tenzin Gyatso").

## Personal life and death

Concert dates in support of Sheik's 2011 album *Covers 80s* were canceled when Sheik sought treatment for [alcoholism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholism "Alcoholism"). In a message to fans on his [Tumblr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumblr "Tumblr") blog, Sheik noted that he had entered a treatment center on the same day his latest album was released and had told his staff, "My record is coming *out* and I'm checking *in*."

Sheik had a daughter with his wife [Nora Ariffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nora_Ariffin "Nora Ariffin"), an actress turned realtor.

He practiced [Nichiren Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nichiren_Buddhism "Nichiren Buddhism") and was a member of the U.S. branch of the worldwide Buddhist association [Soka Gakkai International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soka_Gakkai_International "Soka Gakkai International").

On September 16, 2026, Sheik was reported by his family on his Instagram page to be hospitalized in "critical but stable condition". He died the following day from heart and organ failure leading to [cardiac arrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiac_arrest "Cardiac arrest") in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"), at the age of 56.
