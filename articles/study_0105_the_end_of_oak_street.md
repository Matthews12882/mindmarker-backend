# The End of Oak Street

***The End of Oak Street*** is a 2026 American science fiction [survival film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survival_film "Survival film") written, co-produced, and directed by [David Robert Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Robert_Mitchell "David Robert Mitchell"). It stars [Anne Hathaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hathaway "Anne Hathaway"), [Ewan McGregor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewan_McGregor "Ewan McGregor"), [Maisy Stella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maisy_Stella "Maisy Stella"), and [Christian Convery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Convery "Christian Convery") as a family whose suburban neighborhood has been transported from 1982 into the [age of dinosaurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic "Mesozoic").

*The End of Oak Street* premiered at the [Steven J. Ross Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Museum "Warner Bros. Museum") on August 9, 2026, and was theatrically released in the United States on August 14 by [Warner Bros. Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures "Warner Bros. Pictures"). The film received positive reviews from critics and has grossed $122 million worldwide against a production budget of $80–92 million.

## Plot

On July 10, 1982, aspiring novelist Denise Platt lives with her husband Greg, children Audrey and Brian, and dog Starbuck on Oak Street, in the suburban neighborhood of Flowervale, [Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan "Michigan"). The couple's marriage is strained—Denise privately contemplates divorce, while Greg lies about his work. He has told his family that he does pizza deliveries on the side, but it is actually his only job after being laid off.

The following day, Denise photographs a mysterious plant that suddenly appears in her neighbor's yard. Local librarian Mrs. Valcourt identifies it as *[Pleuromeia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleuromeia "Pleuromeia")*, a type of vegetation which went extinct millions of years ago. A giant pile of animal feces appears in neighbor Mel's yard. Radio broadcasts report increased solar-flare activity. During a storm, a powerful white light suddenly engulfs Flowervale neighborhood, accompanied by a change in [atmospheric pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_pressure "Atmospheric pressure") and a power outage.

The next morning, Denise and Greg search for Starbuck, who went missing in the storm, and discover that Flowervale is surrounded by a [prehistoric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic "Mesozoic") landscape from which [dinosaurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinosaur "Dinosaur") emerge and start hunting and killing people in the neighborhood. Greg and Denise run back to their home while being chased by a vicious *[Coelophysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coelophysis "Coelophysis")*. The family boards up their windows and doors, and Audrey concludes the flashes of light were [wormholes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wormhole "Wormhole") that transported the neighborhood millions of years into the past.

Greg reveals he is aware of Denise's wish to divorce and confesses to losing his job. They witness their neighbor Mel shooting at a flock of *[Pterodactylus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pterodactylus "Pterodactylus")* from his roof with a revolver, before he is violently killed and eaten. Later, Brian and Audrey venture outside after Brian sees Starbuck in their yard. Denise and Greg follow in their car after realizing the children are missing; they crash it while trying to escape from a *[Spinosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinosaurus "Spinosaurus")*, starting a fire. Jeannette Christiansen, Brian's neighborhood crush, joins Brian and Audrey after revealing her parents have been killed. Denise and Greg see a flash of light while searching for the children and follow it to a house containing a wormhole. They refuse to travel through the wormhole without their children, and it disappears. They evade a stampeding herd of dinosaurs, reconcile, and reunite with their children. The family heads back toward their home, but discover that the fire Denise and Greg had started now threatens to engulf the neighborhood.

When an *[Allosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allosaurus "Allosaurus")* attacks the group, Greg is killed while defending the others, leaving Denise and her children devastated. After returning home, the group escapes a *[Titanoboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanoboa "Titanoboa")* and discovers another wormhole, on the roof of Mrs. Valcourt's house. They make their way to the house and observe that the wormhole appears and disappears at regular intervals. Jeannette jumps in and disappears, but Brian misses the opening and falls. An *[Ornithocheirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ornithocheirus "Ornithocheirus")* grabs him, but Starbuck appears and attacks it. The group attempts to shelter inside the house, but are cornered by a group of *[Ornithosuchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ornithosuchus "Ornithosuchus")*; Mrs. Valcourt appears and kills one of the *Ornithosuchus* with a double-barreled shotgun before being mauled by another, giving the group an opportunity to escape back up to the roof. They jump through the wormhole together.

They return to July 11, 1982, and land in a backyard swimming pool, approximately 20 minutes before Flowervale was teleported away. Denise tells the neighborhood's residents to leave the area by calling them from a payphone, while Jeannette runs off to warn her parents. Greg appears after Denise orders a pizza to their location, where he is embraced by her and their children. The powerful white light appears, engulfing the area before them as Flowervale disappears and is replaced by a prehistoric landscape.

Two years later, the military has contained the area while scientists study the prehistoric environment and its wildlife, attempting to investigate the wormholes. Denise's warnings to the disaster's survivors have made her a hero, and her book, titled *The End of Oak Street*, has made her family moderately wealthy. The film ends with the Platts and Christiansens having a barbecue with their neighbors and two identical Jeannettes.

## Production

### Development

In March 2023, an untitled film directed, written and co-produced by [David Robert Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Robert_Mitchell "David Robert Mitchell") was in development at [Warner Bros. Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures "Warner Bros. Pictures"), with [J. J. Abrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._J._Abrams "J. J. Abrams") and Hannah Minghella producing for [Bad Robot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Robot "Bad Robot") and with Matt Jackson and Tommy Harper producing for Jackson Pictures. [Anne Hathaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hathaway "Anne Hathaway") was cast in the lead role.

By February 2024, it was revealed that [Ewan McGregor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewan_McGregor "Ewan McGregor") had been cast, with [Maisy Stella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maisy_Stella "Maisy Stella") and [Christian Convery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Convery "Christian Convery") joining in March. In late March, the title was revealed to be *Flowervale Street*. In April, Jordan Alexa Davis, [P. J. Byrne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._J._Byrne "P. J. Byrne") and [Chris Coy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Coy "Chris Coy") joined the cast.

In January 2026, [Warner Bros. Motion Picture Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Motion_Picture_Group "Warner Bros. Motion Picture Group") co-chairs [Pamela Abdy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamela_Abdy "Pamela Abdy") and [Michael De Luca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_De_Luca "Michael De Luca") revealed that the film would be getting a new title. In March 2026, with the release of the trailer, the official title was announced to be *The End of Oak Street*.

### Filming

[Principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography "Principal photography") began on March 22, 2024, in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London") and [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta "Atlanta"), and wrapped on June 4. Filming on soundstages took place at Electric Owl Studios in [DeKalb County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeKalb_County,_Georgia "DeKalb County, Georgia") while location filming in Atlanta was used to represent Oak Street. The production chose to film on Westwood Avenue SW, Erin Avenue SW, Dover Boulevard SW, Howell Drive SW, and Manford Road SW due to their detached houses, broad lawns and mature trees that were typical of American suburbs during the 1980s when the film is set.

### Post-production

As with the *[Jurassic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic_Park_(franchise) "Jurassic Park (franchise)")* franchise, [Industrial Light & Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Light_%26_Magic "Industrial Light & Magic") became the lead visual effects vendor and handled the creation of the dinosaurs for the film. Jay Cooper served as Production VFX Supervisor.

## Release

### Theatrical

*The End of Oak Street* had its world premiere at the [Steven J. Ross Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Museum "Warner Bros. Museum") on August 9, 2026, and was released theatrically in the United States on August 14 by [Warner Bros. Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures "Warner Bros. Pictures"). It was previously scheduled for May 16, 2025, and March 13, 2026.

### Home media

*The End of Oak Street* released on Digital HD on September 15, 2026. A [DVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD "DVD"), [Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blu-ray "Blu-ray"), and [Ultra HD Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_HD_Blu-ray "Ultra HD Blu-ray") release follows on November 3, 2026.

### Marketing

The film's marketing campaign was criticized on social media for using [generative AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_AI "Generative AI"). In particular, a now-deleted video shared by Warner Bros. featured the dog Starbuck from the film interviewed in the AI-generated dog podcast *Goldie & Frenchie*, owned by DogPack, a social media platform tailored to dog owners.

## Reception

### Box office

As of October 8, 2026[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=The_End_of_Oak_Street&action=edit), *The End of Oak Street* has grossed $54 million in the United States and Canada, and $68 million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $122 million. The film was produced on a budget of $80–92 million. Due to its high production and marketing costs, *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* estimated the film would lose Warner Bros. $25–40 million from its theatrical run.

In the U.S. and Canada, *The End of Oak Street* was released alongside *[Paw Patrol: The Dino Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paw_Patrol:_The_Dino_Movie "Paw Patrol: The Dino Movie")* and *[The Brink of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Brink_of_War "The Brink of War")*, and was projected to gross $20–25 million from 3,446 theaters in its opening weekend. The film made $7.9 million (including $2 million from Thursday previews) on its first day. It went on to debut to $21 million, finishing in third at the box office.

### Critical response

On the [review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator "Review aggregator") website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes"), 86% of 345 critics' reviews are positive, with an average rating of 7.0/10. The website's consensus reads: "Tapping into the primal pleasures of the classic creature feature, *The End of Oak Street* is pulpy good fun carried off with a light touch and a sharp bite." [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), which uses a [weighted average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_arithmetic_mean "Weighted arithmetic mean"), assigned the film a score of 69 out of 100, based on 52 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews. Audiences polled by [CinemaScore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CinemaScore "CinemaScore") gave the film an average grade of "B" on an A+ to F scale.

Zachary Lee of *[RogerEbert.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RogerEbert.com "RogerEbert.com")* awarded *The End of Oak Street* four out of four, praising its combination of family drama, apocalyptic imagery, and dinosaur horror. Lee highlighted the film's themes of family secrets and parental sacrifice, while also calling it "the most thrilling dinosaur movie in ages." *[Associated Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_Press "Associated Press")*' Jake Coyle praised the film's mixture of science fiction, horror and 1980s adventure-film influences, as well as the performances of Anne Hathaway and Ewan McGregor. Clarisse Loughrey of *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent "The Independent")* was similarly enthusiastic, describing the film as largely better than the first *[Jurassic World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic_World "Jurassic World")* and *[Jurassic World Dominion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic_World_Dominion "Jurassic World Dominion")*, praising Mitchell's use of influences from films such as *[Jurassic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic_Park "Jurassic Park")* and *[E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.T._the_Extra-Terrestrial "E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial")*. Nikki Baughan of *[Screen Daily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Daily "Screen Daily")* gave the film a positive review, calling it a "fun, fast-paced creature feature", also noting it "draws effectively on classic Spielbergian adventures" and that "most of the film plays little like *Jurassic Park* on steroids".

Some critics were more mixed. David Rooney of *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")* described the film as "high-end trash", acknowledging its entertainment value while criticizing its reliance on 1980s [Amblin-style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amblin_Entertainment "Amblin Entertainment") nostalgia. Likewise,
[Amy Nicholson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Nicholson "Amy Nicholson") in the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")* wrote that the Spielbergian influences would "almost satisfy our thwarted hopes for Steven Spielberg's own *[Disclosure Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disclosure_Day "Disclosure Day")*" if Mitchell's script "wasn't such a groaner". Nicholson wrote that *The End of Oak Street* is emblematic of Mitchell "need[ing] to prove he can make a big-budget crowd-pleaser" while not capturing the same mood as his earlier work. A two star review from [Peter Bradshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bradshaw "Peter Bradshaw") in *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* concluded the film's Spielberg influences do not "feel like homage so much as a pastiche produced by an AI Spielbergbot". *[Houston Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Chronicle "Houston Chronicle")* praised the film's dinosaur effects, pacing and performances but criticized its plot holes and occasionally illogical character decisions.
