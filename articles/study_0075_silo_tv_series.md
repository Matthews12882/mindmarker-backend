# Silo (TV series)

***Silo*** is an American [science fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction_on_television "Science fiction on television") [dystopian drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dystopian_fiction "Dystopian fiction") television series created by [Graham Yost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Yost "Graham Yost"), based on the *[Silo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silo_(series) "Silo (series)")* trilogy of novels (*Wool*, *Shift*, and *Dust*) by author [Hugh Howey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Howey "Hugh Howey"). Set in a dystopian future where a community exists in a giant underground [silo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silo "Silo") comprising 144 levels, it stars [Rebecca Ferguson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_Ferguson "Rebecca Ferguson") as Juliette Nichols, an engineer who becomes embroiled in the mysteries of its past and present. [Rashida Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashida_Jones "Rashida Jones"), [David Oyelowo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Oyelowo "David Oyelowo"), [Common](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_(rapper) "Common (rapper)"), [Tim Robbins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Robbins "Tim Robbins"), [Harriet Walter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harriet_Walter "Harriet Walter"), [Avi Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avi_Nash "Avi Nash"), [Rick Gomez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Gomez "Rick Gomez"), [Chinaza Uche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinaza_Uche "Chinaza Uche"), [Shane McRae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shane_McRae "Shane McRae"), [Remmie Milner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remmie_Milner "Remmie Milner"), [Alexandria Riley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria_Riley "Alexandria Riley"), [Clare Perkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_Perkins "Clare Perkins"), [Billy Postlethwaite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Postlethwaite "Billy Postlethwaite"), [Steve Zahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Zahn "Steve Zahn"), [Ashley Zukerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashley_Zukerman "Ashley Zukerman"), [Jessica Henwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Henwick "Jessica Henwick"), [Reed Birney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_Birney "Reed Birney"), [Laura Innes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Innes "Laura Innes"), [Jessica Brown Findlay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Brown_Findlay "Jessica Brown Findlay"), [Morven Christie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morven_Christie "Morven Christie"), and [Matt Craven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Craven "Matt Craven") also star.

Development of a film adaptation of *Wool* began in 2012. By the end of the decade, the project was shelved, and was picked up as a series by [Apple TV+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_TV%2B "Apple TV+") in May 2021. Principal photography began in August 2021 and the ten-episode first season premiered on May 5, 2023. The second season premiered on November 15, 2024. The third season premiered on July 3, 2026. All three seasons received positive reviews from critics, particularly for the world-building, production design, and Ferguson's performance. In December 2024, the series was renewed for a fourth and final season, which is set to premiere on July 9, 2027.

## Premise

In a [dystopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dystopian "Dystopian") future where a community exists in a giant [silo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silo "Silo") extending 144 levels underground, 10,000 people live in a society bound by regulations they believe are meant to protect them.

## Production

### Development

The project's development was originally announced as a feature film at [20th Century Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Century_Studios "20th Century Studios"), which entered negotiations to acquire the self-published e-book *[Wool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silo_(series)#Wool "Silo (series)")* by [Hugh Howey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Howey "Hugh Howey") on May 11, 2012. Five days later, 20th Century Fox acquired the rights, with [Ridley Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridley_Scott "Ridley Scott") and [Steven Zaillian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Zaillian "Steven Zaillian") among those attached to produce. On November 28, it was announced that [J Blakeson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J_Blakeson "J Blakeson") was in negotiations to write and direct. It was then announced on June 5, 2015, that [Nicole Perlman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Perlman "Nicole Perlman") would rewrite the screenplay, with Blakeson no longer involved in the project. The film was ultimately shelved as a result of the [acquisition of 21st Century Fox by Disney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acquisition_of_21st_Century_Fox_by_Disney "Acquisition of 21st Century Fox by Disney").

By July 30, 2018, a new iteration of the project was in development for television at [AMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMC_(TV_channel) "AMC (TV channel)"), with [LaToya Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaToya_Morgan "LaToya Morgan") attached to write under her overall deal at [AMC Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMC_Studios "AMC Studios"). The series eventually moved to [Apple TV+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_TV%2B "Apple TV+") on May 20, 2021, receiving an order for ten episodes. [Graham Yost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Yost "Graham Yost") replaced Morgan as creator and writer, marking his third series at Apple TV+ under his overall deal with the network. [Morten Tyldum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morten_Tyldum "Morten Tyldum") was also attached to direct and executive produce, with Yost as [showrunner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showrunner "Showrunner").

*Silo* was renewed for a second season in June 2023. In April 2024, [Rebecca Ferguson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_Ferguson "Rebecca Ferguson") stated in an interview that they are looking to shoot seasons 3 and 4 back-to-back, which would end the series. On December 16, 2024, the series was renewed for both a third and a fourth, final season.

### Writing

Alongside Yost, Jessica Blaire, [Cassie Pappas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassie_Pappas "Cassie Pappas"), Ingrid Escajeda, [Remi Aubuchon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remi_Aubuchon "Remi Aubuchon"), Aric Avelino, Jeffery Wang, Lekethia Dalcoe, and [Fred Golan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Golan "Fred Golan") served as writers for the first season; Sal Calleros, Jenny DeArmitt-Stran, and Katherine DiSavino joined for the second season; and Shelley Birse joined for the third.

According to Yost, the first and second seasons adapt the first book, *Wool*, the third season adapts the second book, *Shift*, and the fourth season adapts the third book in the trilogy, *Dust*.

### Casting

With the series order announcement, it was also announced that [Rebecca Ferguson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_Ferguson "Rebecca Ferguson") had been cast in a lead role. [Tim Robbins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Robbins "Tim Robbins") joined the cast in August 2021, and [Rashida Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashida_Jones "Rashida Jones"), [David Oyelowo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Oyelowo "David Oyelowo"), [Common](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_(rapper) "Common (rapper)"), [Harriet Walter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harriet_Walter "Harriet Walter"), [Avi Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avi_Nash "Avi Nash") and [Chinaza Uche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinaza_Uche "Chinaza Uche") joined in the following months. On July 27, 2024, it was announced at [San Diego Comic-Con](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Comic-Con "San Diego Comic-Con") that [Steve Zahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Zahn "Steve Zahn") had joined the cast for the second season. On January 17, 2025, it was announced that [Ashley Zukerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashley_Zukerman "Ashley Zukerman") and [Jessica Henwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Henwick "Jessica Henwick") had joined the cast as series regulars for the third season.

### Filming

[Principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography "Principal photography") began in late August 2021 in [Hoddesdon, Hertfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoddesdon,_Hertfordshire "Hoddesdon, Hertfordshire"), and was scheduled to last until the second quarter of 2022. Mark Patten, David Luther, and [Laurie Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurie_Rose_(cinematographer) "Laurie Rose (cinematographer)") were cinematographers. Gavin Bocquet was the production designer, credited with the design of the silo. The main set consists of three levels of stairs [decorated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_decorator "Set decorator") to represent particular locations.

The second season began filming in late June 2023 at Hoddesdon Studios, using the same set as season one. Filming was suspended in July due to the [2023 SAG-AFTRA strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_SAG-AFTRA_strike "2023 SAG-AFTRA strike"). Production restarted in early December 2023, and wrapped on March 8, 2024. Filming for season three began in October 2024, also at Hoddesdon Studios, as well as at OMA One and OMA X Film Studios in [Enfield, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enfield,_London "Enfield, London"), and wrapped in May 2025. The fourth and final season began filming in August 2025, and finished in March 2026.

## Release

*Silo* had its special screening during the [2023 Canneseries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Canneseries "2023 Canneseries") on April 14, 2023. The first season premiered on [Apple TV+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_TV%2B "Apple TV+") on May 5, 2023, with the first two episodes available immediately and the rest airing weekly through June 30.

The second season premiered on November 15, 2024. The third season premiered on July 3, 2026. The fourth and final season is set to premiere on July 9, 2027.

## Reception

### Critical response

Critical response of *Silo*

| Season | [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes") | [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic") |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 88% (69 reviews) | 75 (20 reviews) |
| 2 | 92% (53 reviews) | 80 (12 reviews) |
| 3 | 96% (78 reviews) | 77 (12 reviews) |

For the first season, the [review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator "Review aggregator") website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes") reported an 88% approval rating with an average rating of 7.7/10 based on 69 critic reviews. The website's critics consensus reads, "With deft writing, awe-inspiring production design and the inestimable star power of Rebecca Ferguson, *Silo* is a mystery box well worth opening." [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), which uses a weighted average, assigned a score of 75 out of 100 based on 20 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.

[Richard Roeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Roeper "Richard Roeper") of *[Chicago Sun-Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Sun-Times "Chicago Sun-Times")* wrote that the series "holds our interest with intriguing characters and effective twists and turns", and took note of how *Silo* "shifts gears through a number of genres, from conspiracy thriller to big-picture social commentary to police procedural to end-times romance". [Lucy Mangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucy_Mangan "Lucy Mangan") of *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* opined that the "world-building is meticulous" and "the story is equally thrilling". *[Vanity Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanity_Fair_(magazine) "Vanity Fair (magazine)")*'s Richard Lawson found the show to be a "feat of production design", adding that "Ferguson—in all her stern command—lends the series a necessary heft". Barry Hertz of *[Globe and Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globe_and_Mail "Globe and Mail")* also picked up Ferguson's performance as a "standout". Conversely, Brian Lowry of [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN "CNN") believed that the "inherent mystery [...] feels stretched to the point of strained, exacerbated by characters that don't consistently pop". *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")*'s Daniel Fienberg also praised the world-building and Ferguson's performance, but was critical of the performance of Common, whom he termed "the weak link in the cast".

For the second season, Rotten Tomatoes reported a 92% approval rating with an average rating of 6.95/10 based on 53 critic reviews. The website's critics consensus reads, "Rebecca Ferguson's intrepid hero seeks answers while the rest of *Silo*'s ensemble capably holds down the fort in this superb sophomore season." Metacritic assigned a score of 80 out of 100 based on 12 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.

For the third season, Rotten Tomatoes reported a 96% approval rating with an average rating of 8.25/10 based on 78 critic reviews. The website's critics consensus reads, "*Silo* codifies its third season as a masterful dystopian tale with expertly driven character arcs and tighter narrative strings, fulfilling its promise of science fiction excellence." Metacritic assigned a score of 77 out of 100 based on 12 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.
