# Bryan Shelton

**Bryan Shelton** (born December 22, 1965) is an American former professional [tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis "Tennis") player and coach. During his playing career, he won two singles and two doubles [ATP tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_tour "ATP tour") titles, and reached the mixed doubles final at the [1992 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Mixed_doubles "1992 French Open – Mixed doubles"), partnering with [Lori McNeil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lori_McNeil "Lori McNeil"). Shelton played collegiately for [Georgia Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Institute_of_Technology "Georgia Institute of Technology") from 1985 to 1988, and then played professionally from 1989 to 1997.

Following his retirement from the sport, Shelton returned to his alma mater to coach the [Georgia Tech Yellow Jackets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Tech_Yellow_Jackets "Georgia Tech Yellow Jackets") women's tennis team, which won the [NCAA Women's Tennis Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Women%27s_Tennis_Championship "NCAA Women's Tennis Championship") in 2007. He then served as the head coach of the [Florida Gators men's tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Gators_men%27s_tennis "Florida Gators men's tennis") team of the [University of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Florida "University of Florida"), which he coached to the 2021 NCAA Championship. He is the only head coach to have won a national championship in both men and women's NCAA Division I Tennis.

In 2023, Shelton retired from collegiate coaching to coach his son, [Ben Shelton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Shelton "Ben Shelton"), for the latter's professional career.

## Early years

Shelton was born in [Huntsville, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntsville,_Alabama "Huntsville, Alabama"). For high school, he attended [Randolph School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randolph_School "Randolph School") in Huntsville. He played for the Randolph Raiders boys' tennis team, and won the Alabama high school singles championship in 1982–1984.

## College career

Shelton accepted an [athletic scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletic_scholarship "Athletic scholarship") to attend the [Georgia Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Institute_of_Technology "Georgia Institute of Technology") in [Atlanta, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta,_Georgia "Atlanta, Georgia"), where he played for the [Georgia Tech Yellow Jackets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Tech_Yellow_Jackets#Tennis "Georgia Tech Yellow Jackets") men's tennis team from 1985 to 1988. Shelton was the [Atlantic Coast Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Coast_Conference "Atlantic Coast Conference") (ACC) champion in singles in 1985, and he and teammate Richy Gilbert were the ACC champions in doubles 1986. He was recognized as an All-ACC selection during each of his four seasons as a Yellow Jacket, and was named an [All-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-American "All-American") in 1988. Shelton won the [United States Amateur Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Amateur_Championships_(Men%27s_Tennis) "United States Amateur Championships (Men's Tennis)") in 1985. He graduated from Georgia Tech with a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") degree in [industrial engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_engineering "Industrial engineering") in 1989, and was inducted into the Georgia Tech Athletics Hall of Fame in 1993.

## Professional career

Shelton won two singles titles ([Newport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campbell%27s_Hall_of_Fame_Championships "Campbell's Hall of Fame Championships"), 1991 and 1992) during his professional career. He also reached the mixed doubles final at the [1992 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_French_Open "1992 French Open"), partnering [Lori McNeil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lori_McNeil "Lori McNeil"). The right-hander reached his highest individual ranking on the [ATP Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Tour "ATP Tour") on March 23, 1992, when he became number 55 in the world; his highest doubles ranking, 52, occurred on February 28, 1994. He was inducted into the Huntsville-Madison County Athletic Hall of Fame in 2006.

## Coaching

Shelton officially retired from the professional tour in 1997, and was named a [United States Tennis Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Tennis_Association "United States Tennis Association") (USTA) National Coach, a position he held from January 1998 until June 1999. Shelton coached [MaliVai Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MaliVai_Washington "MaliVai Washington"), a 1996 Wimbledon finalist.

Shelton became head coach of the [Georgia Tech Yellow Jackets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Tech_Yellow_Jackets "Georgia Tech Yellow Jackets") women's tennis team in July 1999. In his first season as coach at Georgia Tech, his team went to the second round of the NCAA tournament, upsetting the No. 25 [Washington Huskies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Huskies "Washington Huskies") before falling to the No. 9 [UCLA Bruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA_Bruins "UCLA Bruins"). He was named ACC Coach of the Year in 2002, 2005, and 2007. His 2007 team won the Yellow Jackets' third-straight ACC Championship. They then won Georgia Tech's first NCAA-recognized team championship on May 22, 2007, by defeating UCLA in the finals of the [NCAA Women's Tennis Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Women%27s_Tennis_Championship "NCAA Women's Tennis Championship"). Prior to his coaching tenure, the Georgia Tech women's tennis team had never qualified for the NCAA tournament. Shelton was named the [Intercollegiate Tennis Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercollegiate_Tennis_Association "Intercollegiate Tennis Association") (ITA) Coach of the Year in 2007.

On June 8, 2012, the [University of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Florida "University of Florida") announced that Shelton had been hired as the new head coach of the [Florida Gators men's tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Gators_men%27s_tennis "Florida Gators men's tennis") team.

On June 2, 2023, Shelton announced that he was stepping down from his coaching position.
