# Obsession (2025 film)

***Obsession*** is a 2025 American [supernatural horror film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supernatural_horror_film "Supernatural horror film") written, directed, and edited by [Curry Barker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curry_Barker "Curry Barker"). It follows the shop clerk Bear ([Michael Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Johnston_(actor) "Michael Johnston (actor)")), whose friend Nikki ([Inde Navarrette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inde_Navarrette "Inde Navarrette")) falls dangerously in love with him after he makes a wish. [Cooper Tomlinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_Tomlinson "Cooper Tomlinson"), [Megan Lawless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megan_Lawless "Megan Lawless"), and [Andy Richter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Richter "Andy Richter") appear in supporting roles.

Barker uploaded his short horror film *The Chair* to [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube") in 2023. When producer James Harris approached him to adapt it into a feature film, Barker pitched *Obsession*. It was partly inspired by *[The Simpsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simpsons "The Simpsons")* episode "[Treehouse of Horror II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treehouse_of_Horror_II "Treehouse of Horror II")". The film was shot in Los Angeles in October 2024 on a $750,000 budget. It is Barker's second feature-length film, after *[Milk & Serial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk_%26_Serial "Milk & Serial")* (2024), and first to be released in theaters.

*Obsession* premiered on September 5, 2025, at the [Toronto International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Toronto_International_Film_Festival "2025 Toronto International Film Festival") (TIFF). [Focus Features](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focus_Features "Focus Features") acquired the distribution rights for $14–15 million, the highest price commanded by a [genre film](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/genre_film "wikt:genre film") in TIFF history, and [Jason Blum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Blum "Jason Blum") joined as an executive producer under [Blumhouse Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blumhouse_Productions "Blumhouse Productions"). The film was theatrically released in the US on May 15, 2026, and received positive reviews. It grossed $523 million worldwide and set multiple box-office records as the [tenth highest-grossing film of 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_in_film#Highest-grossing_films "2026 in film") and [fifth highest-grossing horror film of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_horror_films "List of highest-grossing horror films"), unadjusted for inflation.

## Plot

Baron "Bear" Bailey harbors romantic feelings for his friend Nikki Freeman, who he works with at a music store alongside friends, Ian and Sarah. He returns home one night to find his cat, Sandy, dead after consuming [oxycodone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxycodone "Oxycodone"). Bear visits a crystal shop to buy Nikki a crystal necklace, but instead decides to buy her a One Wish Willow, a [novelty toy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novelty_toy "Novelty toy") that claims to grant one wish when broken. That night, when Nikki asks Bear whether he likes her, he is unable to admit his feelings. Frustrated, he breaks the One Wish Willow and wishes that Nikki loved him more than anyone in the world. She immediately reappears and asks to stay with Bear, saying her estranged father is dying of cancer. In bed, Nikki kisses Bear but screams and pulls back.

The next day, Nikki creates a memorial with Sandy's remains, and explains her erratic behavior was the result of [MDMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MDMA "MDMA"). After confessing her mutual feelings, the two begin happily dating. Ian calls Bear and reveals that Nikki had told Sarah that she saw Bear as her "little brother" shortly before they began dating, and that she lied about her father's cancer. When Bear questions Nikki, she reacts with defensiveness and anger. That night, Bear awakens to Nikki watching him sleep, and she cries hysterically, accusing him of not loving her.

The next day, Bear discovers that Nikki has duct taped the front door shut and that the sandwich she prepared him contains Sandy's remains. When Bear calls One Wish Willow's customer service, the representative tells him the wish will only expire when he dies and puts a screaming Nikki on the line, causing Bear to hang up in panic. He returns home to find that Nikki stood in place all day waiting for him, covered in bodily fluids.

Nikki insists on accompanying Bear to a party at Ian's house, against Ian's wishes. During a [Jenga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenga "Jenga") drinking game, Nikki recites a violent and [incestuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incest "Incest") retelling of "[Hansel and Gretel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hansel_and_Gretel "Hansel and Gretel")" she has written. Bear draws a block daring him to kiss the person to his left, Sarah. Nikki moves Sarah and kisses Bear instead, then screams and stabs herself with a broken bottle. Bear drives Nikki to the hospital but brings her home after she refuses treatment.

That night, Sarah texts Bear to meet at the park. As Bear attempts to leave, the real Nikki speaks, saying her obsessive personality is asleep and begs Bear to kill her. He refuses, offended that she would rather die than be with him. At the park, Sarah reveals that Ian and Nikki had been casually hooking up for two years and expresses concern that Nikki is using him for revenge against Ian. After Sarah hints that she is attracted to Bear, Nikki smashes through the car window and repeatedly slams Sarah's head into a brick, killing her. After they move the body, Nikki instructs Bear to go home while she disposes of it.

Bear buys the remaining One Wish Willows from the shop to reverse his wish, but is unable to break one. He explains everything to Ian and begs him to undo the wish. Ian sarcastically wishes for a billion dollars instead, causing cash to rain from the ceiling. Bear returns home with the last One Wish Willow to have Nikki reverse his wish and discovers Sarah's mutilated corpse. Nikki, dressed like Sarah, threatens Bear and herself at gunpoint. Ian arrives and Nikki shoots him dead. Bear locks himself in the bathroom and prepares to shoot himself, but instead consumes the remaining oxycodone. He changes his mind and tries to induce vomiting just as Nikki breaks the last One Wish Willow. Under her wish's influence, Bear exits the bathroom and kisses Nikki before dying from the overdose. Heartbroken, Nikki prepares to shoot herself, but Bear's death causes her to regain control of herself. Horrified by the carnage surrounding her, she wails hysterically.

## Production

### Development

[Curry Barker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curry_Barker "Curry Barker"), part of the YouTube sketch comedy duo That's a Bad Idea with [Cooper Tomlinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_Tomlinson "Cooper Tomlinson"), conceived *Obsession* as a film about a couple obsessed with one another. In July 2023, while gathered with friends to watch an episode of *[It's Always Sunny in Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%27s_Always_Sunny_in_Philadelphia "It's Always Sunny in Philadelphia")* in which he appeared, he watched a rerun of "[Treehouse of Horror II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treehouse_of_Horror_II "Treehouse of Horror II")", a 1991 episode of *[The Simpsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simpsons "The Simpsons")* in which [Homer Simpson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer_Simpson "Homer Simpson") interacts with a [monkey's paw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monkey%27s_paw "Monkey's paw"). This inspired him to incorporate the wish element.

Earlier that year, Barker uploaded his short horror film *The Chair* to his YouTube channel, which impressed producer James Harris of [Tea Shop Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_Shop_Productions "Tea Shop Productions"). Harris contacted Barker's manager to invite Barker to adapt *The Chair* into a feature film, an idea Barker had already written a script for. Barker took the opportunity to [pitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitch_(filmmaking) "Pitch (filmmaking)") *Obsession*, sending a draft, a [one-pager](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/one-pager "wikt:one-pager"), and a [lookbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lookbook_(filmmaking) "Lookbook (filmmaking)"). He signed with the talent agency [William Morris Endeavor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Morris_Endeavor "William Morris Endeavor") and received an offer to direct *Obsession* with a $1 million budget. Around this time, he declined a $2 million offer to rewrite the script to make Bear a hero.

Barker completed the screenplay over eight months while working at a coffee shop and corresponding with Tea Shop Productions. In the meantime, he released his first feature film, *[Milk & Serial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk_%26_Serial "Milk & Serial")*, on YouTube in August 2024. Inspired by [Aaron Sorkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Sorkin "Aaron Sorkin")'s screenwriting [MasterClass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MasterClass "MasterClass"), Barker decided against expanding Nikki's backstory and the lore of the One Wish Willow. Barker developed the design of the One Wish Willow with his mother, a graphic art designer. Producer Haley Nicole Johnson helped him with the hiring of crew members. *Obsession* was funded by Capstone Studios, after Harris shared Barker's script with its founder, [Christian Mercuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Mercuri "Christian Mercuri"), who was familiar with Barker's work. Barker described the screenplay as primarily concerned with obsession rather than the supernatural wish, using the wish as a device to examine how fixation on another person can become destructive.

### Casting

Barker considered casting himself as Bear. In late 2023 or early 2024, the crew shot a screen test for the scene where Bear drops Nikki off at her place and first makes the wish, in which Tomlinson, who had already been cast as Ian, stepped in to play Bear. Casting was underway by August 2024. Barker wrote Nikki as both a threat and a victim and sought an actress capable of playing both a normal young woman and a frightening supernatural presence. Amid a long casting process, Mercuri championed [Inde Navarrette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inde_Navarrette "Inde Navarrette"), who had appeared in three Capstone projects. Navarrette was cast as Nikki after Barker felt she "nailed" a balance between crazy and scary and brought a natural, "[bro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bro_culture "Bro culture")-y kind of sassy" personality to the role that helped make Nikki believable before the horror elements emerged and made "Bear feel so much like he's in the friend zone". After a [chemistry read](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/chemistry_read "wikt:chemistry read") with Navarrette, [Michael Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Johnston_(actor) "Michael Johnston (actor)") was cast as Bear.

*Obsession* marked Navarrette's second film after *[Wander Darkly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wander_Darkly "Wander Darkly")* (2020) and television roles on *[13 Reasons Why](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13_Reasons_Why "13 Reasons Why")* (2020) and *[Superman & Lois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superman_%26_Lois "Superman & Lois")* (2021–2024). Much of Nikki's physical behavior was developed through collaboration between Barker and Navarrette, and her screams, facial expressions, and vocal changes were performed without [computer-generated imagery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer-generated_imagery "Computer-generated imagery") (CGI) or [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_AI "Generative AI"). Scenes of Nikki lurking in the dark incorporated practical makeup effects, partly influenced by an [uncanny valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncanny_valley "Uncanny valley") makeup trend popularized by TikTok in 2023. Barker had the cast watch *[Hereditary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hereditary_(film) "Hereditary (film)")* (2018) and had Navarrette watch *[Midsommar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midsommar "Midsommar")* (2019) and *[Pearl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_(2022_film) "Pearl (2022 film)")* (2022). Navarrette credited [Betty Gabriel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Gabriel "Betty Gabriel") in *[Get Out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Get_Out "Get Out")* (2017), [Toni Collette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toni_Collette "Toni Collette") in *Hereditary*, and [Mia Goth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mia_Goth "Mia Goth") in *Pearl* as inspirations for her performance as Nikki.

### Filming

To plan shots, minimize [camera coverage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camera_coverage "Camera coverage"), and achieve practical in-camera effects, Barker and cinematographer Taylor Clemons [previsualized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Previsualize "Previsualize") the film by 3D scanning locations and recreating them as digital models. They shot the film "center-composed" with "extra head space" to create shots that felt "uncomfortable". [Principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography "Principal photography") began in late October 2024. It was filmed in 26 days, including reshoots after an initial 20-day shoot, in the Los Angeles area, covering five to six script pages daily. Scenes set in Bear's home, a house in [Burbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burbank,_California "Burbank, California") remodeled by production designer Vivian Gray, were shot in the first week and a half. Other locations included the Cassell's Music store in [San Fernando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando,_California "San Fernando, California"), which shut down a year after they wrapped, the Green Man crystal shop and Roguelike Tavern in Burbank, and Little Toni's pizzeria in North Hollywood. Scenes involving conversations in cars were shot on a soundstage using [LED screen technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-set_virtual_production "On-set virtual production").

*Obsession* underwent reshoots for a new opening scene and, according to Barker, to "do a better job of showing why [Bear] doesn't want to fix it right away", adding the scene where Bear confronts Nikki for cutting his hair to emphasize "that he wants to make it work". The production had to forgo reshoots of the party scene after the house where they filmed it burned down in the [January 2025 Southern California wildfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2025_Southern_California_wildfires "January 2025 Southern California wildfires"). For the ending, they filmed one take of Bear trying to make himself throw up after ingesting the pills at Johnston's suggestion that it would fit Bear's cowardly nature. Barker initially wrote and filmed an ending inspired by *[Romeo and Juliet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romeo_and_Juliet "Romeo and Juliet")* where Nikki kills herself after breaking free from Bear's wish. On the advice of his father (who contributed the "[Hansel and Gretel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hansel_and_Gretel "Hansel and Gretel")" monolog), Tomlinson, and others, Barker shot one take of a version where Nikki survives, which he chose for the theatrical cut. According to Barker, the film's budget was at most $750,000.

### Post-production

By March 2025, *Obsession* was in post-production. Barker edited the film on [Adobe Premiere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adobe_Premiere "Adobe Premiere"). He recorded his dialogue as the One Wish Willow customer service representative on his phone while editing from his bedroom, changing lines to fit the edit. After the film's festival premiere in September, the scene depicting Sarah getting her head smashed was cut down by "six or seven smashes" to avoid an NC-17 rating from the [Motion Picture Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_Picture_Association "Motion Picture Association"). [Jason Blum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Blum "Jason Blum") joined the film as an executive producer under his [Blumhouse Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blumhouse_Productions "Blumhouse Productions") banner after the premiere; his involvement was revealed in December 2025 when the first teaser was released.

### Music

Rock Burwell composed the score in his feature film debut. He met Barker in 2024 after a referral for a comedy song on the That's a Bad Idea channel. [Back Lot Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Filmed_Entertainment_Group "Universal Filmed Entertainment Group") digitally released the 18-track album on May 15, 2026, the same date as the film's release. [Cassette tape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassette_tape "Cassette tape"), [CD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_disc "Compact disc"), and [vinyl LP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LP_record "LP record") pressings of the soundtrack are set to release through independent label [Waxwork Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxwork_Records "Waxwork Records") in Q4 2026. Burwell cited [Angelo Badalamenti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelo_Badalamenti "Angelo Badalamenti") (particularly his arrangement of the [*Twin Peaks* soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soundtrack_from_Twin_Peaks "Soundtrack from Twin Peaks")) as a key influence, linking it to the *Obsession* score's approach of "exploring the uncanny valley, finding the space where emotions get warped into something disorienting, where you're no longer sure what's real".

## Release

### Theatrical

*Obsession* premiered during the Midnight Madness block at the [Toronto International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Toronto_International_Film_Festival "2025 Toronto International Film Festival") (TIFF) on September 5, 2025. Two days later, [Focus Features](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focus_Features "Focus Features") entered talks to acquire distribution rights to the film for the world (excluding France, New Zealand, and Russia) for $14–$15 million, the highest price commanded by a genre film in TIFF history, with [Universal Pictures International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures_International "Universal Pictures International") handling distribution outside the United States; the deal officially closed in October 2025, with a screening at the inaugural FocusFest on October 18 at the [Universal Studios Lot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Studios_Lot "Universal Studios Lot"). Other suitors for the film rights included [A24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A24 "A24"), [Neon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neon_(company) "Neon (company)"), and [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix"). The film was theatrically released in the US on May 15, 2026.

In June 2026, art director Sally Choi made a social media post calling for industry reform, noting that she made $300 a day on the non-union production while also serving as [production assistant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_assistant "Production assistant"), [set dresser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_dresser "Set dresser"), graphic designer, and background actor, and that some crew members were volunteers paid in gas and mileage.

In September 2026, executive producer Leonora Ann Darby sued Tea Shop Productions' James Harris and Mark Lane, claiming a "seven-year pattern of unequal treatment and broken compensation" and a contractual failure to reward her promised profits from the theatrical success of *Obsession*.

The film returned to theaters in [4DX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4DX "4DX") at [Regal Cinemas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regal_Cinemas "Regal Cinemas") for a day on October 14, 2026.

### Home media

*Obsession* was released in [premium video on demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premium_video_on_demand "Premium video on demand") format on June 30, 2026. It included behind-the-scenes content and a director's commentary. The film had a physical media release on [DVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD "DVD"), [Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blu-ray "Blu-ray"), and [4K UHD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_HD_Blu-ray "Ultra HD Blu-ray") on July 14, and a streaming release via [Peacock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacock_(streaming_service) "Peacock (streaming service)") on July 17.

## Reception

### Box office

*Obsession* grossed $263 million in the United States and Canada, and $257 million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $520 million.

In the United States and Canada, *Obsession* debuted with $17.2 million, finishing third at the box office behind holdovers *[Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_(2026_film) "Michael (2026 film)")* and *[The Devil Wears Prada 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Devil_Wears_Prada_2 "The Devil Wears Prada 2")*. The film then made $32 million over the subsequent four-day Memorial Day frame, including $24 million over the second weekend (a 39% increase from its opening). Unprecedented for a wide release, especially in the horror genre, it marked the biggest second-weekend increase for a film playing in more than 2,500 theaters outside of the Christmas season. Word-of-mouth and turnout from the 18–34 demographic, making up 75% of audience members, led to its success.

In its third weekend, the film made $27.4 million, becoming the first wide release outside of the Christmas season since *[E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.T._the_Extra-Terrestrial "E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial")* (1982) to increase its earnings for three consecutive weekends. With a total of $105.7 million at the time, it also became Focus Features' highest-grossing film within the United States and Canada, ahead of *[Downton Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downton_Abbey_(film) "Downton Abbey (film)")* (2019; $97 million). On the following Monday, Focus Features postponed its expected digital release of June 2. In its fourth weekend, the film became Focus Features' highest-grossing film of all time worldwide, surpassing *Downton Abbey* ($194.6 million). It outgrossed *[The Blair Witch Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Blair_Witch_Project "The Blair Witch Project")* (1999; $248.6 million) as the top-grossing festival acquisition title of all time in its fifth weekend, and crossed $300 million worldwide by its sixth.

On June 28, it overtook *[Sinners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinners_(2025_film) "Sinners (2025 film)")* (2025; $370.3 million) as the highest-grossing [live-action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live-action "Live-action") English-language original film of the 2020s. On July 6, it surpassed *[Enter the Dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enter_the_Dragon "Enter the Dragon")* (1973; $400 million) as the highest-grossing film with a budget under $1 million, unadjusted for inflation. On August 23, *Obsession* crossed $500 million, becoming the first English-language original film of the 2020s to do so and surpassing *[Elemental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elemental_(2023_film) "Elemental (2023 film)")* (2023; $496 million) in the process. Numerous outlets pointed to the film as a cultural phenomenon, particularly among [Generation Z](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_Z "Generation Z") audiences who drove ticket sales. Disney's *[The Mandalorian and Grogu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mandalorian_and_Grogu "The Mandalorian and Grogu")*, a [franchise tentpole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tentpole "Tentpole") released the weekend after *Obsession*, struggled against the film; the latter eventually surpassed the former's overall lifetime gross despite *The Mandalorian and Grogu*'s $98 million opening. This, alongside the overperformance of [A24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A24 "A24")'s *[Backrooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backrooms_(film) "Backrooms (film)")*, released around the same time, sparked analysis over a potential renewed interest in original [intellectual property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_property "Intellectual property") from audiences.

### Critical response

On the [review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator "Review aggregator") website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes"), 93% of 330 critics' reviews are positive, with an average rating of 8/10. The website's consensus reads: "Taking an icky conceit and twisting it to deviously crowd-pleasing ends, *Obsession* is dauntingly disturbing while also skillfully amusing and thrilling." [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), which uses a [weighted average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_average "Weighted average"), assigned the film a score of 77 out of 100, based on 38 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews. Audiences surveyed by [CinemaScore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CinemaScore "CinemaScore") gave the film an average grade of "A−" on an A+ to F scale, while 70% of those polled by [PostTrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PostTrak "PostTrak") said they would "definitely recommend" it.

Christian Zilko of *[IndieWire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndieWire "IndieWire")* said it was "proof that the [Cregger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zach_Cregger "Zach Cregger")-ification of 2020s horror is in full effect, as its combination of sadistic violence, [ironic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irony "Irony") needle drops, and comedy mined from people responding to tragedy in pathetically self-serving ways will merit plenty of comparisons to *[Barbarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbarian_(2022_film) "Barbarian (2022 film)")* and *[Weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapons_(2025_film) "Weapons (2025 film)")*". *[IGN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IGN "IGN")*'s Matt Donato noted "familiar romance and monkey's paw tropes" but said "Barker's execution takes things to the next level". Lou Thomas of *[Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_(magazine) "Empire (magazine)")* called it "so fresh and exhilarating, one can forgive its familiar origins". In *[Compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_(American_magazine) "Compact (American magazine)")*, Matthew Schmitz said it defined the genre of "[heteropessimist](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/heteropessimism "wikt:heteropessimism") horror". Author [Rob K. Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_K._Henderson "Rob K. Henderson") described it as a "morality tale" that encouraged young men to confess their romantic feelings instead of concealing them.

Navarrette received critical acclaim for her performance. Louis Peitzman of *[Vulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulture_(website) "Vulture (website)")* called it a stand-out performance for Navarrette, especially as her first major role in the horror genre. Katie Rife of *[RogerEbert.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RogerEbert.com "RogerEbert.com")* said her emotional scenes remind the audience of "the human being trapped inside" a body she no longer controls, making the character more tragic. Guy Lodge of *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* praised Navarrette for "acing one of the more physically and emotionally taxing horror leads to come down the pike in a while". Bill Bria of *[SlashFilm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SlashFilm "SlashFilm")* compared Nikki's impact to performances in 1970s horror films such as *[The Exorcist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Exorcist "The Exorcist")* and *[Carrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrie_(1976_film) "Carrie (1976 film)")*. David Friend of [The Canadian Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Canadian_Press "The Canadian Press") said Navarrette gives "somehow one of the funniest and most terrifying performances of the year."

Benjamin Lee of *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* praised the "disturbing sound design" for intensifying the horror elements, while Marshall Shaffer of *[Slant Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slant_Magazine "Slant Magazine")* complimented the film's audiovisual presentation. Some critiqued the film's handling of its themes, particularly for having a minimal Nikki perspective, with Odie Henderson of *[The Boston Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Boston_Globe "The Boston Globe")* writing that the story "tries to say something about female autonomy and male selfishness" but "plays like a ... riff on the old reliable [stand-up routine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand-up_comedy "Stand-up comedy") subject 'girlfriends be crazy'".

## Future

In 2026, Barker said he was considering an anthology television series where each episode explores a new character making a wish, or a sequel, for which he has an idea. His next film, *[Anything but Ghosts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anything_but_Ghosts "Anything but Ghosts")*, includes a nod to the events of *Obsession* in a news report. That's a Bad Idea collaborator Malcolm Kelner, who played a bartender in *Obsession*, reprised his role in social media posts and a short parody film about an imagined action sequel co-starring [T-Pain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-Pain "T-Pain").

*Obsession*'s character Nikki was featured at [Universal's Halloween Horror Nights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal%27s_Halloween_Horror_Nights "Universal's Halloween Horror Nights") for the 2026 season.
