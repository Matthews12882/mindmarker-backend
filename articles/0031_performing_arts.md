# Performing arts

The **performing arts** are [arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_arts "The arts") such as [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music "Music"), [dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance "Dance"), and [drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama "Drama") which are performed for an audience. They are different from the [visual arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_arts "Visual arts"), which produce physical or static [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Work_of_art "Work of art"). Performing arts include a range of disciplines which are performed in front of a live audience, including theatre, music, and dance. Performances may take place in purpose-built buildings, such as theatres and [opera houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_house "Opera house"), as well as in open air festivals, street settings, and recorded formats like film or television.

Performing arts traditions are present in every society. [Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_music "History of music") and [dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_dance "History of dance") date to [prehistoric times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-historic_times "Pre-historic times"), while theatrical forms appear in [Ancient Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece "Ancient Greece"), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India"), and [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China"). Performance served combinations of religious, ceremonial, and entertainment functions. Traditions including Japanese [Noh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noh "Noh") and [Kabuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabuki "Kabuki"), as well as [Indian classical dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_classical_dance "Indian classical dance"), have been recognized by [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO") as Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Western performing arts history spans ancient [Greek tragedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_tragedy "Greek tragedy") and [comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_comedy "Ancient Greek comedy") through medieval mystery plays, the emergence of [opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera "Opera") and [ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballet "Ballet") in the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance "Renaissance"), and the [Romantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanticism "Romanticism") expansion of grand opera. In the modernist revolutions of the early 20th century, figures such as [Isadora Duncan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isadora_Duncan "Isadora Duncan"), [Konstantin Stanislavski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Stanislavski "Konstantin Stanislavski"), and [Sergei Diaghilev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Diaghilev "Sergei Diaghilev") reworked the principles of dance and theatre. Postmodern performance increasingly challenges the boundaries between disciplines.

Live performances before an audience are a form of entertainment. The development of [audio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_recording_and_reproduction "Sound recording and reproduction") and [video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video "Video") recording has allowed for private consumption of the performing arts. In narrative performance, characters express emotions.

## Types

### Theatre

Theatre is the branch of performing arts concerned with acting out stories in front of an audience, using combinations of speech, gesture, music, dance, and spectacle. It is linked to [ritual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritual "Ritual") in ancient cultures across the world.

The most familiar form of theatre is the scripted [play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Play_(theatre) "Play (theatre)"), in which actors portray characters in a narrative unfolding in real time before an audience. Theatre diversified into [musicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_theatre "Musical theatre") integrating song and dance and opera with all the words set to music. In [improvisational theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Improvisational_theatre "Improvisational theatre"), actors invent material instead of following a set script, while performance art explores other disciplines to understand cultural intersections.

The relationship between performers and audience has varied from formal and distant [proscenium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proscenium "Proscenium") productions to more intimate formats, including [thrust stages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrust_stage "Thrust stage"), [black box theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_box_theater "Black box theater"), [environmental theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Site-specific_theatre "Site-specific theatre"), and even found space adapted from a nontheatrical location. Spatial setup shapes the nature of a performance.

### Dance

In the context of performing arts, dance generally refers to human [movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_(physics) "Motion (physics)"), typically rhythmic and to music, used as a form of audience entertainment in a performance setting. Definitions of dance are culturally contingent and range from functional forms such as [folk dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_dance "Folk dance") to codified virtuoso techniques such as [ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballet "Ballet").

[Choreography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choreography "Choreography") is the art of composing dances, while choreographers arrange movements to communicate artistic ideas and meaning. Dance serves both social and artistic functions. It features in ceremonies, rituals, customs, and celebrations. In Black and Indigenous communities, dance is often inseparable from music and communal ritual.

[Modern dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_dance "Modern dance") emerged as a 20th-century response to ballet strictures, emphasizing freer bodily movement and self-expression. Dance forms such as [hip-hop dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip-hop_dance "Hip-hop dance"), [contemporary dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_dance "Contemporary dance"), and [postmodern dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmodern_dance "Postmodern dance") have continued to broaden approaches to movement, choreography, and performance.

### Music

Music is an art form that uses elements such as timbre, pitch, rhythm, and dynamics to organise sound. It can be performed using a variety of instruments and styles and is divided into genres such as [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music"), [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz"), [hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip-hop "Hip-hop"), pop, and rock, etc. As an art form, music can occur in live or recorded formats, and can be [planned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehearsal "Rehearsal") or [improvised.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_improvisation "Musical improvisation")

Jazz combines written arrangements with improvisation, while in [classical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_music "Classical music") Western concert music faithful interpretation of the written score is central. Improvisation is central to traditions from around the world including Indian classical music and West African griot singing. In practice, few types of music are wholly composed or improvised.

### Film

Beginning with early motion-picture technologies such as [Thomas Edison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Edison "Thomas Edison")'s [kinetoscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetoscope "Kinetoscope") in the 1890s, [cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinematography "Cinematography") developed into a mass medium, later shaped by the [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_the_United_States "Cinema of the United States") [studio system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio_system "Studio system"). Film acting differs from stage acting, projecting to a camera rather than to an audience. The performance is then shaped and assembled in [editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editing "Editing"). Acting on camera often requires subtlety rather than the projection required on stage.

Film has become an umbrella term covering cinema and television, with different film cultures around the world accessible by streaming services. Distinct film cultures in South Asia, East Asia, and elsewhere reach wide audiences.

### Opera

In opera, dramatic stories are primarily conveyed through singing, often accompanied by an orchestra. It combines music, drama, and spectacle into a single art form. Opera originated in Italy at the end of the sixteenth century and spread throughout the world, becoming the prestige entertainment of aristocratic, and later bourgeois, society.

The form ranges from smaller [comic operas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic_opera "Comic opera") to spectacular [grand opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_opera "Grand opera"). Operas by [Mozart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_operas_by_Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart "List of operas by Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart"), [Verdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_compositions_by_Giuseppe_Verdi "List of compositions by Giuseppe Verdi"), [Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_works_for_the_stage_by_Richard_Wagner "List of works for the stage by Richard Wagner"), and [Puccini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_compositions_by_Giacomo_Puccini "List of compositions by Giacomo Puccini") remain among the most performed. Wagner's [Gesamtkunstwerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gesamtkunstwerk "Gesamtkunstwerk") concept influenced the modern stage, with artists seeking theater appealing to all the senses.

### Other forms

[Circus arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circus_arts "Circus arts") include [clowning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clown "Clown"), [acrobatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acrobatics "Acrobatics"), [aerials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_silk "Aerial silk"), and [object manipulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_manipulation "Object manipulation"). Ancient Egyptian depictions survive that show acrobatics, which were performed at festive occasions. [Contemporary circus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_circus "Contemporary circus") combines traditional circus techniques with elements of theatre and storytelling. [Cirque du Soleil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cirque_du_Soleil "Cirque du Soleil") is a widely recognised example of this performance style.

The broader field of performing arts includes [musical theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_theatre "Musical theatre"), [magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_(illusion) "Magic (illusion)"), [mime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mime "Mime"), [spoken word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spoken_word "Spoken word"), [puppetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppetry "Puppetry"), [performance art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performance_art "Performance art"), [improv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Improvisational_theatre "Improvisational theatre"), and [stand-up comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand-up_comedy "Stand-up comedy"). Musical theatre and especially the [Broadway musical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_theatre "Broadway theatre") became a dominant theatrical form in the twentieth century. Puppetry encompasses traditions from Indonesian shadow puppetry to Japanese Bunraku to contemporary uses like in *The Lion King*. Stand-up comedy emerged as a distinct form, developing out of variety shows and moving into clubs with important audience reactions. Performance art grew out of avant-garde movements, using the artist's body and presence to create work that resists conventions.

## History

### Ancient and classical periods

As early as the 19th century BCE, a large religious ceremony in Egypt may have had theatre-like elements. The [Dramatic Ramesseum Papyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dramatic_Ramesseum_Papyrus "Dramatic Ramesseum Papyrus") is an ancient Egyptian text that has been interpreted as an early example of dramatic literature, although its classification as a theatrical work remains debated.

Greek playwrights, including [Aeschylus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeschylus "Aeschylus"), [Sophocles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophocles "Sophocles"), and [Euripides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euripides "Euripides"), developed [tragedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tragedy "Tragedy") in the fifth century BCE, while [Aristophanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristophanes "Aristophanes"), [Cratinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cratinus "Cratinus"), and [Menander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menander "Menander") developed [comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comedy "Comedy"). [Greek theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_of_ancient_Greece "Theatre of ancient Greece") was performed in outdoor auditoriums with actors performing in masks. Greek theatre spread throughout the Mediterranean and beyond.

India produced a performing arts tradition. The [Natya Shastra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natya_Shastra "Natya Shastra") is a Sanskrit treatise on dramaturgy that formulated Indian theatrical theory and practice, attributed to [Bharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharata_(sage) "Bharata (sage)") and possibly compiled 200 BCE–200 CE. Dramatists such as [Bhāsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bh%C4%81sa "Bhāsa"), [Kalidasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalidasa "Kalidasa"), and [Bhavabhuti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhavabhuti "Bhavabhuti") produced a rich heritage of dramatic literature. The Sanskrit epics [Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana "Ramayana") and [Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata "Mahabharata") are popular in India and much of [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia").

In China, the dramatic tradition goes back to the [Shang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang_dynasty "Shang dynasty") (c. 1600–1046 BCE) involving music and theatre. [Shadow puppetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_play "Shadow play") emerged during the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty "Han dynasty") (202 BCE–220 CE). During the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty "Tang dynasty") (618–907 CE), [Emperor Xuanzong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Xuanzong_of_Tang "Emperor Xuanzong of Tang") established the [Pear Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pear_Garden "Pear Garden") to train musicians and performers. In the [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty "Yuan dynasty") (1271–1368 CE), the [Zaju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaju "Zaju") variety play reached its peak, having [Beijing opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peking_opera "Peking opera") as a descendant.

### Middle Ages

In medieval Europe, theatre was entwined with the [Christian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Church "Christian Church"), with [mystery plays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mystery_play "Mystery play") adapting Biblical stories and dramatizing sermons. Secular entertainment included itinerant performers like [jongleurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minstrel "Minstrel") who combined singing, telling jokes, clowning, juggling, tumbling, or magic tricks.

In West Africa, [griots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griot "Griot") shared oral history with music and storytelling, reciting genealogy and specializing in musical instruments. They also had social roles like political advisers.

In the medieval Islamic world, the [ta'ziyeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ta%27ziyeh "Ta'ziyeh") were shadow puppet theatre telling religious epic dramas, with Shi'a ta'ziyeh focused on the death of [Husayn ibn Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Husayn_ibn_Ali "Husayn ibn Ali"). The ta'ziyeh was a living tradition through the 1930s when it was banned in Iran.

### Renaissance

The Renaissance, beginning in 15th-century Italy and spreading throughout Europe, saw a revival of classical forms, alongside theatrical innovations. [Domenico da Piacenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domenico_da_Piacenza "Domenico da Piacenza") is credited with the first use of the term *ballo* to describe choreographed court dances. The term eventually became *[ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballet "Ballet")*. The first ballet *per se* is thought to be [Balthasar de Beaujoyeulx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balthasar_de_Beaujoyeulx "Balthasar de Beaujoyeulx")'s *[Ballet Comique de la Reine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballet_Comique_de_la_Reine "Ballet Comique de la Reine")* (1581).

By the mid-16th century the *[commedia dell'arte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commedia_dell%27arte "Commedia dell'arte")* became widely popular in Europe. This improvisational form performed by professionals used [stock characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_character "Stock character"), including servants, old men, and lovers. A professional theatre industry also emerged in England, providing an institutional context for [William Shakespeare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Shakespeare "William Shakespeare")'s plays in the late 16th century.

In 1597, the first opera, [Dafne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dafne "Dafne"), was performed. Throughout the 17th century, opera would rapidly become the entertainment of choice for the [aristocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristocracy "Aristocracy") in most of Europe, and eventually for large numbers of people living in cities and towns throughout Europe.

A [proscenium arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proscenium "Proscenium") and curtain used in Italy during the 17th century established the traditional theatre form that persists to this day. In England, the [Puritans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puritans "Puritans") [banned theatrical performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_theatre_closure_1642 "London theatre closure 1642") until 1660, after which women began to appear in plays. The French introduced formal dance instruction in the late 17th century.

### 18th and 19th centuries

The introduction of the popular [opera buffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic_opera "Comic opera") brought opera to the masses as an accessible form of performance. [Mozart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart "Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart")'s *[The Marriage of Figaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Marriage_of_Figaro "The Marriage of Figaro")* and *[Don Giovanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Giovanni "Don Giovanni")* are 18th century landmarks.

In the early 19th century, the [Romantic movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romantic_movement "Romantic movement") emphasized individual self-expression, emotional directness, and nationalist energies. In opera, this led to the spectacular [grand operas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_opera "Grand opera") of [Giacomo Meyerbeer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_Meyerbeer "Giacomo Meyerbeer") and then to the musical dramas of [Giuseppe Verdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Verdi "Giuseppe Verdi"). The [Gesamtkunstwerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gesamtkunstwerk "Gesamtkunstwerk") (total artwork) of [Richard Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Wagner "Richard Wagner") united dance, music, and poetry into one expression. Influential ballet repertoire included *[Giselle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giselle "Giselle")* (1841) and *[Swan Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_Lake "Swan Lake")* (1877). Romantic ballet provided more prominence to the female dancing body and [pointe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pointe_technique "Pointe technique") work.

The 19th century also saw the expansion of popular performing arts, driven by [urbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization "Urbanization") and the growth of commercial entertainment. [Variety shows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_show "Variety show"), [vaudeville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaudeville "Vaudeville"), and [burlesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burlesque "Burlesque") offered working class entertainment. Gaslight and later [electric lighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_light "Electric light") transformed the visual perception of theatre.

### Modern era

[Modern dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_dance "Modern dance") began in the late 19th and early 20th centuries in response to the restrictions of traditional ballet. Pioneers [Isadora Duncan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isadora_Duncan "Isadora Duncan") and [Loie Fuller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loie_Fuller "Loie Fuller") focused on natural, expressive movements over conventional technique. The arrival of [Sergei Diaghilev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Diaghilev "Sergei Diaghilev")'s [Ballets Russes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballets_Russes "Ballets Russes") (1909–1929) revolutionized ballet. Diaghilev's collaboration brought together choreographers, dancers, composers, authors, visual artists, and [fashion designers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_design "Fashion design"). New ballet companies with strong national identities were founded across Europe starting in the 1930s.

[Konstantin Stanislavski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Stanislavski "Konstantin Stanislavski")'s ["System"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislavski_System "Stanislavski System") revolutionized acting in the early 20th century, introducing psychological realism. [Method acting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_acting "Method acting") exercises are taught in many acting schools.

The motion picture was invented in the 1890s and developed into a global mass market after World War I. Hollywood's studio system created film actors and shaped star image. The subsequent development of [radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio "Radio") and [television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television "Television") affected the diversity of performance.

### Postwar

Following World War II, opera and ballet were built up, supported by state subsidies. [Postmodernism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmodernism "Postmodernism") in the performing arts was largely a phenomenon of the 1970s and 1980s, challenging the boundaries between art forms. In contemporary performing arts, digital technology is being integrated with live performance.

[Animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animation "Animation"), [motion capture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_capture "Motion capture"), and real-time interactivity have extended what is possible on stage. During [COVID-19 lockdowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_lockdowns "COVID-19 lockdowns"), theatres worldwide produced streaming and hybrid formats, spurring innovation and debates about the future of live performance and digital technology.

## Non-Western and Indigenous traditions

### African performing arts

Africa has much cultural diversity, with over 3,000 ethnic groups. Many indigenous African performance traditions incorporate dance, song, music, and mime elements. Performances were communal with the storytellers and audience interacting and participating in [call and response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_and_response "Call and response"), which is characterized by a vocalist singing a phrase that is then echoed or responded to with a new phrase by the other performers and/or audience.

[Griots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griot "Griot") are hereditary musicians specializing in oral history, genealogies, and praise singing with responsibilities well beyond entertainment. Griot performance typically combines virtuoso playing of an instrument such as the [kora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kora_(instrument) "Kora (instrument)") with singing or recitation.

### Eastern performing arts

#### Iran

In [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran"), there are other forms of theatrical events such as *[naqqali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naqqali "Naqqali")* (story telling), *[ta'ziyeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ta%27ziyeh "Ta'ziyeh")*, *ru-howzi*, *[siyah-bazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siyah-bazi "Siyah-bazi")*, *parde-khani*, and *[ma'rekeh-giri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma%27rekeh-giri "Ma'rekeh-giri")*. Prior to the twentieth century, storytelling was the most recognized form of entertainment, although today, some forms still remain. One form, *[Naghali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naqqali "Naqqali"),* was traditionally performed in coffeehouses where the storytellers, or *Naghals (Naqqāls),* only recited sections of a story at a time, thus retaining regular clientele. These stories were based on events of historical or religious importance and many referenced poetry from the [Shahnameh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahnameh "Shahnameh"). These stories were often altered to bond with the atmosphere or mood of the audience.

#### India

Classical dance forms [Bharatanatyam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatanatyam "Bharatanatyam"), [Kathak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathak "Kathak"), [Odissi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odissi "Odissi"), [Manipuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipuri_dance "Manipuri dance"), [Kuchipudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuchipudi "Kuchipudi"), [Kathakali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathakali "Kathakali"), [Mohiniattam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohiniyattam "Mohiniyattam"), and [Sattriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sattriya "Sattriya") are each associated with distinct regional traditions, embodied philosophies, and revival histories. Kerala's [Koodiyattam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koodiyattam "Koodiyattam") is among the oldest surviving theatrical traditions and [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO") declared it a [masterpiece of human heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masterpieces_of_the_Oral_and_Intangible_Heritage_of_Humanity "Masterpieces of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity") in 2001.

Folk theatre in India combines elements such as music, dance, pantomime, poetry, storytelling, visual arts, religion, and festivals. [Bollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bollywood "Bollywood"), the Mumbai-based Hindi-language film industry, has since the mid-20th century synthesized dance, music, and theatre into one of the world's most prolific and influential entertainment industries.

[Indian classical music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_classical_music "Indian classical music"), including the Carnatic and Hindustani traditions, forms an important part of the performing arts, with instruments such as the [mridangam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mridangam "Mridangam") and [tabla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabla "Tabla") serving as key elements of rhythmic expression and performance.

#### China

There are references to theatrical entertainments in China as early as 1500 BCE during the [Shang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang_dynasty "Shang dynasty"); they often involved music, clowning and acrobatic displays.

During the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty "Tang dynasty"), [Emperor Xuanzong of Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Xuanzong_of_Tang "Emperor Xuanzong of Tang") formed an acting school known as the Children of the [Pear Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pear_Garden "Pear Garden") to produce a form of drama that was primarily musical.

In the [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty "Song dynasty"), there were many popular plays involving [acrobatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acrobatic "Acrobatic") and music. These developed in the [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty "Yuan dynasty") into a more sophisticated form with a four- or five-act structure. Yuan drama spread across China and diversified into numerous regional forms, the best known of which is [Beijing Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peking_opera "Peking opera"), which is still popular today.

#### Thailand

In [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand"), it has been a tradition from the Middle Ages to stage plays based on plots drawn from Indian epics. In particular, the theatrical version of Thailand's national epic *[Ramakien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramakien "Ramakien")*, a version of the Indian *[Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana "Ramayana")*, remains popular in Thailand even today.

#### Japan

Japan has produced several distinct and sophisticated theatrical forms. During the 14th century, [Kan'ami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kan%27ami "Kan'ami") and his son [Zeami Motokiyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeami_Motokiyo "Zeami Motokiyo") developed [Noh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noh "Noh") theatre under the patronage of the shogun [Ashikaga Yoshimitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashikaga_Yoshimitsu "Ashikaga Yoshimitsu"), combining masked performance, chanting, instrumental music, and stylized drama. Noh aesthetics, according to Zeami, include [yugen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_aesthetics#Yūgen "Japanese aesthetics") or "refined elegance."

Bunraku is a form of puppet theatre developed in the 17th century, associated with playwright [Chikamatsu Monzaemon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chikamatsu_Monzaemon "Chikamatsu Monzaemon"). It uses large puppets operated by visible puppeteers, with chanting and [shamisen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamisen "Shamisen") accompaniment.

[Kabuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabuki "Kabuki"), which began shortly after [Bunraku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunraku "Bunraku"), is a more accessible form incorporating elaborate costumes, makeup, and stage mechanisms. Originally performed by women, kabuki companies became all-male; [onnagata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onnagata "Onnagata") developed specialized techniques for female representation. Noh, Bunraku, and Kabuki are recognized by [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO") as Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

### Americas

In the [Pre-Columbian era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_era "Pre-Columbian era"), indigenous civilizations of the Americas had established performing arts traditions, before the arrival of Europeans. These included [Aztec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec "Aztec") and [Maya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_civilization "Maya civilization") rituals and ceremonies, which often involved elaborate dances, music, and theatrical performances.

Among the [Mexica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexica "Mexica") (Aztecs) of central Mexico, performance served as social integration involving a religious element. The [tonalpohualli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dnalp%C5%8Dhualli "Tōnalpōhualli") ritual calendar marked important dates with ceremonies involving dancers, singers, and musicians. The huehuetlatolli or "speech of the elders" passed down oratorical performance through generations. Public ceremonies in the [Templo Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Templo_Mayor "Templo Mayor") courtyard could involve thousands of dancers in concentric rings accompanied by drums, flutes, and rattles.

The [Incan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incan "Incan") rituals and festivals also featured music, dances, and theatrical representations of myths and legends. Northwest Coast cultures, such as the [Haida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haida_people "Haida people"), [Tlingit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tlingit "Tlingit"), and [Kwakwaka'wakw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwakwaka%27wakw "Kwakwaka'wakw"), had traditions of storytelling, mask carving, and ceremonial dances. In the [potlatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potlatch "Potlatch"), Kwakwaka'wakw masks can be opened to reveal an animal mask in dramatic transformations.

The colonial period brought a fusion of European and indigenous cultural influences. The Spanish and French wrote and staged theatrical productions. Indigenous peoples incorporated elements of their traditional performing arts into colonial-era productions.

## Industry, funding, and institutions

The performing arts distinguish between commercial economic models for popular music and primarily nonprofit models for organizations like orchestras and ballet companies. [Music labels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_label "Record label") profit from concert ticket sales, merchandise, music publishing, and endorsements. At the other end, orchestras in Europe receive relatively generous public subsidies, and in the United States rely on a mix of ticket sales, state and municipal support, grants, and philanthropy.

Training institutions transmit performing arts practice. [Conservatories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_school "Music school") have a vocational focus with high standards for performance, professionalism, and artistry. Universities combine artistic practice with academic study. In non-Western traditions, knowledge of particular styles is transmitted across generations. The question of who has access to arts education bears on the diversity of the performing arts workforce.

Performing arts venues range from purpose-built [opera houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_house "Opera house") to open-air [amphitheaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphitheatre "Amphitheatre") to digital spaces. The architectural design of venues shapes the performances held within them, influencing [acoustics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architectural_acoustics "Architectural acoustics") and audience interaction. Building flexible performing arts facilities allows a mix of programming to attract audiences.

## Digital technology and contemporary practice

Digital technology has transformed how art is created and distributed. In multimedia work, motion capture combines actor movement with sound and video to expand the possibilities, even allowing interactive installations blurring the line between audience and performer. [Sound design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_design "Sound design") envelops audiences in three-dimensional sonic [spatiality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spatiality_(architecture) "Spatiality (architecture)"). The relationship between live performance and digital mediation has evolved with technology.

The COVID-19 pandemic accelerated experimentation with digital formats in maintaining engagement with audiences. Challenges included unequal access to the internet and the autonomy of viewer attention. Digital formats can attract a public that would not otherwise be able to attend due to location, schedule, or other commitments. The longer-term economics of live performance continue to evolve.
