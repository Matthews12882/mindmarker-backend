# Age of Discovery

The **Age of Discovery** (c. 1418 – c. 1620), also known as the **Age of Exploration**, was part of the [early modern period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_period "Early modern period") and overlapped with the [Age of Sail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Sail "Age of Sail"). It was a period from approximately the 15th to the 17th century, during which [seafarers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seamanship "Seamanship") from European countries explored, colonized, and conquered regions across the globe. The Age of Discovery was a transformative period when previously isolated parts of the world became connected to form the [world-system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World-system "World-system"), and laid the groundwork for [globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization "Globalization"). The extensive overseas exploration, particularly the opening of maritime routes to the [East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Indies "East Indies") and [European colonization of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas "European colonization of the Americas") by the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire "Portuguese Empire") and [Spaniards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire "Spanish Empire"), later joined by the [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire"), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire "French colonial empire"), and [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_colonial_empire "Dutch colonial empire"), spurred [international global trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_trade "International trade"). The interconnected global economy of the 21st century has its origins in the expansion of trade networks during this era. The exploration created European [colonial empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_empire "Colonial empire") and marked an increased adoption of [colonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonialism "Colonialism") as a government policy in several European states. As such, it is sometimes synonymous with the [first wave of European colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_wave_of_European_colonization "First wave of European colonization"). This colonization reshaped power dynamics causing geopolitical shifts in Europe and creating new centers of power beyond Europe.

Portuguese oceanic exploration began with [maritime expeditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_discoveries "Portuguese discoveries") to the [Macaronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macaronesia "Macaronesia") islands, including the [Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Islands "Canary Islands"), as well as [Madeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira "Madeira") and the [Azores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azores "Azores"). It continued with voyages along the coast of [West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Africa "West Africa") in 1434, and culminated in the [establishment of a sea route to India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_discovery_of_the_sea_route_to_India "Portuguese discovery of the sea route to India") in 1498 by [Vasco da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_da_Gama "Vasco da Gama"), which initiated Portugal's maritime and commercial presence in [Kerala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala "Kerala") and the [Indian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean "Indian Ocean"). Spain made the transatlantic [voyages of Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyages_of_Christopher_Columbus "Voyages of Christopher Columbus") (1492–1504), which marked the beginning of colonization in the Americas, the [Magellan expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magellan_expedition "Magellan expedition") (1519–1522), which opened a route from the Atlantic to the Pacific and, under [Juan Sebastián Elcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Sebasti%C3%A1n_Elcano "Juan Sebastián Elcano"), completed the first [circumnavigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumnavigation "Circumnavigation") of the globe. Spain also undertook other major early voyages, including the [conquest of Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_conquest_of_the_Aztec_Empire "Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire") (1519–1521), the [conquest of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_conquest_of_the_Inca_Empire "Spanish conquest of the Inca Empire") (1532–1533), and the [Manila galleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_galleon "Manila galleon") trade route (1565–1815), which linked the Americas and Asia across the Pacific. These Spanish expeditions significantly impacted European perceptions of the world and eventually led to numerous naval expeditions across the Atlantic, Indian, and Pacific Oceans, and land expeditions in the Americas, Asia, Africa, and Oceania that continued into the 19th century, followed by [polar exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_exploration "Polar exploration") in the 20th century.

European exploration initiated the [Columbian exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbian_exchange "Columbian exchange") between the [Old World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World "Old World") (Europe, Asia, and Africa) and [New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World "New World") (Americas). This exchange involved the transfer of plants, animals, human populations (including [slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery "Slavery")), [communicable diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infection "Infection"), and culture across the [Eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Hemisphere "Eastern Hemisphere") and [Western Hemispheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Hemisphere "Western Hemisphere"). The Age of Discovery and [European exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_explorations_after_the_Age_of_Discovery "Major explorations after the Age of Discovery") involved [mapping the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_cartography "History of cartography"), shaping a new worldview and facilitating contact with distant civilizations. The continents drawn by European mapmakers developed from abstract "[blobs](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/blob "wikt:blob")" into the outlines more recognizable to us. Simultaneously, the spread of new diseases, especially [affecting American Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_disease_and_epidemics "Native American disease and epidemics"), led to [rapid declines in some populations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_history_of_the_Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas "Population history of the Indigenous peoples of the Americas"). The era saw widespread enslavement, exploitation and military conquest of [Indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples "Indigenous peoples"), concurrent with the growing economic influence and spread of [Western culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_culture "Western culture"), science, and technology leading to a [faster-than-exponential population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estimates_of_historical_world_population "Estimates of historical world population") world-wide.

## Concept

The concept of discovery has been scrutinized, critically highlighting the history of the core term of this [periodization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodization "Periodization"). The term "age of discovery" is in historical literature and still commonly used. [J. H. Parry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._H._Parry "J. H. Parry"), calling the period the *Age of Reconnaissance*, argues that not only was the era one of European explorations, but it also produced the expansion of geographical knowledge and [empirical science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empirical_science "Empirical science"): "It saw also the first major victories of empirical inquiry over authority, the beginnings of that close association of science, technology, and everyday work which is an essential characteristic of the modern western world." [Anthony Pagden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Pagden "Anthony Pagden") draws on the work of [Edmundo O'Gorman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmundo_O%27Gorman "Edmundo O'Gorman") for the statement that "For all Europeans, the events of October 1492 constituted a 'discovery'. Something of which they had no prior knowledge had suddenly presented itself to their gaze." O'Gorman argues that the physical encounter with new territories was less important than the Europeans' effort to integrate this new knowledge into their worldview, what he calls "the invention of America". Pagden examines the origins of the terms "discovery" and "invention". In English, "discovery" and its forms in romance languages derive from "*disco-operio*, meaning to uncover, to reveal, to expose to the gaze", what was revealed existed previously. Few Europeans during the period used the term "invention" for the European encounters, with the exception of [Martin Waldseemüller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Waldseem%C3%BCller "Martin Waldseemüller"), whose [map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waldseem%C3%BCller_map "Waldseemüller map") first used the term "[America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naming_of_the_Americas "Naming of the Americas")".

A central legal concept of the [discovery doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_doctrine "Discovery doctrine"), expounded by the [United States Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_United_States "Supreme Court of the United States") in 1823, draws on assertions of European powers' right to claim land during their explorations. The concept of "discovery" has been used to enforce colonial claiming and discovery, but has been challenged by [Indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples "Indigenous peoples") and researchers. Many indigenous peoples have fundamentally challenged the concept of colonial claiming of "discovery" over their lands and people, as forced and negating indigenous presence.

The period alternatively called the *Age of Exploration*, has been scrutinized through reflections on the [exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploration "Exploration"). Its understanding and use, has been discussed as being framed and used for colonial ventures, discrimination and [exploitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploitation_colonialism "Exploitation colonialism"), by combining it with concepts such as the [frontier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontier "Frontier") (as in [Frontier Thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontier_Thesis "Frontier Thesis")) and [manifest destiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifest_destiny "Manifest destiny"), up to the contemporary age of [space exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_exploration "Space exploration"). Alternatively, the term *contact*, as in [first contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_contact_(anthropology) "First contact (anthropology)"), has been used to shed more light on the age of discovery and colonialism, using the alternative names of *Age of Contact* or *Contact Period*, discussing it as an "unfinished, diverse project".

## Overview

The Portuguese began systematically exploring the Atlantic coast of Africa in 1418, under the sponsorship of [Prince Henry the Navigator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Henry_the_Navigator "Prince Henry the Navigator"). In 1488, [Bartolomeu Dias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartolomeu_Dias "Bartolomeu Dias") reached the Indian Ocean by this route.

In 1492, the [Catholic Monarchs of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Monarchs_of_Spain "Catholic Monarchs of Spain") funded [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus "Christopher Columbus")'s plan to sail west to reach the [Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indies "Indies"), by crossing the Atlantic. Columbus encountered a continent uncharted by Europeans (though it had been explored and [temporarily colonized by the Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norse_colonization_of_North_America "Norse colonization of North America") 500 years earlier). Later, it was called America after [Amerigo Vespucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerigo_Vespucci "Amerigo Vespucci"), a trader working for [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Portugal "Kingdom of Portugal"). Portugal quickly claimed those lands under the terms of the [Treaty of Alcáçovas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Alc%C3%A1%C3%A7ovas "Treaty of Alcáçovas"), but Castile was able to persuade the Pope, who was Castilian, to issue [four papal bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulls_of_Donation "Bulls of Donation") to divide the world into two regions of exploration, where each kingdom had exclusive rights to claim newly discovered lands. These were modified by the [Treaty of Tordesillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Tordesillas "Treaty of Tordesillas"), ratified by [Pope Julius II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Julius_II "Pope Julius II").

In 1498, a Portuguese expedition commanded by [Vasco da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_da_Gama "Vasco da Gama") reached India by sailing around Africa, opening up direct trade with Asia. While other exploratory fleets were sent from Portugal to northern North America, [Portuguese India Armadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_India_Armadas "Portuguese India Armadas") also extended this Eastern oceanic route, touching South America and opening a circuit from the New World to Asia (starting in 1500 by [Pedro Álvares Cabral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_%C3%81lvares_Cabral "Pedro Álvares Cabral")), and explored islands in the South Atlantic and Southern Indian Oceans. The Portuguese sailed further eastward, to the valuable [Spice Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maluku_Islands "Maluku Islands") in 1512, landing in China one year later. [Japan was reached](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanban_trade "Nanban trade") by the Portuguese in 1543. In 1513, Spanish explorer [Vasco Núñez de Balboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_N%C3%BA%C3%B1ez_de_Balboa "Vasco Núñez de Balboa") crossed the [Isthmus of Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmus_of_Panama "Isthmus of Panama") and reached the "other sea" from the New World. Thus, Europe first received news of the eastern and western Pacific within a one-year span around 1512. East and west exploration overlapped in 1522, when a Spanish expedition sailing westward, led by Portuguese navigator [Ferdinand Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Magellan "Ferdinand Magellan") (and, after [his death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mactan "Battle of Mactan") in what is now the Philippines, by navigator [Juan Sebastián Elcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Sebasti%C3%A1n_Elcano "Juan Sebastián Elcano")), completed the first circumnavigation of the world. Spanish *[conquistadors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquistador "Conquistador")* explored the interior of the Americas, and some of the South Pacific islands. Their main objective was to disrupt Portuguese trade in the East.

From 1495, the French, English, and [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic "Dutch Republic") entered the race of exploration, after learning of Columbus' exploits, defying the Iberian monopoly on maritime trade by searching for new routes. The first expedition was led by [John Cabot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cabot "John Cabot") in 1497 to the north, in the service of England, followed by French expeditions to South America and later to North America. Later expeditions went to the Pacific Ocean around South America, and eventually by following the Portuguese around Africa, into the Indian Ocean; discovering Australia in 1606, New Zealand in 1642, and Hawaii in 1778. From the 1580s to the 1640s, Russians explored and conquered almost the whole of [Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_conquest_of_Siberia "Russian conquest of Siberia"), and [claimed Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_colonization_of_North_America "Russian colonization of North America") in the 1730s.

## Background

### Rise of European trade

After the [fall of the Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Western_Roman_Empire "Fall of the Western Roman Empire") largely severed the connection between Europe and lands further east, Christian Europe was largely a backwater compared to the Muslim world, which conquered and incorporated large territories in the Middle East and North Africa. The Christian [Crusades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades "Crusades") to retake the [Holy Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Land "Holy Land") from the Muslims were not a military success, but they did bring Europe into contact with the Middle East and the valuable goods manufactured or traded there. From the 12th century, the European economy was transformed by the interconnecting of river and sea trade routes.

Before the 12th century, an obstacle to trade east of the [Strait of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar "Strait of Gibraltar"), which connected the Mediterranean to the Atlantic Ocean, was Muslim control of territory, including the Iberian Peninsula, as well as the trade monopolies of Christian city-states on the Italian Peninsula, especially [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice "Republic of Venice") and [Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa "Republic of Genoa"). Economic growth of Iberia followed the [Christian reconquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconquista "Reconquista") of [Al-Andalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus "Al-Andalus") in what is now southern Spain and the [siege of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Lisbon "Siege of Lisbon") (1147 AD), in Portugal. The decline of the [Fatimid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimid_Caliphate "Fatimid Caliphate")'s naval strength, which started before the [First Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Crusade "First Crusade"), helped the maritime Italian states, mainly Venice, Genoa and Pisa, dominate trade in the [Eastern Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Mediterranean "Eastern Mediterranean"), with merchants there becoming wealthy and politically influential. Further changing the mercantile situation in the eastern Mediterranean was the waning of Christian [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire "Byzantine Empire") naval power following the death of Emperor [Manuel I Komnenos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_I_Komnenos "Manuel I Komnenos") in 1180, whose dynasty had made notable treaties and concessions with Italian traders, permitting the use of Byzantine Christian ports. The [Norman conquest of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Conquest "Norman Conquest"), in the late 11th century, allowed for peaceful trade on the [North Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea "North Sea"). The [Hanseatic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League "Hanseatic League"), a confederation of merchant guilds and their towns in north Germany, along the North Sea and Baltic Sea, was instrumental in the commercial development of the region. In the 12th century, the regions of [Flanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Flanders "County of Flanders"), [Hainault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Hainaut "County of Hainaut"), and [Brabant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Brabant "Duchy of Brabant") produced the finest quality textiles in northwest Europe, which encouraged merchants from Genoa and Venice to sail there from the Mediterranean, through the Strait of Gibraltar, and up the Atlantic coast. Nicolòzzo Spinola made the first recorded direct voyage from Genoa to Flanders in 1277.

### Technology: Ship design and the compass

Technological advancements that were important to the Age of Exploration were the adoption of the [magnetic compass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_compass "Magnetic compass") and advances in ship design.

The compass was an addition to the ancient method of navigation based on sightings of the sun and stars. It was invented during the Chinese [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty "Han dynasty") and had been used for navigation in China by the 11th century. It was adopted by Arab traders in the Indian Ocean. The compass spread to Europe by the late 12th or early 13th century. Use of the compass for navigation in the Indian Ocean was first mentioned in 1232. The first mention of use of the compass in Europe was in 1180. The Europeans used a "dry" compass, with a needle on a pivot. The compass card was also a European invention.

The ships of the Age of Discovery post-dated the fusion of the northern European and Mediterranean shipbuilding traditions. Prior to the late 13th/early 14th centuries, northern European ships were typically [clinker built](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinker_(boat_building) "Clinker (boat building)"), with a single mast setting a [square sail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_rig "Square rig") and a centre-line [rudder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_nautical_terms_(M%E2%80%93Z)#rudder "Glossary of nautical terms (M–Z)") hung on the [sternpost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_nautical_terms_(M%E2%80%93Z)#sternpost "Glossary of nautical terms (M–Z)") with [pintles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pintle "Pintle") and [gudgeons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gudgeon "Gudgeon"). Their counterparts in the Mediterranean were built with [carvel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carvel_(boat_building) "Carvel (boat building)") hulls, had one or more masts (depending on size) which set [lateen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateen "Lateen") sails, and were steered with quarter-rudders positioned on the side of the hull.

Trade, pilgrimage and war brought ships from each tradition into the other's region, ultimately leading to the copying of features that were new to each. Over the early 14th century, square sails started to be used in the Mediterranean, with the mainmast setting square rig and the mizzen carrying a lateen sail. In the first two decades of the 15th century this arrangement was copied in northern Europe where, by the late 1430s, some ships were built with carvel hulls. The result of this merging of traditions was the [full-rigged ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full-rigged_ship "Full-rigged ship"), a carvel hull with a sternpost-hung pintle-and-gudgeon rudder and three masts: the foremast and mainmast setting square sails and the mizzen a lateen sail. Alongside this type of vessel, the [caravel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravel "Caravel") was used. This type was also carvel built with a sternpost-hung rudder but could be completely lateen rigged or have some square sails.

Very few wrecks of Age of Discovery ships have been found and archaeologically investigated. More is known about Roman and Greek ships of [classical antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_antiquity "Classical antiquity") than those of this period. The caravel is particularly poorly understood, despite the number of "replicas" that have been constructed. However, a particular set of hull construction characteristics have been identified from wrecks of this time, referred to as Iberian Atlantic shipbuilding tradition. They are found in the [Molasses Reef wreck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molasses_Reef_Wreck "Molasses Reef Wreck"), Highbourne Cay wreck, the [Red Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bay,_Newfoundland_and_Labrador#24M "Red Bay, Newfoundland and Labrador") wreck and some sites in European waters.

### Early geographical knowledge and maps

The *[Periplus of the Erythraean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periplus_of_the_Erythraean_Sea "Periplus of the Erythraean Sea")*, a document from 40 to 60 AD, describes a newly discovered route through the [Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea "Red Sea") to India, with descriptions of the markets in towns around the Red Sea, [Persian Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf "Persian Gulf"), and Indian Ocean, including along the east coast of Africa, which states "for beyond these places the unexplored ocean curves around toward the west, and running along by the regions to the south of Aethiopia and Libya and Africa, it mingles with the western sea (possible reference to the Atlantic Ocean)". European medieval knowledge about Asia beyond the reach of the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire "Byzantine Empire") was sourced in partial reports, often obscured by legends, dating back from the conquests of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great "Alexander the Great") and successors. Another source was the [Radhanite Jewish trade networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radhanite "Radhanite") of merchants established as go-betweens between Europe and the Muslim world during the time of the [Crusader states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusader_states "Crusader states").

In 1154, the [Arab geographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_in_medieval_Islam "Geography in medieval Islam") [Muhammad al-Idrisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_al-Idrisi "Muhammad al-Idrisi") created a description of the world and a [world map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_map "World map"), the [Tabula Rogeriana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabula_Rogeriana "Tabula Rogeriana"), at the court of King [Roger II of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_II_of_Sicily "Roger II of Sicily"), but still Africa was only partially known to either Christians, Genoese and Venetians, or the Arab seamen, and its southern extent was unknown. There were reports of great African [Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara "Sahara"), but the knowledge was limited for the Europeans, to the Mediterranean coast and little else, since the Arab blockade of North Africa precluded exploration inland. Knowledge about the Atlantic African coast was fragmented and derived mainly from [old](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_world_maps "Early world maps") Greek and Roman maps based on Carthaginian knowledge, including [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome "Ancient Rome") exploration of [Mauritania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritania "Mauritania"). The Red Sea was barely known and only trade links with the [maritime republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_republics "Maritime republics"), Venice especially, fostered the collection of accurate maritime knowledge. Indian Ocean trade routes were sailed by Arab traders.

By 1400, a Latin translation of [Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy "Ptolemy")'s *[Geographia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographia "Geographia")* reached Italy from Constantinople. The rediscovery of Roman geographical knowledge was a revelation, both for map-making and worldview, although reinforcing the idea that the Indian Ocean was landlocked.

### Medieval European travel (1241–1438)

A prelude to the Age of Discovery was a series of European expeditions crossing [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia "Eurasia") by land in the late Middle Ages. The [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols "Mongols") had threatened Europe, but Mongol states also unified much of Eurasia and, from 1206 on, the *[Pax Mongolica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pax_Mongolica "Pax Mongolica")* allowed safe trade routes and communication lines from the Middle East to China. The close [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_city-states "Italian city-states") links to the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant "Levant") raised curiosity and commercial interest in countries which lay further east.[*[page needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources "Wikipedia:Citing sources")*] There are a few accounts of merchants from North Africa and the Mediterranean, who traded in the Indian Ocean in late medieval times (see also [Indo-Mediterranean § Medieval era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Mediterranean#Medieval_era "Indo-Mediterranean")).

Christian embassies were sent as far as [Karakorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakorum "Karakorum") during the [Mongol invasions of the Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Syria "Mongol invasion of Syria"), from which they gained a greater understanding of the world. The first of these travellers was [Giovanni da Pian del Carpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_da_Pian_del_Carpine "Giovanni da Pian del Carpine"), dispatched by [Pope Innocent IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Innocent_IV "Pope Innocent IV") to the [Great Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Khan "Great Khan"), who journeyed to [Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolia "Mongolia") and back from 1241 to 1247. Russian prince [Yaroslav of Vladimir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaroslav_of_Vladimir "Yaroslav of Vladimir"), and his sons [Alexander Nevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Nevsky "Alexander Nevsky") and [Andrey II of Vladimir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_II_of_Vladimir "Andrey II of Vladimir"), travelled to the Mongolian capital. Though having strong political implications, their journeys left no detailed accounts. Other travellers followed, like French [André de Longjumeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_de_Longjumeau "André de Longjumeau") and Flemish [William of Rubruck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_of_Rubruck "William of Rubruck"), who reached China through Central Asia. [Marco Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Polo "Marco Polo"), a Venetian merchant, dictated an account of journeys throughout Asia from 1271 to 1295, describing being a guest at the [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty "Yuan dynasty") court of [Kublai Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kublai_Khan "Kublai Khan") in *[Travels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Travels_of_Marco_Polo "The Travels of Marco Polo")*. It was read throughout Europe.

The Muslim fleet guarding the Strait of Gibraltar was defeated by Genoa in 1291. In that year, the Genoese attempted their first Atlantic exploration when merchant brothers [Vadino and Ugolino Vivaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadino_and_Ugolino_Vivaldi "Vadino and Ugolino Vivaldi") sailed from Genoa with two galleys, but disappeared off the Moroccan coast, feeding fears of oceanic travel. From 1325 to 1354, a Moroccan scholar from [Tangier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangier "Tangier"), [Ibn Battuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Battuta "Ibn Battuta"), journeyed through North Africa, the Sahara desert, West Africa, Southern Europe, Eastern Europe, the Horn of Africa, the Middle East, and Asia, having reached China. After returning, he dictated an account to a scholar he met in Granada, *[The Rihla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rihla "The Rihla")* ("The Journey"), the unheralded source on his adventures. Between 1357 and 1371, a book of supposed travels compiled by [John Mandeville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Mandeville "John Mandeville") acquired popularity. Despite the unreliable and often fantastical nature of its accounts, it was used as a reference for the East, Egypt, and the Levant in general, asserting the old belief that Jerusalem was the [centre of the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_mundi "Axis mundi"). Following the period of [Timurid relations with Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_relations_with_Europe "Timurid relations with Europe"), in 1439, [Niccolò de' Conti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niccol%C3%B2_de%27_Conti "Niccolò de' Conti") published an account of his travels as a Muslim merchant to India and Southeast Asia. In 1466–1472, Russian merchant [Afanasy Nikitin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afanasy_Nikitin "Afanasy Nikitin") of [Tver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tver "Tver") travelled to India, which he described in his book *[A Journey Beyond the Three Seas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Journey_Beyond_the_Three_Seas "A Journey Beyond the Three Seas")*.

These overland journeys had little immediate effect. The [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire "Mongol Empire") collapsed almost as quickly as it formed and soon the route to the east became more difficult and dangerous. The [Black Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Death "Black Death") of the 14th century also blocked travel and trade for a time.

### Religion

Religion played a critical role in motivating European [expansionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expansionism "Expansionism"). In 1487, Portuguese envoys [Pero da Covilhã](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pero_da_Covilh%C3%A3 "Pero da Covilhã") and [Afonso de Paiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afonso_de_Paiva "Afonso de Paiva") were sent on a [covert mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covert_operation "Covert operation") to gather intelligence on a potential sea route to [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent") and inquire about [Prester John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prester_John "Prester John"), a [Nestorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nestorianism "Nestorianism") patriarch and king, believed to rule over parts of the subcontinent. Covilhã was warmly received upon his arrival in Ethiopia, but forbidden from leaving.

During the Middle Ages, the spread of Christianity throughout Europe fueled the desire to sermonise in lands beyond. This evangelical effort became a significant part of the military conquests of European powers, like [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire "Portuguese Empire"), [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire "Spanish Empire"), and [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire "French colonial empire"), often leading to the conversion of indigenous peoples, voluntarily or forced.

Religious orders such as the [Franciscans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscans "Franciscans"), [Dominicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Order "Dominican Order"), [Augustinians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustinians "Augustinians"), and [Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuits "Jesuits") partook in most missionary endeavours in the [New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World "New World"). By the late 16th and 17th centuries, the latter's presence increased as they sought to reassert their power and revive the Catholic culture of Europe, which had been damaged by the [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation "Reformation").

### Chinese missions (1405–1433)

The Chinese had wide connections through trade in Asia and been sailing to [Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Peninsula "Arabian Peninsula"), [East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Africa "East Africa"), and Egypt since the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty "Tang dynasty") (AD 618–907). Between 1405 and 1421, the third Ming emperor [Yongle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongle_Emperor "Yongle Emperor") sponsored long range [tributary missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary_system_of_China "Tributary system of China") in the Indian Ocean under the command of admiral [Zheng He](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zheng_He "Zheng He").

A large fleet of new [junk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junk_(ship) "Junk (ship)") ships was prepared for the international diplomatic expeditions. The largest of these junks—that the Chinese termed [*bao chuan* (treasure ships)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_treasure_ship "Chinese treasure ship")—may have measured 121 metres (397 ft), and thousands of sailors were involved. The first expedition departed in 1405. At least seven well-documented expeditions were launched, each bigger and more expensive than the last. The fleets visited Arabia, East Africa, India, [Malay Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Archipelago "Malay Archipelago"), and Thailand (then called Siam), exchanging goods along the way. They presented gifts of gold, silver, [porcelain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcelain "Porcelain"), and [silk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk "Silk"); in return, received such novelties as [ostriches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrich "Ostrich"), [zebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zebra "Zebra"), [camels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel "Camel"), [ivory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory "Ivory"), and [giraffes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giraffe "Giraffe"). After the emperor's death, Zheng He led a final expedition departing from Nanking in 1431 and returning to Beijing in 1433. It is likely this last expedition reached as far as [Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar "Madagascar"). The travels were reported by [Ma Huan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma_Huan "Ma Huan"), a Muslim voyager and translator who accompanied Zheng He on three of the expeditions, his account published as the *[Yingya Shenglan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yingya_Shenglan "Yingya Shenglan")* (Overall Survey of the Ocean's Shores) (1433).

The voyages had a significant and lasting effect on the organization of a [maritime network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Silk_Road "Maritime Silk Road"), using and creating nodes and conduits in its wake, thereby restructuring international and cross-cultural relationships and exchanges. It was especially impactful as no other polity had exerted naval dominance over all sectors of the Indian Ocean, prior to these voyages. The Ming promoted alternative nodes as a strategy to establish control over the network. For instance, due to Chinese involvement, ports such as Malacca (in Southeast Asia), [Cochin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kochi "Kochi") (Malabar Coast), and [Malindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malindi "Malindi") (Swahili Coast) had grown as key alternatives to other established ports. The appearance of the Ming treasure fleet generated and intensified competition among contending polities and rivals, each seeking an alliance with the Ming. The expeditions developed into a maritime trade enterprise, with imperial control over local markets and court-monitored transactions, generating revenue for China and its partners. They boosted regional trade and production, caused a supply shock in Eurasia and led to price spikes in Europe in the early 15th century.

The tributary relations promoted during the voyages manifested a trend toward cross-regional interconnections and early [globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization "Globalization") in Asia and Africa. Diplomatic relations were built on mutually beneficial maritime trade and China's strong naval presence in foreign waters, with Chinese naval superiority being a key factor in these interactions. The voyages brought about the Western Ocean's [regional integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_integration "Regional integration") and increase in [international circulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobilities "Mobilities") of people, ideas, and goods. It provided a platform for [cosmopolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmopolitanism "Cosmopolitanism") discourses, which took place in locations such as the ships of the Ming treasure fleet, the Ming capitals of Nanjing as well as Beijing, and the banquet receptions organized by the Ming court for foreign representatives. Diverse groups of people from maritime countries congregated, interacted, and traveled together as the treasure fleet sailed from and to China. For the first time, the maritime region from China to Africa was under the dominance of a single imperial power and allowed for the creation of a cosmopolitan space.

These long-distance journeys were not followed up, as the Ming dynasty retreated in the *[haijin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haijin "Haijin")*, a policy of [isolationism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isolationism "Isolationism"), having limited maritime trade. Travels were halted abruptly after the emperor's death, as the Chinese lost interest in what they termed barbarian lands, turning inward, and successor emperors felt the expeditions were harmful to the Chinese state; [Hongxi Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongxi_Emperor "Hongxi Emperor") ended further expeditions and [Xuande Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuande_Emperor "Xuande Emperor") suppressed much of the information about Zheng He's voyages.

## Atlantic Ocean (1419–1507)

From the 8th until the 15th century, the [Republic of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice "Republic of Venice") and neighboring [maritime republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_republics "Maritime republics") held the monopoly of European trade with the Middle East. The [silk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road "Silk Road") and [spice trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice_trade "Spice trade"), involving spices, [incense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incense "Incense"), herbs, drugs, and [opium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium "Opium"), made these Mediterranean city-states phenomenally rich. Spices were among the most expensive and demanded products of the Middle Ages, as they were used in [medieval medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_medicine_of_Western_Europe "Medieval medicine of Western Europe"), religious rituals, cosmetics, perfumery, and food additives and preservatives. They were imported from Asia and Africa.

Muslim traders dominated maritime routes throughout the Indian Ocean, tapping source regions in the [Far East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_East "Far East") and shipping for trading emporiums in India, mainly [Calicut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozhikode "Kozhikode"), westward to [Hormuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormuz,_Iran "Hormuz, Iran") in the [Persian Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf "Persian Gulf") and [Jeddah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeddah "Jeddah") in the [Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea "Red Sea"). From there, overland routes led to the Mediterranean. Afterwards, the routes into Asia through the Black Sea and through Armenia and Persia became blocked by wars and banditry as the Mongol states disintegrated. The trade route from the Persian Gulf to the Mediterranean by land is also dangerous due to the unstable territorial control of the Mamluk. The trade from India went almost entirely into the Red Sea. Nevertheless, European spices have not become more expensive as a result.

Forced to reduce activity in the Black Sea, and at war with Venice, the [Genoese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa "Republic of Genoa") had turned to North African trade of wheat, olive oil and a search for silver and gold. Europeans had a constant [deficit in silver and gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_Revolution#Monetary_factors "Commercial Revolution"), as it only went out, spent on eastern trade now cut off. Several European mines were exhausted, The lack of [bullion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullion "Bullion") led to the development of a complex banking system to manage the risks in trade (the first state bank, *[Banco di San Giorgio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Saint_George "Bank of Saint George")*, was founded in 1407 at Genoa). Sailing into the ports of [Bruges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruges "Bruges") and England, Genoese communities were then established in Portugal, who profited from their enterprise and financial expertise.

European sailing had been primarily close to land [cabotage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabotage "Cabotage"), guided by [portolan charts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portolan_chart "Portolan chart"). These charts specified proven ocean routes guided by coastal landmarks: sailors departed from a known point, followed a compass heading, and tried to identify their location by its landmarks. For the first oceanic exploration Europeans used the compass, and advances in [cartography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartography "Cartography") and astronomy. Arab navigational tools like the [astrolabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrolabe "Astrolabe") and [quadrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrant_(instrument) "Quadrant (instrument)") were used for [celestial navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_navigation "Celestial navigation").

The Muslim lands in Asia were more economically developed, had better infrastructure, despite Europe's economic changes brought by the [Black Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Death "Black Death"), allowing for more freedoms. The Islamic [gunpowder empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunpowder_empires "Gunpowder empires") concealed knowledge from European Christian traders about where lucrative locations such as Indonesia were.

### Portuguese exploration

In 1317, King [Denis of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_of_Portugal "Denis of Portugal") made an agreement with Genoese merchant sailor [Manuel Pessanha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Pessanha "Manuel Pessanha"), appointing him first [admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral "Admiral") of the [Portuguese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Navy "Portuguese Navy"), to defend the country against Muslim pirate raids. Outbreaks of [bubonic plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubonic_plague "Bubonic plague") led to depopulation in the second half of the 14th century: only the sea offered alternatives, with most population settling in fishing and trading coastal areas. Between 1325 and 1357, [Afonso IV of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afonso_IV_of_Portugal "Afonso IV of Portugal") encouraged maritime commerce and ordered the first explorations. The [Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Islands "Canary Islands"), already known to the Genoese, were claimed as officially discovered under the patronage of the Portuguese, but in 1344, Castile disputed them.

To ensure their trade monopoly, Europeans, beginning with the Portuguese, attempted to install a Mediterranean system of trade which used military intimidation, to divert trade through ports they controlled; there it could be taxed. In 1415, [Ceuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceuta "Ceuta"), in North Africa, was [conquered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_Ceuta "Conquest of Ceuta") by the Portuguese aiming to control navigation of the African coast. Young prince [Henry the Navigator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_the_Navigator "Henry the Navigator") became aware of profit possibilities in the [trans-Saharan trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Saharan_trade "Trans-Saharan trade") routes. For centuries [slave and gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Saharan_slave_trade "Trans-Saharan slave trade") trade routes linking West Africa with the Mediterranean passed over the Western Sahara Desert, controlled by the Moors. Henry wished to know how far Muslim territories in Africa extended, hoping to bypass them and trade directly with West Africa by sea, find allies in legendary Christian lands like the supposed long-lost kingdom of [Prester John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prester_John "Prester John") and probe whether it was possible to reach the [Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indies "Indies") by sea, the source of the lucrative [spice trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice_trade "Spice trade"). He invested in sponsoring voyages down the coast of [Mauritania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritania "Mauritania"), gathering merchants, shipowners and stakeholders interested in new sea lanes. Soon the Atlantic islands of [Madeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira "Madeira") (1419) and the [Azores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azores "Azores") (1427) were reached. The expedition leader who established settlements on Madeira, was explorer [João Gonçalves Zarco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_Gon%C3%A7alves_Zarco "João Gonçalves Zarco").

Europeans did not know what lay beyond Cape Non ([Cape Chaunar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Chaunar "Cape Chaunar")) on the African coast, and whether it was possible to return once crossed. Nautical myths warned of monsters or an edge of the world, but Henry's navigation challenged such beliefs: starting in 1421, systematic sailing overcame it, reaching the difficult [Cape Bojador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Bojador "Cape Bojador") that in 1434 one of Henry's captains, [Gil Eanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gil_Eanes "Gil Eanes"), finally passed.

From 1440, [caravels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravel "Caravel") were extensively used for the exploration of the coast of Africa. This was an Iberian ship type, used for fishing, commerce and military purposes. It had a sternpost-mounted rudder, a shallow draft helpful in exploring coastlines, a good sailing performance, with a [windward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windward_and_leeward "Windward and leeward") ability. The lateen rig was less useful when sailing downwind – which explains [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus "Christopher Columbus") ([Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language "Italian language"): *Cristoforo Colombo*) re-rigging the [*Niña*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ni%C3%B1a_(ship) "Niña (ship)") with [square rig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_rig "Square rig").

For [celestial navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_navigation "Celestial navigation") the Portuguese used the [ephemerides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephemeris "Ephemeris"), which experienced a remarkable diffusion in the 15th century. These were astronomical charts plotting the location of the stars. Published in 1496 by Jewish astronomer and mathematician [Abraham Zacuto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Zacuto "Abraham Zacuto"), the Almanac Perpetuum included some of these tables for the movements of stars. These revolutionized navigation, allowing the calculation of [latitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latitude "Latitude"). Exact [longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitude "Longitude") remained elusive from mariners for centuries. Using the caravel, systematic exploration continued southerly, advancing one degree a year. [Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegal "Senegal") and [Cape Verde Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap-Vert "Cap-Vert") were reached in 1445 and in 1446, [Álvaro Fernandes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lvaro_Fernandes "Álvaro Fernandes") pushed on almost as far as present-day [Sierra Leone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Leone "Sierra Leone").

In 1453, the [fall of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Constantinople "Fall of Constantinople") to the [Ottomans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turks "Ottoman Turks") was a perceived blow to Christendom and established business links with the East. In 1455, [Pope Nicholas V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_V "Pope Nicholas V") issued the [bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_bull "Papal bull") *[Romanus Pontifex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanus_Pontifex "Romanus Pontifex")* reinforcing the *[Dum Diversas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dum_Diversas "Dum Diversas")* (1452), granting all lands and seas discovered beyond Cape Bojador to King [Afonso V of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afonso_V_of_Portugal "Afonso V of Portugal") and successors, as well as cutting off trade to and permitting increased war against Muslims and pagans, initiating a *[mare clausum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mare_clausum "Mare clausum")* policy in the Atlantic, declaring it close to other states. The king, who had been inquiring of Genoese experts about a seaway to India, commissioned the [Fra Mauro world map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fra_Mauro_map "Fra Mauro map"), which arrived in Lisbon in 1459. In 1456, [Diogo Gomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diogo_Gomes "Diogo Gomes") reached the [Cape Verde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verde "Cape Verde") archipelago. In the next decade captains at the service of Prince Henry, discovered the remaining islands which were occupied during the 15th century. The Gulf of Guinea was reached in the 1460s.

#### After Prince Henry

In 1460, [Pedro de Sintra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_de_Sintra "Pedro de Sintra") reached Sierra Leone. Prince Henry died in November after which, given the meagre revenues, exploration was granted to Lisbon merchant [Fernão Gomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fern%C3%A3o_Gomes "Fernão Gomes") in 1469, who in exchange for the monopoly of trade in the Gulf of Guinea had to explore 100 miles (161 kilometres) each year for five years. With his sponsorship, explorers [João de Santarém](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_de_Santar%C3%A9m "João de Santarém"), [Pedro Escobar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Escobar "Pedro Escobar"), Lopo Gonçalves, [Fernão do Pó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fern%C3%A3o_do_P%C3%B3 "Fernão do Pó"), and Pedro de Sintra made it beyond those goals. They reached the Southern Hemisphere and islands of the Gulf of Guinea, including [São Tomé and Príncipe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Tom%C3%A9_and_Pr%C3%ADncipe "São Tomé and Príncipe") and [Elmina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmina "Elmina") in 1471. There, in what came to be called the "Gold Coast", in today's [Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana "Ghana"), a thriving alluvial gold trade was found among the natives, Arab and [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbers "Berbers") traders.

In 1478, during the [War of the Castilian Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Castilian_Succession "War of the Castilian Succession"), near the coast at [Elmina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmina "Elmina") a [large battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Guinea "Battle of Guinea") was fought between a Castilian armada of 35 caravels, and a Portuguese fleet for the hegemony of the Guinea trade. The war ended with a Portuguese victory, followed by official recognition by the Catholic Monarchs of Portuguese sovereignty over most of the disputed West African territories embodied in the Treaty of Alcáçovas, 1479.

In 1481, [João II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_II_of_Portugal "John II of Portugal") decided to build [São Jorge da Mina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmina_Castle "Elmina Castle") [factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factory_(trading_post) "Factory (trading post)"). In 1482, the [Congo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_River "Congo River") was explored by [Diogo Cão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diogo_C%C3%A3o "Diogo Cão"), who in 1486 continued to [Cape Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Cross "Cape Cross") (modern [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia "Namibia")).

The next crucial breakthrough was in 1488, when [Bartolomeu Dias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartolomeu_Dias "Bartolomeu Dias") rounded the southern tip of Africa, which he named Cabo das Tormentas, "Cape of Storms", then sailing east as far as the mouth of the [Great Fish River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Fish_River "Great Fish River"), proving the Indian Ocean was accessible from the Atlantic. [Pero da Covilhã](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pero_da_Covilh%C3%A3 "Pero da Covilhã"), sent out travelling secretly overland, had reached Ethiopia having collected important information about the Red Sea and Quenia coast, suggesting a sea route to the Indies would soon be forthcoming. Soon the cape was renamed by King [John II of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_II_of_Portugal "John II of Portugal") the *[Cape of Good Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_of_Good_Hope "Cape of Good Hope")*, because of the optimism engendered by the possibility of a sea route to India, proving false the view that had existed since [Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy "Ptolemy") that the Indian Ocean was [land-locked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landlocked_country "Landlocked country").

Based on later stories of the [phantom island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_island "Phantom island") known as [Bacalao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacalao_(phantom_island) "Bacalao (phantom island)") and the carvings on [Dighton Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dighton_Rock "Dighton Rock") some have speculated that Portuguese explorer [João Vaz Corte-Real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_Vaz_Corte-Real "João Vaz Corte-Real") discovered [Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newfoundland "Newfoundland") in 1473, but the sources are considered unreliable.

### Spanish exploration: Columbus's landfall in the Americas

Portugal's Iberian rival, [Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castile "Crown of Castile"), had begun to establish its rule over the Canary Islands in 1402, but became distracted by internal Iberian politics and the repelling of Islamic invasion attempts through most of the 15th century. Following the unification of the crowns of Castile and Aragon, an emerging modern Spain became committed to the search for new trade routes overseas. The [Crown of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon "Crown of Aragon") had been an important maritime power, controlling territories in eastern Spain, southwestern France, major islands like [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicily "Sicily"), [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta "Malta"), and the [Kingdom of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples "Kingdom of Naples") and [Sardinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardinia "Sardinia"), with mainland possessions as far as Greece. In 1492 the [joint rulers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Monarchs "Catholic Monarchs") conquered the [Moorish kingdom of Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Granada "Emirate of Granada"), which had been providing Castile with African goods through tribute, and decided to fund [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus "Christopher Columbus")'s expedition in the hope of bypassing Portugal's monopoly on West African sea routes, to reach "the Indies" (East and South Asia) by travelling west. In 1485 and 1488, Columbus had presented the project to king [John II of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_II_of_Portugal "John II of Portugal"), who rejected it.

On 3 August 1492, Columbus departed from [Palos de la Frontera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palos_de_la_Frontera "Palos de la Frontera"). Land was sighted on 12 October, and Columbus called the island *San Salvador* ([Guanahani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanahani "Guanahani") now in [The Bahamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bahamas "The Bahamas")), in what he thought to be the [East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Indies "East Indies"). Columbus explored the north coast of Cuba and [Hispaniola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispaniola "Hispaniola"), by 5 December. He was received by the native [cacique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacique "Cacique") [Guacanagari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guacanagar%C3%ADx "Guacanagaríx"), who gave him permission to leave some men behind.

Columbus left 39 and founded the settlement of *[La Navidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Navidad "La Navidad")* in what is now [Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiti "Haiti"). Before returning to Spain, he kidnapped 10-25 natives. Only 7-8 of the 'Indians' arrived alive, but they made an impression on [Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville "Seville"). On 15 March 1493, he arrived in [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona "Barcelona"), where he reported to Isabella and Ferdinand. Word of his discovery of new lands [spread throughout Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus%27s_Letter_on_the_First_Voyage "Columbus's Letter on the First Voyage").

Columbus and other Spanish explorers were initially disappointed with their discoveries—unlike Africa or Asia, the Caribbean islanders had little to trade. The islands thus became the focus of colonization efforts. It was not until the continent was explored that Spain found the wealth it had sought.

### Treaty of Tordesillas (1494)

Shortly after Columbus's return from what would be called the "West Indies", a division of influence became necessary to avoid conflict between the Spaniards and Portuguese. On 4 May 1493, two months after Columbus's arrival, the [Catholic Monarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Monarchs "Catholic Monarchs") received a [bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_bull "Papal bull") (*[Inter caetera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_caetera "Inter caetera")*) from [Pope Alexander VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Alexander_VI "Pope Alexander VI") stating all lands west and south of a pole-to-pole line 100 leagues west and south of the [Azores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azores "Azores") or the Cape Verde Islands should belong to Castile and, later, all mainlands and islands then belonging to India. It did not mention Portugal, which could not claim newly discovered lands east of the line.

King [John II of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_II_of_Portugal "John II of Portugal") was displeased with the arrangement, feeling it gave him too little land—preventing him from reaching India, his goal. He negotiated directly with Ferdinand and Isabella to move the line west, allowing him to claim newly discovered lands east of it. In 1494, the [Treaty of Tordesillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Tordesillas "Treaty of Tordesillas") divided the world between Portugal and Spain. Portugal gained control over Africa, Asia, and eastern South America (Brazil), encompassing everything outside Europe east of a line drawn 370 [leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_(unit) "League (unit)") west of the [Cape Verde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verde "Cape Verde") islands. The Spaniards received everything west of this line, including the islands discovered by Columbus on [his first voyage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Voyage_of_Columbus "First Voyage of Columbus"). The dividing line, situated about halfway between Portuguese Cape Verde and Spanish discoveries in the Caribbean, split the known world of Atlantic islands evenly.

In 1500, [Pedro Álvares Cabral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_%C3%81lvares_Cabral "Pedro Álvares Cabral"), initially considering the Brazilian coast as a large island, claimed it for Portugal east of the dividing line. This claim was acknowledged by the Spaniards. Cabral, heading towards India, followed a corridor in the Atlantic negotiated by the treaty for favorable winds. Later the Spanish territory would prove to include huge areas of the continental mainland of North and South America, though Portuguese-controlled Brazil would expand across the line, and settlements by other European powers ignored the treaty.

### The Americas: The New World

Little of the divided area had actually been seen by Europeans, as it was only divided by a geographical definition rather than control on the ground. The desire to compete with the Ottoman Empire and Columbus's first voyage spurred further maritime exploration and, from 1497, other explorers headed west.

#### North America

That year [John Cabot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cabot "John Cabot") ([Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language "Italian language"): *Giovanni Caboto*), also a commissioned Italian, got [letters patent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letters_patent "Letters patent") from King [Henry VII of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VII_of_England "Henry VII of England"). Sailing from [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol "Bristol") Cabot crossed the Atlantic from a northerly latitude hoping the voyage to the "West Indies" would be shorter and made landfall in North America, possibly [Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Newfoundland_and_Labrador "History of Newfoundland and Labrador").

Prior to Cabot's voyage, the furthest European expeditions in the North Atlantic had been by Norse explorers, who established settlements in [Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%27Anse_aux_Meadows "L'Anse aux Meadows") and [Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norse_settlements_in_Greenland "Norse settlements in Greenland"), which were abandoned in the 11th century and mid-15th century, respectively. Merchants from Bristol had sponsored exploratory voyages in the 1480s, which did not report anything of note.

In 1499, [João Fernandes Lavrador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_Fernandes_Lavrador "João Fernandes Lavrador") was licensed by the King of Portugal and together with [Pero de Barcelos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pero_de_Barcelos "Pero de Barcelos") they first sighted [Labrador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labrador "Labrador"), which was granted and named after him. Between 1499 and 1502, the brothers [Gaspar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaspar_Corte-Real "Gaspar Corte-Real") and [Miguel Corte Real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Corte-Real "Miguel Corte-Real") explored and named the coasts of [Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland "Greenland") and Newfoundland. Both explorations are noted in the 1502 [Cantino planisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantino_planisphere "Cantino planisphere").

#### The "True Indies" and Brazil

In 1497, newly crowned King [Manuel I of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_I_of_Portugal "Manuel I of Portugal") sent an exploratory fleet eastwards, fulfilling his predecessor's project of finding a route to the Indies. In July 1499, news spread that the Portuguese had reached the "true Indies", as a letter was dispatched by the Portuguese king to the Spanish Monarchs.

The third expedition by Columbus in 1498 was the beginning of the first successful Spanish colonization in the [West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies "West Indies"), on the island of [Hispaniola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispaniola "Hispaniola"). Despite growing doubts, Columbus refused to accept he had not reached the Indies. He discovered the mouth of the [Orinoco River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orinoco_River "Orinoco River") on the north coast of South America and thought the huge quantity of fresh water coming from it could only be from a continental land mass, which he was certain was Asia.

As shipping between [Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville "Seville") and the West Indies grew, knowledge of the Caribbean islands, Central America and the northern coast of South America increased. One of these Spanish fleets, that of [Alonso de Ojeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alonso_de_Ojeda "Alonso de Ojeda") and Amerigo Vespucci in 1499–1500 reached land at the coast of what is now [Guyana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guyana "Guyana"), where the two seem to have separated in opposite directions. Vespucci sailed southward, discovering the mouth of the [Amazon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_River "Amazon River") in July 1499, and reaching 6°S, in present-day north east Brazil, before turning around.

[Vicente Yáñez Pinzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicente_Y%C3%A1%C3%B1ez_Pinzon "Vicente Yáñez Pinzon") was blown off course and reached what is now the northeast coast of Brazil on 26 January 1500, exploring as far south as the present-day state of [Pernambuco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pernambuco "Pernambuco"). His fleet was the first to fully enter the Amazon River estuary, which he named *Saint Mary's River of the Freshwater Sea*. The land was too far east for the Castilians to claim under the Treaty of Tordesillas, but the discovery created Castilian interest, with a second voyage by Pinzon in 1508 (the [Pinzón–Solís voyage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinz%C3%B3n%E2%80%93Sol%C3%ADs_voyage "Pinzón–Solís voyage")) and a voyage in 1515–1516 by a navigator of the 1508 expedition, [Juan Díaz de Solís](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_D%C3%ADaz_de_Sol%C3%ADs "Juan Díaz de Solís"). The 1515–1516 expedition was spurred on by reports of Portuguese exploration of the region. It ended when de Solís and some of his crew disappeared when exploring the [River Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADo_de_la_Plata "Río de la Plata") in a boat, but what they found reignited Spanish interest, and colonization began in 1531.

In April 1500, the [second Portuguese India Armada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Portuguese_India_Armada_(Cabral,_1500) "2nd Portuguese India Armada (Cabral, 1500)"), headed by [Pedro Álvares Cabral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_%C3%81lvares_Cabral "Pedro Álvares Cabral"), with a crew of expert captains, encountered the Brazilian coast as it swung westward in the Atlantic while performing a large [volta do mar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volta_do_mar "Volta do mar") to avoid becalming in the [Gulf of Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Guinea "Gulf of Guinea"). On 21 April, a mountain was seen and named *[Monte Pascoal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Pascoal "Monte Pascoal")*, and on 22 April Cabral landed. On 25 April, the entire fleet sailed into the harbour they named *[Porto Seguro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto_Seguro "Porto Seguro")*. Cabral perceived that the new land lay east of the line of Tordesillas, and sent an envoy to Portugal with the discovery in letters, including the [letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carta_de_Pero_Vaz_de_Caminha "Carta de Pero Vaz de Caminha") of [Pero Vaz de Caminha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pero_Vaz_de_Caminha "Pero Vaz de Caminha"). Believing the land to be an island, he named it [Ilha de Vera Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilha_de_Vera_Cruz "Ilha de Vera Cruz"). Some historians have suggested the Portuguese may have encountered the South American bulge earlier while sailing the "volta do mar", hence the insistence of John II in moving the line west of Tordesillas in 1494—so his landing in Brazil may not have been accidental; although John's motivation may have simply been to claim new lands in the Atlantic more easily. From the east coast, the fleet then turned eastward towards the southern tip of Africa and India. Cabral was the first captain to touch four continents, leading the first expedition that connected and united Europe, Africa, the New World, and Asia.

At the invitation of King Manuel I of Portugal, Amerigo Vespucci participated as an observer in the exploratory voyages to the east coast of South America. The expeditions became widely known in Europe after two accounts attributed to him, published between 1502 and 1504, suggested the newly discovered lands were not the Indies but a "New World", the *Mundus novus*; this is also the Latin title of a contemporary document based on Vespucci letters to [Lorenzo di Pierfrancesco de' Medici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_di_Pierfrancesco_de%27_Medici "Lorenzo di Pierfrancesco de' Medici"), which had become popular in Europe. It was understood that Columbus had not reached Asia but found a new continent, the Americas. The Americas were named in 1507 by [cartographers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartography "Cartography") [Martin Waldseemüller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waldseem%C3%BCller_map "Waldseemüller map") and [Matthias Ringmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Ringmann "Matthias Ringmann"), after Amerigo Vespucci.

From 1501 to 1502, one of these Portuguese expeditions, led by [Gonçalo Coelho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gon%C3%A7alo_Coelho "Gonçalo Coelho"), sailed south along the coast of South America to the bay of present-day [Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro "Rio de Janeiro"). Vespucci's account states that the expedition reached the latitude "South Pole elevation 52° S", in the "cold" latitudes of what is now southern [Patagonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patagonia "Patagonia"), before turning back. Vespucci wrote that they headed toward the southwest and south, following "a long, unbending coastline", apparently coincident with the southern South American coast. This seems controversial, since he changed part of his description in the subsequent letter, stating a shift, from about 32° S (Southern Brazil) to the south-southeast, to open sea, maintaining that they reached 50°/52° S.

In 1503, [Binot Paulmier de Gonneville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binot_Paulmier_de_Gonneville "Binot Paulmier de Gonneville"), challenging the Portuguese policy of *[mare clausum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mare_clausum "Mare clausum")*, led one of the earliest French [Normand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normandy "Normandy") and [Breton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brittany "Brittany") expeditions to Brazil. He intended to sail to the East Indies, but near the Cape of Good Hope, his ship was diverted to the west by a storm, and landed in the present-day state of [Santa Catarina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Catarina_(state) "Santa Catarina (state)") (southern Brazil), on 5 January 1504.

From 1511 to 1512, Portuguese captains [João de Lisboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_de_Lisboa "João de Lisboa") and Estevão de Fróis reached the [River Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_La_Plata "Rio de La Plata") estuary in present-day [Uruguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruguay "Uruguay") and Argentina, and went as far south as the present-day [Gulf of San Matias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_San_Matias "Gulf of San Matias") at 42°S. The expedition reached a cape extending north to south which they called Cape of "Santa Maria" ([Punta del Este](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punta_del_Este "Punta del Este")); and after 40°S they found a "Cape" or "a point or place extending into the sea", and a "Gulf". After they had navigated for nearly 300 km (186 mi) to round the cape, they again sighted the continent on the other side and steered towards the northwest, but a storm prevented them from making headway. Driven away by the *Tramontane* or north wind, they retraced their course. [Christopher de Haro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_de_Haro "Christopher de Haro"), a [Fleming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flemish_people "Flemish people") of [Sephardic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardic "Sephardic") origin, who would serve the Spanish Crown after 1516, believed the navigators had discovered a southern *strait* to west and Asia.

In 1519, an expedition sent by the Spanish Crown to find a way to Asia was led by the experienced Portuguese navigator [Ferdinand Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Magellan "Ferdinand Magellan"). The fleet explored the rivers and bays as it charted the South American coast, until it found a way to the Pacific Ocean through the [Strait of Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Magellan "Strait of Magellan").

From 1524 to 1525, [Aleixo Garcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleixo_Garcia "Aleixo Garcia"), a Portuguese conquistador, led a private expedition of shipwrecked Castilian and Portuguese adventurers, who recruited about 2,000 [Guaraní Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guaran%C3%AD_people "Guaraní people"). They explored the territories of present-day southern Brazil, [Paraguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguay "Paraguay"), and Bolivia, using the native trail network, the *Peabiru*. They were the first Europeans to cross the [Chaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Chaco "Gran Chaco") and reach the outer territories of the [Inca Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_Empire "Inca Empire") on the hills of the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes "Andes").

## Indian Ocean (1497–1513)

### Vasco da Gama's route to India

Protected from direct Spanish competition by the [Treaty of Tordesillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Tordesillas "Treaty of Tordesillas"), Portuguese eastward exploration and colonization continued apace. Twice, in 1485 and 1488, Portugal officially rejected [Genoese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa "Genoa") [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus "Christopher Columbus")'s idea of reaching India by sailing westwards. King [John II of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_II_of_Portugal "John II of Portugal")'s experts rejected it, for they held the opinion that Columbus's estimation of a travel distance of 2,400 miles (3,860 km) was low, and in part because [Bartolomeu Dias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartolomeu_Dias "Bartolomeu Dias") departed in 1487 trying the rounding of the southern tip of Africa. They believed that sailing east would require a far shorter journey. Dias's return from the [Cape of Good Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_of_Good_Hope "Cape of Good Hope") in 1488, and [Pero da Covilhã](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pero_da_Covilh%C3%A3 "Pero da Covilhã")'s travel to Ethiopia overland indicated that the richness of the [Indian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean "Indian Ocean") was accessible from the Atlantic. A long-overdue expedition was prepared.

In July 1497, a small exploratory fleet of four ships and about 170 men left [Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon "Lisbon") under the command of [Vasco da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_da_Gama "Vasco da Gama"). By December the fleet passed the [Great Fish River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Fish_River "Great Fish River")—where Dias had turned back—and sailed into waters unknown to the Europeans. Sailing into the Indian Ocean, da Gama entered a maritime region that had three different and well-developed trade circuits. The one da Gama encountered connected [Mogadishu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogadishu "Mogadishu") on the east coast of Africa; [Aden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aden "Aden"), at the tip of the Arabian peninsula; the Persian port of Hormuz; [Cambay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambay "Cambay"), in northwestern India; and Calicut, in southwestern India. On 20 May 1498, they arrived at Calicut. The efforts of Vasco da Gama to get favorable trading conditions were hampered by the low value of their goods, compared with the valuable goods traded there.[*[page needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources "Wikipedia:Citing sources")*] Two years and two days after departure, Gama and a survivor crew of 55 men returned in glory to Portugal as the first ships to sail directly from Europe to India. Da Gama's voyage is romanticized in the [Os Lusíadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Os_Lus%C3%ADadas "Os Lusíadas"), an [epic poem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_poetry "Epic poetry") by fellow discovery-era traveler [Luís de Camões](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu%C3%ADs_de_Cam%C3%B5es "Luís de Camões"). The poem is widely regarded as Portugal's greatest literary achievement.

In 1500, a second, larger fleet of thirteen ships and about 1500 men were sent to India. Under the command of [Pedro Álvares Cabral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_%C3%81lvares_Cabral "Pedro Álvares Cabral"), they made the first landfall on the Brazilian coast, giving Portugal its claim. Later, in the Indian Ocean, one of Cabral's ships reached [Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar "Madagascar") (1501), which was partly explored by [Tristão da Cunha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trist%C3%A3o_da_Cunha "Tristão da Cunha") in 1507; [Mauritius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritius "Mauritius") was discovered in 1507, [Socotra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socotra "Socotra") occupied in 1506. In the same year [Lourenço de Almeida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louren%C3%A7o_de_Almeida "Lourenço de Almeida") landed in Sri Lanka, the eastern island named "[Taprobane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Sri_Lanka "Names of Sri Lanka")" in remote accounts of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great "Alexander the Great")'s and 4th-century BC Greek [geographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographer "Geographer") [Megasthenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megasthenes "Megasthenes"). On the Asiatic mainland, the first [factories (trading-posts)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factory_(trading_post) "Factory (trading post)") were established at Kochi and Calicut (1501) and then [Goa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Goa "Old Goa") (1510).

### The "Spice Islands" and China

The Portuguese continued sailing eastward from India, entering a second existing circuit of the Indian Ocean trade, from Calicut and [Quillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kollam "Kollam") in India, to Southeast Asia, including Malacca and [Palembang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palembang "Palembang"). In 1511, [Afonso de Albuquerque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afonso_de_Albuquerque "Afonso de Albuquerque") conquered Malacca for Portugal, then the center of Asian trade. East of Malacca, Albuquerque sent several diplomatic missions: [Duarte Fernandes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duarte_Fernandes "Duarte Fernandes") as the first European envoy to the [Kingdom of Siam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayutthaya_Kingdom "Ayutthaya Kingdom") (modern Thailand).

Learning the location of the so-called "spice islands", heretofore a secret from the Europeans, were the Spice Islands, mainly the [Banda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banda_Islands "Banda Islands"), then the world's only source of [nutmeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutmeg "Nutmeg") and [cloves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clove "Clove"). Reaching these was the main purpose for the Portuguese voyages in the Indian Ocean. Albuquerque sent an expedition led by [António de Abreu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio_de_Abreu "António de Abreu") to Banda (via [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java "Java") and the [Lesser Sunda Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Sunda_Islands "Lesser Sunda Islands")), where they were the first Europeans to arrive in early 1512, after taking a route through which they also reached first the islands of [Buru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buru "Buru"), [Ambon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambon_Island "Ambon Island"), and [Seram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seram "Seram"). From Banda Abreu returned to Malacca, while his vice-captain [Francisco Serrão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Serr%C3%A3o "Francisco Serrão"), after a separation forced by a shipwreck and heading north, reached once again Ambon and sank off [Ternate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ternate "Ternate"), where he obtained a license to build a Portuguese fortress-factory: the Fort of São João Baptista de Ternate, which founded the Portuguese presence in the [Malay Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Archipelago "Malay Archipelago").

In May 1513, [Jorge Álvares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_%C3%81lvares "Jorge Álvares"), one of the Portuguese envoys, reached China. Although he was the first to land on [Lintin Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nei_Lingding_Island "Nei Lingding Island") in the [Pearl River Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River_Delta "Pearl River Delta"), it was [Rafael Perestrello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Perestrello "Rafael Perestrello")—a cousin of the famed [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus "Christopher Columbus")—who became the first European explorer to land on the southern coast of mainland China and trade in [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou "Guangzhou") in 1516, commanding a [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire "Portuguese Empire") vessel with crew from a Malaccan junk that had sailed from Malacca. [Fernão Pires de Andrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fern%C3%A3o_Pires_de_Andrade "Fernão Pires de Andrade") visited Canton in 1517 and opened up trade with China. The Portuguese were defeated by the Chinese in 1521 at the [Battle of Tunmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tunmen "Battle of Tunmen") and in 1522 at the [Battle of Xicaowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Xicaowan "Battle of Xicaowan"), during which the Chinese captured Portuguese [breech-loading swivel guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breech-loading_swivel_gun "Breech-loading swivel gun") and reverse engineered the technology, calling them "Folangji" 佛郎機 ([Frankish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farang "Farang")) guns, since the Portuguese were called "Folangji" by the Chinese. After a few decades, hostilities between the Portuguese and Chinese ceased and in 1557 the Chinese allowed the Portuguese to occupy [Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macau "Macau").

To enforce a trade monopoly, [Muscat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscat "Muscat") and Hormuz in the [Persian Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf "Persian Gulf") were seized by [Afonso de Albuquerque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afonso_de_Albuquerque "Afonso de Albuquerque") in 1507 and in 1515, respectively. He also entered into [diplomatic relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomacy "Diplomacy") with [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Iran "Safavid Iran"). In 1513, while trying to conquer [Aden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aden "Aden"), an expedition led by Albuquerque cruised the [Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea "Red Sea") inside the [Bab al-Mandab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bab_al-Mandab "Bab al-Mandab") and sheltered at [Kamaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamaran "Kamaran") island. In 1521, a force under [António Correia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio_Correia_(admiral) "António Correia (admiral)") conquered [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain "Bahrain"), ushering in a period of almost 80 years of Portuguese rule of the Gulf archipelago. In the Red Sea, [Massawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massawa "Massawa") was the most northerly point frequented by the Portuguese until 1541, when a fleet under [Estevão da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%AAv%C3%A3o_da_Gama_(16th_century) "Estêvão da Gama (16th century)") penetrated as far as [Suez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez "Suez").

## Pacific Ocean (1513–1529)

### Balboa's expedition to the Pacific Ocean

In 1513, about 40 miles (64 kilometres) south of [Acandí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acand%C3%AD "Acandí"), in present-day Colombia, Spanish [Vasco Núñez de Balboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_N%C3%BA%C3%B1ez_de_Balboa "Vasco Núñez de Balboa") heard unexpected news of an "other sea" rich in gold, which he received with great interest. With few resources and using information given by *[caciques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacique "Cacique")*, he journeyed across the [Isthmus of Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmus_of_Panama "Isthmus of Panama") with 190 Spaniards, a few native guides, and a pack of dogs.

Balboa, using a [brigantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigantine "Brigantine") and ten native [canoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canoe "Canoe"), explored the coast, facing battles and dense jungles. On 25 September, after crossing the Chucunaque River mountains, he became the first European to see the Pacific Ocean from the New World. The expedition briefly navigated the Pacific, naming the bay [*San Miguel*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_San_Miguel "Bay of San Miguel") and the sea [*Mar del Sur* (South Sea)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean "Pacific Ocean"). Seeking gold, Balboa traversed cacique lands to the islands, naming the largest Isla Rica (now Isla del Rey) and the group *[Archipiélago de las Perlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_Islands "Pearl Islands")*, names still in use today.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Subsequent developments to the east

From 1515 to 1516, the Spanish fleet led by [Juan Díaz de Solís](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_D%C3%ADaz_de_Sol%C3%ADs "Juan Díaz de Solís") sailed down the east coast of South America as far as [Río de la Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADo_de_la_Plata "Río de la Plata"), which Solís named shortly before he died while trying to find a passage to the "South Sea".

### First circumnavigation

By 1516, several Portuguese navigators conflicting with King [Manuel I of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_I_of_Portugal "Manuel I of Portugal") gathered in [Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville "Seville") to serve the newly crowned [Charles I of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor "Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor"). Among them were explorers Diogo and [Duarte Barbosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duarte_Barbosa "Duarte Barbosa"), [Estêvão Gomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%AAv%C3%A3o_Gomes "Estêvão Gomes"), [João Serrão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_Serr%C3%A3o "João Serrão"), and [Ferdinand Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Magellan "Ferdinand Magellan"), cartographers [Jorge Reinel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Reinel "Jorge Reinel") and [Diogo Ribeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diogo_Ribeiro_(cartographer) "Diogo Ribeiro (cartographer)"), cosmographers Francisco and [Ruy Faleiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rui_Faleiro "Rui Faleiro") and the Flemish merchant [Christopher de Haro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_de_Haro "Christopher de Haro"). Ferdinand Magellan had sailed in India for Portugal up to 1513, when the Spice Islands were reached, and had kept contact with [Francisco Serrão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Serr%C3%A3o "Francisco Serrão") who was living there. Magellan developed the theory that the Spice Islands were in the [Tordesillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Tordesillas "Treaty of Tordesillas") Spanish area, based on studies by Faleiro brothers.

Aware of the efforts of the Spanish to find a route to India by sailing west, Magellan presented his plan to [Charles I of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor "Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor"). The king and Christopher de Haro financed Magellan's expedition. A fleet was put together, and Spanish navigators such as [Juan Sebastián Elcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Sebasti%C3%A1n_Elcano "Juan Sebastián Elcano") joined the enterprise. On 10 August 1519, they departed from Seville with a fleet of five ships—the [caravel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravel "Caravel") [flagship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagship "Flagship") *[Trinidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinidad_(ship) "Trinidad (ship)")* under Magellan's command, and [carracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrack "Carrack") *San Antonio*, *Concepcion*, *Santiago* and *[Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_(ship) "Victoria (ship)")*. They contained a crew of about 237 European men from several regions, with the goal of reaching the Spice Islands by travelling west, trying to reclaim it under Spain's economic and political sphere.

The fleet sailed south, avoiding Portuguese Brazil, and became the first to reach [Tierra del Fuego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierra_del_Fuego "Tierra del Fuego"). Starting on 21 October, they navigated the 373-mile (600 km) [Strait of Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Magellan "Strait of Magellan"), entering the Pacific on 28 November, which Magellan named Mar Pacífico for its calm waters. After crossing the Pacific, Magellan was killed in the Battle of Mactan in the Philippines. Juan Sebastián Elcano completed the voyage, reaching the Spice Islands in 1521. On 6 September 1522, the *Victoria* returned to Spain, completing the first [circumnavigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumnavigation "Circumnavigation") of the globe. Of the original crew, only 18 men completed the circumnavigation; 17 returned later, including twelve captured by the Portuguese and five survivors of the Trinidad. [Antonio Pigafetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Pigafetta "Antonio Pigafetta"), a [Venetian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice "Republic of Venice") scholar, kept a detailed journal that is a key source of information about the voyage.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

This round-the-world voyage gave Spain valuable knowledge of the world and its oceans which later helped in the exploration and settlement of the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_(1565%E2%80%931898) "History of the Philippines (1565–1898)"). Although this was not a realistic alternative to the Portuguese route around Africa (the [Strait of Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Magellan "Strait of Magellan") was too far south, and the Pacific Ocean too vast to cover in a single trip from Spain) successive Spanish expeditions used this information to explore the Pacific Ocean and discovered routes that [opened up trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_galleon "Manila galleon") between [Acapulco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acapulco "Acapulco"), [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain "New Spain") (present-day Mexico) and [Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila "Manila") in the Philippines.

### Westward and eastward exploration meet

Soon after Magellan's expedition, the Portuguese rushed to seize the surviving crew and built a fort in [Ternate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ternate "Ternate"). In 1525, [Charles I of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor "Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor") sent another expedition westward to colonize the Spice Islands, claiming that they were in his zone of the [Treaty of Tordesillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Tordesillas "Treaty of Tordesillas"). The fleet of seven ships and 450 men was led by [García Jofre de Loaísa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garc%C3%ADa_Jofre_de_Loa%C3%ADsa "García Jofre de Loaísa") and included the most notable Spanish navigators: [Juan Sebastián Elcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Sebasti%C3%A1n_Elcano "Juan Sebastián Elcano") and Loaísa, who died then, and the young [Andrés de Urdaneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_de_Urdaneta "Andrés de Urdaneta").

Near the [Strait of Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Magellan "Strait of Magellan") one of the ships was pushed south by a storm, reaching 56° S, where they thought seeing "*earth's end*": so [Cape Horn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Horn "Cape Horn") was crossed for the first time. The expedition reached the islands with great difficulty, docking at [Tidore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidore "Tidore"). The conflict with the Portuguese established in nearby Ternate was inevitable, starting nearly a decade of skirmishes.

As there was not a set eastern limit to the Tordesillas line, both kingdoms organized meetings to resolve the issue. From 1524 to 1529, Portuguese and Spanish experts met at Badajoz-Elvas trying to find the exact location of the [antimeridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimeridian "Antimeridian") of Tordesillas, which would divide the world into two equal hemispheres. Each crown appointed three [astronomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer "Astronomer") and [cartographers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartography "Cartography"), three [pilots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigation "Navigation"), and three [mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician"). [Lopo Homem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lopo_Homem "Lopo Homem"), Portuguese cartographer and cosmographer was on the board, along with cartographer [Diogo Ribeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diogo_Ribeiro_(cartographer) "Diogo Ribeiro (cartographer)") of the Spanish delegation. The board met several times without reaching an agreement: the knowledge at that time was insufficient for an [accurate calculation of longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_longitude "History of longitude"), and each group gave the islands to its sovereign. The issue was settled only in 1529, after a long negotiation, with the signing of [Treaty of Zaragoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Zaragoza "Treaty of Zaragoza"), which allocated the Spice Islands to Portugal and the Philippines to Spain.

From 1525 to 1528, Portugal sent several expeditions around the Spice Islands. [Gomes de Sequeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomes_de_Sequeira "Gomes de Sequeira") and Diogo da Rocha were sent north by the governor of Ternate [Jorge de Menezes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_de_Menezes "Jorge de Menezes"), being the first Europeans to reach the [Caroline Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Islands "Caroline Islands"), which they named *Islands of Gomes de Sequeira*. In 1526, Jorge de Meneses docked on [Biak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biak "Biak") and [Waigeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waigeo "Waigeo") islands, Papua New Guinea. Based on these explorations stands the [theory of Portuguese discovery of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_Portuguese_discovery_of_Australia "Theory of Portuguese discovery of Australia"), one among several competing theories about the early discovery of Australia, supported by Australian historian [Kenneth McIntyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_McIntyre "Kenneth McIntyre"), stating it was discovered by [Cristóvão de Mendonça](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crist%C3%B3v%C3%A3o_de_Mendon%C3%A7a "Cristóvão de Mendonça") and Gomes de Sequeira.

In 1527, [Hernán Cortés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hern%C3%A1n_Cort%C3%A9s "Hernán Cortés") fitted out a fleet to find new lands in the "South Sea" (Pacific Ocean), asking his cousin [Álvaro de Saavedra Cerón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lvaro_de_Saavedra_Cer%C3%B3n "Álvaro de Saavedra Cerón") to take charge. On 31 October 1527, Saavedra sailed from [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain "New Spain"), crossing the Pacific and touring the north of [New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Guinea "New Guinea"), then named *Isla de Oro*. In October 1528, one of the vessels reached the Spice Islands. In his attempt to return to New Spain he was diverted by the northeast [trade winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_wind "Trade wind"), which threw him back, so he tried sailing back down, to the south. He returned to New Guinea and sailed northeast, where he sighted the [Marshall Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Islands "Marshall Islands") and the [Admiralty Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiralty_Islands "Admiralty Islands"), but again was surprised by the winds, which brought him a third time to the Moluccas. This westbound return route was hard to find but was eventually discovered by [Andrés de Urdaneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_de_Urdaneta "Andrés de Urdaneta") in 1565.

## Inland Spanish expeditions (1519–1532)

Rumors of undiscovered islands northwest of [Hispaniola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispaniola "Hispaniola") reached Spain by 1511, ushering King [Ferdinand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_II_of_Aragon "Ferdinand II of Aragon")'s interest in forestalling further exploration. While the Portuguese were making huge gains in the Indian Ocean, the Spanish invested in exploring inland in search of gold and other valuable resources. The members of these expeditions, the [conquistadors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquistadors "Conquistadors"), were not soldiers in an army, but more like [soldiers of fortune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercenary "Mercenary"); they came from a variety of backgrounds including artisans, merchants, clergy, lawyers, lesser nobility and a few freed slaves. They usually supplied their own equipment or were extended credit to purchase it in exchange for a share in profits. They usually had no professional military training, but a number of them had previous experience on other expeditions.

In the Americas, the Spanish encountered large indigenous empires and formed alliances with [indigenous people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas "Indigenous peoples of the Americas") through small expeditions. After establishing Spanish [sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereignty "Sovereignty") and discovering wealth, the crown focused on implementing Spanish state and church institutions. A key element was the 'spiritual conquest' through Christian evangelization. The initial economy relied on a tribute and forced labor under the *[encomienda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encomienda "Encomienda")* system. The discovery of vast silver deposits transformed both the colonial economies of Mexico and Peru and Spain's economy. With global trade networks and valuable American crops, Spain's economy strengthened, enhancing its status as a world power.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

During this time, [pandemics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandemic "Pandemic") of European diseases such as [smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox "Smallpox") decimated the indigenous populations.

In 1512, to reward [Juan Ponce de León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Ponce_de_Le%C3%B3n "Juan Ponce de León") for exploring [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico "Puerto Rico") in 1508, King Ferdinand urged him to seek these new lands. He would become governor of discovered lands but was to finance himself all exploration. With three ships and about 200 men, Léon set out from Puerto Rico in March 1513. In April, they sighted land and named it *[La Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida "Florida")*—because it was [Easter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter "Easter") (Florida) season—believing it was an island, becoming credited as the first European to land in the continent. The arrival location has been disputed between [St. Augustine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Augustine,_Florida "St. Augustine, Florida"), [Ponce de León Inlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponce_de_Le%C3%B3n_Inlet "Ponce de León Inlet") and [Melbourne Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Beach,_Florida "Melbourne Beach, Florida"). They headed south for further exploration and on 8 April encountered a current so strong that it pushed them backward: this was the first encounter with the [Gulf Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Stream "Gulf Stream") that would soon become the primary route for eastbound ships leaving the Spanish Indies bound for Europe. They explored down the coast reaching [Biscayne Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biscayne_Bay "Biscayne Bay"), [Dry Tortugas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_Tortugas "Dry Tortugas") and then sailing southwest in an attempt to circle Cuba to return, reaching [Grand Bahama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Bahama "Grand Bahama") on July.

### Cortés' Mexico and the Aztec Empire

In 1517, Cuba's governor [Diego Velázquez de Cuéllar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez_de_Cu%C3%A9llar "Diego Velázquez de Cuéllar") commissioned a fleet under the command of [Hernández de Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Hern%C3%A1ndez_de_C%C3%B3rdoba_(Yucat%C3%A1n_conquistador) "Francisco Hernández de Córdoba (Yucatán conquistador)") to explore the [Yucatán Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yucat%C3%A1n_Peninsula "Yucatán Peninsula"). They reached the coast where [Mayans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_peoples "Maya peoples") invited them to land. They were attacked at night and only a remnant of the crew returned. Velázquez then commissioned another expedition led by his nephew [Juan de Grijalva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_de_Grijalva "Juan de Grijalva"), who sailed south along the coast to [Tabasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabasco "Tabasco"), part of the [Aztec Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec_Empire "Aztec Empire").

In 1518, Velázquez gave the mayor of the capital of Cuba, [Hernán Cortés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hern%C3%A1n_Cort%C3%A9s "Hernán Cortés"), the command of an expedition to secure the interior of Mexico but, due to an old gripe between them, revoked the charter. In February 1519, Cortés went ahead anyway, in an act of open mutiny. With about 11 ships, 500 men, 13 horses, and a small number of cannons he landed in Yucatán, in [Mayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayan_civilization "Mayan civilization") territory, claiming the land for the Spanish crown. From [Trinidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinidad "Trinidad") he proceeded to [Tabasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabasco "Tabasco") and won a battle against the natives. Among the vanquished was Marina ([La Malinche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Malinche "La Malinche")), his future mistress, who knew both (Aztec) [Nahuatl language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahuatl_language "Nahuatl language") and Maya, becoming a valuable interpreter and counsellor. Cortés learned about the wealthy Aztec Empire through La Malinche,

In July, his men took over [Veracruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veracruz "Veracruz") and he placed himself under direct orders of new king [Charles I of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_I_of_Spain "Charles I of Spain"). There Cortés asked for a meeting with Aztec emperor [Moctezuma II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moctezuma_II "Moctezuma II"), who repeatedly refused. They headed to [Tenochtitlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenochtitlan "Tenochtitlan") and on the way made alliances with several tribes. In October, accompanied by about 3,000 [Tlaxcaltec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tlaxcaltec "Tlaxcaltec") they marched to [Cholula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholula_(Mesoamerican_site) "Cholula (Mesoamerican site)"), the second largest city in central Mexico. Either to instill fear upon the Aztecs waiting for him or (as he later claimed) wishing to make an example when he feared native treachery, they massacred thousands of unarmed members of the nobility gathered at the central plaza and partially burned the city.

On 8 November, Cortés and his large army were welcomed by Moctezuma II in Tenochtitlan, who hoped to learn about them to eventually defeat them. Moctezuma gave lavish gifts, which led Cortés to plunder the city. Cortés claimed the Aztecs saw him as an emissary or incarnation of the god [Quetzalcōātl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quetzalc%C5%8D%C4%81tl "Quetzalcōātl"), though this is contested by few historians. Upon learning that his men had been attacked on the coast, Cortés took Moctezuma hostage in his palace, demanding tribute for King Charles.

Meanwhile, Velasquez sent another expedition, led by [Pánfilo de Narváez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A1nfilo_de_Narv%C3%A1ez "Pánfilo de Narváez"), to oppose Cortès, arriving in Mexico in April 1520 with 1,100 men. Cortés left 200 men in Tenochtitlan and took the rest to confront Narvaez, whom he overcame, convincing his men to join him. In Tenochtitlán, one of Cortés's lieutenants committed a [massacre in the Great Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massacre_in_the_Great_Temple_of_Tenochtitlan "Massacre in the Great Temple of Tenochtitlan"), triggering local rebellion. Cortés speedily returned, attempting the support of Moctezuma but the Aztec emperor was killed, possibly stoned by his subjects. The Spaniards fled for [Tlaxcala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tlaxcala_(Nahua_state) "Tlaxcala (Nahua state)") during the *[Noche Triste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Noche_Triste "La Noche Triste")*, where they managed a narrow escape while their back guard was massacred. Much of the treasure looted was lost during this panicked escape. After [a battle in Otumba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Otumba "Battle of Otumba") they reached Tlaxcala, having lost 870 men. Having prevailed with the assistance of allies and reinforcements from Cuba, Cortés [besieged Tenochtitlán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Tenochtitlan "Fall of Tenochtitlan") and captured its ruler [Cuauhtémoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuauht%C3%A9moc "Cuauhtémoc") in August 1521. As the Aztec Empire ended, he claimed the city for Spain and renamed it Mexico City.

### Pizarro's Peru and the Inca Empire

A first attempt to explore western South America was undertaken in 1522 by [Pascual de Andagoya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascual_de_Andagoya "Pascual de Andagoya"). Native South Americans told him about a gold-rich territory on a river called Pirú. Having reached [San Juan River (Colombia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_River_(Colombia) "San Juan River (Colombia)"), Andagoya fell ill and returned to [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama "Panama"), where he spread the news about "Pirú" as the legendary [El Dorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Dorado "El Dorado"). These, along with the accounts of the success of [Hernán Cortés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hern%C3%A1n_Cort%C3%A9s "Hernán Cortés"), caught the attention of Pizarro.

[Francisco Pizarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Pizarro "Francisco Pizarro") had accompanied [Balboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_N%C3%BA%C3%B1ez_de_Balboa "Vasco Núñez de Balboa") in the crossing of the [Isthmus of Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmus_of_Panama "Isthmus of Panama"). In 1524, he formed a partnership with priest [Hernando de Luque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hernando_de_Luque "Hernando de Luque") and soldier [Diego de Almagro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_de_Almagro "Diego de Almagro") to explore the south, agreeing to divide the profits. They dubbed the enterprise the "*Empresa del Levante*": Pizarro would command, Almagro would provide military and food supplies, and Luque would be in charge of finances and additional provisions.

On 13 September 1524, the first of three expeditions set out to conquer Peru with 80 men and 40 horses. The venture failed, halting in Colombia due to bad weather, hunger, and conflicts with locals; Almagro lost an eye. Their route was marked by *[Puerto Deseado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Deseado "Puerto Deseado")* (desired port), *[Puerto del Hambre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_del_Hambre "Puerto del Hambre")* (port of hunger), and *Puerto quemado* (burned port). Two years later, a second expedition began with reluctant permission from the Governor of Panama. In August 1526, they departed with two ships, 160 men, and horses. Upon reaching the San Juan River, Pizarro explored swampy coasts, while Almagro sought reinforcements. Pizarro's pilot, sailing south and crossing the equator, captured a raft from [Tumbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Tumbes "Department of Tumbes"). To his surprise, the raft carried coveted textiles, ceramics, gold, silver, and emeralds, becoming the expedition's main focus. Almagro later joined with reinforcements, and despite challenging conditions, they reached [Atacames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atacames "Atacames"), where a sizable native population under [Inca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_Empire "Inca Empire") rule was observed, though they did not land.

Pizarro, safe near the coast, sent Almagro and Luque for reinforcements with proof of the [rumoured gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Dorado "El Dorado"). The new governor rejected a third expedition, ordering everyone back to Panama. Almagro and Luque seized the chance to rejoin Pizarro. At *Isla de Gallo*, Pizarro drew a line, presenting the choice between Peru's riches and Panama's poverty. Thirteen men, The Famous Thirteen, stayed and headed to *La Isla Gorgona*, staying seven months until provisions arrived.

They sailed south and by April 1528, reached northwestern Peru's Tumbes region, warmly received by the *Tumpis*. Pizarro's men reported incredible riches, [llama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llama "Llama") sightings, and the natives named them "Children of the Sun" for their fair complexion and brilliant armour. They decided to return to Panama to prepare a final expedition, sailing south through named territories like Cabo Blanco, port of Payta, Sechura, Punta de Aguja, Santa Cruz, and [Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trujillo,_Peru "Trujillo, Peru"), reaching the ninth degree south.

In the spring of 1528, Pizarro sailed for Spain, where he had an interview with king [Charles I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor "Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor"). The king heard of his expeditions in lands rich in gold and silver and promised to support him. The *Capitulación de Toledo* authorized Pizarro to proceed with the [conquest of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_conquest_of_the_Inca_Empire "Spanish conquest of the Inca Empire"). Pizarro was then able to convince many friends and relatives to join: his brothers [Hernándo Pizarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hern%C3%A1ndo_Pizarro "Hernándo Pizarro"), [Juan Pizarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Pizarro_(conquistador) "Juan Pizarro (conquistador)"), [Gonzalo Pizarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonzalo_Pizarro "Gonzalo Pizarro"), and also [Francisco de Orellana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_de_Orellana "Francisco de Orellana"), who would later explore the [Amazon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_River "Amazon River"), as well as his cousin [Pedro Pizarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Pizarro "Pedro Pizarro").

Pizarro's third and final expedition left Panama for Peru on 27 December 1530. With three ships and one hundred and eighty men, they landed near Ecuador and sailed to Tumbes, finding the place destroyed. They entered the interior and established the first Spanish settlement in Peru, [San Miguel de Piura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Miguel_de_Piura "San Miguel de Piura"). One of the men returned with an Incan envoy and an invitation for a meeting. Since the last meeting, the Inca had begun a [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_Civil_War "Inca Civil War") and [Atahualpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atahualpa "Atahualpa") had been resting in northern Peru following the defeat of his brother [Huáscar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hu%C3%A1scar "Huáscar"). After marching for two months, they approached Atahualpa. He refused the Spanish, saying he would be "no man's tributary". There were fewer than 200 Spanish to his 80,000 soldiers, but Pizarro attacked and won against the Incan army in the [Battle of Cajamarca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cajamarca "Battle of Cajamarca"), taking Atahualpa captive at the so-called [Ransom Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ransom_Room "Ransom Room"). Despite fulfilling his promise of filling one room with gold and two with silver, he was convicted for killing his brother and plotting against Pizarro, and was executed.

In 1533, Pizarro invaded [Cuzco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cusco "Cusco") with indigenous troops and wrote to King Charles I:

> This city is the greatest and the finest ever seen in this country or anywhere in the Indies ... it is so beautiful and has such fine buildings that it would be remarkable even in Spain.

— Francisco Pizarro

After the Spanish had sealed the conquest of Peru, [Jauja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jauja "Jauja") in fertile [Mantaro Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantaro_Valley "Mantaro Valley") was established as Peru's provisional capital, but it was too far up in the mountains, and Pizarro founded the city of [Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima "Lima") on 18 January 1535, which Pizarro considered one of the most important acts in his life.

## Major new trade routes (1542–1565)

In 1543, three Portuguese traders accidentally became the first Westerners to reach and [trade with Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanban_trade "Nanban trade"). According to [Fernão Mendes Pinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fern%C3%A3o_Mendes_Pinto "Fernão Mendes Pinto"), who claimed to be in this journey, they arrived at [Tanegashima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanegashima "Tanegashima"), where the locals were impressed by [firearms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firearms_of_Japan "Firearms of Japan") that would be immediately made by the Japanese on a large scale.

The Spanish conquest of the Philippines was ordered by [Philip II of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain "Philip II of Spain"), and [Andrés de Urdaneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_de_Urdaneta "Andrés de Urdaneta") was the designated commander. Urdaneta agreed to accompany the expedition but refused to command and [Miguel López de Legazpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_L%C3%B3pez_de_Legazpi "Miguel López de Legazpi") was appointed instead. The expedition set sail in November 1564. After spending some time on the islands, Legazpi sent Urdaneta back to find a better return route. Urdaneta set sail from San Miguel on the island of [Cebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebu "Cebu") on 1 June 1565, but was obliged to sail as far as [38 degrees North latitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/38th_parallel_north "38th parallel north") to obtain favorable winds.

He reasoned that the [trade winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_wind "Trade wind") of the Pacific might move in a [gyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyre "Gyre") as the Atlantic winds did. If in the Atlantic, ships made the *[Volta do mar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volta_do_mar "Volta do mar")* to pick up winds that would bring them back from Madeira, then, he reasoned, by sailing far to the north before heading east, he would pick up trade winds to bring him back to North America. His hunch paid off, and he hit the coast near [Cape Mendocino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Mendocino "Cape Mendocino"), California, then followed the coast south. The ship reached the port of Acapulco, on 8 October 1565, having traveled 12,000 miles (19,000 kilometres) in 130 days. Fourteen of his crew died; only Urdaneta and Felipe de Salcedo, nephew of López de Legazpi, had strength enough to cast the anchors.

Thus, a cross-Pacific Spanish route was established, between Mexico and the Philippines. For a long time, these routes were used by the [Manila galleons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_galleon "Manila galleon"), thereby creating a trade link joining China, the Americas, and Europe via the combined trans-Pacific and [trans-Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transatlantic_crossing "Transatlantic crossing") routes.

## Northern European involvement (16th–17th centuries)

European nations outside Iberia did not recognize the Treaty of Tordesillas between Portugal and Castile, nor did they recognize Pope Alexander VI's donation of the Spanish finds in the New World. [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_France "Kingdom of France"), the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic "Dutch Republic"), and [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_England "Kingdom of England") each had a long [maritime tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_history_of_Europe "Maritime history of Europe") and had been engaging in [privateering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privateer "Privateer"). Despite Iberian protections, the new technologies and maps soon made their way north.

After the marriage of [Henry VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VIII "Henry VIII") of England and [Catherine of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_of_Aragon "Catherine of Aragon") failed to produce a male heir and Henry failed to obtain a papal dispensation to annul his marriage, he broke with the Roman Catholic Church and established himself as head of the [Church of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_England "Church of England"). This added religious conflict to political conflict. When much of The Netherlands became Protestant, it sought political and religious independence from Catholic Spain. In 1568, the Dutch rebelled against the rule of [Philip II of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain "Philip II of Spain") leading to the [Eighty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighty_Years%27_War "Eighty Years' War"). The war between England and Spain also broke out. In 1580, Philip II became King of Portugal, as heir to its Crown. Although he ruled Portugal and its empire as separate from the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire "Spanish Empire"), the union of the crowns produced a Catholic superpower, which England and the Netherlands challenged.

In the eighty-year Dutch War of Independence, Philip's troops conquered the important trading cities of [Bruges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruges "Bruges") and [Ghent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghent "Ghent"). [Antwerp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antwerp "Antwerp"), then the most important port in the world, fell in 1585.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] The Protestant population was given two years to settle affairs before leaving the city. Many settled in [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam "Amsterdam"). Those were mainly skilled craftsmen, rich merchants of the port cities and refugees that fled religious persecution, particularly [Sephardic Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardic_Jews "Sephardic Jews") from Portugal and Spain and, later, the [Huguenots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huguenots "Huguenots") from France. The [Pilgrim Fathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilgrim_Fathers "Pilgrim Fathers") also spent time there before going to the New World. This mass immigration was an important driving force: a small port in 1585, Amsterdam quickly transformed into one of the most important commercial centres in the world. After the failure of the [Spanish Armada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Armada "Spanish Armada") in 1588, there was a huge expansion of maritime trade even though the defeat of the [English Armada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Armada "English Armada") would confirm the naval supremacy of the Spanish navy over the emergent competitors.

Dutch maritime power rose quickly as Dutch sailors, skilled in navigation and mapmaking, engaged with Portuguese voyages. In 1592, [Cornelis de Houtman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelis_de_Houtman "Cornelis de Houtman") gathered information on the Spice Islands in Lisbon. The same year, [Jan Huyghen van Linschoten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Huyghen_van_Linschoten "Jan Huyghen van Linschoten") published a detailed travel report in Amsterdam, providing navigation instructions for reaching the East Indies and Japan. Following this, Houtman led the Dutch's first exploratory voyage, discovering a new route from Madagascar to the [Sunda Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_Strait "Sunda Strait") and securing a treaty with the [Banten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banten "Banten") Sultan. The Dutch also demonstrated their maritime strength by seizing Malacca from Portugal in 1641, following a series of battles that began in 1602.

Dutch and British interest, fed on new information, led to a movement of commercial expansion, and the foundation of English (1600) and Dutch (1602) [chartered companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chartered_company "Chartered company"). Dutch, French, and English sent ships which flouted the Portuguese monopoly, concentrated mostly on the coastal areas, which proved unable to defend against such a vast and dispersed venture.

### Exploring North America

The 1497 English expedition authorized by [Henry VII of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VII_of_England "Henry VII of England") was led by Italian Venetian [John Cabot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cabot "John Cabot") (Giovanni Caboto); it was the first of a series of French and English missions exploring North America. Mariners from the Italian peninsula played an important role in early explorations, most especially Genoese mariner Christopher Columbus. With its major conquests of central Mexico and Peru and discoveries of silver, Spain put limited efforts into exploring the northern part of the Americas; its resources were concentrated in Central and South America where more wealth had been found. These other European expeditions were initially motivated by the same idea as Columbus, namely a westerly shortcut to the Asian mainland. After the existence of "another ocean" (the Pacific) was confirmed by Balboa in 1513, there still remained the motivation of potentially finding an oceanic [Northwest Passage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Passage "Northwest Passage") for Asian trade. This was not discovered until the early twentieth century, but other possibilities were found, although nothing on the scale of the spectacular ones of the Spanish. In the early 17th century colonists from a number of Northern European states began to settle on the east coast of North America. Between 1520 and 1521, the Portuguese [João Álvares Fagundes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_%C3%81lvares_Fagundes "João Álvares Fagundes"), accompanied by couples of mainland Portugal and the Azores, explored [Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newfoundland_(island) "Newfoundland (island)") and [Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Scotia "Nova Scotia") (possibly reaching the [Bay of Fundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Fundy "Bay of Fundy") on the [Minas Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minas_Basin "Minas Basin")), and established a fishing colony on the [Cape Breton Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Breton_Island "Cape Breton Island") that would last until at least the 1570s or near the end of the century.

In 1524, Italian [Giovanni da Verrazzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_da_Verrazzano "Giovanni da Verrazzano") sailed under the authority of [Francis I of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_I_of_France "Francis I of France"), who was motivated by indignation over the division of the world between Portuguese and Spanish. Verrazzano explored the Atlantic Coast of North America, from [South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina "South Carolina") to Newfoundland, and was the first recorded European to visit what would later become the [Virginia Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Colony "Virginia Colony") and the United States. In the same year [Estêvão Gomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%AAv%C3%A3o_Gomes "Estêvão Gomes"), a Portuguese [cartographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartography "Cartography") who had sailed in Ferdinand Magellan's fleet, explored [Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Scotia "Nova Scotia"), sailing South through [Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine "Maine"), where he entered what is now [New York Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Harbor "New York Harbor"), the [Hudson River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_River "Hudson River") and eventually reached [Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida "Florida") in August 1525. As a result of his expedition, the 1529 [Diogo Ribeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diogo_Ribeiro "Diogo Ribeiro") world map outlines the East coast of North America almost perfectly. From 1534 to 1536, French explorer [Jacques Cartier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Cartier "Jacques Cartier"), believed to have accompanied Verrazzano to Nova Scotia and Brazil, was the first European to travel inland in North America, describing the [Gulf of Saint Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Saint_Lawrence "Gulf of Saint Lawrence"), which he named "[The Country of Canadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Name_of_Canada "Name of Canada")", after [Iroquois names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iroquoian_languages "Iroquoian languages"), claiming what is now Canada for Francis I of France.

Europeans explored the Pacific Coast beginning in the mid-16th century. Spaniard [Francisco de Ulloa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_de_Ulloa "Francisco de Ulloa") explored the Pacific coast of present-day Mexico including the [Gulf of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_California "Gulf of California"), proving that [Baja California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baja_California "Baja California") was a peninsula. Despite his report based on first-hand information, the myth persisted in Europe that California was an [island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_of_California "Island of California"). His account provided the first recorded use of the name "California". [João Rodrigues Cabrilho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Cabrillo "Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo"), a Portuguese navigator sailing for the Spanish Crown, was the first European to set foot in California, landing on 28 September 1542, on the shores of [San Diego Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Bay "San Diego Bay") and claiming California for Spain. He also landed on [San Miguel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Miguel_Island "San Miguel Island"), one of the [Channel Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_Islands_of_California "Channel Islands of California"), and continued as far north as [Point Reyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Reyes "Point Reyes") on the mainland. After his death, the crew continued exploring as far north as [Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon "Oregon").

The English privateer [Francis Drake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Drake "Francis Drake") sailed along the coast in 1579 north of Cabrillo's landing site while circumnavigating the world. Drake had a long and largely successful career attacking Spanish settlements in the Caribbean islands and the mainland so for the English, he was a great hero and fervent Protestant, but for the Spanish, he was "a frightening monster". Drake played a major role in the defeat of the [Spanish Armada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Armada "Spanish Armada") in 1588 but led an armada himself to the Spanish Caribbean that was unsuccessful in dislodging the Spanish. On 5 June 1579, the ship briefly made first landfall at South Cove, Cape Arago, just south of [Coos Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coos_Bay "Coos Bay"), [Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon "Oregon"), and then sailed south while searching for a suitable harbor to repair his damaged ship. On 17 June, Drake and his crew found a protected cove when they landed on the Pacific coast of what is now Northern California near [Point Reyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Reyes "Point Reyes"). While ashore, he claimed the area for Queen [Elizabeth I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_I "Elizabeth I") of England as Nova Albion or [New Albion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Albion "New Albion"). To document and assert his claim, Drake posted an engraved plate of brass to claim sovereignty for Queen Elizabeth and her successors on the throne. Drake's landfalls on the west coast of North America are one small part of his 1577–1580 circumnavigation of the globe, the first captain of his own ship to do so. Drake died in 1596 off the coast of Panama, following injuries from a raid.

From 1609 to 1611, after several voyages on behalf of English merchants to explore a prospective [Northeast Passage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Passage "Northeast Passage") to India, English mariner [Henry Hudson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Hudson "Henry Hudson"), under the auspices of the [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company "Dutch East India Company") (VOC), explored the region around present-day New York City, while looking for a western route to Asia. He explored the [Hudson River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_River "Hudson River") and laid the foundation for [Dutch colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Netherland "New Netherland") of the region. Hudson's final expedition ranged farther north in search of the Northwest Passage, leading to his discovery of the [Hudson Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_Strait "Hudson Strait") and [Hudson Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_Bay "Hudson Bay"). After wintering in [James Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bay "James Bay"), Hudson tried to press on with his voyage in the spring of 1611, but his crew mutinied and they [cast him adrift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marooning "Marooning").

### Search for a northern route

France, the Netherlands, and England sought a sea route to Asia after finding none through Africa or South America. With no route through the Americas, they focused on northern passages, driving European exploration of the Arctic coasts. The idea of a link between the Atlantic and Pacific was first proposed by Russian diplomat [Gerasimov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Gerasimov "Dmitry Gerasimov") in 1525, though Russian [Pomors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomors "Pomors") had explored parts of the route as early as the 11th century.

In 1553, English explorer [Hugh Willoughby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Willoughby "Hugh Willoughby") with chief pilot [Richard Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Chancellor "Richard Chancellor") were sent out with three vessels in search of a passage by London's [Company of Merchant Adventurers to New Lands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_of_Merchant_Adventurers_to_New_Lands "Company of Merchant Adventurers to New Lands"). During the voyage across the [Barents Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barents_Sea "Barents Sea"), Willoughby thought he saw islands to the north, and islands called [Willoughby's Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willoughby%27s_Land "Willoughby's Land") were shown on maps published by [Plancius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrus_Plancius "Petrus Plancius") and [Mercator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerardus_Mercator "Gerardus Mercator") into the 1640s. The vessels were separated by "terrible whirlwinds" in the [Norwegian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Sea "Norwegian Sea") and Willoughby sailed into a bay near the present border between Finland and Russia. His ships with the frozen crews, including Captain Willoughby and his journal, were found by Russian fishermen a year later. Richard Chancellor was able to drop anchor in the [White Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sea "White Sea") and make his way overland to Moscow and [Ivan the Terrible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_the_Terrible "Ivan the Terrible")'s court, opening trade with Russia and the Company of Merchant Adventurers became the [Muscovy Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscovy_Company "Muscovy Company").

In June 1576, English mariner [Martin Frobisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Frobisher "Martin Frobisher") led an expedition consisting of three ships and 35 men to search for a north-west passage around North America. The voyage was supported by the Muscovy Company, the same merchants that hired Hugh Willoughby to find a northeast passage above Russia. Violent storms sank one ship and forced another to turn back but Frobisher and the remaining ship reached the coast of Labrador in July. A few days later they came upon the mouth of what is now [Frobisher Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobisher_Bay "Frobisher Bay"). Frobisher believed it to be the entrance to a north-west passage and named it Frobisher's Strait and claimed [Baffin Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baffin_Island "Baffin Island") for Queen Elizabeth. After some preliminary exploration, Frobisher returned to England. He commanded two subsequent voyages in 1577 and 1578 but failed to find the hoped-for passage. Frobisher brought to England his ships laden with ore, but it was found to be worthless and damaged his reputation as an explorer. He remains an important early historical figure in Canada.

#### Barentsz' Arctic exploration

On 5 June 1594, Dutch cartographer [Willem Barentsz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_Barentsz "Willem Barentsz") departed from [Texel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texel "Texel") in a fleet of three ships to enter the [Kara Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kara_Sea "Kara Sea"), with the hopes of finding the Northeast Passage above [Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberia "Siberia"). At Williams Island the crew encountered a [polar bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_bear "Polar bear") for the first time. They managed to bring it on board, but the bear rampaged and was killed. Barentsz reached the west coast of [Novaya Zemlya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novaya_Zemlya "Novaya Zemlya") and followed it northward, before being forced to turn back in the face of large icebergs.

The following year, Prince [Maurice of Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice,_Prince_of_Orange "Maurice, Prince of Orange") named him chief pilot of a new expedition of six ships, loaded with merchant wares that the Dutch hoped to trade with China. The party came across [Samoyed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samoyedic_peoples "Samoyedic peoples") "wild men" but eventually turned back upon discovering the Kara Sea frozen. In 1596, the States-General offered a high reward for anybody who *successfully* navigated the Northeast Passage. The Town Council of Amsterdam purchased and outfitted two small ships, captained by [Jan Rijp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Rijp "Jan Rijp") and [Jacob van Heemskerck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_van_Heemskerck "Jacob van Heemskerck"), to search for the elusive channel, under the command of Barents. They set off in May, and in June discovered [Bear Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B8rn%C3%B8ya "Bjørnøya") and [Spitsbergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spitsbergen "Spitsbergen"), sighting its northwest coast. They saw a large bay, later called [Raudfjorden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raudfjorden "Raudfjorden") and entered [Magdalenefjorden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalenefjorden "Magdalenefjorden"), which they named *Tusk Bay*, sailing into the northern entrance of [Forlandsundet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forlandsundet "Forlandsundet"), which they called *Keerwyck*, but were forced to turn back because of a shoal. On 28 June they rounded the northern point of [Prins Karls Forland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prins_Karls_Forland "Prins Karls Forland"), which they named *Vogelhoek*, on account of a large number of birds, and sailed south, passing [Isfjorden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isfjorden_(Svalbard) "Isfjorden (Svalbard)") and [Bellsund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellsund "Bellsund"), which were labelled on Barentsz's chart as *Grooten Inwyck* and *Inwyck*.

The ships once again reached Bear Island on 1 July, which led to a disagreement. They parted ways, with Barentsz continuing northeast, while Rijp headed north. Barentsz reached Novaya Zemlya and, to avoid becoming entrapped in ice, headed for the [Kara Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kara_Strait "Kara Strait") but became stuck within the icebergs and floes. Stranded, the 16-man crew was forced to spend the winter on the ice. The crew used lumber from their ship to build a lodge they called *Het Behouden Huys* (The Kept House). Dealing with extreme cold, they used merchant fabrics to make additional blankets and clothing and caught Arctic foxes in primitive traps, as well as polar bears. When June arrived, and the ice had still not loosened its grip on the ship, [scurvy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scurvy "Scurvy")-ridden survivors took two small boats out into the sea. Barentsz died at sea on 20 June 1597, while studying charts. It took seven more weeks for the boats to reach [Kola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kola_(town) "Kola (town)") where they were rescued by a Russian merchant vessel. Only 12 crewmen remained, reaching Amsterdam in November. Two of Barentsz' crewmembers later published their journals, [Jan Huyghen van Linschoten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Huyghen_van_Linschoten "Jan Huyghen van Linschoten"), who had accompanied him on the first two voyages, and [Gerrit de Veer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerrit_de_Veer "Gerrit de Veer") who had acted as the ship's carpenter on the last.

### Australia and New Zealand

*[Terra Australis Ignota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terra_Australis "Terra Australis")* (Latin for 'the unknown land of the south') was a hypothetical continent appearing on European maps from the 15th to the 18th centuries, with roots in a notion introduced by [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle "Aristotle"). It was depicted on the mid-16th-century [Dieppe maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieppe_maps "Dieppe maps"), where its coastline appeared just south of the islands of the East Indies; it was often elaborately charted, with a wealth of fictitious detail. The discoveries reduced the area where the continent could be found. Many cartographers held to Aristotle's opinion, like [Gerardus Mercator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerardus_Mercator "Gerardus Mercator") (1569) and [Alexander Dalrymple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dalrymple "Alexander Dalrymple") even so late as 1767 argued for its existence, with such arguments as that there should be a large landmass in the Southern Hemisphere as a counterweight to the known landmasses in the Northern Hemisphere. As new lands were discovered, they were often assumed to be parts of this hypothetical continent.

[Juan Fernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Fern%C3%A1ndez_(explorer) "Juan Fernández (explorer)"), sailing from Chile in 1576, claimed he had discovered the Southern Continent. [Luís Vaz de Torres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu%C3%ADs_Vaz_de_Torres "Luís Vaz de Torres"), a [Galician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicians "Galicians") navigator working for the Spanish Crown, proved the existence of a passage south of New Guinea, now known as [Torres Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torres_Strait "Torres Strait"). [Pedro Fernandes de Queirós](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Fernandes_de_Queir%C3%B3s "Pedro Fernandes de Queirós"), a Portuguese navigator sailing for the Spanish Crown, saw a large island south of New Guinea in 1606, which he named *Austrialia del Espiritu Santo* (transl. Southern-Austrian Land of the Holy Spirit). He represented this to the King of Spain as the *Terra Australis incognita*, but it was present-day [Vanuatu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanuatu "Vanuatu") rather than Australia or Antarctica.

Dutch navigator and colonial governor, [Willem Janszoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_Janszoon "Willem Janszoon") sailed from the Netherlands for the East Indies for the third time on 18 December 1603, as captain of *[Duyfken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duyfken "Duyfken")* (or *Duijfken*, meaning 'Little Dove'), one of twelve ships of the great fleet of [Steven van der Hagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_van_der_Hagen "Steven van der Hagen"). Once in the Indies, Janszoon was sent to search for other outlets of trade, particularly in "the great land of Nova Guinea and other East and Southlands". On 18 November 1605, *Duyfken* sailed from [Banten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banten_(town) "Banten (town)") to the coast of western [New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Guinea "New Guinea"). Janszoon then crossed the eastern end of the [Arafura Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arafura_Sea "Arafura Sea"), without becoming aware of the Torres Strait, into the [Gulf of Carpentaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Carpentaria "Gulf of Carpentaria"). On 26 February 1606, he made landfall at the [Pennefather River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennefather_River "Pennefather River") on the western shore of [Cape York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_York_Peninsula "Cape York Peninsula") in Queensland, near the modern town of [Weipa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weipa,_Queensland "Weipa, Queensland"). This is the first recorded European landfall on the Australian continent. Janszoon proceeded to chart some 320 kilometres (199 mi) of the coastline, which he thought was a southerly extension of New Guinea. In 1615, [Jacob Le Maire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Le_Maire "Jacob Le Maire") and [Willem Schouten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_Schouten "Willem Schouten")'s rounding of Cape Horn proved that [Tierra del Fuego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierra_del_Fuego "Tierra del Fuego") was a relatively small island.

From 1642 to 1644, [Abel Tasman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_Tasman "Abel Tasman"), also a Dutch explorer and merchant in the service of the VOC, circumnavigated [New Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Holland_(Australia) "New Holland (Australia)") proving that Australia was not part of the mythical southern continent. He was the first known European expedition to reach the islands of [Van Diemen's Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Diemen%27s_Land "Van Diemen's Land") (now [Tasmania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasmania "Tasmania")) and New Zealand and to sight the [Fiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiji "Fiji") islands, which he did in 1643. Tasman, his navigator Visscher, and his merchant Gilsemans also mapped substantial portions of Australia, New Zealand, and the Pacific islands.

## Russian exploration of Siberia (1581–1660)

In the mid-16th century, the [Tsardom of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsardom_of_Russia "Tsardom of Russia") conquered the [Tatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_Tatars "Volga Tatars") khanates of [Kazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanate_of_Kazan "Khanate of Kazan") and [Astrakhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrakhan_Khanate "Astrakhan Khanate"), thus annexing the entire [Volga region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_region "Volga region") and opening the way to the [Ural Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Mountains "Ural Mountains"). The colonization of the new easternmost lands of Russia and further onslaught eastward was led by the rich merchants [Stroganovs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroganov_family "Stroganov family"). Ivan the Terrible granted vast estates near the Urals as well as tax privileges to [Anikey Stroganov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anikey_Stroganov "Anikey Stroganov"), who organized large-scale migration to these lands. The Stroganovs developed farming, hunting, saltworks, fishing, and ore mining on the Urals and established trade with [Siberian tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_Siberia "Indigenous peoples of Siberia").

### Conquest of the Khanate of Sibir

Around 1577, [Semyon Stroganov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semyon_Stroganov "Semyon Stroganov") and other sons of Anikey Stroganov hired a [Cossack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossacks "Cossacks") leader called [Yermak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yermak_Timofeyevich "Yermak Timofeyevich") to protect their lands from the attacks of Khan [Kuchum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuchum "Kuchum"). By 1580, Stroganovs and Yermak came up with the idea of a military expedition to Siberia, to fight Kuchum in his own land. In 1581, Yermak began his voyage into the depths of Siberia. After a few victories over the Khan's army, Yermak's people defeated the main forces of Kuchum on [Irtysh River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irtysh "Irtysh") in the three-day [Battle of Chuvash Cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Chuvash_Cape "Battle of Chuvash Cape") in 1582. The remains of the Khan's army retreated to the [steppes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe "Steppe"), and thus Yermak captured the [Khanate of Sibir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanate_of_Sibir "Khanate of Sibir"), including its capital [Qashliq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qashliq "Qashliq") near modern [Tobolsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobolsk "Tobolsk"). Kuchum still was strong and suddenly attacked Yermak in 1585 in the dead of night, killing most of his people. Yermak was wounded and tried to swim across the Wagay River (Irtysh's tributary), but drowned under the weight of his own [chain mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_mail "Chain mail"). The Cossacks had to withdraw from Siberia completely, but thanks to Yermak's having explored all the main river routes in West Siberia, Russians successfully reclaimed all his conquests just several years later.

### Siberian river routes

In the early 17th century, the eastward movement of Russians was slowed by the internal problems in the country during the [Time of Troubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_of_Troubles "Time of Troubles"). Very soon, exploration and colonization of the huge territories of Siberia resumed, led mostly by Cossacks hunting for valuable [furs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur "Fur") and [ivory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory "Ivory"). While Cossacks came from the Southern Urals, another wave of Russians came by the Arctic Ocean. These were Pomors from the [Russian North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_North "Russian North"), who already had been making fur trade with [Mangazeya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangazeya "Mangazeya") in the north of Western Siberia for quite a long time. In 1607, the settlement of [Turukhansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turukhansk "Turukhansk") was founded on the northern [Yenisey River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yenisey "Yenisey"), near the mouth of [Lower Tunguska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizhnyaya_Tunguska "Nizhnyaya Tunguska"). In 1619, [Yeniseysk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeniseysk "Yeniseysk") [ostrog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrog_(fortress) "Ostrog (fortress)") was founded on the mid-Yenisey at the mouth of the [Upper Tunguska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angara "Angara").

Between 1620 and 1624, a group of fur hunters led by [Demid Pyanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demid_Pyanda "Demid Pyanda") left Turukhansk and explored some 1,430 miles (2,300 kilometres) of the Lower Tunguska, wintering in the proximity of the [Vilyuy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilyuy "Vilyuy") and [Lena Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lena_River "Lena River"). According to later legendary accounts (folktales collected a century after the fact), Pyanda discovered the Lena. He allegedly explored some 1,500 miles (2,400 kilometres) of its length, reaching as far as central [Yakutia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakha_Republic "Sakha Republic"). He returned up the Lena until it became too rocky and shallow, and portaged to the Angara River. In this way, Pyanda may have become the first Russian to meet [Yakuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakuts "Yakuts") and [Buryats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buryats "Buryats"). He built new boats and explored some 870 miles (1,400 kilometres) of the Angara, finally reaching Yeniseysk and discovering that the Angara (a [Buryat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buryat_language "Buryat language") name) and Upper Tunguska (Verkhnyaya Tunguska, as initially known by Russians) are one and the same river.

In 1627, [Pyotr Beketov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Beketov "Pyotr Beketov") was appointed Yenisei [voevoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivode "Voivode") in Siberia. He successfully carried out the voyage to collect taxes from the [Zabaykalye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transbaikal "Transbaikal") Buryats, becoming the first Russian to step in [Buryatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buryatia "Buryatia"). He founded the first Russian settlement there, Rybinsky ostrog. Beketov was sent to the Lena River in 1631, where in 1632 he founded [Yakutsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakutsk "Yakutsk") and sent his Cossacks to explore the [Aldan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldan_(river) "Aldan (river)") and farther down the Lena, to found new fortresses, and to collect taxes.

Yakutsk soon turned into a major starting point for further Russian expeditions eastward, southward and northward. [Maksim Perfilyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maksim_Perfilyev "Maksim Perfilyev"), who earlier had been one of the founders of Yeniseysk, founded [Bratsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bratsk "Bratsk") ostrog on the Angara in 1631. In 1638, Perfilyev became the first Russian to step into Transbaikalia, travelling there from Yakutsk.

In 1643, [Kurbat Ivanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurbat_Ivanov "Kurbat Ivanov") led a group of Cossacks from Yakutsk to the south of the [Baikal Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baikal_Mountains "Baikal Mountains") and discovered [Lake Baikal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Baikal "Lake Baikal"), visiting its [Olkhon Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olkhon_Island "Olkhon Island"). Ivanov later made the first chart and description of Baikal.

### Russians reach the Pacific

In 1639, a group of explorers led by [Ivan Moskvitin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Moskvitin "Ivan Moskvitin") became the first Russians to reach the Pacific Ocean and to discover the [Sea of Okhotsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Okhotsk "Sea of Okhotsk"), having built a winter camp on its shore at the [Ulya River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulya "Ulya") mouth. The Cossacks learned from the locals about the large [Amur River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amur "Amur") far to the south. In 1640, they apparently sailed south, and explored the south-eastern shores of the Okhotsk Sea, perhaps reaching the mouth of the Amur River and possibly discovering the [Shantar Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shantar_Islands "Shantar Islands") on their way back. Based on Moskvitin's account, [Kurbat Ivanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurbat_Ivanov "Kurbat Ivanov") drew the first Russian map of the Far East in 1642.

In 1643, [Vassili Poyarkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassili_Poyarkov "Vassili Poyarkov") crossed the [Stanovoy Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanovoy_Range "Stanovoy Range") and reached the upper [Zeya River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeya_(river) "Zeya (river)") in the country of the [Daurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daur_people "Daur people"), who were paying tribute to the [Manchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_people "Manchu people"). After wintering, in 1644, Poyarkov pushed down the Zeya and became the first Russian to reach the Amur River. He sailed down the Amur and finally discovered the mouth of that great river from land. Since his Cossacks provoked the enmity of the locals behind, Poyarkov chose a different way back. They built boats and in 1645, sailed along the [Sea of Okhotsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Okhotsk "Sea of Okhotsk") coast to the Ulya River and spent the next winter in the huts that had been built by [Ivan Moskvitin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Moskvitin "Ivan Moskvitin") six years earlier. In 1646, they returned to Yakutsk.

In 1644, [Mikhail Stadukhin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Stadukhin "Mikhail Stadukhin") discovered the [Kolyma River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolyma_(river) "Kolyma (river)") and founded [Srednekolymsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srednekolymsk "Srednekolymsk"). A merchant named [Fedot Alekseyevich Popov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fedot_Alekseyevich_Popov "Fedot Alekseyevich Popov") organized a further expedition eastward, and [Semyon Dezhnyov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semyon_Dezhnyov "Semyon Dezhnyov") became a captain of one of the [kochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koch_(boat) "Koch (boat)"). In 1648, they sailed from Srednekolymsk down to the Arctic and after some time they rounded [Cape Dezhnyov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Dezhnyov "Cape Dezhnyov"), thus becoming the first explorers to pass through the [Bering Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bering_Strait "Bering Strait") and discover [Chukotka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chukchi_Peninsula "Chukchi Peninsula") and the [Bering Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bering_Sea "Bering Sea"). All their Kochi and most of their men (including Popov himself) were lost in storms and clashes with the natives. A small group led by Dezhnyov reached the mouth of the [Anadyr River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anadyr_(river) "Anadyr (river)") and sailed up it in 1649, having built new boats from the wreckage. They founded [Anadyrsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anadyrsk "Anadyrsk") and were stranded there until Stadukhin found them, coming from Kolyma by land. Subsequently, Stadukhin set off south in 1651 and discovered [Penzhin Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penzhin_Bay "Penzhin Bay") on the northern coast of the [Okhotsk Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okhotsk_Sea "Okhotsk Sea"). He also may have explored the western shores of [Kamchatka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamchatka_Peninsula "Kamchatka Peninsula").

From 1649 to 1650, [Yerofey Khabarov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerofey_Khabarov "Yerofey Khabarov") became the second Russian to explore the [Amur River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amur_River "Amur River"). Through the [Olyokma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olyokma "Olyokma"), [Tungir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungir "Tungir"), and [Shilka Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shilka_(river) "Shilka (river)") he reached Amur ([Dauriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dauriya "Dauriya")), returned to [Yakutsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakutsk "Yakutsk") and then back to Amur with a larger force in 1650–1653. This time he [was met with armed resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian-Manchu_border_conflicts "Russian-Manchu border conflicts"). He built winter quarters at [Albazin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albazin "Albazin"), then sailed down Amur and found Achansk, which preceded the present-day [Khabarovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khabarovsk "Khabarovsk"), defeating or evading large armies of Daurs, Manchus, Chinese, and Koreans on his way. He charted the Amur in his *Draft of the Amur river*. Subsequently, the Russians held on to the Amur region until 1689, when by the [Treaty of Nerchinsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Nerchinsk "Treaty of Nerchinsk") this land was assigned to the [Qing Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty "Qing dynasty"). It was returned by the [Treaty of Aigun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Aigun "Treaty of Aigun") in 1858.

From 1659 to 1665, [Kurbat Ivanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurbat_Ivanov "Kurbat Ivanov") was the next head of [Anadyrsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anadyrsk "Anadyrsk") ostrog after [Semyon Dezhnev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semyon_Dezhnev "Semyon Dezhnev"). In 1660, he sailed from [Anadyr Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Anadyr "Gulf of Anadyr") to [Cape Dezhnyov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Dezhnyov "Cape Dezhnyov"). Atop his earlier pioneering charts, Ivanov is credited with the creation of the early map of [Chukotka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chukchi_Peninsula "Chukchi Peninsula") and the Bering Strait, which was the first to show on paper (very schematically) the yet undiscovered [Wrangel Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangel_Island "Wrangel Island"), both the [Diomede Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diomede_Islands "Diomede Islands") and Alaska, based on the data collected from the natives of Chukotka.

By the mid-17th century, the Russians established the borders of their country close to modern ones, and explored almost the whole of Siberia, except the eastern Kamchatka and some regions north of the Arctic Circle. The conquest of Kamchatka later would be achieved in the early 1700s by [Vladimir Atlasov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Atlasov "Vladimir Atlasov"), while the discovery of the Arctic coastline and Alaska would be completed by the [Great Northern Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_Expedition "Great Northern Expedition") in 1733–1743.

## Global impact

European overseas expansion led to contact between the Old and New Worlds producing the Columbian exchange. It started the [global silver trade between the Americas, Europe, and China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_silver_trade_from_the_16th_to_19th_centuries "Global silver trade from the 16th to 19th centuries"), and led to direct European involvement in the [Chinese porcelain trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_export_porcelain "Chinese export porcelain"). It involved the transfer of goods unique from one hemisphere to another. From the Old World, Europeans brought cattle, horses, and sheep to the New World, and from the New World Europeans received tobacco, potatoes, tomatoes, and [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize "Maize"). Other items and commodities becoming important in global trade were the tobacco, sugarcane, and cotton crops of the Americas, along with the gold and silver brought from the American continent not only to Europe, but elsewhere in the Old World.

The formation of new transoceanic links and expansion of European influence led to the [Age of Imperialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperialism#Age_of_Imperialism "Imperialism"), which began during the Age of Discovery, during which colonial powers from Europe colonized most territory on the planet. European demand for trade, commodities, colonies and slaves had a drastic impact on the rest of the world; during [European colonization of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas "European colonization of the Americas"), European colonial powers conquered and colonized numerous American Indian nations and cultures, and conducted numerous conversions and attempts at cultural assimilation both voluntary or forced. Combined with the introduction of infectious diseases from Europe, these events led to a [drastic decrease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_history_of_Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas "Population history of Indigenous peoples of the Americas") of the American Indian population. American Indian accounts of European colonization were summarized by scholar Peter Mancall: "The arrival of Europeans brought death, displacement, sorrow, and despair to Native Americans". In some areas, like North America, Central America, Australia, New Zealand, and Argentina, indigenous peoples were poorly treated, driven off their lands, and reduced to dependent minorities in the territory.

Similarly, in [East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Africa "East Africa") and [West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Africa "West Africa"), local states supplied the appetite of [European slave traders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_slave_trade "Atlantic slave trade"), changing the complexion of coastal African states and fundamentally altering the nature of [slavery in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_Africa "Slavery in Africa"), causing impacts on societies and economies deep inland.

In North America, there were many conflicts between European colonists and American Indians. The Europeans had many advantages over the American Indians such as by introducing Eurasian diseases which wiped out 50–90% of the American Indian population because they had not been exposed before and lacked acquired immunity. The Europeans also had advanced technology and weapons to fight against the American Indian population, contributing to their decimation.

Maize and [manioc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava "Cassava") were introduced into Africa in the 16th century by the Portuguese. They are now important staple foods, replacing native African crops. [Alfred W. Crosby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_W._Crosby "Alfred W. Crosby") speculated that increased production of maize, manioc, and other New World crops led to heavier concentrations of population in the areas from which slavers captured their victims.

In the global silver trade, [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty "Ming dynasty") was stimulated by trade with the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire "Portuguese Empire"), [Spaniards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire "Spanish Empire"), and [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_colonial_empire "Dutch colonial empire"). Although global, much of that silver ended up with the Chinese, and China dominated silver imports. Between 1600 and 1800, China received 100 tons of silver on average per year. A large populace near the [Lower Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River_Delta "Yangtze River Delta") averaged hundreds of [taels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tael "Tael") of silver per household in the late 16th century. More than 150,000 tons of silver were shipped from Potosí by the end of the 18th century. From 1500 to 1800, Mexico and Peru produced about 80% of the world's silver, with more than 30% of it eventually ending up in China (largely because European merchants used it to purchase exotic Chinese commodities). In the late 16th and early 17th century, Japan was exporting heavily into China and foreign trade at large. Trade with [European powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_Europe "Early modern Europe") and the Japanese brought in significant amounts of silver, which then replaced copper and paper [banknotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banknote "Banknote") as the common medium of exchange in China. During the last decades of the Ming dynasty, the flow of silver into China was greatly diminished, thereby undermining state revenues and the entire Ming economy. This damage to the economy was compounded by the effects on agriculture of the incipient [Little Ice Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Ice_Age "Little Ice Age"), natural calamities, crop failure, and sudden epidemics. The ensuing breakdown of authority and people's livelihoods allowed rebel leaders such as [Li Zicheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Zicheng "Li Zicheng") to challenge Ming authority.

New crops that had come to Asia from the Americas, via the Spanish colonizers in the 16th century, contributed to Asia's population growth. Although the bulk of imports to China were silver, the Chinese also purchased New World crops from the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire "Spanish Empire"). This included sweet potatoes, maize, and peanuts, foods that could be cultivated in lands where traditional Chinese staple crops—wheat, millet, and rice—could not grow, hence facilitating a rise in the population of China. In the Song dynasty (960–1279), rice had become the major staple crop of the poor; after sweet potatoes were introduced to China around 1560, it gradually became the traditional food of the lower classes.

The arrival of the Portuguese to Japan in 1543 initiated the [Nanban trade period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanban_trade "Nanban trade"), with the Japanese adopting technologies and cultural practices, like the [arquebus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arquebus "Arquebus"), European-style cuirasses, European ships, Christianity, decorative art, and language. After the Chinese had banned direct trade by Chinese merchants with Japan, the Portuguese filled this commercial vacuum as intermediaries. The Portuguese bought Chinese silk and sold it to the Japanese in return for Japanese-mined silver; since silver was more highly valued in China, the Portuguese could then use Japanese silver to buy even larger stocks of Chinese silk. By 1573, after the Spaniards established a trading base in Manila, the Portuguese intermediary trade was trumped by the prime source of incoming silver to China from the Spanish Americas. Although China acted as the cog running the wheel of global trade during the 16th to 18th centuries, Japan's huge contribution of silver exports to China was critical to the world economy and China's liquidity and success with the commodity.

### Economic impact in Europe

As a wider variety of global luxury commodities entered the European markets by sea, previous European markets for [luxury goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxury_good "Luxury good") stagnated. The Atlantic trade largely supplanted pre-existing [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_republics "Maritime republics") and [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League "Hanseatic League") trading powers which had relied on their Baltic, Russian, and Islamic trade links. The new commodities also caused [social change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_change "Social change"), as sugar, spices, silks, and chinawares entered the luxury markets of Europe.

The European economic centre shifted from the Mediterranean to Western Europe. The city of [Antwerp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antwerp "Antwerp"), part of the [Duchy of Brabant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Brabant "Duchy of Brabant"), became "the centre of the *entire* international economy", and the richest city in Europe. Centred in Antwerp first and then [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam "Amsterdam"), the [Dutch Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Golden_Age "Dutch Golden Age") was tightly linked to the Age of Discovery.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

By 1549, the [Portuguese empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire "Portuguese Empire") sent annual trade missions to [Shangchuan Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shangchuan_Island "Shangchuan Island") in China. In 1557, they managed to convince the Ming court to agree on a legal port treaty that would establish Macau as an official Portuguese trade colony. The Portuguese friar [Gaspar da Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaspar_da_Cruz "Gaspar da Cruz") (c. 1520–1570) wrote the first complete book on China published in Europe; it included information on its geography, provinces, royalty, official class, bureaucracy, shipping, architecture, farming, craftsmanship, merchant affairs, clothing, religious and social customs, music and instruments, writing, education, and justice.

From China, the major exports were silk and porcelain, adapted to meet European tastes. The Chinese export porcelains were held in such great esteem in Europe that, in English, *[china](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/china "wikt:china")* became a commonly used synonym for *porcelain*. [Kraak porcelain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraak_porcelain "Kraak porcelain") was among the first Chinese ware to arrive in Europe in significant quantities; only the richest could afford these early imports. Soon the Dutch East India Company established trade with the East, having imported 6 million porcelain items from China to Europe between 1602 and 1682. Kraak, mainly the [blue and white porcelain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_and_white_porcelain "Blue and white porcelain"), was imitated all over the world by potters in [Arita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arita_ware "Arita ware"), Japan and [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_pottery "Persian pottery")—where Dutch merchants turned when the fall of the Ming dynasty rendered Chinese originals unavailable—and ultimately in [delftware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delftware "Delftware"). Dutch and later [English delftware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_delftware "English delftware") inspired by Chinese designs persisted from about 1630 to the mid-18th century alongside European patterns.

[Antonio de Morga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_de_Morga "Antonio de Morga") (1559–1636), a Spanish official in [Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila "Manila"), listed an extensive inventory of goods that were traded by Ming China at the turn of the 16th to 17th century, noting there were "rarities which, did I refer to them all, I would never finish, nor have sufficient paper for it". Ebrey writes of the considerable size of commercial transactions: In one case a galleon to the Spanish territories in the New World carried over 50,000 pairs of silk stockings. In return China imported mostly silver from Peruvian and Mexican mines, [transported via Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_galleon "Manila galleon"). Chinese merchants were active in these trading ventures, and many emigrated to such places as the Philippines and Borneo to take advantage of the new commercial opportunities.

The increase in gold and silver experienced by [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_Spain "Habsburg Spain") coincided with a major inflationary cycle within Spain and Europe, known as the [price revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_revolution "Price revolution"). Spain had amassed large quantities of gold and silver from the New World. In the 1540s, large scale extraction of silver from Mexico began. During the 16th century, Spain held the equivalent of US$1.5 trillion (1990 terms) in gold and silver from [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_New_Spain "Viceroyalty of New Spain"). Being the most powerful European monarch at a time full of war and religious conflicts, the Habsburg rulers spent their wealth in wars and arts across Europe. The spent silver, spread throughout a cash-starved Europe, caused widespread inflation. The inflation was worsened by a growing population with a static production level, low salaries and a rising cost of living, which damaged local industry. Increasingly, Spain became dependent on the revenues flowing in from the mercantile empire, leading to Spain's first bankruptcy in 1557 due to rising military costs. Philip II of Spain defaulted on debt payments in 1557, 1560, 1575, and 1596. The increase in prices as a result of currency circulation fuelled the growth of the commercial [middle class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_class "Middle class") in Europe, the *[bourgeoisie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgeoisie "Bourgeoisie")*, which came to influence the politics and culture of many countries. One effect of the inflation, particularly in Great Britain, was that tenant farmers who held long-term leases from lords saw real decreases in rent. Some lords opted to sell their leased land, giving rise to small, landowning farmers.
