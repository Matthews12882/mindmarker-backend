# Madonna

**Madonna Louise Ciccone** ([/tʃɪˈkoʊni/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*chih-KOH-nee*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"), Italian: [[tʃikˈkoːne]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Italian "Help:IPA/Italian"); born August 16, 1958) is an American singer, songwriter, record producer, and actress. Dubbed the "**Queen of Pop**", she is known for her continual reinvention and versatility in music production, songwriting, and visual presentation. Her works, which concern social, political, [sexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_and_sexuality "Madonna and sexuality"), and [religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_and_religion "Madonna and religion") themes, have drawn acclaim and controversy. Regarded as one of the most influential musicians in history, Madonna broke [gender barriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_inequality "Gender inequality") in [popular music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_music "Popular music") and has had [a significant socio-cultural impact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_impact_of_Madonna "Cultural impact of Madonna") across her career.

Born and raised in [Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan "Michigan"), Madonna moved to New York City in 1978 to pursue a career in dance. After performing as a drummer, guitarist, and vocalist in the rock bands [Breakfast Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakfast_Club_(band) "Breakfast Club (band)") and Emmy, she rose to fame with her debut album, *[Madonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_(album) "Madonna (album)")* (1983). Her eighteen multi-platinum albums include *[Like a Virgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Like_a_Virgin_(album) "Like a Virgin (album)")* (1984), *[True Blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Blue_(album) "True Blue (album)")* (1986), and *[The Immaculate Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Immaculate_Collection "The Immaculate Collection")* (1990)—among the [best-selling albums of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_best-selling_albums "List of best-selling albums")—and *[Confessions on a Dance Floor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confessions_on_a_Dance_Floor "Confessions on a Dance Floor")* (2005), her [best-selling 21st-century album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_best-selling_albums_of_the_21st_century "List of best-selling albums of the 21st century"). *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")* ranked *[Like a Prayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Like_a_Prayer_(album) "Like a Prayer (album)")* (1989), *[Ray of Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_of_Light "Ray of Light")* (1998), and *[Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_(Madonna_album) "Music (Madonna album)")* (2000) among the [greatest albums of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone%27s_500_Greatest_Albums_of_All_Time "Rolling Stone's 500 Greatest Albums of All Time"). Madonna's top-charting [singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_singles_discography "Madonna singles discography") include "[Like a Virgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Like_a_Virgin_(song) "Like a Virgin (song)")", "[Papa Don't Preach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papa_Don%27t_Preach "Papa Don't Preach")", "[La Isla Bonita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Isla_Bonita "La Isla Bonita")", "[Like a Prayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Like_a_Prayer_(song) "Like a Prayer (song)")", "[Vogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vogue_(song) "Vogue (song)")", "[Take a Bow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Take_a_Bow_(Madonna_song) "Take a Bow (Madonna song)")", "[Frozen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frozen_(Madonna_song) "Frozen (Madonna song)")", "[Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_(Madonna_song) "Music (Madonna song)")", "[Hung Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hung_Up "Hung Up")", and "[4 Minutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4_Minutes "4 Minutes")".

[Madonna has starred in films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_filmography "Madonna filmography") such as *[Desperately Seeking Susan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desperately_Seeking_Susan "Desperately Seeking Susan")* (1985), *[Dick Tracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Tracy_(1990_film) "Dick Tracy (1990 film)")* (1990), *[A League of Their Own](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_League_of_Their_Own "A League of Their Own")* (1992), and *[Evita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evita_(1996_film) "Evita (1996 film)")* (1996). Although many of her films were not well-received, she won the [Golden Globe Award for Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Comedy_or_Musical "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress – Motion Picture Comedy or Musical") for portraying [Eva Perón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eva_Per%C3%B3n "Eva Perón") in *Evita*. [Her business endeavors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_and_business "Madonna and business") encompass the entertainment company [Maverick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maverick_(company) "Maverick (company)") (1992–2009), which included Maverick Records—one of the most successful [artist-run labels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanity_label "Vanity label"). Madonna has also pursued [fashion brands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_fashion_brands "Madonna fashion brands"), [written works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_bibliography "Madonna bibliography"), [health clubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_Candy_Fitness "Hard Candy Fitness"), and filmmaking. She contributes to [various charities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philanthropy_and_activism_of_Madonna "Philanthropy and activism of Madonna"), having founded the [Ray of Light Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_of_Light_Foundation "Ray of Light Foundation") in 1998 and [Raising Malawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raising_Malawi "Raising Malawi") in 2006, and advocates for [gender equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminism_of_Madonna "Feminism of Madonna") and [LGBTQ rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_as_a_gay_icon "Madonna as a gay icon").

Madonna is the [best-selling female music artist of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_best-selling_music_artists "List of best-selling music artists"), and the [first female performer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_live_music_artists "List of highest-grossing live music artists") to accumulate US$1 billion from [her concerts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Madonna_concerts "List of Madonna concerts"). She has [twelve number-one singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artists_with_the_most_number-ones_on_the_Billboard_Hot_100_chart "Artists with the most number-ones on the Billboard Hot 100 chart") on the US [*Billboard* Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100 "Billboard Hot 100") and [ten number-one albums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200#Most_number-one_albums "Billboard 200") on the US [*Billboard* 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200"). [Her accolades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Madonna "List of awards and nominations received by Madonna") include seven [Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Awards "Grammy Awards"), two [Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards "Golden Globe Awards"), 27 [MTV Video Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Awards "MTV Video Music Awards"), and [an induction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame_inductees "List of Rock and Roll Hall of Fame inductees") into the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame "Rock and Roll Hall of Fame") in her first year of eligibility. Madonna was the world's [highest-paid female musician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_list_of_the_world%27s_highest-paid_musicians#Female "Forbes list of the world's highest-paid musicians") for a record eleven years across four decades (1980s–2010s). She has become the subject of various [scholarly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_academic_publishing_works_on_Madonna "List of academic publishing works on Madonna"), [literary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliography_of_works_on_Madonna "Bibliography of works on Madonna"), and [artistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_and_contemporary_arts "Madonna and contemporary arts") works, giving rise to an academic subfield known as [Madonna studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_studies "Madonna studies").

## Life and career

### 1958–1978: Early life

Madonna Louise Ciccone was born in [Bay City, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_City,_Michigan "Bay City, Michigan"), on August 16, 1958. Her father, Silvio "Tony" Ciccone, worked as an [optical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_engineering "Optical engineering") and [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_engineering "Military engineering") engineer, as well as a physicist, for [Chrysler Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_Defense "Chrysler Defense") and later [General Dynamics Land Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Dynamics_Land_Systems "General Dynamics Land Systems"); her mother, Madonna Louise ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%A9e "Née") Fortin), was an [X-ray technician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_technician "X-ray technician"). Tony's parents were Italian emigrants from [Pacentro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacentro "Pacentro"), while her mother was of [French-Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French-Canadian "French-Canadian") descent. Since Madonna shared her name with her mother, family members referred to her as "Little Nonnie". Madonna was raised in the Detroit suburbs of [Pontiac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontiac,_Michigan "Pontiac, Michigan") and Avon Township (now [Rochester Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester_Hills "Rochester Hills")), alongside her two older brothers, Anthony and Martin, and her three younger siblings, Paula, [Christopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Ciccone "Christopher Ciccone"), and Melanie.

When Madonna was five years old, her mother died of [breast cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breast_cancer "Breast cancer") on December 1, 1963. In 1966, she adopted Veronica as a [confirmation name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confirmation_name "Confirmation name") upon receiving the [sacrament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confirmation_in_the_Catholic_Church "Confirmation in the Catholic Church") in the Catholic Church. That same year, Tony married the family's housekeeper, Joan Gustafson. They had three children: Joey (who died shortly after his birth in 1967 from a [heart defect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_defect "Heart defect")), Jennifer, and Mario. Madonna attended St. Frederick's and St. Andrew's Catholic elementary schools, as well as West Middle School. She earned high grades—her father gave her a [quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarter_(United_States_coin) "Quarter (United States coin)") for every [A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grading_in_education "Grading in education")—and was notorious for her unconventional behavior. Madonna performed cartwheels and handstands in the hallways between classes, hung upside down from the monkey bars during recess, and lifted her skirt in class to amuse the boys.

In retrospect, Madonna described herself as a "lonely girl who was searching for something", explaining: "I wasn't rebellious in a certain way. I cared about being good at something. I didn't shave under my arms and I didn't wear makeup like normal girls do. But I studied and I got good grades [...] I wanted to be somebody." Her father initially enrolled her in [classical piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_piano "Classical piano") lessons, but she eventually persuaded him to let her study [ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballet "Ballet") instead. Her ballet teacher, Christopher Flynn, inspired her to pursue a career in dance, remarking that she was the best student in the class. While attending [Rochester Adams High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester_Adams_High_School "Rochester Adams High School"), Madonna was a straight-A student and a member of the cheerleading squad. After graduating in January 1976, she received a dance scholarship to the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan "University of Michigan") and spent the summer studying at the [American Dance Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Dance_Festival "American Dance Festival") in [Durham, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham,_North_Carolina "Durham, North Carolina").

In 1978, Madonna left college and moved to New York City. She called the decision "the bravest thing [she'd] ever done"; it was the first time she had ever flown or taken a taxi, and she arrived with "$35 in [her] pocket". She settled in the [Alphabet City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphabet_City "Alphabet City") area of the [East Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Village,_Manhattan "East Village, Manhattan") and supported herself with limited means by working various jobs—including as a hatcheck girl at the [Russian Tea Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Tea_Room "Russian Tea Room"), an elevator operator at [Terrace on the Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrace_on_the_Park "Terrace on the Park"), and a member of dance troupes. Madonna took classes at the [Alvin Ailey American Dance Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvin_Ailey_American_Dance_Theater "Alvin Ailey American Dance Theater"), performed with the [Pearl Lang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_Lang "Pearl Lang") Dance Theater, and studied under dancer and choreographer [Martha Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Graham "Martha Graham"). One night, as she was returning from a rehearsal, two men held her at knifepoint and forced her to perform [fellatio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellatio "Fellatio"). She said later the incident was "a taste of my weakness, it showed me that I still could not save myself in spite of all the strong-girl show. I could never forget it." She was also raped at knifepoint after accepting an offer to use someone's phone in their apartment in New York.

### 1979–1983: Career beginnings, rock bands, and *Madonna*

In 1979, Madonna entered a romantic relationship with musician [Dan Gilroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Gilroy_(musician) "Dan Gilroy (musician)"). During this period, she searched for job opportunities in publications such as *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")*, *[Backstage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backstage_(magazine) "Backstage (magazine)")*, and *[Show Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Show_Business_(magazine) "Show Business (magazine)")*, leading to a successful audition as a backup singer and dancer in Paris for French disco artist [Patrick Hernandez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Hernandez "Patrick Hernandez"). With Hernandez's troupe, she traveled to Tunisia and several disco-oriented European countries before returning to New York that same year. Madonna relocated to an abandoned [synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synagogue "Synagogue") in [Corona, Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corona,_Queens "Corona, Queens"), where Gilroy and his brother Ed resided and practiced. The group slept in the basement and used its meeting space both to rehearse and record music for their band, [Breakfast Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakfast_Club_(band) "Breakfast Club (band)"), for which Madonna sang and played drums and guitar.

Madonna made her acting debut in the low-budget indie film *[A Certain Sacrifice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Certain_Sacrifice "A Certain Sacrifice")*, which was shot in two parts between 1979 and 1981. She unsuccessfully attempted to block its 1985 release through legal action against director Stephen Jon Lewicki. The film was primarily criticized for its explicit sexual and violent content. In 1980, after leaving the Breakfast Club and ending her relationship with Gilroy, Madonna reunited with drummer [Stephen Bray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Bray "Stephen Bray"), whom she had previously dated in Michigan, and together they formed the band Emmy. She and Bray lived and rehearsed at [the Music Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Music_Building "The Music Building") in Manhattan, where they wrote songs and recorded a four-track demo tape. After Madonna left Emmy, Camille Barbone, who ran [Gotham Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotham_Records "Gotham Records") in the Music Building, signed her to a contract with the label in March 1981, working as her manager until February 1982. Her creative partnership with Bray continued for many years.

In 1982, Madonna visited nightclubs to persuade DJs to play her demo, leading [Mark Kamins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Kamins "Mark Kamins") at [Danceteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danceteria "Danceteria") to take an interest in her music and begin a romance with her. He arranged a meeting with [Seymour Stein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seymour_Stein "Seymour Stein"), president of [Sire Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sire_Records "Sire Records")—a subsidiary of [Warner Bros. Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Records "Warner Bros. Records")—after which she signed a two-single deal. Kamins produced her debut single, "[Everybody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everybody_(Madonna_song) "Everybody (Madonna song)")", which was released in October 1982 and promoted with television and nightclub performances. Her second single, the [double A-side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-side_and_B-side "A-side and B-side") "[Burning Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burning_Up_(Madonna_song) "Burning Up (Madonna song)")" / "[Physical Attraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_Attraction_(song) "Physical Attraction (song)")", was released in March 1983. Both "Everybody" and "Burning Up" / "Physical Attraction" reached number three on the [*Billboard* Hot Dance Club Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Dance_Club_Songs "Hot Dance Club Songs") chart. During this period, Madonna was in a relationship with artist [Jean-Michel Basquiat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Michel_Basquiat "Jean-Michel Basquiat") and living in his [SoHo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SoHo "SoHo") loft. Basquiat introduced her to art curator [Diego Cortez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Cortez "Diego Cortez"), who had managed several [punk bands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_band "Punk band"). Cortez declined to manage Madonna when she requested that he do so.

Warner hired [Reggie Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggie_Lucas "Reggie Lucas") to produce her debut studio album, *[Madonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_(album) "Madonna (album)")*. Madonna was dissatisfied with many of the tracks, prompting her to seek additional support. She enlisted DJ [John "Jellybean" Benitez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_%22Jellybean%22_Benitez "John \"Jellybean\" Benitez") to help complete the album, and the two began a brief romantic relationship. Benitez remixed most of the songs and produced "[Holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holiday_(Madonna_song) "Holiday (Madonna song)")", her breakthrough song. *Madonna* was released on July 27, 1983, to generally favorable reviews, and peaked at number eight on the US [*Billboard* 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200"). The album generated two US [*Billboard* Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100 "Billboard Hot 100") top-ten singles: "[Borderline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borderline_(Madonna_song) "Borderline (Madonna song)")" and "[Lucky Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucky_Star_(Madonna_song) "Lucky Star (Madonna song)")". In late 1983, Madonna's new manager, [Freddy DeMann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freddy_DeMann "Freddy DeMann"), arranged a meeting with film producer [Jon Peters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Peters "Jon Peters"), who offered her the role of a club singer in the romantic drama *[Vision Quest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vision_Quest_(film) "Vision Quest (film)")* (1985).

### 1984–1987: *Like a Virgin*, first marriage, *True Blue*, and *Who's That Girl*

In January 1984, Madonna gained further exposure with performances on *[American Bandstand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Bandstand "American Bandstand")* and *[Top of the Pops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_of_the_Pops "Top of the Pops")*. Wanting the material on her second studio album, *[Like a Virgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Like_a_Virgin_(album) "Like a Virgin (album)")*, to be "stronger" than that of her debut, she selected all of the songs herself, five of which she wrote or co-wrote. *Like a Virgin* was released on November 12, 1984, and became her first number-one album in Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Spain, the UK and the US. In the lattermost nation, the album remained atop the *Billboard* 200 for three weeks and is the first album by a woman to sell over five million copies. *Like a Virgin* has sold over 21 million copies worldwide, making it one of the [best-selling albums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_best-selling_albums "List of best-selling albums").

Warner issued "[Like a Virgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Like_a_Virgin_(song) "Like a Virgin (song)")" as the lead single from the album. It was her first number-one on the *Billboard* Hot 100, topping the chart for six consecutive weeks. "Like a Virgin" attracted the attention of conservative organizations, who complained that the song and its accompanying video promoted premarital sex and undermined family values; [moralists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moralist "Moralist") sought to have the song and video banned. Madonna attracted significant media attention for her performance of the song at the inaugural [MTV Video Music Awards in 1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_MTV_Video_Music_Awards "1984 MTV Video Music Awards"). Dressed in a wedding gown and white gloves, she appeared atop a large wedding cake before moving across the stage in a provocative manner. [MTV News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_News "MTV News") later described the performance as one of the most iconic in pop music history.

The second single, "[Material Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Material_Girl "Material Girl")", peaked at number two on the *Billboard* Hot 100. While filming its music video, Madonna began a relationship with actor [Sean Penn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Penn "Sean Penn") and they married on her twenty-seventh birthday in 1985. In *Vision Quest* (1985), Madonna appears as a nightclub singer and performs "[Crazy for You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crazy_for_You_(Madonna_song) "Crazy for You (Madonna song)")"—her second *Billboard* Hot 100 number-one single. That same year, she starred as the titular character in the comedy *[Desperately Seeking Susan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desperately_Seeking_Susan "Desperately Seeking Susan")*, depicting a free-spirited [bohemian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemianism "Bohemianism") drifter whose path intersects with a bored housewife through personal ads. The film included the song "[Into the Groove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Into_the_Groove "Into the Groove")", her first number-one single on the [UK singles chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_singles_chart "UK singles chart"). *Desperately Seeking Susan* was named one of the ten best films of 1985 by [Vincent Canby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Canby "Vincent Canby"), a *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* film critic.

In April 1985, Madonna began her first North American concert series, [the Virgin Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Virgin_Tour "The Virgin Tour"). It coincided with the height of the [Madonna wannabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_wannabe "Madonna wannabe") phenomenon, as many of her young female fans adopted [her fashion style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_of_Madonna "Fashion of Madonna"). Styled by [Maripol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maripol "Maripol"), her look—which included lace tops, skirts over [capri pants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capri_pants "Capri pants"), [fishnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishnet "Fishnet") stockings, [crucifix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crucifix "Crucifix") jewelry, stacked bracelets, and bleached hair—became widely associated with 1980s female fashion trends. Around this time, Madonna released two additional singles for *Like a Virgin*, "[Angel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel_(Madonna_song) "Angel (Madonna song)")" and "[Dress You Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dress_You_Up "Dress You Up")", both of which reached the top five of the *Billboard* Hot 100. In July 1985, *[Penthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penthouse_(magazine) "Penthouse (magazine)")* and *Playboy* published nude photographs of Madonna taken in 1978, when she worked as an [art model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_model "Art model"). She had posed for the images for modest pay, reportedly $25 per session; the photographs were later sold for up to $100,000. The publication caused significant media attention, though Madonna remained unapologetic.

Madonna co-wrote and co-produced every track on her third studio album, *[True Blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Blue_(album) "True Blue (album)")*. Inspired by and dedicated to her husband Penn, the album was released on June 30, 1986, to critical acclaim. It topped the charts in an unprecedented 28 countries worldwide, including the US, where it remained at number one on the *Billboard* 200 for five weeks. All five singles released from the album reached the top five on the *Billboard* Hot 100; "[Live to Tell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_to_Tell "Live to Tell")", "[Papa Don't Preach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papa_Don%27t_Preach "Papa Don't Preach")", and "[Open Your Heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Your_Heart_(Madonna_song) "Open Your Heart (Madonna song)")" made Madonna the second female artist to have three songs from a single album top the chart. "[True Blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Blue_(Madonna_song) "True Blue (Madonna song)")" and "[La Isla Bonita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Isla_Bonita "La Isla Bonita")" also topped the [European singles chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Hot_100_Singles "European Hot 100 Singles"); the latter became the continent's [biggest single of 1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_European_number-one_hits_of_1987 "List of European number-one hits of 1987"). *True Blue* was the best-selling album of 1986, the best-selling album of the 1980s by a female artist, and one of the best-selling albums in history, with 25 million copies sold globally.

Madonna appeared in the film *[Shanghai Surprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_Surprise "Shanghai Surprise")* (1986) alongside Penn, which was critically unsuccessful and earned her the [Golden Raspberry Award for Worst Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Raspberry_Award_for_Worst_Actress "Golden Raspberry Award for Worst Actress"). She made her stage debut the same year in [David Rabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rabe "David Rabe")'s *Goose and Tom-Tom*, which also starred Penn. In 1987, she starred in the film *[Who's That Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who%27s_That_Girl_(1987_film) "Who's That Girl (1987 film)")* and contributed four songs to [its soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who%27s_That_Girl_(soundtrack) "Who's That Girl (soundtrack)"), including "[Who's That Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who%27s_That_Girl_(Madonna_song) "Who's That Girl (Madonna song)")" and "[Causing a Commotion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causing_a_Commotion "Causing a Commotion")", which peaked at numbers one and two on the *Billboard* Hot 100, respectively. In June, she embarked on the [Who's That Girl World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who%27s_That_Girl_World_Tour "Who's That Girl World Tour"), which broke several attendance records, including a performance near Paris attended by over 130,000 people—the [highest for a female artist at the time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-attended_concerts "List of highest-attended concerts"). In 1987, she released *[You Can Dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_Can_Dance "You Can Dance")*, a [remix album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remix_album "Remix album") with reworked versions of songs from her earlier releases. After a turbulent two-year marriage, Madonna filed for divorce from Penn on December 4, 1987, though she later withdrew the petition.

### 1988–1991: *Like a Prayer*, *Dick Tracy*, and *The Immaculate Collection*

Madonna made her [Broadway theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_theatre "Broadway theatre") debut in the play *[Speed-the-Plow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed-the-Plow "Speed-the-Plow")* at the [Royale Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royale_Theatre "Royale Theatre"), running from May to August 1988. According to the [Associated Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_Press "Associated Press"), she filed an assault report against Penn following an alleged incident at their [Malibu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malibu,_California "Malibu, California") residence over New Year's weekend. Madonna filed for divorce on January 5, 1989, and reportedly requested the following week that no criminal charges be pursued. That month, Madonna signed an endorsement agreement with soft-drink company [Pepsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepsi "Pepsi"). In a commercial for the company, she premiered the song "[Like a Prayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Like_a_Prayer_(song) "Like a Prayer (song)")", whose accompanying music video featured [Catholic imagery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_symbols "Christian symbols") such as [stigmata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stigmata "Stigmata") and [cross burning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_burning "Cross burning"), as well as a dream sequence depicting intimacy with a saint, prompting condemnation from the [Vatican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_See "Holy See"). Religious organizations called for a boycott of Pepsi products, leading the company to withdraw the commercial and terminate her sponsorship deal.

"Like a Prayer" was released as the lead single from her fourth studio album, also titled [*Like a Prayer*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Like_a_Prayer_(album) "Like a Prayer (album)"). The song became her seventh number-one single on the *Billboard* Hot 100 and spent three weeks atop the chart. Madonna co-wrote and co-produced her album alongside [Patrick Leonard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Leonard "Patrick Leonard"), Stephen Bray, and [Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_(musician) "Prince (musician)"). In the US, *Like a Prayer* was released on March 21, 1989, to universal acclaim from critics. *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")*'s [J. D. Considine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._D._Considine "J. D. Considine") described it as "proof not only that Madonna should be taken seriously as an artist but that hers is one of the most compelling voices of the Eighties". *Like a Prayer* spent six weeks atop the *Billboard* 200—the longest of any of her albums—and eventually sold 15 million copies worldwide. The singles "[Express Yourself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Express_Yourself_(Madonna_song) "Express Yourself (Madonna song)")" and "[Cherish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherish_(Madonna_song) "Cherish (Madonna song)")" both reached number two in the US, while "[Keep It Together](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keep_It_Together_(song) "Keep It Together (song)")" peaked within the top ten. *Billboard* and MTV named Madonna "Artist of the Decade" (1980s).

In April 1990, she launched the [Blond Ambition World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blond_Ambition_World_Tour "Blond Ambition World Tour"). *Rolling Stone* described it as an "elaborately choreographed, sexually provocative extravaganza" and named it the best tour of 1990. It drew criticism from religious organizations, particularly for her performance of "Like a Virgin", in which she simulated [masturbation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masturbation "Masturbation") while being caressed by two male dancers. Madonna defended the show, stating that it was intended for "open minds" and encouraged audiences to view sexuality differently. [The live recording of the tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blond_Ambition_World_Tour_Live "Blond Ambition World Tour Live") earned Madonna the [Grammy Award for Best Long Form Music Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Long_Form_Music_Video "Grammy Award for Best Long Form Music Video"). Her first documentary film, *[Truth or Dare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truth_or_Dare_(documentary) "Truth or Dare (documentary)")* (titled *In Bed with Madonna* outside North America), was released in May 1991. Chronicling her Blond Ambition World Tour, it became the highest-grossing documentary ever at the time, a record it held until it was surpassed eleven years later.

Madonna portrayed [Breathless Mahoney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breathless_Mahoney "Breathless Mahoney") in the film *[Dick Tracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Tracy_(1990_film) "Dick Tracy (1990 film)")* (1990), directed by and starring [Warren Beatty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Beatty "Warren Beatty") in the title role. The film [topped the US box office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_1990_box_office_number-one_films_in_the_United_States "List of 1990 box office number-one films in the United States") for two consecutive weeks, and [Owen Gleiberman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Gleiberman "Owen Gleiberman") of *[Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")* praised Madonna's performance, saying that he wished she had been given more screen time. To accompany the film, she released the soundtrack album *[I'm Breathless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%27m_Breathless "I'm Breathless")*, with songs largely inspired by 1930s music. It spawned the single "[Vogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vogue_(song) "Vogue (song)")", which topped the charts in more than 30 countries, including the US, where it peaked at number one on the Hot 100 for three weeks. During production, Madonna and Beatty began a relationship that ended shortly after the film's premiere. In October 1990, she recorded a [public service announcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_service_announcement "Public service announcement") supporting [Rock the Vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_the_Vote "Rock the Vote")'s voter registration campaign.

Madonna's first [greatest-hits compilation album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greatest_hits_album "Greatest hits album"), *[The Immaculate Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Immaculate_Collection "The Immaculate Collection")*, was released in November 1990. The album has sold 30 million copies worldwide, making it the best-selling compilation album by a solo artist and one of the best-selling albums in history. The set included two new songs, "[Justify My Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justify_My_Love "Justify My Love")" and "[Rescue Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rescue_Me_(Madonna_song) "Rescue Me (Madonna song)")". Model [Tony Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Ward_(model) "Tony Ward (model)"), Madonna's then-boyfriend, co-starred in the music video for "Justify My Love", which included imagery of [sadomasochism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadomasochism "Sadomasochism"), [bondage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bondage_(BDSM) "Bondage (BDSM)"), same-sex intimacy, and brief nudity. Considered too sexually explicit, the video was banned by MTV. The resulting controversy contributed to the song reaching number one on the *Billboard* Hot 100, becoming her ninth chart-topping single.

### 1992–1997: Maverick, *Erotica*, *Sex*, *Bedtime Stories*, *Evita*, and motherhood

In 1992, Madonna appeared in the film *[A League of Their Own](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_League_of_Their_Own "A League of Their Own")* as a member of an all-women's baseball team. The film reached [number one at the US box office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_1992_box_office_number-one_films_in_the_United_States "List of 1992 box office number-one films in the United States") in its second weekend of release. Madonna also recorded its theme song, "[This Used to Be My Playground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Used_to_Be_My_Playground "This Used to Be My Playground")", which became her tenth *Billboard* Hot 100 number-one single, the most for a woman at the time. In April 1992, Madonna established the entertainment company [Maverick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maverick_(entertainment_company) "Maverick (entertainment company)"), which included a record label, film production unit, and divisions for music publishing, television, book publishing, and merchandising. The venture was a partnership with [Time Warner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Warner "Time Warner") and provided her with a $60 million advance and a 20 percent royalty rate—the highest in the industry at the time. Maverick went on to become one of the most successful [artist-run labels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanity_label "Vanity label") in history, signing successful acts such as [Alanis Morissette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alanis_Morissette "Alanis Morissette") and [Michelle Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_Branch "Michelle Branch").

Later in 1992, Madonna co-sponsored the first museum retrospective of her former partner, artist Jean-Michel Basquiat, at the [Whitney Museum of American Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Museum_of_American_Art "Whitney Museum of American Art") in New York. She released her fifth studio album, *[Erotica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erotica_(album) "Erotica (album)")*, on October 20, 1992, in the US, and published her [coffee table book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_table_book "Coffee table book") *[Sex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_(book) "Sex (book)")* a day later. *Sex* featured sexually provocative and explicit images photographed by [Steven Meisel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Meisel "Steven Meisel") and drew strong negative reactions from critics, the general public, and conservative, [feminist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminist "Feminist") and [anti-porn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-porn "Anti-porn") groups; initial interest propelled the book to the top of [*The New York Times* Best Seller list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times_Best_Seller_list "The New York Times Best Seller list"). A writer from *[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")* described it as an "oversized, overpriced, [and boring] coffee table book of hardcore sexual fantasies".

The widespread backlash overshadowed *Erotica*, which became her lowest-selling album at the time. It reached number two on the US *Billboard* 200, and received favorable reviews from critics. The singles "[Erotica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erotica_(song) "Erotica (song)")" and "[Deeper and Deeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deeper_and_Deeper "Deeper and Deeper")" reached the top ten of the *Billboard* Hot 100 and number one in Italy. During this period, Madonna was in a relationship with rapper [Vanilla Ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanilla_Ice "Vanilla Ice"), to whom she proposed. The pair separated following the release of *Sex*, with Vanilla Ice claiming he had been included in the book without his consent. Her performances in the 1993 films *[Body of Evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_of_Evidence_(1993_film) "Body of Evidence (1993 film)")* and *[Dangerous Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dangerous_Game_(1993_film) "Dangerous Game (1993 film)")* were poorly received. The former featured scenes of sadomasochism and bondage, while she played the abused wife of a filmmaker in the latter.

Madonna launched her fourth concert tour, [the Girlie Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Girlie_Show_(concert_tour) "The Girlie Show (concert tour)"), in September 1993, during which she appeared as a whip-cracking [dominatrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominatrix "Dominatrix") and performed alongside topless dancers. She made [a highly publicized appearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_on_the_Late_Show_with_David_Letterman_in_1994 "Madonna on the Late Show with David Letterman in 1994") on the *[Late Show with David Letterman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Show_with_David_Letterman "Late Show with David Letterman")* in March 1994, using profanity that required broadcast censorship and handing Letterman a pair of her underwear while urging him to smell it. The sexually explicit releases of *Sex* and *Erotica*, along with her film roles and controversial Letterman appearance, prompted commentators to question her dependence on provocative imagery. Madonna briefly dated rapper [Tupac Shakur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupac_Shakur "Tupac Shakur") and basketball player [Dennis Rodman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Rodman "Dennis Rodman") around this time. Biographer [J. Randy Taraborrelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Randy_Taraborrelli "J. Randy Taraborrelli") viewed Madonna's ballad "[I'll Remember](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%27ll_Remember "I'll Remember")"—recorded for the film *[With Honors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/With_Honors_(film) "With Honors (film)")* (1994)—as an effort to soften her provocative image. It peaked at number two on the *Billboard* Hot 100.

With her sixth studio album, *[Bedtime Stories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedtime_Stories_(album) "Bedtime Stories (album)")*, Madonna embraced a gentler sound and image to regain public favor. Released in the US on October 25, 1994, the album debuted at number three on the *Billboard* 200 and produced the singles "[Secret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_(Madonna_song) "Secret (Madonna song)")" and "[Take a Bow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Take_a_Bow_(Madonna_song) "Take a Bow (Madonna song)")", which peaked at numbers three and one on the *Billboard* Hot 100, respectively. "Take a Bow" spent seven weeks atop the chart, making it her longest-running number-one. Madonna sponsored the first major retrospective of [Tina Modotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tina_Modotti "Tina Modotti")'s work at the [Philadelphia Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Museum_of_Art "Philadelphia Museum of Art") in 1995, and later supported exhibitions of Basquiat's paintings at London's [Serpentine Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serpentine_Gallery "Serpentine Gallery") and [Cindy Sherman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cindy_Sherman "Cindy Sherman") at New York City's [Museum of Modern Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Modern_Art "Museum of Modern Art"). In November 1995, Madonna released *[Something to Remember](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Something_to_Remember "Something to Remember")*, a compilation album that comprises Madonna's ballads across the past decade. The record debuted at number six on the *Billboard* 200 and has sold ten million copies worldwide.

> This is the role I was born to play. I put everything of me into this because it was much more than a role in a movie. It was exhilarating and intimidating at the same time. And I am prouder of *Evita* than anything else I have done.

— Madonna talking about her role in *[Evita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evita_(1996_film) "Evita (1996 film)")*

In February 1996, Madonna began filming the musical *[Evita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evita_(1996_film) "Evita (1996 film)")* in Argentina. Having long aspired to portray Argentine political leader [Eva Perón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eva_Per%C3%B3n "Eva Perón"), she wrote to director [Alan Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Parker "Alan Parker") to express her interest in the role. After being cast, she undertook vocal training and studied Argentina's history and Perón's life. During production, Madonna experienced several bouts of illness related to pregnancy and the emotional demands of the film. Upon its release in December 1996, her performance was met with positive reviews from critics. [Richard Corliss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Corliss "Richard Corliss"), writing for *Time*, described the film as well cast and visually impressive, lauding Madonna's ability to exceed expectations. Her portrayal earned her the [Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Musical_or_Comedy "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy").

The [*Evita* soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evita_(soundtrack) "Evita (soundtrack)"), which peaked at number two on the *Billboard* 200, contained songs primarily performed by Madonna. These included "[You Must Love Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_Must_Love_Me "You Must Love Me")" and "[Don't Cry for Me Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don%27t_Cry_for_Me_Argentina#Madonna_version "Don't Cry for Me Argentina")", the latter of which topped the European singles chart. On October 14, 1996, she gave birth to her daughter, [Lourdes "Lola" Maria Ciccone Leon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lourdes_Leon "Lourdes Leon"), with fitness trainer Carlos Leon. According to biographer [Mary Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Cross "Mary Cross"), Madonna—who had expressed concern that her pregnancy might jeopardize *Evita*—fulfilled significant personal goals: "Madonna had at last triumphed on screen and achieved her dream of having a child, both in the same year [...] reinventing herself and her image with the public". Her relationship with Carlos ended in May 1997, and she stated that they were "better off as best friends".

### 1998–2002: *Ray of Light*, *Music*, second marriage, and touring comeback

After the birth of Lourdes, Madonna developed an interest in [Eastern mysticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_mysticism "Eastern mysticism") and [Kabbalah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabbalah "Kabbalah"), introduced to her by actress [Sandra Bernhard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_Bernhard "Sandra Bernhard"). This change in perception influenced her seventh studio album, *[Ray of Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_of_Light "Ray of Light")*. She collaborated with [electronic music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_music "Electronic music") producer [William Orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Orbit "William Orbit") to create a fusion of dance, [pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_music "Pop music"), and [British rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_rock "British rock"). Music critic [Ann Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Powers "Ann Powers") said Madonna sought "a kind of lushness" for the album rather than the party-oriented sounds of 1990s [techno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Techno "Techno") and [rave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rave "Rave") genres, opting instead for a more singer-songwriter approach that Orbit helped her achieve. *Ray of Light* was released on February 22, 1998. It debuted at number two on the *Billboard* 200 with the highest first-week sales for a woman at the time, topped the charts of 17 countries, and eventually sold over 16 million copies globally. Critically acclaimed, *Ray of Light* is widely regarded as Madonna's *[magnum opus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnum_opus "Magnum opus")*.

The album's two highest-charting singles, "[Frozen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frozen_(Madonna_song) "Frozen (Madonna song)")" and "[Ray of Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_of_Light_(song) "Ray of Light (song)")", peaked at numbers two and five on the US *Billboard* Hot 100, with the former becoming her first song to debut at number one in the UK. At the [41st Annual Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/41st_Annual_Grammy_Awards "41st Annual Grammy Awards"), Madonna won four awards, including [Best Pop Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Pop_Album "Best Pop Album") for *Ray of Light*, and [Best Dance Recording](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Dance_Recording "Best Dance Recording") and [Best Short Form Music Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Short_Form_Music_Video "Best Short Form Music Video") for the title track. In 1998, Madonna established the [Ray of Light Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_of_Light_Foundation "Ray of Light Foundation"), a non-profit for women, education, global development, and humanitarian aid. She recorded the single "[Beautiful Stranger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beautiful_Stranger "Beautiful Stranger")" for the film *[Austin Powers: The Spy Who Shagged Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Powers:_The_Spy_Who_Shagged_Me "Austin Powers: The Spy Who Shagged Me")* (1999), which earned her a [Grammy Award for Best Song Written for a Motion Picture, Television or Other Visual Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Song_Written_for_a_Motion_Picture,_Television_or_Other_Visual_Media "Grammy Award for Best Song Written for a Motion Picture, Television or Other Visual Media"). She starred as a yoga teacher in the comedy-drama *[The Next Best Thing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Next_Best_Thing "The Next Best Thing")* (2000), which received negative reviews and opened at number two at the US box office. For the soundtrack, she recorded a cover of [Don McLean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_McLean "Don McLean")'s "[American Pie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Pie_(song) "American Pie (song)")", her ninth UK number-one single.

On September 18, 2000, Madonna released her eighth studio album, *[Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_(Madonna_album) "Music (Madonna album)")*, to critical acclaim. She collaborated with French producer [Mirwais Ahmadzaï](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirwais_Ahmadza%C3%AF "Mirwais Ahmadzaï"), and expressed a preference for working with unconventional, lesser-known artists who "are making music unlike anyone else out there". The album reached number one in over twenty countries and sold four million copies within the first ten days of release. In the US, *Music* debuted atop the *Billboard* 200, becoming her first number-one album in eleven years since *Like a Prayer*. The singles "[Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_(Madonna_song) "Music (Madonna song)")" and "[Don't Tell Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don%27t_Tell_Me_(Madonna_song) "Don't Tell Me (Madonna song)")" peaked at numbers one and four on the *Billboard* Hot 100, respectively; the former became her twelfth song to peak atop the chart. Since its release, *Music* has sold over 11 million units worldwide.

Madonna met director [Guy Ritchie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Ritchie "Guy Ritchie") in mid-1998 at a dinner party hosted by mutual friends [Sting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sting_(musician) "Sting (musician)") and [Trudie Styler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trudie_Styler "Trudie Styler"). She gave birth to their son, Rocco John Ritchie, in Los Angeles on August 11, 2000. She and Rocco experienced complications during the birth due to her [placenta praevia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placenta_praevia "Placenta praevia"). He was christened at [Dornoch Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dornoch_Cathedral "Dornoch Cathedral") in Scotland on December 21, 2000, and Madonna married Ritchie the following day at nearby [Skibo Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skibo_Castle "Skibo Castle"). In June 2001, she launched the [Drowned World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drowned_World_Tour "Drowned World Tour"), her first tour in eight years. It visited cities across the US and Europe, becoming the highest-grossing tour of the year by a solo artist with $75 million in revenue. That same year, she released her second greatest-hits album, *[GHV2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GHV2 "GHV2")*, featuring a selection of her successful songs from the 1990s onward. It debuted at number seven in the US and sold seven million copies worldwide.

In 2002, Madonna starred in Ritchie's *[Swept Away](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swept_Away_(2002_film) "Swept Away (2002 film)")*, a remake of [Lina Wertmüller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lina_Wertm%C3%BCller "Lina Wertmüller")'s [1974 film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swept_Away_(1974_film) "Swept Away (1974 film)"). The film was commercially unsuccessful and universally panned, with [A. O. Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._O._Scott "A. O. Scott"), writing for *The New York Times*, stating that "a role like this one requires the surrender of emotional control, something Madonna seems constitutionally unable to achieve". In May 2002, she appeared in the [West End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_End_theatre "West End theatre") play *[Up for Grabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Up_for_Grabs_(play) "Up for Grabs (play)")* at the [Wyndhams Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyndhams_Theatre "Wyndhams Theatre"), which was poorly received by critics. Later that year, Madonna released "[Die Another Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Another_Day_(song) "Die Another Day (song)")", the theme song for the [James Bond film of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Another_Day "Die Another Day"), in which she also had a [cameo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance "Cameo appearance") role that [Peter Bradshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bradshaw "Peter Bradshaw") of *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* described as "incredibly wooden". The song reached number eight on the *Billboard* Hot 100, and was nominated for the [Golden Globe Award for Best Original Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Original_Song "Golden Globe Award for Best Original Song") and two Grammy Awards.

### 2003–2006: *American Life* and *Confessions on a Dance Floor*

In 2003, Madonna worked with fashion photographer [Steven Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Klein_(artist) "Steven Klein (artist)") on an exhibition installation titled *X-STaTIC Pro=CeSS*. The project was presented from March to May at New York's [Deitch Projects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deitch_Projects "Deitch Projects") gallery and was later shown internationally in a revised version.

Madonna reunited with Ahmadzaï to produce her ninth studio album, *[American Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Life "American Life")*—a reflection of her views on American society. The record, released on April 21, 2003, was met with mixed reception and debuted atop the US *Billboard* 200. *American Life* became her lowest-selling release, with worldwide sales of four million copies by 2005. Four singles were released from the album: "[American Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Life_(song) "American Life (song)")", "[Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_(Madonna_song) "Hollywood (Madonna song)")", "[Nothing Fails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nothing_Fails "Nothing Fails")", and "[Love Profusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Profusion "Love Profusion")". "American Life" was its only track to enter the *Billboard* Hot 100, peaking at number 37. The tracks fared better internationally; "American Life" topped the charts in Canada and Italy, while "Nothing Fails" and "Love Profusion" reached number one in Spain. "American Life" and "Hollywood" both reached number two in the UK.

The original music video of "American Life" caused controversy due to its violence and [anti-war movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-war_movement "Anti-war movement") imagery, and was withdrawn after the [2003 invasion of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_invasion_of_Iraq "2003 invasion of Iraq") began. Madonna voluntarily censored herself due to the political climate of the country. Her performance at the [2003 MTV Video Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_MTV_Video_Music_Awards "2003 MTV Video Music Awards")—in which she kissed singers [Britney Spears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britney_Spears "Britney Spears") and [Christina Aguilera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christina_Aguilera "Christina Aguilera") while performing "Hollywood"—was highly publicized. Shortly afterwards, Madonna collaborated with Spears on the single "[Me Against the Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Me_Against_the_Music "Me Against the Music")" which was released that September. She then released *[Remixed & Revisited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remixed_%26_Revisited "Remixed & Revisited")*, an extended play featuring new remixes of songs from *American Life* and the unreleased "Your Honesty". Madonna signed a contract with [Callaway Arts & Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callaway_Arts_%26_Entertainment "Callaway Arts & Entertainment") to write five children's books. The first, titled *[The English Roses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_English_Roses "The English Roses")*, was published in September 2003 and tells the story of four English schoolgirls struggling with envy and jealousy. The book became the fastest-selling children's picture book at the time, with all proceeds donated to a children's charity.

In March 2004, Madonna and Maverick filed a lawsuit against [Warner Music Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Music_Group "Warner Music Group") and its former parent company, Time Warner, alleging financial mismanagement and poor accounting practices that resulted in significant losses. Warner countersued, claiming Maverick had incurred substantial losses independently. The dispute was settled when Warner acquired the Maverick shares held by Madonna and Ronnie Dashev, making the company a wholly owned subsidiary of Warner Music. Madonna remained signed to Warner under a separate recording contract. She launched the [Re-Invention World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Re-Invention_World_Tour "Re-Invention World Tour") in May, performing across North America and Europe. The tour became 2004's highest-grossing, earning over $120 million, and was later chronicled in her documentary *[I'm Going to Tell You a Secret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%27m_Going_to_Tell_You_a_Secret "I'm Going to Tell You a Secret")* (2005). In November 2004, she was inducted into the [UK Music Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Music_Hall_of_Fame "UK Music Hall of Fame") as one of its five founding members.

In January 2005, Madonna performed [John Lennon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lennon "John Lennon")'s "[Imagine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imagine_(song) "Imagine (song)")" at [Tsunami Aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsunami_Aid "Tsunami Aid") and appeared at the [Live 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_8 "Live 8") benefit concert in London six months later. She initially worked with Ahmadzaï to produce her tenth studio album, *[Confessions on a Dance Floor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confessions_on_a_Dance_Floor "Confessions on a Dance Floor")*, but later turned to [Stuart Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Price "Stuart Price") to attain the sound she was seeking. Structured like a continuous [DJ mixed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJ_mix "DJ mix") club set, the album was released in the US on November 15, 2005, to critical acclaim. Keith Caulfield, writing in *Billboard*, heralded the album as a "welcome return to form for the Queen of Pop". *Confessions on a Dance Floor* topped the charts in a record-breaking 40 countries, won Madonna the [Grammy Award for Best Electronic/Dance Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Electronic/Dance_Album "Grammy Award for Best Electronic/Dance Album") at the [49th Annual Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/49th_Annual_Grammy_Awards "49th Annual Grammy Awards"), and sold over 10 million copies worldwide.

The lead single, "[Hung Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hung_Up "Hung Up")", reached number one in a record-breaking forty-one countries. It sampled [ABBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABBA "ABBA")'s "[Gimme! Gimme! Gimme! (A Man After Midnight)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimme!_Gimme!_Gimme!_(A_Man_After_Midnight) "Gimme! Gimme! Gimme! (A Man After Midnight)")", marking the second time the band permitted such use. ABBA songwriter [Björn Ulvaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B6rn_Ulvaeus "Björn Ulvaeus") called it "a wonderful track—100 per cent solid pop music". The album's second single, "[Sorry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorry_(Madonna_song) "Sorry (Madonna song)")", became Madonna's twelfth number-one single in the UK. Madonna launched the [Confessions Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confessions_Tour "Confessions Tour") in May 2006, which grossed more than $193.7 million, making it the [highest-grossing tour by a woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_concert_tours_by_women "List of highest-grossing concert tours by women") at the time. During the performance of "Live to Tell", she used religious imagery, including a crucifix and a [crown of thorns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_thorns "Crown of thorns"), prompting the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church "Russian Orthodox Church") and the [Federation of Jewish Communities of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Jewish_Communities_of_Russia "Federation of Jewish Communities of Russia") to call for a boycott.

While touring, Madonna founded the charitable organization [Raising Malawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raising_Malawi "Raising Malawi") and helped fund an orphanage in the country. There, she decided to adopt a boy named David Banda in 2006. The adoption provoked public debate, as Malawian law required prospective parents to reside in the country for one year before adopting, a condition Madonna did not meet. Malawi's Minister of Women and Child Development, [Kate Kainja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Kainja "Kate Kainja"), had also blocked officials from traveling to meet Madonna and Ritchie as potential adopters. Speaking on *[The Oprah Winfrey Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Oprah_Winfrey_Show "The Oprah Winfrey Show")*, Madonna explained that Malawi had no formal adoption laws for foreigners and described how Banda had been suffering from [pneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumonia "Pneumonia") after surviving [malaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaria "Malaria") and [tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculosis "Tuberculosis"). Banda's father, Yohane, defended the adoption, saying he understood and supported the arrangement. The adoption was finalized in May 2008.

### 2007–2011: Filmmaking, *Hard Candy*, and business ventures

In July 2007, Madonna released and performed "[Hey You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hey_You_(Madonna_song) "Hey You (Madonna song)")" at the London [Live Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_Earth_concert,_London "Live Earth concert, London") concert. She subsequently announced her departure from Warner Bros. Records and signed a ten-year, $120 million [360 deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/360_deal "360 deal") with [Live Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_Nation "Live Nation"). She produced, wrote, and narrated *[I Am Because We Are](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Am_Because_We_Are "I Am Because We Are")* (2008), a documentary directed by Nathan Rissman that addressed social issues in Malawi. That same year, she directed her first feature film, *[Filth and Wisdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filth_and_Wisdom "Filth and Wisdom")*, which follows three friends pursuing their ambitions. Reviews were largely negative, with *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times "The Times")* describing it as a commendable debut and *[The Daily Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph "The Daily Telegraph")* calling it a promising but imperfect first effort. On March 10, 2008, Madonna was inducted into the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame "Rock and Roll Hall of Fame"), becoming the second female solo artist to achieve it in her first year of eligibility, after [Janis Joplin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janis_Joplin "Janis Joplin") in 1995.

Madonna worked with [Justin Timberlake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Timberlake "Justin Timberlake"), [Timbaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbaland "Timbaland"), and [Pharrell Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharrell_Williams "Pharrell Williams") to produce her eleventh studio album, *[Hard Candy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_Candy_(Madonna_album) "Hard Candy (Madonna album)")*. Released on April 29, 2008, *Hard Candy* debuted atop the charts of 37 countries, including the US *Billboard* 200. The lead single, "[4 Minutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4_Minutes "4 Minutes")", peaked at number three on the *Billboard* Hot 100, becoming her thirty-seventh top-ten entry in the US and surpassing [Elvis Presley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_Presley "Elvis Presley")'s record for the most top-ten songs. In the UK, "4 Minutes" extended her record as the female artist with the most number-one singles. *Rolling Stone*'s Caryn Ganz described *Hard Candy* as "an impressive preview of her upcoming tour", while [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") correspondent Mark Savage criticized it as "an attempt to capture the urban market". She launched the [Sticky & Sweet Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sticky_%26_Sweet_Tour "Sticky & Sweet Tour") in August 2008, her first major venture with Live Nation. Grossing over $408 million, it became the second [highest-grossing tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_concert_tours "List of highest-grossing concert tours") in history and the highest-grossing tour by a solo artist.

In July 2008, Madonna's brother Christopher published the book *[Life with My Sister Madonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_with_My_Sister_Madonna "Life with My Sister Madonna")*, which created a rift between the two due to its unauthorized release. In October, Madonna filed for divorce from Ritchie, citing [irreconcilable differences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreconcilable_differences "Irreconcilable differences"). Two months later, her spokesperson confirmed that a settlement had been reached, granting Ritchie between [£50–60 million](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_expensive_divorces "List of most expensive divorces") ($68.49–82.19 million), including the couple's [London pub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Punch_Bowl_(Mayfair) "The Punch Bowl (Mayfair)"), residence, and [Wiltshire estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashcombe_House,_Wiltshire "Ashcombe House, Wiltshire"). The marriage was formally dissolved through a *[decree nisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decree_nisi "Decree nisi")* at the Principal Registry of the Family Division in [High Holborn, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Holborn "High Holborn"). Custody of their sons, Rocco and David, then aged eight and three, was shared between Ritchie's London home and Madonna's residence in New York, where they lived alongside Lourdes. In May 2009, Madonna applied to adopt Chifundo "Mercy" James from Malawi, but the country's High Court denied the request on the grounds that she was not a resident. She appealed the decision, and in June, the Supreme Court overturned the ruling and granted her permission to adopt her.

Madonna concluded her contract with Warner Records with the release of her third greatest-hits album, *[Celebration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebration_(Madonna_album) "Celebration (Madonna album)")*, in September 2009. The compilation included two new tracks, along with thirty-four songs spanning her career with the label. The album reached number one in multiple countries, including Canada, Germany, Italy, and the UK. She appeared at the [2009 MTV Video Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_MTV_Video_Music_Awards "2009 MTV Video Music Awards") to deliver a tribute to the deceased singer [Michael Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jackson "Michael Jackson"). By the end of the decade, Madonna was recognized as the most successful singles artist of the 2000s in the US and the most-played artist of the decade in the UK. *Billboard* ranked her as the third highest-grossing touring artist of the 2000s, with earnings exceeding $801 million, over 6.3 million attendees, and 244 sold-out performances out of 248 shows. In January 2010, Madonna performed at [Hope for Haiti Now: A Global Benefit for Earthquake Relief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hope_for_Haiti_Now:_A_Global_Benefit_for_Earthquake_Relief "Hope for Haiti Now: A Global Benefit for Earthquake Relief") concert.

Madonna's third live album, *[Sticky & Sweet Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sticky_%26_Sweet_Tour_(album) "Sticky & Sweet Tour (album)")*, was released in April 2010, debuting at number ten in the US. That year, she authorized the television series *[Glee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glee_(TV_series) "Glee (TV series)")* to feature her entire music catalog, leading to [an episode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Power_of_Madonna "The Power of Madonna") composed exclusively of her songs. She also collaborated with her daughter Lourdes to launch the [Material Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_fashion_brands "Madonna fashion brands") clothing line, inspired by her 1980s fashion. In October 2010, she founded the global fitness chain [Hard Candy Fitness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_Candy_Fitness "Hard Candy Fitness"), and in November 2011 introduced a second lifestyle brand, [Truth or Dare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truth_or_Dare_by_Madonna "Truth or Dare by Madonna"), offering footwear, fragrances, lingerie, and accessories. Her second directorial feature was *[W.E.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W.E. "W.E.")* (2011), a biographical drama about the relationship between [King Edward VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Edward_VIII "King Edward VIII") and [Wallis Simpson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallis_Simpson "Wallis Simpson"). The film received an unfavorable critical and commercial response. [Its soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W.E._(soundtrack) "W.E. (soundtrack)") included the [ballad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballad "Ballad") "[Masterpiece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masterpiece_(Madonna_song) "Masterpiece (Madonna song)")", which earned Madonna a Golden Globe Award for Best Original Song.

### 2012–2016: Super Bowl XLVI halftime show, *MDNA*, and *Rebel Heart*

In February 2012, Madonna headlined the [Super Bowl XLVI halftime show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XLVI_halftime_show "Super Bowl XLVI halftime show") at [Lucas Oil Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_Oil_Stadium "Lucas Oil Stadium") in [Indianapolis, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis,_Indiana "Indianapolis, Indiana"). Produced in collaboration with [Cirque du Soleil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cirque_du_Soleil "Cirque du Soleil") and choreographer [Jamie King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_King "Jamie King"), the performance featured guest appearances by [LMFAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMFAO "LMFAO"), [Nicki Minaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicki_Minaj "Nicki Minaj"), [M.I.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.I.A._(rapper) "M.I.A. (rapper)") and [CeeLo Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CeeLo_Green "CeeLo Green"). The broadcast drew 114 million viewers, making it the most-watched Super Bowl halftime show at the time, surpassing the viewership of the game itself. Madonna primarily produced her twelfth studio album, *[MDNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MDNA_(album) "MDNA (album)")*, with Orbit and [Martin Solveig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Solveig "Martin Solveig"). It was released on March 26, 2012, to generally favorable critical reception. *MDNA* was Madonna's first release under a three-album agreement with [Interscope Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interscope_Records "Interscope Records"), signed as part of her 360 deal with Live Nation, which could not distribute recorded music.

*MDNA* became her fifth consecutive studio album to debut at number one on the *Billboard* 200. The lead single, "[Give Me All Your Luvin'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Give_Me_All_Your_Luvin%27 "Give Me All Your Luvin'")", became her record-extending thirty-eighth top-ten single on the *Billboard* Hot 100. She promoted the album with [the MDNA Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_MDNA_Tour "The MDNA Tour"), which began in May 2012 and addressed themes such as violence, human rights, and politics. Grossing over $305 million from 88 sold-out shows, it was the tenth highest-grossing tour and made Madonna the first woman to pass $1 billion in touring revenue. Madonna collaborated with Steven Klein and directed a seventeen-minute film, *[secretprojectrevolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretprojectrevolution "Secretprojectrevolution")*, which was released on [BitTorrent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BitTorrent_(company) "BitTorrent (company)") in September 2013. With the film, she launched the Art for Freedom initiative, which promotes art and free speech to combat global persecution and injustice. The project's website garnered over 3,000 submissions by January 2014.

By 2013, Madonna's Raising Malawi had built ten schools educating 4,000 children in Malawi worth of $400,000. In May 2014, she donated to her [bankrupt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_bankruptcy "Detroit bankruptcy") hometown of Detroit, and that year launched her [MDNA Skin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MDNA_Skin "MDNA Skin") care line in Tokyo. Madonna released her thirteenth studio album, *[Rebel Heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebel_Heart "Rebel Heart")*, on March 10, 2015, three months after thirteen demos leaked online. She collaborated with a large range of producers, including [Avicii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avicii "Avicii"), [Diplo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplo "Diplo"), and [Kanye West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanye_West "Kanye West"). The central theme of the album was introspection along with "genuine statements of personal and careerist reflection". Music critics responded positively towards *Rebel Heart*, calling it Madonna's best effort in a decade.

Madonna began her [Rebel Heart Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebel_Heart_Tour "Rebel Heart Tour") in September 2015, which visited North America, Europe, Asia, and Australia, and grossed $169.8 million from 82 shows. While touring, Madonna became involved in a legal dispute with Ritchie regarding custody of their son Rocco. The conflict arose when Rocco chose to remain in England with Ritchie rather than continue traveling with Madonna. Court proceedings were held in both New York and London, but after several hearings, Madonna withdrew her custody application and opted to settle the matter privately. In October 2016, *Billboard* named her its [Woman of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Women_in_Music#Woman_of_the_Year_Award "Billboard Women in Music"). Her candid and outspoken speech addressing [ageism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ageism "Ageism") and [sexism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexism "Sexism") garnered extensive media attention.

In November 2016, she performed an impromptu acoustic concert at [Washington Square Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Square_Park "Washington Square Park") in support of [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton "Hillary Clinton")'s [2016 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton_2016_presidential_campaign "Hillary Clinton 2016 presidential campaign"). After [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump")'s election victory, Madonna delivered a speech at the [2017 Women's March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Women%27s_March "2017 Women's March") on Washington, where a remark about "blowing up the White House" drew controversy. She later clarified that her comments had been taken out of context and emphasized that she was not advocating violence.

### 2017–2021: Move to Lisbon and *Madame X*

In February 2017, Madonna adopted four-year-old twin sisters, Estere and Stella, from Malawi, and relocated to [Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon "Lisbon"), Portugal, later that year with her children. That July, she inaugurated the [Mercy James Institute for Pediatric Surgery and Intensive Care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercy_James_Institute_for_Pediatric_Surgery_and_Intensive_Care "Mercy James Institute for Pediatric Surgery and Intensive Care") in Malawi, a hospital established by her Raising Malawi foundation. Her live album [*Rebel Heart Tour*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebel_Heart_Tour_(album) "Rebel Heart Tour (album)") was released in September 2017 and won Best Music Video for Western Artists at the 32nd [Japan Gold Disc Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Gold_Disc_Awards "Japan Gold Disc Awards"). That same month, she launched her skincare brand MDNA Skin in selected US stores. Earlier in the year, the auction house Gotta Have Rock and Roll attempted to sell Madonna's personal items, including letters from Tupac Shakur. Although she sought legal action to halt the sale, the court ruled in favor of art dealer Darlene Lutz due to a 2004 agreement authorizing the sale of the items.

While residing in Lisbon, Madonna met Portuguese musician [Dino D'Santiago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dino_D%27Santiago "Dino D'Santiago"), who introduced her to local artists performing [fado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fado "Fado"), [morna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morna_(music) "Morna (music)"), and [samba music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samba_music "Samba music"). She frequently attended their informal "living room sessions", which inspired her fourteenth studio album, *[Madame X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madame_X_(album) "Madame X (album)")*. The album was released on June 14, 2019, and Madonna produced it with various musicians, primarily her longtime collaborator Mirwais and [Mike Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Dean_(music_producer) "Mike Dean (music producer)"). *Madame X* debuted at number one on the *Billboard* 200, her ninth to do so. Its four singles—"[Medellín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medell%C3%ADn_(song) "Medellín (song)")", "[Crave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crave_(Madonna_and_Swae_Lee_song) "Crave (Madonna and Swae Lee song)")", "[I Rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Rise_(song) "I Rise (song)")" and "[I Don't Search I Find](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Don%27t_Search_I_Find "I Don't Search I Find")"—each reached number one on the *Billboard* Dance Club Songs chart, further extending her record for the most chart-topping entries.

Madonna appeared as the interval act at the [Eurovision Song Contest 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2019 "Eurovision Song Contest 2019") and performed "Like a Prayer", and then "[Future](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_(Madonna_and_Quavo_song) "Future (Madonna and Quavo song)")" with rapper [Quavo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quavo "Quavo"). Her [Madame X Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madame_X_Tour "Madame X Tour"), an all-theater concert series held in select cities across North America and Europe, began in September 2019 and grossed over $51.4 million in revenue. That December, Madonna began a relationship with dancer Ahlamalik Williams, who had previously joined her Rebel Heart Tour in 2015. The tour later faced multiple cancellations due to Madonna's recurring knee injury and concluded prematurely on March 8, 2020, after the French government prohibited large gatherings because of the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"). Later that month, she drew criticism for posting a now-deleted video on [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram "Instagram") and [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter"); in it, she sat naked amid rose petals and candles, calling COVID-19 "the great equalizer" that makes everyone equal regardless of status.

In April 2020, Madonna announced financial contributions to the COVID-19 Therapeutics Accelerator, led by the [Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_%26_Melinda_Gates_Foundation "Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation"), the [Wellcome Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellcome_Trust "Wellcome Trust"), and [Mastercard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mastercard "Mastercard"), and donated an additional $1 million to vaccine research. A month later, Madonna revealed she had [tested positive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_testing "COVID-19 testing") for [coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severe_acute_respiratory_syndrome_coronavirus_2 "Severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2") antibodies. She and [Missy Elliott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missy_Elliott "Missy Elliott") contributed guest vocals to [Dua Lipa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dua_Lipa "Dua Lipa")'s single "[Levitating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levitating_(song) "Levitating (song)")", featured on Lipa's 2020 remix album *[Club Future Nostalgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Future_Nostalgia "Club Future Nostalgia")*. Madonna then began developing a biographical film about her life, which she planned to direct. Screenwriters [Erin Cressida Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erin_Cressida_Wilson "Erin Cressida Wilson") and [Diablo Cody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diablo_Cody "Diablo Cody") worked on the script at different stages, and actress [Julia Garner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Garner "Julia Garner") was cast in the lead role before the project was postponed, and ultimately scrapped over budgeting and casting disagreements with [Universal Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures "Universal Pictures"). In October 2021, Madonna released *[Madame X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madame_X_(2021_film) "Madame X (2021 film)")*, a [documentary film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Documentary_film "Documentary film") about her concert tour of the same name, through [Paramount+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount%2B "Paramount+").

### 2022–present: Remix albums, EP, and *Confessions II*

In August 2021, coinciding with her 63rd birthday, Madonna announced her return to [Warner Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Records "Warner Records")—the rebranded successor to Warner Bros. Records—in a global partnership. The deal granted the label rights to her entire recorded music catalog, including her three most recent albums originally released under Interscope. As part of the agreement, she began a series of catalog reissues in 2022 to mark the fortieth anniversary of her recording career. The remix album *[Finally Enough Love: 50 Number Ones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finally_Enough_Love:_50_Number_Ones "Finally Enough Love: 50 Number Ones")* was released in August, following a 16-track abridged edition made available for streaming two months prior. Featuring her 50 number-one songs on *Billboard*'s Dance Club Songs chart, the album celebrated Madonna's long-standing connection to dance music and became her 23rd top-ten entry on the *Billboard* 200.

Between 2022 and 2023, Madonna issued several standalone singles, including "[Back That Up to the Beat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_That_Up_to_the_Beat "Back That Up to the Beat")", a previously unreleased demo from the *Rebel Heart* sessions that went viral on social media. She also contributed to three tracks on [Christine and the Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christine_and_the_Queens "Christine and the Queens")' album *[Paranoïa, Angels, True Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parano%C3%AFa,_Angels,_True_Love "Paranoïa, Angels, True Love")* (2023), and collaborated with [the Weeknd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weeknd "The Weeknd") and [Playboi Carti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playboi_Carti "Playboi Carti") on the single "[Popular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_(The_Weeknd,_Playboi_Carti_and_Madonna_song) "Popular (The Weeknd, Playboi Carti and Madonna song)")", featured on the soundtrack to the television drama *[The Idol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Idol_(TV_series) "The Idol (TV series)")*. In January 2023, Madonna announced [the Celebration Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Celebration_Tour "The Celebration Tour"), her first greatest hits concert tour. The tour was to begin in July, but a month prior, she was hospitalized after being found unresponsive at her New York City residence. She spent five days in intensive care and was placed in a medically induced coma for 48 hours due to a serious bacterial infection following a low-grade fever.

Following her recovery, the Celebration Tour commenced in October 2023 in London, receiving widespread critical acclaim. It concluded the following May with a free concert on [Copacabana Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copacabana_Beach "Copacabana Beach") in [Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro "Rio de Janeiro"), which drew an audience of 1.6 million—setting records for both the [largest standalone concert in history and the highest attendance ever for a female artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most-attended_concerts "List of most-attended concerts") at the time. The tour grossed over $225 million across eighty shows, making Madonna the first woman to surpass $100 million in earnings from six separate concert tours. Around this time, Madonna experienced the deaths of several family members, beginning with that of her eldest brother, Anthony, in February 2023. Her younger brother, Christopher, died in October 2024, two weeks after that of their stepmother, Joan.

In 2025, Madonna released the remix album *[Veronica Electronica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veronica_Electronica "Veronica Electronica")* and the extended play *[Bedtime Stories: The Untold Chapter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedtime_Stories:_The_Untold_Chapter "Bedtime Stories: The Untold Chapter")*, both of which contain previously unreleased demos. In January 2026, she became the face of The One, a [Dolce & Gabbana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolce_%26_Gabbana "Dolce & Gabbana") fragrance. Madonna covered [Patty Pravo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patty_Pravo "Patty Pravo")'s 1968 single "[La bambola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_bambola#Madonna_version "La bambola")" as part of the campaign, which included a short film and photographs. A follow-up to *Confessions on a Dance Floor* (2005), the album was critically acclaimed and debuted atop the *Billboard* 200. By then, Madonna had joined [the Beatles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beatles "The Beatles") as the only acts to score ten number-one hits on the *Billboard* 200, the *Billboard* Hot 100, the UK Albums Chart, and the UK singles chart. ## Artistry

### Influences

When initially starting her career, Madonna's primary musical inspirations were [Debbie Harry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debbie_Harry "Debbie Harry") of [Blondie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blondie_(band) "Blondie (band)") and [David Bowie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bowie "David Bowie"), whose [Diamond Dogs Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond_Dogs_Tour "Diamond Dogs Tour") concert was the first performance she ever attended. Madonna has cited [Nancy Sinatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Sinatra "Nancy Sinatra") as one of her idols and said that her song "[These Boots Are Made for Walkin'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/These_Boots_Are_Made_for_Walkin%27 "These Boots Are Made for Walkin'")" (1965) left a strong impression on her. As a young woman, she sought to expand her artistry through literature, art, and music, developing a particular interest in [classical compositions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_music "Classical music"). She identified [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_music "Baroque music") as her preferred style and admired [Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart "Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart") and [Frédéric Chopin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Chopin "Frédéric Chopin"), praising the "feminine quality" in their work. Among Madonna's other principal influences are [Chrissie Hynde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrissie_Hynde "Chrissie Hynde"), [Aretha Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aretha_Franklin "Aretha Franklin"), [Patti Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patti_Smith "Patti Smith"), [Karen Carpenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Carpenter "Karen Carpenter"), [the Supremes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Supremes "The Supremes"), Joni Mitchell, and [Led Zeppelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Led_Zeppelin "Led Zeppelin"), as well as dancers [Rudolf Nureyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Nureyev "Rudolf Nureyev") and Martha Graham, the latter of which she considers one of her biggest mentors.

Madonna has also been inspired by the works of American writer [James Baldwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Baldwin "James Baldwin"). As a child, Madonna drew inspiration from Hollywood actresses such as [Carole Lombard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carole_Lombard "Carole Lombard"), [Judy Holliday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judy_Holliday "Judy Holliday"), and [Marilyn Monroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marilyn_Monroe "Marilyn Monroe"), admiring their blend of femininity, strength, and sex appeal, and remarking that she "saw [her]self in them". Her music video for "Material Girl" recreated Monroe's performance of "[Diamonds Are a Girl's Best Friend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamonds_Are_a_Girl%27s_Best_Friend "Diamonds Are a Girl's Best Friend")" from the film *[Gentlemen Prefer Blondes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentlemen_Prefer_Blondes_(1953_film) "Gentlemen Prefer Blondes (1953 film)")* (1953). In preparation for her role in *Who's That Girl*, Madonna studied the 1930s [screwball comedies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screwball_comedies "Screwball comedies") of Lombard, whose style she sought to emulate. The visual concept for her "Express Yourself" (1989) video drew influence from [Fritz Lang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Lang "Fritz Lang")'s silent film *[Metropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis_(1927_film) "Metropolis (1927 film)")* (1927), while "Vogue" paid homage to the [golden age of Hollywood glamor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_glamour "Hollywood glamour"), referencing the photography of [Horst P. Horst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horst_P._Horst "Horst P. Horst") and channeling the screen presence of [Marlene Dietrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlene_Dietrich "Marlene Dietrich"), Lombard, and [Rita Hayworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rita_Hayworth "Rita Hayworth").

> Historians, musicians, and anthropologists trace her influences—from African American gospel music to Japanese fashion, Middle Eastern spirituality to feminist art history—and the ways she borrows, adapts, and interprets them.

— [National Geographic Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic_Society "National Geographic Society") on Madonna's influences.

Madonna also drew inspiration from the [visual arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_arts "Visual arts"), particularly Mexican painter [Frida Kahlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frida_Kahlo "Frida Kahlo"). The music video for "[Bedtime Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedtime_Story_(Madonna_song) "Bedtime Story (Madonna song)")" (1995) incorporated imagery influenced by the works of Kahlo and [Remedios Varo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remedios_Varo "Remedios Varo"). A collector of [Tamara de Lempicka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamara_de_Lempicka "Tamara de Lempicka")'s [Art Deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Deco "Art Deco") paintings, Madonna has featured them throughout her music videos and stage productions. Her video for "Hollywood" (2003) paid tribute to the photography of [Guy Bourdin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Bourdin "Guy Bourdin"), though it later prompted a lawsuit filed by Bourdin's son over the unauthorized use of his father's images. The sadomasochistic motifs in [pop artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_artist "Pop artist") [Andy Warhol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Warhol "Andy Warhol")'s [underground films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_film "Underground film") were echoed in the videos for "Erotica" and "Deeper and Deeper".

Madonna's Catholic upbringing has remained a consistent influence throughout her career, from her use of the [rosary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosary "Rosary") as a fashion piece to her musical inputs, including albums such as *Like a Prayer* (1989). In 2011, she attended meetings and services at an [Opus Dei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opus_Dei "Opus Dei") center, a Catholic institution that promotes sanctity through daily life. In a 2015 interview with *Rolling Stone*, Madonna remarked that she has always felt an "inexplicable connection" to Catholicism and stated that it consistently manifests throughout her work.

### Musical style and composition

> I was in Barney's (store) getting a tie for the Grammys and I saw Madonna there. I introduced myself, and I told her that she didn't get the credit she deserved as a songwriter. Which is true, but it's also an awkward thing to tell a huge superstar! She was very cool and gracious. I ended up getting a meeting with her in L.A. a few weeks later.

— [Rick Nowels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Nowels "Rick Nowels"), on co-writing with Madonna.

Madonna's work has been the subject of critical analysis and debate. [Robert M. Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_M._Grant_(economist) "Robert M. Grant (economist)"), author of *Contemporary Strategy Analysis* (2005), observed that her musical career is a continual process of experimentation with new sounds and images. In *Pop Goes the Decade: The Eighties*, Thomas Harrison described her as "an artist who pushed the boundaries" of what a female performer could achieve, both visually and lyrically. Professor Santiago Fouz-Hernández observed that, while not possessing a particularly powerful or wide-ranging voice, Madonna expanded her artistic range through diverse musical, lyrical, and visual styles, "all with the intention of presenting herself as a mature musician".

*Rolling Stone* included Madonna at number 36 on its list of the "[100 Greatest Artists of All Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone%27s_100_Greatest_Artists_of_All_Time "Rolling Stone's 100 Greatest Artists of All Time")" (2004). She has managed all aspects of her career; she writes and produces most of her recordings. Her desire for control in creative decisions was evident as early as her debut album, during which she reportedly disagreed with producer Reggie Lucas over the final mixes. Warner Bros. Records later allowed her to produce her third studio album independently. According to Stan Hawkins, author of *Settling the Pop Score*, Madonna is among the few female artists to have entered the male-dominated space of music production, saying that she is aware of the historical exclusion of women and actively "has set out to change this". Producer Stuart Price similarly remarked that "you don't produce Madonna, you collaborate with her [...] she has her vision and knows how to get it". Although referred to by the media and workers as a "control freak", Madonna has stated that she values creative input from those she works with. She further explained:

> I like to have control over most of the things in my career but I'm not a [tyrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrant "Tyrant"). I don't have to have it on my album that it's written, arranged, produced, directed, and stars Madonna. To me, to have total control means you can lose objectivity. What I like is to be surrounded by really talented, intelligent people that you can trust. And ask them for their advice and get their input.

Madonna developed her early songwriting skills while performing with the Breakfast Club in 1979, and was the sole writer of five tracks on her debut studio album. As a songwriter, Madonna has registered more than 300 works with the [American Society of Composers, Authors and Publishers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_of_Composers,_Authors_and_Publishers "American Society of Composers, Authors and Publishers"), including 18 written entirely by herself. *Rolling Stone* deemed her "an exemplary songwriter with a gift for [hooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hook_(music) "Hook (music)") and indelible lyrics", and said that her compositions, though shaped by collaborations with producers across various genres, are consistently shaped by "her own sensibility and inflected with autobiographical detail". Patrick Leonard, who co-wrote many of her songs, lauded her grasp of [melodic structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melody_type "Melody type") and said that "many times she's singing notes that no one would've thought of but her". [*Spin*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_(magazine) "Spin (magazine)")'s Barry Walters similarly credited her songwriting for the consistency of her music. Madonna was nominated for [induction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Songwriters_Hall_of_Fame_inductees "List of Songwriters Hall of Fame inductees") into the [Songwriters Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songwriters_Hall_of_Fame "Songwriters Hall of Fame") in 2014, 2016, and 2017, and was ranked number 56 on *Rolling Stone*'s list of the "[100 Greatest Songwriters of All Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone%27s_100_Greatest_Songwriters_of_All_Time "Rolling Stone's 100 Greatest Songwriters of All Time")" (2015).

Madonna wrote all the lyrics and partial melodies of "Live to Tell", an [adult contemporary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_contemporary "Adult contemporary") ballad, which was noted as her first musical reinvention.

Madonna's discography is often categorized as pop, [electronica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronica "Electronica"), and dance. Her earliest work began with rock-oriented projects through the bands Breakfast Club and Emmy. With the latter, she recorded tracks that reflected the [punk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock "Punk rock") style of the early 1980s. She abandoned rock before signing with Gotham Records, which dropped her because of their dissatisfaction with her shift to [funk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funk "Funk"). In his book *American Pop*, [Arie Kaplan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arie_Kaplan "Arie Kaplan") referred to Madonna as "a pioneer" of [dance-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance-pop "Dance-pop"). Fouz-Hernández further observed that Madonna's consistent use of dance idioms and her connection with gay and sexually liberated audiences have often been undervalued in contrast to the perceived authenticity of [rock and roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_roll "Rock and roll"), noting that her music "refuses to be defined by narrow boundaries of gender, sexuality, or genre".

The "cold and emotional" ballad "Live to Tell" and its parent album *True Blue* (1986) are regarded as Madonna's first significant musical reinvention. *[PopMatters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PopMatters "PopMatters")* critic Peter Piatkowski described the song as a "deliberate effort to present Madonna as a mature and serious artist". Although she continued to record ballads alongside her more upbeat material, some of her albums, such as *Madonna* (1983) and *Confessions on a Dance Floor* (2005), are composed entirely of dance tracks. With *Ray of Light* (1998), critics credited her with popularizing electronica and integrating it into mainstream pop music. Her other stylistic departures include 1930s [big-band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big-band "Big-band") [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz") on *I'm Breathless* (1990); smooth [R&B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_R%26B "Contemporary R&B") arrangements on *Bedtime Stories* (1994); [operatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera "Opera") [show tunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Show_tunes "Show tunes") on *Evita* (1996); [guitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitar "Guitar")-based [folk music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") on *American Life* (2003); and multilingual influences on *Madame X* (2019).

### Voice and instruments

Madonna possesses a [mezzo-soprano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mezzo-soprano "Mezzo-soprano") [vocal range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocal_range "Vocal range") and is self-conscious about her singing voice. [Mark Bego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Bego "Mark Bego"), author of *Madonna: Blonde Ambition*, described her as "the perfect vocalist for lighter-than-air songs" despite not being a "heavyweight talent". According to Tony Sclafani of [MSNBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSNBC "MSNBC"), her vocal delivery reflects her "rock roots", explaining that, unlike most pop singers who perform songs "straight", Madonna incorporates subtext, [irony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irony "Irony"), aggression, and other vocal idiosyncrasies similar to that of John Lennon and [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan "Bob Dylan"). In her early recordings, Madonna employed a bright, girlish timbre that she later tried to abandon after critics compared her voice to "[Minnie Mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnie_Mouse "Minnie Mouse") on [helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium "Helium")". While filming *Evita* (1996), she took voice training, which expanded her vocal versatility. Of the experience, she said, "I studied with a vocal coach for *Evita* and realized there was a whole piece of my voice I wasn't using. Before, I just believed I had a really limited range and was going to make the most of it."

Madonna plays several musical instruments. She first learned to play the piano as a child. During the late 1970s, she was taught drums and guitar by her then-boyfriend [Dan Gilroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Gilroy_(musician) "Dan Gilroy (musician)"), later joining his band, the Breakfast Club, as a drummer. Madonna then played guitar with the group Emmy. After her commercial breakthrough, she rarely performed instruments publicly until 2000, though she was credited with playing the [cowbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowbell_(instrument) "Cowbell (instrument)") on *Madonna* (1983) and the [synthesizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthesizer "Synthesizer") on *Like a Prayer* (1989). In 1999, she undertook three months of [violin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violin "Violin") training to prepare for a role as a violin teacher in the film *[Music of the Heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_the_Heart "Music of the Heart")* (1999), but withdrew from the project before filming. She returned to performing with the guitar during promotion for *Music* (2000) with guidance from guitarist [Monte Pittman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Pittman "Monte Pittman") to improve her technique. Since then, Madonna has featured guitar performances in her studio recordings and concert tours. She was nominated for the Les Paul Horizon Award at the 2002 [Orville H. Gibson Guitar Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibson_Guitar_Corporation "Gibson Guitar Corporation").

### Music videos and performances

In *The Madonna Companion*, biographers Allen Metz and Carol Benson observed that Madonna used MTV and the music video to leverage her popularity and enhance her recordings more effectively than any other contemporary pop artist. They noticed that many of her songs are closely tied to the imagery of their accompanying videos. Cultural critic [Mark C. Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_C._Taylor_(philosopher) "Mark C. Taylor (philosopher)") described the [postmodern art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmodern_art "Postmodern art") form [par excellence](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/par_excellence "wikt:par excellence") as the music video, declaring Madonna its "reigning queen" in his book *Nots* (1993). He argued that "the most remarkable creation of MTV is Madonna", and that public reactions to her provocative videos were "predictably contradictory". Metz and Benson said that critical and public discussions surrounding some of her most controversial songs have often focused more on their videos and cultural impact than on the songs themselves. According to Morton, "artistically, Madonna's songwriting is often overshadowed by her striking pop videos". Madonna was ranked as the greatest music video artist by MTV in 2003 and *Billboard* in 2020.

Madonna's early music videos fused American and Hispanic [street style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_style "Street style") with flamboyant glamor. Through the visuals, she introduced her avant-garde [Downtown Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Manhattan "Downtown Manhattan") fashion sensibility to an American audience. The recurring use of Hispanic cultural motifs and Catholic symbolism continued into the *True Blue* era. According to author [Douglas Kellner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Kellner "Douglas Kellner"), Madonna's embrace of "multiculturalism" and her "culturally transgressive moves" proved to be effective in appealing to a wide range of young audiences. Scholars have noted that through her visual narratives, Madonna often subverted traditional gender norms by symbolically reversing male dominance. Her use of religious and racial imagery was prominent in the music video for "Like a Prayer", which depicted scenes of an African-American church choir, Madonna's attraction to a black saint statue, and her performance before burning crosses.

Madonna's acting has often been met with negative reviews from film critics. Biographer [Andrew Morton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Morton_(writer) "Andrew Morton (writer)") noted that Madonna publicly dismissed the criticism. Following the critical and commercial failure of *Swept Away* (2002), she vowed never to act in another film. In a retrospective of her screen career titled *Body of Work* (2016) at New York's [Metrograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrograph "Metrograph"), *The Guardian*'s Nigel M. Smith argued that Madonna's struggles in film were largely due to the lack of quality roles available to her, stating that she could "steal a scene for all the right reasons". Her concert tours often re-create her music videos; author Elin Diamond said that the ability to reproduce scenes from Madonna's videos in a live setting enhances the realism of the videos, arguing that "her live performances have become the means by which mediatized representations are naturalized".

According to Taraborrelli, Madonna's concerts, which feature multimedia elements, advanced technology, and elaborate sound design, function as "extravagant show piece[s]" and "walking art show[s]". Chris Nelson of *The New York Times* observed that artists like Madonna redefined standards of live performance with concerts "that included not only elaborate costumes and precision-timed [pyrotechnics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrotechnics "Pyrotechnics") but also highly athletic dancing"—often at the expense of live vocals. A writer for the *[Orlando Sentinel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Sentinel "Orlando Sentinel")* said that she later restructured her stage shows to balance choreography and live singing by remaining stationary during vocally demanding sections while delegating more complex dance sequences to her backup performers. To support simultaneous singing and movement, Madonna began using a hands-free radio-frequency [headset microphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headset_(audio) "Headset (audio)"), secured over the head or ears with a boom extending to the mouth.

## Legacy

> She's a major [historical figure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_figure "Historical figure") and when she passes, the retrospectives will loom larger and larger in history.

— Academic [Camille Paglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Paglia "Camille Paglia") on Madonna (2017).

Madonna's profound influence on [popular music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_music "Popular music") has led critics and scholars to refer to her as the "[Queen of Pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorific_nicknames_in_popular_music "Honorific nicknames in popular music")". Her legacy transcends music and has been examined by sociologists, historians, media scholars, and cultural critics, contributing to the development of [Madonna studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_studies "Madonna studies"), a subfield of American [cultural studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_studies "Cultural studies"). *Rolling Stone* noted that Madonna's omnipresence across television, radio, magazines, and bookstores was an unparalleled pop dynamic not seen since [the Beatles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beatles "The Beatles"). *The Daily Telegraph* remarked that Madonna "changed the world's social history", with scholar Diane Pecknold noticing that "nearly every poll of the greatest or most influential figures in popular culture includes Madonna". Madonna has frequently appeared in rankings of the greatest and most influential musicians.

The [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame "Rock and Roll Hall of Fame") wrote that Madonna helped dissolve gender boundaries. *Spin* wrote in 2020 that the title "Queen of Pop" scarcely captures her impact, stating that "she is Pop" and that she established "the blueprint for what a pop star should be". Sclafani argued that before Madonna, most major music stars were male rock artists, and that her influence helped usher in an era where female solo performers became far more prominent in mainstream popular music. Many female artists in the 21st century—including Britney Spears, [Beyoncé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyonc%C3%A9 "Beyoncé"), [Rihanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rihanna "Rihanna"), [Katy Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katy_Perry "Katy Perry"), and [Lady Gaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Gaga "Lady Gaga")—have cited Madonna as an influence on their careers. Madonna has also influenced male artists, inspiring rock frontmen [Liam Gallagher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liam_Gallagher "Liam Gallagher") of [Oasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oasis_(band) "Oasis (band)") and [Chester Bennington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chester_Bennington "Chester Bennington") of [Linkin Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linkin_Park "Linkin Park") to become musicians.

[Madonna's use of sexual imagery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_and_sexuality "Madonna and sexuality") has ignited widespread discussions regarding sexuality and [feminism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminism "Feminism"). Media scholar [John Fiske](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Fiske_(media_scholar) "John Fiske (media scholar)") remarked that the empowerment Madonna conveys is closely tied to the gratification of asserting control over self-definition, sexuality, and social dynamics. As discussed in *Doing Gender in Media, Art and Culture* (2009), Madonna's status as a female celebrity, performer, and [pop icon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_icon "Pop icon") continues to challenge and reinterpret established feminist discourses. According to lesbian feminist [Sheila Jeffreys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheila_Jeffreys "Sheila Jeffreys"), Madonna embodies what [Monique Wittig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monique_Wittig "Monique Wittig") terms "the category of sex" as a site of power, while deliberately embracing the performance of women's assigned sexual labor. Similarly, communication scholar Sut Jhally has described her as "an almost sacred feminist icon".

Writers and scholars have also highlighted Madonna's engagement with [LGBTQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_culture "LGBTQ culture"), [Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Latin_America "Culture of Latin America"), and [Black culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African-American_culture "African-American culture"). [Matt Cain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Cain_(writer) "Matt Cain (writer)") argued that her work helped bring [marginalized groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marginalized_groups "Marginalized groups") and cultures into mainstream popular culture. One author remarked that "by making culture generally available, Madonna becomes the culture of all social classes". Canadian scholar [Karlene Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlene_Faith "Karlene Faith") similarly said that Madonna's peculiarity lies in her ability to "move freely across diverse cultural terrains", describing her as a "[cult figure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult_figure "Cult figure")" within multiple self-sustaining subcultures even as she achieved mainstream success. She is often regarded as a [gay icon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_as_a_gay_icon "Madonna as a gay icon"), with [GLAAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GLAAD "GLAAD") president [Sarah Kate Ellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Kate_Ellis "Sarah Kate Ellis") affirming that Madonna "always has and always will be the LGBTQ community's greatest [ally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight_ally "Straight ally")", and *[The Advocate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Advocate_(magazine) "The Advocate (magazine)")* characterizing her as "the greatest gay icon".

Madonna has also been studied as a [business figure and entrepreneur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_of_Madonna "Business of Madonna"); she amassed over $1.2 billion in sales during the first decade of her career. According to Gini Gorlinski in *The 100 Most Influential Musicians of All Time* (2010), Madonna's authority and autonomy within the entertainment industry were unprecedented for a woman at the time. Scholars at the [London Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Business_School "London Business School") described her as a "dynamic entrepreneur" whose success model is worth emulating, identifying her vision of success, understanding of the music industry, ability to recognize her own performance limits, work ethic, and adaptability as the basis for her commercial achievements. Morton called Madonna "opportunistic, manipulative, and ruthless—someone who will not stop until she achieves her goals".

Madonna has been criticized from social, moral and political perspectives. Scholars have challenged her use of racialized and minority cultures and argued that her privileged position does little to aid the communities whose imagery she adopts. Madonna has been linked to terms like "Madonna-economy" and "Madonnization", shorthand for [homogenizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_homogenization "Cultural homogenization") American mass culture similar to that of [McDonaldization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonaldization "McDonaldization") and [Cocacolonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocacolonization "Cocacolonization"). Internationally, political and religious commentators have sometimes cast her as a symbol of Western [cultural hegemony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_hegemony "Cultural hegemony") or "cultural terrorism", associating her with [Americanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americanization "Americanization") and the erosion of local traditions. Conservative groups, parental organizations and some philosophers have further criticized her as emblematic of mass culture's corrupting influence on youth, fueling [moral panics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_panic "Moral panic") over sexuality, religion and the commercialization of popular music.

## Achievements

*[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes "Forbes")* estimated Madonna's [net worth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_worth "Net worth") at $850 million as of 2025, making her one of the [wealthiest musicians in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_music_artists_by_net_worth "List of music artists by net worth"). She became *Forbes*'s annual [highest-paid female musician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_list_of_the_world%27s_highest-paid_musicians#Female "Forbes list of the world's highest-paid musicians") 11 times across the 1980s, 1990s, 2000s, and 2010s. Madonna is the [best-selling female music artist of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_best-selling_music_artists "List of best-selling music artists"), and has eighteen albums certified [multi-platinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-platinum "Multi-platinum") in multiple countries. According to the [Recording Industry Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America "Recording Industry Association of America") (RIAA), she is the best-selling female rock artist of the 20th century and the fourth [highest-certified female artist in the US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-certified_music_artists_in_the_United_States "List of highest-certified music artists in the United States"), with 65.5 million certified [album-equivalent units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Album-equivalent_units "Album-equivalent units").

Madonna has generated over US$1.6 billion from ticket sales of her concert tours throughout her career. According to *Billboard* Boxscore, she had become the [highest-grossing female touring artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_live_music_artists "List of highest-grossing live music artists") for 19 years (2004–2023), until being overtaken by [Taylor Swift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift "Taylor Swift"). Madonna has won seven [Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award "Grammy Award") and twenty-seven [MTV Video Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Award "MTV Video Music Award"), including the 1986 [Video Vanguard Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_Vanguard_Award "Video Vanguard Award") for which she became the first female recipient. In Japan, the world's second largest market, Madonna has received seventeen [Gold Disc Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Gold_Disc_Award "Japan Gold Disc Award") from the [Recording Industry Association of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_Japan "Recording Industry Association of Japan"), including the most [Artist of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Gold_Disc_Awards#List_of_the_Artists_of_the_Year "Japan Gold Disc Awards") wins by a solo artist (five). Madonna became the first artist to be named the "[Greatest Pop Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard%27s_Greatest_Pop_Stars "Billboard's Greatest Pop Stars")" of the year twice by *Billboard* (1985 and 1989). *Like a Prayer* (1989), *The Immaculate Collection* (1990), *Ray of Light* (1998), and *Music* (2000) have each been included among *Rolling Stone*'s list titled "[500 Greatest Albums of All Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone%27s_500_Greatest_Albums_of_All_Time "Rolling Stone's 500 Greatest Albums of All Time")".

Throughout her career, Madonna has had twelve songs top the charts in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artists_with_the_most_number-ones_on_the_Billboard_Hot_100_chart "Artists with the most number-ones on the Billboard Hot 100 chart"), thirteen in the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_artists_with_the_most_UK_singles_chart_number_ones "List of artists with the most UK singles chart number ones"), twenty-four in [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_artists_by_number_of_Canadian_number-one_singles_(RPM) "List of artists by number of Canadian number-one singles (RPM)"), and eleven in [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_artists_who_reached_number_one_on_the_Australian_singles_chart "List of artists who reached number one on the Australian singles chart"). At the 40th anniversary of the [GfK Media Control Charts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GfK_Entertainment "GfK Entertainment"), Madonna was ranked as the most successful singles artist in German chart history. According to *[Billboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_(magazine) "Billboard (magazine)")*, she was the most successful solo artist on the *Billboard* Hot 100 by 2021—second overall behind the Beatles—and remains the most successful dance club artist in history. She has achieved 38 top-ten singles on the *Billboard* Hot 100, the most by any artist in pre-[streaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_streaming "Music streaming") era. A dominant [physical singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_single "Physical single") seller, she has the most chart-topping hits on the [Hot 100 Singles Sales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_100_Singles_Sales "Hot 100 Singles Sales") (16) and the [Dance Singles Sales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance_Singles_Sales "Dance Singles Sales") (33) of any artist. With 50 [Dance Club Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance_Club_Songs "Dance Club Songs") chart-toppers, Madonna became the artist with the most number ones on any single [*Billboard* chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_charts "Billboard charts").
