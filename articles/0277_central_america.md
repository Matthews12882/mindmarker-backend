# Central America

**Central America** is a [subregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subregion#North_America "Subregion") of [North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_America "North America"). Its political boundaries are defined as bordering [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico "Mexico") to the north, [Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia "Colombia") to the southeast, the [Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean "Caribbean") to the east, and the [Pacific Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean "Pacific Ocean") to the southwest. Central America is usually defined as consisting of seven countries: [Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize "Belize"), [Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rica "Costa Rica"), [El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Salvador "El Salvador"), [Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemala "Guatemala"), [Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduras "Honduras"), [Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaragua "Nicaragua"), and [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama "Panama"), and also, in specialized contexts, including Mexico. Within Central America is the [Mesoamerican biodiversity hotspot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica#Biodiversity "Mesoamerica"), which extends from southern Mexico to southeastern Panama. Due to the presence of several [active geologic faults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_fault "Active fault") and the [Central America Volcanic Arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_America_Volcanic_Arc "Central America Volcanic Arc"), seismic activity in the region, such as [volcanic eruptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_eruption "Volcanic eruption") and earthquakes, is high, which has caused death, injury, and property damage.

Central America includes portions of two recognized cultural areas: the [Isthmo-Colombian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmo-Colombian_Area "Isthmo-Colombian Area") cultural area, which encompasses modern Panama, Costa Rica, and the eastern portions of Nicaragua and Honduras, along with regions to the east; and the [Mesoamerican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica "Mesoamerica") cultural area, encompassing Guatemala, Belize, El Salvador, and the western portions of Honduras and Nicaragua, along with the southern part of Mexico. Before [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus "Christopher Columbus")'s voyages to the [Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americas "Americas"), hundreds of indigenous groups lived in these areas. From 1502 onward, Spain began its [colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_colonization_of_the_Americas "Spanish colonization of the Americas"). From 1609 to 1821, most Central American territories (except for what became Belize and Panama and including the modern Mexican state of [Chiapas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiapas "Chiapas")) were governed by the viceroyalty of [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain "New Spain") from [Mexico City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico_City "Mexico City") as the [Captaincy General of Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captaincy_General_of_Guatemala "Captaincy General of Guatemala"). On 24 August 1821, Spanish Viceroy [Juan de O'Donojú](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_O%27Donoj%C3%BA "Juan O'Donojú") signed the [Treaty of Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_C%C3%B3rdoba "Treaty of Córdoba"), which established New Spain's independence and autonomy from mainland Spain. On 15 September, the [Act of Independence of Central America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Independence_of_Central_America "Act of Independence of Central America") was enacted to announce Central America's separation from the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire "Spanish Empire"). Some of New Spain's provinces in the Central American region were invaded and annexed to the [First Mexican Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Mexican_Empire "First Mexican Empire"), but in 1823 they seceded from Mexico to form the [Federal Republic of Central America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Central_America "Federal Republic of Central America").

In 1838, Costa Rica, Guatemala, Honduras, and Nicaragua became the first of Central America's states to become independent countries, followed by El Salvador in 1841, Panama in 1903, and Belize in 1981. Despite the dissolution of the [Federal Republic of Central America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Central_America "Federal Republic of Central America"), the five remaining countries, save for Panama and Belize, all preserved and maintained a Central American identity.

The [Spanish-speaking countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_territories_where_Spanish_is_an_official_language "List of countries and territories where Spanish is an official language") officially include both North America and [South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_America "South America") as a single continent, *[América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americas "Americas")*, which is split into four subregions: Central America, the [Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean "Caribbean"), North America ([Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico "Mexico") and [Northern America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_America "Northern America")), and South America.

## Definitions

"Central America" means different things, depending on the context:

* The [United Nations geoscheme for the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_geoscheme_for_the_Americas "United Nations geoscheme for the Americas") defines *Central America* as all states of mainland North America south of the United States, including Mexico, but historically and politically Mexico is considered North American.
* [Middle America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_America_(Americas) "Middle America (Americas)") is usually thought to comprise Mexico in the north and the seven states of *Central America* in the south, as well as the [Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean "Caribbean") in the east. [Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia "Colombia") and [Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuela "Venezuela") of [Caribbean South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_South_America "Caribbean South America") are sometimes included in this subregion. The [Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean "Caribbean") is occasionally excluded from this subregion while [The Guianas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guianas "The Guianas") are infrequently included. According to one source, the term "Central America" was used as a synonym for "[Middle America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_America_(Americas) "Middle America (Americas)")" at least as recently as 1962.
* In [Ibero-America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibero-America "Ibero-America") (Portuguese- and Spanish-speaking American countries), the [Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americas "Americas") are considered a single continent (América), and Central America is considered a [subregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subregion "Subregion") of the [continent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americas "Americas") comprising the seven countries south of Mexico and north of Colombia.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]
* For the people in Costa Rica, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, and Nicaragua, formerly parts of the [Federal Republic of Central America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Central_America "Federal Republic of Central America"), there is a distinction between the Spanish language terms "América Central" and "Centroamérica". Both can be translated into English as "Central America", but "América Central" generally refers to the geographical area of the seven countries between Mexico and Colombia while "Centroamérica" refers to the former members of the Federation, emphasizing the shared culture and history of the region.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]
* In [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language "Portuguese language") as a rule and occasionally in Spanish and other languages, the entirety of the [Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antilles "Antilles") is often included in the definition of Central America. Indeed, the [Dominican Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Republic "Dominican Republic") is a full member of the [Central American Integration System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_Integration_System "Central American Integration System").[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

## History

Central America was formed more than 3 million years ago, as part of the [Isthmus of Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmus_of_Panama "Isthmus of Panama"), when its portion of land connected each side of water.

In the [Pre-Columbian era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_era "Pre-Columbian era"), the northern areas of Central America were inhabited by the [indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas "Indigenous peoples of the Americas") peoples of [Mesoamerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica "Mesoamerica"). Most notable among these were the [Mayans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_peoples "Maya peoples"), who had built numerous cities throughout the region, and the [Aztecs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec "Aztec"), who had created a vast empire. The pre-Columbian cultures of eastern Honduras, Caribbean Nicaragua, most of Costa Rica and Panama were predominantly speakers of the [Chibchan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibchan_languages "Chibchan languages") at the time of European contact and are considered by some culturally different and grouped in the [Isthmo-Colombian Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmo-Colombian_Area "Isthmo-Colombian Area").

Following the Spanish expedition of [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus "Christopher Columbus")'s voyages to the Americas, the Spanish sent many expeditions to the region, and they began their conquest of [Maya territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_civilization "Maya civilization") in 1523. Soon after the [conquest of the Aztec Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_conquest_of_the_Aztec_Empire "Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire"), Spanish [conquistador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquistador "Conquistador") [Pedro de Alvarado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_de_Alvarado "Pedro de Alvarado") commenced the [conquest of northern Central America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_conquest_of_Guatemala "Spanish conquest of Guatemala") for the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire "Spanish Empire"). Beginning with his arrival in [Soconusco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soconusco "Soconusco") in 1523, Alvarado's forces systematically conquered and subjugated most of the major Maya kingdoms, including the [K'iche'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%27iche%27_Kingdom_of_Q%27umarkaj "K'iche' Kingdom of Q'umarkaj"), [Tz'utujil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tz%27utujil_people "Tz'utujil people"), [Pipil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipil_people "Pipil people"), and the [Kaqchikel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaqchikel_people "Kaqchikel people"). By 1528, the conquest of Guatemala was nearly complete, with only the [Petén Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet%C3%A9n_Basin "Petén Basin") remaining outside the Spanish sphere of influence. The last independent Maya kingdoms – the [Kowoj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowoj "Kowoj") and the [Itza people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itza_people "Itza people") – were finally defeated in 1697, as part of the [Spanish conquest of Petén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_conquest_of_Pet%C3%A9n "Spanish conquest of Petén").

In 1538, Spain established the [Real Audiencia of Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Audiencia_of_Panama#First_installation "Real Audiencia of Panama"), which had jurisdiction over all land from the [Strait of Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Magellan "Strait of Magellan") to the [Gulf of Fonseca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Fonseca "Gulf of Fonseca"). This entity was dissolved in 1543, and most of the territory within Central America then fell under the jurisdiction of the *[Audiencia Real de Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Audiencia_of_Guatemala "Real Audiencia of Guatemala")*. This area included the current territories of Costa Rica, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Nicaragua, and the Mexican state of [Chiapas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiapas "Chiapas"), but excluded the lands that would become Belize and Panama. The president of the Audiencia, which had its seat in [Antigua Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigua_Guatemala "Antigua Guatemala"), was the governor of the entire area. In 1609 the area became a [captaincy general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captaincies_of_the_Spanish_Empire "Captaincies of the Spanish Empire") and the governor was also granted the title of captain general. The [Captaincy General of Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captaincy_General_of_Guatemala "Captaincy General of Guatemala") encompassed most of Central America, with the exception of [present-day Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Honduras "British Honduras") and Panama.

### Slavery

Between the 16th and 19th centuries, many enslaved Africans were transported to Central America and used for agriculture, mining, and domestic labor. The exact date of the enslaved African arrival to Central America is difficult to determine. There are many records that establish Spanish requests to travel with enslaved Africans to Guatemala from 1530-1532. Many of the enslaved Africans that arrived in Central American ports were transported through land or boats. Due to the nature of their captures it is unclear as to exactly where they came from, but assumed to be from West Central Africa as most ships departed from there. After their departure most enslaved Africans arrived either in Guatemala or Honduras.

Santiago de Guatemala was established in 1541 and became one of the most important trading posts due to its position between the Valley of Panchoy. Santiago's position was a great way to raise Spanish economic and political positions serving as the seat of the [Audiencia de Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Audiencia_of_Guatemala "Real Audiencia of Guatemala") and outranking other Spanish settlements. Enslaved Africans started arriving through Santiago in 1543, but smaller shipments had previously been made to Honduras through the port of Trujillo.

Arriving in small groups, enslaved Africans were bought based on gender and skill. Three categories were used to describe them: Bozal; which meant they had just arrived and had little knowledge of the Spanish language; Ladino, someone fluent in Spanish; and Criollo, which was someone who had been born outside of Africa. Spanish fluency was often valued more, but if the enslaved African was newly arrived they were also bought and taught different forms of labor in order to resell for a higher price.

Enslaved people in central America were made up of both Indigenous people and recently arrived Africans. In many instances enslaved Africans were used as overseers of the work being done by the enslaved indigenous people. More and more African slaves arrived in Central America due to the fact that many enslaved people were getting sick.

The enslaved Africans who arrived in Central America were for many different forms of labor, such as mining in Honduras and Nicaragua. They timber in Belize, sugar in Guatemala, indigo plantations in El Salvador. Although El Salvador was also known for having cacao, Costa Rica's booming cacao plantations led to the arrival of many enslaved people through Panama.

Enslaved Africans were able to gain their freedom in 1824, but prior to that there were many different actions taken by enslaved people to earn their freedom earlier. During the early conquest of Central America an enslaved man named Juan Bardales was granted his freedom and received a pension after participating in the conquest of Panama and Honduras. This allowed for many other enslaved people to gain their freedom. Another form would be through marriage. Although not always granted freedom for the enslaved person getting married, the child would be free.

The Captaincy General of Guatemala lasted for more than two centuries, but began to fray after a [rebellion in 1811](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1811_Independence_Movement "1811 Independence Movement") which began in the [Intendancy of San Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intendancy_of_San_Salvador "Intendancy of San Salvador"). The Captaincy General formally ended on 15 September 1821, with the signing of the [Act of Independence of Central America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Independence_of_Central_America "Act of Independence of Central America"). Mexican independence was achieved at virtually the same time with the signing of the [Treaty of Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_C%C3%B3rdoba "Treaty of Córdoba") and the [Declaration of Independence of the Mexican Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Independence_of_the_Mexican_Empire "Declaration of Independence of the Mexican Empire"), and the entire region was fully independent from Spanish authority by 28 September 1821.

From its independence from Spain in 1821 until 1823, the former Captaincy General [remained intact as part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_America_under_Mexican_rule "Central America under Mexican rule") of the short-lived [First Mexican Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Mexican_Empire "First Mexican Empire"). When [Agustín de Iturbide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agust%C3%ADn_de_Iturbide "Agustín de Iturbide") abdicated as [emperor of Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_of_Mexico "Emperor of Mexico") on 19 March 1823, Central America again became independent. On 1 July 1823, the Congress of Central America peacefully seceded from Mexico and declared absolute independence from all foreign nations, and the region formed the [Federal Republic of Central America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Central_America "Federal Republic of Central America").[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The Federal Republic of Central America, initially known as the United Provinces of Central America, was a sovereign state that existed from 1823 to 1840. It was composed of five states: Guatemala, Honduras, El Salvador, Nicaragua, and Costa Rica. The federation was established after these regions declared independence from Spain in 1821 and briefly joined the Mexican Empire before breaking away to form their own union. The republic adopted a constitution in 1824, which was inspired by the federal system of the United States. It provided for a federal capital, initially located in Guatemala City, and a president for each of the five constituent states. The constitution abolished slavery and maintained the privileges of the Roman Catholic Church, while restricting suffrage to the upper classes.

The territory that now makes up Belize was heavily contested in a dispute that continued for decades after Guatemala achieved independence. Spain, and later Guatemala, considered this land a [Guatemalan department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_Guatemala "Departments of Guatemala"). In 1862, Britain formally declared it a British colony and named it [British Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Honduras "British Honduras"). It became independent as Belize in 1981.

Panama, situated in the southernmost part of Central America on the [Isthmus of Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmus_of_Panama "Isthmus of Panama"), has for most of its history been culturally and politically linked to South America. Panama was part of the [Province of Tierra Firme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Tierra_Firme "Province of Tierra Firme") from 1510 until 1538 when it came under the jurisdiction of the newly formed *Audiencia Real de Panama*. Beginning in 1543, Panama was administered as part of the [Viceroyalty of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_Peru "Viceroyalty of Peru"), along with all other Spanish possessions in South America. Panama remained as part of the Viceroyalty of Peru until 1739, when it was transferred to the [Viceroyalty of New Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_New_Granada "Viceroyalty of New Granada"), the capital of which was located at [Santa Fé de Bogotá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogot%C3%A1 "Bogotá"). Panama remained as part of the Viceroyalty of New Granada until the disestablishment of that viceroyalty in 1819. A series of [military and political struggles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bol%C3%ADvar%27s_campaign_to_liberate_New_Granada "Bolívar's campaign to liberate New Granada") took place from that time until 1822, the result of which produced the republic of [Gran Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Colombia "Gran Colombia"). After the dissolution of Gran Colombia in 1830, Panama became part of a successor state, the [Republic of New Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_New_Granada "Republic of New Granada"). From 1855 until 1886, Panama existed as the [State of Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Panama "State of Panama"), first within the Republic of New Granada, then within the [Granadine Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granadine_Confederation "Granadine Confederation"), and finally within the [United States of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_Colombia "United States of Colombia"). The United States of Colombia was replaced by the [Republic of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia "Colombia") in 1886. As part of the Republic of Colombia, the State of Panama was abolished and it became the [Isthmus Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmus_Department "Isthmus Department"). Despite the many political reorganizations, Colombia was still deeply plagued by conflict, which eventually led to the [secession of Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_Panama_from_Colombia "Separation of Panama from Colombia") on 3 November 1903. Only after that time did some begin to regard Panama as a North or Central American entity.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

By the 1930s the [United Fruit Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Fruit_Company "United Fruit Company") owned 14,000 square kilometres (3.5 million acres) of land in Central America and the Caribbean and was the single largest land owner in Guatemala. Such holdings gave it great power over the governments of small countries. That was one of the factors that led to the coining of the phrase [banana republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana_republic "Banana republic").

After more than two hundred years of social unrest, violent conflict, and revolution, Central America today remains in a period of political transformation. Poverty, social injustice, and violence are still widespread. Nicaragua is the second poorest country in the western hemisphere, after Haiti.

## Geography

Central America is a part of [North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_America "North America") consisting of a tapering isthmus running from the southern extent of Mexico to the northwestern portion of [South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_America "South America"). Central America has the [Gulf of Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Mexico "Gulf of Mexico"), a body of water within the [Atlantic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Ocean "Atlantic Ocean"), to the north; the Caribbean Sea, also part of the Atlantic Ocean, to the northeast; and the Pacific Ocean to the southwest. Some [physiographists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_geography "Physical geography") define the [Isthmus of Tehuantepec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmus_of_Tehuantepec "Isthmus of Tehuantepec") as the northern geographic border of Central America, while others use the northwestern borders of Belize and Guatemala. From there, the Central American land mass extends southeastward to the [Atrato River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atrato_River "Atrato River"), where it connects to the [Pacific Lowlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific/Choc%C3%B3_natural_region "Pacific/Chocó natural region") in northwestern South America.

Central America has over 70 active volcanoes, 41 which are located in El Salvador, and Guatemala. The volcano with the most activity in Central America is [Santa María](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Mar%C3%ADa_(volcano) "Santa María (volcano)"). Still experiencing frequent eruptions to this day, with the last one beginning in 2013, and still is going on to this day.

Of the many mountain ranges within Central America, the longest are the [Sierra Madre de Chiapas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Madre_de_Chiapas "Sierra Madre de Chiapas"), the [Cordillera Isabelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Isabelia "Cordillera Isabelia") and the [Cordillera de Talamanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_de_Talamanca "Cordillera de Talamanca"). At 4,220 meters (13,850 ft), [Volcán Tajumulco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volc%C3%A1n_Tajumulco "Volcán Tajumulco") is the highest peak in Central America. Between the mountain ranges lie fertile valleys that are suitable for the raising of livestock and for the production of coffee, tobacco, beans and other crops. Most of the population of Honduras, Costa Rica and Guatemala lives in valleys.

[Trade winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_winds "Trade winds") have a significant effect upon the climate of Central America. Temperatures in Central America are highest just prior to the [summer wet season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_season "Wet season"), and are lowest during the [winter dry season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_season "Dry season"), when trade winds contribute to a cooler climate. The highest temperatures occur in April, due to higher levels of sunlight, lower cloud cover and a decrease in trade winds.

### Biodiversity

### Central American forests

Central America is part of the Mesoamerican [biodiversity hotspot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_hotspot "Biodiversity hotspot"), boasting 7% of the world's [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity "Biodiversity"). The [Pacific Flyway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Flyway "Pacific Flyway") is a major north–south [flyway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flyway "Flyway") for [migratory birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_migration "Bird migration") in the Americas, extending from [Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska "Alaska") to [Tierra del Fuego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierra_del_Fuego "Tierra del Fuego"). Due to the funnel-like shape of its land mass, migratory birds can be seen in very high concentrations in Central America, especially in the spring and autumn. As a bridge between North America and South America, Central America has many species from the [Nearctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nearctic_realm "Nearctic realm") and the [Neotropical realms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neotropical_realm "Neotropical realm"). However the southern countries (Costa Rica and Panama) of the region have more biodiversity than the northern countries (Guatemala and Belize), meanwhile the central countries (Honduras, Nicaragua and El Salvador) have the least biodiversity. Over 300 species of the region's flora and fauna are threatened, 107 of which are classified as critically endangered. The underlying problems are [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation "Deforestation"), which is estimated by [FAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAO "FAO") at 1.2% per year in Central America and Mexico combined, [fragmentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat_fragmentation "Habitat fragmentation") of [rainforests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainforest "Rainforest") and the fact that 80% of the vegetation in Central America has already been converted to agriculture.

Efforts to protect fauna and flora in the region are made by creating [ecoregions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecoregion "Ecoregion") and nature reserves. 36% of Belize's land territory falls under some form of official protected status, giving Belize one of the most extensive systems of terrestrial protected areas in the Americas. In addition, 13% of Belize's marine territory are also protected. A large coral reef extends from Mexico to Honduras: the [Mesoamerican Barrier Reef System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerican_Barrier_Reef_System "Mesoamerican Barrier Reef System"). The [Belize Barrier Reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize_Barrier_Reef "Belize Barrier Reef") is part of this. The Belize Barrier Reef is home to a large diversity of plants and animals, and is one of the most diverse [ecosystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystem "Ecosystem") of the world. It is home to 70 hard coral species, 36 [soft coral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octocorallia "Octocorallia") species, 500 species of fish and hundreds of [invertebrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invertebrate "Invertebrate") species.
So far only about 10% of the species in the Belize barrier reef have been discovered.

From 2001 to 2010, 5,376 square kilometers (2,076 sq mi) of forest were lost in the region. In 2010 Belize had 63% of remaining forest cover, Costa Rica 46%, Panama 45%, Honduras 41%, Guatemala 37%, Nicaragua 29%, and El Salvador 21%. Most of the loss occurred in the moist forest [biome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biome "Biome"), with 12,201 square kilometers (4,711 sq mi). Woody vegetation loss was partially set off by a gain in the coniferous forest biome with 4,730 square kilometers (1,830 sq mi), and a gain in the dry forest biome at 2,054 square kilometers (793 sq mi). Mangroves and deserts contributed only 1% to the loss in forest vegetation. The bulk of the [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation_in_Central_America "Deforestation in Central America") was located at the Caribbean slopes of Nicaragua with a loss of 8,574 square kilometers (3,310 sq mi) of forest in the period from 2001 to 2010. The most significant regrowth of 3,050 square kilometers (1,180 sq mi) of forest was seen in the coniferous woody vegetation of Honduras.

### Montane forests

The [Central American pine-oak forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_pine-oak_forests "Central American pine-oak forests") ecoregion, in the [tropical and subtropical coniferous forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_and_subtropical_coniferous_forests "Tropical and subtropical coniferous forests") biome, is found in Central America and southern Mexico. The Central American pine-oak forests occupy an area of 111,400 square kilometers (43,000 sq mi), extending along the mountainous spine of Central America, extending from the [Sierra Madre de Chiapas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Madre_de_Chiapas "Sierra Madre de Chiapas") in Mexico's [Chiapas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiapas "Chiapas") state through the highlands of Guatemala, El Salvador, and Honduras to central Nicaragua. The pine-oak forests lie between 600–1,800 metres (2,000–5,900 ft) elevation, and are surrounded at lower elevations by [tropical moist forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_moist_forests "Tropical moist forests") and [tropical dry forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_dry_forests "Tropical dry forests"). Higher elevations above 1,800 metres (5,900 ft) are usually covered with [Central American montane forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_montane_forests "Central American montane forests"). The Central American pine-oak forests are composed of many species characteristic of temperate North America including [oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak "Oak"), [pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine "Pine"), [fir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fir "Fir"), and [cypress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypress "Cypress").

[Laurel forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurel_forest "Laurel forest") is the most common type of Central American temperate evergreen [cloud forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_forest "Cloud forest"), found in almost all Central American countries, normally more than 1,000 meters (3,300 ft) above sea level. Tree species include [evergreen oaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evergreen_oak "Evergreen oak"), members of the [laurel family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurus "Laurus"), species of *[Weinmannia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weinmannia "Weinmannia")* and *[Magnolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnolia "Magnolia")*, and *[Drimys granadensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drimys_granadensis "Drimys granadensis")*. The cloud forest of [Sierra de las Minas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_de_las_Minas "Sierra de las Minas"), Guatemala, is the largest in Central America. In some areas of southeastern Honduras there are cloud forests, the largest located near the border with Nicaragua. In Nicaragua, cloud forests are situated near the border with Honduras, but many were cleared to grow coffee. There are still some temperate evergreen hills in the north. The only cloud forest in the Pacific coastal zone of Central America is on the [Mombacho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombacho "Mombacho") volcano in Nicaragua. In Costa Rica, there are laurel forests in the Cordillera de [Tilarán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilar%C3%A1n "Tilarán") and [Volcán Arenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volc%C3%A1n_Arenal "Volcán Arenal"), called [Monteverde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monteverde "Monteverde"), also in the [Cordillera de Talamanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_de_Talamanca "Cordillera de Talamanca").

The [Central American montane forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_montane_forests "Central American montane forests") are an ecoregion of the [tropical and subtropical moist broadleaf forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_and_subtropical_moist_broadleaf_forests "Tropical and subtropical moist broadleaf forests") biome, as defined by the [World Wildlife Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wildlife_Fund "World Wildlife Fund"). These forests are of the moist deciduous and the semi-evergreen seasonal subtype of tropical and subtropical moist broadleaf forests and receive high overall rainfall with a warm summer [wet season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_season "Wet season") and a cooler winter dry season. Central American montane forests consist of forest patches located at altitudes ranging from 1,800–4,000 metres (5,900–13,100 ft), on the summits and slopes of the highest mountains in Central America ranging from Southern Mexico, through Guatemala, El Salvador, and Honduras, to northern Nicaragua. The entire ecoregion covers an area of 13,200 square kilometers (5,100 sq mi) and has a [temperate climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_climate "Temperate climate") with relatively high [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation "Precipitation") levels.

Ecoregions are not only established to protect the forests themselves but also because they are habitats for an incomparably rich and often endemic fauna. Almost half of the bird population of the [Talamancan montane forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talamancan_montane_forests "Talamancan montane forests") in Costa Rica and Panama are endemic to this region. Several birds are listed as threatened, most notably the [resplendent quetzal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resplendent_quetzal "Resplendent quetzal") (*Pharomacrus mocinno*), [three-wattled bellbird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-wattled_bellbird "Three-wattled bellbird") (*Procnias tricarunculata*), [bare-necked umbrellabird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bare-necked_umbrellabird "Bare-necked umbrellabird") (*Cephalopterus glabricollis*), and [black guan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_guan "Black guan") (*Chamaepetes unicolor*). Many of the amphibians are endemic and depend on the existence of forest. The [golden toad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_toad "Golden toad") that once inhabited a small region in the Monteverde Reserve, which is part of the Talamancan montane forests, has not been seen alive since 1989 and is listed as extinct by IUCN. The exact causes for its extinction are unknown. Global warming may have played a role, because the development of that frog is typical for this area may have been compromised. Seven small mammals are endemic to the Costa Rica-Chiriqui highlands within the Talamancan montane forest region. [Jaguars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar "Jaguar"), [cougars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cougar "Cougar"), [spider monkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider_monkey "Spider monkey"), as well as [tapirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapir "Tapir"), and [anteaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anteater "Anteater") live in the woods of Central America. The [Central American red brocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_red_brocket "Central American red brocket") is a brocket deer found in Central America's tropical forest.

### Geology

Central America is geologically very active, with [volcanic eruptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Types_of_volcanic_eruptions "Types of volcanic eruptions") and earthquakes occurring frequently, and [tsunamis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsunami "Tsunami") occurring occasionally. Many thousands of people have died as a result of these natural disasters.

Most of Central America rests atop the [Caribbean Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Plate "Caribbean Plate"). This [tectonic plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plate_tectonics "Plate tectonics") converges with the [Cocos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocos_Plate "Cocos Plate"), [Nazca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazca_Plate "Nazca Plate"), and [North American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Plate "North American Plate") plates to form the [Middle America Trench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_America_Trench "Middle America Trench"), a major [subduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subduction "Subduction") zone. The Middle America Trench is situated some 60–160 kilometers (37–99 mi) off the Pacific coast of Central America and runs roughly parallel to it. Many large earthquakes have occurred as a result of seismic activity at the Middle America Trench. For example, subduction of the Cocos Plate beneath the North American Plate at the Middle America Trench is believed to have caused the [1985 Mexico City earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_Mexico_City_earthquake "1985 Mexico City earthquake") that killed as many as 40,000 people. Seismic activity at the Middle America Trench is also responsible for earthquakes in [1902](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1902_Guatemala_earthquake "1902 Guatemala earthquake"), [1942](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1942_Guatemala_earthquake "1942 Guatemala earthquake"), [1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_Nicaragua_earthquake "1956 Nicaragua earthquake"), [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Nicaragua_earthquake "1972 Nicaragua earthquake"), [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_El_Salvador_earthquake "1982 El Salvador earthquake"), [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Nicaragua_earthquake "1992 Nicaragua earthquake"), [January 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2001_El_Salvador_earthquake "January 2001 El Salvador earthquake"), [February 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_2001_El_Salvador_earthquake "February 2001 El Salvador earthquake"), [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2007_Iztapa_earthquake&action=edit&redlink=1 "2007 Iztapa earthquake (page does not exist)"), [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Guatemala_earthquake "2012 Guatemala earthquake"), [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_2014_Nicaragua_earthquake "October 2014 Nicaragua earthquake"), and many other earthquakes throughout Central America.

The Middle America Trench is not the only source of seismic activity in Central America. The [Motagua Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motagua_Fault "Motagua Fault") is an onshore continuation of the [Cayman Trough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayman_Trough "Cayman Trough") which forms part of the tectonic boundary between the North American Plate and the Caribbean Plate. This [transform fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transform_fault "Transform fault") cuts right across Guatemala and then continues offshore until it merges with the Middle America Trench along the [Pacific coast of Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Coast_of_Mexico "Pacific Coast of Mexico"), near [Acapulco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acapulco "Acapulco"). Seismic activity at the Motagua Fault has been responsible for earthquakes in [1717](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1717_Guatemala_earthquake "1717 Guatemala earthquake"), [1773](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1773_Guatemala_earthquake "1773 Guatemala earthquake"), [1902](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1902_Guatemala_earthquake "1902 Guatemala earthquake"), [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Guatemala_earthquake "1976 Guatemala earthquake"), [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Honduras_earthquake "1980 Honduras earthquake"), and [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Honduras_earthquake "2009 Honduras earthquake").

Another onshore continuation of the Cayman Trough is the [Chixoy-Polochic Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chixoy-Polochic_Fault "Chixoy-Polochic Fault"), which runs parallel to, and roughly 80 kilometers (50 mi) to the north, of the Motagua Fault. Though less active than the Motagua Fault, seismic activity at the Chixoy-Polochic Fault is still thought to be capable of producing very large earthquakes, such as the 1816 earthquake of Guatemala.

[Managua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managua "Managua"), the capital of Nicaragua, was devastated by earthquakes in [1931](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_Nicaragua_earthquake "1931 Nicaragua earthquake") and [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Nicaragua_earthquake "1972 Nicaragua earthquake").

Volcanic eruptions are also common in Central America. In 1968 the [Arenal Volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arenal_Volcano "Arenal Volcano"), in Costa Rica, erupted killing 87 people as the 3 villages of Tabacon, Pueblo Nuevo and San Luis were buried under pyroclastic flows and debris. Fertile soils from weathered volcanic lava have made it possible to sustain dense populations in the agriculturally productive highland areas.

## Politics

### Integration

Central America is currently undergoing a process of political, economic and cultural transformation that started in 1907 with the creation of the [Central American Court of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_Court_of_Justice "Central American Court of Justice").

In 1951 the integration process continued with the signature of the San Salvador Treaty, which created the ODECA, the Organization of Central American States. However, the unity of the ODECA was limited by conflicts between several member states.

In 1991, the integration agenda was further advanced by the creation of the [Central American Integration System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_Integration_System "Central American Integration System") (*Sistema para la Integración Centroamericana*, or SICA). SICA provides a clear legal basis to avoid disputes between the member states. SICA membership includes the 7 nations of Central America plus the [Dominican Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Republic "Dominican Republic"), a state that is traditionally considered part of the Caribbean.

On 6 December 2008, SICA announced an agreement to pursue a common currency and common passport for the member nations. No timeline for implementation was discussed.

Central America already has several supranational institutions such as the [Central American Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_Parliament "Central American Parliament"), the [Central American Bank for Economic Integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_Bank_for_Economic_Integration "Central American Bank for Economic Integration") and the [Central American Common Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_Common_Market "Central American Common Market").

On 22 July 2011, President [Mauricio Funes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauricio_Funes "Mauricio Funes") of El Salvador became the first president *[pro tempore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_tempore "Pro tempore")* to SICA. El Salvador also became the headquarters of SICA with the inauguration of a new building.

### Parliament

The Central American Parliament (aka PARLACEN) is a political and parliamentary body of SICA. The parliament started around 1980, and its primary goal was to resolve conflicts in Nicaragua, Guatemala, and El Salvador. Although the group was disbanded in 1986, ideas of unity of Central Americans still remained, so a treaty was signed in 1987 to create the Central American Parliament and other political bodies. Its original members were Guatemala, El Salvador, Nicaragua and Honduras. The parliament is the political organ of Central America, and is part of SICA. New members have since then joined including Panama and the Dominican Republic.

Costa Rica is not a member state of the Central American Parliament and its adhesion remains as a very unpopular topic at all levels of the Costa Rican society due to existing strong political criticism towards the regional parliament, since it is regarded by Costa Ricans as a menace to democratic accountability and effectiveness of integration efforts. Excessively high salaries for its members, legal immunity of jurisdiction from any member State, corruption, lack of a binding nature and effectiveness of the regional parliament's decisions, high operative costs and immediate membership of Central American presidents once they leave their office and presidential terms, are the most common reasons invoked by Costa Ricans against the Central American Parliament.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Foreign relations

Until recently,[*[when?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")*] all Central American countries maintained diplomatic relations with [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan") instead of China. President [Óscar Arias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%93scar_Arias "Óscar Arias") of Costa Rica, however, established diplomatic relations with China in 2007, severing formal diplomatic ties with Taiwan. After breaking off relations with the Republic of China in 2017, Panama established diplomatic relations with the People's Republic of China. In August 2018, El Salvador also severed ties with Taiwan to formally start recognizing the People's Republic of China as sole China, a move many considered lacked transparency due to its abruptness and reports of the Chinese government's desires to invest in the department of La Union while also promising to fund the ruling party's reelection campaign. [President of El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_El_Salvador "President of El Salvador") [Nayib Bukele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nayib_Bukele "Nayib Bukele") broke diplomatic relations with Taiwan and established ties with [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China"). On 9 December 2021, Nicaragua resumed relations with the PRC.

## Economy

Signed in 2004, the [Central American Free Trade Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Republic%E2%80%93Central_America_Free_Trade_Agreement "Dominican Republic–Central America Free Trade Agreement") (CAFTA) is an agreement between the United States, Costa Rica, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Nicaragua, and the [Dominican Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Republic "Dominican Republic"). The treaty is aimed at promoting [free trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_trade "Free trade") among its members.

Guatemala has the largest economy in the region. Its main exports are coffee, sugar, bananas, petroleum, clothing, and [cardamom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardamom "Cardamom"). Of its 10.29 billion dollar annual exports, 40.2% go to the United States, 11.1% to neighboring El Salvador, 8% to Honduras, 5.5% to Mexico, 4.7% to Nicaragua, and 4.3% to Costa Rica.

The region is particularly attractive for companies (especially clothing companies) because of its geographical proximity to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States"), very low wages and considerable tax advantages. In addition, the decline in the prices of coffee and other export products and the structural adjustment measures promoted by the international financial institutions have partly ruined agriculture, favouring the emergence of maquiladoras. This sector accounts for 42 per cent of total exports from El Salvador, 55 per cent from Guatemala, and 65 per cent from Honduras. However, its contribution to the economies of these countries is disputed; raw materials are imported, jobs are precarious and low-paid, and tax exemptions weaken public finances.

They are also criticised for the working conditions of employees: insults and physical violence, abusive dismissals (especially of pregnant workers), working hours, non-payment of overtime. According to Lucrecia Bautista, coordinator of the *maquilas* sector of the audit firm Coverco, "labour law regulations are regularly violated in maquilas and there is no political will to enforce their application. In the case of infringements, the labour inspectorate shows remarkable leniency. It is a question of not discouraging investors." Trade unionists are subject to pressure, and sometimes to kidnapping or murder. In some cases, business leaders have used the services of the [maras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mara_(gang) "Mara (gang)"). Finally, black lists containing the names of trade unionists or political activists are circulating in employers' circles.

Economic growth in Central America is projected to slow slightly in 2014–15, as country-specific domestic factors offset the positive effects from stronger economic activity in the United States.

### Tourism

### Coasts

Tourism in Belize has grown considerably in more recent times, and it is now the second largest industry in the nation. Belizean Prime Minister [Dean Barrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Barrow "Dean Barrow") has stated his intention to use tourism to combat poverty throughout the country. The growth in tourism has positively affected the agricultural, commercial, and finance industries, as well as the construction industry. The results for Belize's tourism-driven economy have been significant, with the nation welcoming almost one million tourists in a calendar year for the first time in its history in 2012. Belize is also the only country in Central America with English as its official language, making this country a comfortable destination for English-speaking tourists.

Costa Rica is the most visited nation in Central America. Tourism in Costa Rica is one of the fastest growing economic sectors of the country, having become the largest source of foreign revenue by 1995. Since 1999, tourism has earned more foreign exchange than bananas, pineapples and coffee exports combined. The tourism boom began in 1987, with the number of visitors up from 329,000 in 1988, through 1.03 million in 1999, to a historical record of 2.43 million foreign visitors and $1.92-billion in revenue in 2013. In 2012 tourism contributed with 12.5% of the country's GDP and it was responsible for 11.7% of direct and indirect employment.

Tourism in Nicaragua has grown considerably recently, and it is now the second largest industry in the nation. Nicaraguan president [Daniel Ortega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Ortega "Daniel Ortega") has stated his intention to use tourism to combat poverty throughout the country. The growth in tourism has positively affected the agricultural, commercial, and finance industries, as well as the construction industry. The results for Nicaragua's tourism-driven economy have been significant, with the nation welcoming one million tourists in a calendar year for the first time in its history in 2010.

### Transport

#### Roads

The [Inter-American Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-American_Highway "Inter-American Highway") is the Central American section of the [Pan-American Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-American_Highway "Pan-American Highway"), and spans 5,470 kilometers (3,400 mi) between [Nuevo Laredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuevo_Laredo "Nuevo Laredo"), Mexico, and [Panama City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_City "Panama City"), Panama. Because of the 87-kilometer (54 mi) break in the highway known as the [Darién Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari%C3%A9n_Gap "Darién Gap"), it is not possible to cross between Central America and South America in an automobile.

### Languages

The official language majority in all Central American countries is [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_language "Spanish language"), except in Belize, where the official language is [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language "English language"). Most people in Belize speak [Belizean Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belizean_Creole "Belizean Creole"). [Mayan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayan_languages "Mayan languages") constitute a [language family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_family "Language family") consisting of about 26 related languages. Guatemala formally recognized 21 of these in 1996. [Xinca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xincan_languages "Xincan languages"), [Miskito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miskito_language "Miskito language"), and [Garifuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garifuna_language "Garifuna language") are also present in Central America.

### Ethnic groups

This region of the continent is very rich in terms of ethnic groups. The majority of the population is [mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo "Mestizo"), with sizable Mayan and African descendent populations present, along with numerous other indigenous groups such as the [Miskito people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miskito_people "Miskito people"). The immigration of Arabs, Jews, Chinese, Europeans and others brought additional groups to the area.

### Religious groups

The predominant religion in Central America is [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity") (95.6%). Beginning with the Spanish colonization of Central America in the 16th century, [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church "Catholic Church") became the most popular religion in the region until the first half of the 20th century. Since the 1960s, there has been an increase in other Christian groups, particularly [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism "Protestantism"), as well as other religious organizations, and individuals identifying themselves as having no religion.
