# History of technology

The **history of technology** is the history of human invention of tools and techniques. Technology includes methods ranging from simple [stone tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_tools "Stone tools") to the complex [genetic engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_engineering "Genetic engineering") and [information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology "Information technology") that has emerged since the 1980s. The term *technology* comes from the Greek words *techne*, meaning art and craft, and *logos*, meaning word and speech. It was first used to describe [applied arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_arts "Applied arts"), but it is now used to describe advancements and changes that affect the environment around us.

New knowledge has enabled people to create new tools. Conversely, many scientific endeavors are made possible by new [technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technologies "Technologies"), such as [scientific instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_instrument "Scientific instrument") that allow us to study nature in greater detail than our natural senses can.

Since much of technology is [applied science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_science "Applied science"), technical history is connected to the [history of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_science "History of science"). Since technology uses resources, technical history is tightly connected to [economic history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_history "Economic history"). From those resources, technology produces other resources, including *technological artifacts* used in everyday life. [Technological change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_change "Technological change") affects, and is affected by, a society's cultural traditions. It is a force for economic growth and a means of developing and projecting economic, political, and military power and wealth.

## Measuring technological progress

Many [sociologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociologists "Sociologists") and [anthropologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropologists "Anthropologists") have created [social theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_theories "Social theories") dealing with [social](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociocultural_evolution "Sociocultural evolution") and [cultural evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_evolution "Cultural evolution"). Some, such as [Lewis H. Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_H._Morgan "Lewis H. Morgan"), [Leslie White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_White "Leslie White"), and [Gerhard Lenski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Lenski "Gerhard Lenski"), have declared technological progress the primary factor driving the development of human civilization. Morgan's concept of three major stages of social evolution (savagery, [barbarism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_culture "Primitive culture"), and [civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization "Civilization")) can be divided by technological milestones, such as fire. White argued that the measure by which to judge the evolution of culture is energy.

For White, "the primary function of culture" is to "harness and control energy." White differentiates between five stages of [human development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_development_(humanity) "Human development (humanity)"): In the first, people use the energy of their own muscles. In the second, they use the energy of [domesticated animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestication_of_animals "Domestication of animals"). In the third, they use plant energy ([agricultural revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_Revolution "Neolithic Revolution")). In the fourth, they learn to use the energy of [natural resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_resource "Natural resource"): coal, oil, gas. In the fifth, they harness [nuclear energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power "Nuclear power"). White introduced the formula P = E/T, where P is the development index, E is a measure of energy consumed, and T is a measure of the efficiency of technical factors in using the energy. In his own words, "culture evolves as the amount of energy harnessed per capita per year is increased, or as the efficiency of the instrumental means of putting the energy to work is increased". [Nikolai Kardashev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Kardashev "Nikolai Kardashev") extrapolated his theory, creating the [Kardashev scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kardashev_scale "Kardashev scale"), which categorizes the energy use of advanced civilizations.

Lenski's approach focuses on information. The more information and knowledge (especially the ability to shape the [natural environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_environment "Natural environment")) a given society has, the more advanced it is. He identifies four stages of human development, based on advances in the [history of communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_communication "History of communication"). In the first stage, information is passed by [genes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene "Gene"). In the second, when humans gain [sentience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentience "Sentience"), they can learn and pass information through experience. In the third, the humans start using signs and develop [logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic "Logic"). In the fourth, they can create symbols, develop language, and write. Advancements in [communications technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communications_technology "Communications technology") translate into advancements in the [economic system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_system "Economic system") and [political system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_system "Political system"), [distribution of wealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_of_wealth "Distribution of wealth"), [social inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_inequality "Social inequality"), and other spheres of social life. He also differentiates societies based on their level of technology, communication, and economy:

* [hunter-gatherer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer "Hunter-gatherer"),
* simple agricultural,
* advanced agricultural,
* [industrial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_society "Industrial society"),
* special (such as fishing societies).

In economics, productivity is a measure of technological progress. Productivity increases when fewer inputs (classically, labor and capital, though some measures include energy and materials) are used to produce a unit of output. Another indicator of technological progress is the development of new products and services, which is necessary to offset unemployment that would otherwise result from reduced labor inputs. In developed countries, productivity growth has been slowing since the late 1970s; however, it has been higher in some sectors, such as manufacturing. For example, employment in manufacturing in the United States declined from over 30% in the 1940s to just over 10% 70 years later. Similar changes occurred in other developed countries. This stage is referred to as *post-industrial*.

In the late 1970s sociologists and anthropologists like [Alvin Toffler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvin_Toffler "Alvin Toffler") (author of *[Future Shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Shock "Future Shock")*), [Daniel Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Bell "Daniel Bell") and [John Naisbitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Naisbitt "John Naisbitt") have approached the theories of [post-industrial societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-industrial_society "Post-industrial society"), arguing that the current era of [industrial society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_society "Industrial society") is coming to an end, and [services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_(economics) "Service (economics)") and information are becoming more important than industry and goods. Some extreme visions of post-industrial society, especially in [fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fictional_technology "Fictional technology"), are strikingly similar to visions of near- and post-[singularity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_singularity "Technological singularity") societies.

#### Stone Age

During most of the **[Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic "Paleolithic")** – the bulk of the Stone Age – all humans lived with limited tools and few permanent settlements. The first major technologies were tied to survival, hunting, and food preparation. Stone tools and weapons, [fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_of_fire_by_early_humans "Control of fire by early humans"), and [clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_clothing_and_textiles "History of clothing and textiles") were major technological developments during this period.

Human ancestors have been using stone and other tools since long before the emergence of *[Homo sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_sapiens "Homo sapiens")* approximately 300,000 years ago. The earliest direct evidence of tool usage was found in [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia "Ethiopia") within the [Great Rift Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Rift_Valley,_Kenya "Great Rift Valley, Kenya"), dating back to 2.5 million years ago. The earliest methods of [stone tool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_tool "Stone tool") making, known as the [Oldowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldowan "Oldowan") "industry", date back to at least 2.3 million years ago. This era of stone tool use is called the *[Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic "Paleolithic")*, or "Old stone age", and spans all of human history up to the development of agriculture approximately 12,000 years ago.

To make a stone tool, a "[core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithic_core "Lithic core")" of hard stone with specific flaking properties (such as [flint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flint "Flint")) was struck with a [hammerstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammerstone "Hammerstone"). This flaking produced sharp edges which could be used as tools, primarily in the form of [choppers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chopper_(archaeology) "Chopper (archaeology)") or [scrapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scraper_(archaeology) "Scraper (archaeology)").[*[better source needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")*] These tools greatly aided the early humans in their [hunter-gatherer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer "Hunter-gatherer") lifestyle to perform a variety of tasks including butchering carcasses (and breaking bones to get at the [marrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone_marrow "Bone marrow")); chopping wood; cracking open nuts; skinning an animal for its hide, and even forming other tools out of softer materials such as bone and wood.

The earliest stone tools were irrelevant, being little more than a fractured rock. In the [Acheulian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acheulian "Acheulian") era, beginning approximately 1.65 million years ago, methods of working these stones into specific shapes, such as [hand axes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hand_axe "Hand axe") emerged. This early Stone Age is described as the [Lower Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Paleolithic "Lower Paleolithic").

The [Middle Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Paleolithic "Middle Paleolithic"), approximately 300,000 years ago, saw the introduction of the [prepared-core technique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prepared-core_technique "Prepared-core technique"), in which multiple blades could be rapidly produced from a single core stone. The [Upper Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Paleolithic "Upper Paleolithic"), beginning approximately 40,000 years ago, saw the introduction of [pressure flaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure_flaking "Pressure flaking"), where a wood, bone, or antler [punch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punch_(engineering) "Punch (engineering)") could be used to shape a stone very finely.

The end of the last Ice Age, about 10,000 years ago, is taken as the end point of the [Upper Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Paleolithic "Upper Paleolithic") and the beginning of the [Epipaleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epipaleolithic "Epipaleolithic") / [Mesolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesolithic "Mesolithic"). The Mesolithic technology included the use of [microliths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microliths "Microliths") as composite stone tools, along with wood, bone, and antler tools.

The later Stone Age, during which the rudiments of agricultural technology were developed, is called the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic "Neolithic") period. During this period, polished stone tools were made from a variety of hard rocks such as [flint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flint "Flint"), [jade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jade "Jade"), [jadeite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jadeite "Jadeite"), and [greenstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenschist "Greenschist"), largely by working exposures as quarries, but later the valuable rocks were pursued by tunneling underground, the first steps in mining technology. The polished axes were used for forest clearance and the establishment of crop farming, and were so effective that they remained in use when bronze and iron appeared. These stone axes were used alongside a continued use of stone tools such as a range of [projectiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projectile_point "Projectile point"), knives, and [scrapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scraper_(archaeology) "Scraper (archaeology)"), as well as tools made from organic materials such as wood, bone, and antler.

Stone Age cultures developed [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_music "Prehistoric music") and engaged in organized [warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_warfare "Prehistoric warfare"). Stone Age humans developed ocean-worthy [outrigger canoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outrigger_canoe "Outrigger canoe") technology, leading to [migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Papua_New_Guinea "History of Papua New Guinea") across the [Malay Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Archipelago "Malay Archipelago"), across the Indian Ocean to [Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar "Madagascar") and also across the Pacific Ocean, which required knowledge of the ocean currents, weather patterns, sailing, and [celestial navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_navigation "Celestial navigation").

Although Paleolithic cultures left no written records, the shift from nomadic life to settlement and agriculture can be inferred from a range of archaeological evidence. Such evidence includes ancient tools, [cave paintings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_painting "Cave painting"), and other [prehistoric art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_art "Prehistoric art"), such as the [Venus of Willendorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus_of_Willendorf "Venus of Willendorf"). Human remains also provide direct evidence, both through the examination of bones and the study of [mummies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mummy "Mummy"). Scientists and historians have drawn significant inferences about the lifestyles and cultures of various prehistoric peoples, especially their technology.

### Ancient

#### Copper and Bronze Ages

Metallic copper occurs on the surface of weathered copper ore deposits, and copper was used before copper [smelting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smelting "Smelting") was known. Copper smelting is believed to have originated when the technology of pottery [kilns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiln "Kiln") allowed sufficiently high temperatures. The concentration of various elements such as arsenic increases with depth in copper ore deposits, and smelting of these ores yields [arsenical bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenical_bronze "Arsenical bronze"), which can be sufficiently [work hardened](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Work_hardening "Work hardening") to be suitable for making tools.

[Bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze "Bronze") is an alloy of copper with tin; the latter being found in relatively few deposits globally, led to a long time elapsing before true tin bronze became widespread. (See: [Tin sources and trade in ancient times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin_sources_and_trade_in_ancient_times "Tin sources and trade in ancient times")) Bronze was a major advancement over stone as a material for making tools, both because of its mechanical properties, such as strength and ductility, and because it could be cast in molds to make intricately shaped objects. Bronze significantly advanced shipbuilding technology with better tools and bronze nails. Bronze nails replaced the old method of attaching the hull's boards with a cord threaded through drilled holes. Better ships enabled long-distance trade and the advance of civilization.

This technological trend apparently began in the [Fertile Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertile_Crescent "Fertile Crescent") and spread outward over time.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] These developments were not, and still are not, universal. The [three-age system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-age_system "Three-age system") does not accurately describe the technology history of groups outside of [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia "Eurasia"), and does not apply at all in the case of some isolated populations, such as the [Spinifex People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinifex_People "Spinifex People"), the [Sentinelese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentinelese_people "Sentinelese people"), and various Amazonian tribes, which still make use of Stone Age technology, and have not developed agricultural or metal technology. These villages preserve traditional customs in the face of global modernity, exhibiting remarkable resistance to rapid technological advancement.

#### Iron Age

Before iron smelting was developed, the only iron obtained was from meteorites, which are usually identified by having nickel content. [Meteoric iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteoric_iron "Meteoric iron") was rare and valuable, but was sometimes used to make tools and other implements, such as fish hooks.

The Iron Age involved the adoption of [iron smelting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomery "Bloomery") technology. It generally replaced bronze and enabled the production of tools that were stronger, lighter, and cheaper to make than their bronze equivalents. The raw materials to make iron, such as ore and limestone, are far more abundant than copper and especially tin ores. Consequently, iron was produced in many areas.

It was not possible to mass-manufacture steel or pure iron because of the high temperatures required. Furnaces could reach melting temperature, but the crucibles and molds needed for melting and casting had not been developed. Steel could be produced by [forging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forging "Forging") bloomery iron to reduce the carbon content in a somewhat controllable way, but steel produced by this method was not homogeneous. In many Eurasian cultures, the Iron Age was the last major step before the development of writing, though this was not universally the case.

In Europe, large [hill forts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_fort "Hill fort") were built either as refuges in times of war or as permanent settlements. In some cases, existing Bronze Age forts were expanded. The pace of land clearance using the more effective iron axes increased, providing more farmland to support the growing population.

#### Mesopotamia

[Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia "Mesopotamia") (modern Iraq) and its peoples ([Sumerians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumer "Sumer"), [Akkadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkad_(city) "Akkad (city)"), [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyria "Assyria") and [Babylonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonia "Babylonia")) lived in cities from c. 4000 BC, and developed a sophisticated architecture in mud-brick and stone, including the use of the [true arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_arch "True arch"). The walls of Babylon were so massive that they were quoted as a [Wonder of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonder_of_the_World "Wonder of the World"). They developed extensive water systems; canals for transport and irrigation in the alluvial south, and catchment systems stretching for tens of kilometers in the hilly north. Their palaces had sophisticated drainage systems.

Writing was invented in Mesopotamia, using the [cuneiform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuneiform "Cuneiform") script. Many records on clay tablets and stone inscriptions have survived. These civilizations were early adopters of bronze technologies, which they used for tools, weapons, and monumental statuary. By 1200 BC, they could cast objects 5 m long in a single piece.

Several of the six classic [simple machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_machines "Simple machines") were invented in Mesopotamia. Mesopotamians have been credited with the invention of the wheel. The [wheel and axle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel_and_axle "Wheel and axle") mechanism first appeared with the [potter's wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potter%27s_wheel "Potter's wheel"), invented in [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia "Mesopotamia") (modern Iraq) during the 5th millennium BC. This led to the invention of the [wheeled vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheeled_vehicle "Wheeled vehicle") in Mesopotamia during the early 4th millennium BC. Depictions of wheeled [wagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagon "Wagon") found on [clay tablet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_tablet "Clay tablet") [pictographs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pictographs "Pictographs") at the [Eanna district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruk#Eanna_District "Uruk") of [Uruk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruk "Uruk") are dated between 3700 and 3500 BC. The [lever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lever "Lever") was used in the [shadoof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadoof "Shadoof") water-lifting device, the first [crane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crane_(machine) "Crane (machine)") machine, which appeared in Mesopotamia circa 3000 BC, and then in [ancient Egyptian technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egyptian_technology "Ancient Egyptian technology") circa 2000 BC. The earliest evidence of [pulleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulley "Pulley") date back to Mesopotamia in the early 2nd millennium BC.

The [screw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screw_(simple_machine) "Screw (simple machine)"), the last of the simple machines to be invented, first appeared in Mesopotamia during the [Neo-Assyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Assyrian "Neo-Assyrian") period (911–609) BC. The Assyrian King [Sennacherib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sennacherib "Sennacherib") (704–681 BC) claims to have invented automatic sluices and to have been the first to use water [screw pumps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screw_pump "Screw pump"), of up to 30 tons weight, which were cast using two-part clay molds rather than by the '[lost wax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost-wax_casting "Lost-wax casting")' process. The Jerwan Aqueduct (c. 688 BC) is made with stone arches and lined with waterproof concrete.

The [Babylonian astronomical diaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonian_astronomical_diaries "Babylonian astronomical diaries") spanned 800 years. They enabled meticulous astronomers to plot the motions of the planets and to predict eclipses.

The earliest evidence of [water wheels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_wheel "Water wheel") and [watermills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watermill "Watermill") date back to the [ancient Near East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Near_East "Ancient Near East") in the 4th century BC, specifically in the [Persian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Empire "Persian Empire") before 350 BC, in the regions of Mesopotamia (Iraq) and [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia "Persia") (Iran). This pioneering use of [water power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_power "Water power") constituted the first human-devised motive force not to rely on muscle power (besides the [sail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sail "Sail")).

#### Egypt

The [Egyptians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptians "Egyptians"), known for building pyramids centuries before the advent of modern tools, invented and used many simple machines, such as the [ramp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclined_plane "Inclined plane"), to aid construction. Historians and archaeologists have found evidence that the [pyramids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramid "Pyramid") were built using three of the [Six Simple Machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_machine "Simple machine"), from which all machines are based. These machines are the [inclined plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclined_plane "Inclined plane"), the [wedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedge "Wedge"), and the [lever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lever "Lever"), which allowed the ancient Egyptians to move millions of limestone blocks which weighed approximately 3.5 tons (7,000 lbs.) each into place to create structures like the [Great Pyramid of Giza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza "Great Pyramid of Giza"), which is 481 feet (147 meters) high.

They also made writing medium similar to paper from [papyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papyrus "Papyrus"), which Joshua Mark states is the foundation for modern paper. Papyrus is a plant (Cyperus papyrus) that grew in plentiful amounts in the Egyptian Delta and throughout the Nile River Valley during ancient times. The papyrus was harvested by field workers and brought to processing centers, where it was cut into thin strips. The strips were then laid out side by side and covered in plant resin. The second layer of strips was laid perpendicular to the first, then both were pressed together until the sheet was dry. The sheets were then joined to form a roll, which was later used for writing.

Egyptian society made several significant advances during the dynastic periods in many areas of technology. According to Hossam Elanzeery, they were the first civilization to use timekeeping devices such as sundials, shadow clocks, and obelisks. They successfully leveraged their knowledge of astronomy to create a calendar model that society still uses today. They developed shipbuilding technology that enabled them to progress from papyrus-reed vessels to cedar-wood ships, while also pioneering the use of rope trusses and stem-mounted rudders. The Egyptians also used their knowledge of anatomy to lay the foundation for many modern medical techniques and to practice the earliest known form of neuroscience. Elanzeery also states that they used advanced mathematical science, as evidenced by the construction of the pyramids.

Ancient Egyptians also pioneered many food technologies that form the basis of modern food processing. Based on paintings and reliefs found in tombs, as well as archaeological artifacts, scholars like Paul T Nicholson believe that the Ancient Egyptians established systematic farming practices, engaged in cereal processing, brewed beer and baked bread, processed meat, practiced viticulture and created the basis for modern wine production, and created condiments to complement, preserve and mask the flavors of their food.

#### Indus Valley

The [Indus Valley Civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilization "Indus Valley Civilization"), situated in a resource-rich region (in modern [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan") and northwestern India), is notable for its early use of city planning, [sanitation technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanitation_of_the_Indus_Valley_Civilisation "Sanitation of the Indus Valley Civilisation"), and plumbing. Indus Valley construction and architecture, called '[Vaastu Shastra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaastu_Shastra "Vaastu Shastra")', suggests a thorough understanding of materials engineering, hydrology, and sanitation.

#### China

The Chinese made many first-known discoveries and developments. Major [technological contributions from China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_science_and_technology_in_China "History of science and technology in China") include the earliest known form of the [binary code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_code "Binary code") and epigenetic sequencing, early [seismological detectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seismometer "Seismometer"), [matches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Match "Match"), paper, [Helicopter rotor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopter_rotor "Helicopter rotor"), [Raised-relief map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raised-relief_map "Raised-relief map"), the double-action piston pump, [cast iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cast_iron "Cast iron"), water powered blast furnace [bellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellows "Bellows"), the iron [plough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plough "Plough"), the multi-tube [seed drill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seed_drill "Seed drill"), the wheelbarrow, the parachute, the [compass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass "Compass"), the [rudder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudder "Rudder"), the [crossbow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossbow "Crossbow"), the [South Pointing Chariot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Pointing_Chariot "South Pointing Chariot") and gunpowder. China also developed deep-well drilling, which it used to extract brine for making salt. Some of these wells, which were as deep as 900 meters, produced natural gas used to evaporate brine.

Other Chinese discoveries and inventions from the medieval period include [block printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_printing "Block printing"),  [movable-type printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movable_type "Movable type"), phosphorescent paint, the endless power [chain drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_drive "Chain drive"), and the clock escapement mechanism. The solid-fuel [rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket "Rocket") was invented in China about 1150, nearly 200 years after the invention of [gunpowder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunpowder "Gunpowder") (which acted as the rocket's fuel). Decades before the West's age of exploration, the Chinese emperors of the [Ming Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_Dynasty "Ming Dynasty") also sent [large fleets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_voyages "Treasure voyages") on maritime voyages, some of which reached Africa.

#### Hellenistic Mediterranean

The [Hellenistic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period "Hellenistic period") of [Mediterranean history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Mediterranean_region "History of the Mediterranean region") began in the 4th century BC with [Alexander's conquests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander%27s_conquests "Alexander's conquests"), which led to the emergence of a [Hellenistic civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_civilization "Hellenistic civilization") representing a synthesis of [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece "Ancient Greece") and [Near-Eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-East "Near-East") cultures in the [Eastern Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Mediterranean "Eastern Mediterranean") region, including the [Balkans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkans "Balkans"), [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant "Levant") and [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt"). With [Ptolemaic Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_Egypt "Ptolemaic Egypt") as its intellectual center and Greek as the lingua franca, the Hellenistic civilization included [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks_in_Egypt "Greeks in Egypt"), [Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptians "Egyptians"), Jewish, [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_people "Persian people"), and [Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia "Phoenicia") scholars and engineers who wrote in Greek.

Hellenistic engineers of the Eastern Mediterranean were responsible for several [inventions and improvements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_technology "Ancient Greek technology") to existing technology. The [Hellenistic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period "Hellenistic period") saw a sharp increase in technological advancement, fostered by a climate of openness to new ideas, the blossoming of a mechanistic philosophy, and the establishment of the [Library of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Alexandria "Library of Alexandria") in [Ptolemaic Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_Egypt "Ptolemaic Egypt") and its close association with the adjacent [museion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museion "Museion"). In contrast to the typically anonymous inventors of earlier ages, ingenious minds such as [Archimedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedes "Archimedes"), [Philo of Byzantium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philo_of_Byzantium "Philo of Byzantium"), [Heron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_of_Alexandria "Hero of Alexandria"), [Ctesibius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ctesibius "Ctesibius"), and [Archytas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archytas "Archytas") remain known by name to posterity.

Ancient agriculture, as in any period before the modern age the primary mode of production and subsistence, and its irrigation methods, were considerably advanced by the invention and widespread application of several previously unknown water-lifting devices, such as the vertical [water-wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water-wheel "Water-wheel"), the compartmented wheel, the water [turbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbine "Turbine"), [Archimedes' screw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedes%27_screw "Archimedes' screw"), the bucket-chain and pot-garland, the [force pump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_pump "Force pump"), the [suction pump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suction_pump "Suction pump"), the double-action [piston pump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piston_pump "Piston pump") and quite possibly the [chain pump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_pump "Chain pump").

In music, the [water organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_organ "Water organ"), invented by Ctesibius and subsequently improved, constituted the earliest instance of a keyboard instrument. In time-keeping, the introduction of the inflow [clepsydra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_clock "Water clock") and its mechanization by the dial and pointer, the application of a [feedback system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feedback "Feedback"), and the [escapement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escapement "Escapement") mechanism far superseded the earlier outflow clepsydra.

Innovations in mechanical technology included the newly devised right-angled [gear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gear "Gear"), which would become particularly important to the operation of mechanical devices. Hellenistic engineers also devised [automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automata "Automata") such as suspended ink pots, automatic [washstands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washstand "Washstand"), and doors, primarily as toys, which, however, featured new useful mechanisms such as the [cam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cam_(mechanism) "Cam (mechanism)") and [gimbals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimbal "Gimbal").

The [Antikythera mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antikythera_mechanism "Antikythera mechanism"), a kind of [analogous computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_computer "Analog computer") working with a [differential gear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_gear "Differential gear"), and the [astrolabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrolabe "Astrolabe") both show great refinement in astronomical science.

In other fields, ancient Greek innovations include the [catapult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catapult "Catapult") and the [gastraphetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastraphetes "Gastraphetes") crossbow in warfare, hollow bronze-casting in metallurgy, the [dioptra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioptra "Dioptra") for surveying, in infrastructure the [lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lighthouse "Lighthouse"), [central heating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_heating "Central heating"), a [tunnel excavated from both ends by scientific calculations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunnel_of_Eupalinos "Tunnel of Eupalinos"), and the [ship trackway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diolkos "Diolkos"). In transport, great progress was made with the invention of the [winch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winch "Winch") and the [odometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odometer "Odometer").

Further newly created techniques and items were [spiral staircases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ancient_spiral_stairs "List of ancient spiral stairs"), the [chain drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_drive "Chain drive"), [sliding calipers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliper "Caliper"), and showers.

#### Roman Empire

The [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire "Roman Empire") expanded from [Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Italy "Roman Italy") across the entire [Mediterranean region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_region "Mediterranean region") between the 1st century BC and 1st century AD. Its most advanced and economically productive provinces outside of Italia were the [Eastern Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Empire "Eastern Roman Empire") provinces in the [Balkans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkans "Balkans"), [Asia Minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia_Minor "Asia Minor"), [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Egypt "Roman Egypt"), and the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant "Levant"), with [Roman Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Egypt "Roman Egypt") in particular being the wealthiest Roman province outside of Italia.

The Roman Empire developed an intensive and sophisticated agriculture, expanded upon existing iron working technology, created [laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_law "Roman law") providing for individual ownership, advanced stone masonry technology, advanced [road-building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_road "Roman road") (exceeded only in the 19th century), military engineering, civil engineering, spinning and weaving and several different machines like the [Gallic reaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaper "Reaper") that helped to increase productivity in many sectors of the Roman economy. [Roman engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Roman_engineering "Ancient Roman engineering") were the first to build monumental arches, [amphitheatres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Roman_amphitheatres "List of Roman amphitheatres"), [aqueducts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_aqueduct "Roman aqueduct"), [public baths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermae "Thermae"), [true arch bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_bridge "Roman bridge"), [harbours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbour "Harbour"), reservoirs and dams, vaults and domes on a very large scale across their Empire. Notable Roman inventions include the [book (Codex)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex "Codex"), [glass blowing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass_blowing "Glass blowing"), and concrete. Because Rome was located on a volcanic peninsula with sand containing suitable crystalline grains, the concrete the Romans formulated was especially durable. Some of their buildings have lasted 2000 years, to the present day.

In Roman Egypt, the inventor [Hero of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_of_Alexandria "Hero of Alexandria") was the first to experiment with a [wind-powered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind-power "Wind-power") mechanical device (see [Heron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_of_Alexandria "Hero of Alexandria")'s windwheel) and even created the earliest [steam-powered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam-power "Steam-power") device (the [aeolipile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeolipile "Aeolipile")), opening up new possibilities in harnessing natural forces. He also devised a [vending machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vending_machine "Vending machine"). However, his inventions were primarily toys, rather than practical machines.

#### Inca, Maya, and Aztec

The engineering skills of the [Inca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca "Inca") and [Maya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_peoples "Maya peoples") were great, even by today's standards. An example of this exceptional engineering is the use of pieces weighing upwards of one ton in their stonework, placed together so that not even a blade can fit into the cracks. Inca villages used irrigation canals and [drainage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage "Drainage") systems, making agriculture very efficient. While some claim that the Incas were the first inventors of [hydroponics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroponics "Hydroponics"), their agricultural technology was still soil-based, if advanced.

Though the [Maya civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_civilization "Maya civilization") did not incorporate metallurgy or wheel technology into their architecture, they developed complex writing and astronomical systems and sculptural works in stone and flint. Like the Inca, the Maya also had command of fairly advanced agricultural and construction technology. The Maya are also responsible for creating the first pressurized water system in Mesoamerica, located in the Maya site of [Palenque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palenque "Palenque").

The main contribution of the [Aztec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec "Aztec") rule was a system of communication between the conquered cities and the widespread use of the ingenious agricultural technology of [chinampas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinampas "Chinampas"). In [Mesoamerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica "Mesoamerica"), without draft animals for transport (nor, as a result, wheeled vehicles), the roads were designed for travel on foot, just as in the Inca and Mayan civilizations. The Aztec, subsequently to the Maya, inherited many of the technologies and intellectual advancements of their predecessors: the [Olmec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olmec "Olmec") (see [Native American inventions and innovations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_inventions_and_innovations "Native American inventions and innovations")).

### Medieval to early modern

One of the most significant developments of the medieval period was the rise of economies in which water and wind power were more important than animal and human muscle power. Most water and wind power was used for milling grain. Water power was also used to blow air into [blast furnace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blast_furnace "Blast furnace"), to pulp rags for papermaking, and to felt wool. The [Domesday Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domesday_Book "Domesday Book") recorded 5,624 water mills in Great Britain in 1086, being about one per thirty families.

#### Islamic world

The Muslim [caliphates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate "Caliphate") united large areas that had previously traded little with one another, including the Middle East, North Africa, Central Asia, the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula "Iberian Peninsula"), and parts of the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent"). The science and technology of earlier empires in the region, including the Mesopotamian, Egyptian, Persian, Hellenistic, and Roman empires, were inherited by the [Muslim world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_world "Muslim world"), where Arabic replaced Syriac, Persian, and Greek as the region's lingua franca. Significant advances were made in the region during the [Islamic Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Golden_Age "Islamic Golden Age") (8th–16th centuries).

The [Arab Agricultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Agricultural_Revolution "Arab Agricultural Revolution") occurred during this period. It was a transformation in agriculture from the [8th to the 13th century in the Islamic region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Age_of_Islam "Golden Age of Islam") of the [Old World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World "Old World"). The economy established by [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab "Arab") and other [Muslim traders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_economics_in_the_world "Islamic economics in the world") across the Old World enabled the diffusion of many crops and farming techniques throughout the Islamic world, as well as the adaptation of crops and techniques from and to regions outside it. Advances were made in [animal husbandry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_husbandry "Animal husbandry"), [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation "Irrigation"), and farming, with the help of new technology such as the [windmill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windmill "Windmill"). These changes made agriculture much more productive, supporting population growth, urbanisation, and greater social stratification.

Muslim engineers in the Islamic world made extensive use of [hydropower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydropower "Hydropower"), as well as early uses of [tidal power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_power "Tidal power") and [wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power "Wind power"). [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel "Fossil fuel") such as petroleum, and large factory complexes (*tiraz* in Arabic). A variety of industrial mills were employed in the Islamic world, including [fulling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulling "Fulling") mills, [gristmills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gristmill "Gristmill"), [hullers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_huller "Rice huller"), [sawmills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawmill "Sawmill"), [ship mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_mill "Ship mill"), [stamp mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamp_mill "Stamp mill"), [steel mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_mill "Steel mill"), and [tide mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tide_mill "Tide mill"). By the 11th century, every province throughout the Islamic world had these industrial mills in operation. Muslim engineers also employed [water turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_turbine "Water turbine") and [gears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gear "Gear") in mills and water-raising machines, and pioneered the use of [dams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dams "Dams") as a source of water power, used to provide additional power to [watermills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watermill "Watermill") and water-raising machines. Many of these technologies were transferred to medieval Europe.

[Wind-powered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind-power "Wind-power") machines used to grind grain and pump water, the windmill and [wind pump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_pump "Wind pump"), first appeared in what are now [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran"), [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan"), and Pakistan by the 9th century. They were used to grind grains and draw up water, and used in the gristmilling and sugarcane industries. [Sugar mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane_mill "Sugarcane mill") first appeared in the [medieval Islamic world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Golden_Age "Islamic Golden Age"). They were first driven by watermills, and then windmills from the 9th and 10th centuries in what are today [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan"), Pakistan and [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran"). Crops such as [almonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almond "Almond") and [citrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrus "Citrus") fruit were brought to Europe through [Al-Andalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus "Al-Andalus"), and sugar cultivation was gradually adopted across Europe. Arab merchants dominated trade in the Indian Ocean until the arrival of the Portuguese in the 16th century.

The Muslim world adopted [papermaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papermaking "Papermaking") from China. The earliest [paper mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper_mill "Paper mill") appeared in [Abbasid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Dynasty "Abbasid Dynasty")-era [Baghdad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdad "Baghdad") during 794–795. The knowledge of [gunpowder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunpowder "Gunpowder") was also transmitted from China via predominantly Islamic countries, where formulas for pure [potassium nitrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium_nitrate "Potassium nitrate") were developed.

The [spinning wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinning_wheel "Spinning wheel") was invented in the [Islamic world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_world "Islamic world") by the early 11th century. It was later widely adopted in Europe, where it was adapted into the [spinning jenny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinning_jenny "Spinning jenny"), a key device during the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution "Industrial Revolution"). The [crankshaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crankshaft "Crankshaft") was invented by [Al-Jazari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Jazari "Al-Jazari") in 1206,[*[page range too broad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources "Wikipedia:Citing sources")*] and is central to modern machinery such as the [steam engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_engine "Steam engine"), [internal combustion engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_combustion_engine "Internal combustion engine") and [automatic controls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_control "Automatic control"). The [camshaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camshaft "Camshaft") was also first described by Al-Jazari in 1206.

Early [programmable machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Program_(machine) "Program (machine)") were also invented in the Muslim world. The first [music sequencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_sequencer "Music sequencer"), a programmable [musical instrument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_instrument "Musical instrument"), was an automated flute player invented by the [Banu Musa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banu_Musa "Banu Musa") brothers and described in their *[Book of Ingenious Devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Ingenious_Devices "Book of Ingenious Devices")* in the 9th century. In 1206, Al-Jazari invented programmable [automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automata "Automata")/[robots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robot "Robot"). He described four [automaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automaton "Automaton") musicians, including two drummers operated by a programmable [drum machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drum_machine "Drum machine"), each capable of playing different rhythms and drum patterns. The [castle clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_clock "Castle clock"), a [hydropowered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydropower "Hydropower") mechanical [astronomical clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_clock "Astronomical clock") invented by Al-Jazari, was an early [programmable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_programming "Computer programming") [analog computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_computer "Analog computer").

In the [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire "Ottoman Empire"), a practical impulse [steam turbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_turbine "Steam turbine") was invented in 1551 by [Taqi ad-Din Muhammad ibn Ma'ruf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taqi_ad-Din_Muhammad_ibn_Ma%27ruf "Taqi ad-Din Muhammad ibn Ma'ruf") in [Ottoman Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Egypt "Ottoman Egypt"). He described a method for rotating a [spit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotisserie "Rotisserie") using a jet of steam acting on rotary vanes around the periphery of a wheel. Known as a [steam jack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_jack "Steam jack"), a similar device for rotating a spit was also later described by [John Wilkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wilkins "John Wilkins") in 1648.

#### Medieval Europe

While medieval technology has long been depicted as a step backward in the evolution of Western technology, a generation of medievalists (such as the American historian of science [Lynn White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynn_Townsend_White_Jr. "Lynn Townsend White Jr.")) stressed, from the 1940s onwards, the innovative character of many medieval techniques. Genuine medieval contributions include, for example, [mechanical clocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_clock "Mechanical clock"), [spectacles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectacles "Spectacles"), and vertical [windmills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windmill "Windmill"). Medieval ingenuity was also displayed in the invention of seemingly inconspicuous items such as the [watermark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watermark "Watermark") and the [functional button](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Push_button "Push button"). In navigation, the foundation to the subsequent [Age of Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery "Age of Discovery") was laid by the introduction of pintle-and-gudgeon [rudders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudder "Rudder"), [lateen sails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateen_sail "Lateen sail"), the [dry compass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_compass "Dry compass"), the horseshoe, and the [astrolabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrolabe "Astrolabe").

Significant advances were also made in military technology with the development of [plate armour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plate_armour "Plate armour"), steel [crossbows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossbow "Crossbow") and [cannons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannon "Cannon"). The Middle Ages are perhaps best known for their architectural heritage: While the invention of the [rib vault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rib_vault "Rib vault") and [pointed arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arch "Arch") gave rise to the high rising [Gothic style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture "Gothic architecture"), the ubiquitous medieval fortifications gave the era the almost proverbial title of the 'age of castles'.

[Papermaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papermaking "Papermaking"), a 2nd-century Chinese technology, was carried to the Middle East when a group of Chinese papermakers was captured in the 8th century. Papermaking technology was spread to Europe by the [Umayyad conquest of Hispania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_conquest_of_Hispania "Umayyad conquest of Hispania"). A paper mill was established in Sicily in the 12th century. In Europe, the fiber to make pulp for making paper was obtained from linen and cotton rags. [Lynn Townsend White Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynn_Townsend_White_Jr. "Lynn Townsend White Jr.") credited the spinning wheel with increasing the supply of rags, which led to cheap paper, which was a factor in the development of printing.

#### Renaissance technology

Before the development of modern engineering, mathematics was used by artisans and craftsmen, such as [millwrights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millwright "Millwright"), clock makers, instrument makers, and surveyors. Aside from these professions, universities were not believed to have had much practical significance to technology.

A standard reference for the state of the mechanical arts during the Renaissance is the mining engineering treatise *[De re metallica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_re_metallica "De re metallica")* (1556), which also contains sections on geology, mining, and chemistry. *De re metallica* was the standard chemistry reference for the next 180 years. Among the water-powered mechanical devices in use were [ore stamping mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamp_mill "Stamp mill"), forge hammers, blast bellows, and suction pumps.

Due to the casting of cannon, the [blast furnace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blast_furnace "Blast furnace") came into widespread use in France in the mid-15th century. The blast furnace had been used in China since the 4th century BC.

The invention of the movable cast metal type [printing press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing_press "Printing press"), whose pressing mechanism was adapted from an olive screw press, (c. 1441) lead to a tremendous increase in the number of books and the number of titles published. Movable ceramic type had been used in China for a few centuries and woodblock printing dated back even further.

The era is marked by such profound technical advancements as [linear perspective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perspective_(graphical) "Perspective (graphical)"),  [double-shell domes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_del_Fiore "Santa Maria del Fiore"), and [Bastion fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastion_fortress "Bastion fortress"). Notebooks of the Renaissance artist-engineers such as [Taccola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taccola "Taccola") and [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci "Leonardo da Vinci") give a deep insight into the mechanical technology then known and applied. Architects and engineers were inspired by the structures of [Ancient Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome "Ancient Rome"), and men like [Brunelleschi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filippo_Brunelleschi "Filippo Brunelleschi") created the large dome of [Florence Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Cathedral "Florence Cathedral") as a result. He was awarded one of the first patents ever issued to protect an ingenious [crane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crane_(machine) "Crane (machine)") he designed to raise the large masonry stones to the top of the structure. Military technology developed rapidly with the widespread use of the [cross-bow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-bow "Cross-bow") and ever more powerful [artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artillery "Artillery"), as the city-states of Italy were usually in conflict with one another. Powerful families like the [Medici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medici "Medici") were strong patrons of the arts and sciences. [Renaissance science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_science_in_the_Renaissance "History of science in the Renaissance") spawned the [Scientific Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_Revolution "Scientific Revolution"); science and technology began a cycle of mutual advancement.

#### Age of Exploration

An improved sailing ship, the nau or [carrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrack "Carrack"), enabled the [Age of Exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Exploration "Age of Exploration") with the [European colonization of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas "European colonization of the Americas"), epitomized by [Francis Bacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Bacon_(philosopher) "Francis Bacon (philosopher)")'s *[New Atlantis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Atlantis "New Atlantis")*. Pioneers like [Vasco da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_da_Gama "Vasco da Gama"), [Cabral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_%C3%81lvares_Cabral "Pedro Álvares Cabral"), [Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Magellan "Ferdinand Magellan"), and [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus "Christopher Columbus") explored the world in search of new trade routes for their goods and contacts with Africa, India, and China to shorten the journey compared with traditional routes overland. They produced new maps and charts which enabled mariners to explore further with greater confidence. Navigation was generally difficult, however, owing to the [problem of longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_longitude "History of longitude") and the absence of accurate [chronometers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_chronometer "Marine chronometer"). European powers rediscovered the idea of the [civil code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_code "Civil code"), which had been lost since the time of the Ancient Greeks.

#### Pre–Industrial Revolution

The [stocking frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stocking_frame "Stocking frame"), which was invented in 1598, increased a knitter's number of knots per minute from 100 to 1000.

Mines were becoming increasingly deep and expensive to drain with horse-powered bucket-and-chain pumps and wooden piston pumps. Some mines used as many as 500 horses. Horse-powered pumps were replaced by the [Savery steam pump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Savery#Steam-powered_pump "Thomas Savery") (1698) and the [Newcomen steam engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcomen_atmospheric_engine "Newcomen atmospheric engine") (1712).

### Industrial Revolution (1760–1830s)

The revolution was driven by cheap energy in the form of coal, produced in ever-increasing amounts from the abundant resources of [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain "Great Britain"). The British [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution "Industrial Revolution") is characterized by developments in textile machinery, mining, [metallurgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgy "Metallurgy"), transport, and the invention of [machine tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_tool "Machine tool").

Before the invention of machinery to spin yarn and weave cloth, spinning was done using the spinning wheel, and weaving was done on a hand-and-foot-operated loom. It took from three to five spinners to supply one weaver. The invention of the [flying shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_shuttle "Flying shuttle") in 1733 doubled the output of a weaver, creating a shortage of spinners. The [spinning frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinning_frame "Spinning frame") for wool was invented in 1738. The [spinning jenny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinning_jenny "Spinning jenny"), invented in 1764, was a machine that used multiple spinning wheels; however, it produced low-quality thread. The [water frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_frame "Water frame") patented by Richard Arkwright in 1767 produced a better quality thread than the spinning jenny. The [spinning mule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinning_mule "Spinning mule"), patented in 1779 by [Samuel Crompton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Crompton "Samuel Crompton"), produced a high-quality thread. The [power loom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_loom "Power loom") was invented by Edmund Cartwright in 1787.

In the mid-1750s, the [steam engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_engine "Steam engine") was applied to the water-power-constrained iron, copper, and lead industries to power blast bellows. These industries were located near the mines, some of which used steam engines for pumping. Steam engines were too powerful for leather bellows, so cast-iron blowing cylinders were developed in 1768. Steam-powered blast furnaces reached higher temperatures, allowing the use of more lime in the iron blast-furnace feed. (Lime-rich slag was not free-flowing at the previously used temperatures.) With a sufficient lime ratio, sulfur from coal or coke fuel reacts with the slag so that the sulfur does not contaminate the iron. Coal and coke were cheaper and more abundant fuels. As a result, iron production rose significantly during the last decades of the 18th century. Coal converted to [coke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coke_(fuel) "Coke (fuel)") fueled higher temperature [blast furnaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blast_furnace "Blast furnace") and produced cast iron in much larger amounts than before, allowing the creation of a range of structures such as [The Iron Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Iron_Bridge "The Iron Bridge"). Cheap coal meant that industry was no longer constrained by water resources that drove the mills, although water remained a valuable source of power.

The steam engine helped drain the mines, allowing access to more coal reserves and increasing coal output. The development of the high-pressure steam engine made locomotives possible; a transport revolution followed. The steam engine which had existed since the early 18th century, was practically applied to both [steamboat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steamboat "Steamboat") and railway transportation. The [Liverpool and Manchester Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_and_Manchester_Railway "Liverpool and Manchester Railway"), the first purpose-built railway line, opened in 1830, with the [Rocket locomotive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_locomotive "Rocket locomotive") of [Robert Stephenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Stephenson "Robert Stephenson") among its first working [locomotives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_locomotive "Steam locomotive").

Manufacture of ships' pulley [blocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_and_tackle "Block and tackle") by all-metal machines at the [Portsmouth Block Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Block_Mills "Portsmouth Block Mills") in 1803 instigated the age of sustained [mass production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_production "Mass production"). [Machine tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_tools "Machine tools") used by engineers to manufacture parts began in the first decade of the century, notably by [Richard Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Roberts_(engineer) "Richard Roberts (engineer)") and [Joseph Whitworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Whitworth "Joseph Whitworth"). The development of [interchangeable parts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interchangeable_parts "Interchangeable parts") through what is now called the [American system of manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_system_of_manufacturing "American system of manufacturing") began in the firearms industry at the U.S. Federal arsenals in the early 19th century; it became widely used by the end of the century.

Until the [Enlightenment era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlightenment_era "Enlightenment era"), little progress was made in [water supply and sanitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_water_supply_and_sanitation "History of water supply and sanitation"), and the engineering skills of the Romans were largely neglected throughout Europe. The first documented use of [sand filters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand_filter "Sand filter") to purify the water supply dates to 1804, when the owner of a bleachery in [Paisley, Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paisley,_Scotland "Paisley, Scotland"), John Gibb, installed an experimental filter and sold his unwanted surplus to the public. The first treated public water supply in the world was installed by engineer [James Simpson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Simpson_(engineer) "James Simpson (engineer)") for the [Chelsea Waterworks Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_Waterworks_Company "Chelsea Waterworks Company") in London in 1829. The first screw-down [water tap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tap_(valve) "Tap (valve)") was patented in 1845 by Guest and Chrimes, a brass foundry in [Rotherham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotherham "Rotherham"). The practice of water treatment soon became mainstream, and the virtues of the system were made starkly apparent after the investigations of the physician [John Snow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Snow_(physician) "John Snow (physician)") during the [1854 Broad Street cholera outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1854_Broad_Street_cholera_outbreak "1854 Broad Street cholera outbreak") demonstrated the role of the water supply in spreading the cholera epidemic.

### Second Industrial Revolution (1860s–1914)

The 19th century saw astonishing developments in transportation, construction, manufacturing, and communication technologies originating in Europe. After a recession at the end of the 1830s and a general slowdown in major inventions, the [Second Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Industrial_Revolution "Second Industrial Revolution") was a period of rapid innovation and industrialization that began in the 1860s, around 1870, and lasted until [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"). It included the rapid development of chemical, electrical, petroleum, and steel technologies, connected to highly structured technological research.

[Telegraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telegraphy "Telegraphy") developed into a practical technology in the 19th century to help run the railways safely. Along with the development of telegraphy was the patenting of the first telephone. March 1876 marks the date that Alexander Graham Bell officially patented his version of an "electric telegraph". Although Bell is noted with the creation of the telephone, it is still debated about who actually developed the first working model.

Building on improvements in vacuum pumps and materials research, [incandescent light bulbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incandescent_light_bulb "Incandescent light bulb") became practical for general use in the late 1870s. Edison Electric Illuminating Company, founded by Thomas Edison with financial backing from [Spencer Trask](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spencer_Trask "Spencer Trask"), built and managed the first electric power network. Electrification was rated the most important technical development of the 20th century as the foundational infrastructure for modern civilization. This invention had a profound effect on the workplace because factories could now have second and third shift workers.

Shoe production was mechanized during the mid 19th century. Mass production of [sewing machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewing_machine "Sewing machine") and [agricultural machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_machinery "Agricultural machinery") such as reapers occurred in the mid to late 19th century. Bicycles were mass-produced beginning in the 1880s.

Steam-powered factories became widespread, although the conversion from water power to steam occurred in England earlier than in the U.S. [Ironclad warships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironclad_warship "Ironclad warship") were found in battle starting in the 1860s, and played a role in the opening of Japan and China to trade with the West.

Between 1825 and 1840, [photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photography "Photography") was introduced. For much of the rest of the century, many engineers and inventors tried to combine it and the much older technique of [projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projector "Projector") to create a complete illusion or a complete documentation of reality. Color photography was usually included in these ambitions, and the introduction of the [phonograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonograph "Phonograph") in 1877 seemed to promise the addition of [synchronized sound recordings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_film "Sound film"). Between 1887 and 1894, the first successful short [cinematographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinematography "Cinematography") presentations were established.

### 20th century

[Mass production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_production "Mass production") brought [automobiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile "Automobile") and other high-tech goods to the masses of consumers. [Military research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_research "Military research") and development sped advances including electronic [computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computing "Computing") and [jet engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_engine "Jet engine"). Radio and [telephony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephony "Telephony") greatly improved and expanded their reach to larger user populations. However, near-universal access would not be possible until [mobile phones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phones "Mobile phones") became affordable to residents of the [developing world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_world "Developing world") in the late 2000s and early 2010s.

Energy and engine technology improvements included [nuclear power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power "Nuclear power"), developed after the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project "Manhattan Project"), which heralded the new [Atomic Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_Age "Atomic Age"). [Rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket "Rocket") development led to long range missiles and the first [space age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_age "Space age") that lasted from the 1950s with the launch of Sputnik to the mid-1980s.

[Electrification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrification "Electrification") spread rapidly in the 20th century. At the beginning of the century, electric power was, for the most part, only available to wealthy people in a few major cities. By 2019, an estimated 87 percent of the world's population had access to electricity.

[Birth control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_control "Birth control") also became widespread during the 20th century. [Electron microscopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_microscopes "Electron microscopes") were very powerful by the late 1970s, and genetic theory and knowledge were expanding, leading to developments in [genetic engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_engineering "Genetic engineering").

The first "[test tube baby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_tube_baby "Test tube baby")" [Louise Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louise_Brown "Louise Brown") was born in 1978, which led to the first successful [gestational surrogacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestational_surrogacy "Gestational surrogacy") pregnancy in 1985 and the first pregnancy by [ICSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intracytoplasmic_sperm_injection "Intracytoplasmic sperm injection") in 1991, which is the implanting of a single sperm into an egg. [Preimplantation genetic diagnosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preimplantation_genetic_diagnosis "Preimplantation genetic diagnosis") was first performed in late 1989 and led to successful births in July 1990. These procedures have become relatively common.

Computers were connected by means of local area, [telecom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_engineering "Telecommunications engineering") and [fiber optic networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber-optic_network "Fiber-optic network"), powered by the [optical amplifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_amplifier "Optical amplifier") that ushered in the [Information Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_Age "Information Age"). This [optical networking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_networking "Optical networking") technology exploded the capacity of the Internet beginning in 1996 with the launch of the first high-capacity [wave division multiplexing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavelength-division_multiplexing "Wavelength-division multiplexing") (WDM) system by [Ciena Corp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciena "Ciena"). WDM, as the common basis for telecom backbone networks, increased transmission capacity by orders of magnitude, thus enabling the mass commercialization and popularization of the Internet and its widespread impact on culture, economics, business, and society.

The commercial availability of the first portable cell phone in 1981 and the first pocket-sized phone in 1985, both developed by Comvik in Sweden, coupled with the first transmission of data over a cellular network by [Vodafone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vodafone "Vodafone") (formerly [Racal-Millicom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racal-Millicom "Racal-Millicom")) in 1992, were the breakthroughs that led directly to the form and function of smartphones today. By 2014, the global total of mobile-cellular subscriptions had nearly reached parity with the world's human population and the Supreme Court of the United States of America has ruled that a mobile phone was a private part of a person. Providing consumers with wireless access to each other and to the Internet, the mobile phone stimulated one of the most important technology revolutions in human history.

The Human Genome Project sequenced and identified all three billion chemical units in human DNA to find the genetic roots of disease and develop treatments. The project became feasible due to two technical advances in the late 1970s: gene mapping using restriction fragment length polymorphism (RFLP) markers and DNA sequencing. Sequencing was invented by Frederick Sanger and, separately, by Dr. Walter Gilbert. Gilbert also conceived of the Human Genome Project on May 27, 1985, and first publicly advocated it at the first International Conference on Genes and Computers in August 1985. The U.S. Federal Government sponsored the Human Genome Project, which began on October 1, 1990, and was declared complete in 2003.

The massive data analysis resources necessary for running transatlantic research programs such as the [Human Genome Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Genome_Project "Human Genome Project") and the [Large Electron–Positron Collider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_Electron%E2%80%93Positron_Collider "Large Electron–Positron Collider") led to a necessity for distributed communications, causing Internet protocols to be more widely adopted by researchers and also creating a justification for [Tim Berners-Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Berners-Lee "Tim Berners-Lee") to create the [World Wide Web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Web "World Wide Web").

[Vaccination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaccination "Vaccination") spread rapidly to the developing world from the 1980s onward due to many successful humanitarian initiatives, greatly reducing childhood mortality in many poor countries with limited medical resources.

### 21st century

In the early 21st century, research is ongoing into [quantum computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_computers "Quantum computers"), [gene therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_therapy "Gene therapy") (introduced 1990), [3D printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_printing "3D printing") (introduced 1981), [nanotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanotechnology "Nanotechnology") (introduced 1985), [bioengineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioengineering "Bioengineering")/[biotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotechnology "Biotechnology"), [nuclear technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_technology "Nuclear technology"), [advanced materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materials_science "Materials science") (e.g., graphene), the [scramjet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scramjet "Scramjet") and [drones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmanned_combat_air_vehicle "Unmanned combat air vehicle") (along with [railguns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railgun "Railgun") and high-energy laser beams for military uses), [superconductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity "Superconductivity"), the [memristor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memristor "Memristor"), and green technologies such as [alternative fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_fuel "Alternative fuel") (e.g., [fuel cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_cells "Fuel cells"), self-driving electric and [plug-in hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plug-in_hybrid "Plug-in hybrid") cars), [augmented reality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augmented_reality "Augmented reality") devices and [wearable electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wearable_electronics "Wearable electronics"), [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence"), and more efficient and powerful [LEDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LEDs "LEDs"), [solar cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_cells "Solar cells"), [integrated circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuits "Integrated circuits"), [wireless power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireless_power "Wireless power") devices, engines, and [batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_(electricity) "Battery (electricity)").

[Large Hadron Collider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_Hadron_Collider "Large Hadron Collider"), the largest single machine ever built, was constructed between 1998 and 2008. The understanding of [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics "Particle physics") is expected to expand with better instruments including larger [particle accelerators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_accelerator "Particle accelerator") such as the LHC and better [neutrino detectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrino_detector "Neutrino detector"). [Dark matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_matter "Dark matter") is sought via underground detectors and observatories like [LIGO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LIGO "LIGO") have started to detect [gravitational waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_wave "Gravitational wave").

Genetic engineering technology continues to improve, and the importance of [epigenetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigenetics "Epigenetics") on development and inheritance has also become increasingly recognized.

New [spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceflight "Spaceflight") technology and [spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacecraft "Spacecraft") are also being developed, like the Boeing's [Orion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orion_(spacecraft) "Orion (spacecraft)") and SpaceX's [Dragon 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Dragon_2 "SpaceX Dragon 2"). New, more capable [space telescopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_observatory "Space observatory"), such as the [James Webb Space Telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope "James Webb Space Telescope"), which was launched to orbit in December, 2021, and the [Extremely Large Telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremely_Large_Telescope "Extremely Large Telescope"), have been designed. The [International Space Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station "International Space Station") was completed in the 2000s, and [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA") and [ESA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESA "ESA") plan a [human mission to Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_mission_to_Mars "Human mission to Mars") in the 2030s. The [Variable Specific Impulse Magnetoplasma Rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variable_Specific_Impulse_Magnetoplasma_Rocket "Variable Specific Impulse Magnetoplasma Rocket") (VASIMR) is an electro-magnetic thruster for spacecraft propulsion and is expected to be tested in 2015.[*[needs update](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")*]

The [Breakthrough Initiatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Initiatives "Breakthrough Initiatives") project plans to send [the first ever spacecraft to visit another star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Starshot "Breakthrough Starshot"), which will consist of numerous super-light chips driven by [Electric propulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_propulsion "Electric propulsion") in the 2030s, and receive images of the [Proxima Centauri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proxima_Centauri "Proxima Centauri") system, along with, possibly, the [potentially habitable planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_potentially_habitable_exoplanets "List of potentially habitable exoplanets") [Proxima Centauri b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proxima_Centauri_b "Proxima Centauri b"), by midcentury.

2004 saw the [first crewed commercial spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceShipOne_flight_15P "SpaceShipOne flight 15P") when [Mike Melvill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Melvill "Mike Melvill") crossed the [boundary of space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_of_space "Boundary of space") on June 21, 2004.
