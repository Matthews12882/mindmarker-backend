# Stella Lefty

**Stella Lefkofsky** (born August 13, 2002), better known by her stage name **Stella Lefty**, is an American [singer-songwriter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singer-songwriter "Singer-songwriter"). Her 2026 [country-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_pop "Country pop") single "[Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_(Stella_Lefty_song) "Boston (Stella Lefty song)")" peaked at number two on the US [*Billboard* Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100 "Billboard Hot 100").

## Early life and education

Stella Lefkofsky was born on August 13, 2002, and was raised in [Glencoe, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glencoe,_Illinois "Glencoe, Illinois"). Her father is billionaire businessman [Eric Lefkofsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Lefkofsky "Eric Lefkofsky"), a co-founder of [Groupon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groupon "Groupon") and [Mediaocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediaocean "Mediaocean") and the founder and CEO of [Tempus AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempus_AI "Tempus AI").

Lefty started writing songs and taking guitar lessons as a child but initially did not perform publicly because of her stage fright. She grew up listening mostly to country and pop musicians, including [Taylor Swift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift "Taylor Swift"), [Miley Cyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miley_Cyrus "Miley Cyrus"), [Tim McGraw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_McGraw "Tim McGraw"), and [Brad Paisley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Paisley "Brad Paisley"). After graduating from [New Trier High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Trier_High_School "New Trier High School"), she attended [Tulane University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulane_University "Tulane University") in 2020 to study [public health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health "Public health"), with "no plan of doing anything in music". Lefty graduated from Tulane University in 2024. She is a sister of the [Pi Beta Phi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pi_Beta_Phi "Pi Beta Phi").[*[better source needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")*] While studying abroad in [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam "Amsterdam"), she began sharing her earliest song compositions on [TikTok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TikTok "TikTok").

## Career

In 2022, Lefty began posting videos of her singing song covers and original songs to her [TikTok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TikTok "TikTok") account. In 2024, she released her debut single "Kiss Me", which gained popularity on TikTok. Record producer [J Kash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Kasher_Hindlin "Jacob Kasher Hindlin") soon messaged her online and the two began collaborating after she moved to [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles"). She released her debut [extended play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_play "Extended play") (EP) *Tragic, Really*, in 2025. In January 2026, she released the single "Thinkin' Bout You", which she wrote in collaboration with friends Grace Enger and [Sadie Jean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadie_Jean "Sadie Jean"). Her song "The Kill" was released in February of that year as part of the soundtrack for the film *[Scream 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scream_7 "Scream 7")*. Shortly thereafter, she signed with [Atlantic Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Records "Atlantic Records")' country music imprint, Atlantic Outpost.

In 2026 her [country pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_pop "Country pop") single "[Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_(Stella_Lefty_song) "Boston (Stella Lefty song)")" debuted on the [*Billboard* Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100 "Billboard Hot 100") chart at number 95 in April and climbed to the top-20 a month later. In May 2026, she released her second EP, *[Is This Heaven?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Is_This_Heaven%3F "Is This Heaven?")*, which featured the previously-released songs "Boston", "Thinking 'Bout You", and "I Know I Know" and a collaboration with [Vincent Mason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Mason_(singer) "Vincent Mason (singer)"), "Something to Lose". *Is This Heaven?* debuted at number 38 on the [*Billboard* 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200") chart and at number nine on the [Top Country Albums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Country_Albums "Top Country Albums") chart with 20,000 units sold.

In March 2026, it was announced that Lefty would perform as an opening act for [Tucker Wetmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Wetmore "Tucker Wetmore")'s Brunette World Tour. Lefty quietly dropped out of those dates without explanation before posting an apology to her [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram "Instagram") stories for having not announced her departure from the tour earlier.

Lefty was nominated for [Best New Artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Award_for_Best_New_Artist "MTV Video Music Award for Best New Artist") and [Best Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Award_for_Best_Country "MTV Video Music Award for Best Country") (for "Boston") at the [2026 MTV Video Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_MTV_Video_Music_Awards "2026 MTV Video Music Awards"). Her debut studio album *[Long Way Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Way_Home_(Stella_Lefty_album) "Long Way Home (Stella Lefty album)")* was released on August 21, 2026. For *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")*, Laura Snapes gave the album two out of five stars, calling Lefty's songwriting on it "stunningly unremarkable" and the album overall "indistinct and trope-ridden", while Abby Jones of *[Paste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paste_(magazine) "Paste (magazine)")* gave it a "D-" rating for its "tragically nearsighted" perspective, "painfully unimaginative" formula, and "unremarkable instrumentals". Lefty announced her [Long Way Home Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Way_Home_(Stella_Lefty_album)#Long_Way_Home_Tour "Long Way Home (Stella Lefty album)"), set to begin in January 2027.

Lefty performed at the [Grand Ole Opry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Ole_Opry "Grand Ole Opry") for the first time in September 2026. The same month, it was reported that she had parted with [Disruptor Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disruptor_Records "Disruptor Records"), which she had been in partnership with "since the beginning of her career."

On October 23, 2026, Lefty and [Bailey Zimmerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bailey_Zimmerman "Bailey Zimmerman") are set to release a song together entitled "My Kinda Love" for the soundtrack of the third season of [Nobody Wants This](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobody_Wants_This "Nobody Wants This").

## Musical style

Lefty's music has been described as country, with her describing the genre as "what I love with my fucking heart and soul" while expressing ambivalence toward performing it despite not being from the South. She has described her voice as raspy. For *[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker "The New Yorker")*, Brady Brickner-Wood described Lefty's singing voice as an affected "raspy Southern twang" and her songs as "projecting the uncomplicated joy of a wide-eyed college graduate who goes to the bar on the weekends and falls in love at first sight".

## Personal life

As of July 2026[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Stella_Lefty&action=edit), Lefty is in a relationship with fellow singer-songwriter [Vincent Mason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Mason_(singer) "Vincent Mason (singer)").

## Public image

Upon her rise to fame in mid-2026, Lefty was widely criticized online as both a "[nepo baby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepo_baby "Nepo baby")" and an "[industry plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industry_plant "Industry plant")" due to her father's billionaire status, with many claiming that the opportunities she had been given were solely a result of her family's wealth and that the success of her song "Boston" had been manufactured. [Adam Alpert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Alpert "Adam Alpert"), Lefty's manager and the founder of [Disruptor Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disruptor_Records "Disruptor Records"), stated in response to the criticisms that "industry plants don't exist" and that those who were deemed as such had just made "great songs that people like".

### Interpolations and plagiarism accusations

Social media users and critics have denounced Lefty for releasing songs that sound similar to the work of other artists without providing proper credit, coining the name "Stella Thefty" in response to the appropriation.

Lefty was accused online of plagiarizing the melody and structure of [Noah Kahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah_Kahan "Noah Kahan")'s 2022 song "[Stick Season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stick_Season_(song) "Stick Season (song)")" to make "Boston", with Miranda Wollen of *Paste* deriding the latter as a "poor imitation" of the former and dubbing Lefty a "Noah Kahan copycat". Kahan received a writing credit on the song after members of his team reached out to her to note the similarities between the two songs, though she stated that she "wasn't thinking about Noah Kahan" while writing it, which critics and social media users expressed disbelief at and contrasted with her numerous covers of "Stick Season" on TikTok.

Lefty's song "Summer You Were Mine", also from *Long Way Home*, interpolates [the Chicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Chicks "The Chicks")' song "[There's Your Trouble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/There%27s_Your_Trouble "There's Your Trouble")", which briefly received backlash when its writers, [Tia Sillers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tia_Sillers "Tia Sillers") and [Mark Selby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Selby_(musician) "Mark Selby (musician)"), were uncredited on streaming platforms. Atlantic attributed the omission to a technical error and confirmed that permission had been received to interpolate the song prior to its release. The album's closing song "Why Wouldn't We" was described online as copying the melody and call-and-response lyrics from [Blake Shelton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blake_Shelton "Blake Shelton")'s 2017 song "[I'll Name the Dogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%27ll_Name_the_Dogs "I'll Name the Dogs")". She faced further accusations that the chorus of her song "Misery", from *Tragic, Really*, was musically similar to the chorus of "[A Million Dreams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Million_Dreams "A Million Dreams")" from the 2017 musical film *[The Greatest Showman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Greatest_Showman "The Greatest Showman")* and that the verses of her standalone single "Seven Eleven" used nearly the same melody as [Wheatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheatus "Wheatus")'s "[Teenage Dirtbag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teenage_Dirtbag "Teenage Dirtbag")" (which she had also previously covered). Abby Jones, writing for [*Paste*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paste_(magazine) "Paste (magazine)"), found resemblances between Lefty's song "Good at Leaving", from *Long Way Home*, and the [Justin Bieber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Bieber "Justin Bieber") song "[Ghost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_(Justin_Bieber_song) "Ghost (Justin Bieber song)")". As of September 2026, none of Lefty's aforementioned work credits songwriters outside of her writing team.
