# Sylvester Stallone

**Sylvester Gardenzio Stallone** (born July 6, 1946) is an American actor and filmmaker. Known for his raw portrayals of vulnerable, working-class characters, he is considered a leading actor of the 1980s. Stallone has earned [numerous accolades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Sylvester_Stallone "List of awards and nominations received by Sylvester Stallone") over a 58-year career, including a [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globes "Golden Globes") and a [Critics' Choice Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Awards "Critics' Choice Awards"), and has been nominated for three [Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards") and two [BAFTA Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Awards "British Academy Film Awards"). He is also, along with [Harrison Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrison_Ford "Harrison Ford"), one of two actors in history to have starred in a box office number-one film across six consecutive decades. [Films in which he has appeared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone_filmography "Sylvester Stallone filmography") have grossed over $7.5 billion worldwide.

Struggling as an actor for a number of years upon moving to New York City in 1969, Stallone found gradual work in films such as *[The Lords of Flatbush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lords_of_Flatbush "The Lords of Flatbush")* (1974). He achieved his greatest critical and commercial success starting in 1976 by writing the iconic role of boxer [Rocky Balboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Balboa "Rocky Balboa") as a character he believed he was suited to portray, in the [first film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky "Rocky") of the successful [*Rocky* franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_(franchise) "Rocky (franchise)"), and then held on to control of the production rights until he was cast in the starring role. In 1977, he became the third actor in history to be nominated for two Academy Awards for [Best Original Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Screenplay "Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay") and [Best Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor "Academy Award for Best Actor").

He portrayed the [PTSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-traumatic_stress_disorder "Post-traumatic stress disorder")-plagued soldier [John Rambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rambo "John Rambo") in *[First Blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Blood "First Blood")* (1982), a role he would play across five [*Rambo* films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rambo_(franchise) "Rambo (franchise)") (1982–2019) which he also co-wrote. From the mid-1980s to the late 1990s, Stallone would go on to become one of Hollywood's highest-paid actors acting in action films such as *[Cobra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobra_(1986_film) "Cobra (1986 film)")* (1986), *[Tango and Cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tango_%26_Cash "Tango & Cash")* (1989), *[Cliffhanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cliffhanger_(1993_film) "Cliffhanger (1993 film)")* (1993), *[Demolition Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition_Man_(film) "Demolition Man (film)")* (1993), and *[The Specialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Specialist "The Specialist")* (1994). At the height of his career, Stallone was known for his [rivalry with](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwarzenegger%E2%80%93Stallone_rivalry "Schwarzenegger–Stallone rivalry") [Arnold Schwarzenegger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Schwarzenegger "Arnold Schwarzenegger").

Stallone continued his established roles in [*Rocky Balboa*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Balboa_(film) "Rocky Balboa (film)") (2006) and [*Rambo*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rambo_(2008_film) "Rambo (2008 film)") (2008) before launching *[The Expendables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Expendables_(franchise) "The Expendables (franchise)")* film franchise (2010–present), in which he wrote most of the films and starred as the mercenary [Barney Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_The_Expendables_characters#Barney_Ross "List of The Expendables characters"). In 2013, he starred in the successful film [*Escape Plan*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escape_Plan_(film) "Escape Plan (film)") and appeared in [its sequels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escape_Plan_(film_series) "Escape Plan (film series)"). In 2015, he returned to *Rocky* again with *[Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creed_(film) "Creed (film)")*, in which a retired Rocky mentors former rival [Apollo Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Creed "Apollo Creed")'s son [Adonis Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adonis_Creed "Adonis Creed"). The film brought Stallone widespread praise and his first Golden Globe Award, as well as a third Academy Award nomination, having been first nominated for the same role 40 years prior. He also starred in the sequel *[Creed II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creed_II "Creed II")* (2018) and portrayed [Stakar Ogord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starhawk_(character) "Starhawk (character)") in the [Marvel Cinematic Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe "Marvel Cinematic Universe") films *[Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardians_of_the_Galaxy_Vol._2 "Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 2")* (2017) and *[Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardians_of_the_Galaxy_Vol._3 "Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 3")* (2023).

Regarded as an [icon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_icon "Cultural icon") of action cinema, Stallone is credited with helping redefine the [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_the_United_States "Cinema of the United States") action hero. He has occasionally ventured from the action genre, with mixed results. He starred in the comedies *[Oscar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_(1991_film) "Oscar (1991 film)")* (1991) and *[Stop! Or My Mom Will Shoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop!_Or_My_Mom_Will_Shoot "Stop! Or My Mom Will Shoot")* (1992); the first film failed at box office but the second one had modest success. He also starred in the 1997 drama *[Cop Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cop_Land "Cop Land")*, for which he temporarily shed his sculpted physique and gained weight for his role as a powerless sheriff. In television, he has starred in the [Paramount+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount%2B "Paramount+") crime series *[Tulsa King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulsa_King "Tulsa King")* (2022–present). In addition to his film work, Stallone is a noted art collector and painter, and has written books on fitness.

## Early life

Sylvester Gardenzio Stallone was born in the [Hell's Kitchen, Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hell%27s_Kitchen,_Manhattan "Hell's Kitchen, Manhattan"), in New York City on July 6, 1946, the elder son of celebrity [astrologer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrology "Astrology") and [women's professional wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_professional_wrestling "Women's professional wrestling") promoter [Jacqueline "Jackie" Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_Stallone "Jackie Stallone") (née Labofish; 1921–2020) and hairdresser [Francesco "Frank" Stallone Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Stallone_Sr. "Frank Stallone Sr.") (1919–2011), who opened up and ran a group of hair salons in [Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland "Maryland") and was an avid [polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polo "Polo") player. His mother was an American from Washington, D.C., with [Breton French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bretons "Bretons") and [Ukrainian Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Jewish "Ukrainian Jewish") ancestry, while his father was an Italian immigrant from [Gioia del Colle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gioia_del_Colle "Gioia del Colle") who moved to the U.S. in the 1930s. His younger brother is actor and musician [Frank Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Stallone "Frank Stallone"). Many biographies of Stallone indicate that his birth name is "Michael Sylvester Gardenzio Stallone" and his mother explained in an interview that she originally named him "Tyrone" because she admired the actor [Tyrone Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrone_Power "Tyrone Power"), but Stallone's father changed it to "Sylvester". His nickname as a child was "Binky" but he chose to go by the nickname of Mike/Michael after schoolmates began calling him "Stinky". His middle name "Gardenzio" is an alteration of the Italian given name "[Gaudenzio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaudenzio "Gaudenzio")" and he usually shortened it to "Enzio".

Complications during Stallone's birth forced his mother's obstetricians to use two pairs of [forceps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forceps_in_childbirth "Forceps in childbirth") while delivering him, accidentally severing a nerve in the process. This caused paralysis of the lower left side of his face (including parts of his lip, tongue, and chin) which gave him his signature snarling look and slurred speech. As a result, he was bullied in his childhood, with which he coped by getting into bodybuilding and acting. A guidance counselor once told Stallone's mother: "Your son is suited to run a sorting machine or to be an assistant electrician, primarily in the area of elevator operations"; nevertheless, Sylvester aspired to be an actor and a screenwriter.

Stallone spent part of his infancy in foster and boarding care, rejoining and moving back with his family to Maryland when he was five. In the early 1950s, his father moved the family to his mother's native Washington, D.C. to open a beauty school. In 1954, his mother opened a women's gym called Barbella's. He initially stayed with his father following his parents' divorce when he was 11, but joined his remarried mother in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia") when he was 15.

At one point, Stallone was voted "Most Likely to End Up in the Electric Chair" in high school; nevertheless, Stallone attended Notre Dame Academy and [Abraham Lincoln High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln_High_School_(Philadelphia) "Abraham Lincoln High School (Philadelphia)") in Philadelphia, and [Charlotte Hall Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Hall_Military_Academy "Charlotte Hall Military Academy") in [Charlotte Hall, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Hall,_Maryland "Charlotte Hall, Maryland"), prior to attending [Miami Dade College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Dade_College "Miami Dade College"). He spent two years, from September 1965 to June 1967, at the [American College of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_College_of_Switzerland "American College of Switzerland"). He returned to the United States to study as a drama major at the [University of Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Miami "University of Miami"), from 1967 to 1969, but did not graduate. Decades later, after Stallone's request that his acting and life experiences be accepted in exchange for his remaining needed college credits to graduate, he was granted a [Bachelor of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Fine_Arts "Bachelor of Fine Arts") (BFA) degree by the University of Miami in 1998.

## Film and stage career

### 1968–1976: Early roles to breakthrough

Until 1969, he appeared on the stage under the name Mike Stallone; in 1970, he started using the stage name Sylvester E. Stallone. While attending the University of Miami, Stallone had a role in the drama *That Nice Boy* (aka *The Square Root*), filmed in 1968. Moreover, he and [John Herzfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Herzfeld "John Herzfeld") worked together in 1969 on a low-budget self-produced film called "Horses".

Stallone had his first starring role in the [softcore pornography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Softcore_pornography "Softcore pornography") feature film *[The Party at Kitty and Stud's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Party_at_Kitty_and_Stud%27s "The Party at Kitty and Stud's")* (1970). He was paid US$200 for two days' work. Stallone later explained that he had done the film out of desperation after being evicted from his apartment and finding himself homeless for several days. He has also said that he slept three weeks in the [Port Authority Bus Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Authority_Bus_Terminal "Port Authority Bus Terminal") in New York City prior to seeing a casting notice for the film. In the actor's words, "it was either do that movie or rob someone, because I was at the end – the very end – of my rope". The film was released several years later as *Italian Stallion*, in order to cash in on Stallone's newfound fame (the new title was taken from Stallone's nickname since *Rocky*). Stallone also starred in the erotic [off-Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-Broadway "Off-Broadway") stage play *Score* which ran for 23 performances at the Martinique Theatre from October 28 to November 15, 1971, and was later made into the 1974 film *[Score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Score_(1974_film) "Score (1974 film)")* by [Radley Metzger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radley_Metzger "Radley Metzger").

After moving to New York City, Stallone shared an apartment with his girlfriend, Sasha Czack, an aspiring actress who supported them by working as a waitress. Stallone took odd jobs around this time, including being a cleaner at a zoo, and a theater usher; he was fired from the latter for scalping tickets. He furthered his writing skills by frequenting a local library, and became interested in the works of [Edgar Allan Poe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Allan_Poe "Edgar Allan Poe").

In 1972, Stallone was on the verge of giving up on having an acting career; in what he later described as a low point, he tried and failed to get a job as an extra in *[The Godfather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Godfather "The Godfather")*. Instead, he was relegated to a background role in another Hollywood hit, *[What's Up, Doc?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What%27s_Up,_Doc%3F_(1972_film) "What's Up, Doc? (1972 film)")*, starring [Barbra Streisand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbra_Streisand "Barbra Streisand"). Stallone is hardly visible in his two appearances.

Stallone happened to be acting in a play that a friend invited him to partake in, and an agent in attendance thought that Stallone fit the role of Stanley, a main character in *[The Lords of Flatbush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lords_of_Flatbush "The Lords of Flatbush")*, which had a start-stop schedule from 1972 to 1974 over budget issues. Stallone, around mid-1973, achieved his first proper starring role, in the independent film *[No Place to Hide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Place_to_Hide_(1970_film) "No Place to Hide (1970 film)")*, playing a man who is associated with a New York–based urban [terrorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist "Terrorist") movement, with a jewelry-seller as his love interest. The film was re-cut and retitled *Rebel* years later, this second version featuring Stallone as its star. In 1990, this film was re-edited with [outtakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outtake "Outtake") from the original film and newly shot matching footage, then [redubbed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubbing_(filmmaking) "Dubbing (filmmaking)") – in the style of [Woody Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Allen "Woody Allen")'s *[What's Up, Tiger Lily?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What%27s_Up,_Tiger_Lily%3F "What's Up, Tiger Lily?")* – into a parody of itself titled *A Man Called... Rainbo*.

Stallone's other first few film roles were minor, and included brief uncredited appearances in *[M\*A\*S\*H](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M*A*S*H_(film) "M*A*S*H (film)")* (1970), as a soldier sitting at a table; *[Pigeons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigeons_(film) "Pigeons (film)")* (1970), as a party guest; Woody Allen's *[Bananas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bananas_(film) "Bananas (film)")* (1971), as a subway thug; in the psychological thriller *[Klute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klute "Klute")* (1971), as an extra dancing in a club; and in the [Jack Lemmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Lemmon "Jack Lemmon") film *[The Prisoner of Second Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prisoner_of_Second_Avenue "The Prisoner of Second Avenue")* (1975), as a youth. In the latter film, Jack Lemmon's character chases, tackles, and mugs Stallone, thinking that Stallone's character is a pickpocket. He had his second starring role in 1974, in *The Lords of Flatbush*. In 1975, he played supporting roles in *[Farewell, My Lovely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farewell,_My_Lovely_(1975_film) "Farewell, My Lovely (1975 film)")*; *[Capone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capone_(1975_film) "Capone (1975 film)")*; and *[Death Race 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_Race_2000 "Death Race 2000")*. He made guest appearances on the TV series *[Police Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_Story_(1973_TV_series) "Police Story (1973 TV series)")* and *[Kojak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kojak "Kojak")*. He is also supposedly in *[Mandingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandingo_(film) "Mandingo (film)")*. It is often said that his scene was deleted.

Stallone gained worldwide fame with his starring role in the smash hit *[Rocky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky "Rocky")* (1976), a sports drama about a struggling boxer, [Rocky Balboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Balboa "Rocky Balboa"), taking on heavyweight champion [Apollo Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Creed "Apollo Creed"). On March 24, 1975, Stallone saw the [Muhammad Ali vs. Chuck Wepner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_vs._Chuck_Wepner "Muhammad Ali vs. Chuck Wepner") fight. That night Stallone went home, and after three days he had completed the first draft of *Rocky*. Stallone subsequently denied that Wepner provided any inspiration for the script; however, Wepner filed a lawsuit which was eventually settled with Stallone for an undisclosed amount. Other possible inspirations for the film may have included [Rocky Graziano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Graziano "Rocky Graziano")'s autobiography *Somebody Up There Likes Me*, and [the film of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somebody_Up_There_Likes_Me_(1956_film) "Somebody Up There Likes Me (1956 film)"). Stallone attempted to sell the script to multiple studios, with the intention of playing the lead role himself. Irwin Winkler and Robert Chartoff became interested and offered Stallone US$350,000 for the rights, but they had their own casting ideas for the lead role, including [Robert Redford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Redford "Robert Redford") and [Burt Reynolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burt_Reynolds "Burt Reynolds"). Stallone refused to sell unless he played the lead character – and, eventually, after a substantial budget cut to compromise, it was agreed he could be the star. Upon its release, critic [Roger Ebert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ebert "Roger Ebert") stated that Stallone could become the next [Marlon Brando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlon_Brando "Marlon Brando").

In 1977, at the [49th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/49th_Academy_Awards "49th Academy Awards"), *Rocky* was nominated for ten [Oscars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award "Academy Award"), including [Best Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor "Academy Award for Best Actor") and [Best Original Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Writing_Original_Screenplay "Academy Award for Writing Original Screenplay") nominations for Stallone. The film went on to win the Academy Awards for [Best Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture"), [Best Directing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Director "Academy Award for Best Director"), and [Best Film Editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Film_Editing "Academy Award for Best Film Editing"). *Rocky* has since been inducted into the [National Film Registry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Film_Registry "National Film Registry") and had its props placed in the [Smithsonian Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian_Institution "Smithsonian Institution"). Stallone's use of the front entrance to the [Philadelphia Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Museum_of_Art "Philadelphia Museum of Art") in the *Rocky* series led the area to be nicknamed the [Rocky Steps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Steps "Rocky Steps"), where a statue of the character, owned by Stallone, stood at the bottom until 2026 except for a brief period where it was in [South Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Philadelphia "South Philadelphia"). The character was also voted into the [International Boxing Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Boxing_Hall_of_Fame "International Boxing Hall of Fame").

### 1978–1989: Subsequent success

Stallone made his directorial debut and starred in the 1978 film *[Paradise Alley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise_Alley "Paradise Alley")*, a family drama in which he played one of three brothers who get involved in [professional wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_wrestling "Professional wrestling"). That same year, he starred in [Norman Jewison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Jewison "Norman Jewison")'s *[F.I.S.T.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F.I.S.T._(film) "F.I.S.T. (film)")*, a social drama in which he plays a warehouse worker, very loosely modeled on [James Hoffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hoffa "James Hoffa"), who becomes involved in labor union leadership. In 1979, he wrote, directed (replacing [John G. Avildsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_G._Avildsen "John G. Avildsen")), and starred in *[Rocky II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_II "Rocky II")*. The sequel became a major success, grossing US$200 million.

In 1981, he starred alongside [Michael Caine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Caine "Michael Caine") and soccer star [Pelé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pel%C3%A9 "Pelé") in *[Escape to Victory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escape_to_Victory "Escape to Victory")*, a sports drama in which he plays a [prisoner of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner_of_war "Prisoner of war") involved in a [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi "Nazi") propaganda soccer game. That same year, he starred in the thriller *[Nighthawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nighthawks_(1981_film) "Nighthawks (1981 film)")*, in which he plays a New York city cop who plays a cat-and-mouse game with a foreign terrorist, played by [Rutger Hauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutger_Hauer "Rutger Hauer").

In 1982, Stallone starred as [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam-American_War "Vietnam-American War") veteran [John Rambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rambo "John Rambo"), a former [Green Beret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Special_Forces "United States Army Special Forces"), in the action film *[First Blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Blood "First Blood")*, an adaptation of the [eponymous novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Blood_(novel) "First Blood (novel)") by [David Morell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Morrell_(writer) "David Morrell (writer)"), though the script was significantly altered by Stallone during the film's production, which was both a critical and box-office success. Critics praised Stallone's performance, saying he made Rambo seem human, as opposed to the way he is portrayed in the book of the same name. It launched the [Rambo franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rambo_(franchise) "Rambo (franchise)"). That year *[Rocky III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_III "Rocky III")* was released in which Stallone wrote, directed, and starred. The second sequel became a box-office success. In preparation for these roles, Stallone embarked upon a vigorous training regimen, which often meant six days a week in the gym and further sit-ups in the evenings. Stallone claims to have reduced his [body fat percentage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_fat_percentage "Body fat percentage") to his all-time low of 2.8% for *Rocky III*.

In 1983, he directed *[Staying Alive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staying_Alive_(1983_film) "Staying Alive (1983 film)")*, the sequel to *[Saturday Night Fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Fever "Saturday Night Fever")*, starring [John Travolta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Travolta "John Travolta"). This was the only film Stallone directed that he did not star in. *Staying Alive* was universally panned by film critics. Despite being a critical failure, *Staying Alive* was a commercial success. The film opened with the [biggest weekend for a musical film ever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_musicals#Timeline_of_highest-grossing_openings "List of highest-grossing musicals") (at the time) with a gross of $12,146,143 from 1,660 screens. Overall, the film grossed nearly $65 million in the US box office against its $22 million budget. Worldwide it grossed $127 million. Though the US box-office intake was significantly less than the $139.5 million earned by *Saturday Night Fever*, the film nevertheless ranked in the top ten most financially successful films of 1983.

During the 1980s, Stallone was considered one of the biggest action film stars in the world, along with [Arnold Schwarzenegger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Schwarzenegger "Arnold Schwarzenegger"). The [Schwarzenegger-Stallone rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwarzenegger-Stallone_rivalry "Schwarzenegger-Stallone rivalry") continued for years; they attacked each other in the press, and tried to surpass the other with more on-screen killings and larger weapons.

Stallone occasionally attempted, albeit unsuccessfully, roles in different genres. In 1984, he co-wrote and starred alongside [Dolly Parton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_Parton "Dolly Parton") in the comedy film *[Rhinestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinestone_(film) "Rhinestone (film)")*, where he played a wannabe country music singer. For the [*Rhinestone* soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinestone_(film_soundtrack) "Rhinestone (film soundtrack)"), he performed a song. Stallone turned down the lead male role in *[Romancing the Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romancing_the_Stone "Romancing the Stone")* in order to make *Rhinestone* instead, a decision he later regretted.

In 1985, Stallone continued his success with the *Rocky* and *Rambo* franchises with *[Rocky IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_IV "Rocky IV")* and *[Rambo: First Blood Part II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rambo:_First_Blood_Part_II "Rambo: First Blood Part II").* Stallone has portrayed these two characters in a total of 13 films. Stallone met former [Mr. Olympia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Olympia "Mr. Olympia") [Franco Columbu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Columbu "Franco Columbu") to develop his character's appearance for the film *Rocky IV*, just as if he were preparing for the Mr. Olympia competition. That meant two workouts a day, six days a week. Both films were major financial successes.

It was around 1985 that Stallone was signed to a remake of the 1939 [James Cagney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cagney "James Cagney") classic *[Angels with Dirty Faces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angels_with_Dirty_Faces "Angels with Dirty Faces")*. The film would form part of his multi-picture deal with Cannon Films and was to co-star [Christopher Reeve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Reeve "Christopher Reeve") and be directed by [Menahem Golan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menahem_Golan "Menahem Golan"). The re-making of such a beloved classic was met with disapproval by *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* and horror by top critic [Roger Ebert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ebert "Roger Ebert"). Cannon opted to make the action film *[Cobra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobra_(1986_film) "Cobra (1986 film)")* which was released in 1986 and became a box-office success. It led to the setting up of his production company White Eagle Enterprises.

In 1987, he starred in the family drama *[Over the Top](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over_the_Top_(1987_film) "Over the Top (1987 film)")* as a struggling trucker who tries to make amends with his estranged son and enters an [arm wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arm_wrestling "Arm wrestling") competition. This was poorly received by critics and was a box-office failure. In 1989, he co-starred alongside [Kurt Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Russell "Kurt Russell") in the [buddy cop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddy_cop "Buddy cop") action film *[Tango & Cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tango_%26_Cash "Tango & Cash")*, which did solid business domestically and overseas, grossing US$57 million in foreign markets and over US$120 million worldwide. Stallone became a boxing promoter in the 1980s. His boxing promoting company, Tiger Eye Productions, signed world champion boxers [Sean O'Grady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_O%27Grady_(boxer) "Sean O'Grady (boxer)") and [Aaron Pryor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Pryor "Aaron Pryor").

### 1990–1999: Career fluctuations

Stallone began the 1990s starring in the fifth installment of the *Rocky* franchise, *[Rocky V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_V "Rocky V")*. This film brought back the first film's director, John G. Avildsen, and was intended to be the final installment in the series at the time. It was considered a [box-office disappointment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box_office_bomb "Box office bomb") and received negative reviews.

Stallone next appeared in [John Landis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Landis "John Landis")' period comedy *[Oscar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_(1991_film) "Oscar (1991 film)")* which was both a critical and box-office failure. In 1992, he appeared in [Roger Spottiswoode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Spottiswoode "Roger Spottiswoode")'s action comedy *[Stop! Or My Mom Will Shoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop!_Or_My_Mom_Will_Shoot "Stop! Or My Mom Will Shoot")* which was also both a critical and box-office disaster. Stallone signed onto the film based on rumors that Schwarzenegger was interested in the lead. Schwarzenegger said that, knowing the script's quality was poor, he publicly faked interest in starring for producers to lure Stallone.

In 1993, he made a comeback with [Renny Harlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renny_Harlin "Renny Harlin")'s action thriller *[Cliffhanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cliffhanger_(1993_film) "Cliffhanger (1993 film)")*, which was a success in the US, grossing US$84 million, and worldwide, grossing US$171 million. Later that year, he starred in the [futuristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futuristic "Futuristic") action film *[Demolition Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition_Man_(film) "Demolition Man (film)")* directed by [Marco Brambilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Brambilla "Marco Brambilla"), co-starring [Wesley Snipes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesley_Snipes "Wesley Snipes") and [Sandra Bullock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_Bullock "Sandra Bullock"). On [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes") the film has an approval rating of 60% based on 42 reviews, with an average rating of 5.43/10. The site's consensus reads: "A better-than-average sci-fi shoot-em-up with a satirical undercurrent, *Demolition Man* is bolstered by strong performances by Stallone, Snipes, and Bullock." The film debuted at No. 1 at the box office. *Demolition Man* grossed $58,055,768 by the end of its box-office run in North America and $159,055,768 worldwide.

His string of hits continued with 1994's *[The Specialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Specialist "The Specialist")* co-starring [Sharon Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Stone "Sharon Stone") and directed by [Luis Llosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Llosa "Luis Llosa"), which opened in the U.S. on October 7. While the critical reception was overwhelmingly negative, the film was a commercial success. In its opening weekend it made $14,317,765 and ended up making back its budget with $57,362,582 at the domestic box office while making another $113,000,000 overseas, giving it a worldwide gross of $170,362,582.

In 1995, he played the [title character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judge_Dredd "Judge Dredd") (from the British comic book *[2000 AD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_AD_(comics) "2000 AD (comics)")*) in the science fiction action film *[Judge Dredd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judge_Dredd_(1995_film) "Judge Dredd (1995 film)")*. His overseas box-office appeal saved the domestic box-office disappointment of *Judge Dredd*, which cost almost US$100 million and barely made its budget back, with a worldwide tally of US$113 million. Despite the film's poor box-office performance, Stallone signed a three-picture deal with [Universal Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures "Universal Pictures") for $60 million, making him the second star after [Jim Carrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Carrey "Jim Carrey") to receive $20 million per film. The deal expired in February 2000 without him making any films, however, so he received no payment.

That year, he also appeared in the thriller *[Assassins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassins_(1995_film) "Assassins (1995 film)")* with [Julianne Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julianne_Moore "Julianne Moore") and [Antonio Banderas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Banderas "Antonio Banderas"). That same year, Stallone, along with an all-star cast of celebrities, appeared in the [Trey Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trey_Parker "Trey Parker") and [Matt Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Stone "Matt Stone") short comedy film *[Your Studio and You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Your_Studio_and_You "Your Studio and You")* commissioned by the [Seagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seagram "Seagram") Company for a party celebrating their acquisition of [Universal Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Studios,_Inc. "Universal Studios, Inc.") and the MCA Corporation. Stallone speaks in his Rocky Balboa voice with subtitles translating what he is saying. At one point, Stallone starts yelling about how can they use his Balboa character, that he left it in the past; the narrator calms him with a [wine cooler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine_cooler "Wine cooler") and calling him "brainiac." In response, Stallone says, "Thank you very much." He then looks at the wine cooler and exclaims, "Stupid cheap studio!"

In 1996, he starred in the [disaster film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disaster_film "Disaster film") *[Daylight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daylight_(1996_film) "Daylight (1996 film)")* as a disgraced former emergency services chief who attempts to rescue survivors of an underground tunnel explosion. *Daylight* also underperformed at the domestic box office, grossing $33 million, but did better overseas and grossed a total of $158 million worldwide. In 1997, Stallone was cast against type as an overweight sheriff in the crime drama *[Cop Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cop_Land "Cop Land")* in which he starred alongside [Robert De Niro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_De_Niro "Robert De Niro") and [Ray Liotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Liotta "Ray Liotta"). The film was critically well-received and was a modest success at the box office, earning $63 million on a $15 million budget, and Stallone's performance earned him the [Stockholm International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_International_Film_Festival "Stockholm International Film Festival") Best Actor Award. In 1998, he did voice-over work for the animated film *[Antz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antz "Antz")*, which was a success domestically.

### 2000–2005: Declining years

In 2000, Stallone starred in the thriller *[Get Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Get_Carter_(2000_film) "Get Carter (2000 film)")*, a remake of the 1971 British film of the [same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Get_Carter "Get Carter"), but the film was poorly received by both critics and audiences. Stallone's career declined considerably after his subsequent films *[Driven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driven_(2001_film) "Driven (2001 film)")* (2001), *[Avenging Angelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avenging_Angelo "Avenging Angelo")* (2002) and *[D-Tox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-Tox "D-Tox")* (2002) were also critical and commercial failures.

In 2003, he played a villainous role in the third installment of the *[Spy Kids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spy_Kids_(film_series) "Spy Kids (film series)")* series: *[Spy Kids 3-D: Game Over](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spy_Kids_3-D:_Game_Over "Spy Kids 3-D: Game Over")*, which was a huge box-office success (almost US$200 million worldwide). Stallone also had a cameo appearance in the 2003 French film *[Taxi 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxi_3 "Taxi 3")* as a passenger. Also that year, Stallone started to regain prominence for his supporting role in the [neo-noir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-noir "Neo-noir") crime drama *[Shade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shade_(film) "Shade (film)")* which was only released in a limited fashion but was praised by critics. He was also attached to star and direct a film tentatively titled *Rampart Scandal*, which was to be about the murder of rappers [Tupac Shakur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupac_Shakur "Tupac Shakur") and [The Notorious B.I.G.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Notorious_B.I.G. "The Notorious B.I.G.") and the surrounding [Los Angeles Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Police_Department "Los Angeles Police Department") corruption scandal. It was later titled *Notorious* but was shelved.

In 2005, alongside [Sugar Ray Leonard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_Ray_Leonard "Sugar Ray Leonard"), he was the co-presenter of the [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC "NBC") reality television boxing competition series *[The Contender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Contender_(TV_series) "The Contender (TV series)")*. That same year he also made a guest appearance in two episodes of the television series *[Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_(TV_series) "Las Vegas (TV series)")*. That year, Stallone also inducted [professional wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_wrestling "Professional wrestling") icon [Hulk Hogan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulk_Hogan "Hulk Hogan"), who appeared in *Rocky III* as a wrestler named Thunderlips, into the WWE Hall of Fame; Stallone was also the person who offered Hogan the cameo in *Rocky III*. In August, Stallone released his book *Sly Moves* which claimed to be a guide to fitness and nutrition as well as a candid insight into his life and works from his own perspective. The book also contained many photographs of Stallone throughout the years as well as pictures of him performing exercises.

### 2006–present: franchise films

After a three-year hiatus from films, Stallone had a comeback in 2006 with the sixth installment of the *Rocky* series, *[Rocky Balboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Balboa_(film) "Rocky Balboa (film)")*, which was a critical and commercial hit. After the critical and box-office failure of the previous installment *[Rocky V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_V "Rocky V")*, Stallone had decided to write, direct and star in a sixth installment which would be a more appropriate climax to the series. The total domestic box office came to US$70.3 million (and US$155.7 million worldwide). The budget of the film was only US$24 million. His performance in *Rocky Balboa* has been praised and garnered mostly positive reviews. That year, the development [*Death Wish*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_Wish_(2018_film) "Death Wish (2018 film)") remake began, when Stallone announced that he would be directing and starring in a remake of [the 1974 film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_Wish_(1974_film) "Death Wish (1974 film)"). Stallone said, "Instead of the [Charles Bronson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Bronson "Charles Bronson") character being an architect, my version would have him as a very good cop who had incredible success without ever using his gun. So when the attack on his family happens, he's really thrown into a moral dilemma in proceeding to carry out his revenge." He later told the publication that he was no longer involved. In a 2009 interview with [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV "MTV"), though, Stallone stated that he was again considering the project. However the role went to [Bruce Willis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Willis "Bruce Willis"), with [Eli Roth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli_Roth "Eli Roth") as director.

Stallone partnered with a beverage company producing an upscale [bottled water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottled_water "Bottled water") brand called Sly Water.

In 2006, Stallone expressed his desire to direct a film about *[Musa Dagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musa_Dagh "Musa Dagh")*, adapted by [Franz Werfel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Werfel "Franz Werfel")'s novel *[The Forty Days of Musa Dagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Forty_Days_of_Musa_Dagh "The Forty Days of Musa Dagh")*, detailing the [genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_genocide "Armenian genocide") of its Armenian community in 1915.

In 2008, Stallone reprised his other famous role in the fourth installment of the [*Rambo* franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rambo_(franchise) "Rambo (franchise)"), titled simply *[Rambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rambo_(2008_film) "Rambo (2008 film)")* (*John Rambo* in some countries where the first film was titled *Rambo*). The film opened in 2,751 theaters on January 25, 2008, grossing US$6,490,000 on its opening day and US$18,200,000 over its opening weekend. Its box office was US$113,244,290 worldwide with a budget of US$50 million.

In July 2009, Stallone made a cameo appearance in the [Bollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bollywood "Bollywood") film *[Kambakkht Ishq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kambakkht_Ishq "Kambakkht Ishq")*, where he played himself.

It was announced on December 7, 2010, that Stallone was voted into the [International Boxing Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Boxing_Hall_of_Fame "International Boxing Hall of Fame") in the non-participant category.

Also that year, Stallone wrote, directed and starred in the ensemble action film *[The Expendables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Expendables_(2010_film) "The Expendables (2010 film)")*. The film, which was filmed during summer/winter 2009, was released on August 13, 2010. Joining him in the film were fellow action stars [Jason Statham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Statham "Jason Statham"), [Jet Li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Li "Jet Li"), and [Dolph Lundgren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolph_Lundgren "Dolph Lundgren"), as well as [Terry Crews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Crews "Terry Crews"), [Mickey Rourke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickey_Rourke "Mickey Rourke"), [Randy Couture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Couture "Randy Couture"), [Eric Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Roberts "Eric Roberts"), and [Stone Cold Steve Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Cold_Steve_Austin "Stone Cold Steve Austin"), and cameos by fellow '80s action icons Bruce Willis and [Arnold Schwarzenegger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Schwarzenegger "Arnold Schwarzenegger"). The film took US$34,825,135 in its opening weekend, going straight in at No. 1 in the US box office. The figure marked the biggest opening weekend in Stallone's career. In summer 2010, Brazilian company O2 Filmes released a statement saying it was still owed more than US$2 million for its work on the film. In 2011, Stallone provided the voice of a lion in [Kevin James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_James "Kevin James")' comedy *[Zookeeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zookeeper_(film) "Zookeeper (film)")*.

*[The Expendables 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Expendables_2 "The Expendables 2")* was released August 17, 2012; the sequel received a positive critical reception of 67% on [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes"), as opposed to the original's 41%. As well as returning cast members from the first film, the ensemble cast also included [Jean-Claude Van Damme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Claude_Van_Damme "Jean-Claude Van Damme") and [Chuck Norris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Norris "Chuck Norris"). That year, Stallone co-wrote the book for the [Broadway musical adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky:_The_Musical "Rocky: The Musical") of *Rocky*.

In 2013, Stallone starred in the action film *[Bullet to the Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullet_to_the_Head "Bullet to the Head")*, directed by [Walter Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Hill_(director) "Walter Hill (director)"), based upon Alexis Nolent's French graphic novel *Du Plomb Dans La Tete*. Also in 2013, he starred in the action thriller *[Escape Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escape_Plan_(film) "Escape Plan (film)")*, along with Arnold Schwarzenegger and [Jim Caviezel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Caviezel "Jim Caviezel"), and in the sports comedy drama *[Grudge Match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grudge_Match "Grudge Match")* alongside [Robert De Niro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_De_Niro "Robert De Niro"), harkening back to the *Rocky* franchise. Stallone was reported to be developing an English-language remake of the Spanish film *[No Rest for the Wicked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Rest_for_the_Wicked_(2011_film) "No Rest for the Wicked (2011 film)")*, though the project was shelved. That year Stallone was credited as writer for the Jason Statham action film vehicle *[Homefront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homefront_(2013_film) "Homefront (2013 film)").*

*[The Expendables 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Expendables_3 "The Expendables 3")*, the third installment in the ensemble action film series, was released on August 15, 2014. The returning ensemble cast also added [Wesley Snipes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesley_Snipes "Wesley Snipes"), [Antonio Banderas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Banderas "Antonio Banderas"), [Mel Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mel_Gibson "Mel Gibson") and [Harrison Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrison_Ford "Harrison Ford"). This film was negatively received by both critics and audiences and became the lowest-grossing film in [the series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Expendables_(film_series) "The Expendables (film series)").

In 2015, Stallone reprised his role as Rocky Balboa in a spin-off-sequel film, *[Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creed_(film) "Creed (film)")*, which focused on Adonis "Donnie" Creed, the son of his deceased friend/rival, [Apollo Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Creed "Apollo Creed"), becoming a professional boxer, played by [Michael B. Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_B._Jordan "Michael B. Jordan"). The film, directed by [Ryan Coogler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Coogler "Ryan Coogler"), received critical acclaim. Portraying the iconic cinematic boxer for the seventh time in a span of 40 years, Stallone's portrayal of the character received widespread acclaim and accolades, including the [Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture "Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actor – Motion Picture"), and his third [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards") nomination, this time for [Best Supporting Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor "Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor").

In 2017, Stallone appeared in *[Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardians_of_the_Galaxy_Vol._2 "Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 2")* as [Stakar Ogord / Starhawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starhawk_(character) "Starhawk (character)"), the leader of a Ravagers faction. In 2018, he co-starred in *[Escape Plan 2: Hades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escape_Plan_2:_Hades "Escape Plan 2: Hades")* with [Dave Bautista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Bautista "Dave Bautista") which was released straight to home-video. Upon wrapping production, he announced via his social media page that work on the third movie titled *[Escape Plan: The Extractors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escape_Plan:_The_Extractors "Escape Plan: The Extractors")*, began immediately thereafter. In July, Stallone announced that he had finished a script for a sequel to *Creed*, with a plot including the return of [Ivan Drago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Drago "Ivan Drago") from *Rocky IV*. That year, Stallone was featured in [Derek Wayne Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_Wayne_Johnson "Derek Wayne Johnson")'s *John G. Avildsen: King of the Underdogs*, a documentary about director [John G. Avildsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_G._Avildsen "John G. Avildsen").

*[Creed II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creed_II "Creed II")* went into production in March 2018, with a scheduled release on Thanksgiving 2018. Stallone was originally slated to direct before the appointment of [Steven Caple Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Caple_Jr. "Steven Caple Jr."), in his feature film directorial debut. *Creed II* was released in the United States by [Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer "Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer") on November 21, 2018. The film received generally positive reviews from critics and it went on to debut to $35.3 million in its opening weekend (a five-day total of $55.8 million), marking the biggest debut ever for a live-action release over Thanksgiving.

On July 23, 2019, in an interview with [*Variety*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)"), Stallone said that a Rocky sequel and prequel are in development. Producer [Irwin Winkler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irwin_Winkler "Irwin Winkler") said "We're very high on it" and that negotiations are underway for Stallone to write and star in the feature. "We're very anxious to make it." Stallone said the plot of the film would be about Rocky befriending a young fighter who is an undocumented immigrant. "Rocky meets a young, angry person who got stuck in this country when he comes to see his sister. He takes him into his life, and unbelievable adventures begin, and they wind up south of the border. It's very, very timely." Stallone said. Stallone also said there are "ongoing discussions" about a Rocky prequel television series, which he hopes will land on a streaming service and the series will likely follow a young Rocky Balboa as a professional boxing hopeful. Stallone said producer Irwin Winkler is hesitant on making the series saying that "There was some conflict there, yes. He felt in his mind that "Rocky" was primarily a feature film, and he didn't see it as being translated for cable, so there was a big bone of contention." That year, Stallone hand-picked Derek Wayne Johnson to direct and produce a documentary on the making of the original *[Rocky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky "Rocky")*, entitled *[40 Years of Rocky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/40_Years_of_Rocky "40 Years of Rocky")* (2020). The documentary features Stallone narrating behind-the-scenes footage from the making of the film.

Stallone formed a film studio named Balboa Productions with Braden Aftergood in March 2018, where Stallone will serve as co-producer for each of their projects. The studio signed a multi-year collaboration deal with Starlight Culture Entertainment to develop projects for film and television. In May 2018, a fifth installment in the *Rambo* franchise was announced, and in August 2018, [Adrian Grünberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Gr%C3%BCnberg "Adrian Grünberg") was confirmed as the director. *[Rambo: Last Blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rambo:_Last_Blood "Rambo: Last Blood")* began filming by September 2018, with a script co-written by Stallone, who also reprised his role as [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War") veteran John Rambo. The plot centers around Rambo infiltrating a [Mexican drug cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Drug_War "Mexican Drug War") to rescue a family friend's daughter. The film, which was released on September 20, 2019, in the United States, grossed $18.9 million in its opening weekend, the best debut of the franchise. The film grossed $91 million worldwide against a production budget of $50 million.

In late 2020, Stallone lent his voice as Rambo to the fighting video game *[Mortal Kombat 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortal_Kombat_11 "Mortal Kombat 11")*, as part of the game's 2nd Kombat Pack. In 2021, he voiced [King Shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Shark "King Shark") in the [DC Extended Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Extended_Universe "DC Extended Universe") film *[The Suicide Squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Suicide_Squad_(film) "The Suicide Squad (film)")*. In 2022, Stallone starred in *[Samaritan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaritan_(film) "Samaritan (film)")*, a dark interpretation of the [superhero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superhero "Superhero") genre, from a script written by Bragi Schut. In November 2022, Stallone made his [streaming television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_television "Streaming television") debut in *[Tulsa King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulsa_King "Tulsa King")*, in which he plays a Mafia boss. The 9-episode series was created by [Taylor Sheridan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan "Taylor Sheridan") and [Terence Winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terence_Winter "Terence Winter") and premiered on [Paramount+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount%2B "Paramount+"). A second season premiered on September 15, 2024, with a third season set to premiere in 2026.

In May 2023, Stallone and his immediate family (wife and three daughters) starred in a reality television series *[The Family Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Family_Stallone "The Family Stallone")*. Seven days after launch, the series was renewed for a second season. That same month, Stallone reprised his role as Stakar Ogord in *[Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardians_of_the_Galaxy_Vol._3 "Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 3")*. *[Sly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sly_(film) "Sly (film)")*, a documentary film about Stallone by [Thom Zimny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thom_Zimny "Thom Zimny"), premiered as the closing film of the [2023 Toronto International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Toronto_International_Film_Festival "2023 Toronto International Film Festival").

In October 2023, he reprised role as Barney Ross in *[Expend4bles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expend4bles "Expend4bles")*, which was a critical and commercial failure, surpassing *The Expendable 3* to become the lowest-grossing film in the franchise.

#### Upcoming projects

Following the releases of *Creed II* and *Rambo V: Last Blood*, Balboa Productions has had an extensive production slate. A film depicting the life of [Jack "Galveston Giant" Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Johnson "Jack Johnson"), the first African-American boxing heavyweight champion, is in development. The project was announced after Stallone's instrumental involvement in helping get Johnson a posthumous pardon from US President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump").

Stallone is also scheduled to star in the film adaptation of *[Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_(Huggins_novel) "Hunter (Huggins novel)")*, a story which had originally been planned as the premise for *Rambo V: Last Blood*. The story centers around Nathaniel Hunter, a professional tracker who is hired to hunt a half-human beast created as an experiment of a secret agency. A feature-length adaptation of the biographical novel *Ghost: My Thirty Years as an FBI Undercover Agent* by Michael McGowan and Ralph Pezzullo about McGowan's career of over 50 undercover missions will follow, though there is no screenwriter attached to the project. Additionally, a film centered around black ops troops, being written by retired Army Ranger Max Adams, is also in development. Stallone wrote *[A Working Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Working_Man "A Working Man")* in collaboration with [David Ayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ayer "David Ayer") from a book series created by [Chuck Dixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Dixon "Chuck Dixon"), which was initially intended to be a television series, but later became a feature film starring [Jason Statham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Statham "Jason Statham"). The television production slate includes a series adaptation of Charles Sailor's *Second Son* being written by Rob Williams.

In May 2020, Stallone announced that a sequel to 1993's *[Demolition Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition_Man_(film) "Demolition Man (film)")* is in the works: "I think it's coming. We're working on it right now with Warner Brothers. It's looking fantastic. So, that should come out, that's going to happen". Stallone has continued to express his passion in directing a film based on [Edgar Allan Poe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Allan_Poe "Edgar Allan Poe")'s life, a script he has been preparing for years. He has also mentioned that he would like to adapt [Nelson DeMille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_DeMille "Nelson DeMille")'s novel *[The Lion's Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lion%27s_Game "The Lion's Game")*.

In March 2026, it was announced by *[TMZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TMZ "TMZ")* that Stallone and [Quentin Tarantino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quentin_Tarantino "Quentin Tarantino") would co-direct and co-write a 6-part miniseries featuring gangsters, showgirls, boxing, and music. The series is set to be shot in black and white, using 1930s cameras.

## Multiple tasks in media

In 1977, for the first *[Rocky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky "Rocky")*, Stallone became the third man in history to receive the two nominations for best actor and best screenplay, after [Charlie Chaplin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Chaplin "Charlie Chaplin") and [Orson Welles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orson_Welles "Orson Welles"). Stallone is known for his recurring roles as [Rocky Balboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Balboa "Rocky Balboa"), [John Rambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rambo "John Rambo"), and Barney Ross. Stallone wrote and starred in all of six [*Rocky* films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_(franchise)#Films) "Rocky (franchise)"), while directing four of the sequels. Stallone starred in and co-wrote the five films of the [*Rambo* franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rambo_(franchise) "Rambo (franchise)") and also directed the [fourth one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rambo_(2008_film) "Rambo (2008 film)"). Stallone wrote, directed, and took the lead role in the [first installment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Expendables_(2010_film) "The Expendables (2010 film)") of [*The Expendables* films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Expendables_(franchise) "The Expendables (franchise)"). Stallone directed, wrote, and starred in *[Paradise Alley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise_Alley "Paradise Alley")*. [John Travolta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Travolta "John Travolta") starred in *[Staying Alive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staying_Alive_(1983_film) "Staying Alive (1983 film)"),* a sequel of *[Saturday Night Fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Fever "Saturday Night Fever")*, which Stallone wrote and directed. Stallone wrote and starred in [*Cobra*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobra_(1986_film) "Cobra (1986 film)"), and [*Driven*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driven_(2001_film) "Driven (2001 film)"). Stallone co-wrote and starred in *[F.I.S.T.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F.I.S.T._(film) "F.I.S.T. (film)")*, *[Rhinestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinestone_(film) "Rhinestone (film)")*, [*Over the Top*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over_the_Top_(1987_film) "Over the Top (1987 film)"), [*Cliffhanger*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cliffhanger_(1993_film) "Cliffhanger (1993 film)"), and *[Creed II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creed_II "Creed II")*.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Asked in February 2008 which of the icons (Rocky or Rambo) he would rather be remembered for, Stallone said "it's a tough one, but *Rocky* is my first baby, so Rocky." He also stated that Rocky could be interpreted as the "[conscious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscious "Conscious")" and Rambo as the "[unconscious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconscious_mind "Unconscious mind")" of the same character.

Stallone has occasionally sung in his films. He sang "Too Close to Paradise" for *Paradise Alley* (1978), with the music provided by [Bill Conti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Conti "Bill Conti") (who also collaborated with Stallone in prior years, having recorded the famous "[Gonna Fly Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonna_Fly_Now "Gonna Fly Now")" theme for his [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award "Academy Award")-nominated film, *Rocky* (1976) which was a U.S. No. 1 hit). In *[Rocky III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_III "Rocky III")* (1982), Stallone (as Rocky Balboa) sang "Take Me Back" to his on-screen wife, Adrian ([Talia Shire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talia_Shire "Talia Shire")), as they lay in bed. The song was first performed by singer and younger brother, [Frank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Stallone "Frank Stallone"), who had a small role in the original *Rocky*. For *Rhinestone* (1984), Stallone sang such songs as "Drinkenstein" as well as duets with his co-star, and actual [country music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music") star, [Dolly Parton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_Parton "Dolly Parton"). He also performed two songs when he guest-starred on *[The Muppet Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Muppet_Show "The Muppet Show")* in the 1980s, at the height of his career. The last time Stallone sang in a film was in *[Grudge Match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grudge_Match "Grudge Match")* (2013) when he and [Robert De Niro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_De_Niro "Robert De Niro") performed "[The Star-Spangled Banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Star-Spangled_Banner "The Star-Spangled Banner")" together.

## Personal life

### Marriages and children

Stallone has been married three times. He has two sons from his first marriage and three daughters from his third marriage.

At age 28, on December 28, 1974, he married Sasha Czack. They had two sons, [Sage Moonblood Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sage_Stallone "Sage Stallone") (1976–2012), who died of [heart disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronary_artery_disease "Coronary artery disease") at age 36, and Seargeoh (b. 1979), who was diagnosed with [autism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autism "Autism") at an early age. Seargeoh also made a cameo as Rocky Balboa's son in *[Rocky II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_II "Rocky II")* as an infant. The couple divorced on February 14, 1985.

Stallone married model and actress [Brigitte Nielsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigitte_Nielsen "Brigitte Nielsen") on December 15, 1985, in [Beverly Hills, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beverly_Hills,_California "Beverly Hills, California"). The marriage lasted two years and the subsequent divorce was highly publicized by the [tabloid press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabloid_press "Tabloid press").

In 1988, Stallone met model [Jennifer Flavin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Flavin "Jennifer Flavin") and they were in a relationship until 1994, when Stallone told her he was having a child with model [Janice Dickinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janice_Dickinson "Janice Dickinson"). After the February 1994 birth, DNA tests confirmed he was not the father and Stallone ended his engagement to Dickinson. After a brief 1995 engagement with model [Angie Everhart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angie_Everhart "Angie Everhart"), he and Flavin rekindled their relationship.

In May 1997, Stallone and Jennifer Flavin married. The couple has three daughters, Sophia, [Sistine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sistine_Stallone "Sistine Stallone"), and Scarlet. His daughters were chosen to share the role of [Golden Globe Ambassador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Ambassador "Golden Globe Ambassador") at the [74th Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/74th_Golden_Globe_Awards "74th Golden Globe Awards"). On August 19, 2022, [Palm Beach County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Beach_County,_Florida "Palm Beach County, Florida") records showed that after 25 years of marriage, Jennifer Flavin had filed for "[dissolution of marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divorce "Divorce") and other relief". On September 23, 2022, the couple reconciled.

### Family and friends

Stallone maintains a relationship with his brother [Frank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Stallone "Frank Stallone") who contributed the theme songs to *[Rambo: First Blood Part II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rambo:_First_Blood_Part_II "Rambo: First Blood Part II")*, and [*Staying Alive*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staying_Alive_(1983_film) "Staying Alive (1983 film)"). In 1983, Frank's song "[Far from Over](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_from_Over_(Frank_Stallone_song) "Far from Over (Frank Stallone song)")", for *Staying Alive*, reached the #10 U.S. hit. Frank appears in minor roles, bit parts, and provides music in many films starring Sylvester, most notably in the [*Rocky* films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_(film_series) "Rocky (film series)"), where Frank played a street corner singer and contributed songs. Stallone's 48-year-old half-sister, Toni Ann Filiti, died of lung cancer on August 26, 2012. Stallone was a close friend of [Joe Spinell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Spinell "Joe Spinell"), but they had a falling out during the shooting of their final collaboration, *[Nighthawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nighthawks_(1981_film) "Nighthawks (1981 film)")*, in 1981, and Spinell died in 1989.

### Injuries

Known for physically demanding roles and his willingness to do the majority of his own stunts, Stallone has suffered various injuries during his acting career. During the filming of *[Escape to Victory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escape_to_Victory "Escape to Victory")*, he broke a finger trying to save a [penalty kick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_kick_(association_football) "Penalty kick (association football)") against [Pelé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pel%C3%A9 "Pelé"). For a scene in *Rocky IV*, he told [Dolph Lundgren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolph_Lundgren "Dolph Lundgren"), "Punch me as hard as you can in the chest." He later said, "Next thing I know, I was in intensive care at [St. John's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_John%27s_Health_Center "Saint John's Health Center") for four days. It's stupid!" While filming a fight scene with ["Stone Cold" Steve Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Cold_Steve_Austin "Stone Cold Steve Austin") for *[The Expendables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Expendables_(2010_film) "The Expendables (2010 film)")*, he broke his neck and required the insertion of a metal plate.

### Legal issues

In February 2001, an exotic dancer named Margie Carr filed a lawsuit against Stallone, accusing him of rape while at a Santa Monica fitness center where they both worked out on February 26, 2000. A lawyer for Stallone denied the claim, saying she sold the story to *[Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globe_(tabloid) "Globe (tabloid)")* the month before the lawsuit.

In 2007, customs officials in Australia discovered 48 vials of the synthetic human growth hormone [Jintropin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jintropin "Jintropin") in Stallone's luggage. In a court hearing on May 15, he pleaded guilty to two charges of possessing a controlled substance.

In 2013, it was reported that Stallone paid a $2 million lump-sum settlement alongside a monthly amount and a trust for psychiatric and medical expenses to his half-sister Toni-Ann Filiti in 1987. Filiti threatened to file a lawsuit accusing him of abuse. Representatives for Stallone and Filiti's mother Jacqueline Stallone denied the allegations, accusing Filiti of [blackmailing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackmail "Blackmail") him and being a drug addict. However, Filiti's son, Edd Filiti, supported the claims, saying his mother "screamed about" abuse at the hands of her half-brother "over and over," before her death in 2012.

In 2016, a report from the Las Vegas Metropolitan Police Department was published stating that Stallone was accused of sexual assault by a 16-year-old girl while he was shooting a film in [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas "Las Vegas") in 1986. The teen reportedly said that Stallone, then 40, forced her into a [threesome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threesome "Threesome") with his bodyguard. A representative for Stallone denied the allegation. Stallone's ex-wife, [Brigitte Nielsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigitte_Nielsen "Brigitte Nielsen"), later came to his defense, saying that she was with him at the time of the alleged assault. Stallone's *Over the Top* costar [David Mendenhall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Mendenhall "David Mendenhall") also defended Stallone, denying claims that he introduced Stallone to the girl in question.

In November 2017, a woman accused Stallone of sexually assaulting her at his [Santa Monica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica,_California "Santa Monica, California") office in the early 1990s. Stallone denied the claim. His attorney revealed the accuser filed a report after an entertainment website declined to pick up the story. Stallone's attorneys also stated that while the actor had a consensual relationship with the accuser in 1987, they had two witnesses who refuted the claims. In June 2018, the Los Angeles District Attorney's office confirmed an investigation, stating that the Santa Monica Police Department had presented a [sex crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_crimes "Sex crimes") case against Stallone to a special prosecution task force for review. In October 2018, the Los Angeles District Attorney's office made the decision not to charge Stallone for the alleged assault, as no witnesses corroborated the allegations. Stallone in turn filed a police report regarding her lying on an official document.

### Religious views

Stallone was baptized and raised a devout [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic "Catholic") but stopped going to church as his acting career progressed. He rediscovered his childhood faith when his daughter was born ill in 1996, and he again became a strict Catholic by late 2006. The same year, he was interviewed by [Pat Robertson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Robertson "Pat Robertson") from the [Christian Broadcasting Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Broadcasting_Network "Christian Broadcasting Network")'s *[700 Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_700_Club "The 700 Club")*. He stated that he spent much of his previous time in Hollywood "losing his way" because temptation abounded, but later put things "in God's hands". However, he told *[GQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GQ "GQ")* magazine in 2010, "I'm pretty spiritual; I believe a lot in the spirit of man. I'm certainly not an atheist ... I was baptized Catholic, but I don't belong to a structured church. I have no opposition to it. I think there's great nuggets of knowledge in there, some wonderful rules to live by. Then the flip side is the amount of agony that's caused." As of 2023, Stallone is a churchgoing [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic "Catholic") again.

### Politics

Stallone has supported several [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States) "Republican Party (United States)") politicians but does not identify as a member of the Republican Party. He has also donated to the [Democratic National Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_National_Committee "Democratic National Committee") and to [Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)"), including Senators [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden") and [Chris Dodd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Dodd "Chris Dodd"). He is a staunch advocate of [gun control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_control "Gun control") despite his otherwise [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States "Conservatism in the United States") views, and has been described as "the most anti-gun celebrity in Hollywood". In 1994, Stallone donated $1,000 to Republican Congressman [Rick Santorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Santorum "Rick Santorum")'s [Senate campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_United_States_Senate_election_in_Pennsylvania "1994 United States Senate election in Pennsylvania"). In 2008, Stallone endorsed [John McCain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCain "John McCain") for [that year's presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election "2008 United States presidential election"). In 2015, upon the advice of a [Vedic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedas "Vedas") scholar, Stallone performed a [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism") *Tithi Shradh* ritual (done for those who died by accident or murder) at [Haridwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haridwar "Haridwar") for his son Sage.

Stallone expresses support for recognition of the [Armenian genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_genocide "Armenian genocide"). He attended the 2017 film premiere for *[The Promise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Promise_(2016_film) "The Promise (2016 film)")*, a movie set during the genocide.

In the run-up to the [2016 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election "2016 United States presidential election"), Stallone described [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") as a "[Dickensian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens#Characters "Charles Dickens") character" and "larger than life" but did not endorse Trump or anyone else in the [Republican primaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Republican_Party_presidential_primaries "2016 Republican Party presidential primaries"). That same year, he declined an offer to become Chair of the [National Endowment for the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Endowment_for_the_Arts "National Endowment for the Arts"), citing a desire to work on issues related to veterans. In 2023, Stallone donated $11,600 to [independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_politician "Independent politician") Senator [Kyrsten Sinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrsten_Sinema "Kyrsten Sinema")'s [re-election campaign in Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_Senate_election_in_Arizona "2024 United States Senate election in Arizona"). Later that year, he and his family spent time with [Pope Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Francis "Pope Francis") in a private meeting at the Vatican. After Trump's win in the [2024 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election "2024 United States presidential election"), Stallone attended an event at [Mar-a-Lago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mar-a-Lago "Mar-a-Lago"). During a speech, Stallone praised Trump as a "mythical character" and the "second [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington "George Washington")". He also compared him to his character [Rocky Balboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Balboa "Rocky Balboa").

On January 16, 2025, it was announced by Trump that Stallone would serve in a new role as a Special Ambassador to [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood,_Los_Angeles "Hollywood, Los Angeles"), sharing the role with fellow actors [Jon Voight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Voight "Jon Voight") and [Mel Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mel_Gibson "Mel Gibson"). Trump stated that he wants these actors to make Hollywood "stronger than ever before" by bringing back business lost to "foreign countries". In September 2026, Stallone said that he never agreed to the position and was blindsided by Trump's announcement. He recalled telling Trump he would have rejected the appointment if asked.
