# World War II

**World War II**, or the **Second World War** (1 September 1939 – 2 September 1945), was a [global conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_conflict "Global conflict") between two [coalitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition "Coalition"): the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II "Allies of World War II") and the [Axis powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers "Axis powers"). [Nearly all of the world's countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_by_country "World War II by country") participated, with many engaging in [total war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_war "Total war") on an unprecedented scale. World War II was the [deadliest conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_wars_by_death_toll "List of wars by death toll") in history, causing the [deaths of 60 to 75 million people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_casualties "World War II casualties"), a majority of whom were civilians. Millions died as a result of massacres, starvation, disease, and [genocides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide "Genocide"), including [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust "The Holocaust"). After the Allied victory, [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied-occupied_Germany "Allied-occupied Germany"), [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied-occupied_Austria "Allied-occupied Austria"), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Japan "Occupation of Japan"), and [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_Korea#Post–World_War_II "Division of Korea") were occupied, and [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_trials "Nuremberg trials") and [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Military_Tribunal_for_the_Far_East "International Military Tribunal for the Far East") leaders were tried for [war crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_crime "War crime").

The [causes of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causes_of_World_War_II "Causes of World War II") included unresolved tensions in the [aftermath of World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aftermath_of_World_War_I "Aftermath of World War I") and the rise of [fascism in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism_in_Europe "Fascism in Europe") and [militarism in Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militarism_in_Japan "Militarism in Japan"). Key events preceding the war included [Japan's invasion of Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan%27s_invasion_of_Manchuria "Japan's invasion of Manchuria") in 1931, the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War "Spanish Civil War"), the outbreak of the [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War "Second Sino-Japanese War") in 1937, and Germany's [annexations of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss "Anschluss") and [the Sudetenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Agreement "Munich Agreement"). World War II is generally considered to have begun on 1 September 1939, when [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany"), under [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler"), [invaded Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland "Invasion of Poland"), after which the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_declaration_of_war_on_Germany_(1939) "United Kingdom declaration of war on Germany (1939)") and [France declared war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_declaration_of_war_on_Germany_(1939) "French declaration of war on Germany (1939)") on Germany. Poland was also invaded by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") in mid-September, and was partitioned between the two states under the [Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molotov%E2%80%93Ribbentrop_Pact "Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact"). In 1940, the Soviet Union [annexed the Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_the_Baltic_states "Occupation of the Baltic states") and [parts of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_War "Winter War") and [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_occupation_of_Bessarabia_and_Northern_Bukovina "Soviet occupation of Bessarabia and Northern Bukovina"), while Germany [conquered Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_campaign "Norwegian campaign"), [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_invasion_of_Denmark_(1940) "German invasion of Denmark (1940)"), [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_invasion_of_Belgium_(1940) "German invasion of Belgium (1940)"), [Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_invasion_of_Luxembourg "German invasion of Luxembourg"), and the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_invasion_of_the_Netherlands "German invasion of the Netherlands"). After the [fall of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_France "Fall of France") in June 1940, the war continued, mainly between Germany, now assisted by [Fascist Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_Italy "Fascist Italy"), and the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire") and [British Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Commonwealth "British Commonwealth"), with [fighting in the Balkans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkans_campaign_(World_War_II) "Balkans campaign (World War II)"), [Mediterranean, Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_and_Middle_East_theatre_of_World_War_II "Mediterranean and Middle East theatre of World War II"), [East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_campaign_(World_War_II) "East African campaign (World War II)"), the aerial [Battle of Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Britain "Battle of Britain"), [the Blitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Blitz "The Blitz"), and the naval [Battle of the Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Atlantic "Battle of the Atlantic"). By mid-1941, [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_in_Yugoslavia "World War II in Yugoslavia") and [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_occupation_of_Greece "Axis occupation of Greece") had also been defeated by [Axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers "Axis powers") countries. In June 1941, Germany [invaded the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invaded_the_Soviet_Union "Invaded the Soviet Union"), opening the [Eastern Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_(World_War_II) "Eastern Front (World War II)").

In December 1941, Japan attacked American and British territories [in Asia and the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War#Japanese_offensives,_1941–1942 "Pacific War"), including [Pearl Harbor in Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor "Attack on Pearl Harbor"), leading the United States to enter the war against the Axis. Japan conquered much of coastal China and Southeast Asia, but its advances in the Pacific were halted in June 1942 at the [Battle of Midway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Midway "Battle of Midway"). In early 1943, Axis forces were defeated in [North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_African_campaign "North African campaign") and at [Stalingrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Stalingrad "Battle of Stalingrad") in the Soviet Union. An Allied invasion of Italy in July resulted in the [fall of its fascist regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Fascist_regime_in_Italy "Fall of the Fascist regime in Italy"), and Allied offensives in the Pacific and the Soviet Union forced the Axis to retreat on all fronts. In 1944, the Western Allies [invaded France at Normandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normandy_landings "Normandy landings"), opening [a new front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Front_(World_War_II) "Western Front (World War II)"), and the Soviet Union advanced into [Central Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Europe "Central Europe"). Japan also suffered major setbacks, including the crippling of its navy by the United States, the loss of key Western Pacific islands, and defeats in [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma_campaign "Burma campaign"). The war in Europe concluded with the liberation of [German-occupied territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German-occupied_Europe "German-occupied Europe") and an invasion of Germany itself, which culminated in the [fall of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Berlin "Battle of Berlin") and [Germany's unconditional surrender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Instrument_of_Surrender "German Instrument of Surrender") on [8 May 1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8_May_1945 "8 May 1945"). On 6 and 9 August, the US [dropped atomic bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bombings_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki "Atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki") on Hiroshima and Nagasaki, followed by a [Soviet invasion of Japanese-occupied Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_Manchuria "Soviet invasion of Manchuria"). Japan announced its [unconditional surrender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrender_of_Japan "Surrender of Japan") on 15 August and signed [a surrender document](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Instrument_of_Surrender "Japanese Instrument of Surrender") on [2 September 1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_over_Japan_Day "Victory over Japan Day").

World War II transformed the political, economic, and social structures of the world and established the foundation of international relations for the rest of the 20th century and into the 21st century. The United Nations was created to foster international cooperation and prevent future conflicts, with the victorious great powers—China, France, the Soviet Union, the United Kingdom, and the United States—becoming the [permanent members](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_members_of_the_United_Nations_Security_Council "Permanent members of the United Nations Security Council") of its [Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Council "Security Council"). The Soviet Union and the United States emerged as rival superpowers, setting the stage for the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War"). In the wake of Europe's devastation, the influence of its great powers waned, triggering the [decolonisation of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation_of_Africa "Decolonisation of Africa") and [of Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation_of_Asia "Decolonisation of Asia"). Many countries whose industries had been damaged moved towards [economic recovery and expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post%E2%80%93World_War_II_economic_expansion "Post–World War II economic expansion").

## Start and end dates

Most historians agree that World War II began with the [German invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_invasion_of_Poland "German invasion of Poland") on 1 September 1939 and the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_declaration_of_war_on_Germany_(1939) "United Kingdom declaration of war on Germany (1939)") and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_declaration_of_war_on_Germany_(1939) "French declaration of war on Germany (1939)") declarations of war on Germany two days later. Dates for the beginning of the [Pacific War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War "Pacific War") include the start of the [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War "Second Sino-Japanese War") on 7 July 1937, or the earlier [Japanese invasion of Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_invasion_of_Manchuria "Japanese invasion of Manchuria") on 18 September 1931. Other proposed starting dates include the [Italian invasion of Abyssinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Italo-Ethiopian_War "Second Italo-Ethiopian War") on 3 October 1935. The British historian [Antony Beevor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antony_Beevor "Antony Beevor") views the beginning of World War II as the [Battles of Khalkhin Gol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_of_Khalkhin_Gol "Battles of Khalkhin Gol") fought between [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan "Empire of Japan") and the forces of Mongolia and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") from May to September 1939. Others view the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War "Spanish Civil War") as the start of or prelude to World War II.

The date of the war's end is also not universally agreed upon. It was generally accepted at the time that the war ended with the [armistice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice "Armistice") of 15 August 1945 ([V-J Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-J_Day "V-J Day")), rather than with the formal [surrender of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrender_of_Japan "Surrender of Japan") on 2 September 1945, which officially [ended the war in Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_of_World_War_II_in_Asia "End of World War II in Asia"). A [peace treaty between Japan and the Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_San_Francisco "Treaty of San Francisco") was signed in 1951. A 1990 [treaty regarding Germany's future](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Final_Settlement_with_Respect_to_Germany "Treaty on the Final Settlement with Respect to Germany") allowed the [reunification of East and West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification "German reunification") to take place. No formal peace treaty between Japan and the Soviet Union was ever signed, although the state of war between the two countries was terminated by the [Soviet–Japanese Joint Declaration of 1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Japanese_Joint_Declaration_of_1956 "Soviet–Japanese Joint Declaration of 1956"), which also restored full diplomatic relations between them.

## Background

### Aftermath of World War I

[World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I") radically altered the European political map. The most prominent nations of the [Central Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Powers "Central Powers") each lost territory in their respective peace treaties at the conclusion of the conflict. New [nation-states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation-states "Nation-states") were created out of the dissolution of the [Austro-Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian "Austro-Hungarian"), [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire "Ottoman Empire"), and [Russian Empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire").

To prevent a future world war, the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations") was established in 1920 by the [Paris Peace Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Peace_Conference_(1919%E2%80%931920) "Paris Peace Conference (1919–1920)"). The organisation's primary function was to prevent armed conflict through collective security, military, and [naval disarmament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Naval_Treaty "Washington Naval Treaty"), as well as settling international disputes through peaceful negotiations and arbitration.

Despite strong pacifist sentiment [after World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aftermath_of_World_War_I "Aftermath of World War I"), [irredentist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irredentist "Irredentist") and [revanchist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revanchist "Revanchist") [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism "Nationalism") had emerged in several European states. These sentiments were especially pronounced in Germany due to the significant territorial, colonial, and financial losses imposed by the [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles "Treaty of Versailles"). Under the treaty, Germany lost around 13 per cent of its home territory and all [its overseas possessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_colonial_empire "German colonial empire"), while German annexation of other states was prohibited, [reparations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I_reparations "World War I reparations") were imposed, and limits were placed on the size and capability of the country's [armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichswehr "Reichswehr").

### Germany and Italy

The [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire "German Empire") was dissolved in the [German revolution of 1918–1919](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_revolution_of_1918%E2%80%931919 "German revolution of 1918–1919"), and a democratic government, later known as the [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic "Weimar Republic"), was created. The [interwar period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_period "Interwar period") saw strife between supporters of the new republic and hardline opponents on both the political right and left. Italy, as an [entente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entente_(alliance) "Entente (alliance)") ally, had made some post-war territorial gains; however, [Italian nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_nationalism "Italian nationalism") were angered that the [promises made](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_London_(1915) "Treaty of London (1915)") by the United Kingdom and France to secure Italian entrance into the war were not fulfilled in the peace settlement. From 1922 to 1925, the [fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_fascism "Italian fascism") movement led by [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini "Benito Mussolini") seized power in Italy with a nationalist, [totalitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totalitarian "Totalitarian"), and [class collaborationist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_collaboration "Class collaboration") agenda that abolished [representative democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy "Representative democracy"), repressed socialist, left-wing, and liberal forces and pursued an aggressive expansionist foreign policy aimed at making Italy a world power, promising the creation of a "New Roman Empire".

[Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler"), after an [unsuccessful attempt to overthrow the German government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer_Hall_Putsch "Beer Hall Putsch") in 1923, eventually [became the chancellor of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler%27s_rise_to_power "Adolf Hitler's rise to power") in 1933 when President [Paul von Hindenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_von_Hindenburg "Paul von Hindenburg") and the [Reichstag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichstag_(Weimar_Republic) "Reichstag (Weimar Republic)") appointed him. The Nazis soon abolished parliamentary democracy, espousing a [radical, racially motivated revision of the world order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Order_(Nazism) "New Order (Nazism)"), and began a massive [rearmament campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_rearmament "German rearmament"). Following Hindenburg's death in 1934, Hitler proclaimed himself *Führer* of Germany. France, seeking to secure its alliance with Italy, [allowed Italy a free hand in Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Italian_Agreement_of_1935 "Franco-Italian Agreement of 1935"), which Italy desired as a colonial possession. The situation was aggravated in early 1935 when the [Territory of the Saar Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_the_Saar_Basin "Territory of the Saar Basin") was legally reunited with Germany, and Hitler repudiated the [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles "Treaty of Versailles"), accelerated his rearmament programme, and introduced [conscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription "Conscription").

### European treaties

The United Kingdom, France and Italy formed the [Stresa Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stresa_Front "Stresa Front") in April 1935 to contain Germany, a key step towards [military globalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_globalisation "Military globalisation"); however, that June, the United Kingdom made an [independent naval agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-German_Naval_Agreement "Anglo-German Naval Agreement") with Germany, easing prior restrictions. The Soviet Union, concerned by Germany's [goals of capturing vast areas of Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drang_nach_Osten "Drang nach Osten"), drafted a treaty of mutual assistance with France. Before taking effect, though, the [Franco-Soviet pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Soviet_pact "Franco-Soviet pact") was required to go through the bureaucracy of the League of Nations, which rendered it essentially toothless. The United States, concerned with events in Europe and Asia, passed the [Neutrality Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrality_Acts_of_the_1930s "Neutrality Acts of the 1930s") in August of the same year.

Hitler defied the Versailles and [Locarno Treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locarno_Treaties "Locarno Treaties") by [remilitarising the Rhineland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remilitarisation_of_the_Rhineland "Remilitarisation of the Rhineland") in March 1936, encountering little opposition due to the policy of [appeasement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appeasement "Appeasement"). In October 1936, Germany and Italy formed the [Rome–Berlin Axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome%E2%80%93Berlin_Axis "Rome–Berlin Axis"). A month later, Germany and Japan signed the [Anti-Comintern Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Comintern_Pact "Anti-Comintern Pact"), which Italy joined the following year.

### Asia

The [Kuomintang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuomintang "Kuomintang") party in China launched a [unification campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Expedition "Northern Expedition") against [regional warlords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warlord_Era "Warlord Era") and nominally unified China in the mid-1920s, but was soon embroiled in [a civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War "Chinese Civil War") against its former [Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Communist_Party "Chinese Communist Party") (CCP) allies and [new regional warlords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Plains_War "Central Plains War"). In 1931, an [increasingly militaristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statism_in_Sh%C5%8Dwa_Japan "Statism in Shōwa Japan") [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan "Empire of Japan"), which had long sought influence in China as the first step of what its government saw as the country's [right to rule Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakk%C5%8D_ichiu "Hakkō ichiu"), staged the [Mukden incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukden_incident "Mukden incident") as a pretext to [invade Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_invasion_of_Manchuria "Japanese invasion of Manchuria") and establish the [puppet state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_state "Puppet state") of [Manchukuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchukuo "Manchukuo").

China appealed to the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations") to stop the Japanese invasion of Manchuria. Japan withdrew from the League of Nations after being [condemned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lytton_Report "Lytton Report") for its incursion into Manchuria. The two nations then fought several battles, in [Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_28_incident "January 28 incident"), [Rehe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Rehe "Battle of Rehe"), and [Hebei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_of_the_Great_Wall "Defense of the Great Wall"), until the [Tanggu Truce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanggu_Truce "Tanggu Truce") was signed in 1933. Thereafter, Chinese volunteer forces continued the resistance to Japanese aggression in [Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacification_of_Manchukuo "Pacification of Manchukuo"), and [Chahar and Suiyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actions_in_Inner_Mongolia_(1933%E2%80%931936) "Actions in Inner Mongolia (1933–1936)"). After the 1936 [Xi'an Incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi%27an_Incident "Xi'an Incident"), the Kuomintang and [CCP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CCP "CCP") forces agreed on a ceasefire to present [a united front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_United_Front "Second United Front") to oppose Japan.

## Pre-war events

### Italian invasion of Ethiopia (1935)

The [Second Italo-Ethiopian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Italo-Ethiopian_War "Second Italo-Ethiopian War") was a [colonial war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_war "Colonial war") that began in October 1935 and ended in May 1936. The war began with the invasion of the [Ethiopian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Empire "Ethiopian Empire") (also known as [Abyssinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology_of_Ethiopia "Etymology of Ethiopia")) by the armed forces of the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy "Kingdom of Italy") (*Regno d'Italia*), which was launched from [Italian Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Somaliland "Italian Somaliland") and [Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Eritrea "Italian Eritrea"). The war resulted in the [military occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_occupation "Military occupation") of Ethiopia and its [annexation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation "Annexation") into the newly created colony of [Italian East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_East_Africa "Italian East Africa") (*Africa Orientale Italiana*); in addition it exposed the weakness of the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations") as a force to preserve peace. Both Italy and Ethiopia were member nations, [but the League did little](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appeasement "Appeasement") when the former clearly violated [Article X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_x "Article x") of the League's [Covenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covenant_of_the_League_of_Nations "Covenant of the League of Nations"). The United Kingdom and France supported imposing sanctions on Italy for the invasion, although the sanctions were not fully enforced and failed to end the Italian invasion. Italy subsequently dropped its objections to [Germany's goal of absorbing Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss "Anschluss").

### Spanish Civil War (1936–1939)

When civil war broke out in Spain, Hitler and Mussolini lent military support to the [Nationalist rebels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_faction_(Spanish_Civil_War) "Nationalist faction (Spanish Civil War)"), led by General [Francisco Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Franco "Francisco Franco"). Italy supported the Nationalists to a greater extent than the Nazis: Mussolini sent more than 70,000 ground troops, 6,000 aviation personnel, and 720 aircraft to Spain. The Soviet Union supported the existing government of the [Spanish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Republic "Spanish Republic"). More than 30,000 foreign volunteers, known as the [International Brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Brigades "International Brigades"), also fought against the Nationalists. Both Germany and the Soviet Union used this [proxy war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proxy_war "Proxy war") as an opportunity to test in combat their most advanced weapons and tactics. The Nationalists won the civil war in April 1939; Franco, now dictator, remained officially neutral during World War II but [generally favoured the Axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_during_World_War_II "Spain during World War II"). His largest collaboration with Germany was the sending of [volunteers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Division "Blue Division") to fight on the [Eastern Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_(World_War_II) "Eastern Front (World War II)").

### Japanese invasion of China (1937)

In July 1937, Japan captured the former Chinese imperial capital of [Peking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peking "Peking") after instigating the [Marco Polo Bridge incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Polo_Bridge_incident "Marco Polo Bridge incident"), which culminated in the Japanese campaign to invade all of China following years of [tension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_invasion_of_Manchuria "Japanese invasion of Manchuria") and [low-level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_28_incident "January 28 incident") [conflicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suiyuan_campaign "Suiyuan campaign"). The Soviets quickly signed a [non-aggression pact with China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Soviet_Non-Aggression_Pact "Sino-Soviet Non-Aggression Pact") to lend [materiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materiel "Materiel") support, effectively ending China's [prior cooperation with Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Germany_relations_(1912%E2%80%931949) "China–Germany relations (1912–1949)").

From September to November, the Japanese attacked [Taiyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Taiyuan "Battle of Taiyuan"), engaged the [Kuomintang Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuomintang_Army "Kuomintang Army") [around Xinkou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Xinkou "Battle of Xinkou"), fought [communist forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Communist_Party "Chinese Communist Party") [in Pingxingguan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Pingxingguan "Battle of Pingxingguan") and wrestled control over China's northern railway network. Nationalist [Generalissimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalissimo "Generalissimo") [Chiang Kai-shek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Kai-shek "Chiang Kai-shek") deployed his [best army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German-trained_divisions_of_the_National_Revolutionary_Army "List of German-trained divisions of the National Revolutionary Army") to [defend Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Shanghai "Battle of Shanghai"), but after three months of heavy fighting, Shanghai fell. The Japanese continued to push Chinese forces back, [capturing the capital Nanking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Nanking "Battle of Nanking") in December 1937.

In March 1938, Nationalist Chinese forces won their [first major victory at Taierzhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Taierzhuang "Battle of Taierzhuang"), but [ultimately lost control of the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Xuzhou "Battle of Xuzhou") of [Xuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuzhou "Xuzhou") in May. In June 1938, Chinese forces stalled the Japanese advance by [flooding the Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_Yellow_River_flood "1938 Yellow River flood"); buying time for the Chinese to prepare their defences at [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan "Wuhan") at heavy cost to the local civilian population, but the [city was taken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Wuhan "Battle of Wuhan") by October after heavy fighting along the Yangtze River.

Japanese military victories did not destroy [Chinese resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Japanese_resistance_volunteers_in_China "Anti-Japanese resistance volunteers in China"); instead, the Chinese government relocated inland to [Chongqing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongqing "Chongqing") and continued the war. Aiming to break Chinese morale, Japanese aircraft began [striking cities in the Sichuan basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Chongqing "Bombing of Chongqing") in a bombing campaign, killing tens of thousands of civilians.

### Soviet–Japanese border conflicts

In the mid-to-late 1930s, Japanese forces in [Manchukuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchukuo "Manchukuo") had sporadic border clashes with the Soviet Union and Mongolia. The Japanese doctrine of [Hokushin-ron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokushin-ron "Hokushin-ron"), which emphasised Japan's expansion northward, was favoured by the Imperial Army during this time. This policy would prove difficult to maintain in light of the Japanese defeat at [Khalkin Gol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_of_Khalkhin_Gol "Battles of Khalkhin Gol") in 1939, the ongoing Second [Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Japanese_War_(1931-1945) "Sino-Japanese War (1931-1945)") and ally Nazi Germany pursuing neutrality with the Soviets. Japan and the Soviet Union eventually signed a [Neutrality Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrality_Pact "Neutrality Pact") in April 1941, and Japan adopted the doctrine of [Nanshin-ron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanshin-ron "Nanshin-ron"), promoted by the Navy, which took its focus southward and eventually led to war with the United States and the Western Allies.

### European occupations and agreements

In Europe, Germany and Italy were becoming more aggressive. In March 1938, Germany [annexed Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss "Anschluss"), again provoking [little response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appeasement "Appeasement") from other European powers. Encouraged, Hitler began pressing German claims on the [Sudetenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudetenland "Sudetenland"), an area of [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia "Czechoslovakia") with a predominantly [ethnic German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans "Germans") population. Soon the United Kingdom and France followed the appeasement policy of British Prime Minister [Neville Chamberlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neville_Chamberlain "Neville Chamberlain") and conceded this territory to Germany in the [Munich Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Agreement "Munich Agreement"), which was made against the wishes of the Czechoslovak government, in exchange for a promise of no further territorial demands. Soon afterwards, Germany and Italy forced Czechoslovakia to [cede additional territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Vienna_Award "First Vienna Award") to Hungary, and Poland annexed the [Trans-Olza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Olza "Trans-Olza") region of Czechoslovakia.

Although all of Germany's stated demands had been satisfied by the agreement, privately Hitler was furious that British interference had prevented him from seizing all of Czechoslovakia in one operation. In subsequent speeches Hitler attacked British and Jewish "war-mongers" and in January 1939 [secretly ordered a major build-up of the German navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plan_Z "Plan Z") to challenge British naval supremacy. In March 1939, [Germany invaded the remainder of Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Czechoslovakia_(1938%E2%80%931945) "Occupation of Czechoslovakia (1938–1945)") and subsequently split it into the German [Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate_of_Bohemia_and_Moravia "Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia") and a pro-German [client state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Client_state "Client state"), the [Slovak Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovak_Republic_(1939%E2%80%931945) "Slovak Republic (1939–1945)"). Hitler also delivered an [ultimatum to Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1939_German_ultimatum_to_Lithuania "1939 German ultimatum to Lithuania") on 20 March 1939, forcing the concession of the [Klaipėda Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaip%C4%97da_Region "Klaipėda Region"), formerly the German *Memelland*.

Following further demands from Hitler regarding the [Free City of Danzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_City_of_Danzig "Free City of Danzig") and the [Polish corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_corridor "Polish corridor"), the United Kingdom and France [guaranteed their support for Polish independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Polish_alliance#British_assurance_to_Poland "Anglo-Polish alliance"). When [Italy conquered Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_invasion_of_Albania "Italian invasion of Albania") in April 1939, the same guarantee was extended to the [Kingdoms of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania "Kingdom of Romania") and [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Greece "Kingdom of Greece"). Shortly after the [Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Polish_alliance "Franco-Polish alliance")-[British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Polish_alliance "Anglo-Polish alliance") pledge to Poland, Germany and Italy formalised their own alliance with the [Pact of Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pact_of_Steel "Pact of Steel"). Hitler accused the United Kingdom and Poland of trying to encircle Germany and renounced the [Anglo-German Naval Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-German_Naval_Agreement "Anglo-German Naval Agreement") and the [German–Polish declaration of non-aggression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German%E2%80%93Polish_declaration_of_non-aggression "German–Polish declaration of non-aggression").

The situation became a crisis in late August as Germany concentrated its forces near the Polish border. On 23 August, the Soviet Union signed [a non-aggression pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molotov%E2%80%93Ribbentrop_Pact "Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact") with Germany, after tripartite negotiations for a military alliance between France, the United Kingdom, and Soviet Union had stalled. This pact had a secret protocol that defined German and Soviet spheres of influence: Lithuania for Germany; Finland, Estonia, Latvia, and [Bessarabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessarabia "Bessarabia") for the Soviet Union; and Poland to be partitioned between the two powers. The pact ensured that Germany would not face a war with the Soviet Union when it invaded Poland. Immediately afterwards, Hitler ordered the attack to proceed on 26 August, but upon hearing that the United Kingdom had concluded a formal mutual assistance pact with Poland and that Italy would maintain neutrality, he decided to delay it.

Germany offered Britain an alliance if Britain helped Germany gain [Danzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gda%C5%84sk "Gdańsk") (Gdańsk), the Polish corridor and its former colonies, but Britain replied that it would honour its guarantee to Poland. On 29 August, Hitler demanded that a Polish [plenipotentiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plenipotentiary "Plenipotentiary") travel to Berlin by the following day to negotiate a solution to the crisis, but Britain rejected the timeline as unreasonable. On the night of 30–31 August, the British ambassador [Nevile Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevile_Henderson "Nevile Henderson") met German Foreign Minister [Joachim von Ribbentrop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joachim_von_Ribbentrop "Joachim von Ribbentrop"). Ribbentrop handed him Hitler's demands regarding Poland then announced that the deadline for Poland's acceptance had already passed.

## Course of the war

### War breaks out in Europe (1939–1940)

On 1 September 1939, Germany [invaded Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invaded_Poland "Invaded Poland") after [having staged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Himmler "Operation Himmler") several [false flag border incidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gleiwitz_incident "Gleiwitz incident") as a pretext to initiate the invasion. The first German attack of the war came [against the Polish defences at Westerplatte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Westerplatte "Battle of Westerplatte"). The United Kingdom responded with an [ultimatum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimatum "Ultimatum") for Germany to cease military operations, and on 3 September, after the ultimatum was ignored, Britain and France declared war on Germany. During the [Phoney War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoney_War "Phoney War") period, the alliance provided no direct military support to Poland, outside of a [cautious French probe into the Saarland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saar_Offensive "Saar Offensive"). The Western Allies also began a [naval blockade of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockade_of_Germany_(1939%E2%80%931945) "Blockade of Germany (1939–1945)"), which aimed to damage the country's economy and war effort. Germany responded by ordering [U-boat warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_warfare#World_War_II "Submarine warfare") against Allied merchant and warships, which would later escalate into the [Battle of the Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Atlantic "Battle of the Atlantic").
On 8 September, German troops reached the suburbs of [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw "Warsaw"). The [Polish counter-offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Bzura "Battle of the Bzura") to the west halted the German advance for several days, but it was outflanked and encircled by the *[Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht "Wehrmacht")*. Remnants of the Polish army broke through to [besieged Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Warsaw_(1939) "Siege of Warsaw (1939)"). On 17 September 1939, two days after signing a [cease-fire with Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_of_Khalkhin_Gol "Battles of Khalkhin Gol"), the [Soviet Union invaded Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_Poland "Soviet invasion of Poland") under the supposed pretext that the Polish state had ceased to exist. On 27 September, the Warsaw garrison surrendered to the Germans, and [the last large operational unit of the Polish Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Operational_Group_Polesie "Independent Operational Group Polesie") [surrendered on 6 October](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kock_(1939) "Battle of Kock (1939)"). Despite the military defeat, Poland never surrendered; instead, it formed the [Polish government-in-exile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_government-in-exile "Polish government-in-exile") and a [clandestine state apparatus remained](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Underground_State "Polish Underground State") in occupied Poland. A significant part of Polish military personnel [evacuated to Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Bridgehead "Romanian Bridgehead") and Latvia; many of them later [fought against the Axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_Poland_during_World_War_II "Military history of Poland during World War II") in other theatres of the war.

Germany [annexed western Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_areas_annexed_by_Nazi_Germany "Polish areas annexed by Nazi Germany") and [occupied central Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Government "General Government"); the Soviet Union [annexed eastern Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territories_of_Poland_annexed_by_the_Soviet_Union "Territories of Poland annexed by the Soviet Union"). Small shares of Polish territory were transferred to [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_evolution_of_Poland#World_War_II "Territorial evolution of Poland") and [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovak_invasion_of_Poland "Slovak invasion of Poland"). On 6 October, Hitler made a public peace overture to the United Kingdom and France but said that the future of Poland was to be determined exclusively by Germany and the Soviet Union. The proposal was rejected and Hitler ordered an immediate offensive against France, which was postponed until the spring of 1940 due to bad weather.

After the outbreak of war in Poland, Stalin threatened Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania with military invasion, forcing the three [Baltic countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_countries "Baltic countries") to sign [pacts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Background_of_the_occupation_of_the_Baltic_states#Soviet_ultimatums_and_occupation "Background of the occupation of the Baltic states") allowing the creation of Soviet military bases in these countries; in October 1939, significant Soviet military contingents were moved there. Finland refused to sign a similar pact and rejected ceding part of its territory to the Soviet Union. [The Soviet Union invaded Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_War#Soviet_invasion "Winter War") in November 1939, and was subsequently expelled from the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations") for this crime of aggression. Despite overwhelming numerical superiority, Soviet military success during the [Winter War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_War "Winter War") was modest, and the Finno–Soviet war ended in March 1940 with [some Finnish concessions of territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Peace_Treaty "Moscow Peace Treaty").

In June 1940, the Soviet Union [occupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_occupation_of_the_Baltic_states_(1940) "Soviet occupation of the Baltic states (1940)") the entire territories of Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania, as well as the Romanian regions of [Bessarabia, Northern Bukovina, and the Hertsa region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_occupation_of_Bessarabia_and_Northern_Bukovina "Soviet occupation of Bessarabia and Northern Bukovina"). In August 1940, Hitler imposed the [Second Vienna Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vienna_Award "Second Vienna Award") on Romania which led to the transfer of [Northern Transylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Transylvania "Northern Transylvania") to Hungary. In September 1940, Bulgaria demanded [Southern Dobruja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Dobruja "Southern Dobruja") from Romania with German and Italian support, leading to the [Treaty of Craiova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Craiova "Treaty of Craiova"). The loss of one-third of Romania's 1939 territory caused a coup against King [Carol II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_II "Carol II"), turning Romania into a fascist dictatorship under Marshal [Ion Antonescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_Antonescu "Ion Antonescu"), with a course set towards the Axis in the hopes of a German guarantee. Meanwhile, German–Soviet political relations and economic co-operation gradually stalled, and both states began preparations for war.

### Western Europe (1940–1941)

In April 1940, [Germany invaded Denmark and Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Weser%C3%BCbung "Operation Weserübung") to protect shipments of [iron ore from Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_iron-ore_industry_during_World_War_II "Swedish iron-ore industry during World War II"), which the Allies were [attempting to cut off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Wilfred "Operation Wilfred"). [Denmark capitulated after six hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_invasion_of_Denmark_(1940) "German invasion of Denmark (1940)"), and [despite Allied support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_campaign "Norwegian campaign"), Norway was conquered within two months. [British discontent over the Norwegian campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway_Debate "Norway Debate") led to the resignation of Prime Minister [Neville Chamberlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neville_Chamberlain "Neville Chamberlain"), who was replaced by [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill "Winston Churchill") on 10 May 1940.

On the same day, Germany [launched an offensive against France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_France "Battle of France"). To circumvent the strong [Maginot Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maginot_Line "Maginot Line") fortifications on the Franco-German border, Germany directed its attack at the neutral nations of [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_invasion_of_Belgium_(1940) "German invasion of Belgium (1940)"), the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_invasion_of_the_Netherlands "German invasion of the Netherlands"), and [Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_invasion_of_Luxembourg "German invasion of Luxembourg"). The Germans carried out a flanking manoeuvre through the [Ardennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardennes "Ardennes") region, which was mistakenly perceived by the Allies as an impenetrable natural barrier against armoured vehicles. By successfully implementing new *[Blitzkrieg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blitzkrieg "Blitzkrieg")* tactics, [the *Wehrmacht*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht "Wehrmacht") rapidly advanced to the Channel and cut off the Allied forces in Belgium, trapping the bulk of the Allied armies in a cauldron on the Franco-Belgian border near Lille. The United Kingdom was able [to evacuate a significant number of Allied troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunkirk_evacuation "Dunkirk evacuation") from the continent by early June, although they had to abandon almost all their equipment.

On 10 June, [Italy invaded France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_invasion_of_France "Italian invasion of France"), declaring war on both France and the United Kingdom. The Germans turned south against the weakened French army, and [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_in_World_War_II "Paris in World War II") fell to them on 14 June. Eight days later [France signed an armistice with Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_22_June_1940 "Armistice of 22 June 1940"); it was divided into [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_military_administration_in_occupied_France_during_World_War_II "German military administration in occupied France during World War II") and [Italian occupation zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_occupation_of_France "Italian occupation of France"), and an unoccupied [rump state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rump_state "Rump state") under the [Vichy Regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_Regime "Vichy Regime"), which, though officially neutral, was generally aligned with Germany. France kept its fleet, which the [United Kingdom attacked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Mers-el-K%C3%A9bir "Attack on Mers-el-Kébir") on 3 July in an attempt to prevent its seizure by Germany.

The air [Battle of Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Britain "Battle of Britain") began in early July with [Luftwaffe attacks on shipping and harbours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanalkampf "Kanalkampf"). The [German campaign for air superiority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adlertag "Adlertag") started in August but its failure to defeat [RAF Fighter Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Fighter_Command "RAF Fighter Command") forced the indefinite postponement of the [proposed German invasion of Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Sea_Lion "Operation Sea Lion"). The German [strategic bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_bombing "Strategic bombing") offensive intensified with night attacks on London and other cities in [the Blitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Blitz "The Blitz"), but largely ended in May 1941 after failing to significantly disrupt the British war effort.

Using newly captured French ports, the German Navy [enjoyed success](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Atlantic#June_1940_–_February_1941_(The_Happy_Time) "Battle of the Atlantic") against an over-extended [Royal Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Navy "Royal Navy"), using [U-boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-boat "U-boat") against British shipping [in the Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Atlantic "Battle of the Atlantic"). The British [Home Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Fleet "Home Fleet") scored a significant victory on 27 May 1941 by [sinking the German battleship *Bismarck*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_battle_of_Bismarck "Last battle of Bismarck").

In November 1939, the United States was assisting China and the Western Allies, and had amended the [Neutrality Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrality_Acts_of_the_1930s "Neutrality Acts of the 1930s") to allow "[cash and carry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_and_carry_(World_War_II) "Cash and carry (World War II)")" purchases by the Allies. In 1940, following the German capture of Paris, the size of the [United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy "United States Navy") was [significantly increased](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-Ocean_Navy_Act "Two-Ocean Navy Act"). In September the United States further agreed to a [trade of American destroyers for British bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destroyers-for-bases_deal "Destroyers-for-bases deal"). Still, a large majority of the American public continued to oppose any direct military intervention in the conflict well into 1941. In December 1940, President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt "Franklin D. Roosevelt") accused Hitler of planning world conquest and ruled out any negotiations as useless, calling for the United States to become an "[arsenal of democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_of_democracy "Arsenal of democracy")" and promoting [Lend-Lease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lend-Lease "Lend-Lease") programmes of military and humanitarian aid to support the British war effort; Lend-Lease was later extended to the other Allies, including the Soviet Union after it was [invaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa "Operation Barbarossa") by Germany. The United States started strategic planning to prepare for a full-scale offensive against Germany.

At the end of September 1940, the [Tripartite Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripartite_Pact "Tripartite Pact") formally united Japan, Italy, and Germany as the [Axis powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers "Axis powers"). The Tripartite Pact stipulated that any country—with the exception of the Soviet Union—that attacked any Axis Power would be forced to go to war against all three. The Axis expanded in November 1940 when [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary_(1920%E2%80%931946) "Kingdom of Hungary (1920–1946)"), [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovak_Republic_(1939%E2%80%931945) "Slovak Republic (1939–1945)"), and [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania "Kingdom of Romania") joined. [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania_in_World_War_II "Romania in World War II") and [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_in_World_War_II "Hungary in World War II") later made major contributions to the Axis war against the Soviet Union, in Romania's case partially to recapture [territory ceded to the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_occupation_of_Bessarabia_and_Northern_Bukovina "Soviet occupation of Bessarabia and Northern Bukovina").

### Mediterranean (1940–1941)

In early June 1940, the Italian *[Regia Aeronautica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regia_Aeronautica "Regia Aeronautica")* [attacked and besieged Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Malta_(World_War_II) "Siege of Malta (World War II)"), a British possession. From late summer to early autumn, Italy [conquered British Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_invasion_of_British_Somaliland "Italian invasion of British Somaliland") and made an [incursion into British-held Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_invasion_of_Egypt "Italian invasion of Egypt"). In October, [Italy attacked Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Italian_War "Greco-Italian War"), but the attack was repulsed with heavy Italian casualties; the campaign ended within months with minor territorial changes. To assist Italy and prevent Britain from gaining a foothold, Germany prepared to invade the Balkans, which would threaten Romanian oil fields and strike against British dominance of the Mediterranean.

In December 1940, British Empire forces began counter-offensives against [Italian forces in Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Compass "Operation Compass") and [Italian East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_campaign_(World_War_II) "East African campaign (World War II)"). The offensives were successful; by early February 1941, Italy had lost control of eastern Libya, and large numbers of Italian troops had been taken prisoner. The [Italian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regia_Marina "Regia Marina") also suffered significant defeats, with the Royal Navy putting three Italian battleships out of commission after a [carrier attack at Taranto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Taranto "Battle of Taranto"), and neutralising several more warships at the [Battle of Cape Matapan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cape_Matapan "Battle of Cape Matapan").

Italian defeats prompted Germany to [deploy an expeditionary force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Sonnenblume "Operation Sonnenblume") to North Africa; at the end of March 1941, [Erwin Rommel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Rommel "Erwin Rommel")'s [Afrika Korps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrika_Korps "Afrika Korps") [launched an offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Desert_campaign "Western Desert campaign") which drove back Commonwealth forces. In less than a month, Axis forces advanced to western Egypt and [besieged the port of Tobruk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Tobruk "Siege of Tobruk"). In November Commonwealth forces launched a [counter-offensive in North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Crusader "Operation Crusader") and reclaimed all the gains the Germans and Italians had made.

By late March 1941, [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsardom_of_Bulgaria_(1908%E2%80%931946) "Tsardom of Bulgaria (1908–1946)") and [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia "Yugoslavia") signed the [Tripartite Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripartite_Pact "Tripartite Pact"); however, the Yugoslav government was [overthrown two days later](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat "Yugoslav coup d'état") by pro-British nationalists. Germany and Italy responded with simultaneous invasions of both [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Yugoslavia "Invasion of Yugoslavia") and [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_invasion_of_Greece "German invasion of Greece"), commencing on 6 April 1941 with a [massive bombing of Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_bombing_of_Belgrade "German bombing of Belgrade"); both nations were forced to surrender within the month. The airborne [invasion of the Greek island of Crete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Crete "Battle of Crete") at the end of May completed the German conquest of the Balkans. Armed resistance soon emerged in [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_in_Yugoslavia "World War II in Yugoslavia") and [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_resistance "Greek resistance") and continued until the end of the war.

In the Middle East in May, Commonwealth forces [quashed an uprising in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Iraqi_War "Anglo-Iraqi War") which had been supported by German aircraft from bases within Vichy-controlled [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_for_Syria_and_the_Lebanon "Mandate for Syria and the Lebanon"). Between June and July, British-led forces [invaded the French possessions of Syria and Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign "Syria–Lebanon campaign"), assisted by the [Free French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_French "Free French").

### Axis attack on the Soviet Union (1941)

With the situation in Europe and Asia relatively stable, Germany, Japan, and the Soviet Union made preparations for war. With the Soviets wary of mounting tensions with Germany, and the Japanese planning to take advantage of the European War by seizing resource-rich European possessions in Southeast Asia, the two powers signed the [Soviet–Japanese Neutrality Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Japanese_Neutrality_Pact "Soviet–Japanese Neutrality Pact") in April 1941. By contrast, the Germans were steadily making preparations for an attack on the Soviet Union, massing forces on the Soviet border.

Hitler believed that the United Kingdom's refusal to end the war was based on the hope that the United States and the Soviet Union would enter the war against Germany. On 31 July 1940, Hitler decided that the Soviet Union should be eliminated and aimed for the conquest of [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic "Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic"), the [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states "Baltic states"), and [Byelorussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byelorussian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic "Byelorussian Soviet Socialist Republic"). However, other senior German officials like Ribbentrop saw an opportunity to create a Euro-Asian bloc against the British Empire by inviting the Soviet Union into the Tripartite Pact. In November 1940, [negotiations took place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German%E2%80%93Soviet_Axis_talks "German–Soviet Axis talks") to determine if the Soviet Union would join the pact. The Soviets showed some interest but asked for concessions from Finland, Bulgaria, Turkey, and Japan that Germany considered unacceptable. On 18 December 1940, Hitler issued the directive to prepare for an invasion of the Soviet Union. On 22 June 1941, Germany, supported by Italy and Romania, invaded the Soviet Union in [Operation Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa "Operation Barbarossa"), with Germany accusing the Soviets of [plotting against them](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_offensive_plans_controversy "Soviet offensive plans controversy"); they were joined shortly by Finland and Hungary. The primary targets of this surprise offensive were the [Baltic region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_region "Baltic region"), Moscow and Ukraine, with the [ultimate goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_goal_(military) "Strategic goal (military)") of ending the 1941 campaign near the [Arkhangelsk–Astrakhan line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%E2%80%93A_line "A–A line")—from the [Caspian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_Sea "Caspian Sea") to the [White Seas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sea "White Sea"). Hitler's objectives were to eliminate the Soviet Union as a military power, exterminate [communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism "Communism"), generate *[Lebensraum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebensraum "Lebensraum")* ("living space") by [dispossessing the native population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalplan_Ost "Generalplan Ost"), and guarantee access to the strategic resources needed to defeat Germany's remaining rivals.

Although the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army "Red Army") was preparing for strategic [counter-offensives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-offensive "Counter-offensive") before the war, Operation Barbarossa forced the [Soviet supreme command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stavka "Stavka") to adopt [strategic defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_defence "Strategic defence"). During the summer, the Axis made significant gains into Soviet territory, inflicting immense losses in both personnel and materiel, mainly in massive [encirclements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encirclement "Encirclement") around [Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bia%C5%82ystok%E2%80%93Minsk "Battle of Białystok–Minsk"), [Smolensk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Smolensk_(1941) "Battle of Smolensk (1941)"), and [Uman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Uman "Battle of Uman").

Nazi policy entailed that Wehrmacht subject Soviet [POWs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner_of_war "Prisoner of war") (Prisoners of war) to murderous treatment, executing all Jewish and Communist POWs immediately per the [Commissar Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissar_Order "Commissar Order"), and subjecting the remainder to forced marches to open-air concentration camps, where they were to be [deliberately starved to death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_atrocities_committed_against_Soviet_prisoners_of_war "German atrocities committed against Soviet prisoners of war"). By the end of the winter of 1941, 2.8 million Soviet POWs had died in German captivity. Some 3.3 million Soviet POWs would die in German captivity by the war's end in total, a nearly 60% mortality rate.

By mid-August, however, the German [Army High Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberkommando_des_Heeres "Oberkommando des Heeres") decided to [suspend the offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Smolensk_(1941) "Battle of Smolensk (1941)") of a considerably depleted [Army Group Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Group_Centre "Army Group Centre"), and to divert the [2nd Panzer Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Panzer_Army "2nd Panzer Army") to reinforce troops advancing towards central Ukraine and Leningrad. The [Kiev offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kiev_(1941) "Battle of Kiev (1941)") was overwhelmingly successful, resulting in encirclement and elimination of four Soviet armies, and made possible further [advance into Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_campaign "Crimean campaign") and industrially-developed eastern Ukraine (the [First Battle of Kharkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Battle_of_Kharkov "First Battle of Kharkov")).

The diversion of three-quarters of the Axis troops and the majority of their air forces from France and the central Mediterranean to the [Eastern Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_(World_War_II) "Eastern Front (World War II)") prompted the United Kingdom to reconsider its [grand strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_strategy "Grand strategy"). In July, the UK and the Soviet Union formed a [military alliance against Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Soviet_Agreement "Anglo-Soviet Agreement") and in August, the United Kingdom and the United States jointly issued the [Atlantic Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Charter "Atlantic Charter"), which outlined British and American goals for the post-war world. In late August the British and Soviets [invaded neutral Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Soviet_invasion_of_Iran "Anglo-Soviet invasion of Iran") to secure the [Persian Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Corridor "Persian Corridor"), Iran's [oil fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_fields "Oil fields"), and preempt any Axis advances through Iran toward the Baku oil fields or India.

By October, Axis powers had achieved [operational objectives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_objective "Operational objective") in Ukraine and the Baltic region, with only the sieges of [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Leningrad "Siege of Leningrad") and [Sevastopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Sevastopol_(1941%E2%80%931942) "Siege of Sevastopol (1941–1942)") continuing. A major [offensive against Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Moscow "Battle of Moscow") was renewed; after two months of fierce battles in increasingly harsh weather, the German army almost reached the outer suburbs of Moscow, where the exhausted troops were forced to suspend the offensive. Large territorial gains were made by Axis forces, but their campaign had failed to achieve its main objectives: two key cities remained in Soviet hands, the Soviet [capability to resist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_capability "Military capability") was not broken, and the Soviet Union retained a considerable part of its military potential. The *blitzkrieg* [phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_(combat) "Phase (combat)") of the war in Europe had ended.

By early December, freshly mobilised [reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_reserve_force "Military reserve force") allowed the Soviets to achieve numerical parity with Axis troops. This, as well as [intelligence data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Sorge#Wartime_intelligence "Richard Sorge") which established that a minimal number of Soviet troops in the East would be sufficient to deter any attack by the Japanese [Kwantung Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwantung_Army "Kwantung Army"), allowed the Soviets to begin a [massive counter-offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_campaign_of_1941%E2%80%931942 "Winter campaign of 1941–1942") that started on 5 December all along the front and pushed German troops 100–250 kilometres (62–155 mi) west.

### War breaks out in the Pacific (1941)

Following the Japanese [false flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_flag "False flag") [Mukden incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukden_incident "Mukden incident") in 1931, the Japanese shelling of the American [gunboat USS *Panay*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Panay_incident "USS Panay incident") in 1937, and the 1937–1938 [Nanjing massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_massacre "Nanjing massacre"), [Japanese-American relations deteriorated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan%E2%80%93United_States_relations#1937–1941 "Japan–United States relations"). In 1939, the United States notified Japan that it would not be extending its trade treaty and American public opinion opposing [Japanese expansionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_militarism "Japanese militarism") led to a series of economic sanctions—the [Export Control Acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Export_Control_Act "Export Control Act")—which banned US exports of chemicals, minerals and military parts to Japan, and increased economic pressure on the Japanese regime. During 1939 Japan launched its [first attack against Changsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Changsha_(1939) "Battle of Changsha (1939)"), but was repulsed by late September. Despite [several offensives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1939%E2%80%931940_Winter_Offensive "1939–1940 Winter Offensive") by both sides, by 1940 the war between China and Japan was at a stalemate. To increase pressure on China by blocking supply routes, and to better position Japanese forces in the event of a war with the Western powers, Japan invaded and [occupied northern Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_invasion_of_French_Indochina "Japanese invasion of French Indochina") in September 1940.

Chinese nationalist forces launched a large-scale [counter-offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1939%E2%80%931940_Winter_Offensive "1939–1940 Winter Offensive") in early 1940. In August, [Chinese communists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_communists "Chinese communists") launched an [offensive in Central China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Regiments_Offensive "Hundred Regiments Offensive"); in retaliation, Japanese armies in North China implemented the [Three Alls policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Alls_policy "Three Alls policy"), a massive scorched earth initiative to depopulate regions deemed hostile to Japanese occupation. Continued antipathy between Chinese communist and nationalist forces [culminated in armed clashes in January 1941](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Fourth_Army_incident "New Fourth Army incident"), effectively ending their co-operation. In March, the Japanese 11th army attacked the headquarters of the nationalist Chinese 19th army but was repulsed during the [Battle of Shanggao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Shanggao "Battle of Shanggao"). In September, Japan attempted to [take the city of Changsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Changsha_(1941) "Battle of Changsha (1941)") again and clashed with Chinese nationalist forces.

German successes in Europe prompted Japan to increase pressure on European governments in Southeast Asia. The Dutch government agreed to provide Japan with oil supplies from the [Dutch East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_Indies "Dutch East Indies"), but negotiations for additional access to their resources ended in failure in June 1941. In July 1941 Japan sent troops to southern Indochina, threatening British and Dutch possessions in the Far East. The United States, the United Kingdom, and other Western governments reacted to this move with a freeze on Japanese assets and a total oil [embargo](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/embargo "wikt:embargo"). At the same time, Japan was [planning an invasion of the Soviet Far East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kantokuen "Kantokuen"), intending to take advantage of the German invasion in the west, but abandoned the operation after the sanctions.

Since early 1941, the United States and Japan had been engaged in negotiations in an attempt to improve their strained relations and end the war in China. Japan advanced a number of proposals which were dismissed by the Americans as inadequate. At the same time the United States, the United Kingdom, and the Netherlands engaged in secret discussions for the joint defence of their territories, in the event of a Japanese attack against any of them. Roosevelt reinforced the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_the_Philippines "Commonwealth of the Philippines") (an American protectorate scheduled for independence in 1946) and warned Japan that the United States would react to Japanese attacks against any "neighboring countries".

Frustrated at the lack of progress and pressured by American–British–Dutch sanctions, especially in oil, Japan prepared for war. Emperor [Hirohito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirohito "Hirohito"), after initial hesitation about Japan's chances of victory, began to favour Japan's entry into the war. As a result, Prime Minister [Fumimaro Konoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fumimaro_Konoe "Fumimaro Konoe") resigned. Hirohito refused the recommendation to appoint [Prince Naruhiko Higashikuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Naruhiko_Higashikuni "Prince Naruhiko Higashikuni") in his place, choosing War Minister [Hideki Tojo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hideki_Tojo "Hideki Tojo") instead. On 3 November, [Osami Nagano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osami_Nagano "Osami Nagano") explained in detail the plan of the [attack on Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor "Attack on Pearl Harbor") to the Emperor. On 5 November, Hirohito approved in imperial conference the operations plan for the war. On 20 November, the new government presented an interim proposal as its final offer. It called for the end of American aid to China and for lifting the embargo on the supply of oil and other resources to Japan. In exchange, Japan promised not to launch any attacks in Southeast Asia and to withdraw its forces from southern Indochina. The American counter-proposal of 26 November required that Japan evacuate all of China without conditions and conclude non-aggression pacts with all Pacific powers. That meant Japan was essentially forced to choose between abandoning its ambitions in China, or seizing the natural resources it needed in the [Dutch East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_Indies "Dutch East Indies") by force; the Japanese military did not consider the former an option, and many officers considered the oil embargo an unspoken declaration of war.

Japan planned to seize European colonies in Asia to create a large defensive perimeter stretching into the Central Pacific. The Japanese would then be free to exploit the resources of Southeast Asia while exhausting the over-stretched Allies by fighting a defensive war. To prevent American intervention while securing the perimeter, it was further planned to neutralise the [United States Pacific Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Pacific_Fleet "United States Pacific Fleet") and the American military presence in the Philippines from the outset. On 7 December 1941 (8 December in Asian time zones), Japan attacked British and American holdings with near-simultaneous [offensives against Southeast Asia and the Central Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_expansion_(1941%E2%80%9342) "Japanese expansion (1941–42)"). These included an [attack on the American fleets at Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor "Attack on Pearl Harbor") and the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_campaign_(1941%E2%80%931942) "Philippines campaign (1941–1942)"), as well as invasions of [Guam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Guam_(1941) "Battle of Guam (1941)"), [Wake Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Wake_Island "Battle of Wake Island"), [Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayan_campaign "Malayan campaign"), [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_invasion_of_Thailand "Japanese invasion of Thailand"), and [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Hong_Kong "Battle of Hong Kong").

These attacks led the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_declaration_of_war_on_Japan "United States declaration of war on Japan"), [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_declaration_of_war_on_Japan "United Kingdom declaration of war on Japan"), China, Australia, and several other states to formally declare war on Japan, whereas the Soviet Union, being heavily involved in large-scale hostilities with European Axis countries, maintained its neutrality agreement with Japan. Germany, followed by the other Axis states, declared war on the United States in solidarity with Japan, citing as justification the American attacks on German war vessels that had been ordered by Roosevelt.

### Axis advance stalls (1942–1943)

On 1 January 1942, the [Allied Big Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Policemen "Four Policemen")—the Soviet Union, China, the United Kingdom, and the United States—and 22 smaller or exiled governments issued the [Declaration by United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_by_United_Nations "Declaration by United Nations"), thereby affirming the [Atlantic Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Charter "Atlantic Charter") and agreeing not to sign a [separate peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separate_peace "Separate peace") with the Axis powers.

During 1942, Allied officials debated on the appropriate [grand strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_strategy "Grand strategy") to pursue. All agreed that [defeating Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe_first "Europe first") was the primary objective. The Americans favoured a straightforward, [large-scale attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Sledgehammer "Operation Sledgehammer") on Germany through France. The Soviets demanded a second front. The British argued that military operations should target peripheral areas to wear out German strength, leading to increasing demoralisation, and bolstering [resistance forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resistance_during_World_War_II "Resistance during World War II"); Germany itself would be subject to a heavy bombing campaign. An offensive against Germany would then be launched primarily by Allied armour, without using large-scale armies. Eventually, the British persuaded the Americans that a landing in France was infeasible in 1942 and they should instead focus on driving the Axis out of North Africa.

At the [Casablanca Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca_Conference "Casablanca Conference") in early 1943, the Allies reiterated the statements issued in the 1942 Declaration and demanded the [unconditional surrender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconditional_surrender "Unconditional surrender") of their enemies. The British and Americans agreed to continue to press the initiative in the Mediterranean by invading Sicily to fully secure the Mediterranean supply routes. Although the British argued for further operations in the Balkans to bring Turkey into the war, in May 1943, the Americans extracted a British commitment to limit Allied operations in the Mediterranean to an invasion of the Italian mainland, and to invade France in 1944.

### Pacific (1942–1943)

Japanese forces achieved naval victories in the [South China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinking_of_Prince_of_Wales_and_Repulse "Sinking of Prince of Wales and Repulse"), [Java Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Java_Sea "Battle of the Java Sea"), and [Indian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean_raid "Indian Ocean raid"), and [bombed the Allied naval base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Darwin "Bombing of Darwin") at [Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin,_Northern_Territory "Darwin, Northern Territory"), Australia. On 16 April, 7,000 British soldiers were encircled by the Japanese 33rd Division during the [Battle of Yenangyaung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Yenangyaung "Battle of Yenangyaung") in Burma and rescued by the Chinese 38th Division. Despite stubborn [resistance by Filipino and US forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_campaign_(1941%E2%80%931942) "Philippines campaign (1941–1942)"), the [Philippine Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Commonwealth "Philippine Commonwealth") was eventually captured in May, forcing its government into exile. Following the capture of Bataan, Japanese armies forced some 75,000 Filipino and American prisoners on a [42km death march](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bataan_Death_March "Bataan Death March"), resulting in thousands of deaths.

By the end of April, Japan and its ally [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand_in_World_War_II "Thailand in World War II") had conquered [Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayan_campaign "Malayan campaign"), [the Dutch East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_Indies_campaign "Dutch East Indies campaign"), [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Singapore "Fall of Singapore"), [Rabaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Rabaul_(1942) "Battle of Rabaul (1942)"), and most of [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_invasion_of_Burma "Japanese invasion of Burma"), inflicting severe losses on Allied troops and taking a large number of prisoners. Japanese advances were accompanied by numerous atrocities, including the [Sook Ching massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sook_Ching_massacre "Sook Ching massacre") in Singapore. The only Allied success against Japan was a Chinese [victory at Changsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Changsha_(1941%E2%80%931942) "Battle of Changsha (1941–1942)"). The Japanese victories left it overconfident and overextended.

In early May 1942, Japan initiated operations to [capture Port Moresby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Mo "Operation Mo") by [amphibious assault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_assault "Amphibious assault") and thus sever communications and supply lines between the United States and Australia. The planned invasion was thwarted when an Allied task force, centred on two American fleet carriers, fought Japanese naval forces to a draw in the [Battle of the Coral Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Coral_Sea "Battle of the Coral Sea"). Japan's next plan, motivated by the earlier [Doolittle Raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doolittle_Raid "Doolittle Raid"), was to seize [Midway Atoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midway_Atoll "Midway Atoll") and lure American carriers into battle to be eliminated; as a diversion, Japan would also send forces to [occupy the Aleutian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleutian_Islands_campaign "Aleutian Islands campaign") in Alaska. In mid-May, Japan started the [Zhejiang-Jiangxi campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhejiang-Jiangxi_campaign "Zhejiang-Jiangxi campaign") in China, with the goal of inflicting retribution on the Chinese who aided the surviving American airmen in the Doolittle Raid by destroying Chinese air bases and fighting against the Chinese 23rd and 32nd Army Groups. In early June, Japan put its operations into action, but the Americans had broken [Japanese naval codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_naval_codes "Japanese naval codes") in late May and were fully aware of the plans and order of battle, and used this knowledge to achieve a decisive [victory at Midway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Midway "Battle of Midway") over the [Imperial Japanese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Navy "Imperial Japanese Navy").

With its capacity for aggressive action greatly diminished as a result of the Midway battle, Japan attempted to capture [Port Moresby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Moresby "Port Moresby") by an [overland campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokoda_Track_campaign "Kokoda Track campaign") in the [Territory of Papua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Papua "Territory of Papua"). The Americans planned a counterattack against Japanese positions in the southern Solomon Islands, primarily [Guadalcanal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalcanal "Guadalcanal"), as a first step towards capturing [Rabaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabaul "Rabaul"), the main Japanese base in the South Pacific.

Both plans started in July, but by mid-September, the [Battle for Guadalcanal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalcanal_campaign "Guadalcanal campaign") took priority for the Japanese, and troops in New Guinea were ordered to withdraw from the Port Moresby area to the [northern part of the island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oro_Province "Oro Province"), where they faced Australian and United States troops in the [Battle of Buna–Gona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Buna%E2%80%93Gona "Battle of Buna–Gona"). Guadalcanal soon became a focal point for both sides with heavy commitments of troops and ships in the battle for Guadalcanal, with Japanese forces suffering massive losses in the attrition, especially amongst their elite pilots. By the start of 1943, the Japanese were defeated on the island and [withdrew their troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Ke "Operation Ke"). In Burma, Commonwealth forces mounted two operations. The first was a disastrous [offensive into the Arakan region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakan_campaign_(1942%E2%80%931943) "Arakan campaign (1942–1943)") in late 1942 that forced a retreat back to India by May 1943. The second was the [insertion of irregular forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Longcloth "Operation Longcloth") behind Japanese frontlines in February which, by the end of April, had achieved mixed results.

### Eastern Front (1942–1943)

Despite considerable losses, in early 1942 Germany and its allies stopped a major Soviet offensive in [central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Russia "Central Russia") and [southern Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Russia "Southern Russia"), keeping most territorial gains they had achieved during the previous year. In May, the Germans defeated Soviet offensives in the [Kerch Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Kerch_Peninsula "Battle of the Kerch Peninsula") and at [Kharkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_Kharkov "Second Battle of Kharkov"). The fortress city of Sevastopol, which the Red Army had held out against Axis siege for nearly 250 days, was finally seized with the use of massive artillery bombardments and poison gas.

In June 1942 Germany launched its main [summer offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Blue "Case Blue") against southern Russia, to seize the [oil fields of the Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_industry_in_Azerbaijan "Petroleum industry in Azerbaijan") and occupy the [Kuban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuban "Kuban") [steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe "Steppe"), while maintaining positions on the northern and central areas of the front. The Germans split [Army Group South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Group_South "Army Group South") into two groups: [Army Group A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Group_A "Army Group A") advanced to the lower [Don River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_River "Don River") and struck south-east to the Caucasus, while [Army Group B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Group_B "Army Group B") headed towards the [Volga River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_River "Volga River"). The Soviet Union decided to make its stand at Stalingrad on the Volga.

By mid-November, the Germans had [nearly taken Stalingrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Stalingrad "Battle of Stalingrad") in bitter [street fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_warfare "Urban warfare"). The Soviet Union began its second winter counter-offensive, starting with an [encirclement of the German Sixth Army at Stalingrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Uranus "Operation Uranus"), and an assault on the [Rzhev salient near Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Mars "Operation Mars"), though the latter failed. By early February 1943, the German army had taken tremendous losses; German troops at Stalingrad had been defeated, and the front-line had been pushed back beyond its position before the summer offensive. In mid-February, after the Soviet push had tapered off, the Germans launched another [attack on Kharkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Battle_of_Kharkov "Third Battle of Kharkov"), creating a [salient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salient_(military) "Salient (military)") in their front line around the Soviet city of [Kursk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk "Kursk").

### Western Europe/Atlantic and Mediterranean (1942–1943)

Exploiting poor American naval command decisions, [the German navy ravaged Allied shipping off the American Atlantic coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Happy_Time "Second Happy Time"). The Germans launched a North African offensive in January 1942, pushing the British back to positions at the [Gazala line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gazala#Gazala_line "Battle of Gazala") by early February, followed by a temporary lull in combat which Germany used to prepare for their upcoming offensives. Concerns that the Japanese might use bases in [Vichy-held Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Madagascar "French Madagascar") caused the British to [invade the island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Madagascar "Battle of Madagascar") in early May 1942. An Axis [offensive in Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gazala "Battle of Gazala") forced an Allied retreat deep inside Egypt until Axis forces were [stopped at El Alamein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Battle_of_El_Alamein "First Battle of El Alamein"). On the Continent, raids of Allied [commandos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commando "Commando") on strategic targets, culminating in the failed [Dieppe Raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieppe_Raid "Dieppe Raid"), demonstrated the Western Allies' inability to launch an invasion of continental Europe without much better preparation, equipment, and operational security.

In August 1942, the Allies succeeded in repelling a [second attack against El Alamein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Alam_el_Halfa "Battle of Alam el Halfa") and, at a high cost, managed to [deliver desperately needed supplies to the besieged Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Pedestal "Operation Pedestal"). A few months later, the Allies [commenced an attack of their own](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_El_Alamein "Second Battle of El Alamein") in Egypt, dislodging the Axis forces and beginning a drive west across Libya. This attack was followed up shortly after by [Anglo-American landings in French North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Torch "Operation Torch"), which resulted in the region joining the Allies. Hitler responded to the French colony's defection by ordering the [occupation of Vichy France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Anton "Case Anton"); although Vichy forces did not resist this violation of the armistice, they managed to [scuttle their fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuttling_of_the_French_fleet_at_Toulon "Scuttling of the French fleet at Toulon") to prevent its capture by German forces. Axis forces in Africa withdrew into Tunisia, which was [conquered by the Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisian_campaign "Tunisian campaign") in May 1943. German operations in the Atlantic also suffered. By [May 1943, as Allied counter-measures became increasingly effective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_May_(1943) "Black May (1943)"), the resulting sizeable German submarine losses forced a temporary halt of the German Atlantic naval campaign.

In June 1943, the British and Americans began [a strategic bombing campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Bomber_Offensive "Combined Bomber Offensive") against Germany with a goal to disrupt the [war economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_economy "War economy"), reduce [morale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morale "Morale"), and "[de-house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dehousing "Dehousing")" the civilian population. The [firebombing of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Hamburg_in_World_War_II "Bombing of Hamburg in World War II") was among the first attacks in this campaign, inflicting significant casualties and considerable losses on infrastructure of this important industrial centre.

### Allies gain momentum (1943–1944)

After the Guadalcanal campaign, the Allies initiated several operations against Japan in the Pacific. In May 1943, Canadian and US forces were sent to [eliminate Japanese forces from the Aleutians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleutian_Islands_campaign#Allied_response "Aleutian Islands campaign"). Soon after, the United States, with support from Australia, New Zealand, and Pacific Islander forces, began major ground, sea and air operations to [isolate Rabaul by capturing surrounding islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Cartwheel "Operation Cartwheel"), and [breach the Japanese Central Pacific perimeter at the Gilbert and Marshall Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_and_Marshall_Islands_campaign "Gilbert and Marshall Islands campaign"). By the end of March 1944, the Allies had completed both of these objectives and had also [neutralised the major Japanese base at Truk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Hailstone "Operation Hailstone") in the [Caroline Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Islands "Caroline Islands"). In April, the Allies launched an operation to [retake Western New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_New_Guinea_campaign "Western New Guinea campaign").

In the Soviet Union, both the Germans and the Soviets spent the spring and early summer of 1943 preparing for large offensives in central Russia. On 5 July 1943, Germany [attacked Soviet forces around the Kursk Bulge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kursk "Battle of Kursk"). Within a week, German forces had exhausted themselves against the Soviets' well-constructed defences, and for the first time in the war, Hitler cancelled an operation before it had achieved tactical or operational success. This decision was partially affected by the Western Allies' [invasion of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_invasion_of_Sicily "Allied invasion of Sicily") launched on 9 July, which, combined with previous Italian failures, resulted in the [ousting and arrest of Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Fascist_regime_in_Italy "Fall of the Fascist regime in Italy") later that month.

On 12 July 1943, the Soviets launched their own [counter-offensives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Kutuzov "Operation Kutuzov"), thereby nearly completely dispelling any chance of German victory or even stalemate in the east. The Soviet victory at Kursk marked the end of German superiority, giving the Soviet Union the initiative on the Eastern Front. The Germans tried to stabilise their eastern front along the hastily fortified [Panther–Wotan line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panther%E2%80%93Wotan_line "Panther–Wotan line"), but the Soviets broke through it at [Smolensk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smolensk_operation "Smolensk operation") and the [Lower Dnieper Offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Dnieper "Battle of the Dnieper").

On 3 September 1943, the Western Allies [invaded the Italian mainland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_invasion_of_Italy "Allied invasion of Italy"), following [Italy's armistice with the Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Cassibile "Armistice of Cassibile") and the ensuing German occupation of Italy. Germany, with the help of local fascists, responded to the armistice by [disarming Italian forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Achse "Operation Achse") that were in many places without superior orders, seizing military control of Italian areas, and creating a series of defensive lines. German special forces then [rescued Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Sasso_raid "Gran Sasso raid"), who then soon established a new client state in German-occupied Italy named the [Italian Social Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Social_Republic "Italian Social Republic"), causing an [Italian civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_civil_war "Italian civil war"). The Western Allies fought through several lines until reaching the [main German defensive line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_Line "Winter Line") in mid-November.

In November 1943, [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt "Franklin D. Roosevelt") and Winston Churchill met with [Chiang Kai-shek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Kai-shek "Chiang Kai-shek") [in Cairo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo_Conference "Cairo Conference") and then with Joseph Stalin [in Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_Conference "Tehran Conference"). The former conference determined the post-war return of Japanese territory and the military planning for the [Burma campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma_campaign "Burma campaign"), while the latter included agreement that the Western Allies would invade Europe in 1944 and that the Soviet Union would declare war on Japan within three months of Germany's defeat.

From November 1943, during the seven-week [Battle of Changde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Changde "Battle of Changde"), the Chinese awaited Allied relief as they forced Japan to fight a costly war of attrition. In January 1944, the Allies launched a [series of attacks in Italy against the line at Monte Cassino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Monte_Cassino "Battle of Monte Cassino") and tried to outflank it with [landings at Anzio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Anzio "Battle of Anzio").

On 27 January 1944, [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_Front "Leningrad Front") troops launched [a major offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Leningrad#Soviet_relief_of_the_siege "Siege of Leningrad") that expelled German forces from the [Leningrad region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_region "Leningrad region"), thereby ending the [most lethal siege in history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_battles_by_casualties#Sieges_and_urban_combat "List of battles by casualties"). The [following Soviet offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad%E2%80%93Novgorod_offensive "Leningrad–Novgorod offensive") was [halted on the pre-war Estonian border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Narva_(1944) "Battle of Narva (1944)") by the German [Army Group North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Group_North "Army Group North") aided by [Estonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_Estonia_during_World_War_II#Estonians_in_Nazi_German_military_units "German occupation of Estonia during World War II") hoping to [re-establish national independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonian_government-in-exile#Failure_to_reestablish_independence "Estonian government-in-exile"). This delay slowed subsequent Soviet operations in the [Baltic Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Sea "Baltic Sea") region. By late May 1944, the Soviets had [liberated Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_offensive "Crimean offensive"), [largely expelled Axis forces from Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper%E2%80%93Carpathian_offensive "Dnieper–Carpathian offensive"), and made [incursions into Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Jassy%E2%80%93Kishinev_offensive "First Jassy–Kishinev offensive"), which were repulsed by the Axis troops. The Allied offensives in Italy had succeeded and, at the cost of allowing several German divisions to retreat, Rome was captured on 4 June.

The Allies had mixed success in mainland Asia. In March 1944, the Japanese launched the first of two invasions, [an operation against Allied positions in Assam, India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_U-Go "Operation U-Go"), and soon besieged Commonwealth positions at [Imphal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Imphal "Battle of Imphal") and [Kohima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kohima "Battle of Kohima"). In May 1944, British and Indian forces mounted a counter-offensive that drove Japanese troops back to Burma by July, and Chinese forces that had [invaded northern Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Northern_Burma_and_Western_Yunnan "Battle of Northern Burma and Western Yunnan") in late 1943 [besieged Japanese troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Myitkyina "Siege of Myitkyina") in [Myitkyina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myitkyina "Myitkyina"). The [second Japanese invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Ichi-Go "Operation Ichi-Go") of China aimed to destroy China's main fighting forces, secure railways between Japanese-held territory and capture Allied airfields. By June, the Japanese had conquered the province of [Henan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henan "Henan") and begun a [new attack on Changsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Changsha_(1944) "Battle of Changsha (1944)").

### Allied offensives (1944)

On 6 June 1944 (commonly known as [D-Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-Day "D-Day")), after three years of Soviet pressure, the Western Allies [invaded northern France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Overlord "Operation Overlord"). After reassigning several Allied divisions from Italy, they also [attacked southern France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Dragoon "Operation Dragoon"). These landings were successful and led to the defeat of the [German Army units in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falaise_pocket "Falaise pocket"). The [liberation of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_Paris "Liberation of Paris") on 25 August by the [local resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Resistance "French Resistance") was assisted by the [Free French Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_French_Forces "Free French Forces"), both led by General [Charles de Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle "Charles de Gaulle"), and the Western Allies continued to [push back German forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siegfried_Line_campaign "Siegfried Line campaign") in western Europe during the latter part of the year. An attempt to advance into northern Germany spearheaded by [a major airborne operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Market_Garden "Operation Market Garden") in the Netherlands failed. After that, the Western Allies slowly pushed into Germany, but [failed to cross the Roer river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Queen "Operation Queen"). In Italy, the Allied advance slowed due to the [last major German defensive line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_Line "Gothic Line").

On 22 June, the Soviets launched [a strategic offensive in Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Bagration "Operation Bagration") that nearly destroyed the German [Army Group Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Group_Centre "Army Group Centre"). Soon after that, [another Soviet strategic offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lvov%E2%80%93Sandomierz_offensive "Lvov–Sandomierz offensive") forced German troops from Western Ukraine and Eastern Poland. The Soviet Red Army however halted in the [Praga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praga "Praga") district on the other side of the [Vistula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vistula "Vistula") as the Germans quelled the [Warsaw Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Uprising "Warsaw Uprising") initiated by the [Home Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Army "Home Army") (the main faction of the [Polish resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_resistance_movement_in_World_War_II "Polish resistance movement in World War II"), loyal to the non-communist government-in exile), killing over 150,000 Poles. The [national uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovak_National_Uprising "Slovak National Uprising") in [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovak_Republic_(1939%E2%80%931945) "Slovak Republic (1939–1945)") was also quelled by the Germans. The Soviet [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army "Red Army")'s [strategic offensive in eastern Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Jassy%E2%80%93Kishinev_offensive "Second Jassy–Kishinev offensive") cut off and destroyed the [considerable German troops there](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Group_South_Ukraine "Army Group South Ukraine") and triggered [a successful coup d'état in Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1944_Romanian_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat "1944 Romanian coup d'état") and [in Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1944_Bulgarian_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat "1944 Bulgarian coup d'état"), followed by those countries' shift to the Allied side.

In September 1944, Soviet troops advanced into [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Federal_Yugoslavia "Democratic Federal Yugoslavia") and forced the rapid withdrawal of German Army Groups [E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Group_E "Army Group E") and [F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Group_F "Army Group F") in [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_occupation_of_Greece "Axis occupation of Greece"), [Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_Albania "German occupation of Albania"), and Yugoslavia to rescue them from being cut off. By this point, the communist-led [Partisans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Partisans "Yugoslav Partisans") under Marshal [Josip Broz Tito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josip_Broz_Tito "Josip Broz Tito"), who had led an [increasingly successful guerrilla campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_in_Yugoslavia "World War II in Yugoslavia") against the occupation since 1941, controlled much of the territory of Yugoslavia and engaged in delaying efforts against German forces further south. In northern [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_the_Military_Commander_in_Serbia "Territory of the Military Commander in Serbia"), the Soviet [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army "Red Army"), with limited support from Bulgarian forces, assisted the Partisans in a joint [liberation of the capital city of Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_offensive "Belgrade offensive") on 20 October. A few days later, the Soviets launched a [massive assault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_offensive "Budapest offensive") against [German-occupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Panzerfaust "Operation Panzerfaust") Hungary that lasted until [the fall of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Budapest "Siege of Budapest") in February 1945. Unlike rapid Soviet victories in the Balkans, [bitter Finnish resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuation_War "Continuation War") to the [Soviet offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyborg%E2%80%93Petrozavodsk_offensive "Vyborg–Petrozavodsk offensive") in the [Karelian Isthmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelian_Isthmus "Karelian Isthmus") denied the Soviets occupation of Finland and led to a [Soviet-Finnish armistice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Armistice "Moscow Armistice") on relatively mild conditions, although Finland was obligated to [fight their German former allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapland_War "Lapland War").

By the start of July 1944, Commonwealth forces in Southeast Asia had repelled the Japanese sieges in [Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam "Assam"), pushing the Japanese back to the [Chindwin River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chindwin_River "Chindwin River") while the Chinese captured Myitkyina. In September 1944, Chinese forces [captured Mount Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mount_Song "Battle of Mount Song") and reopened the [Burma Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma_Road "Burma Road"). In China, the Japanese had more successes, having finally [captured Changsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Changsha_(1944) "Battle of Changsha (1944)") in mid-June and the city of [Hengyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Hengyang "Battle of Hengyang") by early August. Soon after, they invaded the province of [Guangxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangxi "Guangxi"), winning major engagements against Chinese forces at [Guilin and Liuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Guilin%E2%80%93Liuzhou "Battle of Guilin–Liuzhou") by the end of November and successfully linking up their forces in China and Indochina by mid-December.

In the Pacific, US forces continued to push back the Japanese perimeter. In mid-June 1944, they began their [offensive against the Mariana and Palau islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariana_and_Palau_Islands_campaign "Mariana and Palau Islands campaign") and decisively defeated Japanese forces in the [Battle of the Philippine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Philippine_Sea "Battle of the Philippine Sea"). These defeats led to the resignation of the Japanese Prime Minister, [Hideki Tojo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hideki_Tojo "Hideki Tojo"), and provided the United States with air bases to launch intensive heavy bomber attacks on the Japanese home islands. In late October, American forces [invaded the Filipino island of Leyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Leyte "Battle of Leyte"); soon after, Allied naval forces scored another large victory in the [Battle of Leyte Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Leyte_Gulf "Battle of Leyte Gulf"), one of the largest naval battles in history.

### Axis collapse and Allied victory (1944–1945)

On 16 December 1944, Germany made a last attempt to split the Allies on the Western Front by using most of its remaining reserves to launch [a massive counter-offensive in the Ardennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Bulge "Battle of the Bulge") and [along the French-German border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Northwind_(1944) "Operation Northwind (1944)"), hoping to encircle large portions of Western Allied troops and prompt a political settlement after capturing their primary supply port at [Antwerp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antwerp "Antwerp"). By 16 January 1945, this offensive had been repulsed with no strategic objectives fulfilled. In Italy, the Western Allies remained stalemated at the German defensive line. In mid-January 1945, the Red Army attacked in Poland, [pushing from the Vistula to the Oder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vistula%E2%80%93Oder_offensive "Vistula–Oder offensive") river in Germany, and [overran East Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Prussian_offensive "East Prussian offensive"). On 4 February, Soviet, British, and US leaders met for the [Yalta Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yalta_Conference "Yalta Conference"). They agreed on the occupation of post-war Germany, and on when the Soviet Union would join the war against Japan.

In February, the Soviets [entered Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesian_offensives "Silesian offensives") and [Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pomeranian_offensive "East Pomeranian offensive"), while the [Western Allies entered western Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Allied_invasion_of_Germany "Western Allied invasion of Germany") and closed to the [Rhine River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine_River "Rhine River"). By March, the Western Allies crossed the Rhine [north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Plunder "Operation Plunder") and [south](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remagen "Remagen") of the [Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr_(river) "Ruhr (river)"), [encircling the German Army Group B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr_pocket "Ruhr pocket"). In early March, in an attempt to protect its last oil reserves in Hungary and retake Budapest, Germany launched [its last major offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Spring_Awakening "Operation Spring Awakening") against Soviet troops near [Lake Balaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Balaton "Lake Balaton"). Within two weeks, the offensive had been repulsed, the Soviets advanced to [Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_offensive "Vienna offensive"), and captured the city. In early April, Soviet troops [captured Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_K%C3%B6nigsberg "Battle of Königsberg"), while the Western Allies finally [pushed forward in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_1945_offensive_in_Italy "Spring 1945 offensive in Italy") and swept across western Germany capturing [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Hamburg "Capture of Hamburg") and [Nuremberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Nuremberg_(1945) "Battle of Nuremberg (1945)"). [American and Soviet forces met at the Elbe river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbe_Day "Elbe Day") on 25 April, leaving unoccupied pockets in southern Germany and around Berlin.

Soviet troops then [stormed and captured Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Berlin "Battle of Berlin") in late April. In Italy, [German forces surrendered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrender_at_Caserta "Surrender at Caserta") on 29 April, while the [Italian Social Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Social_Republic "Italian Social Republic") capitulated two days later. On 30 April, the [Reichstag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichstag_building "Reichstag building") was captured, signalling the military defeat of Nazi Germany.

Major changes in leadership occurred on both sides during this period. On 12 April, President Roosevelt died and was succeeded by his vice-president, [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman "Harry S. Truman"). Benito Mussolini [was killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Benito_Mussolini "Death of Benito Mussolini") by [Italian partisans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_partisans "Italian partisans") on 28 April. On 30 April, [Hitler committed suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler_committed_suicide "Hitler committed suicide") in his [headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%BChrerbunker "Führerbunker"), and was succeeded by [Grand Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Admiral "Grand Admiral") [Karl Dönitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_D%C3%B6nitz "Karl Dönitz") (as [President of the Reich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Reich "President of the Reich")) and [Joseph Goebbels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Goebbels "Joseph Goebbels") (as [Chancellor of the Reich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_of_the_Reich "Chancellor of the Reich")). Goebbels also committed suicide on the following day and was replaced by [Lutz Graf Schwerin von Krosigk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutz_Graf_Schwerin_von_Krosigk "Lutz Graf Schwerin von Krosigk"), in what would later be known as the [Flensburg Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flensburg_Government "Flensburg Government"). [Total and unconditional surrender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Instrument_of_Surrender "German Instrument of Surrender") in Europe was signed [on 7 and 8 May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_in_Europe_Day "Victory in Europe Day"), to be effective by the end of [8 May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_Day_(9_May) "Victory Day (9 May)"). German Army Group Centre [resisted in Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_offensive "Prague offensive") until 11 May. On 23 May, all remaining members of the German government were arrested by Allied forces in [Flensburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flensburg "Flensburg"). On 5 June, all German political and military institutions were placed under Allied control through the [Berlin Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Declaration_(1945) "Berlin Declaration (1945)").

In the Pacific theatre, American forces accompanied by the forces of the [Philippine Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Commonwealth "Philippine Commonwealth") advanced [in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_campaign_(1944%E2%80%931945) "Philippines campaign (1944–1945)"), [clearing Leyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Leyte "Battle of Leyte") by the end of April 1945. They [landed on Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Luzon "Battle of Luzon") in January 1945 and [recaptured Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Manila_(1945) "Battle of Manila (1945)") in March, during which Japanese forces [killed 100,000 Filipino civilians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_massacre "Manila massacre") in the city. Fighting continued on Luzon, [Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mindanao "Battle of Mindanao"), and other islands of the Philippines until the [end of the war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_of_World_War_II_in_Asia "End of World War II in Asia").

Meanwhile, the [United States Army Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Forces "United States Army Air Forces") launched [a massive firebombing campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_raids_on_Japan "Air raids on Japan") of strategic cities in Japan in an effort to destroy Japanese war industry and civilian morale. A devastating [bombing raid on Tokyo of 9–10 March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Tokyo "Bombing of Tokyo") was the deadliest conventional bombing raid in history.

In May 1945, Australian troops [landed in Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo_campaign "Borneo campaign"), overrunning the oilfields there. British, American, and Chinese forces defeated the Japanese in northern [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma_campaign "Burma campaign") in March, and the British pushed on to reach [Rangoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangoon "Rangoon") by 3 May. Chinese forces started a counterattack in the [Battle of West Hunan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_West_Hunan "Battle of West Hunan") that occurred between 6 April and 7 June 1945. American naval and amphibious forces also moved towards Japan, taking [Iwo Jima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Iwo_Jima "Battle of Iwo Jima") by March, and [Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Okinawa "Battle of Okinawa") by the end of June. At the same time, a naval blockade by [submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_submarines_in_the_Pacific_War "Allied submarines in the Pacific War") was strangling Japan's economy and drastically reducing its ability to supply overseas forces.

On 11 July, Allied leaders [met in Potsdam, Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam_Conference "Potsdam Conference"). They [confirmed earlier agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam_Agreement "Potsdam Agreement") about Germany, and the American, British, and Chinese governments reiterated the demand for unconditional surrender of Japan, specifically stating that "[the alternative for Japan is prompt and utter destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam_Declaration "Potsdam Declaration")". During this conference, the United Kingdom [held its general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1945_United_Kingdom_general_election "1945 United Kingdom general election"), and [Clement Attlee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clement_Attlee "Clement Attlee") replaced Churchill as Prime Minister.

The call for unconditional surrender was rejected by the Japanese government, which believed it would be capable of negotiating for more favourable surrender terms. In early August, the United States [dropped atomic bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bombings_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki "Atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki") on the Japanese cities of [Hiroshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshima "Hiroshima") and [Nagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagasaki "Nagasaki"). Between the two bombings, the Soviets, pursuant to the Yalta agreement, [declared war on Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Japanese_War "Soviet–Japanese War"), [invaded Japanese-held Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_Manchuria "Soviet invasion of Manchuria") and quickly defeated the [Kwantung Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwantung_Army "Kwantung Army"), which was the largest Japanese fighting force. These two events persuaded previously adamant Imperial Army leaders to accept surrender terms. The Red Army also captured the [southern part of Sakhalin Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_South_Sakhalin "Soviet invasion of South Sakhalin") and the [Kuril Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_the_Kuril_Islands "Soviet invasion of the Kuril Islands"). On the night of 9–10 August 1945, Emperor Hirohito ordered the Japanese cabinet to accept the terms demanded by the Allies in the [Potsdam Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam_Declaration "Potsdam Declaration"). On 15 August, the Emperor communicated this decision to the Japanese people through a speech broadcast on the radio ([*Gyokuon-hōsō*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirohito_surrender_broadcast "Hirohito surrender broadcast"), literally "broadcast in the Emperor's voice"). On 15 August 1945, [Japan surrendered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_surrendered "Japan surrendered"), with the [surrender documents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Instrument_of_Surrender "Japanese Instrument of Surrender") finally signed at [Tokyo Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Bay "Tokyo Bay") on the deck of the American battleship [USS *Missouri*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Missouri_(BB-63) "USS Missouri (BB-63)") on 2 September 1945, ending the war.

## Aftermath

The Allies established occupation administrations in [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied-occupied_Austria "Allied-occupied Austria") and [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied-occupied_Germany "Allied-occupied Germany"), both of which were initially divided between western and eastern occupation zones controlled by the Western Allies and the Soviet Union, respectively. However, their paths soon diverged. In Germany, the [western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trizone "Trizone") and [eastern occupation zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_occupation_zone_in_Germany "Soviet occupation zone in Germany") officially ended in 1949, with the respective zones becoming separate countries, West Germany and East Germany. In Austria, however, occupation continued until 1955, when a joint settlement between the Western Allies and the Soviet Union permitted the reunification of Austria as a democratic state officially non-aligned with any political bloc (although in practice having better relations with the Western Allies). A [denazification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denazification "Denazification") program in Germany led to the prosecution of Nazi war criminals in the [Nuremberg trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_trials "Nuremberg trials") and the removal of ex-Nazis from power, although this policy moved towards amnesty and re-integration of ex-Nazis into West German society.

Germany lost a quarter of its pre-war (1937) territory. Among the eastern territories, [Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesia "Silesia"), [Neumark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neumark "Neumark"), and most of [Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomerania "Pomerania") were taken over by Poland, and [East Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Prussia "East Prussia") was divided between Poland and the Soviet Union, followed by the [expulsion to Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_and_expulsion_of_Germans_(1944%E2%80%931950) "Flight and expulsion of Germans (1944–1950)") of the nine million Germans from these provinces, as well as three million Germans from the [Sudetenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudetenland "Sudetenland") in Czechoslovakia. By the 1950s, one-fifth of West Germans were refugees from the east. The Soviet Union also took over the Polish provinces east of the [Curzon Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curzon_Line "Curzon Line"), from which [two million Poles were expelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_population_transfers_(1944%E2%80%931946) "Polish population transfers (1944–1946)"). Northeastern Romania, parts of eastern Finland, and the [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states "Baltic states") were [annexed into the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_re-occupation_of_the_Baltic_states_(1944) "Soviet re-occupation of the Baltic states (1944)"). Italy [lost its monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1946_Italian_institutional_referendum "1946 Italian institutional referendum"), [colonial empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Empire "Italian Empire"), and some [European territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_between_Italy_and_the_Allied_Powers "Treaty of Paris between Italy and the Allied Powers").

In an effort to maintain [world peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_peace "World peace"), the Allies formed the United Nations, which officially came into existence on 24 October 1945, and adopted the [Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Declaration_of_Human_Rights "Universal Declaration of Human Rights") in 1948 as a common standard for all [member nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_states_of_the_United_Nations "Member states of the United Nations"). The [great powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_powers "Great powers") that were the victors of the war—France, China, the United Kingdom, the Soviet Union, and the United States—became the [permanent members](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_members_of_the_United_Nations_Security_Council "Permanent members of the United Nations Security Council") of the UN's [Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Council "Security Council"). The five permanent members remain so to the present, although there have been two seat changes, [between](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_2758 "United Nations General Assembly Resolution 2758") the Republic of China and the People's Republic of China in 1971, and between the Soviet Union and its [successor state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Successor_state "Successor state"), the Russian Federation, following the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union "Dissolution of the Soviet Union") in 1991. The alliance between the Western Allies and the Soviet Union had begun to deteriorate even before the war was over.

Besides Germany, the rest of Europe was also divided into Western and Soviet [spheres of influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spheres_of_influence "Spheres of influence"). Most eastern and central European countries fell into the [Soviet sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc "Eastern Bloc"), which led to the establishment of communist-led regimes, with full or partial support of the Soviet occupation authorities. As a result, East Germany, [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_People%27s_Republic "Polish People's Republic"), [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_People%27s_Republic "Hungarian People's Republic"), [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Romania "Socialist Republic of Romania"), [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Republic_of_Bulgaria "People's Republic of Bulgaria"), [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovak_Socialist_Republic "Czechoslovak Socialist Republic"), and [Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Socialist_Republic_of_Albania "People's Socialist Republic of Albania") became Soviet [satellite states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_state#Soviet_satellite_states "Satellite state"). Communist [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia "Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia") conducted a fully [independent policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement "Non-Aligned Movement"), causing [tension with the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tito%E2%80%93Stalin_split "Tito–Stalin split"). A [communist uprising in Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Civil_War "Greek Civil War") was put down with Anglo-American support and the country remained aligned with the West.

Post-war division of the world was formalised by two international military alliances, the United States-led [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO") and the Soviet-led [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact "Warsaw Pact"). The long period of political tensions and military competition between them—the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War")—would be accompanied by an unprecedented [arms race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arms_race "Arms race") and a number of [proxy wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proxy_war "Proxy war") throughout the world.

In Asia, the United States led the [occupation of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Japan "Occupation of Japan") and [administered Japan's former islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trust_Territory_of_the_Pacific_Islands "Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands") in the Western Pacific, while the Soviets annexed [South Sakhalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Sakhalin "South Sakhalin") and the [Kuril Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuril_Islands "Kuril Islands"). Korea, formerly [under Japanese colonial rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_under_Japanese_rule "Korea under Japanese rule"), was [divided and occupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_Korea "Division of Korea") by the Soviet Union in the North and the United States in the South between 1945 and 1948. Separate republics emerged on both sides of the 38th parallel in 1948, each claiming to be the legitimate government for all of Korea, which led ultimately to the [Korean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_War "Korean War").

In China, nationalist and communist forces resumed [the civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War "Chinese Civil War") in June 1946. Communist forces prevailed and established the People's Republic of China on the mainland, while nationalist forces retreated to Taiwan in 1949. In the Middle East, the Arab rejection of the [United Nations Partition Plan for Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine "United Nations Partition Plan for Palestine") and the [creation of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creation_of_Israel "Creation of Israel") marked the escalation of the [Arab–Israeli conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_conflict "Arab–Israeli conflict"). While European powers attempted to retain some or all of their [colonial empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_empire "Colonial empire"), their losses of prestige and resources during the war rendered this unsuccessful, leading to [decolonisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation "Decolonisation").

The global economy suffered heavily from the war, although participating nations were affected differently. The United States emerged much richer than any other nation, leading to a [baby boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-20th_century_baby_boom "Mid-20th century baby boom"), and by 1950 its gross domestic product per person was much greater than that of any of the other powers, and it dominated the world economy. The Allied occupational authorities pursued a policy of [industrial disarmament in Western Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_plans_for_German_industry_after_World_War_II "Allied plans for German industry after World War II") from 1945 to 1948. Due to international trade interdependencies, this policy led to an economic stagnation in Europe and delayed European recovery from the war for several years.

At the [Bretton Woods Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bretton_Woods_Conference "Bretton Woods Conference") in July 1944, the Allied nations drew up an economic framework for the post-war world. The agreement created the [International Monetary Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund "International Monetary Fund") (IMF) and the [International Bank for Reconstruction and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Bank_for_Reconstruction_and_Development "International Bank for Reconstruction and Development") (IBRD), which later became part of the [World Bank Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank_Group "World Bank Group"). The [Bretton Woods system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bretton_Woods_system "Bretton Woods system") lasted until 1973. Recovery began with the mid-1948 [currency reform in West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Mark "Deutsche Mark"), and was sped up by the liberalisation of European economic policy that the US [Marshall Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Plan "Marshall Plan") economic aid (1948–1951) both directly and indirectly caused. The post-1948 West German recovery has been called the [German economic miracle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wirtschaftswunder#West_Germany "Wirtschaftswunder"). Italy also experienced an [economic boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_economic_miracle "Italian economic miracle") and the [French economy rebounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trente_Glorieuses "Trente Glorieuses"). By contrast, the United Kingdom was in a state of economic ruin, and although receiving a quarter of the total Marshall Plan assistance, more than any other European country, it continued in relative economic decline for decades. The Soviet Union, despite enormous human and material losses, also experienced rapid increases in production in the immediate post-war era, having seized and transferred most of Germany's industrial plants and exacted [war reparations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_reparations "World War II reparations") from its satellite states. Japan recovered much later. China returned to its pre-war industrial production by 1952.

## Impact

### Casualties

An estimated 60 million to more than 75 million people died in the war including at least 20 million who died from deprivation, famine and disease. Civilian deaths have been estimated to comprise 67% to 80% of all direct and indirect deaths from the war. The Soviet Union had the highest overall death toll (estimated at 20 million to 28 million), followed by China (at least 15 million), Germany (6 million to 8.7 million) and Poland (5 million to 6.5 million).

The countries which sustained the most military deaths were the Soviet Union (about 8.7 million), Germany (around 5.3 million), China (2 million to 3 million) and Japan (1.7 million to 2.5 million). Of the 20 million to 25 million military deaths in the war, the majority were of German and Soviet soldiers, including prisoners of war (POWs), on the Eastern Front.

The high civilian death toll relative to military deaths was unusual for major wars up to that time. Some 10 million to 15 million people died of starvation and disease in China and the Soviet Union, and 8 million to 10 million in India and under Japanese occupation elsewhere in Asia and the Pacific. Around 15 million civilians died in genocides and other deliberate killings, while millions in total died in concentration camps, aerial bombings and in combat zones.

### Genocide, war crimes, and crimes against humanity

In [trials following the war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Axis_war_crime_trials "List of Axis war crime trials"), representatives of the Axis powers and their accomplices were convicted of numerous [war crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_crime "War crime"), [crimes against humanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimes_against_humanity "Crimes against humanity"), and complicity in [genocides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide "Genocide"). The Nazis killed about 6 million Jews in a racially motivated genocide known as the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust "Holocaust"). They also killed millions of [Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs "Slavs"), over 130 thousand [Romani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people "Romani people"), and members of other groups that they considered racially inferior. Almost 300,000 people with mental and physical disabilities were also [systematically killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aktion_T4 "Aktion T4") in Germany and its occupied territories.

The killing of civilians and POWs through massacres and deliberate starvation was especially common in the Eastern European and Asia-Pacific theatres. German forces on the Eastern Front destroyed or confiscated available food, massacred civilians in reprisals and routinely shot prisoners. The Japanese killed millions of civilians in occupied areas through massacres and [scorched earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scorched_earth "Scorched earth") strategies; for example, in their "[kill all, burn all, loot all](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Alls_policy "Three Alls policy")" policy in China. In the [Nanjing massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_massacre "Nanjing massacre"), between 40,000 and 200,000 Chinese civilians and POWs were killed. Japan also [used biological weapons in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_731 "Unit 731") and in [early conflicts against the Soviets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_of_Khalkhin_Gol "Battles of Khalkhin Gol"). The Soviet Union was responsible for imprisoning, deporting and often [executing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NKVD_prisoner_massacres "NKVD prisoner massacres") hundreds of thousands of civilians and POWs from occupied or annexed territories. This included the [Katyn massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katyn_massacre "Katyn massacre") of 22,000 Polish officers and intellectuals.

War crimes were also committed in civil wars and conflicts between resistance groups in occupied territories. In Yugoslavia, numerous war crimes, including the massacre of civilians and prisoners, were committed by Axis forces and the Axis-aligned Croatian [Ustaše](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usta%C5%A1e "Ustaše"). The main resistance groups, the Serbian-Nationalist [Chetniks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chetniks "Chetniks") and the Communist-led [partisans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Partisans "Yugoslav Partisans"), also committed massacres and persecutions of their enemies. In Poland, about 100,000 Poles were killed by the [Ukrainian Insurgent Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Insurgent_Army "Ukrainian Insurgent Army") in the [Volhynia massacres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volhynia_massacres "Volhynia massacres") between 1943 and 1945. About 10,000 Ukrainians were killed by Poles in reprisal attacks. In Greece, Axis forces were mainly responsible for civilian deaths through deliberate starvation and reprisal massacres, although the major resistance forces, [ELAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ELAS "ELAS") and [EDES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDES "EDES"), also committed war crimes.

The Soviet Union, Japan and Germany inflicted high death rates on POWs through executions, starvation, forced labour and other mistreatment. The Soviet Union and Japan had not ratified the [Geneva Convention on Prisoners of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Convention_on_Prisoners_of_War "Geneva Convention on Prisoners of War"), and Germany regarded itself as exempt from the convention on the Eastern Front. About a third of those taken prisoner by the Japanese died, as did almost 60 per cent of Soviet prisoners of the Germans. About a third of Germans taken prisoner by the Soviet Union died in captivity. The mortality rate of German and Japanese prisoners of the Western allies was 1 to 2 per cent.

Germany, Japan and the Soviet Union made extensive use of forced labour of foreign civilians and POWs. In greater Germany, there were 7.6 million foreign workers (including POWs undertaking forced labour) and about 500,000 slave labourers in concentration camps by late 1944. Those civilians and POWs from Soviet-occupied territories who were deported to the Soviet Union were usually imprisoned in the Soviet forced labour camps, known as the [gulag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulag "Gulag"). Between 200,000 and one million Soviet POWs and civilians repatriated from German camps were also sent to the gulag as alleged Axis collaborators where many died from malnutrition, the harsh climate and overwork. The Japanese conscripted millions of foreign civilians and POWs to undertake forced labour in Japan and its occupied territories where they suffered harsh treatment and high death rates. Up to 200,000 Korean and Chinese women were forced into sex slavery.

While troops of all the major belligerents committed rapes, the rape of civilians was particularly widespread among the German, Japanese and Soviet military. The post-war international military tribunals found the evidence of rape by German forces "overwhelming". Twenty-nine Japanese defendants, mostly generals, were convicted of complicity in mass rape. Soviet soldiers also committed mass rapes in occupied territories, especially Germany.

War crimes were committed by the Western Allied powers, but not on the same scale as those of the Axis powers and the Soviet Union. The Western Allies prosecuted a number of war crimes committed by their own forces, but Allied war crimes were not prosecuted by the post-war international military tribunals. There has been continued debate over whether the [area bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_bombing "Area bombing") of cities in Germany and Japan, and the [atomic bombing of Hiroshima and Nagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bombing_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki "Atomic bombing of Hiroshima and Nagasaki"), were war crimes.

### Occupation

In Europe, occupation came in two forms. In Western, Northern, and Central Europe (France, Norway, Denmark, the Low Countries, and the [annexed portions of Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate_of_Bohemia_and_Moravia "Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia")) Germany established economic policies through which it collected roughly 69.5 billion reichsmarks (27.8 billion US dollars) by the end of the war; this figure does not include the [plunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_plunder "Nazi plunder") of industrial products, military equipment, raw materials and other goods. Thus, the income from occupied nations was over 40 per cent of the income Germany collected from taxation, a figure which increased to nearly 40 per cent of total German income as the war went on.

In the East, the intended gains of *[Lebensraum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebensraum "Lebensraum")* were never attained as fluctuating front-lines and Soviet [scorched earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scorched_earth "Scorched earth") policies denied resources to the German invaders. Unlike in the West, the [Nazi racial policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_racial_policy "Nazi racial policy") encouraged extreme brutality against what it considered to be the "[inferior people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Untermensch "Untermensch")" of [Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_peoples "Slavic peoples") descent; most German advances were thus followed by [mass atrocities and war crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalplan_Ost "Generalplan Ost"). The Nazis [killed an estimated 2.8 million ethnic Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_crimes_against_the_Polish_nation "Nazi crimes against the Polish nation") in addition to Polish-Jewish [victims of the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victims_of_the_Holocaust "Victims of the Holocaust"). Although by 1942 [resistance groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resistance_during_World_War_II "Resistance during World War II") formed in most occupied territories, the assessments of the effectiveness of [Soviet partisans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_partisans "Soviet partisans") and [French Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Resistance "French Resistance") suggests that they did not significantly hamper German operations until late 1943.

In Asia, Japan termed nations under its occupation as being part of the [Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_East_Asia_Co-Prosperity_Sphere "Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere"), essentially a Japanese [hegemony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegemony "Hegemony") which it claimed was for purposes of liberating colonised peoples. Although Japanese forces were sometimes welcomed as liberators from European domination, [Japanese war crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_war_crimes "Japanese war crimes") frequently turned local public opinion against them. During Japan's initial conquest, it captured 4,000,000 barrels (640,000 m3) of oil (~550,000 tonnes) left behind by retreating Allied forces; and by 1943, was able to get production in the Dutch East Indies up to 50 million barrels (7,900,000 m3) of oil (~6.8 million tonnes), 76 per cent of its 1940 output rate.

### Home fronts and production

In the 1930s, Britain and the United States together controlled almost 75% of world mineral output—essential for projecting military power.

In Europe, before the outbreak of the war, the Allies had significant advantages in both population and economics. In 1938, the Western Allies (United Kingdom, France, Poland and the British Dominions) had a 30 per cent larger population and a 30 per cent higher gross domestic product than the European Axis powers (Germany and Italy); including colonies, the Allies had more than a 5:1 advantage in population and a nearly 2:1 advantage in GDP. In Asia at the same time, China had roughly six times the population of Japan but only an 89 per cent higher GDP; this reduces to three times the population and only a 38 per cent higher GDP if Japanese colonies are included.

The United States produced about two-thirds of all munitions used by the Allies in World War II, including warships, transports, warplanes, artillery, tanks, trucks, and ammunition. Although the Allies' economic and population advantages were largely mitigated during the initial rapid blitzkrieg attacks of Germany and Japan, they became the decisive factor by 1942, after the United States and Soviet Union joined the Allies and the war evolved into one of [attrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attrition_warfare "Attrition warfare"). While the Allies' ability to out-produce the Axis was partly due to more access to natural resources, other factors, such as Germany and Japan's reluctance to employ women in the [labour force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_force "Labour force"), Allied [strategic bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_bombing_during_World_War_II "Strategic bombing during World War II"), and Germany's late shift to a [war economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_economy "War economy") contributed significantly. Additionally, neither Germany nor Japan planned to fight a protracted war, and had not equipped themselves to do so. Germany, Japan and the Soviet Union used millions of [slave labourers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery "Slavery") in war-related industries.

### Advances in technology and its application

Aircraft were used for [reconnaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconnaissance_aircraft "Reconnaissance aircraft"), as [fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighter_aircraft "Fighter aircraft"), [bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomber "Bomber"), and [ground-support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_air_support "Close air support"), and each role developed considerably. Innovations included [airlift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airlift "Airlift") (the capability to quickly move limited high-priority supplies, equipment, and personnel); and [strategic bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_bombing "Strategic bombing") (the bombing of enemy industrial and population centres to destroy the enemy's ability to wage war). [Anti-aircraft weaponry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-aircraft_weaponry "Anti-aircraft weaponry") also advanced, including defences such as [radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar "Radar") and surface-to-air artillery, in particular the introduction of the [proximity fuze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proximity_fuze "Proximity fuze"). The use of the [jet aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_aircraft "Jet aircraft") was pioneered and led to jets becoming standard in air forces worldwide.

Advances were made in nearly every aspect of [naval warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_warfare "Naval warfare"), most notably with [aircraft carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_carrier "Aircraft carrier") and [submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine "Submarine"). Although [aeronautical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeronautical "Aeronautical") warfare had relatively little success at the start of the war, [actions at Taranto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Taranto "Battle of Taranto"), [Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor "Attack on Pearl Harbor"), and the [Coral Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Coral_Sea "Battle of the Coral Sea") established the carrier as the dominant capital ship (in place of the battleship). In the Atlantic, [escort carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escort_carrier "Escort carrier") became a vital part of Allied convoys, increasing the effective protection radius and helping to close the [Mid-Atlantic gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Atlantic_gap "Mid-Atlantic gap"). Carriers were also more economical than [battleships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battleship "Battleship") due to the relatively low cost of aircraft and because they are not required to be as heavily armoured. Submarines, which had proved to be an effective weapon during [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), were expected by all combatants to be important in the second. The British focused development on [anti-submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_warfare "Anti-submarine warfare") [weaponry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_weapon "Anti-submarine weapon") and tactics, such as [sonar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonar "Sonar") and convoys, while Germany focused on improving its offensive capability, with designs such as the [Type VII submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_VII_submarine "Type VII submarine") and [wolfpack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfpack_(naval_tactic) "Wolfpack (naval tactic)") tactics. Gradually, improving Allied technologies such as the [Leigh Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leigh_Light "Leigh Light"), [Hedgehog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedgehog_(weapon) "Hedgehog (weapon)"), [Squid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squid_(weapon) "Squid (weapon)"), and [homing torpedoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_24_mine "Mark 24 mine") proved effective against German submarines.

[Land warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_warfare "Land warfare") changed from the static frontlines of [trench warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trench_warfare "Trench warfare") of World War I, which had relied on improved [artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artillery "Artillery") that outmatched the speed of both [infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry "Infantry") and [cavalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavalry "Cavalry"), to increased mobility and [combined arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_arms "Combined arms"). The [tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank "Tank"), which had been used predominantly for infantry support in the First World War, had evolved into the primary weapon. In the late 1930s, tank design was considerably more advanced than it had been during World War I, and [advances continued throughout the war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanks_in_World_War_II "Tanks in World War II") with increases in speed, armour and firepower. At the start of the war, most commanders thought enemy tanks should be met by tanks with superior specifications. This idea was challenged by the poor performance of the relatively light early tank guns against armour, and German doctrine of avoiding tank-versus-tank combat. This, along with Germany's use of combined arms, were among the key elements of their highly successful blitzkrieg tactics across Poland and France. Many means of [destroying tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-tank_warfare "Anti-tank warfare"), including [indirect artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indirect_fire "Indirect fire"), [anti-tank guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-tank_gun "Anti-tank gun") (both towed and [self-propelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-propelled_artillery "Self-propelled artillery")), [mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-tank_mine "Anti-tank mine"), short-ranged infantry antitank weapons, and other tanks were used. Even with large-scale mechanisation, infantry remained the backbone of all forces, and throughout the war, most infantry were equipped similarly to World War I. The portable machine gun spread, a notable example being the German [MG 34](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MG_34 "MG 34"), and various [submachine guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submachine_gun "Submachine gun") which were suited to [close combat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_combat "Close combat") in urban and jungle settings. The [assault rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assault_rifle "Assault rifle"), a late war development incorporating many features of the rifle and submachine gun, became the standard post-war infantry weapon for most armed forces.

Most major belligerents attempted to solve the problems of complexity and security involved in using large [codebooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codebook "Codebook") for [cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptography "Cryptography") by designing [ciphering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cipher "Cipher") machines, the most well-known being the German [Enigma machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enigma_machine "Enigma machine"). Development of [SIGINT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIGINT "SIGINT") (*sig*nals *int*elligence) and [cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis "Cryptanalysis") enabled the countering process of decryption. Notable examples were the Allied decryption of [Japanese naval codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_naval_codes "Japanese naval codes") and British [Ultra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_(cryptography) "Ultra (cryptography)"), a [pioneering method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombe#The_British_Bombe "Bombe") for decoding Enigma that benefited from information given to the United Kingdom by the [Polish Cipher Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cipher_Bureau_(Poland)#Gift_to_allies "Cipher Bureau (Poland)"), which had been decoding early versions of Enigma before the war. Another component of [military intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_intelligence "Military intelligence") was [deception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deception "Deception"), which the Allies used to great effect in operations such as [Mincemeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Mincemeat "Operation Mincemeat") and [Bodyguard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Bodyguard "Operation Bodyguard").

Other technological and engineering feats achieved during, or as a result of, the war include the world's first programmable computers ([Z3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z3_(computer) "Z3 (computer)"), [Colossus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus_computer "Colossus computer"), and [ENIAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ENIAC "ENIAC")), [guided missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-1_flying_bomb "V-1 flying bomb") and [modern rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-2_rocket "V-2 rocket"), the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project "Manhattan Project")'s development of [nuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon "Nuclear weapon"), [operations research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operations_research "Operations research"), the development of [artificial harbours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulberry_harbour "Mulberry harbour"), and [oil pipelines under the English Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Pluto "Operation Pluto"). Although [penicillin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penicillin "Penicillin") was discovered before the war, the [development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_penicillin "History of penicillin") of industrial production technology as well as the mass production and use began during the war.
