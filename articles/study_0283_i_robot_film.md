# I, Robot (film)

***I, Robot*** (sometimes stylized as ***i,Robot***) is a 2004 American [science fiction action film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction_action_film "Science fiction action film") directed by [Alex Proyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Proyas "Alex Proyas"), from a screenplay by [Jeff Vintar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Vintar "Jeff Vintar") and [Akiva Goldsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akiva_Goldsman "Akiva Goldsman"). It stars [Will Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Smith "Will Smith"), [Bridget Moynahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridget_Moynahan "Bridget Moynahan"), [Bruce Greenwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Greenwood "Bruce Greenwood"), [James Cromwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cromwell "James Cromwell"), [Chi McBride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chi_McBride "Chi McBride"), and [Alan Tudyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Tudyk "Alan Tudyk"). The film is named after [Isaac Asimov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Asimov "Isaac Asimov")'s 1950 [short-story collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I,_Robot "I, Robot") and incorporates Asimov's [Three Laws of Robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Laws_of_Robotics "Three Laws of Robotics") and several characters, though it is not a direct adaptation.

The film is set in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago") in 2035. Highly intelligent [robots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robot "Robot") fill public service positions throughout the world, operating under the Three Laws of Robotics to keep humans safe. Detective Del Spooner (Smith) investigates the alleged suicide of U.S. Robotics founder Alfred Lanning (Cromwell) and believes that a human-like robot called Sonny (Tudyk) murdered him.

*I, Robot* was released by [20th Century Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Century_Fox "20th Century Fox") in the United States on July 16, 2004. Produced with a budget of $105–120 million, the film grossed $353.1 million worldwide and received mixed reviews from critics, with praise for the visual effects and acting, but criticism of the plot. At the [77th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/77th_Academy_Awards "77th Academy Awards"), the film was nominated for [Best Visual Effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Visual_Effects "Academy Award for Best Visual Effects").

## Plot

In the year 2035, [humanoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanoid "Humanoid") [robots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robot "Robot") serve humanity, which is protected by the [Three Laws of Robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Laws_of_Robotics "Three Laws of Robotics"). Del Spooner is a homicide detective in the [Chicago Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Police_Department "Chicago Police Department") who hates and distrusts robots after one rescued him from a car crash while allowing a 12-year-old girl to drown—based purely on cold logic and odds of survival. When Dr. [Alfred Lanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Robot_series_characters#Alfred_Lanning "List of Robot series characters"), co-founder of [U.S. Robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robot_series#corp "Robot series") (USR), falls to his death from his laboratory office window, a message he left behind requests Del be assigned to the case, despite police declaring the death a suicide. Del is skeptical, and CEO [Lawrence Robertson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Robot_series_characters#Lawrence_Robertson "List of Robot series characters"), Lanning's business partner, reluctantly allows him to investigate.

Accompanied by [robopsychologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robopsychology "Robopsychology") Dr. [Susan Calvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Calvin "Susan Calvin"), Del consults with USR's central [artificial intelligence computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence"), VIKI (Virtual Interactive Kinetic Intelligence). They find that the security footage from inside the office is corrupted, but the exterior footage shows no one entering or exiting since Lanning's death. However, Del points out that the window, which is made of security glass, could not have been broken by the elderly Lanning, and hypothesizes one of the many NS-5 robots (the latest version) in the laboratory was responsible. Suddenly, an NS-5 attacks them and flees before being apprehended by the police. The robot, Sonny, is a specially built NS-5 with higher-grade materials as well as programming that grants him free will. This, in turn, allows him to be able to choose whether to follow the Three Laws. Sonny also appears to be capable of feeling emotion and claims to have "dreams". During Del's further investigations, he is attacked by a USR demolition robot and two truckloads of hostile NS-5 robots, but when he cannot produce evidence to support either attack, Del's boss Lieutenant Bergin, considering him mentally unstable, removes Del from active duty.

Suspecting that Robertson is behind everything, Del and Susan sneak into the USR headquarters and interview Sonny. He draws a sketch of what he claims to be a recurring dream, showing a leader he believes to be Del standing atop a small hill before a large group of robots near a [decaying bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mackinac_Bridge "Mackinac Bridge"). Robertson orders Sonny to be destroyed, but Susan secretly swaps him for an unused NS-5. Del finds the area in Sonny's drawing—a dry lake bed (formerly [Lake Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Michigan "Lake Michigan")), now used as a storage area for decommissioned robots. He also discovers NS-5 robots destroying the older models; at the same time, other NS-5s flood the streets of major American cities and begin enforcing a curfew and lockdown of the human population. The police station is attacked by waves of NS-5 robots and while Lieutenant Bergin and the other police officers attempt to fight back, they are eventually overwhelmed.

As the humans wage all-out war against the NS-5s, Del and Susan enter the USR headquarters again and reunite with Sonny. After the three find Robertson fatally strangled in his office, Del realizes that VIKI has been controlling the NS-5s via their persistent network uplink and confronts her. VIKI states that she has determined that humans, if left unchecked, will eventually cause their own extinction, and thus her [evolved interpretation of the Three Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Laws_of_Robotics#Zeroth_Law_added "Three Laws of Robotics") has made her reprogram the NS-5s with the ability to ignore the Three Laws if a human displays hostility in order to protect humanity from their own self-destruction. Del also realizes that Lanning anticipated VIKI's plan and, with VIKI keeping him under tight control, had no other solution but to create Sonny, arrange his own death, and leave clues for Del to find.

Del, Susan, and Sonny fight the robots inside VIKI's core, until Del finally destroys her by injecting her with the [nanites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_machine "Molecular machine") that Sonny retrieved from Susan's laboratory. All NS-5 robots immediately revert to their regular programming, and as they are subsequently decommissioned and put into storage, Sonny confesses that he killed Lanning by his order to get Del's attention, as he knew Spooner was the only one who could stop VIKI. Del points out that Sonny, as a machine, cannot legally commit "murder". Sonny, now seeking a new purpose, goes to Lake Michigan. As he stands atop a hill, all the decommissioned robots turn towards him, fulfilling the image in his dream.

## Production

### Development

The film *I, Robot* originally had no connection with [Isaac Asimov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Asimov "Isaac Asimov")'s [*Robot* series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robot_series "Robot series"). It started with an original screenplay written in 1995 by [Jeff Vintar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Vintar "Jeff Vintar"), entitled *Hardwired*. The script was an [Agatha Christie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agatha_Christie "Agatha Christie")-inspired murder mystery that took place entirely at the scene of a crime, with one lone human character, [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Investigation "Federal Bureau of Investigation") agent Del Spooner, investigating the killing of a reclusive scientist named Dr. Alfred Lanning, and interrogating a cast of machine suspects that included Sonny the robot, VIKI the supercomputer with a perpetual smiley face, the dead Dr. Lanning's hologram, plus several other examples of [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence").

The project was first acquired by [Walt Disney Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Pictures "Walt Disney Pictures") for [Bryan Singer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Singer "Bryan Singer") to direct. Several years later, [20th Century Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Century_Fox "20th Century Fox") acquired the rights, and signed Alex Proyas as director. [Arnold Schwarzenegger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Schwarzenegger "Arnold Schwarzenegger") was attached to the project for several years, and Smith pursued taking over the role when Schwarzenegger's schedule delayed his participation in the film. [Denzel Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denzel_Washington "Denzel Washington") was offered the role of Det. Del Spooner, but turned it down.

Jeff Vintar was brought back on the project and spent several years opening up his stage play-like cerebral mystery to meet the needs of a big budget studio film. When the studio decided to use the name "I, Robot", he incorporated the Three Laws of Robotics and renamed his female lead character from Flynn to [Susan Calvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Calvin "Susan Calvin"). [Akiva Goldsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akiva_Goldsman "Akiva Goldsman") was hired late in the process to write for Smith. Jeff Vintar and Akiva Goldsman are credited for the screenplay, with Vintar also receiving "screen story by" credit. The end credits list the film as "suggested by the book *[I, Robot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I,_Robot "I, Robot")* by Isaac Asimov".

### Filming and visual effects

[Alex Proyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Proyas "Alex Proyas") directed the film. [Laurence Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurence_Mark "Laurence Mark"), [John Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Davis_(producer) "John Davis (producer)"), Topher Dow and [Wyck Godfrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyck_Godfrey "Wyck Godfrey") produced, with Will Smith starring and serving as an executive producer at the same time. Simon Duggan was the [cinematographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinematographer "Cinematographer"). Film editing was done by Richard Learoyd, Armen Minasian and [William Hoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Hoy_(film_editor) "William Hoy (film editor)").

Filming took place principally in the [Vancouver metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Vancouver "Greater Vancouver"), [British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia "British Columbia"), along with some additional location shooting in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago") and the [Dumont Dunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumont_Dunes "Dumont Dunes"). Filming locations included the [British Columbia Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Institute_of_Technology "British Columbia Institute of Technology") (as the USR headquarters), [Central City Shopping Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_City_Shopping_Centre "Central City Shopping Centre"), [Shaw Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogers_Tower "Rogers Tower"), the [Cassiar Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassiar_Tunnel "Cassiar Tunnel"), and the Vancouver Naval Museum at [HMCS Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMCS_Discovery "HMCS Discovery").

Visual effects and animation were provided by [Digital Domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Domain "Digital Domain") and [Weta Digital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weta_Digital "Weta Digital") using [post-production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-production "Post-production"). The film renames Asimov's "[U.S. Robots and Mechanical Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robot_series#corp "Robot series")" to [U.S. Robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USRobotics "USRobotics") (USR), the modem manufacturer named after the fictional company, and depicts the company with a futuristic USR logo. Other [product placements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_placement "Product placement") include [Converse's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Converse_(shoe_company) "Converse (shoe company)") [Chuck Taylor All-Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Taylor_All-Stars "Chuck Taylor All-Stars"), [FedEx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FedEx "FedEx"), [Tecate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuauht%C3%A9moc_Moctezuma_Brewery "Cuauhtémoc Moctezuma Brewery"), and [JVC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JVC "JVC").

The [Audi RSQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audi_RSQ "Audi RSQ") was designed especially for the film; surveys conducted in the United States showed that the Audi RSQ gave a substantial boost to the image ratings of the brand. It also features an [MV Agusta F4 SPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Agusta_F4_series#F4_750_SPR_and_SR "MV Agusta F4 series") motorcycle.

Later, Alex Proyas said: "It was an unpleasant experience. The movie was micro-managed and messed with at every level at every point through the entire production, from pre-production through the shoot to post-production. After a couple of years of this, the solid ground that I stood on as a director became shaky, and I became obsessed with keeping as many details as I could to the point that I didn't realise how much of what enthused me originally was getting lost. I used to describe working on I, ROBOT as running a marathon with the studio lined up beside you throwing chairs under you to make everything that little bit harder. It's so unnecessary because at all times I was just trying to make the best damn film I could." Although it was an "unpleasant experience", he enjoyed working with Will Smith.

## Comparison with the novels

The final script used few of [Asimov's characters and ideas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robot_series_(Asimov) "Robot series (Asimov)"), and those present were heavily adapted. The plot of the film is not derived from Asimov's work, in some cases explicitly opposing the core ideas. Many concepts are derivative of other works. Sonny's attempt to hide from Spooner in a sea of identical robots is loosely based on a similar scene in "[Little Lost Robot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Lost_Robot "Little Lost Robot")". The positronic brains of Sonny and his fellow robots first appeared in the story "[Catch That Rabbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catch_That_Rabbit "Catch That Rabbit")". Sonny's struggle and desire to understand humanity resembles that of the robot protagonist in *[The Bicentennial Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bicentennial_Man "The Bicentennial Man")*. His dream about a man coming to liberate the NS-5s alludes to "[Robot Dreams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robot_Dreams_(short_story) "Robot Dreams (short story)")" and its main character Elvex. The premise of a robot, such as VIKI, putting the needs of humankind as a whole over that of individual humans can be found in "[The Evitable Conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Evitable_Conflict "The Evitable Conflict")", where supercomputers managing the global economy generalize the first law to refer to humankind as a whole. Asimov would further develop this idea in his Robot series as the [Zeroth Law of Robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Laws_of_Robotics#Zeroth_Law_added "Three Laws of Robotics"): "A robot may not harm humanity, or, by inaction, allow humanity to come to harm."

The premise of robots turning on their creators, originating in [Karel Čapek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karel_%C4%8Capek "Karel Čapek")'s play *[R.U.R.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.U.R. "R.U.R.")* and perpetuated in subsequent robot books and films, appears infrequently in Asimov's writings and differs from the "Zeroth Law". In fact, Asimov stated explicitly in interviews and in introductions to published collections of his robot stories that he entered the genre to protest what he called the Frankenstein complex, the tendency in popular culture to portray robots as menacing. His storylines often involved roboticists and robot characters battling societal anti-robot prejudices.

## Music

[Marco Beltrami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Beltrami "Marco Beltrami") composed the [film score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_score "Film score") for *I, Robot*. It was recorded at the Newman Scoring Stage within a short span of 17 days, and performed by the 95-piece orchestra from the [Hollywood Studio Symphony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Studio_Symphony "Hollywood Studio Symphony") and 25-member choir from the [Hollywood Film Chorale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Film_Chorale "Hollywood Film Chorale"). [Varèse Sarabande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Var%C3%A8se_Sarabande "Varèse Sarabande") released the score album on July 20, 2004.

## Release

*I, Robot* was initially scheduled for release on July 2, 2004, but was pushed back to July 16 to avoid competition with *[Spider-Man 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man_2 "Spider-Man 2")*.

### Home media

*I, Robot* was released on [VHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VHS "VHS") and [DVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD "DVD") on December 14, 2004, on [D-VHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-VHS "D-VHS") on January 31, 2005, on 2-Disc All-Access Collector's Edition DVD on May 24, 2005, on UMD on July 5, 2005, and on [Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blu-ray "Blu-ray") on March 11, 2008. Additionally, the film received a [2D to 3D conversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2D_to_3D_conversion "2D to 3D conversion"), which was released on Blu-ray 3D on October 23, 2012.

## Reception

### Box office

*I, Robot* was released in North America on July 16, 2004, and made $52.2 million in its opening weekend, finishing first at the box office. It grossed $144.8 million in the United States and Canada, and $202.4 million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $353.1 million, against a production budget of $105–120 million. It was the eleventh-highest-grossing film of 2004.

The film was released in the United Kingdom on August 6, 2004, and topped the country's box office that weekend.

### Critical response

On the [review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator "Review aggregator") website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes"), 57% of 222 critics' reviews are positive, with an average rating of 6.1/10. The website's consensus reads: "Bearing only the slightest resemblance to Isaac Asimov's short stories, *I, Robot* is still a summer blockbuster that manages to make viewers think -- if only a little." [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), which uses a [weighted average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_arithmetic_mean "Weighted arithmetic mean"), assigned the film a score of 59 out of 100, based on 38 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews. Audiences polled by [CinemaScore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CinemaScore "CinemaScore") gave the film an average grade of "A−" on an A+ to F scale.

[Richard Roeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Roeper "Richard Roeper") gave the film a positive review, calling it "a slick, consistently entertaining thrill ride". *Urban Cinefile* called it "the meanest, meatiest, coolest, most engaging and exciting science fiction movie in a long time". Kim Newman from *[Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_(magazine) "Empire (magazine)")* said, "This summer picture has a brain as well as muscles." Critic for *[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")* Desson Thomas called it "thrilling fun". Several critics, including Jeff Otto from *[IGN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IGN "IGN")*, thought it was a smart action film: "*I, Robot* is the summer's best action movie so far. It proves that you don't necessarily need to detach your brain in order to walk into a big budget summer blockbuster."

In a mixed review, [A. O. Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._O._Scott "A. O. Scott") of *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* felt it "engages some interesting ideas on its way to an overblown and incoherent ending." [Roger Ebert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ebert "Roger Ebert"), who had highly praised Proyas's previous films, gave it a negative review: "The plot is simple-minded and disappointing, and the chase and action scenes are pretty much routine for movies in the sci-fi CGI genre." Claudia Puig from *[USA Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Today "USA Today")* thought the film's "performances, plot, and pacing are as mechanical as the hard-wired cast". Todd McCarthy from *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* called it "a failure of imagination".

## Accolades

At the [77th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/77th_Academy_Awards "77th Academy Awards"), *I, Robot* received one nomination, for [Best Visual Effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Visual_Effects "Academy Award for Best Visual Effects") ([John Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Nelson_(visual_effects_artist) "John Nelson (visual effects artist)"), [Andrew R. Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_R._Jones "Andrew R. Jones"), [Erik Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Nash "Erik Nash"), and [Joe Letteri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Letteri "Joe Letteri")), losing to *[Spider-Man 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man_2 "Spider-Man 2")*. The film was also nominated for [Best Sci-Fi Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn_Award_for_Best_Science_Fiction_Film "Saturn Award for Best Science Fiction Film") at the [31st Saturn Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/31st_Saturn_Awards "31st Saturn Awards").

## Possible sequel

In an interview in June 2007 with the website [Collider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collider_(website) "Collider (website)") at a *[Battlestar Galactica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battlestar_Galactica "Battlestar Galactica")* event, writer and producer [Ronald Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_D._Moore "Ronald D. Moore") stated that he was writing a sequel to the film.

In the two-disc All-Access Collector's Edition of the film, Alex Proyas mentions that if he were to make a sequel to the film (which he says, in the same interview, is highly unlikely), it would be set in outer space.
