# South Asia

**South Asia** is the southern sub-region of [Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia "Asia") that is defined in both geographical and [ethnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnicity "Ethnicity")-[cultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture "Culture") terms. South Asia, with a population of 2.04 billion, contains a quarter (25%) of the world's population. As commonly conceptualised, the modern [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_(polity) "State (polity)") of South Asia include [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh"), [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan "Bhutan"), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India"), the [Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldives "Maldives"), [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal "Nepal"), [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan"), and [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka"), with [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan") also often included, which may also be classified as part of [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia"). South Asia borders [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia") to the northeast, Central Asia to the northwest, [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia "West Asia") to the west and [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia") to the east. Southeast Asia and [Maritime South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral_South_Asia "Littoral South Asia") are the only subregions of Asia that lie partly within the [Southern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Hemisphere "Southern Hemisphere"). The [British Indian Ocean Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Ocean_Territory "British Indian Ocean Territory") and two out of [26 atolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atolls_of_Maldives "Atolls of Maldives") of the Maldives in South Asia lie entirely within the Southern Hemisphere. Topographically, it is dominated by the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent") and is bounded by the [Indian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean "Indian Ocean") in the south, and the [Himalayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayas "Himalayas"), [Karakoram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakoram "Karakoram"), and [Pamir Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamir_Mountains "Pamir Mountains") in the north.

[Settled life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic "Neolithic") emerged on the Indian subcontinent in the western margins of the [Indus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River "Indus River") [Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Basin "Indus Basin") 9,000 years ago, evolving gradually into the [Indus Valley Civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation "Indus Valley Civilisation") of the third millennium BCE. By 1200 BCE, an [archaic form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-language "Proto-language") of [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit "Sanskrit"), an [Indo-European language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_language "Indo-European language"), had [diffused](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-cultural_diffusion "Trans-cultural diffusion") into India from the northwest, with the [Dravidian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravidian_languages "Dravidian languages") being supplanted in the northern and western regions. By 400 BCE, [stratification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_stratification "Social stratification") and [exclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_exclusion "Social exclusion") by [caste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caste "Caste") had emerged within [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism"), and [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism") and [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism "Jainism") had arisen, proclaiming [social orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_order "Social order") unlinked to heredity.

In the early medieval era, [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity"), [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam"), [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism "Judaism"), and [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism "Zoroastrianism") became established on South Asia's southern and western coasts. [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims "Muslims") armies from Central Asia intermittently overran the plains of northern India, eventually founding the [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate "Delhi Sultanate") in the 13th century, and drawing the region into the cosmopolitan [networks of medieval Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Golden_Age "Islamic Golden Age"). The Islamic [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire "Mughal Empire"), in 1526, ushered in two centuries of relative peace, leaving a legacy of luminous architecture. Gradually expanding [rule of the British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_rule_in_India "Company rule in India") followed, turning most of South Asia into a colonial economy, but also consolidating its [sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereignty "Sovereignty"). [British Crown rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj") began in 1858. The rights promised to Indians were granted slowly, but [technological changes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution "Industrial Revolution") were introduced, and modern ideas of education and the public life took root. In 1947, the [British Indian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj") was [partitioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India "Partition of India") into two independent [dominions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion#India_and_Pakistan "Dominion"), a Hindu-majority [Dominion of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_India "Dominion of India") and a Muslim-majority [Dominion of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Pakistan "Dominion of Pakistan"), amid large-scale loss of life and an unprecedented migration. The 1971 [Bangladesh Liberation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War "Bangladesh Liberation War"), a [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War_in_Asia#South_Asia "Cold War in Asia") episode resulting in [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan "East Pakistan")'s secession, was the most recent instance of a new nation being formed in the region.

South Asia has a total area of 5.2 million km2 (2 million sq.mi), which is 10% of the Asian continent. The population of South Asia is estimated to be 2.04 billion or about one-fourth of the world's population, making it both the [most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_continents_and_continental_subregions_by_population "List of continents and continental subregions by population") and the [most densely populated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density "Population density") geographical region in the world.

In 2022, South Asia had the world's largest populations of [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus "Hindus"), [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims "Muslims"), [Sikhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhs "Sikhs"), Jains, and Zoroastrians. South Asia alone accounts for 90.47% of Hindus, 95.5% of Sikhs, and 31% of Muslims worldwide, as well as 35 million [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christians "Christians") and 25 million Buddhists.

The [South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Association_for_Regional_Cooperation "South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation") (SAARC) is an economic cooperation organisation in the region which was established in 1985 and includes all of the South Asian nations.

## Definition

### Ambiguity

The geographical extent is not clear cut as systemic and foreign policy orientations of its constituents are quite asymmetrical. Beyond the core territories of the [Indian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj") (territories of the former [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj")), there is a high degree of variation as to which other countries are included in South Asia. There is no clear boundary – geographical, geopolitical, socio-cultural, economical, or historical – between South Asia and other parts of Asia, especially Southeast Asia and West Asia. Most of this region is peninsular, resting on the [Indian Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Plate "Indian Plate") and being isolated from the rest of Asia by mountain barriers; it resembles a diamond delineated by the Himalayas on the north, the [Hindu Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush "Hindu Kush") in the west, and the [Arakan Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chin_Hills "Chin Hills") in the east, also extending southward into the Indian Ocean with the [Arabian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Sea "Arabian Sea") to the southwest and the [Bay of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Bengal "Bay of Bengal") to the southeast.

The common definition of South Asia is largely inherited from the administrative boundaries of the Indian Empire, with several exceptions. The current territories of [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh"), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India"), and [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan") which were the core territories of the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire") from 1857 to 1947 also form the core territories of South Asia. [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal "Nepal") and [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan "Bhutan"), two independent countries that were British protectorates but not apart of the British Raj, and the [island countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_South_Asia "Island South Asia") of Sri Lanka and the Maldives are generally included. By various definitions based on substantially different reasons, the [British Indian Ocean Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Ocean_Territory "British Indian Ocean Territory") and the [Tibet Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region "Tibet Autonomous Region") may be included as well. [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar") (Burma), a former British colony and now largely considered a part of Southeast Asia, is also sometimes included. [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan") is also included by some sources.

### Organisational definitions

The [South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Association_for_Regional_Cooperation "South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation") (SAARC), a contiguous block of countries, started in 1985 with seven countries – Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, the Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan, and Sri Lanka – and admitted Afghanistan as an eighth member in 2007. China and Myanmar have also applied for the status of full members of SAARC. The [South Asia Free Trade Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia_Free_Trade_Agreement "South Asia Free Trade Agreement") admitted Afghanistan in 2011.

The [World Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank "World Bank") and [United Nations Children's Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Children%27s_Fund "United Nations Children's Fund") (UNICEF) recognise the eight SAARC countries as South Asia, while the [Hirschman–Herfindahl index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herfindahl_index "Herfindahl index") of the [United Nations Economic and Social Commission for Asia and the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Economic_and_Social_Commission_for_Asia_and_the_Pacific "United Nations Economic and Social Commission for Asia and the Pacific") for the region excludes Afghanistan from South Asia. The Population Information Network (POPIN) excludes Maldives which is included as a member of Pacific POPIN subregional network. The United Nations Statistics Division's [scheme of subregions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_geoscheme "United Nations geoscheme"), for statistical purpose, includes [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran") along with all eight members of the SAARC as part of [Southern Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Asia_(UN_geoscheme) "Southern Asia (UN geoscheme)").

### Indian subcontinent

The terms "[Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent")" and "South Asia" are sometimes used interchangeably. The Indian subcontinent is largely a geological term referring to the land mass that drifted northeastwards from ancient [Gondwana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondwana "Gondwana"), colliding with the Eurasian plate nearly 55 million years ago, towards the end of [the Palaeocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleocene "Paleocene"). This geological region largely includes Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan, and Sri Lanka. Historians [Catherine Asher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_Asher "Catherine Asher") and Cynthia Talbot state that the term "Indian subcontinent" describes a natural physical landmass in South Asia that has been relatively isolated from the rest of Eurasia.

The use of the term *Indian subcontinent* began in the British Empire, and has been a term particularly common in its successors. South Asia as the preferred term is particularly common when scholars or officials seek to differentiate this region from East Asia. According to historians [Sugata Bose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugata_Bose "Sugata Bose") and [Ayesha Jalal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayesha_Jalal "Ayesha Jalal"), the Indian subcontinent has come to be known as South Asia "in more recent and neutral parlance." This "neutral" notion refers to the concerns of Pakistan and Bangladesh, particularly given the recurring conflicts between India and Pakistan, wherein the dominant placement of "India" as a prefix before the subcontinent might offend some political sentiments. In Pakistan, even the term "South Asia" was considered too India-centric and was "banned" within the International Relations department of Karachi University until 1989 after the death of [Zia ul Haq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zia_ul_Haq "Zia ul Haq"). This region has also been labelled as "India" (in its classical and [pre-modern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_kingdoms_of_India "Middle kingdoms of India") sense) and "[Greater India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_India "Greater India")".

### Asian context

According to Robert M. Cutler – a scholar of political science at [Carleton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carleton_University "Carleton University"), the terms South Asia, Southwest Asia, and Central Asia are distinct, but the confusion and disagreements have arisen due to the geopolitical movement to enlarge these regions into Greater South Asia, [Greater Southwest Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Middle_East "Greater Middle East"), and [Greater Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Central_Asia "Greater Central Asia"). The frontier of Greater South Asia, states Cutler, between 2001 and 2006 has been geopolitically extended to eastern Iran and western Afghanistan in the west, and in the north to northeastern Iran, northern Afghanistan, and southern [Uzbekistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbekistan "Uzbekistan").

Identification with a South Asian identity was found to be significantly low among respondents in an older two-year survey across Bangladesh, India, Nepal, Pakistan, and Sri Lanka.

## History

### Pre-history

The history of core South Asia begins with evidence of human activity of *[Homo sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomically_modern_humans "Anatomically modern humans"),* as long as 75,000 years ago, or with earlier hominids including *[Homo erectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_erectus "Homo erectus")* from about 500,000 years ago. The earliest prehistoric culture have roots in the [Mesolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesolithic "Mesolithic") sites as evidenced by the rock paintings of [Bhimbetka rock shelters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhimbetka_rock_shelters "Bhimbetka rock shelters") dating to a period of 30,000 BCE or older, as well as Neolithic times.

### Ancient era

The [Indus Valley Civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation "Indus Valley Civilisation"), which spread and flourished in the northwestern part of South Asia from c. 3300 to 1300 BCE in present-day [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan"), [North India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_India "North India"), and [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan"), was the first major civilisation in South Asia. A sophisticated and technologically advanced urban culture developed in the [Mature Harappan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mature_Harappan "Mature Harappan") period, from 2600 to 1900 BCE. According to anthropologist [Possehl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_Possehl "Gregory Possehl"), the Indus Valley Civilisation provides a logical, if somewhat arbitrary, starting point for South Asian religions, but these links from the Indus religion to later-day South Asian traditions are subject to scholarly dispute.

The Vedic period, named after the Vedic religion of the [Indo-Aryans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_peoples "Indo-Aryan peoples"), lasted from c. 1900 to 500 BCE. The Indo-Aryans were [Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-Europeans "Proto-Indo-Europeans")-speaking pastoralists who migrated into north-western India after the collapse of the Indus Valley Civilisation. Linguistic and archaeological data show a cultural change after 1500 BCE, with the linguistic and religious data showing links with Indo-European languages and religion. By about 1200 BCE, the Vedic culture and agrarian lifestyle were established in the northwest and northern Gangetic plain of South Asia. Rudimentary state-forms appeared, of which the [Kuru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuru_Kingdom "Kuru Kingdom")-Pañcāla union was the most influential. The first recorded [state-level society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_(polity) "State (polity)") in South Asia existed around 1000 BCE. The [Brahmanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmana "Brahmana") and [Aranyakas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aranyaka "Aranyaka"), and the [Upanishads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upanishads "Upanishads") into which they merged, began to ask the meaning of a ritual, adding increasing levels of philosophical and metaphysical speculation, or "[Hindu synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_synthesis "Hindu synthesis")".

Increasing urbanisation of South Asia between 800 and 400 BCE, and possibly the spread of urban diseases, contributed to the rise of ascetic movements and of new ideas which challenged the orthodox [Brahmanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmanism "Brahmanism").[*[failed verification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability "Wikipedia:Verifiability")*] These ideas led to [Śramana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sramana "Sramana") movements, of which [Mahavira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahavira "Mahavira") (c. 549–477 BCE), proponent of [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism "Jainism"), and [Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddha "Buddha") (c. 563 – c. 483), founder of [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"), were the most prominent icons.

The Greek army led by [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great "Alexander the Great") stayed in the [Hindu Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush "Hindu Kush") region of South Asia for several years and then later moved into the Indus valley region. Later, the [Maurya Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire "Maurya Empire") extended over much of South Asia in the 3rd century BCE. Buddhism spread beyond South Asia, through northwest into Central Asia. The [Bamiyan Buddhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamiyan_Buddhas "Bamiyan Buddhas") of Afghanistan and the [Edicts of Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edicts_of_Ashoka "Edicts of Ashoka") suggest that the Buddhist monks spread Buddhism (Dharma) in eastern provinces of the [Seleucid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire "Seleucid Empire"), and possibly even farther into West Asia. The Theravada school spread south from India in the 3rd century BCE, to Sri Lanka, later to Southeast Asia. Buddhism, by the last centuries of the 1st millennium BCE, was prominent in the Himalayan region, Gandhara, Hindu Kush region, and Bactria.

From about 500 BCE through about 300 CE, the Vedic-Brahmanic synthesis or "Hindu synthesis" continued. Classical Hindu and Sramanic (particularly Buddhist) ideas spread within South Asia, as well as outside South Asia. The [Gupta Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire "Gupta Empire") ruled over a large part of the region between the 4th and 7th centuries, a period that saw the construction of major temples, monasteries and universities such as the [Nalanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nalanda "Nalanda"). During this era, and through the 10th century, numerous cave monasteries and temples such as the [Ajanta Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajanta_Caves "Ajanta Caves"), [Badami cave temples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badami_cave_temples "Badami cave temples"), and [Ellora Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellora_Caves "Ellora Caves") were built in South Asia.

### Medieval era

Islam came as a political power in the fringe of South Asia in 8th century CE when the Arab general [Muhammad bin Qasim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_bin_Qasim "Muhammad bin Qasim") conquered [Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindh "Sindh") and [Multan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multan "Multan") in southern Punjab, in modern-day Pakistan. By 962 CE, Hindu and Buddhist kingdoms in South Asia were under a wave of raids from Muslim armies from Central Asia. Among them was [Mahmud of Ghazni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_of_Ghazni "Mahmud of Ghazni"), who raided and plundered kingdoms in north India from east of the Indus river to west of Yamuna river seventeen times between 997 and 1030. Mahmud of Ghazni raided the treasuries but retracted each time, only extending Islamic rule into western Punjab.

The wave of raids on north Indian and western Indian kingdoms by Muslim warlords continued after Mahmud of Ghazni, plundering and looting these kingdoms. The raids did not establish or extend permanent boundaries of their Islamic kingdoms. The Ghurid Sultan [Mu'izz al-Din Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mu%27izz_al-Din_Muhammad "Mu'izz al-Din Muhammad") began a systematic war of expansion into North India in 1173. He sought to carve out a principality for himself by expanding the Islamic world, and thus laid the foundation for the Muslim kingdom that became the [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate "Delhi Sultanate"). Some historians chronicle the Delhi Sultanate from 1192 due to the presence and geographical claims of Mu'izz al-Din in South Asia by that time.

The Delhi Sultanate covered varying parts of South Asia and was ruled by a series of dynasties: [Mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_dynasty_(Delhi) "Mamluk dynasty (Delhi)"), [Khalji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalji_dynasty "Khalji dynasty"), [Tughlaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tughlaq_dynasty "Tughlaq dynasty"), [Sayyid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayyid_dynasty "Sayyid dynasty"), and [Lodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lodi_dynasty "Lodi dynasty") dynasties. [Muhammad bin Tughlaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_bin_Tughlaq "Muhammad bin Tughlaq") came to power in 1325, launched a war of expansion and the Delhi Sultanate reached it largest geographical reach over the South Asian region during his 26-year rule. A Sunni Sultan, Muhammad bin Tughlaq persecuted non-Muslims such as Hindus, as well as non-Sunni Muslims such as Shia and Mahdi sects.

Revolts against the Delhi Sultanate sprang up in many parts of South Asia during the 14th century.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] In the northeast, the [Bengal Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Sultanate "Bengal Sultanate") became independent in 1346 CE. It remained in power through the early 16th century. The state religion of the sultanate was Islam. In [South India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_India "South India"), the Hindu [Vijayanagara Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayanagara_Empire "Vijayanagara Empire") came to power in 1336 and persisted until the middle of the 16th century. It was ultimately defeated and destroyed by an alliance of the Muslim [Deccan sultanates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_sultanates "Deccan sultanates") at the [Battle of Talikota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Talikota "Battle of Talikota").

About 1526, the Punjab governor [Daulat Khan Lodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daulat_Khan_Lodi "Daulat Khan Lodi") reached out to the [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_people "Mughal people") [Babur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur "Babur") and invited him to attack the Delhi Sultanate. Babur defeated and killed [Ibrahim Lodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Khan_Lodi "Ibrahim Khan Lodi") in the [Battle of Panipat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Panipat_(1526) "Battle of Panipat (1526)") in 1526. The death of Ibrahim Lodi ended the Delhi Sultanate, and the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire "Mughal Empire") replaced it.

### Modern era

The [modern history period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_era "Modern era") of South Asia, that is the 16th century onwards, witnessed the establishment of the Mughal Empire, with Sunni Islam theology. The first ruler was Babur had [Turco-Mongol roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turco-Mongol_tradition "Turco-Mongol tradition") and his realm included the northwestern and [Indo-Gangetic Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Gangetic_Plain "Indo-Gangetic Plain") regions of South Asia. Several regions of South Asia were largely under [Hindu kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_king "Hindu king") such as those of the [Vijayanagara Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayanagara_Empire "Vijayanagara Empire") and the [Kingdom of Mewar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Mewar "Kingdom of Mewar"). Parts of modern [Telangana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telangana "Telangana") and [Andhra Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andhra_Pradesh "Andhra Pradesh") were under local Muslim rulers, the Deccan Sutanates.

The Mughal Empire continued its wars of expansion after Babur's death. With the fall of the Rajput kingdoms and Vijayanagara, its boundaries encompassed almost the entirety of the Indian subcontinent. The Mughal Empire was marked by a period of artistic exchanges and a Central Asian and South Asian architecture synthesis, with remarkable buildings such as the [Taj Mahal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taj_Mahal "Taj Mahal").

However, this time also marked an extended period of [religious persecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_violence_in_India#Mughal_Empire "Religious violence in India"). Two of the religious leaders of [Sikhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism "Sikhism"), [Guru Arjan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru_Arjan "Guru Arjan"), and [Guru Tegh Bahadur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru_Tegh_Bahadur "Guru Tegh Bahadur") were arrested under orders of the Mughal emperors after their revolts and were executed when they refused to convert to Islam. Religious taxes on non-Muslims called *[jizya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jizya "Jizya")* were imposed. Buddhist, Hindu, and Sikh temples were desecrated. However, not all Muslim rulers persecuted non-Muslims. [Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar "Akbar"), a Mughal ruler for example, sought religious tolerance and abolished jizya.

The death of [Aurangzeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurangzeb "Aurangzeb") and the collapse of the Mughal Empire, which marks the beginning of modern India, in the early 18th century, provided opportunities for the [Marathas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire "Maratha Empire"), [Sikhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_Empire "Sikh Empire"), [Mysoreans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore_Kingdom "Mysore Kingdom"), and [Nawabs of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawabs_of_Bengal "Nawabs of Bengal") to exercise control over large regions of the Indian subcontinent. By the mid-18th century, India was a major [proto-industrializing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-industrialization "Proto-industrialization") region.

Maritime trading between South Asia and European merchants began at the turn of the 16th century after the [Portuguese discovery of the sea route to India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_discovery_of_the_sea_route_to_India "Portuguese discovery of the sea route to India"). British, French, and Portuguese colonial interests struck treaties with these rulers and established their trading ports. In northwestern South Asia, a large region was consolidated into the Sikh Empire by [Ranjit Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranjit_Singh "Ranjit Singh"). After the defeat of the [Nawab of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawab_of_Bengal "Nawab of Bengal") and [Tipu Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tipu_Sultan "Tipu Sultan") and his [French allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Indian_Alliances "Franco-Indian Alliances"), [British traders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_rule_in_India "Company rule in India") went on to dominate much of South Asia through [divide and rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divide_and_rule "Divide and rule") tactics by the early 19th century. The region experienced significant [de-industrialisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De-industrialisation_of_India "De-industrialisation of India") in its first few decades of British rule. Control over the subcontinent was then [transferred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj") to the British government after the [Indian Rebellion of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857 "Indian Rebellion of 1857"), with the British cracking down to some extent afterwards.

An increase of famines and extreme poverty characterised the colonial period, though railways built with British technology eventually provided crucial famine relief by increasing food distribution throughout India. Millions of South Asians began to migrate [throughout the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_diaspora#Colonial_era "South Asian diaspora"), impelled by the economic/[labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_indenture_system "Indian indenture system") needs and opportunities presented by the British Empire. The introduction of [Western political thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_values_(West) "Western values (West)") inspired a growing Indian intellectual movement, and so by the 20th century, British rule began to be challenged by the [Indian National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Congress "Indian National Congress"), which sought full independence under the leadership of [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi "Mahatma Gandhi").

Britain, under pressure from Indian freedom fighters, increasingly gave self-rule to British India. By the 1940s, two rival camps had emerged among independence activists: those who [favored a separate nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_nationalism_in_South_Asia "Muslim nationalism in South Asia") for Indian Muslims, and those who [wanted a united India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composite_nationalism "Composite nationalism"). As [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") raged, over 2 million Indians fought for Britain; by the end of the war, Britain was greatly weakened, and thus decided to grant independence to the vast majority of South Asians in 1947, though this coincided with the [Partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India "Partition of India") into a Hindu-majority India and a Muslim-majority Pakistan, which resulted in significant displacement and violence and harder religious divides in the region.

### Contemporary era

In 1947, the newly independent India and Pakistan had to decide how to deal with the hundreds of [princely states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_states "Princely states") that controlled much of the subcontinent, as well as what to do with the remaining European (non-British) colonies. A combination of referendums, military action, and negotiated accessions took place in rapid succession, leading to the political integration of the [vast majority of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_integration_of_India "Political integration of India") and [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_states_of_Pakistan "Princely states of Pakistan") within a few years.

India and Pakistan [clashed several times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_wars_and_conflicts "Indo-Pakistani wars and conflicts") in the decades after independence, with [disputes over Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir_conflict "Kashmir conflict") playing a significant role. In 1971, the eastern half of Pakistan seceded with help from India and became the People's Republic of Bangladesh after the traumatic [Bangladesh Liberation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War "Bangladesh Liberation War"). This, along with India and Pakistan gaining nuclear weapons soon afterwards, increased tensions between the two countries. The [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War") decades also [contributed to the divide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War_in_Asia#South_Asia "Cold War in Asia"), as Pakistan aligned with the West and India with the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"); other factors include the time period after the 1962 [Sino-Indian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Indian_War "Sino-Indian War"), which saw India and China move apart while Pakistan and China built closer relations.

Pakistan has been beset with [terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_in_Pakistan "Terrorism in Pakistan"), [economic issues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_Pakistan "Poverty in Pakistan"), and [military dominance of its government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_coups_in_Pakistan "Military coups in Pakistan") since independence, with none of its Prime Ministers having completed a full 5-year term in office. India has grown significantly, having slashed its rate of extreme poverty to below 20% and surpassed Pakistan's GDP per capita in the 2010s due to [economic liberalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalisation_in_India "Economic liberalisation in India") from the 1980s onward. Bangladesh, having struggled greatly for decades due to conflict with and economic exploitation by Pakistan, is now one of the fastest-growing countries in the region, beating India in terms of [GDP per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP_per_capita "GDP per capita"). Afghanistan has gone through [several invasions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasions_of_Afghanistan#Soviet_invasions:_1929,_1930,_and_1979 "Invasions of Afghanistan") and Islamist regimes, with many of its refugees having gone to Pakistan and other parts of South Asia and bringing back cultural influences such as [cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket "Cricket"). [Religious nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_nationalism "Religious nationalism") has grown across the region, with persecution causing millions of Hindus and Christians to flee Pakistan and Bangladesh, and [Hindu nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindutva "Hindutva") having grown in India with the election of the [Bharatiya Janata Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatiya_Janata_Party "Bharatiya Janata Party") in 2014.

A recent phenomenon has been that of India and China [fighting on their border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Indian_border_dispute "Sino-Indian border dispute"), as well as vying for [dominance of South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_of_Pearls_(Indian_Ocean) "String of Pearls (Indian Ocean)"), with China partnering with Pakistan and using its superior economy to attract countries surrounding India, while [the United States and other countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrilateral_Security_Dialogue "Quadrilateral Security Dialogue") have strengthened ties with India to counter China in the broader [Indo-Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pacific "Indo-Pacific"). (See also: [2025 India–Pakistan standoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_India%E2%80%93Pakistan_standoff "2025 India–Pakistan standoff"))

## Geography

According to Saul Cohen, early colonial era strategists treated South Asia with East Asia, but in reality, the South Asia region excluding Afghanistan is a distinct [geopolitical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geopolitics "Geopolitics") region separated from other nearby geostrategic realms, one that is geographically diverse. The region is home to a variety of geographical features, such as [glaciers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacier "Glacier"), [rainforests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainforest "Rainforest"), [valleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley "Valley"), [deserts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert "Desert"), and [grasslands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grassland "Grassland") that are typical of much larger continents. It is surrounded by three water bodies – the [Bay of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Bengal "Bay of Bengal"), the Indian Ocean and the [Arabian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Sea "Arabian Sea") – and has acutely varied climate zones. The tip of the Indian Peninsula had the highest quality pearls.

### Indian Plate

Most of this region is resting on the [Indian Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Plate "Indian Plate"), the northerly portion of the [Indo-Australian Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Australian_Plate "Indo-Australian Plate"), separated from the rest of the [Eurasian Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Plate "Eurasian Plate"). The Indian Plate includes most of South Asia, forming a land mass which extends from the [Himalayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayas "Himalayas") into a portion of the basin under the Indian Ocean, including parts of [South China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_China "Southwest China") and Eastern [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia"), as well as [Kunlun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunlun_Mountains "Kunlun Mountains") and [Karakoram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakoram "Karakoram") ranges, and extending up to but not including [Ladakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladakh "Ladakh"), [Kohistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohistan_District,_Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa "Kohistan District, Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa"), the [Hindu Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush "Hindu Kush") range, and [Balochistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochistan "Balochistan"). It may be noted that [geophysically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geophysics "Geophysics") the [Yarlung Tsangpo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarlung_Tsangpo_River "Yarlung Tsangpo River") in Tibet is situated at the outside the border of the regional structure, while the [Pamir Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamir_Mountains "Pamir Mountains") in Tajikistan are situated inside that border.

The [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent") formerly formed part of the [supercontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercontinent "Supercontinent") [Gondwana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondwana "Gondwana"), before rifting away during the [Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous "Cretaceous") period and colliding with the Eurasian Plate about 50–55 million years ago and giving birth to the [Himalayan range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayan_range "Himalayan range") and the [Tibetan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Plateau "Tibetan Plateau"). It is the [peninsular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsula "Peninsula") region south of the [Himalayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalaya "Himalaya") and [Kuen Lun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuen_Lun "Kuen Lun") mountain ranges and east of the [Indus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River "Indus River") and the [Iranian Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Plateau "Iranian Plateau"), extending southward into the Indian Ocean between the Arabian Sea (to the southwest) and the Bay of Bengal (to the southeast).

### Climate

The climate of this vast region varies considerably from area to area from tropical monsoon in the south to temperate in the north. The variety is influenced by not only the altitude but also by factors such as proximity to the seacoast and the seasonal impact of the [monsoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon "Monsoon"). Southern parts are mostly hot in summers and receive rain during monsoon periods. The northern belt of [Indo-Gangetic plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Gangetic_plain "Indo-Gangetic plain") also is hot in summer, but cooler in winter. The mountainous north is colder and receives snowfall at higher altitudes of Himalayan ranges.

As the [Himalayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayas "Himalayas") block the north-Asian bitter cold winds, the temperatures are considerably moderate in the plains down below. For the most part, the climate of the region is called the monsoon climate, which keeps the region humid during summer and dry during winter, and favours the cultivation of [jute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jute "Jute"), tea, rice, and various vegetables in this region.

South Asia is largely divided into four broad climate zones:

* The northern Indian edge and northern Pakistani uplands have a dry subtropical [continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_climate "Continental climate")
* The far south of India and southwest Sri Lanka have an [equatorial climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equatorial_climate "Equatorial climate")
* Most of the peninsula has a tropical climate with variations:
  + Hot [subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropics "Subtropics") climate in northwest India
  + Cool winter hot [tropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_climate "Tropical climate") in Bangladesh
  + Tropical [semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate "Semi-arid climate") in the center
* The Himalayas and most of the [Hindu Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush "Hindu Kush") have an [Alpine climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_climate "Alpine climate")

Maximum relative humidity of over 80% has been recorded in [Khasi and Jaintia Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khasi_and_Jaintia_Hills "Khasi and Jaintia Hills") and Sri Lanka, while the area adjustment to Pakistan and western India records lower than 20%–30%. Climate of South Asia is largely characterised by monsoons. South Asia depends critically on monsoon rainfall. Two monsoon systems exist in the region:

* The summer monsoon: Wind blows from the southwest to most parts of the region. It accounts for 70%–90% of the annual precipitation.
* The winter monsoon: Wind blows from the northeast. Dominant in Sri Lanka and Maldives.

The warmest period of the year precedes the monsoon season (March to mid June). In the summer the low pressures are centered over the [Indus-Gangetic Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus-Gangetic_Plain "Indus-Gangetic Plain") and high wind from the Indian Ocean blows towards the center. The monsoons are the second coolest season of the year because of high humidity and cloud covering. But, at the beginning of June, the jetstreams vanish above the [Tibetan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Plateau "Tibetan Plateau"), low pressure over the [Indus Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley "Indus Valley") deepens and the [Intertropical Convergence Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intertropical_Convergence_Zone "Intertropical Convergence Zone") (ITCZ) moves in. The change is violent. Moderately vigorous monsoon depressions form in the Bay of Bengal and make landfall from June to September.

#### Climate change

South Asian countries are expected to experience more flooding in the future as the monsoon pattern intensifies. Across Asia as a whole, 100-year extremes in vapour transport (directly related to extreme precipitation) would become 2.6 times more frequent under 1.5 °C (2.7 °F) of global warming, yet 3.9 and 7.5 times more frequent under 2 °C (3.6 °F) and 3 °C (5.4 °F). In parts of South Asia, they could become up to 15 times more frequent. At the same time, up to two-thirds of glacier ice in the Hindu Kush region may melt by 2100 under high warming, and these glaciers feed the water basin of over 220 million people. As glacier meltwater flow diminishes after 2050, [Hydropower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydropower "Hydropower") generation would become less predictable and reliable, while agriculture would become more reliant on the intensified monsoon than ever before. Around 2050, people living in the [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges "Ganges") and [Indus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus "Indus") river basins (where up to 60% of non-monsoon irrigation comes from the glaciers) may be faced with severe water scarcity due to both climate and socioeconomic reasons.

By 2030, major Indian cities such as [Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai "Mumbai"), [Kolkata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkata "Kolkata"), [Cuttack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuttack "Cuttack"), and [Kochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kochi "Kochi") are expected to end up with much of their territory below the tide level. In Mumbai alone, failing to adapt to this would result in damages of US$112–162 billion by 2050, which would nearly triple by 2070. [Sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise "Sea level rise") in Bangladesh will displace 0.9–2.1 million people by 2050 and may force the relocation of up to one third of power plants by 2030. [Food security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_security "Food security") will become more uneven, and some South Asian countries could experience significant social impacts from global food price volatility. [Infectious diarrhoea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectious_diarrhoea "Infectious diarrhoea") mortality and [dengue fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dengue_fever "Dengue fever") incidence is also likely to increase across South Asia. Parts of South Asia would also reach "critical health thresholds" for [heat stress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_stress "Heat stress") under all but the lowest-emission [climate change scenarios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_scenario "Climate change scenario"). Under the high-emission scenario, 40 million people (nearly 2% of the South Asian population) may be driven to internal migration by 2050 due to climate change.

India is estimated to have the world's highest [social cost of carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_cost_of_carbon "Social cost of carbon") - meaning that it experiences the greatest impact from [greenhouse gas emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions "Greenhouse gas emissions"). Other estimates describe Bangladesh as the country most likely to be the worst-affected. In the 2017 edition of [Germanwatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanwatch "Germanwatch")'s *Climate Risk Index*, Bangladesh and Pakistan ranked sixth and seventh respectively as the countries most affected by climate change in the period from 1996 to 2015, while India ranked fourth among the list of countries most affected by climate change in 2015. Some research suggests that South Asia as a whole would lose 2% of its [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP "GDP") to climate change by 2050, while these losses would approach 9% by the end of the century under the most intense climate change scenario.

### Regions

#### Northern South Asia

[Ethnolinguistically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnolinguistic_group "Ethnolinguistic group"), [Northern South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_South_Asia "Northern South Asia") is predominantly [Indo-Aryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_peoples "Indo-Aryan peoples"), along with [Iranic populations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranic_peoples "Iranic peoples") in Afghanistan and [Balochistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochistan "Balochistan"), and diverse linguistic communities near the Himalayas. Its borders have been heavily contested (primarily [between India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_disputes_of_India "Territorial disputes of India") and its neighbours [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_border_dispute "Indo-Pakistani border dispute") and [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Indian_border_dispute "Sino-Indian border dispute"), as well as [separatist movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurgency_in_Northeast_India "Insurgency in Northeast India") in Northeast India.) This tension in the region has contributed to difficulties in sharing river waters among Northern South Asian countries; climate change is projected to exacerbate the issue. Dominated by the [Indo-Gangetic Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Gangetic_Plain "Indo-Gangetic Plain"), the region is home to about half a billion people and is the poorest South Asian subregion.

#### Northwestern South Asia

Northwestern South Asia is the site of many of the first civilisations of the world, such as the [Indus Valley Civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation "Indus Valley Civilisation"). It was historically the most conquered region of South Asia because it is the first region that invading armies coming from the west had to cross to enter the Indian subcontinent; because of these many invasions, Northwestern South Asia has significant influences from various sources outside of South Asia, mainly from the [Muslim world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_world "Muslim world").

#### Eastern South Asia

[Eastern South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_South_Asia "Eastern South Asia") includes [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh"), [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan "Bhutan"), [eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India "East India") and [northeastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_India "Northeast India") parts of India, and [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal "Nepal"). Major Hindu and Buddhist kingdoms and empires of the region include [Kikata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kikata_kingdom "Kikata kingdom"), [Videha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Videhas "Kingdom of the Videhas"), [Vṛji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vajjika_League "Vajjika League"), [Magadha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magadha_(Mahajanapada) "Magadha (Mahajanapada)"), [Nanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanda_Empire "Nanda Empire"), [Gangaridai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangaridai "Gangaridai"), [Mauryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire "Maurya Empire"), [Anga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anga "Anga"), [Kalinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinga_(historical_region) "Kalinga (historical region)"), [Kamarupa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamarupa "Kamarupa"), [Samatata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samatata "Samatata"), [Kanva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanva_dynasty "Kanva dynasty"), [Gupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire "Gupta Empire"), [Pala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pala_Empire "Pala Empire"), [Gauda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauda_kingdom "Gauda kingdom"), [Sena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sena_Empire "Sena Empire"), [Khadga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khadga_dynasty "Khadga dynasty"), [Candra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candra_dynasty "Candra dynasty"), and [Deva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deva_dynasty "Deva dynasty"). Geographically, it lies between the [Eastern Himalayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Himalaya "Eastern Himalaya") and the [Bay of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Bengal "Bay of Bengal"). Two of the world's largest rivers, the [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges "Ganges") and the [Brahmaputra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra "Brahmaputra"), flow into the sea through the [Bengal region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_region "Bengal region"). The region includes the world's largest delta, the [Ganges Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges_Delta "Ganges Delta"), and has a climate ranging from [alpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_climate "Alpine climate") and [subalpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montane_ecosystems#Subalpine_zone "Montane ecosystems") to [subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropics "Subtropics") and [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropics "Tropics"). Since Nepal, Bhutan, Northeast India and parts of East India are [landlocked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landlocked_country "Landlocked country"), the coastlines of Bangladesh and East India (in [West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bengal "West Bengal") and [Odisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odisha "Odisha")) serve as the principal gateways to the region. With more than 441 million inhabitants, Eastern South Asia is home to 6% of the world's population and 25% of South Asia's population.

#### Central-West Coastal South Asia

In ancient history, Western India was divided into three great states according to [Hwen Thsang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang "Xuanzang"), namely [Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindh "Sindh") (which comprised the whole valley of the [Indus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River "Indus River") from the [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_region "Punjab region") to the sea, including the [Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River_Delta "Indus River Delta") and the island of [Kutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutch "Kutch")), [Gurjaradesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurjaradesa "Gurjaradesa") (which comprised Western [Rajputana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajputana "Rajputana") and the [Indian Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thar_Desert "Thar Desert")), and Balabhi (which comprised the [peninsula of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathiawar "Kathiawar"), with a small portion of the adjacent coast). Before the [partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India "Partition of India"), the now-Pakistani territories of [Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindh "Sindh") and [Balochistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochistan_(Pakistan) "Balochistan (Pakistan)") were also included in this region. In art history, the term normally covers just Gujarat and Rajasthan, which tend to move together in terms of style. Western India has the [second-largest gross domestic product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_states_and_union_territories_by_GDP "List of Indian states and union territories by GDP") of any region in India.

#### Central South Asia

The [Holkars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holkars "Holkars") of [Indore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indore "Indore"), [Scindias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scindia "Scindia") of [Gwalior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior "Gwalior"), [Puars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pawar "Pawar") of [Dewas Junior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewas_Junior "Dewas Junior"), [Dewas Senior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewas_Senior "Dewas Senior") and [Dhar State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhar_State "Dhar State") were powerful families of the [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire "Maratha Empire") which were based in Central India. The territories that now comprises [Madhya Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhya_Pradesh "Madhya Pradesh") and [Chhattisgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhattisgarh "Chhattisgarh") were ruled by numerous [princes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_State "Princely State") who entered into [subsidiary alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsidiary_alliance "Subsidiary alliance") with the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj").

After [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purna_Swaraj "Purna Swaraj"), the states of [Madhya Bharat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhya_Bharat "Madhya Bharat"), [Vindhya Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vindhya_Pradesh "Vindhya Pradesh"), and [Bhopal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhopal "Bhopal") were merged into Madhya Pradesh in 1956. In 2000, the new state of [Chhattisgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhattisgarh "Chhattisgarh") was carved out of [Madhya Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhya_Pradesh "Madhya Pradesh").

#### Southern South Asia

[Southern South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_South_Asia "Southern South Asia") includes [South India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_India "South India"), the [Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldives "Maldives"), and [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka"), and is predominantly [Dravidian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravidian_peoples "Dravidian peoples"). Southern South Asia was a hub of global trade in ancient times because of its position in the important Indian Ocean corridor.

Historically, governments throughout Southern South Asia adopted Sanskrit for public political expression beginning around 300 CE and ending around 1300, resulting in greater integration into the broader [Indian cultural sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_India "Greater India"). This significantly influenced the languages of the region, making all of the major Dravidian languages except for Tamil highly [Sanskritised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskritisation_(linguistics) "Sanskritisation (linguistics)"). Tamil influence in the region is quite significant, with prominent empires such as the [Chola dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola_dynasty "Chola dynasty") taking [Tamil culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_culture "Tamil culture") to Sri Lanka and beyond South Asia, and Sri Lanka having an ancient [Tamil minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_Tamils "Sri Lankan Tamils") and a Dravidian-influenced majority language of [Sinhala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhala_language "Sinhala language").

### Languages

There are numerous languages in South Asia. The spoken languages of the region are largely based on geography and shared across religious boundaries, but the written script is sharply divided by religious boundaries. In particular, Muslims of South Asia such as in Afghanistan and Pakistan use the [Arabic alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_alphabet "Arabic alphabet") and Persian [Nastaliq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasta%CA%BFl%C4%ABq_script "Nastaʿlīq script"). Till 1952, Muslim-majority Bangladesh (then known as East Pakistan) also mandated only the Nastaliq script, but after that adopted regional scripts and particularly Bengali, after the [Language Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language_movement "Bengali language movement") for the adoption of Bengali as the official language of the then East Pakistan. Non-Muslims of South Asia, and some Muslims in India, on the other hand, use scripts such as those derived from [Brahmi script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmi_script "Brahmi script") for Indo-European languages and non-Brahmi scripts for [Dravidian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravidian_languages "Dravidian languages") and others.

The [Nagari script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagari_script "Nagari script") has been the *[primus inter pares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primus_inter_pares "Primus inter pares")* of the traditional South Asian scripts. The [Devanagari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devanagari "Devanagari") script is used for over 120 South Asian languages, including [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi "Hindi"), [Marathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathi_language "Marathi language"), [Nepali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_language "Nepali language"), [Pali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pali "Pali"), [Konkani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konkani_language "Konkani language"), [Bodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodo_language "Bodo language"), [Sindhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhi_language "Sindhi language"), and [Maithili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maithili_language "Maithili language") among other languages and dialects, making it one of the most used and [adopted writing systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_writing_systems_by_adoption "List of writing systems by adoption") in the world. The Devanagari script is also used for classical Sanskrit texts.

The largest spoken language in this region is [Hindustani language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustani_language "Hindustani language"), followed by Bengali, Telugu, Tamil, Marathi, Gujarati, Kannada, and Punjabi. In the modern era, new syncretic languages developed in the region such as [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu "Urdu") that are used by the Muslim community of [northern South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_South_Asia "Northern South Asia") (particularly Pakistan and northern states of India). The Punjabi language spans three religions: Islam, Hinduism, and Sikhism. The spoken language is similar, but it is written in three scripts. The [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhs "Sikhs") use [Gurmukhi alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurmukhi_alphabet "Gurmukhi alphabet"), [Muslim Punjabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_Punjabi "Muslim Punjabi") in [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan") use the [Nastaliq script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nastaliq_script "Nastaliq script"), while [Hindu Punjabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Punjabi "Hindu Punjabi") in India use the [Gurmukhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurmukhi "Gurmukhi") or [Nāgarī script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C4%81gar%C4%AB_script "Nāgarī script"). The Gurmukhi and Nāgarī scripts are distinct but close in their structure, but the Persian Nastaliq script is very different.

[Sino-Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Tibetan "Sino-Tibetan") languages are spoken across northern belts of the region in the [Himalayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayas "Himalayas") areas, often using the [Tibetan script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_script "Tibetan script"). These languages are predominantly spoken in Bhutan and Nepal as well as parts of Burma and northern India in the state of [Sikkim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikkim "Sikkim") and the [Ladakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladakh "Ladakh") region. The national language of Bhutan is [Dzongkha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzongkha "Dzongkha"), while [Lepcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lepcha_language "Lepcha language"), [Limbu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limbu_language "Limbu language"), [Gurung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurung "Gurung"), [Magar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magar_language "Magar language"), [Rai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rai_languages "Rai languages"), [Newari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newar_language "Newar language"), [Tamang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamang_language "Tamang language"), [Tshangla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tshangla "Tshangla"), [Thakali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thakali_language "Thakali language"), and [Sikkimese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikkimese_language "Sikkimese language") are also spoken in Bhutan, Nepal and Sikkim, and [Ladakhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladakhi_language "Ladakhi language") is spoken in Ladakh. Both [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism") and [Bon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon "Bon") are often predominant in areas where these languages are present. Some areas in [Gilgit-Baltistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilgit-Baltistan "Gilgit-Baltistan") also speak [Balti language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balti_language "Balti language"), however speakers write with the [Urdu script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu_alphabet "Urdu alphabet"). The Tibetan script fell out of use in Pakistani [Baltistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltistan "Baltistan") hundreds of years ago upon the region's adoption of [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam").

English, with British spelling, is commonly used in urban areas and is a major economic [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca "Lingua franca") of South Asia (see also [South Asian English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_English "South Asian English")).

### Religions

In 2010, South Asia had the world's largest population of [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus "Hindus"), about 510 million [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims "Muslims"), over 27 million [Sikhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhs "Sikhs"), 35 million [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christians "Christians") and over 25 million [Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhists "Buddhists"). Hindus make up about 68% or about 900 million and Muslims at 31% or 510 million of the overall South Asia population, while Buddhists, Jains, Christians, and Sikhs constitute most of the rest. The Hindus, Buddhists, Jains, Sikhs, and Christians are concentrated in India, Nepal, Sri Lanka, and Bhutan, while the Muslims are concentrated in Afghanistan (99%), Bangladesh (90%), Pakistan (96%), and Maldives (100%). With all major religions practised in the subcontinent, South Asia is known for its religious diversity and one of the most religiously diverse regions on earth.

[Indian religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_religions "Indian religions") are the religions that originated in the Indian subcontinent; namely [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism"), [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism "Jainism"), [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"), and [Sikhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism "Sikhism"). The Indian religions are distinct yet share terminology, concepts, goals and ideas, and from South Asia spread into East Asia and Southeast Asia. Early Christianity and Islam were introduced into coastal regions of South Asia by merchants who settled among the local populations. Later [Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindh "Sindh"), [Balochistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochistan_(Pakistan) "Balochistan (Pakistan)"), and parts of the [Punjab region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_region "Punjab region") saw conquest by the [Arab caliphates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummayad "Ummayad") along with an influx of Muslims from Persia and Central Asia, which resulted in spread of both Shia and Sunni Islam in parts of northwestern region of South Asia. Subsequently, under the influence of Muslim rulers of the Islamic sultanates and the Mughal Empire, Islam spread in South Asia. About [one-third of the world's Muslims are from South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_South_Asia "Islam in South Asia").

### Migration

[Ancient migrations into South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peopling_of_India "Peopling of India") shaped the region's unique demographics, and later migrations often corresponded with conquest, as with the [British diaspora in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britons_in_India "Britons in India").

Contemporary immigration to South Asian countries often takes a uniquely regional form; for example, most [migrants in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_India "Immigration to India") are from other South Asian countries, and 97% of [Bangladeshi migrants in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshis_in_India "Bangladeshis in India") live in the Bangladesh-bordering regions of India ([East India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India "East India") and [Northeast India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_India "Northeast India")).

#### Diaspora

The [South Asian diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_diaspora "South Asian diaspora") is the group of people whose ancestral origins lie in South Asia, but who live outside the region. With more than 44 million people within the cultural [diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaspora "Diaspora"), it is one of the largest in the world. The term encompasses all whose ancestral and cultural origins lie in the modern nations of [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India"), [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan"), [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh"), [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka"), [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan "Bhutan"), the [Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldives "Maldives"), and [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal "Nepal"), with [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan") often included. The diaspora is considered a distinct ethnic group by many nations’ [censuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census "Census"), with it having the largest number of immigrants in the world.

## Religion

In 2010, South Asia had the world's largest population of [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus "Hindus"), about 510 million [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims "Muslims"), over 27 million Sikhs, 35 million Christians and over 25 million [Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhists "Buddhists"). Hindus make up about 60 percent or about 900 million and Muslims at 34 percent or 510 million of the overall South Asia population, while Buddhists, [Jains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism "Jainism"), [Zoroastrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism "Zoroastrianism"), Sikhs, and [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity") constitute most of the rest. The Hindus, Buddhists, Jains, Sikhs, Zoroastrians, and Christians are concentrated in India, Nepal, Sri Lanka and Bhutan, while the Muslims are concentrated in Afghanistan (99%), Bangladesh (91%), Pakistan (96%) and Maldives (100%).

[Indian religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_religions "Indian religions") (also known as Dharmic religions) are the religions that originated in the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent"); namely [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism"), [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism "Jainism"), [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism") and [Sikhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism "Sikhism"). The Indian religions are distinct yet share terminology, concepts, goals and ideas, and from South Asia spread into East Asia and Southeast Asia. Early Christianity and Islam were introduced into coastal regions of South Asia by merchants who settled among the local populations. Later [Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindh "Sindh"), [Balochistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochistan_(Pakistan) "Balochistan (Pakistan)"), and parts of the [Punjab region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_region "Punjab region") saw conquest by the [Arab caliphates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummayad "Ummayad") along with an influx of Muslims from Persia and Central Asia, which resulted in spread of both Shia and Sunni Islam in parts of northwestern region of South Asia. Subsequently, under the influence of Muslim rulers of the Islamic sultanates and the Mughal Empire, Islam spread in South Asia. About [one-third of the world's Muslims are from South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_South_Asia "Islam in South Asia").

## Economy

India is the largest economy in the region (US$4.18 trillion) and makes up almost 82% of the South Asian economy; it is the [world's 6th largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(nominal) "List of countries by GDP (nominal)") in nominal terms and the [world's 3rd largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(PPP) "List of countries by GDP (PPP)") by purchasing power adjusted exchange rates (US$17.64 trillion). India is the member of [G-20 major economies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-20_major_economies "G-20 major economies") and [BRICS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRICS "BRICS") from the region. It is the fastest-growing major economy in the world and one of the world's fastest registering a growth of 7.2% in FY 2022–23.

India is followed by Bangladesh, which has a GDP of ($446 billion). It has the fastest GDP growth rate in Asia. It is one of the emerging and growth-leading economies of the world, and is also listed among the [Next Eleven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_O%27Neill,_Baron_O%27Neill_of_Gatley#Next_Eleven "Jim O'Neill, Baron O'Neill of Gatley") countries. It is also one of the fastest-growing middle-income countries. It has the [world's 33rd largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(nominal) "List of countries by GDP (nominal)") GDP in nominal terms and is the [25th largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(PPP) "List of countries by GDP (PPP)") by purchasing power adjusted exchange rates ($1.476 trillion). Bangladesh's economic growth was 6.4% in 2022. The next one is Pakistan, which has an economy of ($340 billion). Next is [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka"), which has the 2nd highest GDP per capita and the 4th largest economy in the region. According to a [World Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank "World Bank") report in 2015, driven by a strong expansion in India, coupled with favourable oil prices, from the last quarter of 2014 South Asia became the fastest-growing region in the world.

While [in East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_East_Asia "Economy of East Asia"), regional trade accounts for 50% of total trade, it accounts for only a little more than 5% in South Asia. Certain parts of South Asia are significantly wealthier than others; the four Indian states of [Maharashtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra "Maharashtra"), [Tamil Nadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_Nadu "Tamil Nadu"), [Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat "Gujarat"), and [Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnataka "Karnataka") are projected to account for almost 50% of India's GDP by 2030, while the five [South Indian states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_India "South India") comprising 20% of India's population are expected to contribute 35% of India's GDP by 2030.

### Poverty

According to the World Bank's 2011 report, based on 2005 ICP PPP, about 24.6% of the South Asian population was below the international poverty line of $1.25/day. Bhutan, Maldives and Sri Lanka had the lowest number of people below the poverty line, with 2.4%, 1.5% and 4.1% respectively.

According to the 2023 MPI (multidimensional poverty index) report by the UN, around 20% of South Asians are poor.

51.7% of Afghanistan's population was under the MPI poverty threshold in 2019, while 24.1% of Bangladesh's population was under the threshold in 2021. India lifted 415 million people from MPI-poverty from 2005/06 to 2019/21; 16.4% of India's population was MPI-poor in 2019/2021, compared to 55.1% in 2005/2006. 10% of India's population was under the international poverty line of $2.15/day in 2021.

## Education

One of the key challenges in assessing the quality of education in South Asia is the vast range of contextual difference across the region, complicating any attempt to compare between countries. In 2018, 11.3 million children at the primary level and 20.6 million children at the lower secondary level were out-of-school in South Asia, while millions of children completed primary education without mastering the foundational skills of basic numeracy and literacy.

According to UNESCO, 241 million children between six and fourteen years or 81 per cent of the total were not learning in Southern and Central Asia in 2017. Only sub-Saharan Africa had a higher rate of children not learning. Two-thirds of these children were in school, sitting in classrooms. Only 19% of children attending primary and lower secondary schools attain a minimum proficiency level in reading and mathematics. According to a citizen-led assessment, only 48% in Indian public schools and 46% of children in Pakistan public schools could read a class two level text by the time they reached class five. This poor quality of education in turn has contributed to some of the highest drop-out rates in the world, while over half of the students complete secondary school with acquiring requisite skills.

In South Asia, classrooms are teacher-centred and rote-based, while children are often subjected to corporal punishment and discrimination. Different South Asian countries have different education structures. While by 2018 India and Pakistan has two of the most developed and increasingly decentralised education systems, Bangladesh still had a highly centralised system, and Nepal is in a state of transition from a centralised to a decentralised system. In most South Asian countries children's education is theoretically free; the exceptions are the Maldives, where there is no constitutionally guaranteed free education, as well as Bhutan and Nepal, where fees are charged by primary schools. But parents are still faced with unmanageable secondary financial demands, including private tuition to make up for the inadequacies of the education system.

The larger and poorer countries in the region, like India and Bangladesh, struggle financially to get sufficient resources to sustain an education system required for their vast populations, with an added challenge of getting large numbers of out-of-school children enrolled into schools. Their capacity to deliver inclusive and equitable quality education is limited by low levels of public finance for education, while the smaller emerging middle-income countries like Sri Lanka, Maldives, and Bhutan have been able to achieve universal primary school completion, and are in a better position to focus on quality of education.

Children's education in the region is also adversely affected by natural and human-made crises including natural hazards, political instability, rising extremism and civil strife that makes it difficult to deliver educational services. Afghanistan and India are among the top ten countries with the highest number of reported disasters due to natural hazards and conflict. The precarious security situation in Afghanistan is a big barrier in rolling out education programmes on a national scale.

According to UNICEF, girls face incredible hurdles to pursue their education in the region, while UNESCO estimated in 2005 that 24 million girls of primary-school age in the region were not receiving any formal education. Between 1900 and 2005, most of the countries in the region had shown progress in girls' education with Sri Lanka and the Maldives significantly ahead of the others, while the gender gap in education has widened in Pakistan and Afghanistan. Bangladesh made the greatest progress in the region in the period increasing girls' secondary school enrollment from 13 per cent to 56 per cent in ten years.

With about 21 million students in 700 universities and 40 thousand colleges India had one of the largest higher education systems in the world in 2011, accounting for 86% of all higher-level students in South Asia. Bangladesh (two million) and Pakistan (1.8 million) stood at distant second and third positions in the region. In Nepal (390,000) and Sri Lanka (230,00) the numbers were much smaller. Bhutan with only one university and Maldives with none hardly had between them about 7000 students in higher education in 2011. The gross enrollment ratio in 2011 ranged from about 10% in Pakistan and Afghanistan to above 20% in India, much below the global average of 31%.

## Health and nutrition

According to [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization "World Health Organization") (WHO), South Asia is home to two out of the three countries in the world still affected by polio, Pakistan and Afghanistan, with 306 & 28 polio cases registered in 2014 respectively. Attempts to eradicate polio have been badly hit by opposition from militants in both countries, who say the program is cover to spy on their operations. Their attacks on immunisation teams have claimed 78 lives since December 2012.

The [World Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank "World Bank") estimates that India is one of the highest ranking countries in the world for the number of children suffering from [malnutrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malnutrition "Malnutrition"). The prevalence of underweight children in India is among the highest in the world and is nearly double that of [sub-Saharan Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-Saharan_Africa "Sub-Saharan Africa") with dire consequences for mobility, mortality, productivity, and economic growth.

According to the World Bank, 64% of South Asians lived in rural areas in 2022. In 2008, about 75% of South Asia's poor lived in rural areas and most relied on agriculture for their livelihood according to the UN's Food and Agricultural Organisation.

In 2021, approximately 330 million people in the region were malnourished. A 2015 report says that Nepal reached both the WFS target as well as MDG and is moving towards bringing down the number of undernourished people to less than 5% of the population. Bangladesh reached the MDG target with the National Food Policy framework – with only 16.5% of the population undernourished. In India, the malnourished comprise just over 15 per cent of the population. While the number of malnourished people in the neighbourhood has shown a decline over the last 25 years, the number of under-nourished in Pakistan displays an upward trend. There were 28.7 million hungry in Pakistan in the 1990s – a number that has steadily increased to 41.3 million in 2015 with 22% of the population malnourished. Approximately 194.6 million people are undernourished in India, which accounts for the highest number of people suffering from hunger in any single country.

The 2006 report stated, "the low status of women in South Asian countries and their lack of nutritional knowledge are important determinants of high prevalence of underweight children in the region." Corruption and the lack of initiative on the part of the government has been one of the major problems associated with nutrition in India. Illiteracy in villages has been found to be one of the major issues that need more government attention. The report mentioned that although there has been a reduction in malnutrition due to the [Green Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Revolution "Green Revolution") in South Asia, there is concern that South Asia has "inadequate feeding and caring practices for young children."

India is a [secular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secular_state "Secular state") [federative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation "Federation") [parliamentary republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system "Parliamentary system") with the [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_India "Prime Minister of India") as head of government. With the most populous functional democracy in world and the world's longest written constitution, India has been stably sustaining the political system it adopted in 1950 with no regime change except that by a democratic election. India's sustained democratic freedoms are unique among the world's newer establishments. Since the [formation of its republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Day_(India) "Republic Day (India)") abolishing [British law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India_Act_1935 "Government of India Act 1935"), it has remained a democracy with civil liberties, an active Supreme Court, and a largely independent press. India leads region in [Democracy Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_Index "Democracy Index"). It has a [multi-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system "Multi-party system") in its internal regional politics whereas alternative transfer of powers to alliances of Indian [left-wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Progressive_Alliance "United Progressive Alliance") and [right-wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Alliance "National Democratic Alliance") political parties in national government provide it with characteristics of a [two-party state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-party_system "Two-party system"). India has been facing notable internal religious conflicts and separatism however [consistently becoming more and more stable with time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_Fragile_States_Index "List of countries by Fragile States Index").

The foundation of Pakistan lies in the [Pakistan movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_movement "Pakistan movement") which began in [colonial India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj") based on [Islamic nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_nationalism_in_South_Asia "Muslim nationalism in South Asia"). Pakistan is a [federal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_republic "Federal republic") [parliamentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system "Parliamentary system") [Islamic republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_republic "Islamic republic") and was the world's first country to adopt Islamic republic system to modify its republican status under its otherwise secular constitution in 1956. Pakistan's governance is one of the most conflicted in the world. The [military rule and the unstable governments in Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_coups_in_Pakistan "Military coups in Pakistan") have become a concern for the South Asian region. Out of 22 appointed [Pakistani Prime ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Prime_Ministers_of_Pakistan "List of Prime Ministers of Pakistan"), none of them have ever been able to complete a full term in office. The nature of [Pakistani politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Pakistan "Politics of Pakistan") can be characterised as a [multi-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system "Multi-party system").

The [unitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state "Unitary state") [semi-presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-presidential_system "Semi-presidential system") constitutional republic of [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka") is the oldest sustained democracy in Asia. Tensions between the [Sinhalese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhalese_people "Sinhalese people") and the [Tamils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_Tamils "Sri Lankan Tamils") led to the emergence of the [Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_Tigers_of_Tamil_Eelam "Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam"), a separatist [Sri Lankan Tamil militant group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_Tamil_militant_groups "Sri Lankan Tamil militant groups") and the outbreak of the [Sri Lankan civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_civil_war "Sri Lankan civil war"). The war, which ended in 2009, would undermine the country's stability for more than two and a half decades. Sri Lanka, however, has been leading the region in [HDI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index "Human Development Index") with per capita GDP well ahead of India, Pakistan, and Bangladesh. Sri Lanka has a [multi-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system "Multi-party system"), and the political situation in Sri Lanka has been dominated by an increasingly assertive ideology of [Sinhalese nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhalese_nationalism "Sinhalese nationalism").

[Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh") is a [unitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state "Unitary state") [parliamentary republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system "Parliamentary system"). The law of Bangladesh defines it as Islamic as well as secular. The nature of [Bangladeshi politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Bangladesh "Politics of Bangladesh") can be characterised as a [multi-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system "Multi-party system"). Bangladesh is a unitary state and [parliamentary democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_democracy "Parliamentary democracy"). Bangladesh also stands out as one of the few Muslim-majority democracies. "It is a moderate and generally secular and tolerant — though sometimes this is getting stretched at the moment — alternative to [violent extremism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violent_extremism "Violent extremism") in a very troubled part of the world", said [Dan Mozena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Mozena "Dan Mozena"), the US ambassador to Bangladesh. Although Bangladesh's legal code is secular, more citizens are embracing a conservative version of [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam"), with some pushing for [sharia law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharia_law "Sharia law"), analysts say. Experts say that the rise in conservatism reflects the influence of foreign-financed Islamic charities and the more austere version of Islam brought home by migrant workers in [Persian Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf "Persian Gulf") countries.

By the 18th century, the Hindu [Gorkha Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorkha_Kingdom "Gorkha Kingdom") achieved the [unification of Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Nepal "Unification of Nepal"). Hinduism became the state religion and Hindu laws were formulated as national policies. A small oligarchic group of Gorkha region based [Hindu Thakuri and Chhetri political families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nepali_political_clans "List of Nepali political clans") dominated the national politics, military and civic affairs until the abdication of the [Rana dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rana_dynasty "Rana dynasty") regime and [establishment of Parliamentary democratic system in 1951](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951_Nepalese_revolution "1951 Nepalese revolution"), which was twice suspended by Nepalese monarchs, in 1960 and 2005. It was the last Hindu state in world before [becoming a secular democratic republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Day_(Nepal) "Republic Day (Nepal)") in 2008. The country's modern development suffered due to the various significant events like the [1990 Nepalese revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Nepalese_revolution "1990 Nepalese revolution"), 1996–2006 [Nepalese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepalese_Civil_War "Nepalese Civil War"), [April 2015 Nepal earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_2015_Nepal_earthquake "April 2015 Nepal earthquake"), and the [2015 Nepal blockade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Nepal_blockade "2015 Nepal blockade") by India leading to the grave [2015–2017 Nepal humanitarian crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal_humanitarian_crisis_(2015-2017) "Nepal humanitarian crisis (2015-2017)"). There is also a huge turnover in the office of the [Prime Minister of Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_prime_ministers_of_Nepal "List of prime ministers of Nepal") leading to serious concerns of [political instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_instability "Political instability"). The country has been ranked one of the poor countries in terms of GDP per capita but it has one of the lowest levels of [hunger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunger "Hunger") problem in South Asia. When the [stability of the country ensured as late as recent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_Fragile_States_Index "List of countries by Fragile States Index"), it has also made considerable progress in development indicators outpacing many other South Asian states.

[Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan") has been a [unitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state "Unitary state") [theocratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theocracy "Theocracy") [Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_state "Islamic state") [emirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate "Emirate") since 2021. Afghanistan has been suffering from one of the most unstable regimes on earth as a result of [multiple foreign invasions, civil wars, revolutions and terrorist groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Afghanistan "War in Afghanistan"). Persisting instability for decades have left the country's economy stagnated and torn and it remains one of the most poor and [least developed countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_developed_country "Least developed country") on the planet, leading to the influx of Afghan refugees to neighbouring countries like [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran").

[Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan "Bhutan") is a Buddhist state with a [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy "Constitutional monarchy"). The country has been ranked as the least corrupt and most peaceful country in the region, with the most economic freedom, in 2016.

[Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldives "Maldives") is a unitary presidential republic with [Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam "Sunni Islam") strictly as the state religion.

### Regional politics

India has been the [dominant geopolitical power in the region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_power "Regional power") and alone accounts for most part of the landmass, population, economy and military expenditure in the region. India is a [major economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G20 "G20"), member of [G4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G4_nations "G4 nations"), has world's [third highest military budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_military_expenditures "List of countries by military expenditures") and exerts strong [cultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_India "Greater India") and [political influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_hegemony "Regional hegemony") over the region. Sometimes referred as a [great power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_power "Great power") or [emerging superpower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerging_superpower "Emerging superpower") primarily attributed to its large and expanding economic and military abilities, India acts as fulcrum of South Asia.

[Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh"), [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan"), and [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka") are [middle powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_power "Middle power") with sizeable populations and economies with significant impact on regional politics.

During the [Partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India "Partition of India") in 1947, subsequent violence and territorial disputes left relations between India and Pakistan sour and very hostile and [various confrontations and wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_wars_and_conflicts "Indo-Pakistani wars and conflicts") which largely shaped the politics of the region and contributed to the [emergence of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War "Bangladesh Liberation War") as an independent country. With [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia "Yugoslavia"), India founded the [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement "Non-Aligned Movement") but later entered into an [agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Soviet_Treaty_of_Friendship_and_Cooperation "Indo-Soviet Treaty of Friendship and Cooperation") with the former [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") following Western support for Pakistan. Amid the [Indo-Pakistani War of 1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1971 "Indo-Pakistani War of 1971"), US sent its [USS *Enterprise*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Enterprise_(CVN-65) "USS Enterprise (CVN-65)") to the Indian Ocean, which was perceived by India as a [nuclear threat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_threat "Nuclear threat"). India's [nuclear test in 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smiling_Buddha "Smiling Buddha") pushed Pakistan's nuclear program, which concluded in nuclear tests in [Chagai-I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagai-I "Chagai-I") in 1998, just 18 days after India's [series of nuclear tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokhran-II "Pokhran-II") for [thermonuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermonuclear_weapon "Thermonuclear weapon").

The [Soviet invasion of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War "Soviet–Afghan War") in 1979 accelerated efforts to form a union to restrengthen deteriorating regional security. After agreements, the union, known as the [South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Association_for_Regional_Cooperation "South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation") (SAARC), was finally established in Dhaka in December 1985. However, deterioration of [India-Pakistan ties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India%E2%80%93Pakistan_relations "India–Pakistan relations") have led India to emphasise more on sub-regional groups [SASEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia_Subregional_Economic_Cooperation "South Asia Subregional Economic Cooperation"), [BIMSTEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BIMSTEC "BIMSTEC"), and [BBIN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBIN "BBIN").

[Populism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populism "Populism") is a general characteristic of internal politics of India.
