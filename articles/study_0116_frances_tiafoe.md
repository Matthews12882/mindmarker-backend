# Frances Tiafoe

**Frances Tiafoe Jr.** ([/tiˈɑːfoʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*tee-AH-foh*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"); born January 20, 1998) is an American professional [tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis "Tennis") player. He has a career-high singles ranking of world No. 8 achieved on September 14, 2026, and a best doubles ranking of No. 160, reached in November 2021. Tiafoe has won four [ATP Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Tour "ATP Tour") singles titles across all three surfaces. His best performances at the [majors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis)#Tournaments "Grand Slam (tennis)") have been three semifinals at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Open_(tennis) "US Open (tennis)"). He is the current American No. 2 in men's singles.

The son of [Sierra Leonean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Leone "Sierra Leone") immigrants, Tiafoe was first trained at the [Junior Tennis Champions Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_Tennis_Champions_Center "Junior Tennis Champions Center") (JTCC), where his father worked as the head of maintenance. At 15, Tiafoe won the 2013 [Orange Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunlop_Orange_Bowl "Dunlop Orange Bowl"), the tournament's youngest-ever boys' singles champion. As a teenager, he won the [US Junior National Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boys%27_Junior_National_Tennis_Championship "Boys' Junior National Tennis Championship") and enjoyed success on the [ATP Challenger Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Challenger_Tour "ATP Challenger Tour"), reaching nine finals and winning four titles.

Tiafoe broke into the top 100 of the ATP rankings in 2016. At the [2019 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Australian_Open "2019 Australian Open") and [2025 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open "2025 French Open"), he reached the quarterfinals, and at the [2022 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 US Open – Men's singles"), he reached the semifinals of a major for the first time, a feat he repeated at the [2024 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 US Open – Men's singles") and the [2026 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2026 US Open – Men's singles").

## Early life and background

Tiafoe (nicknamed "Big Foe" or simply "Foe") was born on January 20, 1998, along with his twin brother, Franklin, in [Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland "Maryland"), to Constant (better known as Frances Sr.) Tiafoe and Alphina Kamara, immigrants from [Sierra Leone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Leone "Sierra Leone"). His father immigrated to the United States in 1993, while his mother joined him in 1996 to escape the [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Leone_Civil_War "Sierra Leone Civil War") in their country. In 1999, his father began working as a day laborer on a construction crew that built the [Junior Tennis Champions Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_Tennis_Champions_Center "Junior Tennis Champions Center") (JTCC) in [College Park, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_Park,_Maryland "College Park, Maryland"). When the facility was completed, he was hired as the on-site custodian and given a spare office to live in at the center. The Tiafoe brothers lived with their father at the center for five days a week for the next 11 years. They took advantage of their living situation to start playing tennis regularly at age 4. They stayed with their mother when she was not working night shifts as a nurse.

When Tiafoe and his brother were 5 years old, their father arranged for them to begin training at the JTCC, bypassing their usual fees. When Tiafoe was 8 years old, Misha Kouznetsov began coaching him at the center, taking interest in him after seeing his work ethic and interest in the sport. Kouznetsov helped sponsor Tiafoe's participation in tournaments as he progressed through the junior ranks. He continued to coach Tiafoe for nine years until he moved to the USTA National Training Center in [Boca Raton, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boca_Raton,_Florida "Boca Raton, Florida"). Tiafoe's brother Franklin stayed in Maryland, where he attended [Laurel Springs School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurel_Springs_School "Laurel Springs School"), an accredited, NCAA approved online school, while playing high school tennis at [DeMatha Catholic High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeMatha_Catholic_High_School "DeMatha Catholic High School") and later played college tennis at [Salisbury University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salisbury_University "Salisbury University").

## Juniors

Tiafoe won enough high-level junior titles to reach world No. 2 in the [International Tennis Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Tennis_Federation "International Tennis Federation") (ITF) junior rankings. His success at the juniors combined with his unusual upbringing helped him rise to national prominence before he turned pro. At the age of 14, Tiafoe won his first prestigious international tournament at [Les Petits As](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Petits_As "Les Petits As") in France. The following year, in December 2013, Tiafoe became the youngest player to win the [Orange Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunlop_Orange_Bowl "Dunlop Orange Bowl"), one of the highest-tier Grade A events on the [ITF Junior Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITF_Junior_Circuit "ITF Junior Circuit"). He defeated compatriot [Stefan Kozlov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Kozlov "Stefan Kozlov") in the final a month before turning 16. Several months later, he also won the Easter Bowl, a second-tier Grade B1 event.

With these two big titles, Tiafoe was the top seed at the [2014 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Boys%27_singles "2014 French Open – Boys' singles") junior tournament, where he was upset in the second round. He then lost at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Boys%27_singles "2014 Wimbledon Championships – Boys' singles") to the eventual champion [Noah Rubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah_Rubin_(tennis) "Noah Rubin (tennis)"). Tiafoe produced his best result at a junior Grand Slam tournament at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Boys%27_singles "2014 US Open – Boys' singles"), where he reached the semifinals before losing a tight match to [Quentin Halys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quentin_Halys "Quentin Halys"). That was the last ITF tournament he would play at the junior level. In August 2015, Tiafoe capped his junior career by winning the [USTA Junior National Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boy%27s_Junior_National_Tennis_Championship "Boy's Junior National Tennis Championship") at the age of 17. Tiafoe defeated Stefan Kozlov in the final in a five-set match, taking the first two sets and the last. With the win, he earned a wildcard into the main draw at the [2015 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_US_Open_(tennis) "2015 US Open (tennis)").

## Professional

### 2014–15: French and US Open debuts

Tiafoe made his [ATP Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Tour "ATP Tour") main-draw debut at the age of 16 and a half after being granted a [wildcard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_card_(sports) "Wild card (sports)") by his home tournament, the [2014 Washington Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Citi_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2014 Citi Open – Men's singles"). He lost to [Evgeny Donskoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgeny_Donskoy "Evgeny Donskoy") in his first career tour-level match. At the [2014 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles_qualifying "2014 US Open – Men's singles qualifying"), Tiafoe received a wildcard into the qualifying draw but lost to 11th seed [Tatsuma Ito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatsuma_Ito "Tatsuma Ito"). In the doubles tournament, he was awarded a wildcard into the main draw with [Michael Mmoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Mmoh "Michael Mmoh"). The two teenagers picked up their first career win at the ATP level in the first round by defeating veterans [Víctor Estrella Burgos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%ADctor_Estrella_Burgos "Víctor Estrella Burgos") and [Teymuraz Gabashvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teymuraz_Gabashvili "Teymuraz Gabashvili"), before losing in the second round. In March 2015, he claimed his first professional title by winning the [ITF Futures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITF_Men%27s_Circuit "ITF Men's Circuit") tournament at [Bakersfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakersfield,_California "Bakersfield, California"). He officially turned pro the following month.

In April 2015, Tiafoe broke through onto the [ATP Challenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Challenger "ATP Challenger") level. After starting the month ranked below the top 800 in the world, he put together a string of solid results that pushed him into the top 300 in the world by the time the first set of ATP rankings were published in May. Even though his ranking was too low to gain direct entry into any of the three American clay-court Challenger events, Tiafoe reached the quarterfinals of [Sarasota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Sarasota_Open "2015 Sarasota Open") as a qualifier, then reached the semifinals of [Savannah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Savannah_Challenger "2015 Savannah Challenger") as a wildcard, and finally reached his first career Challenger final at [Tallahassee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Tallahassee_Tennis_Challenger "2015 Tallahassee Tennis Challenger") with a special exempt. At the last tournament, he defeated top seed [Facundo Bagnis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facundo_Bagnis "Facundo Bagnis") to notch his first win against a top-100 opponent. Tiafoe's performance at these events was good enough to win the 2015 Har-Tru Challenge and earn the only American wildcard spot into the main draw of the [2015 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_French_Open "2015 French Open"). In his [Grand Slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis) "Grand Slam (tennis)") debut, Tiafoe lost his first-round match to [Martin Kližan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Kli%C5%BEan "Martin Kližan"). Nonetheless, he became the first 17-year-old American to play in the main draw of the men's singles tournament since [Michael Chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Chang "Michael Chang") and [Pete Sampras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Sampras "Pete Sampras") in 1989.

At the [Winston-Salem Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Winston-Salem_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2015 Winston-Salem Open – Singles") in August, Tiafoe entered the main draw as a qualifier and won his first ATP Tour-level match defeating [James Duckworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Duckworth_(tennis) "James Duckworth (tennis)") in a third-set tiebreaker. He then made his main-draw debut at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2015 US Open – Men's singles") with the wildcard he earned from winning the junior national championship. He would lose to the No. 22 seed, [Viktor Troicki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Troicki "Viktor Troicki"), in the first round. Tiafoe continued his success on the Challenger Tour and reached a second final at [Knoxville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knoxville_Challenger "Knoxville Challenger") losing to [Dan Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Evans_(tennis) "Dan Evans (tennis)"). Driven by his success at the Challenger level, Tiafoe climbed to a year-end ranking of 176, cracking the top 200 a few months before turning 18 years old.

### 2016: Challenger titles and top 100

In the 2016 season, Tiafoe consistently produced deep Challenger-level tournament runs but was unable to break through in his few opportunities at the ATP Tour level. At the [Indian Wells Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Indian_Wells_Masters "2016 Indian Wells Masters"), Tiafoe was awarded a wild card into the main draw and won his first-round match against his compatriot rival, No. 80 [Taylor Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Fritz "Taylor Fritz"), in their first ever ATP-level match. This would turn out to be his only ATP match win of the year. He lost his next match to [David Goffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Goffin "David Goffin") in a third-set tiebreak. Tiafoe's best performance in the clay-court season came at [Tallahassee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Tallahassee_Tennis_Challenger "2016 Tallahassee Tennis Challenger") where he avenged his loss to [Facundo Arguello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facundo_Arg%C3%BCello_(tennis) "Facundo Argüello (tennis)") in the final the previous year by knocking him out in the first round. For the second consecutive year, he was able to reach the final, this time losing to fellow teenager [Quentin Halys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quentin_Halys "Quentin Halys").

Tiafoe began his return to the North American hardcourts by reaching his second Challenger final of the year at [Winnetka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnetka_Challenger "Winnetka Challenger") before losing to top-seeded [Yoshihito Nishioka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshihito_Nishioka "Yoshihito Nishioka"). He then reached his third consecutive Challenger final in the United States at [Lexington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Kentucky_Bank_Tennis_Championships "2016 Kentucky Bank Tennis Championships"). The following week at [Granby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Challenger_Banque_Nationale_de_Granby "2016 Challenger Banque Nationale de Granby"), Tiafoe reached his fourth Challenger final in five such events in North America. He defeated [Marcelo Arévalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcelo_Ar%C3%A9valo "Marcelo Arévalo") in the final to capture his first Challenger title and climb to a career-high ranking of No. 123 in the world. Tiafoe was awarded a wildcard into the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_US_Open_(tennis) "2016 US Open (tennis)"), his only Grand Slam main draw of the year. He faced off against American veteran [John Isner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Isner "John Isner") in the first round and won the first two sets, but eventually lost the match in a fifth-set tiebreak. In October, Tiafoe cracked the top 100 for the first time by winning the maiden event at [Stockton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Stockton_ATP_Challenger "2016 Stockton ATP Challenger"), defeating fellow American [Noah Rubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah_Rubin_(tennis) "Noah Rubin (tennis)") in the final. He finished the year ranked 108, making him the highest-ranked player at his age for the second year in a row.

### 2017: Doubles final

At the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Australian_Open "2017 Australian Open"), Tiafoe began the year by reaching the main draw of a Grand Slam through qualifying for the first time and then recording his first career major match win over [Mikhail Kukushkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Kukushkin "Mikhail Kukushkin"). To close out the winter hard court season, he also qualified for the [Miami Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2017 Miami Open – Men's singles") and won his first round match before falling to [Roger Federer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Federer "Roger Federer"). Tiafoe followed up a good start to the year on the hard courts with a very impressive clay court season that helped his ranking climb to No. 65 in the world. He began with the [US Men's Clay Court Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_U.S._Men%27s_Clay_Court_Championships "2017 U.S. Men's Clay Court Championships") in Houston, where he reached his first career ATP final in the doubles event, after partnering with veteran [Dustin Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dustin_Brown_(tennis) "Dustin Brown (tennis)") as a wildcard entry. He then continued his success on clay by winning back-to-back Challenger titles over the next three weeks at the [Sarasota Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Sarasota_Open "2017 Sarasota Open") on green clay and the [Open du Pays d'Aix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Open_du_Pays_d%27Aix "2017 Open du Pays d'Aix") on red clay, the former of which included a victory over former top-10 player [Jürgen Melzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%BCrgen_Melzer "Jürgen Melzer") in the semifinals.

In his [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wimbledon_Championships "Wimbledon Championships") debut, Tiafoe defeated [Robin Haase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Haase "Robin Haase") in four sets for his first win over an opponent ranked in the top-50. He followed this up with his first top-10 win over world No. 7, [Alexander Zverev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Zverev "Alexander Zverev"), at the [Cincinnati Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati_Masters "Cincinnati Masters") in August. Tiafoe gained more prominence after taking [Roger Federer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Federer "Roger Federer") to five sets in his first-round match on [Arthur Ashe Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Ashe_Stadium "Arthur Ashe Stadium") at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_US_Open_(tennis) "2017 US Open (tennis)"). In September, captain [John McEnroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McEnroe "John McEnroe") chose Tiafoe to replace [Juan Martín del Potro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Mart%C3%ADn_del_Potro "Juan Martín del Potro") for Team World in the inaugural [Laver Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Laver_Cup "2017 Laver Cup"), which mostly featured players in the top-25 of the ATP rankings. He lost his only match to [Marin Čilić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marin_%C4%8Cili%C4%87 "Marin Čilić"). Tiafoe achieved a year-end ranking inside the top 100 for the first time, but only managed to be named the first alternate for the inaugural [Next Generation ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Next_Generation_ATP_Finals "2017 Next Generation ATP Finals").

### 2018: First ATP Tour title and top 50

Tiafoe entered the year having struggled on the ATP Tour, with just nine wins in 38 matches. Nonetheless, he would quadruple that number of wins by the end of the season. At the inaugural [New York Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Open_(tennis) "New York Open (tennis)"), Tiafoe reached his first career quarterfinal at an ATP Tour-level event before losing to top seed [Kevin Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Anderson_(tennis) "Kevin Anderson (tennis)"). The following week, Tiafoe entered the [Delray Beach Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Delray_Beach_Open "2018 Delray Beach Open") as a wildcard and won his first ATP title after beating [Peter Gojowczyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Gojowczyk "Peter Gojowczyk") in the final. He became the first wildcard entry to win the tournament. Along the way, he defeated his idol and world No. 10, [Juan Martín del Potro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Mart%C3%ADn_del_Potro "Juan Martín del Potro"), as well as fellow Next Gen players [Hyeon Chung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyeon_Chung "Hyeon Chung") and [Denis Shapovalov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Shapovalov "Denis Shapovalov"). With the victory, he became the youngest American to win an ATP title since [Andy Roddick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Roddick "Andy Roddick") won the [US Clay Court Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_U.S._Men%27s_Clay_Court_Championships "2002 U.S. Men's Clay Court Championships") in 2002. Tiafoe's win streak was snapped in the first round of the [Indian Wells Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_BNP_Paribas_Open "2018 BNP Paribas Open") by his compatriot [Ernesto Escobedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernesto_Escobedo "Ernesto Escobedo"). However, he bounced back at the [Miami Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Miami_Open "2018 Miami Open") to reach the fourth round, a career-best at a Masters event. He upset [Tomáš Berdych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Berdych "Tomáš Berdych") in the third round before losing to [Kevin Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Anderson_(tennis) "Kevin Anderson (tennis)") for the second time this year.

Tiafoe continued his success at the ATP level into the clay-court season. He played at the [Portugal Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Estoril_Open "2018 Estoril Open") for the first time and reached his second ATP final of the year, despite needing to save three match points in the first round. He upset the defending champion, No. 11 [Pablo Carreño Busta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Carre%C3%B1o_Busta "Pablo Carreño Busta"), in the semifinals before losing to hometown favorite [João Sousa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_Sousa "João Sousa") in the final. Nonetheless, he became the youngest American to reach a clay-court final in Europe since [Andre Agassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andre_Agassi "Andre Agassi") played in the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_French_Open "1990 French Open") final in 1990.

Tiafoe followed up on this success at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Wimbledon_Championships "2018 Wimbledon Championships") by picking up his first win over a seeded opponent at a major against No. 30, [Fernando Verdasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Verdasco "Fernando Verdasco"), en route to reaching the third round of a major for the first time. With this performance, he also broke into the top-50 of the ATP rankings after the tournament.

During the [US Open Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Open_Series "US Open Series"), Tiafoe had his best result at the [Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Rogers_Cup "2018 Rogers Cup"). He defeated two top-30 players, including hometown favorite [Milos Raonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milos_Raonic "Milos Raonic"), before losing to No. 5 [Grigor Dimitrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigor_Dimitrov "Grigor Dimitrov") in a third-set tiebreak. At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_US_Open_(tennis) "2018 US Open (tennis)"), Tiafoe won his first career match at the event against No. 29, [Adrian Mannarino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Mannarino "Adrian Mannarino"), before losing to fellow Next Gen player [Alex de Minaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_de_Minaur "Alex de Minaur") in the second round. Following the last major event of the year, Tiafoe made his Davis Cup debut for the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Davis_Cup_team "United States Davis Cup team") in the semifinal against [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_Davis_Cup_team "Croatia Davis Cup team"). After easily losing to [Marin Čilić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marin_%C4%8Cili%C4%87 "Marin Čilić") in straight sets, Tiafoe played in the fifth and decisive rubber against [Borna Ćorić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borna_%C4%86ori%C4%87 "Borna Ćorić"). Despite taking a two set to one lead, he ultimately lost the match as the United States lost the tie. For the second straight year, Tiafoe replaced [Juan Martín del Potro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Mart%C3%ADn_del_Potro "Juan Martín del Potro") in the [Laver Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Laver_Cup "2018 Laver Cup"). He again lost his only match, this time to Dimitrov. At the end of the season, Tiafoe qualified for the [Next Gen ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Next_Generation_ATP_Finals "2018 Next Generation ATP Finals"), having just barely failed to qualify the previous year. He defeated [Hubert Hurkacz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Hurkacz "Hubert Hurkacz") in his round robin group, but lost to [Jaume Munar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaume_Munar "Jaume Munar") and the eventual champion, [Stefanos Tsitsipas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefanos_Tsitsipas "Stefanos Tsitsipas"), as he did not advance out of the group. Tiafoe finished the year ranked No. 39 in the world.

### 2019: Top 30 debut

Tiafoe's biggest result of the year came at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Australian_Open "2019 Australian Open"), where he made it to the quarterfinals of a major for the first time. During the tournament, he upset No. 5 [Kevin Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Anderson_(tennis) "Kevin Anderson (tennis)") in the second round as well as No. 20, [Grigor Dimitrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigor_Dimitrov "Grigor Dimitrov"), in the fourth round before losing to No. 2, [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal "Rafael Nadal"). As a result of this run, he reached a career-high ranking of world No. 29, on February 11, 2019.

Tiafoe could not build on this success during the rest of the season. He did not win multiple matches at a tournament again until reaching the quarterfinals at the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Miami_Open "2019 Miami Open"), where he lost to [Denis Shapovalov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Shapovalov "Denis Shapovalov"). He could not defend the points from his title at the [2019 Delray Beach Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Delray_Beach_Open "2019 Delray Beach Open") or his runner-up at the [2019 Estoril Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Estoril_Open "2019 Estoril Open") a year earlier, losing in the first round at the former and in the quarterfinals at the latter. Tiafoe closed out the clay-court season with a first-round loss at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_French_Open "2019 French Open") to [Filip Krajinović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filip_Krajinovi%C4%87 "Filip Krajinović") in which he struggled with an illness. He faced more difficult draws at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Wimbledon_Championships "2019 Wimbledon Championships") and the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_US_Open_(tennis) "2019 US Open (tennis)"), losing to No. 10 [Fabio Fognini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabio_Fognini "Fabio Fognini") in the opening round of the former and No. 7 [Alexander Zverev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Zverev "Alexander Zverev") in the second round of the latter, despite pushing both opening to five sets. At the end of the season, Tiafoe qualified for the [Next Generation ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Next_Generation_ATP_Finals "2019 Next Generation ATP Finals"). He was placed in a round-robin group with [Ugo Humbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugo_Humbert "Ugo Humbert"), [Mikael Ymer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikael_Ymer "Mikael Ymer"), and [Jannik Sinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jannik_Sinner "Jannik Sinner"). After an opening-match loss to Sinner, Tiafoe defeated Humbert and Ymer to advance to knockout rounds. There, he was defeated by top seed [Alex de Minaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_de_Minaur "Alex de Minaur"). Tiafoe finished the season ranked No. 47 in the world.

### 2020: US Open fourth round

Tiafoe dropped out of the top 50 in February, after losing his quarterfinal points from the 2019 Australian Open when he was defeated in the first round of the [2020 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Australian_Open "2020 Australian Open") by [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev "Daniil Medvedev").

At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_US_Open_(tennis) "2020 US Open (tennis)"), Tiafoe advanced to the third round of the tournament for the first time by defeating [Andreas Seppi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Seppi "Andreas Seppi") of Italy, then [John Millman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Millman "John Millman") of Australia. In the third round, he knocked out [Márton Fucsovics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1rton_Fucsovics "Márton Fucsovics") of Hungary in straight sets and moved onto the round of 16, becoming at 22 the youngest American man to advance that far in the US Open since [Donald Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Young_(tennis) "Donald Young (tennis)") in 2011. He lost in straight sets in the round of 16 to the tournament's third seed, [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev "Daniil Medvedev") of Russia. Tiafoe finished the season ranked No. 59 in the world.

### 2021: First top-5 win, ATP 500 final

Tiafoe started the year at the [Delray Beach Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Delray_Beach_Open "2021 Delray Beach Open"), where he was beaten by [Cameron Norrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Norrie "Cameron Norrie") in the quarterfinals. He then lost in the second round of the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Australian_Open "2021 Australian Open") and in the first or second rounds of several [ATP 250](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_250 "ATP 250") tournaments. At the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Miami_Open "2021 Miami Open"), he beat top-30 player [Dan Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Evans_(tennis) "Dan Evans (tennis)") in a comeback, but lost in the round of 16 to No.1 seed [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev "Daniil Medvedev"). He did not have good results during the European Clay Swing as he could not qualify for the main draw of the [Madrid Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Mutua_Madrid_Open "2021 Mutua Madrid Open") or the [Rome Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Italian_Open_(tennis) "2021 Italian Open (tennis)") and he lost in the first round of the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_French_Open "2021 French Open") to [Steve Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Johnson_(tennis) "Steve Johnson (tennis)"), despite being up two sets to love.

Tiafoe started his grass-court season by defeating [Denis Kudla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Kudla "Denis Kudla") to win the [Nottingham Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Nottingham_Open "2021 Nottingham Open"). Tiafoe reached the quarterfinals at the [Queen's Club Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Queen%27s_Club_Championships "2021 Queen's Club Championships"), where he lost to [Denis Shapovalov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Shapovalov "Denis Shapovalov"). Later that grass-court season, Tiafoe had one of his greatest career wins in the first round of [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2021 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles"), beating world No. 4 and [2021 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2021 French Open – Men's singles") finalist, [Stefanos Tsitsipas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefanos_Tsitsipas "Stefanos Tsitsipas"), in straight sets to gain his first win against a top-5 player. Tiafoe became just the second American man since 2010 to defeat a top-three seed at a major, joining [Sam Querrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Querrey "Sam Querrey"), who did it at Wimbledon in both [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2016 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles") and [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2017 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles"). He then defeated [Vasek Pospisil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasek_Pospisil "Vasek Pospisil") in the second round in straight sets to reach the third round at Wimbledon for the second time in his career, where he lost to [Karen Khachanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Khachanov "Karen Khachanov"). At the [2020 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "Tennis at the 2020 Summer Olympics – Men's singles"), Tiafoe lost in the second round to Tsitsipas, who took his revenge for the Wimbledon loss.

At the [Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_National_Bank_Open "2021 National Bank Open"), Tiafoe lost in the second round of qualifying to [Emil Ruusuvuori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Ruusuvuori "Emil Ruusuvuori"), but was awarded a lucky-loser spot in the main draw after fellow American [Sebastian Korda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Korda "Sebastian Korda") withdrew. He defeated qualifier [Yoshihito Nishioka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshihito_Nishioka "Yoshihito Nishioka") in the first round, and then earned his second top-10 victory of the year by upsetting 10th-ranked home favorite [Denis Shapovalov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Shapovalov "Denis Shapovalov") in the second round. He lost in the third round to [Gaël Monfils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ga%C3%ABl_Monfils "Gaël Monfils"). The following week at the [Western & Southern Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Western_%26_Southern_Open "2021 Western & Southern Open"), Tiafoe defeated [Ugo Humbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugo_Humbert "Ugo Humbert"), but lost in the second round to [Diego Schwartzman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Schwartzman "Diego Schwartzman").

At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2021 US Open – Men's singles"), he reached the fourth round for a second consecutive year by defeating fifth seed and world No. 7, [Andrey Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Rublev "Andrey Rublev") in a five-set match. Tiafoe was the first American man to reach the second week at the US Open in consecutive years since [Andy Roddick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Roddick "Andy Roddick") and [Mardy Fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mardy_Fish "Mardy Fish") in 2011–12. He was then defeated by [Félix Auger-Aliassime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Auger-Aliassime "Félix Auger-Aliassime").

Tiafoe had to enter the main draw via qualifying in [Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Erste_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 Erste Bank Open – Singles"), beating [Alex Molčan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Mol%C4%8Dan "Alex Molčan") and [Lucas Miedler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_Miedler "Lucas Miedler"), both in three sets. In the main draw, Tiafoe beat [Dušan Lajović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du%C5%A1an_Lajovi%C4%87 "Dušan Lajović") in the first round before winning his second match of the year against Tsitsipas, after recovering from a break down in the third set. Tiafoe then beat Diego Schwartzman to reach his first ATP 500 semifinal, where he beat [Jannik Sinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jannik_Sinner "Jannik Sinner") after trailing in the second set to reach his first ATP 500 final. He would lose to Alexander Zverev in straight sets in the final.

### 2022: US Open semifinal, top 20, American No. 2

Tiafoe saw success in the European Clay swing of the season. At the [Portugal Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Estoril_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 Estoril Open – Singles"), he defeated [Monte-Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Monte-Carlo_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 Monte-Carlo Masters – Singles") finalist [Alejandro Davidovich Fokina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Davidovich_Fokina "Alejandro Davidovich Fokina") in the quarterfinals and [Sebastian Korda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Korda "Sebastian Korda") in the semifinals. He lost to [Sebastián Báez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A1n_B%C3%A1ez "Sebastián Báez") in the final, but reached the top 25 in rankings on May 2, 2022. In his next event, the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 Mutua Madrid Open – Men's singles"), Tiafoe lost to [Cristian Garín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristian_Gar%C3%ADn "Cristian Garín") in the first round. Tiafoe, who had played in the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Open "French Open") six times previously, recorded his first victory at Roland Garros in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 French Open – Men's singles") with a first-round win over [Benjamin Bonzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Bonzi "Benjamin Bonzi"). In the second round, he lost to [David Goffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Goffin "David Goffin") in four sets. At the [Wimbledon Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles"), he reached the round of 16 for the first time at this major. He lost again to Goffin this time, in a tight five-set match that lasted 4 hours and 36 minutes.

At the [Atlanta Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Atlanta_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 Atlanta Open – Singles"), Tiafoe reached the semifinals where he lost to [Jenson Brooksby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenson_Brooksby "Jenson Brooksby") in straight sets. In his next event, at his home tournament of [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Citi_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 Citi Open – Men's singles"), Tiafoe reached the quarterfinals, but let five match points slip away and lost to [Nick Kyrgios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Kyrgios "Nick Kyrgios") in three sets. At the same tournament, he also reached the semifinals in [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Citi_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_doubles "2022 Citi Open – Men's doubles") with [Alex De Minaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_De_Minaur "Alex De Minaur"). The next week, at the [Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 National Bank Open – Men's singles"), Tiafoe reached the second round with a win over [Benjamin Bonzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Bonzi "Benjamin Bonzi") before losing to No. 10 seed [Taylor Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Fritz "Taylor Fritz"). His win against Bonzi lifted him to a then-career-high ranking of No. 24, which also made him the No. 2 American player. At the [Cincinnati Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Western_%26_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 Western & Southern Open – Men's singles"), Tiafoe reached the second round with a win over No. 12 seed [Matteo Berrettini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Berrettini "Matteo Berrettini").

Tiafoe reached the fourth round of the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 US Open – Men's singles") after defeating 14th seed [Diego Schwartzman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Schwartzman "Diego Schwartzman") in the third round. In the fourth round, he recorded the biggest win of his career over No. 2 seed [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal "Rafael Nadal") to reach the quarterfinals. With his win over the 22-time Grand Slam champion, he became the first American to defeat Nadal at a Major since wildcard [James Blake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Blake_(tennis) "James Blake (tennis)") at the [2005 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2005 US Open – Men's singles"). At age 24, he also became the youngest American man to reach the US Open quarterfinals since [Andy Roddick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Roddick "Andy Roddick") in [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2006 US Open – Men's singles"). He was only the third American to defeat Nadal in a Grand Slam after Roddick ([2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2004 US Open – Men's singles")) and Blake (2005). Next, he defeated No. 9 seed [Andrey Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Rublev "Andrey Rublev") to reach his first major semifinal in his career, becoming the first American man to reach the semifinals in Flushing Meadows since 2006 when Andy Roddick reached the championship match, and the first black American man since [Arthur Ashe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Ashe "Arthur Ashe") in [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "1972 US Open – Men's singles"). In the semifinals, Tiafoe lost to [Carlos Alcaraz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Alcaraz "Carlos Alcaraz") in a five set hard-fought match lasting more than four hours. He saved a match point against him in the fourth set, after coming back from two sets to one down, but lost in the decider.

Tiafoe then participated in the [Laver Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Laver_Cup "2022 Laver Cup"), defeating [Stefanos Tsitsipas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefanos_Tsitsipas "Stefanos Tsitsipas") to seal the victory for Team World.

### 2023: United Cup champion, two titles, top 10

Tiafoe started the season at the inaugural [United Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_United_Cup "2023 United Cup"), as the No. 2 American male player, where the United States team became champion by defeating Italy in the final.

He reached the top 15 following the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 Australian Open – Men's singles") where he reached the third round.

At [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 BNP Paribas Open – Men's singles"), Tiafoe reached his first Masters 1000 semifinal, defeating [Marcos Giron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcos_Giron "Marcos Giron"), [Jason Kubler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Kubler "Jason Kubler"), qualifier [Alejandro Tabilo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Tabilo "Alejandro Tabilo"), and 2021 Indian Wells champion [Cameron Norrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Norrie "Cameron Norrie") (the biggest win of his season by ranking), all without dropping a set, before losing to fifth seed [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev "Daniil Medvedev"). At the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 Miami Open – Men's singles"), he was defeated by [Lorenzo Sonego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Sonego "Lorenzo Sonego") in the third round.

Tiafoe reached his sixth final and won his first title outside hard courts at the [U.S. Men's Clay Court Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_U.S._Men%27s_Clay_Court_Championships_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 U.S. Men's Clay Court Championships – Singles"). Due to multiple rain delays, he played and won four matches all in straight sets over the course of two days, defeating [Gijs Brouwer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gijs_Brouwer "Gijs Brouwer") in less than an hour in the semifinal and [Tomás Martín Etcheverry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1s_Mart%C3%ADn_Etcheverry "Tomás Martín Etcheverry") in the final. This tournament victory moved him to world No. 11 on April 10, 2023.

In May, Tiafoe competed at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 French Open – Men's singles") as the No. 12 seed and reached the third round of this Major for the first time, losing to No. 22 and eventual semifinalist [Alexander Zverev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Zverev "Alexander Zverev") in four sets.

During the grass court season, Tiafoe reached his seventh final and first on grass at the [Stuttgart Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_BOSS_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 BOSS Open – Singles"), defeating [Jiří Lehečka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ji%C5%99%C3%AD_Lehe%C4%8Dka "Jiří Lehečka"), sixth seed [Lorenzo Musetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Musetti "Lorenzo Musetti"), and [Marton Fucsovics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marton_Fucsovics "Marton Fucsovics") along the way. In the final, he defeated [Jan-Lennard Struff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan-Lennard_Struff "Jan-Lennard Struff") after saving a championship point in the final-set tiebreak. Tiafoe reached the world's top 10 for the first time in his career on June 19, 2023. With his titles on clay and on grass during the season, he completed the set of titles on three different surfaces. Seeded 10th at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles"), his highest career Grand Slam seeding, he reached the third round, losing to 21st seed [Grigor Dimitrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigor_Dimitrov "Grigor Dimitrov") in straight sets.

At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 US Open – Men's singles"), Tiafoe lost in the quarterfinals to compatriot [Ben Shelton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Shelton "Ben Shelton") in four sets.

### 2024: US Open semifinal, Cincinnati final

Tiafoe reached the semifinals at his home tournament, the [Delray Beach Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Delray_Beach_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2024 Delray Beach Open – Singles"), losing to third seed and compatriot [Tommy Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Paul_(tennis) "Tommy Paul (tennis)"), the eventual runner-up. At the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 Miami Open – Men's singles") he lost in the second round to Australian [Christopher O'Connell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_O%27Connell "Christopher O'Connell"), having received a [bye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_tennis_terms#B "Glossary of tennis terms") at the tournament.

At the [U.S. Men's Clay Court Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_U.S._Men%27s_Clay_Court_Championships_%E2%80%93_Singles "2024 U.S. Men's Clay Court Championships – Singles"), where he was the defending champion, Tiafoe defeated [James Duckworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Duckworth_(tennis) "James Duckworth (tennis)"), [Jordan Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Thompson_(tennis) "Jordan Thompson (tennis)"), and [Luciano Darderi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luciano_Darderi "Luciano Darderi") en route to his eighth career final and fourth on clay. He lost to top seed [Ben Shelton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Shelton "Ben Shelton") in the first all-African-American men's singles tennis final in the [Open Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Era "Open Era").

During the American Summer swing, he reached the semifinals for the first time in [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Mubadala_Citi_DC_Open "2024 Mubadala Citi DC Open") upsetting top seed [Andrey Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Rublev "Andrey Rublev"), his first top 10 win of the season, and first since 2022, before losing to eventual champion and fourth seed [Sebastian Korda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Korda "Sebastian Korda"). Next he also reached the semifinals in [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Cincinnati_Open "2024 Cincinnati Open") for only the second time in his career at a Masters level, with wins over [Alejandro Davidovich Fokina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Davidovich_Fokina "Alejandro Davidovich Fokina"), 14th seed [Lorenzo Musetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Musetti "Lorenzo Musetti"), [Jiří Lehečka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ji%C5%99%C3%AD_Lehe%C4%8Dka "Jiří Lehečka"), winning on the sixth match point, and finally fifth seed [Hubert Hurkacz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Hurkacz "Hubert Hurkacz") by retirement, his 200th career win. He was the first American male player in the Cincinnati semifinals since [John Isner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Isner "John Isner") in 2017. He reached his first Masters and biggest final of his career with a three-set stunning defeat of 15th seed [Holger Rune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holger_Rune "Holger Rune"), coming from behind in the third set and saving two match points, but he lost in straight sets to [Jannik Sinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jannik_Sinner "Jannik Sinner").

At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 US Open – Men's singles") he reached a third consecutive quarterfinal with wins over [Aleksandar Kovacevic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandar_Kovacevic_(tennis) "Aleksandar Kovacevic (tennis)"), [Alexander Shevchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Shevchenko_(tennis) "Alexander Shevchenko (tennis)"), 13th seed Ben Shelton in five sets, and 28th [Alexei Popyrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Popyrin "Alexei Popyrin") in four sets, joining American players Pete Sampras, Andre Agassi and Andy Roddick in achieving the milestone. He reached the semifinals for the second time after the retirement of tenth seed [Grigor Dimitrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigor_Dimitrov "Grigor Dimitrov") in the fourth set, setting up an all all-American semifinal with [Taylor Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Fritz "Taylor Fritz"), the first at a Grand Slam since 2005 at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_US_Open_(tennis) "2005 US Open (tennis)"), when [Andre Agassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andre_Agassi "Andre Agassi") and [Robby Ginepri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robby_Ginepri "Robby Ginepri") met. He would go on to lose in 5 sets.

After the US Open, Tiafoe went through a tough period on the tour, with a record of three wins and five losses before ending his season following the [Paris Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Masters "Paris Masters"). This slump included a confrontation with chair umpire Jimmy Pinoargote during the [Shanghai Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_Masters_(tennis) "Shanghai Masters (tennis)"). Following his second round loss to [Roman Safiullin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Safiullin "Roman Safiullin"), Tiafoe verbally abused the umpire after receiving several warnings during the match for exceeding the time allowed to serve. In November, the ATP fined him a total of $120,000 for his actions, $60,000 for verbal abuse and $60,000 for aggravated behaviour.

### 2025: French Open quarterfinal

In the first round of the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 Australian Open – Men's singles"), Tiafoe struggled against [Arthur Rinderknech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Rinderknech "Arthur Rinderknech"), eventually winning in five sets despite throwing up during the fourth set. He was defeated by [Fábián Marozsán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A1bi%C3%A1n_Marozs%C3%A1n "Fábián Marozsán") in five sets, losing in the second round of the Australian Open for the second straight season.He defeated Alex Michelsen in a five set thriller to reach the 2026 US Open Men’s Singles Semifinals.

At the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 French Open – Men's singles"), Tiafoe defeated [Roman Safiullin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Safiullin "Roman Safiullin"), [Pablo Carreno Busta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Carreno_Busta "Pablo Carreno Busta"), 23rd seed [Sebastian Korda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Korda "Sebastian Korda") and [Daniel Altmaier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Altmaier "Daniel Altmaier"), without dropping a set, to reach his first quarterfinal at Roland Garros. As a result he returned to No. 13 in the singles rankings on June 9, 2025.

### 2026: Second Cincinnati final, 3rd US Open semifinal, world No. 8

Tiafoe reached his first final of the year in [Acapulco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Abierto_Mexicano_Telcel_%E2%80%93_Singles "2026 Abierto Mexicano Telcel – Singles") as the 8th seed, losing to [Flavio Cobolli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flavio_Cobolli "Flavio Cobolli") in the final in straight sets. His success continued into the sunshine double, where he reached the fourth round of [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_BNP_Paribas_Open_-_Men%27s_singles "2026 BNP Paribas Open - Men's singles") and his fifth Masters 1000 quarterfinal in [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Miami_Open_-_Men%27s_singles "2026 Miami Open - Men's singles"). He capped off the first North American swing of the year by reaching the semifinals in [Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_U.S._Men%27s_Clay_Court_Championships_-_Singles "2026 U.S. Men's Clay Court Championships - Singles").

Tiafoe won his fourth title in [Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Halle_Open_-_Singles "2026 Halle Open - Singles"), beating top 10 players [Flavio Cobolli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flavio_Cobolli "Flavio Cobolli"), [Felix Auger-Aliassime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Auger-Aliassime "Felix Auger-Aliassime") and [Taylor Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Fritz "Taylor Fritz") on his way there. He reached the third round of [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Wimbledon_Championships_-_Men%27s_singles "2026 Wimbledon Championships - Men's singles").

Tiafoe's success on American soil continued. He reached his second Masters 1000 final in [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Cincinnati_Open_-_Men%27s_singles "2026 Cincinnati Open - Men's singles"), again upsetting Auger-Aliassime before losing to [Arthur Fils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Fils "Arthur Fils") in three sets in the final. At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_US_Open_-_Men%27s_singles "2026 US Open - Men's singles"), Tiafoe defeated [Martin Damm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Damm_(born_2003) "Martin Damm (born 2003)") in five sets before beating [Rei Sakamoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rei_Sakamoto "Rei Sakamoto"), [Valentin Vacherot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentin_Vacherot "Valentin Vacherot") and world no. 8 [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev "Daniil Medvedev") in straight sets to reach his fourth US Open quarterfinal. There, he came back from a two set deficit to defeat [Alex Michelsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Michelsen "Alex Michelsen") and reach his third US Open semifinal, which he lost to Ben Shelton in four sets. His performance in the US Open gave Tiafoe a career-high ranking of world no. 8.

## World TeamTennis

Tiafoe has played three seasons of [World TeamTennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_TeamTennis "World TeamTennis"), all with his hometown team the [Washington Kastles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Kastles "Washington Kastles"), making his debut in 2017. He was to return to the Kastles during the 2020 WTT season, but did not play after testing positive for [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19 "COVID-19").

## Playing style

Like many of his top-ranked American contemporaries such as [Jack Sock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sock "Jack Sock") and [Sam Querrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Querrey "Sam Querrey"), Tiafoe plays an aggressive offensive game that relies on a big serve and powerful forehand. At 6 feet 2 inches tall, Tiafoe can launch serves at over 140 mph, and regularly hits first serves between 120 and 140 mph. After facing him at the [2016 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2016 US Open – Men's singles"), [John Isner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Isner "John Isner") said that Tiafoe could return his serve — widely regarded as one of the best in the game — as well as any player on tour outside of [Novak Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic"). He also said that Tiafoe's second serve could use improvement. Tiafoe's most unusual shot is his forehand, which carries heavy topspin and is driven by an unusual arm motion. When Tiafoe won the [2018 Delray Beach Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Delray_Beach_Open "2018 Delray Beach Open"), he credited an improved serve for his better play in the tournament.

## Coaches

From age 8 to 17, Tiafoe was coached at the [Junior Tennis Champions Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_Tennis_Champions_Center "Junior Tennis Champions Center") in Maryland by Misha Kouznetsov, who had played college tennis at [UMBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_Baltimore_County "University of Maryland, Baltimore County") and coached tennis at [Robert Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Morris_University "Robert Morris University"). Kouznetsov left his job at the JTCC to work with Tiafoe full-time. After Tiafoe moved to Florida to train with the USTA, he was coached by [José Higueras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Higueras "José Higueras") from Spain, who had led fellow Americans [Michael Chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Chang "Michael Chang") and [Jim Courier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Courier "Jim Courier") to Grand Slam titles. Tiafoe also worked with [Nicolás Todero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Todero "Nicolás Todero") while at USTA.

[Robby Ginepri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robby_Ginepri "Robby Ginepri"), a former [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Open_(tennis) "US Open (tennis)") semifinalist, began coaching Tiafoe in the fall of 2016. Tiafoe's friend Zack Evenden started to help coach him shortly before he won his first ATP title in 2018. Evenden took over as Tiafoe's primary coach before the start of the 2019 season.

In the 2020 season, Tiafoe hired former top-10 player [Wayne Ferreira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Ferreira "Wayne Ferreira") as an additional coach. In July 2021, Tiafoe and Evenden parted ways by mutual agreement, and Ferreira became his primary coach. In December 2023, Tiafoe parted with Ferreira and reunited with former USTA coach [Diego Moyano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Moyano "Diego Moyano").

In July 2024 Tiafoe hired [David Witt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Witt "David Witt"); the two parted ways in October 2025. The team shakeup also included parting ways with his five-year coach Jordi Arconada. Since January 2026 he has been coached by Dr. Mark Kovacs, a physiologist and biomechanics researcher.

Starting in January 2025, Tiafoe is sponsored by [lululemon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lululemon_Athletica "Lululemon Athletica") and is a brand ambassador for them. Tiafoe was previously sponsored by Nike since May 2016. He was also previously sponsored by Adidas. ## Personal life

Tiafoe made known his relationship with former tennis player turned social media influencer [Ayan Broomfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayan_Broomfield "Ayan Broomfield") in 2018.

His tennis idol growing up was [Juan Martín del Potro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Mart%C3%ADn_del_Potro "Juan Martín del Potro"), in part because the Argentine was the first pro to sign a tennis ball for him. The two first faced each other at [Acapulco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Abierto_Mexicano_Telcel "2017 Abierto Mexicano Telcel") in 2017, with del Potro winning the match in a third-set tiebreak. Tiafoe picked up his first win over his idol at the [2018 Delray Beach Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Delray_Beach_Open "2018 Delray Beach Open") en route to his first career ATP title.

Tiafoe likes to watch and play basketball, and is a big fan of fellow Washington, D.C. area native [Kevin Durant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Durant "Kevin Durant"). He is also a fan of Washington area sports teams, including the [Washington Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Spirit "Washington Spirit") in the [National Women's Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Women%27s_Soccer_League "National Women's Soccer League"), [Washington Mystics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Mystics "Washington Mystics") in the [Women's National Basketball Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_National_Basketball_Association "Women's National Basketball Association"), [Washington Wizards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Wizards "Washington Wizards") in the [National Basketball Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Basketball_Association "National Basketball Association"), [Washington Commanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Commanders "Washington Commanders") in the [National Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League "National Football League"), and the [Washington Capitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Capitals "Washington Capitals") in the [National Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Hockey_League "National Hockey League").

Due to Tiafoe's connections to the [Junior Tennis Champions Center (JTCC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_Tennis_Champions_Center "Junior Tennis Champions Center") in [College Park, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_Park,_Maryland "College Park, Maryland"), and his childhood in [Hyattsville, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyattsville,_Maryland "Hyattsville, Maryland"), many fans of the University of [Maryland Terrapins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland_Terrapins "Maryland Terrapins") are also fans of Tiafoe. Tiafoe is regularly covered by [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park "University of Maryland, College Park") Media and Fan Sites. After Tiafoe defeated Rafael Nadal and reached the semifinals of the US Open in 2022, he attended a [Maryland Terrapins football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland_Terrapins_football "Maryland Terrapins football") game where he was honored for his accomplishments. Tiafoe also attends [Maryland Terrapins Basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland_Terrapins_men%27s_basketball "Maryland Terrapins men's basketball") games. Tiafoe has a long-term relationship with [University of Maryland Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland_Terrapins_football "Maryland Terrapins football") coach [Mike Locksley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Locksley "Mike Locksley"), whom he met as a teenager, and has appeared on Locksley's podcast.

Tiafoe has embraced his position both as one of the few players of color on the ATP Tour and as a potential role model to youngsters in general, saying, "That's one of my biggest motivations – to get more black people playing tennis... But I'm just trying to inspire everyone, doesn't matter what race... especially younger people."

Tiafoe played in the [2023 NBA Celebrity All-Star Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_All-Star_Celebrity_Game#2023_NBA_Celebrity_All-Star_Game "NBA All-Star Celebrity Game").
