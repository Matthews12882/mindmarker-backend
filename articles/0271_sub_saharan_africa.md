# Sub-Saharan Africa

**Sub-Saharan Africa** is the areas and regions of the continent of [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa "Africa") that lie south of the [Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara "Sahara"). These include [Central Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Africa "Central Africa"), [East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Africa "East Africa"), [Southern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Africa "Southern Africa"), and [West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Africa "West Africa"). Geopolitically, in addition to the [African countries and territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states_and_dependent_territories_in_Africa "List of sovereign states and dependent territories in Africa") that are situated fully in that specified region, the term may also include polities that only have part of their territory located in that region, per the definition of the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_geoscheme_for_Africa "United Nations geoscheme for Africa") (UN). This is considered a non-standardised geographical region with the number of countries included varying from 46 to 48 depending on the organisation describing the region (e.g. [UN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations"), [WHO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization "World Health Organization"), [World Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank "World Bank"), etc.). The [African Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_the_African_Union "Regions of the African Union") (AU) uses a different regional breakdown, recognising all 55 member states on the continent—grouping them into five distinct and standard regions.

The term serves as a grouping counterpart to [North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Africa "North Africa"), which is instead grouped with the definition of [MENA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_and_North_Africa "Middle East and North Africa") (i.e. [Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East "Middle East") and North Africa) as it is part of the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world "Arab world"), and most North African states are likewise members of both the [Arab League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_League "Arab League") and the [AU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union "African Union"). However, while they are also [member states of the Arab League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_states_of_the_Arab_League "Member states of the Arab League"), the [Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comoros "Comoros"), [Djibouti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djibouti "Djibouti"), [Mauritania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritania "Mauritania"), and [Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somalia "Somalia") (and sometimes [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan "Sudan")) are all geographically considered to be part of sub-Saharan Africa. Overall, the [UN Development Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Development_Programme "United Nations Development Programme") applies the "sub-Saharan" classification to 46 of Africa's 55 countries, excluding Djibouti, [SADR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahrawi_Arab_Democratic_Republic "Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic"), Somalia, and Sudan.

Since around [3900 BCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_humid_period#End "African humid period"), the Saharan and sub-Saharan regions of Africa have been separated by the extremely harsh climate of the sparsely populated Sahara, forming an effective barrier that is interrupted only by the [Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile "Nile") in Sudan, though navigation on the Nile was blocked by the [Sudd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudd "Sudd") and [the river's cataracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cataracts_of_the_Nile "Cataracts of the Nile"). The [Sahara pump theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara_pump_theory "Sahara pump theory") explains how [flora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_history_of_Africa#Flora "Natural history of Africa") and [fauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna_of_Africa "Fauna of Africa") (including *[Homo sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recent_African_origin_of_modern_humans "Recent African origin of modern humans")*) left Africa to penetrate [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia "Eurasia") and beyond. African [pluvial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pluvial "Pluvial") periods are associated with a "[Wet Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_humid_period "African humid period")" phase, during which larger lakes and more rivers existed.

## Nomenclature and conceptual criticism

Geographers historically divided the region into several distinct [ethnographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnography "Ethnography") sections based on each area's respective inhabitants. The concept of "sub-Saharan Africa" has been criticised as a [racist construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_racism "Scientific racism") intended to separate Africa. Critics from various countries have provided arguments supporting the interconnectedness of continental Africa, pointing to historical and cultural connections, as well as [trade between North, West, and East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Saharan_trade "Trans-Saharan trade"). Another source of criticism has been that traditional racial taxonomy which has arbitrarily divided separated Africa into a white "northern" Africa and black "southern" Africa, has overlooked [common genetic links](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_history_of_Africa "Genetic history of Africa") and regional diversity across the continent.

Commentators in Arabic in the [medieval period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-classical_history "Post-classical history") used the general term *bilâd as-sûdân* ("Land of the Blacks") for the vast [Sudan region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_(region) "Sudan (region)") (an expression denoting [Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Africa "Central Africa") and [West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Africa "West Africa")), or sometimes extending from the coast of West Africa to [Western Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Sudan "Western Sudan"). Its equivalent in [Southeast Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Africa "Southeast Africa") was *[Zanj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanj "Zanj")* ("Country of the Blacks"), which referred primarily to the [Swahili coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_coast "Swahili coast").

The geographers drew an explicit ethnographic distinction between the Sudan region and its analogue Zanj, from the area to their extreme east on the [Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea "Red Sea") coast in the [Horn of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horn_of_Africa "Horn of Africa"). In modern-day Ethiopia and [Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrea "Eritrea") was *[Al-Habash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Habash "Al-Habash")* or Abyssinia, which was inhabited by the *Habash* or Abyssinians, who were the forebears of the [Habesha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habesha_peoples "Habesha peoples"). In northern Somalia was *[Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_(region) "Barbara (region)")* or the *Bilad al-Barbar* ("Land of the [Berbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbers "Berbers")"), which was inhabited by the Eastern *Baribah* or *Barbaroi*, as the ancestors of the [Somalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_people "Somali people") were referred to by medieval Arab and ancient [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks "Greeks") geographers, respectively.

## List of countries and regional organisation

Sub-Saharan Africa is composed of 49 countries. Only six African countries are not geopolitically a part of sub-Saharan Africa: [Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeria "Algeria"), [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt"), [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya "Libya"), [Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco "Morocco"), [Tunisia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisia "Tunisia") and [Western Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Sahara "Western Sahara") (claimed by Morocco); they form the [UN subregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_subregion "UN subregion") of [Northern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Africa "Northern Africa"), which also makes up the largest bloc of the [Arab World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_World "Arab World"). Nevertheless, some international organisations include Sudan as part of North Africa. Although a long-standing member of the [Arab League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_League "Arab League"), Sudan has around 30% non-Arab populations in the west ([Darfur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darfur "Darfur"), [Masalit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masalit_people "Masalit people"), [Zaghawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaghawa_people "Zaghawa people")), far north ([Nubian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubian_people "Nubian people")) and south ([Kordofan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kordofan "Kordofan"), [Nuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuba "Nuba")), and a large mixed native Nubian and Arab population (not just Arabized), that represents the majority at 70% hence its inclusion in North Africa. Geopolitically, culturally, historically and economically, Sudan has long standing relationships with the wider Arab world. It's most notable connections flow North to [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt") and East across the Red Sea to [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia "Saudi Arabia"), [Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar "Qatar"), [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAE "UAE") and Sudan is seen as a geostrategic Red Sea state in the wider Middle East and North African region.. [Mauritania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritania "Mauritania") and [Niger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niger "Niger") only include a band of the Sahel along their southern borders. All other African countries have at least significant portions of their territory within sub-Saharan Africa.

## History

### Prehistory

According to [palaeontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleontology "Paleontology"), early hominid skull anatomy was similar to that of their close cousins, the great African forest [apes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_ape "Great ape"), [gorilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorilla "Gorilla") and [chimpanzee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_chimpanzee "Common chimpanzee"). However, they had adopted a [bipedal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biped "Biped") locomotion and freed hands, giving them a crucial advantage enabling them to live in both forested areas and on the open [savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savanna "Savanna") at a time when Africa was drying up, with savanna encroaching on forested areas. This occurred 10 million to 5 million years ago.

By 3 million years ago several [australopithecine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australopithecus "Australopithecus") [hominid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominid "Hominid") species had developed throughout [Southern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Africa "Southern Africa"), [East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Africa "East Africa"), and [Central Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Africa "Central Africa"). They were tool users rather than tool manufacturers. The next major evolutionary step occurred around 2.3 million BCE, when primitive [stone tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithic_technology "Lithic technology") were used to scavenge the carcasses of animals killed by other predators, both for their meat and their marrow. In hunting, *H. habilis* was most likely not capable of competing with large predators and was more prey than hunter, although *H. habilis* likely did steal eggs from nests and may have been able to catch small [game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_(food) "Game (food)") and weakened larger prey such as cubs and older animals. The tools were classed as [Oldowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldowan "Oldowan").

Roughly 1.8 million years ago, *[Homo ergaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_ergaster "Homo ergaster")* first appeared in the fossil record in Africa. From *Homo ergaster*, *[Homo erectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_erectus "Homo erectus")* (upright man) evolved 1.5 million years ago. Some of the earlier representatives of this species were small-brained and used primitive [stone tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithic_technology "Lithic technology"), much like *[H. habilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_habilis "Homo habilis")*. The brain later grew in size, and *H. erectus* eventually developed a more complex stone tool technology called the [Acheulean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acheulean "Acheulean"). Potentially the first hominid to engage in hunting, *H. erectus* mastered the art of making fire. They were the first hominids to leave Africa, going on to colonise the entire [Old World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World "Old World"), and perhaps later on giving rise to *[Homo floresiensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_floresiensis "Homo floresiensis")*. Although some recent writers suggest that *[H. georgicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._georgicus "H. georgicus")*, a *H. habilis* [descendant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lineage_(anthropology) "Lineage (anthropology)"), was the first and most primitive hominid to ever live outside Africa, many scientists consider *H. georgicus* to be an early and primitive member of the *H. erectus* species.

The fossil and genetic evidence shows *[Homo sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_sapiens "Homo sapiens")* developed in East and Southern Africa by around 350,000 to 260,000 years ago and gradually migrated across the continent in waves. Between 50,000 and 60,000 years ago, their expansion [out of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-origin_hypothesis "Single-origin hypothesis") launched the colonisation of the planet by modern humans. By 10,000 BCE, *Homo sapiens* had spread to all corners of the world. This dispersal of the human species is suggested by linguistic, cultural and genetic evidence.

During the 11th millennium [BP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Before_Present "Before Present"), pottery was independently invented in West Africa, with the earliest pottery there dating to about 9,400 BC from central Mali. It spread throughout the [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel "Sahel") and southern [Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara "Sahara").

After the Sahara became a desert, it did not present a totally impenetrable barrier for travellers between north and south because of the application of animal husbandry towards carrying water, food, and supplies across the desert. Prior to the introduction of the [camel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel "Camel"), the use of oxen, mule, and horses for desert crossing was common, and trade routes followed chains of [oases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oasis "Oasis") that were strung across the desert. The [trans-saharan trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-saharan_trade "Trans-saharan trade") was in full motion by 500 BCE with [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage "Carthage") being a major economic force for its establishment. It is thought that the camel was first brought to [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt") after the [Persian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire "Achaemenid Empire") conquered Egypt in 525 BCE, although large herds did not become common enough in North Africa for camels to be the pack animal of choice for the trans-saharan trade.

### West Africa

The [Bantu expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_expansion "Bantu expansion") is a major migration movement that originated in West Central Africa (possibly around Cameroon) around 2500 BCE, reaching East and Central Africa by 1000 BCE and Southern Africa by the early centuries CE.

The [Djenné-Djenno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djenn%C3%A9-Djenno "Djenné-Djenno") city-state flourished from 250 BCE to 900 CE and was influential to the development of the [Ghana Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Empire "Ghana Empire"). The [Nok culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nok_culture "Nok culture") of Nigeria (lasting from 1,500 BCE to 200 CE) is known from a type of [terracotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terracotta "Terracotta") figure. There were a number of medieval empires of the southern Sahara and the Sahel, based on [trans-Saharan trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Saharan_trade "Trans-Saharan trade"), including the Ghana Empire and the [Mali Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_Empire "Mali Empire"), [Songhai Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songhai_Empire "Songhai Empire"), the [Kanem Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanem_Empire "Kanem Empire") and the subsequent [Bornu Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bornu_Empire "Bornu Empire"). They built stone structures like in [Tichit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tichit "Tichit"), but mainly constructed in [adobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adobe "Adobe"). The [Great Mosque of Djenne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Mosque_of_Djenne "Great Mosque of Djenne") is most reflective of Sahelian architecture and is the largest adobe building in the world.

In the forest zone, several states and empires such as [Bono State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bono_state "Bono state"), [Akwamu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akwamu "Akwamu") and others emerged. The [Ashanti Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashanti_Empire "Ashanti Empire") arose in the 18th century in modern-day [Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana "Ghana"). The [Kingdom of Nri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Nri "Kingdom of Nri") was established by the [Igbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igbo_people "Igbo people") in the 11th century, was famous for having a priest-king who wielded no military power. Nri was a rare African state which was a haven for freed slaves and outcasts who sought refuge in their territory. Other major states included the kingdoms of [Ifẹ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ife "Ife") and [Oyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyo_Empire "Oyo Empire") in the western block of Nigeria which became prominent about 700–900 and 1400 respectively, and were centres of [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_people "Yoruba people") culture. The Yoruba built massive mud walls around their cities, the most famous being [Sungbo's Eredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sungbo%27s_Eredo "Sungbo's Eredo"). Another prominent kingdom in southwestern Nigeria was the [Kingdom of Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Benin "Kingdom of Benin"), whose power lasted between the 15th and 19th century. Their dominance reached as far as the well-known city of Eko which was named [Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos "Lagos") by the Portuguese traders and other early European settlers. The Edo-speaking people of Benin are known for their famous bronze casting and rich coral, wealth, ancient science and technology and the [Walls of Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walls_of_Benin "Walls of Benin"), one of the longest man-made structures on the world.

In the 18th century, the Oyo and the [Aro Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aro_Confederacy "Aro Confederacy") were responsible for most of the slaves exported from modern-day Nigeria, selling them to [European slave traders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_slave_trade "Atlantic slave trade"). Following the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars "Napoleonic Wars"), the British expanded their influence into the Nigerian interior. In 1885, British claims to a West African sphere of influence received international recognition, and in the following year the [Royal Niger Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Niger_Company "Royal Niger Company") was chartered under the leadership of [Sir George Goldie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Taubman_Goldie "George Taubman Goldie"). In 1900, the company's territory came under the control of the British government, which moved to consolidate its hold over the area of modern Nigeria. On 1 January 1901, Nigeria [became](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Nigeria "Colonial Nigeria") a [British protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_protectorate "British protectorate") as part of the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire"), the foremost world power at the time. Nigeria was granted its [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence "Independence") in 1960 during the period of [decolonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonization "Decolonization").

### Central Africa

Archeological finds in Central Africa provide evidence of human settlement that may date back over 10,000 years. According to Zangato and Holl, there is evidence of iron-smelting in the [Central African Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_African_Republic "Central African Republic") and [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon "Cameroon") that may date back to 3,000 to 2,500 BCE. Extensive walled sites and settlements have recently been found in [Zilum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology_of_Zilum "Archaeology of Zilum"), [Chad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad "Chad"). The area is located approximately 60 km (37 mi) southwest of [Lake Chad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Chad "Lake Chad"), and has been [radiocarbon dated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiocarbon_dating "Radiocarbon dating") to the first millennium BCE.

Trade and improved agricultural techniques supported more sophisticated societies, leading to the early civilisations of [Sao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sao_civilization "Sao civilization"), [Kanem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanem_Empire "Kanem Empire"), [Bornu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bornu_Empire "Bornu Empire"), [Shilluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shilluk_Kingdom "Shilluk Kingdom"), [Baguirmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Baguirmi "Kingdom of Baguirmi"), and [Wadai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadai_Empire "Wadai Empire").

Following the [Bantu Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_Migration "Bantu Migration") into Central Africa, during the 14th century, the [Luba Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luba_Kingdom "Luba Kingdom") in southeast Congo came about under a king whose political authority derived from religious, spiritual legitimacy. The kingdom controlled agriculture and regional trade of salt and iron from the north and copper from the Zambian/Congo copper belt.

Rival kingship factions which split from the Luba Kingdom later moved among the Lunda people, marrying into its elite and laying the foundation of the [Lunda Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunda_Empire "Lunda Empire") in the 16th century. The ruling dynasty centralised authority among the Lunda under the Mwata Yamyo or Mwaant Yaav. The Mwata Yamyo's legitimacy, like that of the Luba king, came from being viewed as a spiritual religious guardian. This [imperial cult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_cult "Imperial cult") or system of divine kings was spread to most of central Africa by rivals in kingship migrating and forming new states. Many new states received legitimacy by claiming descent from the Lunda dynasties.

The [Kingdom of Kongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kongo "Kingdom of Kongo") existed from the Atlantic west to the Kwango river to the east. During the 15th century, the Bakongo farming community was united with its capital at [M'banza-Kongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%27banza-Kongo "M'banza-Kongo"), under the king title, [Manikongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manikongo "Manikongo"). Other significant states and peoples included the [Kuba Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuba_Kingdom "Kuba Kingdom"), producers of the famous raffia cloth, the [Eastern Lunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Lunda "Eastern Lunda"), [Bemba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bemba_people "Bemba people"), [Burundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burundi "Burundi"), [Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwanda "Rwanda"), and the [Kingdom of Ndongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Ndongo "Kingdom of Ndongo").

### East Africa

#### Sudan

[Nubia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubia "Nubia"), covered by present-day northern [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan "Sudan") and southern [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt"), was referred to as "[Aethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aethiopia "Aethiopia")" ("land of the burnt face") by the [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece "Ancient Greece"). Nubia in her greatest phase is considered sub-Saharan Africa's oldest urban civilisation. Nubia was a major source of gold for the ancient world. Nubians built famous structures and numerous pyramids. Sudan, the site of ancient Nubia, has more pyramids than anywhere else in the world.[*[better source needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")*]

#### Horn of Africa

The [Axumite Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axumite_Empire "Axumite Empire") spanned the southern Sahara, south Arabia and the Sahel along the western shore of the [Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea "Red Sea"). Located in northern [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia "Ethiopia") and [Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrea "Eritrea"), Aksum was deeply involved in the trade network between India and the Mediterranean. Growing from the proto-Aksumite [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age "Iron Age") period (c. 4th century BCE), it rose to prominence by the 1st century CE. The Aksumites constructed monolithic [stelae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stele "Stele") to cover the graves of their kings, such as [King Ezana's Stele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Ezana%27s_Stele "King Ezana's Stele"). The later [Zagwe dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagwe_dynasty "Zagwe dynasty"), established in the 12th century, built churches out of solid rock. These rock-hewn structures include the [Church of St. George at Lalibela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Saint_George,_Lalibela "Church of Saint George, Lalibela").

In [ancient Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_somalia#Ancient "History of somalia"), city-states flourished such as [Opone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opone "Opone"), [Mosyllon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Guardafui "Cape Guardafui") and [Malao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malao_(ancient) "Malao (ancient)") that competed with the [Sabaeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabaeans "Sabaeans"), [Parthians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthia "Parthia") and [Axumites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axumite_Empire "Axumite Empire") for the wealthy [Indo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India")–[Greco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Roman "Greco-Roman")–[Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome "Ancient Rome") trade.

In the Middle Ages several powerful Somali empires dominated the region's trade, including the [Ajuran Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajuran_Sultanate "Ajuran Sultanate"), which excelled in [hydraulic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic "Hydraulic") [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering "Engineering") and fortress building, the [Sultanate of Adal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Adal "Sultanate of Adal"), whose General [Ahmed Gurey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Gurey "Ahmed Gurey") was the first African commander in history to use cannon warfare on the continent during [Adal's conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abyssinian%E2%80%93Adal_War "Abyssinian–Adal War") of the [Ethiopian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Empire "Ethiopian Empire"), and the [Geledi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geledi_Sultanate "Geledi Sultanate"), whose military dominance forced governors of the [Omani empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oman "Oman") north of the city of [Lamu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamu "Lamu") to pay tribute to the [Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_people "Somali people") Sultan [Ahmed Yusuf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Yusuf_(Gobroon) "Ahmed Yusuf (Gobroon)").

#### Southeast Africa

According to the theory of [recent African origin of modern humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recent_African_origin_of_modern_humans "Recent African origin of modern humans"), the mainstream position held within the scientific community, all humans originate from either Southeast Africa or the Horn of Africa. During the first millennium CE, [Nilotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilotic "Nilotic") and [Bantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_languages "Bantu languages")-speaking peoples [moved into the region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_expansion "Bantu expansion"), and the latter now account for three-quarters of Kenya's population.

On the coastal section of Southeast Africa, a mixed Bantu community developed through contact with [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim "Muslim") [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab "Arab") and [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_people "Persian people") traders, leading to the development of the mixed Arab, Persian and African [Swahili City States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_Coast "Swahili Coast"). The [Swahili culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_culture "Swahili culture") that emerged from these exchanges evinces many Arab and Islamic influences not seen in traditional Bantu culture, as do the many [Afro-Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Arab "Afro-Arab") members of the Bantu [Swahili people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_people "Swahili people"). With its original speech community centered on the coastal parts of [Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania "Tanzania") (particularly [Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar "Zanzibar")) and [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya "Kenya") – a seaboard referred to as the [Swahili Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_Coast "Swahili Coast") – the Bantu [Swahili language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_language "Swahili language") contains many [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language "Arabic language") [loan-words](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loan-words "Loan-words") as a consequence of these interactions.

The earliest Bantu inhabitants of the Southeast coast of Kenya and Tanzania encountered by these later Arab and Persian settlers have been variously identified with the trading settlements of [Rhapta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhapta "Rhapta"), [Azania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azania "Azania") and [Menouthias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menouthias "Menouthias") referenced in early Greek and Chinese writings from 50 CE to 500 CE. These early writings perhaps document the first wave of Bantu settlers to reach Southeast Africa during their migration.

Between the 14th and 15th centuries, large medieval Southeast African kingdoms and states emerged, such as the [Buganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buganda "Buganda"), [Bunyoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunyoro "Bunyoro") and [Karagwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karagwe "Karagwe") kingdoms of Uganda and Tanzania.

During the early 1960s, the Southeast African nations achieved independence from colonial rule.

### Southern Africa

Settlements of [Bantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_languages "Bantu languages")-speaking peoples, who were [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron "Iron")-using agriculturists and herdsmen, were already present south of the [Limpopo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limpopo_River "Limpopo River") by the 4th or 5th century displacing and absorbing the original [Khoisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoisan_languages "Khoisan languages") speakers. They slowly moved south, and the earliest ironworks in modern-day [KwaZulu-Natal Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KwaZulu-Natal "KwaZulu-Natal") are believed to date from around 1050. The southernmost group was the [Xhosa people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xhosa_people "Xhosa people"), whose language incorporates certain linguistic traits from the earlier Khoisan inhabitants. They reached the [Fish River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Fish_River "Great Fish River") in today's [Eastern Cape Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Cape "Eastern Cape"). [Monomotapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monomotapa "Monomotapa") was a medieval kingdom (c. 1250–1629), which existed between the [Zambezi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambezi "Zambezi") and [Limpopo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limpopo_River "Limpopo River") rivers of [Southern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Africa "Southern Africa") in the territory of modern-day [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe "Zimbabwe") and [Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique "Mozambique"). Its old capital was located at [Great Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Zimbabwe "Great Zimbabwe").

In 1487, [Bartolomeu Dias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartolomeu_Dias "Bartolomeu Dias") became the first European to reach the southernmost tip of Africa. In 1652, a [victualling station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victualler "Victualler") was established at the [Cape of Good Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_of_Good_Hope "Cape of Good Hope") by [Jan van Riebeeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_van_Riebeeck "Jan van Riebeeck") on behalf of the [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company "Dutch East India Company"). For most of the 17th and 18th centuries, the slowly expanding settlement was a [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Empire "Dutch Empire") possession. In 1795, the Dutch colony was captured by the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain "Kingdom of Great Britain") during the [French Revolutionary Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolutionary_Wars "French Revolutionary Wars"). The British intended to use [Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town "Cape Town") as a major port on the route to [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia") and [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent"). It was later returned to the Dutch in 1803, but soon afterward the Dutch East India Company declared bankruptcy, and the Dutch (now under French control) and the British found themselves at war again. The British captured the Dutch possession yet again at the [Battle of Blaauwberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blaauwberg "Battle of Blaauwberg"), commanded by [Sir David Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_David_Baird,_1st_Baronet "Sir David Baird, 1st Baronet"). The [Zulu Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulu_Kingdom "Zulu Kingdom") was a Southern African tribal state in what is now [KwaZulu-Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KwaZulu-Natal "KwaZulu-Natal") in southeastern South Africa. The small kingdom gained world fame during and after their defeat in the [Anglo-Zulu War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Zulu_War "Anglo-Zulu War"). During the 1950s and early 1960s, most sub-Saharan African nations achieved independence from colonial rule.

## Geography

### Climate zones and ecoregions

Sub-Saharan Africa has a wide variety of [climate zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_zone "Climate zone") or [biomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biome "Biome"). [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa "South Africa") and the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Democratic Republic of the Congo") in particular are considered [megadiverse countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megadiverse_countries "Megadiverse countries"). It has a dry winter season and a wet summer season.

* The [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel "Sahel") extends across all of Africa at a latitude of about 10° to 15° N. Countries that include parts of the Sahara Desert proper in their northern territories and parts of the Sahel in their southern region include [Mauritania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritania "Mauritania"), [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali "Mali"), [Niger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niger "Niger"), [Chad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad "Chad"), and [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan "Sudan"). The Sahel has a [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate "Hot semi-arid climate").
* South of the Sahel, a belt of [savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savanna "Savanna") (the [West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Sudanian_savanna "West Sudanian savanna") and [East Sudanian savannas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Sudanian_savanna "East Sudanian savanna")) stretch from the Atlantic Ocean to the [Ethiopian Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Highlands "Ethiopian Highlands"). The more humid [Guinean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinean_forest%E2%80%93savanna_mosaic "Guinean forest–savanna mosaic") and [Northern Congolian forest–savanna mosaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Congolian_forest%E2%80%93savanna_mosaic "Northern Congolian forest–savanna mosaic") lie between the savannas and the equatorial forests.
* The [Horn of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horn_of_Africa "Horn of Africa") includes [hot desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_desert_climate "Hot desert climate") along the coast but a [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate "Hot semi-arid climate") can be found much more in the interior, contrasting with savanna and [moist broadleaf forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tropical_and_subtropical_moist_broadleaf_forests_ecoregions "List of tropical and subtropical moist broadleaf forests ecoregions") in the [Ethiopian Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Highlands "Ethiopian Highlands").
* [Tropical Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Africa "Tropical Africa") encompasses [tropical rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest "Tropical rainforest") stretching along the southern coast of West Africa and across most of Central Africa (the [DR Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Democratic Republic of the Congo")) west of the [African Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Great_Lakes "African Great Lakes").
* In East Africa, woodlands, savannas, and grasslands are found in the equatorial zone, including the [Serengeti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serengeti "Serengeti") ecosystem in Tanzania and Kenya.
* Distinctive [Afromontane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afromontane "Afromontane") forests, grasslands, and shrublands are found in the high mountains and mountain ranges of eastern Africa, from the Ethiopian Highlands to South Africa.
* South of the equatorial forests, the [Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Congolian_forest%E2%80%93savanna_mosaic "Western Congolian forest–savanna mosaic") and [Southern Congolian forest–savanna mosaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Congolian_forest%E2%80%93savanna_mosaic "Southern Congolian forest–savanna mosaic") are transition zones between the tropical forests and the [miombo woodland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miombo_woodland "Miombo woodland") belt that spans the continent from [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola "Angola") to [Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique "Mozambique") and [Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania "Tanzania").
* The [Namib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namib "Namib") and [Kalahari Deserts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalahari_Desert "Kalahari Desert") lie in Southern Africa, and are surrounded by semi-deserts including the [Karoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karoo "Karoo") region of South Africa. The [Bushveld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bushveld "Bushveld") grasslands lie to the east of the deserts.
* The [Cape Floristic Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Floristic_Region "Cape Floristic Region") is at Africa's southern tip, and is home to diverse subtropical and temperate forests, woodlands, grasslands, and shrublands.

## Economy

In the mid-2010s, private capital flew to sub-Saharan Africa – primarily from members of [BRICS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRICS "BRICS"), private-sector investment portfolios, and remittances – began to exceed official development assistance.

As of 2011, Africa is one of the fastest developing regions in the world. Six of the world's ten fastest-growing economies over the previous decade were situated below the Sahara, with the remaining four in East and Central Asia. According to the [World Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank "World Bank"), the economic growth rate in the region had risen to 4.7% in 2013. This continued rise was attributed to increasing investment in infrastructure and resources as well as steady expenditure per household.

In 2019, 424 million people in sub-Saharan Africa were reportedly living in severe poverty. In 2022, 460 million people—an increase of 36 million in only three years—were anticipated to be living in extreme poverty as a result of the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic") and the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine "Russian invasion of Ukraine"). Sub-Saharan Africa's [government debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_debt "Government debt") rose from 28% of [gross domestic product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_domestic_product "Gross domestic product") in 2012 to 50% of gross domestic product in 2019. The COVID-19 pandemic caused it to rise to 57% of [gross domestic product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_domestic_product "Gross domestic product") in 2021.

Sub-Saharan Africa was severely harmed when government revenue declined from 22% of GDP in 2011 to 17% in 2021. 15 African nations were (or are) at significant risk of debt, and 7 were (2021) in financial crisis according to the IMF.
The region went on to receive IMF [Special Drawing Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Drawing_Rights "Special Drawing Rights") of $23 billion in 2021 to assist critical public spending.

As of 2009[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sub-Saharan_Africa&action=edit), 50% of Africa was rural with no access to electricity. In 2021, Africa generated 889 TWh of electricity, amounting to 3.13% of the global market share. Many countries were (as of 2009) affected by power shortages.

The percentage of residences with access to electricity in sub-Saharan Africa is the lowest in the world. In some remote regions, fewer than one in every 20 households has electricity.

Because of rising prices in commodities such as coal and oil, thermal sources of energy are proving to be too expensive for power generation. Sub-Saharan Africa has the potential to generate 1,750 TWh of energy, of which only 7% has been explored. The failure to exploit its full energy potential is largely due to significant underinvestment, as at least four times as much (approximately $23 billion a year) and what is currently spent is invested in operating high cost power systems and not on expanding the infrastructure.

African governments are taking advantage of the readily available water resources to broaden their energy mix. Hydro Turbine Markets in sub-Saharan Africa generated revenues of $120.0 million in 2007 and was estimated to reach $425.0 million.[*[when?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")*]
Asian countries, notably China, India, and Japan, are playing an active role in power projects across the African continent. The majority of these power projects are hydro-based because of China's vast experience in the construction of hydro-power projects and part of the Energy & Power Growth Partnership Services programme.

With electrification numbers, sub-Saharan Africa with access to the Sahara and being in the tropical zones has massive potential for solar [photovoltaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaic "Photovoltaic") electrical potential. Six hundred million people could be served with electricity based on its photovoltaic potential.[*[failed verification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability "Wikipedia:Verifiability")*]
In 2003, China promised to train 10,000 technicians from Africa and other developing countries in the use of solar energy technologies over the next five years. Training African technicians to use solar power is part of the China-Africa science and technology cooperation agreement signed by Chinese science minister [Xu Guanhua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xu_Guanhua "Xu Guanhua") and African counterparts during premier Wen Jiabao's visit to Ethiopia in December 2003.

The [New Partnership for Africa's Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Partnership_for_Africa%27s_Development "New Partnership for Africa's Development") (NEPAD) is developing an integrated, continent-wide energy strategy. This has been funded by, amongst others, the [African Development Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Development_Bank "African Development Bank") (AfDB) and the EU-Africa Infrastructure Trust Fund. These projects must be sustainable, involve a cross-border dimension and/or have a regional impact, involve public and private capital, contribute to poverty alleviation and economic development, and involve at least one country in sub-Saharan Africa.

Renewable Energy Performance Platform was established by the [European Investment Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Investment_Bank "European Investment Bank") and the [United Nations Environment Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Environment_Programme "United Nations Environment Programme") with a five-year goal of improving energy access for at least two million people in sub-Saharan Africa. It has so far invested around $45 million to renewable energy projects in 13 countries in sub-Saharan Africa. [Solar power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_power "Solar power") and [hydropower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydropower "Hydropower") are among the energy methods used in the projects.

### Oil and minerals

The region is a major exporter to the world of [gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold "Gold"), [uranium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium "Uranium"), [chromium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromium "Chromium"), [vanadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanadium "Vanadium"), [antimony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony "Antimony"), [coltan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coltan "Coltan"), [bauxite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite "Bauxite"), [iron ore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_ore "Iron ore"), [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper "Copper"), and [manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese "Manganese"). South Africa, along with [Gabon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabon "Gabon") and [Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana "Ghana"), collectively supplies over 60% of global [manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese "Manganese"), and is also a major exporter of [chromium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromium "Chromium"). A 2001 estimate is that 42% of the world's reserves of chromium may be found in South Africa. South Africa is the largest producer of [platinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum "Platinum"), with 80% of the total world's annual mine production and 88% of the world's platinum reserve. Sub-Saharan Africa produces 33% of the world's bauxite, with Guinea as the major supplier. Zambia is a major producer of copper. The Democratic Republic of the Congo is a major source of coltan. Production from DR Congo is very small, but the country has 80% of the proven reserves in Africa, which are 80% of those worldwide. Sub-Saharan Africa is a major producer of gold, producing up to 30% of global production. Major suppliers are South Africa, Ghana, Zimbabwe, Tanzania, Guinea, and Mali. South Africa had been first in the world in terms of gold production since 1905, but in 2007 it moved to second place, according to GFMS, the precious metals consultancy. Uranium is major commodity from the region. Significant suppliers are Niger, Namibia, and South Africa. Namibia was the number one supplier from sub-Saharan Africa in 2008. The region produces 49% of the world's [diamonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamonds "Diamonds").

Sub-Saharan Africa has been the focus of an intense race for oil by the West, China, India, and other emerging economies, even though it holds only 10% of proven oil reserves, less than the Middle East. This race has been referred to as the second [Scramble for Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scramble_for_Africa "Scramble for Africa"). All reasons for this global scramble come from the reserves' economic benefits. Transportation cost is low and no pipelines have to be laid as in Central Asia. Almost all reserves are offshore, so political turmoil within the host country will not directly interfere with operations. Sub-Saharan oil is viscous, with a very low sulfur content. This quickens the refining process and effectively reduces costs. New sources of oil are being located in sub-Saharan Africa more frequently than anywhere else. Of all new sources of oil, 1⁄3 are in sub-Saharan Africa.

Sub-Saharan Africa is a key player in the global [minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral "Mineral") market, producing over 70% of the world's [cobalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobalt "Cobalt") and hosting about 50% of its reserves in the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Democratic Republic of the Congo") (DRC). The region also harbors significant [lithium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium "Lithium") deposits in [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe "Zimbabwe"), DR Congo, and [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali "Mali").

### Agriculture

Sub-Saharan Africa has more variety of grains than anywhere in the world. Between 13,000 and 11,000 BCE wild grains began to be collected as a source of food in the cataract region of the Nile, south of Egypt. The collecting of wild grains as source of food spread to Syria, parts of Turkey, and Iran by the eleventh millennium BCE. By the tenth and ninth millennia southwest Asians domesticated their wild grains, wheat, and barley after the notion of collecting wild grains spread from the Nile.

Numerous crops have been domesticated in the region and spread to other parts of the world. These crops included [sorghum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorghum "Sorghum"), [castor beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castor_beans "Castor beans"), [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee "Coffee"), [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton "Cotton"), [okra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okra "Okra"), [black-eyed peas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-eyed_peas "Black-eyed peas"), [watermelon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watermelon "Watermelon"), [gourd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gourd "Gourd"), and pearl [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet "Millet"). Other domesticated crops included [teff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teff "Teff"), [enset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enset "Enset"), [African rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_rice "African rice"), [yams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yam_(vegetable) "Yam (vegetable)"), [kola nuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kola_nut "Kola nut"), [oil palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elaeis_guineensis "Elaeis guineensis"), and [raffia palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raffia_palm "Raffia palm").

Domesticated animals include the [guinea fowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea_fowl "Guinea fowl") and the [donkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donkey "Donkey").

Agriculture represents 20% to 30% of GDP and 50% of exports. In some cases, 60% to 90% of the labor force are employed in agriculture. Most agricultural activity is subsistence farming. This has made agricultural activity [vulnerable to climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_vulnerability "Climate change vulnerability") and global warming. As of right now Sub-Saharan Africa has degraded land covering one million square kilometres. Biotechnology has been advocated to create high yield, pest and environmentally resistant crops in the hands of small farmers. The [Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_and_Melinda_Gates_Foundation "Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation") is a strong advocate and donor to this cause. Biotechnology and GM crops have met resistance both by natives and environmental groups.

Cash crops include cotton, coffee, tea, cocoa, sugar, and tobacco.

The [OECD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OECD "OECD") says Africa has the potential to become an agricultural superbloc if it can unlock the wealth of the savannahs by allowing farmers to use their land as collateral for credit. There is such international interest in sub-Saharan agriculture, that the World Bank increased its financing of African agricultural programs to $1.3 billion in the 2011 fiscal year. Recently, there has been a trend to purchase large tracts of land in sub-Sahara for agricultural use by developing countries.
Early in 2009, [George Soros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Soros "George Soros") highlighted a new farmland buying frenzy caused by growing population, scarce water supplies and climate change. Chinese interests bought up large swathes of Senegal to supply it with sesame. Aggressive moves by China, South Korea, and Gulf states to buy vast tracts of agricultural land in sub-Saharan Africa could soon be limited by a new global international protocol.

### Low productivity of subsistance farmers

Compared to South America and Asia, Sub-Saharan Africa has experienced persistently low crop yield productivity for more than 50 years. As mentioned above, one factor influencing low productivity might be low access to credit studies. Many studies have focused on factors such as access to credit, and [time preferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_preference "Time preference") as key explanations for low productivity of small farmers in Sub-Saharan Africa. However, recent studies have explored an alternative mechanism: quality of agricultural inputs. Indeed, usage of high-quality inputs, such as seeds and fertiliser, would substantially increase productivity of [subsistance farmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsistence_agriculture "Subsistence agriculture"). However, there is a small take up rate of this product by farmers, despite acknowledging their benefits.

#### Structure of the agricultural market

First, it is important to explain the structure of the agricultural input market in countries of the Sub-Saharan Africa. The majority of seeds and fertilizers are traded informally, often of uncertain quality, either exchanged between farmers or reused from previous harvests, in the case of seeds. Only a small number of farmers purchase inputs from the formal market, which is generally considered to offer higher-quality products. We can illustrate this with the example of the Nigerian stem market. Indeed, only 1% of the stems sold in the Nigerian market are certified, while the remaining 99% are traded informally—either as recycled stems from local varieties or as uncertified stems derived from previously improved varieties.

#### Uncertainty about quality of agricultural inputs

Recent studies explain that farmers mistrust the quality of fertilisers and seeds sold in the formal market. The study realized in Uganda infers quality across fertilizer and hybrid seeds varies significantly due to adulteration: sellers tend to mix the fertilizer with poor soil. However, one study realized in Tanzania finds no adulteration in agricultural inputs, but do find that visual appearance of input sold in the formal market is degraded. Whether one study or the other is correct, both papers highlight uncertainty about fertilizer's quality, thus generating mistrust among farmers and consequently generates a low take-up rate.

Another factor highlighting uncertainty about quality is the presence of relatively homogenous pricing among different quality of the same inputs. While in most markets price is a good indicator of quality. the higher the quality, the higher the price- , in this case, uniform pricing prevents farmers from using price as a signal of input quality. Consequently, the inability of farmers to infer the quality, either through physical appearance or price, creates [asymmetric information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_asymmetry "Information asymmetry") in the market, leading an inefficient market.

Some papers have tried to understand why sellers do not simply charge higher prices when they offer high-quality seeds and fertilizers, using a [Bayesian learning model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayesian_inference "Bayesian inference"). It is challenging for a company to sell high-quality inputs at a [premium price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premium_pricing "Premium pricing") and build a reputation in the long term, meaning that farmers will trust the seller regarding the quality of its products. Indeed, as farmers are highly suspicious -due to fear of being scammed- their [willingness to pay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willingness_to_pay "Willingness to pay") for high-quality products is very low. So even if the firm sells high-quality products, it will take too long for farmers to start trusting the firm, and for the seller to become profitable.

#### Policy recommendation

The literature advises to actually improve farmers perception of true quality itself, and not to improve the quality of the products which already meet the standards. This can be done by providing more information about inputs quality, how they should look like, where they come from, making firms more reliable. That would allow more companies to enter the market and sell formally higher inputs, instead of farmers using their own seed or using seed of unknown quality bought in informal markets.

### Science and technology

### Tourism

The development of [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism "Tourism") in this region has been identified as having the ability to create jobs and improve the economy. South Africa, Namibia, Mauritius, Botswana, Ghana, Cape Verde, Tanzania and Kenya have been identified as having well developed tourism industries. [Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town "Cape Town") and the surrounding area is very popular with tourists.

### Infrastructure

According to researchers at the Overseas Development Institute, the lack of infrastructure in many [developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_countries "Developing countries") represents one of the most significant limitations to [economic growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_growth "Economic growth") and achievement of the [Millennium Development Goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Development_Goals "Millennium Development Goals") (MDGs). Infrastructure investments and maintenance can be very expensive, especially in such as areas as landlocked, rural and sparsely populated countries in Africa.

Infrastructure investments contributed to Africa's growth, and increased investment is necessary to maintain growth and tackle poverty. The returns to investment in infrastructure are very significant, with on average 30–40% returns for [telecommunications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications "Telecommunications") (ICT) investments, over 40% for [electricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity "Electricity") generation and 80% for roads.

In Africa, it is argued that in order to meet the MDGs, infrastructure investments would need to reach about 15% of GDP (around $93 billion a year). Currently, the source of financing varies significantly across sectors. Some sectors are dominated by state spending, others by overseas [development aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_aid "Development aid") (ODA) and yet others by private investors. In sub-Saharan Africa, the state spends around $9.4 billion out of a total of $24.9 billion. In [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation "Irrigation"), SSA states represent almost all spending; in transport and energy a majority of investment is state spending; in [ICT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_and_communication_technologies "Information and communication technologies") and [water supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_supply "Water supply") and [sanitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanitation "Sanitation"), the private sector represents the majority of capital expenditure. Overall, aid, the private sector and non-OECD financiers between them exceed state spending. The private sector spending alone equals state capital expenditure, though the majority is focused on ICT infrastructure investments. External financing increased from $7 billion (2002) to $27 billion (2009). China, in particular, has emerged as an important investor.

#### Transport

Less than 40% of rural Africans live within two kilometers of an all-season road, the lowest level of rural accessibility in the developing world. Spending on roads averages just below 2% of GDP with varying degree among countries. This compares with 1% of GDP that is typical in industrialised countries, and 2–3% of GDP found in fast-growing emerging economies. Although the level of expenditure is high relative to the size of Africa's economies, it remains small in absolute terms, with low-income countries spending an average of about US$7 per capita per year.

## Demographics

### Major cities

Sub-Saharan Africa has several large cities. [Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos "Lagos") is a city in the [Nigerian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria "Nigeria") [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Nigeria "States of Nigeria") of [Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_State "Lagos State"). The city, with its adjoining [conurbation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conurbation "Conurbation"), is [the most populous in Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nigerian_cities_by_population "List of Nigerian cities by population"), and the second-most populous in [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa "Africa") after [Cairo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo "Cairo"), Egypt. It is one of the fastest-growing cities in the world, and also one of the most populous [urban agglomerations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_agglomeration "Urban agglomeration"). Lagos is a major [financial centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_centre "Financial centre") in Africa; this [megacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megacity "Megacity") has the highest GDP, and also houses [Apapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apapa "Apapa"), one of the largest and busiest ports on the continent.

[Dar es Salaam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar_es_Salaam "Dar es Salaam") is the former capital of, as well as the most populous city in, [Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania "Tanzania"); it is a regionally important economic centre. It is located on the [Swahili coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_coast "Swahili coast").

[Johannesburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannesburg "Johannesburg") is the largest city in [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa "South Africa"). It is the [provincial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province "Province") capital and largest city in [Gauteng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauteng "Gauteng"), which is the wealthiest province in South Africa. While Johannesburg is not one of [South Africa's three capital cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa#Politics_and_government "South Africa"), it is the seat of the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_South_Africa "Constitutional Court of South Africa"). The city is located in the mineral-rich [Witwatersrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witwatersrand "Witwatersrand") range of hills, and is the centre of a large-scale gold and diamond trade.

[Nairobi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nairobi "Nairobi") is the capital and the largest city of [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya "Kenya"). The name comes from the [Maasai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maasai_language "Maasai language") phrase *Enkare Nyrobi*, which translates to "cool water", a reference to the [Nairobi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nairobi_River "Nairobi River") which flows through the city. The city is popularly referred to as the Green City in the Sun.

Other major cities in sub-Saharan Africa include [Abidjan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abidjan "Abidjan"), [Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town "Cape Town"), [Kinshasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinshasa "Kinshasa"), [Luanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luanda "Luanda"), [Mogadishu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogadishu "Mogadishu") and [Addis Ababa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addis_Ababa "Addis Ababa").

### Population

According to the 2022 revision of the [World Population Prospects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Population_Prospects "World Population Prospects"), the population of sub-Saharan Africa was 1.1 billion in 2019. The current growth rate is 2.3%. The UN predicts for the region a population between 2 and 2.5 billion by 2050 with a population density of 80 per km2 compared to 170 for Western Europe, 140 for Asia and 30 for the Americas.

Sub-Saharan African countries top the [list of countries and territories by fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_territories_by_fertility_rate "List of countries and territories by fertility rate") with 40 of the highest 50, all with [TFR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate "Total fertility rate") greater than 4 in 2008. All are above the world average except [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa "South Africa") and [Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychelles "Seychelles"). More than 40% of the population in sub-Saharan countries is [younger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Nigeria "Demographics of Nigeria") than 15 years old, as well as in [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan "Sudan"), with the exception of South Africa.

### Languages and ethnic groups

Sub-Saharan Africa contains over 1,500 languages.

#### Afroasiatic

With the exception of the extinct [Sumerian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumerian_language "Sumerian language") (a [language isolate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_isolate "Language isolate")) of [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia "Mesopotamia"), Afroasiatic has the oldest documented history of any language family in the world. Egyptian was recorded as early as 3200 BCE. The [Semitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitic_languages "Semitic languages") branch was recorded as early as 2900 BCE in the form of the [Akkadian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_language "Akkadian language") of Mesopotamia ([Assyria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyria "Assyria") and [Babylonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonia "Babylonia")) and circa 2500 BCE in the form of the [Eblaite language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eblaite_language "Eblaite language") of northeastern [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria "Syria").

The distribution of the [Afroasiatic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afroasiatic_languages "Afroasiatic languages") within Africa is principally concentrated in North Africa and the Horn of Africa. Languages belonging to the family's [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_languages "Berber languages") branch are mainly spoken in the north, with its speech area extending into the Sahel (northern Mauritania, northern Mali, northern Niger). The [Cushitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cushitic_languages "Cushitic languages") branch of Afroasiatic is centered in the Horn, and is also spoken in the Nile Valley and parts of the African Great Lakes region. Additionally, the Semitic branch of the family, in the form of [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic "Arabic"), is widely spoken in the parts of Africa that are within the Arab world. [South Semitic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Semitic_languages "South Semitic languages") are also spoken in parts of the Horn of Africa (Ethiopia, Eritrea). The [Chadic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chadic_languages "Chadic languages") branch is distributed in Central and West Africa. [Hausa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausa_language "Hausa language"), its most widely spoken language, serves as a [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca "Lingua franca") in West Africa (Niger, Ghana, Togo, Benin, Cameroon, and Chad).

#### Khoisan

Several languages included under the term [Khoi-San](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoi-San "Khoi-San") are indigenous to [Southern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Africa "Southern Africa") and [Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania "Tanzania"), although the [Khoi languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoi_languages "Khoi languages") appear to have moved to their current locations not long before the [Bantu expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_expansion "Bantu expansion"). In Southern Africa, their speakers are the [Khoikhoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoikhoi "Khoikhoi") and [San](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_people "San people") (Bushmen), in Southeast Africa, the [Sandawe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandawe_people "Sandawe people") and [Hadza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadza_people "Hadza people").

#### Niger–Congo

The [Niger–Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niger%E2%80%93Congo_languages "Niger–Congo languages") family is the largest in the world in terms of the number of languages (1,436) it contains. The vast majority of languages of this family are [tonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonal_language "Tonal language"), such as [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_language "Yoruba language") and [Igbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igbo_language "Igbo language"). However, others such as [Fulani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulani_language "Fulani language"), [Wolof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolof_language "Wolof language") and [Kiswahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiswahili "Kiswahili") are not. A major branch of the Niger–Congo languages is [Bantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_languages "Bantu languages"), which covers a greater geographic area than the rest of the family. Bantu speakers represent the majority of inhabitants in southern, central and southeastern Africa, though [San](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_people "San people"), [Pygmy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pygmy "Pygmy"), and [Nilotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilotic "Nilotic") groups, respectively, can also be found in those regions. Bantu-speakers can also be found in parts of [Central Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Africa "Central Africa") such as [Gabon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabon "Gabon"), [Equatorial Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equatorial_Guinea "Equatorial Guinea"), and southern [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon "Cameroon"). [Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_language "Swahili language"), a Bantu language with many [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language "Arabic language"), [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farsi "Farsi"), and other Middle Eastern and South Asian [loan words](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loan_word "Loan word"), developed as a *[lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca "Lingua franca")* for trade between the different peoples in southeastern Africa. In the [Kalahari Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalahari_Desert "Kalahari Desert") of Southern Africa, the distinct people known as Bushmen (also "San", closely related to, but distinct from "[Hottentots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoikhoi "Khoikhoi")") have long been present. The San evince unique physical traits, and are the indigenous people of southern Africa. Pygmies are the pre-Bantu indigenous peoples of Central Africa.

#### Nilo-Saharan

The [Nilo-Saharan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilo-Saharan_languages "Nilo-Saharan languages") are concentrated in the upper parts of the [Chari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chari_River "Chari River") and [Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile "Nile") rivers of Central Africa and Southeast Africa. They are principally spoken by [Nilotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilotic "Nilotic") peoples and are also spoken in Sudan among the [Fur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_people "Fur people"), [Masalit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masalit_people "Masalit people"), [Nubian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubian_people "Nubian people"), and [Zaghawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaghawa_people "Zaghawa people") peoples and in West and Central Africa among the [Songhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songhai_people "Songhai people"), [Zarma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zarma_people "Zarma people"), and [Kanuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanuri_people "Kanuri people"). The [Old Nubian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Nubian_language "Old Nubian language") is also a member of this family.

### Religion

The principal religions of Sub-Saharan Africa are [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity"), [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam") and [traditional African religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_African_religions "Traditional African religions"), with Christianity being the largest religion, and [religious syncretism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_syncretism "Religious syncretism") being also common. African countries below the Sahara are largely Christian, while those above the Sahara, in [North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Africa "North Africa"), are predominantly Islamic. There are also Muslim majorities in parts of the Horn of Africa ([Djibouti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djibouti "Djibouti") and [Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somalia "Somalia")) and in the Sahel and Sudan regions ([the Gambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gambia "The Gambia"), [Sierra Leone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Leone "Sierra Leone"), [Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea "Guinea"), [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali "Mali"), [Niger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niger "Niger"), [Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegal "Senegal"), [Burkina Faso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burkina_Faso "Burkina Faso") and [Chad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad "Chad")), as well as significant Muslim communities in [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia "Ethiopia") and [Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrea "Eritrea"), and on the Swahili Coast ([Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania "Tanzania"), [Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique "Mozambique") and [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya "Kenya")). [West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Africa "West Africa") is the only subregion of sub-Saharan Africa which has a Muslim majority population, and [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria "Nigeria") has the largest Muslim population in sub-Saharan Africa.

[Mauritius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritius "Mauritius") is the only country in [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa "Africa") to have a [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu "Hindu") majority. In 2012, sub-Saharan Africa constituted in absolute terms the [world's third largest Christian population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_by_country "Christianity by country"), after Europe and [Latin America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_America "Latin America") respectively. In 2012, sub-Saharan Africa also constituted in absolute terms the [world's third largest Muslim population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_by_country "Islam by country"), after [Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia "Asia") and the [Middle East and North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_and_North_Africa "Middle East and North Africa") respectively.

[Traditional African religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_African_religions "Traditional African religions") are also commonly practiced across sub-Saharan Africa, with these religions being especially common in [South Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Sudan "South Sudan"), [Guinea Bissau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea_Bissau "Guinea Bissau"), [Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique "Mozambique"), and [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon "Cameroon"). Traditional African religions can be broken down into linguistic cultural groups, with common themes. Among [Niger–Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niger%E2%80%93Congo_languages "Niger–Congo languages")-speakers is a belief in a creator god or higher deity, along with ancestor spirits, territorial spirits, evil caused by human ill will and neglecting ancestor spirits, and priests of territorial spirits. New world religions such as [Santería](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santer%C3%ADa "Santería"), [Vodun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_African_Vodun "West African Vodun"), and [Candomblé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candombl%C3%A9 "Candomblé"), would be derived from this world. Among [Nilo-Saharan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilo-Saharan_languages "Nilo-Saharan languages") speakers is the belief in Divinity; evil is caused by divine judgement and retribution; prophets as middlemen between Divinity and man. Among [Afro-Asiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Asiatic_languages "Afro-Asiatic languages")-speakers is [henotheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henotheism "Henotheism"), the belief in one's own gods but accepting the existence of other gods; evil here is caused by malevolent spirits. The Semitic [Abrahamic religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrahamic_religion "Abrahamic religion") of [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism "Judaism") is comparable to the latter world view. [San religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_religion "San religion") is non-theistic but a belief in a Spirit or Power of existence which can be tapped in a trance-dance; trance-healers.

Generally, traditional African religions are united by an ancient complex [animism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animism "Animism") and [ancestor worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestor_worship "Ancestor worship").

Traditional religions in sub-Saharan Africa often display complex ontology, cosmology and metaphysics. Mythologies, for example, demonstrated the difficulty fathers of creation had in bringing about order from chaos. Order is what is right and natural and any deviation is chaos. [Cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology "Cosmology") and [ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology "Ontology") is also neither simple or linear. It defines duality, the material and immaterial, male and female, heaven and earth. Common principles of being and becoming are widespread: Among the Dogon, the principle of *Amma* (being) and *Nummo* (becoming), and among the Bambara, *Pemba* (being) and *Faro* (becoming).

Sub-Saharan traditional divination systems display great sophistication. For example, the bamana sand divination uses well established symbolic codes that can be reproduced using four bits or marks. ### Education

Forty per cent of African scientists live in [OECD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OECD "OECD") countries, predominantly in Europe, the United States and Canada. This has been described as an African [brain drain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_drain "Brain drain"). Despite this, sub-Saharan African universities tripled between 1991 and 2005, expanding at an annual rate of 8.7%, which is one of the highest regional growth rates in the world. In the last 10 to 15 years interest in pursuing university-level degrees abroad has increased.

According to the CIA, low global literacy rates are concentrated in sub-Saharan Africa, [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia "West Asia") and [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia"). However, literacy rates in sub-Saharan Africa vary significantly between countries. The highest registered literacy rate in the region is in [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe "Zimbabwe") (90.7%; 2003 est.), while the lowest literacy rate is in [South Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Sudan "South Sudan") (27%).

Research on [human capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_capital "Human capital") formation was able to determine, that the [numeracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numeracy "Numeracy") levels of sub-Saharan Africa and Africa, in general, were higher than numeracy levels in South Asia. In the 1940s more than 75% of the population of sub-Saharan Africa was numerate. The numeracy of the West African countries, Benin and Ghana, was even higher with more than 80% of the population being numerate. In contrast, numeracy in South Asia was only around 50%.

Higher diversity in Sub-Saharan African countries has been found to lead to a poorer economy. Researchers have argued that this is because of ethnic favouritism in their politics. Sub-Saharan leaders are more likely to provide better resources to their coethnic groups when in power. A study found that, on average, children of the favoured ethnic group are 2.25% more likely to attend primary school and 1.80% more likely to complete primary school. A 1% increase in GDP is associated with a 1.5% increase in the ethnic favouritism effect on primary school attendance.

Sub-Saharan African countries spent an average of 0.3% of their GDP on science and technology in 2007. This represents an increase from US$1.8 billion in 2002 to US$2.8 billion in 2007, a 50% increase in spending.

#### Major progress in access to education

At the World Conference held in [Jomtien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jomtien_Beach "Jomtien Beach"), Thailand in 1990, delegates from 155 countries and representatives of some 150 organisations gathered with the goal to promote [universal primary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Primary_Education "Universal Primary Education") and the radical reduction of illiteracy before the end of the decade. The [World Education Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Education_Forum "World Education Forum"), held ten years later in [Dakar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakar "Dakar"), Senegal, provided the opportunity to reiterate and reinforce these goals. This initiative contributed to having education made a priority of the [Millennium Development Goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Development_Goals "Millennium Development Goals") in 2000, with the aim of achieving universal schooling (MDG2) and eliminating gender disparities, especially in primary and secondary education (MDG3). Since the World Education Forum in Dakar, considerable efforts have been made to respond to these demographic challenges in terms of education. The amount of funds raised has been decisive. Between 1999 and 2010, public spending on education as a percentage of [gross national product (GNP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_national_product "Gross national product") increased by 5% per year in sub-Saharan Africa, with major variations between countries, with percentages varying from 1.8% in Cameroon to over 6% in Burundi. As of 2015, governments in sub-Saharan Africa spend on average 18% of their total budget on education, against 15% in the rest of the world.

In the years immediately after the Dakar Forum, the efforts made by the African States towards achieving [EFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_For_All "Education For All") produced multiple results in sub-Saharan Africa. The greatest advance was in access to primary education, which governments had made their absolute priority. The number of children in a primary school in sub-Saharan Africa thus rose from 82 million in 1999 to 136.4 million in 2011. In Niger, for example, the number of children entering school increased by more than three-and-a-half times between 1999 and 2011. In Ethiopia, over the same period, over 8.5 million more children were admitted to primary school. The net rate of first-year access in sub-Saharan Africa has thus risen by 19 points in 12 years, from 58% in 1999 to 77% in 2011. Despite the considerable efforts, the latest available data from the [UNESCO Institute for Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_Institute_for_Statistics "UNESCO Institute for Statistics") estimates that, for 2012, there were still 57.8 million children who were not in school. Of these, 29.6 million were in sub-Saharan Africa alone, a figure which has not changed for several years. Many sub-Saharan countries have notably included the first year of secondary school in basic education. In Rwanda, the first year of secondary school was attached to primary education in 2009, which significantly increased the number of pupils enrolled at this level of education. In 2012, the primary completion rate (PCR) – which measures the proportion of children reaching the final year of primary school – was 70%, meaning that more than three out of ten children entering primary school do not reach the final primary year.

Literacy rates have gone up in sub-Saharan Africa, and internet access has improved considerably. At least 39 countries in sub-Saharan Africa have some large-scale school feeding programs, which can improve access to education. In aggregate, 16% of school-age children (and 25% of primary school-age children) in the region benefit from school meal programs, and about 82% of the funding for these programs is provided by governments. Nonetheless, a lot must yet happen for this region to catch up. The statistics show that the literacy rate for sub-Saharan Africa was 65% in 2017. In other words, one-third of the people aged 15 and above were unable to read and write. The comparative figure for 1984 was an illiteracy rate of 49%. In 2017, only about 22% of Africans were internet users at all, according to the International Telecommunication Union (ITU).

### Health

Health challenges in Sub-Saharan Africa include [HIV/AIDS in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS_in_Africa "HIV/AIDS in Africa"), [malaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaria "Malaria"), [neglected tropical diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neglected_tropical_diseases "Neglected tropical diseases"), [tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculosis "Tuberculosis"), [onchocerciasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onchocerciasis "Onchocerciasis"), [maternal mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternal_mortality "Maternal mortality") and [infant mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality "Infant mortality").

In 1987, the [Bamako Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamako_Initiative "Bamako Initiative") conference organized by the [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization "World Health Organization") was held in [Bamako](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamako "Bamako"), the capital of [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali "Mali"), and helped reshape the health policy of sub-Saharan Africa. The new strategy dramatically increased accessibility through community-based [healthcare reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_reform "Healthcare reform"), resulting in more efficient and equitable provision of services.[*[self-published source?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Self-published_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")*] A comprehensive approach strategy was extended to all areas of health care, with subsequent improvement in the health care indicators and improvement in health care efficiency and cost.

In 2011, sub-Saharan Africa was home to 69% of all people living with [HIV/AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS "HIV/AIDS") worldwide. In response, a number of initiatives have been launched to educate the public on HIV/AIDS. Among these are combination prevention programmes, considered to be the most effective initiative, the [abstinence, be faithful, use a condom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstinence,_be_faithful,_use_a_condom "Abstinence, be faithful, use a condom") campaign, and the [Desmond Tutu HIV Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desmond_Tutu_HIV_Foundation "Desmond Tutu HIV Foundation")'s outreach programs. According to a 2013 special report issued by the [Joint United Nations Programme on HIV/AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_United_Nations_Programme_on_HIV/AIDS "Joint United Nations Programme on HIV/AIDS") (UNAIDS), the number of HIV positive people in Africa receiving anti-retro viral treatment in 2012 was over seven times the number receiving treatment in 2005, with an almost 1 million added in the last year alone. The number of AIDS-related deaths in sub-Saharan Africa in 2011 was 33 per cent less than the number in 2005. The number of new HIV infections in sub-Saharan Africa in 2011 was 25 per cent less than the number in 2001.

[Life expectancy at birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy_at_birth "Life expectancy at birth") in sub-Saharan Africa increased from 40 years in 1960 to 61 years in 2017.

[Malaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaria "Malaria") is an endemic illness in sub-Saharan Africa, where the majority of malaria cases and deaths worldwide occur. Routine immunisation has been introduced in order to prevent [measles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measles "Measles"). [Onchocerciasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onchocerciasis "Onchocerciasis") ("river blindness"), a common cause of [blindness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blindness "Blindness"), is also endemic to parts of the region. More than 99% of people affected by the illness worldwide live in 31 countries therein. In response, the African Programme for Onchocerciasis Control (APOC) was launched in 1995 with the aim of controlling the disease. [Maternal mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternal_mortality "Maternal mortality") is another challenge, with more than half of maternal deaths in the world occurring in sub-Saharan Africa. However, there has generally been progress here as well, as a number of countries in the region have halved their levels of maternal mortality since 1990. Additionally, the [African Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union "African Union") in July 2003 ratified the [Maputo Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maputo_Protocol "Maputo Protocol"), which pledges to prohibit [female genital mutilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Female_genital_mutilation "Female genital mutilation") (FGM). Somalia, Guinea, Djibouti, Sierra Leone and Mali have the highest prevalence of FGM in the world. [Infibulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infibulation "Infibulation"), the most extreme form of FGM, is concentrated primarily in [Northeast Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Africa "Northeast Africa").

National health systems vary between countries. In [Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana "Ghana"), most health care is provided by the government and largely administered by the [Ministry of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Health,_Ghana "Ministry of Health, Ghana") and [Ghana Health Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Health_Services "Ghana Health Services"). The healthcare system has five levels of providers: health posts which are first-level primary care for rural areas, health centers and clinics, district hospitals, regional hospitals, and tertiary hospitals. These programs are funded by the government of Ghana, financial credits, Internally Generated Fund (IGF), and Donors-pooled Health Fund.
[Ebola virus disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebola_virus_disease "Ebola virus disease"), which was first identified in 1976, occasionally occurs in outbreaks in tropical regions of Sub-Saharan Africa. The 2013–2016 [Western African Ebola virus epidemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_African_Ebola_virus_epidemic "Western African Ebola virus epidemic") originated in Guinea, later spreading to neighbouring Liberia and Sierra Leone.

## Culture

Sub-Saharan Africa is diverse, with many communities, villages, and cities, each with their own beliefs and traditions. Traditional African Societies are communal, they believe that the needs of the many far outweigh an individual's needs and achievements. Essentially, an individual's keep must be shared with other extended family members. Extended families are made up of various individuals and families who have shared responsibilities within the community. This extended family is one of the core aspects of every African community. "An African will refer to an older person as auntie or uncle. Siblings of parents will be called father or mother rather than uncle and aunt. Cousins will be called brother or sister". This system can be very difficult for outsiders to understand; however, it is no less important. "Also reflecting their communal ethic, Africans are reluctant to stand out in a crowd or to appear different from their neighbours or colleagues, a result of social pressure to avoid offense to group standards and traditions." Women also have a very important role in African culture because they take care of the house and children. Traditionally, in many cultures "men do the heavy work of clearing and ploughing the land, women sow the seeds, tend the fields, harvest the crops, haul the water, and bear the major burden for growing the family's food". Despite their work in the fields, women are expected to be subservient to men in some African cultures. "When young women migrate to cities, this imbalance between the sexes, as well as financial need, often causes young women of lower economic status, who lack education and job training, to have sexual relationships with older men who are established in their work or profession and can afford to support a girlfriend or two".

### Art

The oldest [abstract art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_art "Abstract art") in the world is a shell necklace, dated to 82,000 years, in the Cave of Pigeons in [Taforalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taforalt "Taforalt"), eastern Morocco. The second-oldest abstract form of art, and the oldest rock art, is found in the [Blombos Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blombos_Cave "Blombos Cave") at the Cape in South Africa, dated 77,000 years. Sub-Saharan Africa has some of the oldest and most varied style of rock art in the world.

Although sub-Saharan African art is very diverse, there are some common themes. One is the use of the human figure. Second, there is a preference for [sculpture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sculpture "Sculpture"). Sub-Saharan African art is meant to be experienced in three dimensions, not two. A house is meant to be experienced from all angles. Third, art is meant to be performed. Sub-Saharan Africans have a specific name for masks. The name incorporates the sculpture, the dance, and the spirit that incorporates the mask. The name denotes all three elements. Fourth, art that serves a practical function. The artist and craftsman are not separate. A sculpture shaped like a hand can be used as a stool. Fifth, the use of [fractals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractals "Fractals") or non-linear scaling. The shape of the whole is the shape of the parts at different scales. Before the discovery of fractal geometry, Leopold Sedar Senghor, Senegal's first president, referred to this as "dynamic symmetry". [William Fagg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Buller_Fagg "William Buller Fagg"), a British art historian, has compared it to the logarithmic mapping of natural growth by biologist D'Arcy Thompson. Lastly, sub-Saharan African art is visually abstract, instead of naturalistic. Sub-Saharan African art represents spiritual notions, social norms, ideas, values, etc. An artist might exaggerate the head of a sculpture in relation to the body not because he does not know anatomy but because he wants to illustrate that the head is the seat of knowledge and wisdom.

The visual abstraction of African art was very influential in the works of [modernist artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernist_art "Modernist art") like [Pablo Picasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Picasso "Pablo Picasso"), [Henri Matisse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Matisse "Henri Matisse"), and [Jacques Lipchitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Lipchitz "Jacques Lipchitz").

### Music

Traditional sub-Saharan African music is as diverse as the region's various populations. The common perception of sub-Saharan African music is that it is rhythmic music centered around the drums. This is partially true. A large part of sub-Saharan music, mainly among speakers of [Niger–Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niger%E2%80%93Congo "Niger–Congo") and [Nilo-Saharan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilo-Saharan "Nilo-Saharan") languages, is rhythmic and centered around the drum. Sub-Saharan music is polyrhythmic, usually consisting of multiple rhythms in one composition. Dance involves moving multiple body parts. These aspects of sub-Saharan music has been transferred to the new world by enslaved sub-Saharan Africans and can be seen in its influence on music forms as [samba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samba "Samba"), [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz"), [rhythm and blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhythm_and_blues "Rhythm and blues"), [rock and roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_roll "Rock and roll"), [salsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salsa_music "Salsa music"), [reggae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggae "Reggae") and [rap music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rap_music "Rap music").

Some forms of sub-Saharan African music use strings, horns, and very little poly-rhythms. Music from the eastern Sahel and along the Nile, among the [Nilo-Saharan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilo-Saharan "Nilo-Saharan"), made extensive use of strings and horns in ancient times. Among the [Afro-Asiatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Asiatic "Afro-Asiatic") of [Northeast Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horn_of_Africa "Horn of Africa"), there is extensive use of [string instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Ethiopia "Music of Ethiopia") and the [pentatonic scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentatonic_scale "Pentatonic scale"). Dancing involves swaying body movements and footwork. Among the [San](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_people "San people") is extensive use of string instruments with emphasis on footwork.

Modern sub-Saharan African music has been influenced by music from the New World (Jazz, Salsa, Rhythm and Blues etc.) vice versa being influenced by enslaved sub-Saharan Africans. Popular styles are [Mbalax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbalax "Mbalax") in [Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegal "Senegal") and [Gambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambia "Gambia"), [Highlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highlife "Highlife") in [Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana "Ghana"), [Zoblazo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoblazo "Zoblazo") in [Ivory Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory_Coast "Ivory Coast"), [Makossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makossa "Makossa") in [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon "Cameroon"), [Soukous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soukous "Soukous") in the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Democratic Republic of the Congo"), [Kizomba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kizomba "Kizomba") in [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola "Angola"), and [Mbaqanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbaqanga "Mbaqanga") in [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa "South Africa"). New World styles like Salsa, R&B/Rap, Reggae, and Zouk also have widespread popularity.

### Clothing

Like most of the world, sub-Saharan Africans have adopted Western-style clothing. In some countries like Zambia, used Western clothing has flooded markets, causing great angst in the retail community. Sub-Saharan Africa boasts its own traditional [clothing style](http://www.hamillgallery.com/SITE/Textiles.html). Cotton seems to be the dominant material.

In East Africa, one finds extensive use of cotton clothing. Shemma, shama, and [kuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuta_(clothing) "Kuta (clothing)") are types of Ethiopian clothing. [Kanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanga_(African_garment) "Kanga (African garment)") are [Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_culture "Swahili culture") cloth that comes in rectangular shapes, made of pure cotton, and put together to make clothing. [Kitenges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitenge "Kitenge") are similar to kangas and [kikoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kikoy "Kikoy"), but are of a thicker cloth, and have an edging only on a long side. [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya "Kenya"), [Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda "Uganda"), [Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania "Tanzania"), and [South Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Sudan "South Sudan") are some of the African countries where kitenge is worn. In [Malawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawi "Malawi"), [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia "Namibia") and [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia "Zambia"), kitenge is known as Chitenge. One of the unique materials, which is not a fiber and is used to make clothing is barkcloth, an innovation of the Baganda people of Uganda. It came from the Mutuba tree ([Ficus natalensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ficus_natalensis "Ficus natalensis")). In Madagascar a type of draped cloth called [lamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamba_(garment) "Lamba (garment)") is worn.

In West Africa, again cotton is the material of choice. In the Sahel and other parts of West Africa the [boubou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boubou_(clothing) "Boubou (clothing)") and [kaftan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaftan "Kaftan") style of clothing are featured. [Kente cloth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kente_cloth "Kente cloth") is created by the [Akan people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akan_people "Akan people") of Ghana and Ivory Coast, from silk of the various moth species in West Africa. Kente comes from the [Akan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akan_language "Akan language") [twi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twi "Twi") word *kenten* which means basket. It is sometimes used to make [dashiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dashiki "Dashiki") and [kufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kufi "Kufi"). Adire is a type of Yoruba cloth that is starch resistant. Raffia cloth and barkcloth are also utilised in the region.

In Central Africa, the Kuba people developed raffia cloth from the raffia plant fibers. It was widely used in the region. Barkcloth was also extensively used.

In Southern Africa, one finds numerous uses of animal hide and skins for clothing. The Ndau in central Mozambique and the Shona mix hide with barkcloth and cotton cloth. Cotton cloth is referred to as machira. Xhosa, Tswana, Sotho, and Swazi also made extensive use of hides. Hides come from cattle, sheep, goat, and elephant. Leopard skins were coveted and were a symbol of kingship in Zulu society. Skins were tanned to form leather, dyed, and embedded with beads.

### Media

In 2007, radio was the major source of information in sub-Saharan Africa. Average coverage stands at more than a third of the population. Countries such as [Gabon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabon "Gabon"), [Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychelles "Seychelles"), and [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa "South Africa") boast almost 100% penetration. Only five countries—Burundi, [Djibouti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djibouti "Djibouti"), [Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrea "Eritrea"), [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia "Ethiopia"), and [Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somalia "Somalia")—still have a penetration of less than 10%. [Broadband penetration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadband "Broadband") outside of South Africa has been limited where it is exorbitantly expensive. Access to the internet via cell phones is on the rise.

Television is the second major source of information. Because of power shortages, the spread of television viewing has been limited. Eight per cent have television, a total of 62 million. Those in the television industry view the region as an untapped green market. Digital television and pay for service are on the rise.

### Cuisine

[Sub-Saharan African cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_cuisine "African cuisine") is very diverse. A lot of regional overlapping occurs, but there are dominant elements region by region.

West African cuisine can be described as starchy, flavorfully spicey. Dishes include [fufu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fufu "Fufu"), [kenkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenkey "Kenkey"), [couscous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Couscous "Couscous"), [garri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garri "Garri"), foutou, and [banku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banku_(dish) "Banku (dish)"). Ingredients are of native starchy tubers, [yams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yam_(vegetable) "Yam (vegetable)"), [cocoyams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddoe "Eddoe"), and [cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava "Cassava"). Grains include [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet "Millet"), [sorghum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorghum "Sorghum"), and [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice "Rice"), usually in the Sahel. Oils include [palm oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_oil "Palm oil") and [shea butter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shea_butter "Shea butter") (Sahel). One finds recipes that mix [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_as_food "Fish as food") and [meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meat "Meat"). Beverages are [palm wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_wine "Palm wine") (sweet or sour) and [millet beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet_beer "Millet beer"). [Roasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roasting "Roasting"), [baking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baking "Baking"), [boiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boiling "Boiling"), [frying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frying "Frying"), [mashing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mashing "Mashing"), and [spicing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spicing "Spicing") are all cooking techniques.

Southeast African cuisine, especially those of the Swahili people, reflects its Islamic, geographical Indian Ocean cultural links. Dishes include [ugali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugali "Ugali"), [sukuma wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukuma_wiki "Sukuma wiki"), and [halva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halva "Halva"). Spices such as [curry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curry "Curry"), [saffron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffron "Saffron"), [cloves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clove "Clove"), [cinnamon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinnamon "Cinnamon"), [pomegranate juice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomegranate_juice "Pomegranate juice"), [cardamon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardamon "Cardamon"), [ghee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghee "Ghee"), and [sage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvia_officinalis "Salvia officinalis") are used, especially among Muslims. Meat includes [cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle "Cattle"), [sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep "Sheep"), and [goats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goat "Goat"), but is rarely eaten since meat is viewed as a luxury.

In the [Horn of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horn_of_Africa "Horn of Africa"), pork and non-fish seafood are avoided by Christians and Muslims. Dairy products and all meats are avoided during lent by Ethiopians. [Maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize "Maize") (corn) is a major staple. [Cornmeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornmeal "Cornmeal") is used to make ugali, a popular dish with different names. [Teff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teff "Teff") is used to make [injera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injera "Injera") or canjeero (Somali) bread. Other important foods include [enset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enset "Enset"), [noog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noog "Noog"), [lentils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lentil "Lentil"), rice, [banana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana "Banana"), [leafy greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leafy_greens "Leafy greens"), [chili peppers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chili_pepper "Chili pepper"), [coconut milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut_milk "Coconut milk"), and [tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomato "Tomato"). Beverages are coffee (domesticated in Ethiopia), [chai tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chai_tea "Chai tea"), fermented [beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer "Beer") from banana or millet. Cooking techniques include roasting and [marinating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinating "Marinating").

Central African cuisine connects with all major regions of sub-Saharan Africa: Its cuisine reflects that. [Ugali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugali "Ugali") and fufu are eaten in the region. Central African cuisine is very starchy and spicy hot. Dominant crops include [plantains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooking_banana "Cooking banana"), cassava, [peanuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut "Peanut"), chillis, and okra. Meats include [beef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beef "Beef"), [chicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_as_food "Chicken as food"), and sometimes exotic meats called [bushmeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bushmeat "Bushmeat") ([antelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antelope "Antelope"), [warthog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warthog "Warthog"), [crocodile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crocodile "Crocodile")). Widespread spicy hot fish cuisine is one of the differentiating aspects. [Mushroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edible_mushroom "Edible mushroom") is sometimes used as a meat substitute.

Traditional Southern African cuisine surrounds meat. Traditional society typically focused on raising sheep, goats, and especially cattle. Dishes include [braai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braai "Braai") (barbecue meat), [sadza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadza "Sadza"), [bogobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogobe "Bogobe"), [pap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pap_(food) "Pap (food)") (fermented cornmeal), [milk products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk_product "Milk product") ([buttermilk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buttermilk "Buttermilk"), [yoghurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoghurt "Yoghurt")). Crops utilised are sorghum, maize (corn), pumpkin beans, leafy greens, and [cabbage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabbage "Cabbage"). Beverages include ting (fermented sorghum or maize), [milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk "Milk"), [chibuku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibuku "Chibuku") (milky beer). Influences from the Indian and Malay communities can be seen in its use of curries, [sambals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambal "Sambal"), [pickled fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickled_fish "Pickled fish"), [fish stews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_stew "Fish stew"), [chutney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chutney "Chutney"), and [samosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samosa "Samosa"). European influences can be seen in cuisines like [biltong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biltong "Biltong") (dried beef strips), [potjies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potjie "Potjie") (stews of maize, [onions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onion "Onion"), tomatoes), [French wines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_wine "French wine"), and crueler or [koeksister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koeksister "Koeksister") (sugar syrup cookie).

### Sports

Football (soccer) is the most popular sport in sub-Saharan Africa. Sub-Saharan men are its main patrons. Major competitions include the [African Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAF_Champions_League "CAF Champions League"), a competition for the best clubs on the continent and the [Confederation Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAF_Confederation_Cup "CAF Confederation Cup"), a competition primarily for the national cup winner of each African country. The [Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa_Cup_of_Nations "Africa Cup of Nations") is a competition of 16 national teams from various African countries held every two years. South Africa hosted the [2010 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup "2010 FIFA World Cup"), a first for a sub-Saharan country. In 2010, Cameroon played in the [World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup "FIFA World Cup") for the sixth time, which is the current record for a sub-Saharan team. In [1996 Nigeria won the Olympic gold for football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_at_the_1996_Summer_Olympics "Nigeria at the 1996 Summer Olympics"). In 2000 Cameroon maintained the continent's supremacy by winning the title too. Momentous achievements for sub-Saharan African football. Famous sub-Saharan football stars include [Abedi Pele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abedi_Pele "Abedi Pele"), [Emmanuel Adebayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Adebayor "Emmanuel Adebayor"), [George Weah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Weah "George Weah"), [Michael Essien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Essien "Michael Essien"), [Didier Drogba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Drogba "Didier Drogba"), [Roger Milla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Milla "Roger Milla"), [Nwankwo Kanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nwankwo_Kanu "Nwankwo Kanu"), [Jay-Jay Okocha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay-Jay_Okocha "Jay-Jay Okocha"), [Bruce Grobbelaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Grobbelaar "Bruce Grobbelaar"), [Samuel Eto'o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Eto%27o "Samuel Eto'o"), [Kolo Touré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolo_Tour%C3%A9 "Kolo Touré"), [Yaya Touré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaya_Tour%C3%A9 "Yaya Touré"), [Sadio Mané](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadio_Man%C3%A9 "Sadio Mané") and [Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-Emerick_Aubameyang "Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang"). The most talented sub-Saharan African football players find themselves courted and sought after by European leagues. There are currently more than 1000 Africans playing for European clubs. Sub-Saharan Africans have found themselves the target of racism by European fans. FIFA has been trying hard to crack down on racist outburst during games.

[Rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_union "Rugby union") is popular in sub-Saharan Africa. The [Confederation of African Rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_of_African_Rugby "Confederation of African Rugby") governs rugby games in the region. South Africa is a major force in the game and won the Rugby World Cup in [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Rugby_World_Cup "1995 Rugby World Cup"), [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Rugby_World_Cup "2007 Rugby World Cup"), [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Rugby_World_Cup "2019 Rugby World Cup") and [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Rugby_World_Cup "2023 Rugby World Cup"). Africa is also allotted one guaranteed qualifying place in the [Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_World_Cup "Rugby World Cup").

Boxing is a popular sport. [Battling Siki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battling_Siki "Battling Siki") is the first world champion to come out of sub-Saharan Africa. Countries such as Nigeria, Ghana and South Africa have produced numerous professional world champions such as [Dick Tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Tiger "Dick Tiger"), [Hogan Bassey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hogan_Bassey "Hogan Bassey"), [Gerrie Coetzee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerrie_Coetzee "Gerrie Coetzee"), [Samuel Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Peter "Samuel Peter"), [Azumah Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azumah_Nelson "Azumah Nelson") and [Jake Matlala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Matlala "Jake Matlala").

Cricket has a following. The [African Cricket Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Cricket_Association "African Cricket Association") is an international body which oversees cricket in African countries. South Africa and Zimbabwe have their own governing bodies. In 2003 the [Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_World_Cup "Cricket World Cup") was held in South Africa, first time it was held in sub-Saharan Africa.

Over the years, [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia "Ethiopia") and [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya "Kenya") have produced many notable long-distance athletes. Each country has federations that identify and cultivate top talent. Athletes from Ethiopia and Kenya hold, save for two exceptions, all the men's outdoor records for Olympic distance events from 800m to the [marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathon "Marathon"). Famous runners include [Haile Gebrselassie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haile_Gebrselassie "Haile Gebrselassie"), [Kenenisa Bekele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenenisa_Bekele "Kenenisa Bekele"), [Paul Tergat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Tergat "Paul Tergat"), and [John Cheruiyot Korir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cheruiyot_Korir "John Cheruiyot Korir").
