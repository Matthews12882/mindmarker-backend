# Middle East

The **Middle East** or **West Asia** is a geo-political region that is generally defined as encompassing all of [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt") and all of [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia "West Asia") except for the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus "Caucasus"). It roughly corresponds with what was historically referred to in [Western Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Europe "Western Europe") as the [Near East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_East "Near East"), which was juxtaposed with the [Far East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_East "Far East"). Unlike West Asia's adherence to [continental boundaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_boundaries "Continental boundaries"), the Middle East is demarcated by following [political borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border#Political_borders "Border") and consequently includes [East Thrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Thrace "East Thrace") in [Southeast Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Europe "Southeast Europe") because it is a part of [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey "Turkey"). Since the early 20th century, the term "Middle East" has grown increasingly prominent in international discourse, although it is often prone to causing confusion or disagreement on account of vague and changing definitions of what countries and regions it spans. The related terms "[Middle East and North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_and_North_Africa "Middle East and North Africa")" and "[Greater Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Middle_East "Greater Middle East")" denote other existing concepts of the region that involve extending the "Middle East" to incorporate most [Muslim-majority countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim-majority_countries "Muslim-majority countries").

Today, the bulk of Middle Eastern countries ([13 out of 18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mashriq "Mashriq")) are part of the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world "Arab world"). The three most populous Middle Eastern countries are Egypt, [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran"), and Turkey, while [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia "Saudi Arabia") is the largest Middle Eastern country by area. [Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabs "Arabs") are the most widespread [ethnic group in the region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_the_Middle_East "Ethnic groups in the Middle East"), followed by [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people "Turkish people"), [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persians "Persians"), [Kurds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurds "Kurds"), [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews"), and [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrians "Assyrians"). Significant geographical features or regions that constitute the Middle East are the [Arabian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Peninsula "Arabian Peninsula"), [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia "Anatolia"), the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant "Levant") and [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia "Mesopotamia") (together comprising the [Fertile Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertile_Crescent "Fertile Crescent")), and the [Iranian plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_plateau "Iranian plateau").

The [history of the Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Middle_East "History of the Middle East") is among the richest ever recorded, as it was the first region to be [settled by modern humans outside of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recent_African_origin_of_modern_humans "Recent African origin of modern humans") and the first region in which [writing systems were developed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invention_of_writing "Invention of writing"). Situated at the intersection of [Africa, Europe, and Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Eurasia "Afro-Eurasia"), the Middle East was home to several [cradles of civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cradles_of_civilization "Cradles of civilization") and has been recognized by both local and foreign populations as a region of great strategic importance for millennia. All of the core [Abrahamic religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrahamic_religions "Abrahamic religions") ([Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism "Judaism"), [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity"), and [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam")) originated and have their holiest sites in the Middle East.

Most parts of the Middle East exhibit a hot and arid climate, especially in the Arabian and Egyptian regions. However, a number of major rivers provide irrigation to support agriculture in certain areas, such as the [Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile "Nile") in Egypt, [the Tigris and the Euphrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigris%E2%80%93Euphrates_river_system "Tigris–Euphrates river system") in Mesopotamia, and the [Jordan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_River "Jordan River") in the Levant. Conversely, the Levantine coast and most of Turkey collectively boast a relatively temperate [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate "Mediterranean climate"), which yields dry summers with cool and wet winters. A group of Middle Eastern countries that are located along the [Persian Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf "Persian Gulf")—commonly called the [Gulf Arab states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Arab_states "Gulf Arab states")—have vast reserves of petroleum, which has [served as a critical economic resource](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrostate "Petrostate") and [accordingly influenced regional politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_politics "Petroleum politics"). Owing to the Middle East's aridity and pervasive dependence on the [fossil fuel industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel_industry "Fossil fuel industry"), it is one of the most vulnerable regions to [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_the_Middle_East_and_North_Africa "Climate change in the Middle East and North Africa") and [water scarcity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_conflict_in_the_Middle_East_and_North_Africa "Water conflict in the Middle East and North Africa").

## Terminology

The term "Middle East" may have originated with the [India Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_Office "India Office") of the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland "United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland") in the 1850s. However, it became more widely known through the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States"), particularly after American naval strategist [Alfred Thayer Mahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Thayer_Mahan "Alfred Thayer Mahan") used the term to "designate the area between [Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Peninsula "Arabian Peninsula") and [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent")" in 1902. During this period, the United Kingdom was vying for influence in [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia") as part of the "[Great Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Game "Great Game")" against the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire"), which was in the process of [conquering the entire region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_conquest_of_Central_Asia "Russian conquest of Central Asia").

Realizing the "Middle East" to be a region of immense strategic value centred around the [Persian Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf "Persian Gulf"), Mahan took the view that, beyond the [Suez Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Canal "Suez Canal") in [British-ruled Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Egypt_under_the_British "History of Egypt under the British"), the Persian Gulf was the most important passage for the United Kingdom in the context of keeping in check any possible Russian advance towards [British-ruled India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj"). Mahan first used the term in his September 1902 article "The Persian Gulf and International Relations" for the British journal *[National Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Review_(London) "National Review (London)")* in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London").

> The Middle East, if I may adopt a term which I have not seen, will some day need [its Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta_Protectorate "Malta Protectorate"), as well as [its Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Gibraltar "Capture of Gibraltar"); it does not follow that either will be in the Persian Gulf. Naval force has the quality of mobility which carries with it the privilege of temporary absences; but it needs to find on every scene of operation established bases of refit, of supply, and in case of disaster, of security. The [British Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Navy "British Navy") should have the facility to concentrate in force if occasion arise, about [Aden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aden_Province "Aden Province"), India, and the Persian Gulf.

Mahan's article was reprinted in the British newspaper *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times "The Times")* and followed by a 20-article series entitled "The Middle Eastern Question" by British historian and diplomat [Valentine Chirol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentine_Chirol "Valentine Chirol"). In this series, which began in October 1902, he would expand the definition of "Middle East" to include "those regions of Asia which extend to the borders of India or command the approaches to India." After the series ended in 1903, *The Times* removed quotation marks from subsequent uses of the term.

Until [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), it was customary to refer to areas centred on [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey "Turkey") and the [Eastern Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Mediterranean "Eastern Mediterranean") as the "[Near East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_East "Near East")" in juxtaposition with the "[Far East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_East "Far East")" centred on [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_(1912%E2%80%931949) "Republic of China (1912–1949)"), India, and [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan "Empire of Japan"). The Middle East was then defined as the area from [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia "Mesopotamia") to [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_rule_in_Burma "British rule in Burma")—namely, the area between the Near East and the Far East—which now broadly corresponds with [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia").

In the late 1930s, the [Cairo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo "Cairo")-based [Middle East Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_Command "Middle East Command") was established for the [British Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Armed_Forces "British Armed Forces"). After that time, the term "Middle East" gained broader usage across both Europe and the United States. The [Middle East Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_Institute "Middle East Institute"), for example, was founded in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C.") in 1946.

While "[Southwest Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Asia "Southwest Asia")" or "Swasia" have been presented sparsely as alternatives, the inclusion of Egypt (located entirely in [North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Africa "North Africa") except for the [Sinai Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_Peninsula "Sinai Peninsula")) among those countries that are counted as part of the Middle East challenges the usefulness of such terms. Furthermore, the core term "Asia" itself is [Eurocentric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocentrism "Eurocentrism"), having been expanded upon from a term coined by the [ancient Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greeks "Ancient Greeks") to refer to [contemporary Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory_of_Anatolia "Prehistory of Anatolia").

### Usage and criticism

The description *Middle* has also led to some confusion over changing definitions. Before the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), "Near East" was used in English to refer to the [Balkans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkans "Balkans") and the [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire "Ottoman Empire"), while "Middle East" referred to the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus "Caucasus"), [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia "Persia"), and Arabian lands, and sometimes [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan"), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") and others. In contrast, "[Far East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_East "Far East")" referred to the countries of [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia") (e.g. [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China"), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan"), and [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea "Korea")).

With the collapse of the Ottoman Empire in 1918, "Near East" largely fell out of common use in English, while "Middle East" came to be applied to the emerging independent countries of the [Islamic world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_world "Islamic world"). However, the usage "Near East" was retained by a variety of academic disciplines, including [archaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology "Archaeology") and [ancient history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_history "Ancient history"). In their usage, the term describes an area identical to the term *Middle East*, which is not used by these disciplines (see [ancient Near East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Near_East "Ancient Near East")).[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The first official use of the term "Middle East" by the [United States government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_government_of_the_United_States "Federal government of the United States") was in the 1957 [Eisenhower Doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenhower_Doctrine "Eisenhower Doctrine"), which pertained to the [Suez Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Crisis "Suez Crisis"). Secretary of State [John Foster Dulles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Foster_Dulles "John Foster Dulles") defined the Middle East as "the area lying between and including [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya "Libya") on the west and [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan") on the east, [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria "Syria") and [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq "Iraq") on the North and the Arabian peninsula to the south, plus the [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan "Sudan") and [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia "Ethiopia")." In 1958, the [State Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_State "United States Department of State") explained that the terms "Near East" and "Middle East" were interchangeable, and defined the region as including only [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt"), Syria, [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel"), [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon "Lebanon"), [Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan "Jordan"), Iraq, [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia "Saudi Arabia"), [Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuwait "Kuwait"), [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain "Bahrain"), and [Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar "Qatar").

Since the late 20th century, scholars and journalists from the region, such as journalist Louay Khraish and historian [Hassan Hanafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Hanafi "Hassan Hanafi") have criticized the use of "Middle East" as a [Eurocentric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocentrism "Eurocentrism") and [colonialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonialism "Colonialism") term.

The [Associated Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_Press "Associated Press") Stylebook of 2004 says that Near East formerly referred to the farther west countries while Middle East referred to the eastern ones, but that now they are synonymous. It instructs:

> Use *Middle East* unless *Near East* is used by a source in a story. *Mideast* is also acceptable, but *Middle East* is preferred.

### Translations

European languages have adopted terms similar to *Near East* and *Middle East*. Since these are based on a relative description, the meanings depend on the country and are generally different from the English terms. In [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language "German language") the term *[Naher Osten](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naher_Osten "de:Naher Osten")* (Near East) is still in common use (nowadays the term *Mittlerer Osten* is more and more common in press texts translated from English sources, albeit having a distinct meaning).

In the four Slavic languages, [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language") [Ближний Восток](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%91%D0%BB%D0%B8%D0%B6%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%B9_%D0%92%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D0%BA "ru:Ближний Восток") or *Blizhniy Vostok*, [Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_language "Bulgarian language") [Близкия Изток](https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%91%D0%BB%D0%B8%D0%B7%D1%8A%D0%BA_%D0%98%D0%B7%D1%82%D0%BE%D0%BA "bg:Близък Изток"), [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language "Polish language") *[Bliski Wschód](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bliski_Wsch%C3%B3d "pl:Bliski Wschód")* or [Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_language "Croatian language") *[Bliski istok](https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bliski_istok "hr:Bliski istok")* (terms meaning *Near East* are the only appropriate ones for the region).

However, some European languages do have "Middle East" equivalents, such as [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language "French language") [Moyen-Orient](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moyen-Orient "fr:Moyen-Orient"), [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_language "Swedish language") [Mellanöstern](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellan%C3%B6stern "sv:Mellanöstern"), [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_language "Spanish language") [Oriente Medio or Medio Oriente](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriente_Medio "es:Oriente Medio"), [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language "Greek language") is Μέση Ανατολή (*Mesi Anatoli*), and [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language "Italian language") [Medio Oriente](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medio_Oriente "it:Medio Oriente").

Perhaps because of the political influence of the United States and Europe, and the prominence of Western press, the Arabic equivalent of *Middle East* (Arabic: الشرق الأوسط *ash-Sharq al-Awsaṭ*) has become standard usage in the mainstream Arabic press. It comprises the same meaning as the term "Middle East" in North American and Western European usage. The designation, *[Mashriq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mashriq "Mashriq")*, also from the Arabic root for *East*, also denotes a variously defined region around the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant "Levant"), the eastern part of the Arabic-speaking world (as opposed to the *[Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb "Maghreb")*, the western part). Even though the term originated in the West, countries of the Middle East that use languages other than Arabic also use that term in translation. For instance, the [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language "Persian language") equivalent for Middle East is خاورمیانه (*Khāvar-e miyāneh*), the Hebrew is המזרח התיכון (*hamizrach hatikhon*), and the Turkish is Orta Doğu.

## Countries and territory

### Countries and territory usually considered within the Middle East

Traditionally included within the Middle East are [Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Peninsula "Arabian Peninsula"), [Asia Minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia "Anatolia"), [East Thrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Thrace "East Thrace"), [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt"), [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran"), the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant "Levant"), [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia "Mesopotamia"), and the [Socotra Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socotra_Governorate "Socotra Governorate"). The region includes 17 [UN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN "UN")-recognized countries and one [British Overseas Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Overseas_Territories "British Overseas Territories").

### Other definitions of the Middle East

Various concepts are often paralleled to the Middle East, most notably the [Near East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_East "Near East"), [Fertile Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertile_Crescent "Fertile Crescent"), and [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant "Levant"). These are geographical concepts, which refer to large sections of the modern-day Middle East, with the Near East being the closest to the Middle East in its geographical meaning. Due to it primarily being Arabic speaking, the [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb "Maghreb") region of North Africa is sometimes included.

"[Greater Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Middle_East "Greater Middle East")" is a [political](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_geography "Political geography") term coined by the [second Bush administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_George_W._Bush "Presidency of George W. Bush") in the first decade of the 21st century to denote various countries, pertaining to the [Muslim world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_world "Muslim world"), specifically [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan"), [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran"), [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan"), and [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey "Turkey"). Various [Central Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia") countries are sometimes also included.

## History

The Middle East lies at the juncture of [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa "Africa") and [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia "Eurasia") and of the [Indian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean "Indian Ocean") and the [Mediterranean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Sea "Mediterranean Sea") (see also: [Indo-Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Mediterranean "Indo-Mediterranean")). It is the birthplace and [spiritual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirituality "Spirituality") center of religions such as [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity"), [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam"), [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism "Judaism"), [Manichaeism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manichaeism "Manichaeism"), [Yezidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yezidi "Yezidi"), [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze "Druze"), [Yarsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarsan "Yarsan"), and [Mandeanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandeanism "Mandeanism"), and in Iran, [Mithraism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mithraism "Mithraism"), [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism "Zoroastrianism"), [Manicheanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manicheanism "Manicheanism"), and the [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith "Baháʼí Faith"). Throughout its history the Middle East has been a major center of world affairs; a strategically, economically, politically, culturally, and religiously sensitive area. The region is one of the regions where [agriculture was independently discovered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origins_of_agriculture_in_West_Asia "Origins of agriculture in West Asia"), and from the Middle East it was spread, during the Neolithic, to different regions of the world such as Europe, the Indus Valley and Eastern Africa.

Prior to the formation of civilizations, advanced cultures formed all over the Middle East during the [Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age "Stone Age"). The search for agricultural lands by agriculturalists, and pastoral lands by herdsmen meant different migrations took place within the region and shaped its ethnic and demographic makeup.

The Middle East is widely and most famously known as the [cradle of civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cradle_of_civilization "Cradle of civilization"). The world's earliest civilizations, [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia "Mesopotamia") ([Sumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumer "Sumer"), [Akkad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_Empire "Akkadian Empire"), [Assyria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyria "Assyria"), and [Babylonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonia "Babylonia")), [ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt "Ancient Egypt") and [Kish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kish_civilization "Kish civilization") in the Levant, all originated in the Fertile Crescent and [Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile "Nile") Valley regions of the ancient Near East. These were followed by the [Hittite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittites "Hittites"), [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks "Greeks"), [Hurrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurrians "Hurrians") and [Urartian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urartian "Urartian") civilisations of [Asia Minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia_Minor "Asia Minor"); [Elam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elam "Elam"), [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Iran "History of Iran") and [Median](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medes "Medes") civilizations in [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran"), as well as the civilizations of the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Levant "History of the Levant") (such as [Ebla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebla "Ebla"), [Mari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mari,_Syria "Mari, Syria"), [Nagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_Brak "Tell Brak"), [Ugarit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugarit "Ugarit"), [Canaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaan "Canaan"), [Aramea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aramea "Aramea"), [Mitanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitanni "Mitanni"), [Phoenicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia "Phoenicia"), and [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Israel "Ancient Israel")) and the [Arabian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Peninsula "Arabian Peninsula") ([Magan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majan_(civilization) "Majan (civilization)"), [Sheba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheba "Sheba"), [Ubar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantis_of_the_Sands "Atlantis of the Sands")). The Near East was first largely unified under the [Neo Assyrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo_Assyrian_Empire "Neo Assyrian Empire"), then the [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire "Achaemenid Empire") followed later by the [Macedonian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonian_Empire "Macedonian Empire") and after this to some degree by the Iranian empires (namely the [Parthian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthian_Empire "Parthian Empire") and [Sasanian Empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire "Sasanian Empire")), the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire "Roman Empire"), and the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire "Byzantine Empire"). The region served as the intellectual and economic center of the Roman Empire and played an exceptionally important role due to its periphery on the Sasanian Empire. Thus, the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome "Ancient Rome") stationed up to five or six of their legions in the region for the sole purpose of defending it from Sasanian and Bedouin raids and invasions.

From the 4th century CE onward, the Middle East became the center of the two main powers at the time, the Byzantine Empire and the Sasanian Empire. However, it would be the later [Islamic Caliphates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate "Caliphate") of the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages "Middle Ages"), or [Islamic Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Golden_Age "Islamic Golden Age") which began with the [Islamic conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_conquests "Early Muslim conquests") of the region in the 7th century AD, that would first unify the entire Middle East as a distinct region and create the dominant [Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam") [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab "Arab") ethnic identity that largely (but not exclusively) persists today. The 4 caliphates that dominated the Middle East for more than 600 years were the [Rashidun Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidun_Caliphate "Rashidun Caliphate"), the [Umayyad Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate "Umayyad Caliphate"), the [Abbasid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate "Abbasid Caliphate"), and the [Fatimid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimid_Caliphate "Fatimid Caliphate"). Additionally, the [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols "Mongols") would come to dominate the region, the [Kingdom of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Armenia_(Antiquity) "Kingdom of Armenia (Antiquity)") would incorporate parts of the region to their domain, the [Seljuks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Empire "Seljuk Empire") would rule the region and spread Turko-Persian culture, and the [Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks "Franks") would found the [Crusader states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusader_states "Crusader states") that would stand for roughly two centuries. Josiah Russell estimates the population of what he calls "Islamic territory" as roughly 12.5 million in 1000 – [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia "Anatolia") 8 million, [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_(region) "Syria (region)") 2 million, and [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt") 1.5 million. From the 16th century onward, the Middle East came to be dominated, once again, by two main powers: the [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire "Ottoman Empire") and [Safavid Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Iran "Safavid Iran").

The modern Middle East began after [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), when the Ottoman Empire, which was allied with the [Central Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Powers "Central Powers"), was defeated by the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I "Allies of World War I") and [partitioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitioning_of_the_Ottoman_Empire "Partitioning of the Ottoman Empire") into a number of separate nations, initially under [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine "Mandatory Palestine") and [French Mandates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Mandate "French Mandate"). Other defining events in this transformation included the establishment of Israel in 1948 and the eventual departure of European powers, notably [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom") and [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France") by the end of the 1960s. They were supplanted in some part by the rising influence of the United States from the 1970s onward.

In the 20th century, the region's significant stocks of [crude oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crude_oil "Crude oil") gave it new strategic and economic importance. Mass production of oil began around 1945, with Saudi Arabia, Iran, Kuwait, Iraq, and the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates "United Arab Emirates") having large quantities of oil. Estimated [oil reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_reserves#Estimated_reserves_by_country "Oil reserves"), especially in Saudi Arabia and Iran, are some of the highest in the world, and the international oil cartel [OPEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OPEC "OPEC") is dominated by Middle Eastern countries.

During the Cold War, the Middle East was a theater of ideological struggle between the two superpowers and their allies: [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO") and the United States on one side, and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") and [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact "Warsaw Pact") on the other, as they competed to influence regional allies. Besides the political reasons there was also the "ideological conflict" between the two systems. Moreover, as [Louise Fawcett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louise_Fawcett "Louise Fawcett") argues, among many important areas of contention, or perhaps more accurately of anxiety, were, first, the desires of the superpowers to gain strategic advantage in the region, second, the fact that the region contained some two-thirds of the world's oil reserves in a context where oil was becoming increasingly vital to the economy of the Western world [...] Within this contextual framework, the United States sought to divert the Arab world from Soviet influence. Throughout the 20th and 21st centuries, the region has experienced both periods of relative peace and tolerance and periods of conflict particularly between [Sunnis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni "Sunni") and [Shiites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiite "Shiite").

## Geography

### Climate change

In 2018, the [MENA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MENA "MENA") region emitted 3.2 billion tonnes of carbon dioxide and produced 8.7% of global [greenhouse gas emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions "Greenhouse gas emissions") (GHG). These emissions are mostly from the [energy sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_industry "Energy industry"), an integral component of many [Middle Eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Eastern "Middle Eastern") and [North African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Africa "North Africa") economies due to the extensive [oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum "Petroleum") and [natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas "Natural gas") reserves that are found within the region. The Middle East region is one of the most [vulnerable to climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_vulnerability "Climate change vulnerability"). The impacts include increase in [drought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drought "Drought") conditions, aridity, [heatwaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_wave "Heat wave") and [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise "Sea level rise").

Sharp global temperature and sea level changes, shifting precipitation patterns and increased frequency of [extreme weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_weather "Extreme weather") events are some of the main impacts of climate change as identified by the [Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intergovernmental_Panel_on_Climate_Change "Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change") (IPCC). The MENA region is especially vulnerable to such impacts due to its arid and semi-arid environment, facing climatic challenges such as low rainfall, high temperatures and dry soil. The climatic conditions that foster such challenges for MENA are projected by the IPCC to worsen throughout the 21st century. If [greenhouse gas emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions "Greenhouse gas emissions") are not significantly reduced, part of the MENA region risks becoming uninhabitable before the year 2100.

[Climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change "Climate change") is expected to put significant strain on already [scarce water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_conflict_in_the_Middle_East_and_North_Africa "Water conflict in the Middle East and North Africa") and agricultural resources within the MENA region, threatening the national security and political stability of all included countries. Over 60 percent of the region's population lives in high and very high water-stressed areas compared to the global average of 35 percent. This has prompted some MENA countries to engage with the issue of climate change on an international level through environmental accords such as the [Paris Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Agreement "Paris Agreement"). Law and policy are also being established on a national level amongst MENA countries, with a focus on the development of renewable energies.

## Economy

Middle Eastern economies range from being very poor (such as Gaza Strip and Yemen) to extremely wealthy nations (such as Qatar and the United Arab Emirates). According to the [International Monetary Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund "International Monetary Fund"), the three largest Middle Eastern economies in nominal GDP in 2023 were Saudi Arabia ($1.06 trillion), Turkey ($1.03 trillion), and Israel ($0.54 trillion). For nominal GDP per person, the highest ranking countries are Qatar ($83,891), Israel ($55,535), the United Arab Emirates ($49,451) and Cyprus ($33,807). Turkey ($3.6 trillion), Saudi Arabia ($2.3 trillion), and Iran ($1.7 trillion) had the largest economies in terms of [GDP PPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(PPP) "List of countries by GDP (PPP)"). For GDP PPP per person, the highest-ranking countries are Qatar ($124,834), the United Arab Emirates ($88,221), Saudi Arabia ($64,836), Bahrain ($60,596) and Israel ($54,997). The lowest-ranking country in the Middle East, in terms of GDP nominal per capita, is Yemen ($573).

The economic structure of Middle Eastern nations are different because while some are heavily dependent on export of only oil and oil-related products (Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates and Kuwait), others have a highly diverse economic base (such as Cyprus, Israel, Turkey and Egypt). Industries of the Middle Eastern region include oil and oil-related products, agriculture, cotton, cattle, dairy, textiles, leather products, surgical instruments, defence equipment (guns, ammunition, tanks, submarines, fighter jets, UAVs, and missiles). Banking is an important sector, especially for United Arab Emirates and Bahrain.

With the exception of Cyprus, Turkey, Egypt, Lebanon and Israel, tourism has been a relatively undeveloped area of the economy, in part because of the socially conservative nature of the region as well as political turmoil in certain regions. Since the end of the COVID pandemic however, countries such as the United Arab Emirates, Bahrain, and Jordan have begun attracting greater numbers of tourists because of improving tourist facilities and the relaxing of tourism-related restrictive policies.

Unemployment is high in the Middle East and North Africa region, particularly among people aged 15–29, a demographic representing 30% of the region's population. The total regional unemployment rate in 2025 is 10.8%, and among youth is as high as 28%.

## Demographics

### Ethnic groups

[Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabs "Arabs") constitute the largest ethnic group in the Middle East, followed by various [Iranian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_peoples "Iranian peoples") and then by [Turkic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_people "Turkic people") ([Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people "Turkish people"), [Azeris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azeris "Azeris"), [Syrian Turkmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Turkmen "Syrian Turkmen"), and [Iraqi Turkmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Turkmen "Iraqi Turkmen")). Native ethnic groups of the region include, in addition to Arabs, [Arameans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arameans_in_Syria "Arameans in Syria"), [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people "Assyrian people"), [Baloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baloch_peoples "Baloch peoples"), [Berbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbers "Berbers"), [Copts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copts "Copts"), [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze "Druze"), [Greek Cypriots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Cypriots "Greek Cypriots"), [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews"), [Kurds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurds "Kurds"), [Lurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lurs "Lurs"), [Mandaeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaeans "Mandaeans"), [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_people "Persian people"), [Samaritans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaritans "Samaritans"), [Shabaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shabak_people "Shabak people"), [Tats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tat_people_(Iran) "Tat people (Iran)"), and [Zazas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zazas "Zazas"). European ethnic groups that form a diaspora in the region include [Albanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanians "Albanians"), [Bosniaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosniaks "Bosniaks"), [Circassians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassians "Circassians") (including [Kabardians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabardians "Kabardians")), [Crimean Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Tatars "Crimean Tatars"), [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks "Greeks"), [Franco-Levantines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levantines_(Latin_Christians) "Levantines (Latin Christians)"), [Italo-Levantines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levantines_(Latin_Christians)#Italian_Levantines "Levantines (Latin Christians)"), and [Iraqi Turkmens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Turkmens "Iraqi Turkmens"). Among other migrant populations are [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese "Overseas Chinese"), [Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipinos_in_Saudi_Arabia "Filipinos in Saudi Arabia"), [Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indians_in_Saudi_Arabia "Indians in Saudi Arabia"), [Indonesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesians_in_Saudi_Arabia "Indonesians in Saudi Arabia"), [Pakistanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistanis "Pakistanis"), [Pashtuns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns "Pashtuns"), [Romani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people "Romani people"), and [Afro-Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Arab "Afro-Arab").

### Migration

"Migration has always provided an important vent for labor market pressures in the Middle East. For the period between the 1970s and 1990s, the Arab states of the Persian Gulf in particular provided a rich source of employment for workers from Egypt, Yemen and the countries of the Levant, while Europe had attracted young workers from North African countries due both to proximity and the legacy of colonial ties between France and the majority of North African states."

According to the [International Organization for Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Organization_for_Migration "International Organization for Migration"), there are 13 million first-generation migrants from [Arab nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_nations "Arab nations") in the world, of which 5.8 reside in other Arab countries. Expatriates from Arab countries contribute to the circulation of financial and human capital in the region and thus significantly promote regional development. In 2009 Arab countries received a total of US$35.1 billion in [remittance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remittance "Remittance") in-flows and remittances sent to [Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan "Jordan"), [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt"), and [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon "Lebanon") from other Arab countries are 40 to 190 per cent higher than trade revenues between these and other Arab countries. In [Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somalia "Somalia"), the [Somali Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Civil_War "Somali Civil War") has greatly increased the size of the [Somali diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_diaspora "Somali diaspora"), as many of the best educated Somalis left for Middle Eastern countries as well as [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe "Europe") and [North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_America "North America").

Non-Arab Middle Eastern countries such as [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey "Turkey"), [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel"), and [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran") are also subject to important migration dynamics.

A fair proportion of those migrating from Arab nations are from ethnic and religious minorities facing persecution and are not necessarily ethnic Arabs, Iranians or Turks.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] Large numbers of [Kurds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurds "Kurds"), [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews"), [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people "Assyrian people"), [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks "Greeks"), and [Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians "Armenians") as well as many [Mandaeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaeans "Mandaeans") have left nations such as Iraq, Iran, Syria and Turkey for these reasons during the last century. In Iran, many religious minorities such as [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christians "Christians"), [Baháʼís](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith "Baháʼí Faith"), Jews, and [Zoroastrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism "Zoroastrianism") have left since the [Islamic Revolution of 1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Revolution "Iranian Revolution").

### Languages

The six top languages, in terms of numbers of speakers, are [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_dialects "Arabic dialects"), [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language "Persian language"), [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language "Turkish language"), [Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_languages "Kurdish languages"), [Modern Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_languages "Hebrew languages") and [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Greek "Modern Greek"). About 20 minority languages are also spoken in the Middle East.

Arabic, with all its dialects, is the most widely spoken language in the Middle East, with [Literary Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Standard_Arabic "Modern Standard Arabic") being official in all North African and in most West Asian countries. Arabic dialects are also spoken in some adjacent areas in neighbouring Middle Eastern non-Arab countries. It is a member of the [Semitic branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitic_languages "Semitic languages") of the Afro-Asiatic languages. Several [Modern South Arabian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_South_Arabian_languages "Modern South Arabian languages") such as [Mehri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehri_language "Mehri language") and [Soqotri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soqotri_language "Soqotri language") are also spoken in Yemen and Oman. Another Semitic language is [Aramaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aramaic_language "Aramaic language") and its dialects are spoken mainly by [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people "Assyrian people") and [Mandaeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaeans "Mandaeans"), with [Western Aramaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Neo-Aramaic "Western Neo-Aramaic") still spoken in two villages near Damascus, Syria. There is also an [Oasis Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_languages "Berber languages")-speaking community in Egypt where the language is also known as [Siwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siwa_language "Siwa language"). It is a non-Semitic Afro-Asiatic sister language.

[Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language "Persian language") is the second most spoken language. While it is primarily spoken in [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran") and some border areas in neighbouring countries, the country is one of the region's largest and most populous. It belongs to the [Indo-Iranian branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Iranian_languages "Indo-Iranian languages") of the family of [Indo-European languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_languages "Indo-European languages"). Other Western Iranic languages spoken in the region include [Achomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achomi_language "Achomi language"), [Daylami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daylami_language "Daylami language"), [Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_languages "Kurdish languages") dialects, [Semmani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semnani_language "Semnani language"), [Lurish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luri_language "Luri language"), amongst many others.

The close third-most widely spoken language, [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language "Turkish language"), is largely confined to Turkey, which is also one of the region's largest and most populous countries, but it is present in areas in neighboring countries. It is a member of the [Turkic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_languages "Turkic languages"), which have their origins in East Asia. Another Turkic language, [Azerbaijani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_language "Azerbaijani language"), is spoken by Azerbaijanis in Iran.

The fourth-most widely spoken language, [Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_language "Kurdish language"), is spoken in the countries of Iran, Iraq, Syria, and Turkey, [Sorani Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorani "Sorani") is the second official language in Iraq (instated after the 2005 constitution) after Arabic.

[Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_languages "Hebrew languages") is the official language of [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel"), with Arabic given a special status after the [2018 Basic law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Law:_Israel_as_the_Nation-State_of_the_Jewish_People "Basic Law: Israel as the Nation-State of the Jewish People") lowered its status from an official language prior to 2018. Hebrew is spoken and used by over 80% of Israel's population, the other 20% using Arabic. Modern Hebrew only began being spoken in the 20th century after being [revived](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revival_of_the_Hebrew_language "Revival of the Hebrew language") in the late 19th century by Elizer Ben-Yehuda (Elizer Perlman) and [European Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jews "Ashkenazi Jews") settlers, with the [first native Hebrew speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itamar_Ben-Avi "Itamar Ben-Avi") being born in 1882.

[Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Greek "Modern Greek") is one of the two official languages of [Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus "Cyprus"), and the country's main language. Small communities of Greek speakers exist all around the Middle East; until the 20th century it was also widely spoken in Asia Minor (being the second most spoken language there, after Turkish) and Egypt. During the antiquity, [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek "Ancient Greek") was the [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca "Lingua franca") for many areas of the western Middle East and until the Muslim expansion it was widely spoken there as well. Until the late 11th century, it was also the main spoken language in [Asia Minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia_Minor "Asia Minor"); after that it was gradually replaced by the Turkish language as the Anatolian Turks expanded and the local Greeks were assimilated, especially in the interior.

[English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language "English language") is one of the official languages of Akrotiri and Dhekelia. It is also commonly taught and used as a foreign second language, in countries such as [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt"), [Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan "Jordan"), [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran"), [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq "Iraq"), [Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar "Qatar"), [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain "Bahrain"), [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates "United Arab Emirates"), and [Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuwait "Kuwait"). It is also a main language in some Emirates of the United Arab Emirates. It is also spoken as native language by Jewish immigrants from Anglophone countries (UK, US, Australia) in Israel and understood widely as second language there.

[French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language "French language") is taught and used in many government facilities and media in [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon "Lebanon"), and is taught in some primary and secondary schools of [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt") and [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria "Syria"). [Maltese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_language "Maltese language"), a Semitic language mainly spoken in Europe, is used by the [Franco-Maltese diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_in_Egypt "Maltese in Egypt") in Egypt. Due to widespread immigration of [French Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Jews "French Jews") to [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Jews_in_Israel "French Jews in Israel"), it is the native language of approximately 200,000 Jews in Israel.

[Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language "Armenian language") speakers are to be found in the region. [Georgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_language "Georgian language") is spoken by the Georgian diaspora.

[Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language") is spoken by a large portion of the Israeli population, because of [emigration in the late 1990s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliyah_from_the_Commonwealth_of_Independent_States_in_the_1990s "Aliyah from the Commonwealth of Independent States in the 1990s"). Russian today is a popular unofficial language in use in [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel"); news, radio and sign boards can be found in Russian around the country after Hebrew and Arabic. [Circassian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Caucasian_languages "Northwest Caucasian languages") is also spoken by the diaspora in the region and by almost all Circassians in Israel who speak Hebrew and English as well.

The largest [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language "Romanian language")-speaking community in the Middle East is found in [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel"), where as of 1995[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Middle_East&action=edit) Romanian is spoken by 5% of the population.

[Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language "Bengali language"), [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi "Hindi"), and [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu "Urdu") are widely spoken by migrant communities in many Middle Eastern countries, such as Saudi Arabia (where 20–25% of the population is South Asian), the United Arab Emirates (where 50–55% of the population is South Asian), and Qatar, which have large numbers of [Pakistani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan"), [Bangladeshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh"), and [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") immigrants.

### Religion

The Middle East is very diverse when it comes to [religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups "Major religious groups"), many of which originated there. [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam") is the largest religion in the Middle East, but other faiths that originated there, such as [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism "Judaism") and [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity"), are also well represented. [Christian communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_the_Middle_East "Christianity in the Middle East") have [played a vital role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Role_of_Christianity_in_civilization "Role of Christianity in civilization") in the Middle East, and they represent 78% of [Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus "Cyprus") population, and 40.5% of [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon "Lebanon"), where the [Lebanese president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Lebanon "President of Lebanon"), half of the cabinet, and half of the parliament follow one of the various Lebanese Christian rites. There are also important minority religions like the [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith "Baháʼí Faith"), [Yarsanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarsanism "Yarsanism"), [Yazidism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazidis "Yazidis"), [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism "Zoroastrianism"), [Mandaeism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaeism "Mandaeism"), [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze "Druze"), and [Shabakism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shabak_people#Religious_beliefs "Shabak people"), and in ancient times the region was home to [Mesopotamian religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Mesopotamian_religion "Ancient Mesopotamian religion"), [Canaanite religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Canaanite_religion "Ancient Canaanite religion"), [Manichaeism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manichaeism "Manichaeism"), [Mithraism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mithraic_mysteries "Mithraic mysteries") and various [monotheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotheist "Monotheist") [gnostic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnostic "Gnostic") sects.

## Culture

### Sport

The Middle East has recently become more prominent in hosting global sport events due to its wealth and desire to diversify its economy.

The [South Asian diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_diaspora "South Asian diaspora") is a major backer of [cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket "Cricket") in the region.
