# Robert Kraft

**Robert Hearth Kenneth Kraft** (born June 5, 1941) is an American businessman. He is the chairman and chief executive officer (CEO) of the [Kraft Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraft_Group "Kraft Group"), a diversified [holding company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holding_company "Holding company") with assets in paper and packaging, sports and entertainment, [real estate development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate_development "Real estate development"), and a [private equity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_equity "Private equity") portfolio. Since 1994, Kraft has owned the [New England Patriots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_Patriots "New England Patriots") of the [National Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League "National Football League") (NFL). He also owns the [New England Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_Revolution "New England Revolution") of [Major League Soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Soccer "Major League Soccer") (MLS), which he founded in 1996. In 2017, he founded the [Boston Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Uprising "Boston Uprising"), an [Overwatch League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overwatch_League "Overwatch League") esports team that competed from 2017 until it disbanded in January 2024. According to *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes "Forbes")*, he had an estimated net worth of $11.1 billion as of July 2024.

## Early life

Kraft was born in [Brookline, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookline,_Massachusetts "Brookline, Massachusetts"), the son of Sarah Bryna (Webber) and Harry Kraft, a dress manufacturer in Boston's [Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown,_Boston "Chinatown, Boston"). His mother was born in [Halifax, Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_Nova_Scotia "Halifax, Nova Scotia"); his father was a lay leader at Congregation Kehillath Israel in Brookline and wanted his son to become a rabbi. The Krafts were a [Modern Orthodox Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Orthodox_Judaism "Modern Orthodox Judaism") family. Robert attended the [Edward Devotion School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Devotion_School "Edward Devotion School") and graduated from [Brookline High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookline_High_School "Brookline High School"). As a child, he sold newspapers outside [Braves Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braves_Field "Braves Field") in Boston. During high school, Kraft was unable to participate in most sports because it interfered with his after-school Hebrew studies and observance of the Sabbath.

Kraft attended [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") on an academic scholarship and he served as class president. He played tennis and safety on the school's freshman and [lightweight football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sprint_Football "Sprint Football") teams. During that time, Kraft lived in [Carman Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carman_Hall "Carman Hall"). He met [Myra Hiatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myra_Kraft "Myra Kraft") at a delicatessen in [Boston's Back Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_Bay,_Boston "Back Bay, Boston") in 1962, and they married in June 1963. Kraft graduated from Columbia that year, and in 1965 he received an MBA from [Harvard Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Business_School "Harvard Business School").

## Business career

Kraft began his professional career with the Rand-Whitney Group, a [Worcester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcester,_Massachusetts "Worcester, Massachusetts")-based packaging company run by his father-in-law, [Jacob Hiatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Hiatt "Jacob Hiatt"). In 1968, he gained control of the company through a [leveraged buyout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leveraged_buyout "Leveraged buyout"). Kraft remains the chairman. In 1972, he founded International Forest Products, a trader of physical paper commodities. The two companies are the largest privately held paper and packaging companies in the United States.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] Kraft has said he started the company out of a hunch that the increase in international communications and transportation would lead to an expansion of global trade in the late 20th century.

International Forest Products became a top 100 US exporter/importer in 1997 and in 2001 was ranked 7th on the *[Journal of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Commerce "Journal of Commerce")*'s list in that category. In 1991, Kraft said of the business, "We do things for a number of companies, including [Avon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avon_Products "Avon Products"), [Kodak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodak "Kodak"), cosmetics companies, candies, toys." The company produced both corrugated and folding cartons, which he said were "used to package everything from the Patriot missile, to mints, to [Estee Lauder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Est%C3%A9e_Lauder_Companies "The Estée Lauder Companies"), Indiana Glass, and Polaroid." Kraft acquired interests in other areas, and ultimately formed the Kraft Group as an umbrella for them in 1998.

Kraft was an investor in New England Television Corp., which gained control of the channel 7 license for Boston in 1982, and he became a director of the board in 1983, after the newly licensed station, [WNEV-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHDH_(TV) "WHDH (TV)"), signed on, replacing the former WNAC-TV. In 1986, Kraft was named president of the corporation. In 1991, he exercised his option to sell his shares for an estimated $25 million.

## Sports ownership

### Boston Lobsters

In 1974, Kraft, Harold Bayne, Herbert Hoffman, Bob Mades, and Paul Slater purchased the [Boston Lobsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Lobsters_(1974%E2%80%931978) "Boston Lobsters (1974–1978)") of [World TeamTennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_TeamTennis "World TeamTennis") (WTT). The group spent heavily to lure a number of top players, including [Martina Navratilova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martina_Navratilova "Martina Navratilova"), and the Lobsters became one of the best teams in WTT. After the 1978 season, Kraft announced that the franchise would fold. The league itself folded soon thereafter.

After the Lobsters folded, Kraft was also mentioned as a bidder for the [Boston Red Sox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Red_Sox "Boston Red Sox") and the [Boston Celtics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Celtics "Boston Celtics").

### New England Patriots

Kraft has been a [New England Patriots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_Patriots "New England Patriots") fan since their [American Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Football_League "American Football League") days and had been a season ticket holder since 1971, when the team moved to [Schaefer Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schaefer_Stadium "Schaefer Stadium"). In 1985, he bought a 10-year option on [Foxboro Raceway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_State_Raceway "Bay State Raceway"), a horse track adjacent to the stadium, and the purchase prevented Patriots owner [Billy Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Sullivan_(American_football) "Billy Sullivan (American football)") from holding non-Patriot events at the stadium while races were being held. Kraft took advantage of the fact that the Sullivans owned the stadium but not the surrounding land; it was the beginning of a quest to buy the stadium and the Patriots. Sullivan's family was reeling from a series of bad investments, principally [The Jackson Five](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jackson_Five "The Jackson Five") [1984 Victory Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_Tour_(The_Jacksons_tour) "Victory Tour (The Jacksons tour)"), for which they had to pledge Sullivan Stadium as collateral. Those problems ultimately forced Sullivan to sell controlling interest of the team in 1988, while the stadium lapsed into bankruptcy.

In 1988, Kraft outbid several competitors to buy the stadium out of bankruptcy court from Sullivan for $22 million. The stadium was considered outdated and nearly worthless, but the purchase included the stadium's lease to the Patriots, which ran through 2001. Kraft bid on the [Patriots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_Patriots "New England Patriots") franchise as well, but he lost to [Victor Kiam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Kiam "Victor Kiam"). Sullivan and Kiam then tried to move the team to [Jacksonville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville "Jacksonville"), but Kraft refused to let them break the lease. Kiam was nearly brought down by bad investments of his own and was forced to sell the Patriots to [James Orthwein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Orthwein "James Orthwein") in 1992.

In 1994, Orthwein offered Kraft $75 million to buy out the remainder of the team's lease at Foxboro Stadium so he could move it to [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis "St. Louis"), but Kraft declined. Orthwein was no longer interested in operating the team in New England and decided to sell it. But the operating covenant required that any potential buyer negotiate with Kraft. With this in mind, Kraft launched what amounted to a [hostile takeover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hostile_takeover "Hostile takeover"), offering $172 million for an outright purchase. Future [St. Louis/Los Angeles Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Rams "Los Angeles Rams") owner [Stan Kroenke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stan_Kroenke "Stan Kroenke") offered more money ($200 million) with the intent to move to St. Louis. But Orthwein would have been saddled with all relocation expenses and any legal expenses from breaking the lease. When Kraft made known that he would go to court to enforce the covenant and force Kroenke to stay in Foxboro, Orthwein had little choice but to accept Kraft's offer, the highest ever made for an NFL team at the time.

Kraft said that his passion for the Patriots led him to "break every one of my financial rules" in pursuit of the team. He still believes he "overpaid" for the franchise. Kraft keeps a Victory Tour poster in his office as a reminder of what set in motion the events that allowed him to buy the Patriots. After the NFL approved the sale, the Patriots sold out their entire [1994 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_New_England_Patriots_season "1994 New England Patriots season"), the first full sell-out in franchise history. Every Patriots home game–preseason, regular season, and playoffs–has been sold out since. In 2023, the Patriots were one of the NFL's most valuable franchises, estimated by *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes "Forbes")* to be worth $7 billion.

In 1998, Kraft considered moving the Patriots to [Hartford, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartford,_Connecticut "Hartford, Connecticut"), based on an offer that the state of Connecticut would finance a new stadium, but he terminated the deal just before it became binding, instead building a new stadium in Foxborough with Massachusetts infrastructure funding. In 2002, Kraft financed a $350-million stadium for the Patriots initially called [CMGI Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMGI_Field "CMGI Field") but renamed [Gillette Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillette_Stadium "Gillette Stadium"). In 2007, he began to develop the land around Gillette Stadium, creating [Patriot Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriot_Place "Patriot Place"), a $375-million open-air shopping and entertainment center. The development included "[The Hall at Patriot Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hall_at_Patriot_Place "The Hall at Patriot Place") presented by Raytheon", a multi-story museum and hall of fame attached to the stadium, and the "CBS Scene", a [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS "CBS")-themed restaurant.

On January 27, 2000, Kraft traded a first round [draft pick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draft_(sports) "Draft (sports)") to the [New York Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Jets "New York Jets") for the rights to hire [Bill Belichick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Belichick "Bill Belichick") as head coach. The trade was criticized but proved successful: Belichick led the Patriots to six Super Bowl championships, nine conference championships, and 16 division titles. In the sixth round of the 2000 draft, the Patriots acquired quarterback [Tom Brady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Brady "Tom Brady"), who would be the team's starter from 2001 to 2019. The relationship between Kraft, Belichick, and Brady has been credited with producing [one of the most successful sports dynasties in football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brady%E2%80%93Belichick_era "Brady–Belichick era"), although in later years the personal relationship between the three grew strained.

Under Kraft's ownership, the Patriots experienced sustained success for the first time in franchise history. While they appeared in [Super Bowl XX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XX "Super Bowl XX") under the Sullivans, this was one of only six playoff appearances in 34 years. Indeed, that [Super Bowl season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_New_England_Patriots_season "1985 New England Patriots season") saw only the second playoff victory in franchise history. The Patriots have made the playoffs 21 times in Kraft's 27 years as owner. They have won 19 [AFC East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_East "AFC East") titles, including all but three since 2001 and 11 in a row from 2009 to 2019. The Patriots represented the AFC in the [Super Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl "Super Bowl") in [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXI "Super Bowl XXXI") (lost), [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXVI "Super Bowl XXXVI") (won), [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXVIII "Super Bowl XXXVIII") (won), [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXIX "Super Bowl XXXIX") (won), [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XLII "Super Bowl XLII") (lost), [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XLVI "Super Bowl XLVI") (lost), [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XLIX "Super Bowl XLIX") (won), [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LI "Super Bowl LI") (won), [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LII "Super Bowl LII") (lost), [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LIII "Super Bowl LIII") (won), and [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LX "Super Bowl LX") (lost). After having never won more than 11 games before Kraft's arrival, the team has won at least 12 games 14 times, including finishing the [2007 regular season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_New_England_Patriots_season "2007 New England Patriots season") undefeated before losing to the [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_New_York_Giants_season "2007 New York Giants season") in Super Bowl XLII.

Of Kraft's role in helping settle the [NFL lockout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_NFL_lockout "2011 NFL lockout") before the [2011 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_NFL_season "2011 NFL season"), Indianapolis Colts center [Jeff Saturday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Saturday "Jeff Saturday") said, "He is a man who helped us save football."

In 2005, during a visit to [Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg "Saint Petersburg"), Kraft gave Russian President [Vladimir Putin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Putin "Vladimir Putin") his third [Super Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl "Super Bowl") ring. In a statement released after the visit, Kraft said he did so out of "respect and admiration" for the Russian people and Putin's leadership. Kraft later said he had not originally intended to give Putin the ring and that his statement had been issued under pressure from the White House after Putin had kept it. The ring is on display with state gifts at the Kremlin.

Former Patriot [Ryan O'Callaghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_O%27Callaghan "Ryan O'Callaghan") wrote that Kraft supported him when he publicly came out as gay in 2017. According to O'Callaghan, Kraft invited him to a reception and said "What you did took a lot of courage. I'm so proud of you" and that he would be "forever a Patriot".

In June 2024, Kraft enshrined Tom Brady into the [Patriots Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriots_Hall_of_Fame "Patriots Hall of Fame") and announced that Brady's jersey number 12 would be retired. Over 60,000 Patriots fans attended the ceremony.

### Soccer

In 1996, Kraft founded the [New England Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_Revolution "New England Revolution"), a charter member of [Major League Soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Soccer "Major League Soccer") which began playing alongside the Patriots at Foxboro Stadium. Kraft also owned the [San Jose Clash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_Clash "San Jose Clash") (later [San Jose Earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_Earthquakes "San Jose Earthquakes")) from 1998 to 2000.

In November 2005, Kraft met with [Rick Parry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Parry "Rick Parry"), the chief executive of English [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_League "Premier League") team [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C. "Liverpool F.C."). Kraft was rumored to be interested in investing money into the 2004–05 [Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League "UEFA Champions League") winners. He told [BBC Radio 5 Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_5_Live "BBC Radio 5 Live"): "Liverpool is a great brand and it's something our family respects a lot. We're always interested in opportunities and growing, so you never know what can happen." However, the club was eventually sold to American duo [George Gillett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_N._Gillett_Jr. "George N. Gillett Jr.") and [Tom Hicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Hicks "Tom Hicks"). Liverpool is now owned by [Fenway Sports Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenway_Sports_Group "Fenway Sports Group"), owners of fellow Boston-based sport team the [Boston Red Sox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Red_Sox "Boston Red Sox").

In October 2017, Kraft said that he was "still intrigued" by the possibility of buying a [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_League "Premier League") football club, but that he was concerned about the lack of a [salary cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salary_cap "Salary cap") in British football.

In 2017, Kraft was named the Honorary Chairman of the board of directors for the successful joint [Canadian-Mexican-American bid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada%E2%80%93Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_2026_FIFA_World_Cup_bid "Canada–Mexico–United States 2026 FIFA World Cup bid") for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup "2026 FIFA World Cup").

In 2019, Kraft hired [Bruce Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Arena "Bruce Arena") as head coach and sporting director of the New England Revolution. In 2020, the team had their first playoff win in six years.

### Esports

[Blizzard Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blizzard_Entertainment "Blizzard Entertainment") announced in July 2017 that Kraft bought ownership in the [Boston Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Uprising "Boston Uprising"), one of the first seven teams for the professional [esports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esports "Esports") [Overwatch League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overwatch_League "Overwatch League"). They played in Season 1 of the Overwatch League. Preseason for the league began December 6, 2017, and the regular season started on January 10, 2018. Boston Uprising finished third in the Overwatch League's inaugural season.

## Philanthropy

Myra and Robert Kraft have donated hundreds of millions of dollars to charity, including education, child- and women-related issues, [healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare "Healthcare"), [youth sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_sports "Youth sports") and American and Israeli causes. Among the institutions the Krafts have supported are [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"), [Harvard Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Business_School "Harvard Business School"), [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University "Brandeis University"), the [College of the Holy Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_the_Holy_Cross "College of the Holy Cross"), [Boston College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_College "Boston College"), [Tufts University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufts_University "Tufts University"), [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University "Rice University"), [Yeshiva University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva_University "Yeshiva University"), the [Belmont Hill School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belmont_Hill_School "Belmont Hill School"), and the [Boys & Girls Clubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BGCA "BGCA") of Boston.

In 1989, the Krafts launched the Passport to Israel Fund, in collaboration with Combined Jewish Philanthropies of Greater Boston (CJP), to help parents send their [teenage children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolescence "Adolescence") to [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel"). One its projects is supporting [American Football Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Football_Israel "American Football Israel"), including Kraft Family Stadium in [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem "Jerusalem") and the [Kraft Family Israel Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Football_League "Israeli Football League"). In 1990, Kraft, his wife, and his father-in-law funded a joint professorship between [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University "Brandeis University") and the [College of the Holy Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_the_Holy_Cross "College of the Holy Cross"), forming the Kraft-Hiatt endowed chairs in comparative religion—the first inter-religious endowed chairs in the United States.

In 2000, Kraft donated $11.5 million to construct the Columbia/Barnard [Hillel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillel_International "Hillel International"), which is made of the white stone used in Jerusalem. In 2007, after a $5 million payment to Columbia's intercollegiate athletics program, the playing field at Columbia's Lawrence A. Wien Stadium at the [Baker Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baker_Field "Baker Field") Athletics Complex was named Robert K. Kraft Field.

In 2011, the Krafts pledged $20 million to [Partners HealthCare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partners_HealthCare "Partners HealthCare") to launch the Kraft Family National Center for Leadership and Training in Community Health, an initiative designed to improve access to quality healthcare at New England community health centers. The Krafts supported the [Dana–Farber Cancer Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dana%E2%80%93Farber_Cancer_Institute "Dana–Farber Cancer Institute") in Boston.

After the [Boston Marathon bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Marathon_bombing "Boston Marathon bombing") in 2013, Kraft announced he would match up to $100,000 in donations made for the victims through the New England Patriots Charitable Foundation.

In 2017, Kraft announced a contribution of $6 million to build the first regulation-size American football field in [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel"). In June 2017, Kraft and several [NFL Hall of Famers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_Football_Hall_of_Fame "Pro Football Hall of Fame") traveled to Israel for the grand opening of the Kraft Family Sports Campus. Since then, he has led additional "Touchdown in Israel" trips to Israel, with Patriots and Hall of Famers. Also in 2017, Kraft funded a new van as part of the Kraft Center for Community Health at [Massachusetts General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_General_Hospital "Massachusetts General Hospital") to help combat the [opioid crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opioid_epidemic "Opioid epidemic") in Boston. The vans allow those with opioid addiction to seek health services in their neighborhoods.

In 2018, Kraft donated $10 million to Combined Jewish Philanthropies of Greater Boston for the renovation of its headquarters in [downtown Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Boston "Downtown Boston"). In 2019, Kraft and [Chelsea Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_F.C. "Chelsea F.C.") owner [Roman Abramovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Abramovich "Roman Abramovich") hosted "Final Whistle on Hate", a soccer match between the [New England Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_Revolution "New England Revolution") and Chelsea F.C. to raise money to combat [antisemitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism "Antisemitism"). The match raised an estimated $4 million, with Kraft personally contributing $1 million.

The next month, he pledged $100,000 to the families of seven motorcyclists killed in a crash the month before. Kraft donated $20,000 and attended the memorial in Worcester to honor fallen firefighter Christopher Roy on the first anniversary of his death. Kraft teamed with recording artists [Jay-Z](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay-Z "Jay-Z") and [Meek Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meek_Mill "Meek Mill"), Fanatics executive chairman [Michael G. Rubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_G._Rubin "Michael G. Rubin"), and others to announce REFORM Alliance, a criminal justice reform foundation. In coordination with REFORM Alliance, Kraft invited more than 50 children aged 5 to 17 to fly on the Patriots' team charter to attend a game against the Buffalo Bills at Gillette Stadium. The children all had parents who had been incarcerated for technical probation violations. As of 2019, Kraft had led 27 missions to Israel.

In 2020, Kraft partnered with Chinese company [Tencent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tencent "Tencent") to purchase 1.2 million [N95 masks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N95_mask "N95 mask") to donate to medical workers in New York and Massachusetts to help combat the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"), sending the Patriots' private team plane to China to pick up the supplies. Kraft initially negotiated for 1.7 million masks, but only 1.2 million fit on board. They were allowed three hours on the ground in China at [Shenzhen Bao'an International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Bao%27an_International_Airport "Shenzhen Bao'an International Airport"). The plane was delivered 500,000 vaccines to [El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Salvador "El Salvador") in May 2021. Using the Patriots truck, they distributed 300,000 masks in New York City, 900,000 in Massachusetts, and 100,000 in Rhode Island.

In May 2020, Kraft put his Super Bowl LI ring up for auction with proceeds designated to help feed those facing [food insecurity as a result of the COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_security_during_the_COVID-19_pandemic "Food security during the COVID-19 pandemic").

Kraft lent the Patriots team plane to the [University of Rhode Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rhode_Island "University of Rhode Island") Rams football team in October 2021 after the team's charter flight fell through. He covered all costs despite the URI Rams expecting to pay expenses through the team's budget.

In April 2022, Harvard Business School announced the creation of the Robert K. Kraft Family Fellowship Fund, committing $24 million to benefit potential students with limited means to attend HBS.

On October 30, 2022, the Foundation to Combat Antisemitism (FCAS), which Kraft founded, sponsored an ad encouraging people to denounce hate against Jewish people. The ad aired during NFL games and was titled "Stand Up to Jewish Hate". It came in response to antisemitic comments made by [Kanye West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanye_West "Kanye West") and [Kyrie Irving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrie_Irving "Kyrie Irving"). Kraft invested $25 million in the "Stand Up to Jewish Hate" campaign, which launched through the foundation in March 2023 to raise awareness of online antisemitism. The foundation's executive director said the ads would air during the NFL draft, the NBA and NHL playoffs, and on social media. In October 2025, the Foundation to Combat Antisemitism rebranded as [Blue Square Alliance Against Hate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Square_Alliance_Against_Hate "Blue Square Alliance Against Hate").

2026 reporting on the Kraft Family Foundation described Jewish causes as one of its largest areas of grantmaking and listed [Machne Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machne_Israel "Machne Israel") and [Chabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chabad "Chabad") of Southampton among recent grantees, alongside institutions such as the [Jewish Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Museum_(Manhattan) "Jewish Museum (Manhattan)") and Friends of [United Hatzalah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Hatzalah "United Hatzalah").

In September 2026, Kraft donated $10 million toward a $25 million campaign to convert Kutz Hall at [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University "Brandeis University") into the Robert K. Kraft Family Center for Jewish Life and Leadership, a planned home for the university's Hillel chapter.

In October 2026, Kraft donated $5 million toward the construction of a $25 million [Chabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chabad "Chabad") center in [Chestnut Hill, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chestnut_Hill,_Massachusetts "Chestnut Hill, Massachusetts"). The facility was planned to be named the Kraft Family Chabad at Chestnut Hill.

## Personal life

In June 1963, Kraft married [Myra Nathalie Hiatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myra_Kraft "Myra Kraft"), a 1964 graduate of [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University "Brandeis University") and the daughter of the late [Worcester, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcester,_Massachusetts "Worcester, Massachusetts"), businessman and philanthropist [Jacob Hiatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Hiatt "Jacob Hiatt"). She died on July 20, 2011, of [ovarian cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovarian_cancer "Ovarian cancer"), aged 68. The Krafts were members of Temple Emanuel in [Newton, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton,_Massachusetts "Newton, Massachusetts"). A patch bearing Kraft's initials (MHK) appeared on the Patriots' uniform jersey throughout the [2011 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_New_England_Patriots_season "2011 New England Patriots season"). The couple had four sons: [Jonathan Kraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Kraft "Jonathan Kraft"), [Daniel Kraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Kraft "Daniel Kraft"), [Joshua Kraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_Kraft "Joshua Kraft"), and David Kraft.

In June 2012, Kraft began dating actress [Ricki Noel Lander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricki_Noel_Lander "Ricki Noel Lander"). In July 2012, Kraft assisted Lander in creating an audition video for a role in *[The Internship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Internship "The Internship")*. Lander and Kraft broke up in 2018.

In early 2022, Kraft's engagement to [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City")-based [ophthalmologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophthalmologist "Ophthalmologist") Dana Blumberg was announced by [Tommy Hilfiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Hilfiger "Tommy Hilfiger") at the inaugural [amfAR Gala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AmfAR_Gala "AmfAR Gala") Palm Beach event. Kraft and Blumberg reportedly began dating in 2017. They married in New York City in October 2022. [Ed Sheeran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Sheeran "Ed Sheeran") performed at the wedding.

## Politics and advocacy

### Support for Israel

*[The Intercept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Intercept "The Intercept")* reported in 2026 that, according to tax records dating back to 2006, Kraft has donated to various [pro-Israel organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category:Zionist_organizations_in_the_United_States "Category:Zionist organizations in the United States"): Kraft Family Foundation had donated $196,000 to the [Friends of the Israel Defense Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends_of_the_Israel_Defense_Forces "Friends of the Israel Defense Forces") and $123,000 to other organizations directly serving soldiers and veterans of the Israeli military. The Kraft Group limited liability company has given $3 million to the United Democracy Project [super political action committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_PAC "Super PAC") (super PAC) of the [American Israel Public Affairs Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Israel_Public_Affairs_Committee "American Israel Public Affairs Committee") (AIPAC) since 2022. Kraft's foundation has also given $63,500 to the [Committee for Accuracy in Middle East Reporting and Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_for_Accuracy_in_Middle_East_Reporting_and_Analysis "Committee for Accuracy in Middle East Reporting and Analysis") (CAMERA), including a $36,000 contribution in 2018 with the memo line "fighting antisemitism". In 2011–12, it also gave approximately $50,000 to [The Investigative Project on Terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Investigative_Project_on_Terrorism "The Investigative Project on Terrorism"). From 2008 to 2018, it gave $150,000 to the [Middle East Media and Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_Media_and_Research_Institute "Middle East Media and Research Institute") (MEMRI).

Kraft has also organized visits to Israel for NFL players, including [Tom Brady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Brady "Tom Brady").

In June 2019, Kraft received Israel's [Genesis Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Genesis_Prize "2019 Genesis Prize"). At the ceremony in Jerusalem, he pledged $20 million to establish [Foundation to Combat Antisemitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundation_to_Combat_Antisemitism "Foundation to Combat Antisemitism") (rebranded as [Blue Square Alliance Against Hate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Square_Alliance_Against_Hate "Blue Square Alliance Against Hate")), which he pledged would combat the Palestinian-led [Boycott, Divestment, and Sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boycott,_Divestment_and_Sanctions "Boycott, Divestment and Sanctions") (BDS) movement against Israel, saying that it "and other efforts to delegitimize and undermine the State of Israel are the most disconcerting times for the country I love so much". Israeli Prime Minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Netanyahu "Benjamin Netanyahu") said, "Israel does not have a more loyal friend than Robert Kraft."

In April 2024, during the [Gaza war protests at Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_war_protests_at_Columbia_University "Gaza war protests at Columbia University"), Kraft stopped funding the university "over the treatment of Jewish students and faculty during pro-Palestinian protests at the campus", citing "virulent hate". According to Adam Saifer and Fahad Ahmad, Kraft is among "billionaire donors" who "have used their vast wealth—and the threat of withdrawing it—to demand a severe crackdown on student protesters expressing solidarity with Palestine" since October 2023.

Kraft led efforts to remove [Macklemore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macklemore "Macklemore") from [Ed Sheeran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Sheeran "Ed Sheeran")'s 2026 [Loop Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loop_Tour "Loop Tour") after he said "[free Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Palestine "Free Palestine")" onstage and performed "[Hind's Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hind%27s_Hall "Hind's Hall")", a song inspired by the [Gaza solidarity protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Solidarity_Encampment_(Columbia_University) "Gaza Solidarity Encampment (Columbia University)") at Columbia and the ["Hinds Hall" occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Solidarity_Encampment_(Columbia_University)#April_30,_2024:_"Hind's_Hall"_occupation_of_Hamilton_Hall,_subsequent_raid,_and_arrests "Gaza Solidarity Encampment (Columbia University)") of [Hamilton Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_Hall_(Columbia_University) "Hamilton Hall (Columbia University)"), renamed by protesters in honor of [Hind Rajab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Hind_Rajab "Killing of Hind Rajab"), a [Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian "Palestinian") girl in the [Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Strip "Gaza Strip") killed by the [Israel Defense Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Defense_Forces "Israel Defense Forces") (IDF) during the [Gaza war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_war "Gaza war"). Kraft called Sheeran to say he would not be allowed to perform at [Gillette Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillette_Stadium "Gillette Stadium"), which is owned by the Kraft Group, if Macklemore was not removed from the tour. Macklemore was subsequently dropped from the tour.

Hours after Macklemore posted a detailed account of his perspective on being removed from the tour on social media, Kraft released a statement in which he spoke of his long fight against antisemitism, agreed with Macklemore on the suffering of the Palestinians, evoked the responsibility of Hamas, invited Macklemore to meet, and said they had "peace for all people" as a common goal.

According to Philissa Cramer in *[Haaretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haaretz "Haaretz")*, "Kraft inherited the position as the most prominent Jewish billionaire supporting Israel in the United States" from [Sheldon Adelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheldon_Adelson "Sheldon Adelson"), though she contrasts his attitude with Adelson's position that "Palestinian is an invented identity, unworthy of recognition."

### US politics

Kraft is a member of the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)"), but has supported candidates of both major parties (Democrats and [Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States) "Republican Party (United States)")) financially. He was elected chairman of the [Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton,_Massachusetts "Newton, Massachusetts") Democratic City Committee when he was 27. Kraft considered running against Representative [Philip J. Philbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_J._Philbin "Philip J. Philbin") in 1970 but chose not to, citing the loss of privacy and strain on his family that politics would have caused. Kraft was further discouraged from entering politics by the 1970 suicide of his friend State Representative [H. James Shea Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._James_Shea_Jr. "H. James Shea Jr.")

Kraft has a friendship with [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") that dates to the 1990s. In 2017, he donated $1 million to Trump's [inauguration day celebrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump "First inauguration of Donald Trump"). Kraft had also donated to Trump's presidential campaign. In 2020, NBA coach [Gregg Popovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Popovich "Gregg Popovich") called Kraft "hypocritical" for supporting Trump while claiming to advocate for social justice. It was reported that Kraft had distanced himself from Trump after the [January 6, 2021 attack on the US Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_6_United_States_Capitol_attack "January 6 United States Capitol attack"). But in early 2025, reports emerged that the two had resumed speaking to each other and were once again being described by media as allies. In February 2025, Trump named Kraft's wife, Dana Blumberg, to the board of the [Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Center_for_the_Performing_Arts "Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts"). Blumberg was appointed to replace [Carlos Elizondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Elizondo "Carlos Elizondo") as part of a greater effort to purge board members and exert control over it.

Since 2017, Kraft has owned two [Boeing 767](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_767 "Boeing 767") private jets used by the New England Patriots. As of June 2022, one of these had been used for at least three flights deporting migrants from the US to Honduras. Neither Kraft nor the Patriots commented on this, but they later canceled their contract with the charter airline that operated those flights; in 2024, they signed a new charter contract with [Omni Air International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omni_Air_International "Omni Air International"), which also [operates deportation flights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omni_Air_International#Deportations_and_involvement_with_ICE "Omni Air International") on behalf of [ICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_and_Customs_Enforcement "Immigration and Customs Enforcement").

## Legal issues

In 2019, Kraft was among 25 people facing first-degree misdemeanor charges for soliciting prostitution at a day spa. His attorney electronically entered a not-guilty plea, and later submitted a court filing where Kraft waived arraignment, pled not guilty to all charges and requested a jury trial. A memo filed by Kraft's attorneys revealed that hidden cameras had been installed when investigators entered the facility under the guise of suspected [human trafficking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_trafficking "Human trafficking"). A Palm Beach County judge ruled that prosecutors could not use the videos in their case, citing privacy concerns. A Florida appeals court also ruled that Kraft's [constitutional rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_rights "Constitutional rights") were violated, and all the charges were dropped. U.S. District Judge [Rodolfo Ruiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodolfo_Ruiz "Rodolfo Ruiz") ordered the videos to be destroyed.

In 2026, it was revealed that Kraft had kept a relationship with [Jeffrey Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein "Jeffrey Epstein") and that Epstein had recommended Kraft use his lawyer to defeat the charges.
