# United Kingdom

The **United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland**, commonly known as the **United Kingdom** (**UK**) or **Britain**, is a country in [Northwestern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_Europe "Northwestern Europe"), off the coast of [the continental mainland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_mainland "European mainland"). It comprises [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England "England"), [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland "Scotland"), [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales "Wales") and [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland "Northern Ireland"), with a population of over 69 million in 2025. The UK includes the island of [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain "Great Britain"), the north-eastern part of the island of [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland "Ireland"), and most of [the smaller islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_the_United_Kingdom "List of islands of the United Kingdom") within the [British Isles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Isles "British Isles"), covering 94,354 square miles (244,376 km2). It shares [a land border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland%E2%80%93United_Kingdom_border "Republic of Ireland–United Kingdom border") with the [Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland "Republic of Ireland") and is surrounded by the [Atlantic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Ocean "Atlantic Ocean"), the [North Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea "North Sea"), the [English Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Channel "English Channel"), the [Celtic Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_Sea "Celtic Sea") and the [Irish Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Sea "Irish Sea"), while maintaining sovereignty over the [Crown Dependencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Dependencies "Crown Dependencies") and the [British Overseas Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Overseas_Territories "British Overseas Territories"). The capital and largest city of England and the UK is [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London"); [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh "Edinburgh"), [Cardiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff "Cardiff") and [Belfast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belfast "Belfast") are the national capitals of Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, respectively.

The British Isles have been continuously inhabited since the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic "Neolithic"). In AD 43 the [Roman conquest of Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_conquest_of_Britain "Roman conquest of Britain") began. The [Roman departure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_of_Roman_rule_in_Britain "End of Roman rule in Britain") between 383 and 410 was followed by [Anglo-Saxon settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_settlement_of_Britain "Anglo-Saxon settlement of Britain") beginning around 450. In 1066 the [Normans conquered England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Conquest "Norman Conquest"). Over the 17th century the role of the [British monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_monarchy "British monarchy") was reduced, particularly as a result of the [English Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Civil_War "English Civil War"). In 1707 the Kingdom of England and the [Kingdom of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Scotland "Kingdom of Scotland") united under the [Treaty of Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Union "Treaty of Union") to create the [Kingdom of Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain "Kingdom of Great Britain"). The [Acts of Union 1800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acts_of_Union_1800 "Acts of Union 1800") incorporated the [Kingdom of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Ireland "Kingdom of Ireland") to create the [United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland "United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland") in 1801. Most of Ireland [seceded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_Ireland "Partition of Ireland") from the UK in 1922 as the [Irish Free State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Free_State "Irish Free State"), and the [Royal and Parliamentary Titles Act 1927](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_and_Parliamentary_Titles_Act_1927 "Royal and Parliamentary Titles Act 1927") created the present United Kingdom.

The UK [became the first industrialised country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_industrialisation#Industrial_Revolution_in_Europe "History of industrialisation") and was the world's [foremost power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foremost_power "Foremost power") for the majority of the 19th and early 20th centuries, particularly during the *[Pax Britannica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pax_Britannica "Pax Britannica")* between 1815 and 1914. The [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire") was the leading [economic power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_power "Economic power") for most of the 19th century, a position supported by [its agricultural prosperity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Agricultural_Revolution "British Agricultural Revolution"), its role as a dominant [trading nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trading_nation "Trading nation"), a massive industrial capacity, [significant technological achievements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_British_innovations_and_discoveries_in_the_19th_century "List of British innovations and discoveries in the 19th century"), and the rise of [19th-century London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19th-century_London "19th-century London") as the world's principal financial centre. At its height in the 1920s, the empire encompassed around a quarter of the world's landmass and population, and was the [largest in history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_empires#Empires_at_their_greatest_extent "List of largest empires"). However, [its involvement in the First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_United_Kingdom_during_the_First_World_War "History of the United Kingdom during the First World War") and [in the Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_the_United_Kingdom_during_World_War_II "Military history of the United Kingdom during World War II") damaged [Britain's economic power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_British_Empire "Economy of the British Empire"), and a global wave of [decolonisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation "Decolonisation") led to the independence of most British colonies.

The UK is a [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy "Constitutional monarchy") and [parliamentary democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_democracy "Parliamentary democracy") with three distinct jurisdictions: [England and Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_law "English law"), [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scots_law "Scots law"), and [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Northern_Ireland "Law of Northern Ireland"). Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their own governments and parliaments which control various [devolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution_in_the_United_Kingdom "Devolution in the United Kingdom") matters. A [developed country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_country "Developed country") with [an advanced economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_United_Kingdom "Economy of the United Kingdom"), the UK ranks amongst the [largest economies by nominal GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(nominal) "List of countries by GDP (nominal)") and is one of the world's [largest exporters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_exports "List of countries by exports") and [importers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_imports "List of countries by imports"). As a [nuclear state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_state "Nuclear state") with one of the [highest defence budgets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_military_expenditures "List of countries by military expenditures"), the UK maintains [one of the strongest militaries in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Armed_Forces "British Armed Forces"). [British culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_the_United_Kingdom "Culture of the United Kingdom") is globally influential, notably in the [Anglosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglosphere "Anglosphere") and [the Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations "Commonwealth of Nations"); [its soft power influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicisation "Anglicisation") is observable in the legal and political systems of many [former colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_that_have_gained_independence_from_the_United_Kingdom "List of countries that have gained independence from the United Kingdom"), and in its exports [of language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English-speaking_world "English-speaking world"), [literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_literature "British literature"), [theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_of_the_United_Kingdom "Theatre of the United Kingdom"), [cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_the_United_Kingdom "Cinema of the United Kingdom"), [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_the_United_Kingdom "Music of the United Kingdom"), [media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media_in_the_United_Kingdom "Mass media in the United Kingdom"), [art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_of_the_United_Kingdom "Art of the United Kingdom") and [sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_the_United_Kingdom "Sport in the United Kingdom"). A [great power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_power "Great power"), the UK is part of [numerous international organisations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_the_United_Kingdom#International_organisations "Foreign relations of the United Kingdom").

## Etymology and terminology

The [Acts of Union 1707](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acts_of_Union_1707 "Acts of Union 1707") declared that the [Kingdom of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_England "Kingdom of England") and the [Kingdom of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Scotland "Kingdom of Scotland") were "United into One Kingdom by the Name of Great Britain". The term "United Kingdom" has occasionally been used for the former [Kingdom of Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain "Kingdom of Great Britain"), although its official name from 1707 to 1800 was simply "Great Britain". The [Acts of Union 1800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acts_of_Union_1800 "Acts of Union 1800") formed the [United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland "United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland"). Following the [partition of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_Ireland "Partition of Ireland") and the independence of the [Irish Free State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Free_State "Irish Free State") in 1922, which left [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland "Northern Ireland") as the only part of the island of Ireland within the United Kingdom, the name was changed in 1927 to the "United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland".

Although the United Kingdom is a sovereign country, England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland are widely referred to as [countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countries_of_the_United_Kingdom "Countries of the United Kingdom"). The UK Prime Minister's website has used the phrase "countries within a country" to describe it. Some statistical summaries, such as those for the twelve [NUTS 1 regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NUTS_of_the_United_Kingdom "NUTS of the United Kingdom"), refer to Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland as "regions". Northern Ireland is also referred to as a "province". With regard to Northern Ireland, the descriptive name used "can be controversial, with the choice often revealing one's political preferences".

The term "Great Britain" conventionally refers to the island of Great Britain, or politically to England, Scotland and Wales in combination. It is sometimes used as a loose synonym for the United Kingdom as a whole. The word *England* is occasionally used incorrectly to refer to the United Kingdom as a whole, a mistake principally made by people from outside the UK.

The term "Britain" is used as a synonym for [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain "Great Britain"), but also sometimes for the United Kingdom. Usage is mixed: the [style guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Style_guide "Style guide") of the [UK Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_United_Kingdom "Government of the United Kingdom") prefers the term "UK" rather than "Britain" or "British" (except when referring to embassies), while other government documents acknowledge that both terms refer to the United Kingdom and that elsewhere "British government" is used at least as frequently as "United Kingdom government". The UK [Permanent Committee on Geographical Names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Committee_on_Geographical_Names_for_British_Official_Use "Permanent Committee on Geographical Names for British Official Use") recognises "United Kingdom", "UK" and "U.K." as shortened and abbreviated geopolitical terms for the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland in its toponymic guidelines; it does not list "Britain" but notes that "it is only the one specific nominal term 'Great Britain' which invariably excludes Northern Ireland". The [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") historically preferred to use "Britain" as shorthand only for Great Britain, though the present style guide does not take a position except that "Great Britain" excludes Northern Ireland.

The adjective "British" is commonly used to refer to matters relating to the United Kingdom and is used in law to refer to United Kingdom citizenship and [nationality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_nationality_law "British nationality law"). People of the United Kingdom use several different terms to describe their national identity and may identify themselves as being [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_people "British people"), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_people "English people"), [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_people "Scottish people"), [Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_people "Welsh people"), [Northern Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_of_Northern_Ireland "People of Northern Ireland") or [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_people "Irish people"); or as having a combination of different national identities.

## History

### Prior to the Treaty of Union

Settlement by [Cro-Magnons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cro-Magnon "Cro-Magnon") of what is now the United Kingdom occurred in waves beginning by about 30,000 years ago. The island has been continuously inhabited since the last retreat of ice around 11,500 years ago. By the end of the [region's prehistoric period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_Britain "Prehistoric Britain"), the population is thought to have belonged largely to a culture termed [Insular Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insular_Celts "Insular Celts"), comprising [Brittonic Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britons_(historical) "Britons (historical)") and [Gaelic Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaelic_Ireland "Gaelic Ireland").

The [Roman conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_conquest_of_Britain "Roman conquest of Britain"), beginning in AD 43, and the 400-year [rule of southern Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Britain "Roman Britain"), was followed by an invasion by [Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_peoples "Germanic peoples") [Anglo-Saxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon "Anglo-Saxon") settlers, reducing the Brittonic area mainly [to what was to become Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales#Post-Roman_era "Wales"), [Cornwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornwall "Cornwall") and, until the latter stages of the Anglo-Saxon settlement, the [Hen Ogledd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hen_Ogledd "Hen Ogledd") (northern England and parts of southern Scotland). Most of the [region settled by the Anglo-Saxons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_England "Anglo-Saxon England") became unified as the Kingdom of England in the 10th century. [Gaelic speakers in north-west Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A1l_Riata "Dál Riata") (with connections to the north-east of Ireland and traditionally supposed to have migrated from there in the 5th century) united with the [Picts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picts "Picts") to create the [Kingdom of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Scotland "Kingdom of Scotland") in the 9th century.

In 1066, the [Normans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normans "Normans") invaded England from northern France. After [conquering England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_conquest_of_England "Norman conquest of England") they seized [large parts of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_invasion_of_Wales "Norman invasion of Wales"), [conquered much of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_invasion_of_Ireland "Norman invasion of Ireland") and were invited to settle in Scotland, bringing to each country [feudalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudalism "Feudalism") on the Northern French model and [Norman-French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman-French "Norman-French") culture. The [Anglo-Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Normans "Anglo-Normans") [ruling class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruling_class "Ruling class") greatly influenced, but eventually assimilated with, the local cultures. Subsequent [medieval English kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Plantagenet "House of Plantagenet") completed the [conquest of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_Wales "Conquest of Wales") and tried unsuccessfully [to annex Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wars_of_Scottish_Independence "Wars of Scottish Independence"). Asserting its independence in the 1320 [Declaration of Arbroath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Arbroath "Declaration of Arbroath"), Scotland maintained its independence thereafter, albeit in [near-constant conflict with England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Scottish_Wars "Anglo-Scottish Wars").

In 1215, [Magna Carta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_Carta "Magna Carta") was the first document to state that no government was above the law and that citizens have rights protecting them.

The English monarchs, through inheritance of [substantial territories in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angevin_Empire "Angevin Empire") and claims to the French crown, were also heavily involved in conflicts in France, most notably the [Hundred Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Years%27_War "Hundred Years' War"), while the [Kings of Scots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Scottish_monarchs "List of Scottish monarchs") were in [an alliance with the French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auld_Alliance "Auld Alliance") during this period. [Early modern Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_Britain "Early modern Britain") saw religious conflict resulting from the [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation "Reformation") and the introduction of [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant "Protestant") state churches in each country. The [English Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Reformation "English Reformation") ushered in political, constitutional, social and cultural change in the 16th century and [established](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_religion "State religion") the [Church of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_England "Church of England"). It defined a national identity for England and slowly, but profoundly, changed people's religious beliefs. Wales was [fully incorporated into the Kingdom of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laws_in_Wales_Acts_1535_and_1542 "Laws in Wales Acts 1535 and 1542"), and Ireland was constituted as a kingdom in personal union with the English crown. During the [Plantation of Ulster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantation_of_Ulster "Plantation of Ulster") in the 17th century, the lands of the Catholic Gaelic nobility were confiscated by the Crown and granted to Protestant settlers from England and Scotland.

The [Union of the Crowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_the_Crowns "Union of the Crowns") occurred in 1603 when [James VI of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_VI_of_Scotland "James VI of Scotland") inherited the thrones of England and Ireland, subsequently moving his court from Edinburgh to London. Although unified under a single monarch, the three realms remained entirely separate political entities, preserving their respective parliaments, judicial systems, and established churches.

By the mid-17th century, all three kingdoms [were involved in a series of connected wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wars_of_the_Three_Kingdoms "Wars of the Three Kingdoms") (including the [English Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Civil_War "English Civil War")) which led to the temporary overthrow of the monarchy, with [the execution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Execution_of_Charles_I "Execution of Charles I") of [King Charles I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_I_of_England "Charles I of England"), and the establishment of the short-lived [unitary republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_republic "Unitary republic") of the [Commonwealth of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_England "Commonwealth of England"), Scotland and Ireland.

Although the [monarchy was restored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restoration_(1660) "Restoration (1660)"), the [Interregnum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interregnum_(1649%E2%80%931660) "Interregnum (1649–1660)") along with the [Glorious Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glorious_Revolution "Glorious Revolution") of 1688 and the subsequent [Bill of Rights 1689](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_of_Rights_1689 "Bill of Rights 1689") in England and [Claim of Right Act 1689](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claim_of_Right_Act_1689 "Claim of Right Act 1689") in Scotland ensured that, unlike much of the rest of Europe, [royal absolutism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_absolutism "Royal absolutism") would not prevail, and a professed Catholic could never accede to the throne. The [British constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_constitution "British constitution") would develop on the basis of [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy "Constitutional monarchy") and the [parliamentary system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system "Parliamentary system"). With the founding of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") in 1660, science was greatly encouraged. During this period, particularly in England, the development of [naval power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_navy "English navy") and an interest in [voyages of discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery "Age of Discovery") led to the acquisition and settlement of overseas colonies, particularly in North America and the Caribbean.

Though previous attempts at uniting the two kingdoms within Great Britain in 1606, 1667 and 1689 had proved unsuccessful, the attempt initiated in 1705 led to the [Treaty of Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Union "Treaty of Union") of 1706 being agreed and ratified by both parliaments.

### Union of England and Scotland

On 1 May 1707 the Kingdom of Great Britain was formed, the result of the [Acts of Union 1707](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acts_of_Union_1707 "Acts of Union 1707") between the [Kingdom of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_England "Kingdom of England") and [Kingdom of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Scotland "Kingdom of Scotland"). In the 18th century cabinet government developed under [Robert Walpole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Walpole "Robert Walpole"), who is considered the *[de facto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_facto "De facto")* first [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom "Prime Minister of the United Kingdom") from 1721 to 1742. A series of [Jacobite uprisings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobite_uprisings "Jacobite uprisings") sought to remove the Protestant [House of Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Hanover "House of Hanover") from the throne and restore the Catholic [House of Stuart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Stuart "House of Stuart"). In 1746, the Jacobites were defeated at the [Battle of Culloden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Culloden "Battle of Culloden"), after which the [Scottish Highlanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Highlanders "Scottish Highlanders") were forcibly assimilated into Scotland by revoking the feudal independence of [clan chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_clan_chief "Scottish clan chief"). The British colonies in North America that broke away in the [American War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War "American Revolutionary War") became the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States"). British imperial ambition turned towards Asia, particularly to [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj").

British merchants played a leading part in the [Atlantic slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_slave_trade "Atlantic slave trade"), mainly between 1662 and 1807 when British or British-colonial [slave ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_ships "Slave ships") transported nearly 3.3 million slaves from Africa. The slaves were taken to [work on plantations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantation_economy "Plantation economy"), principally [in the Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_British_West_Indies "History of the British West Indies") but also [in North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_America "British America"). However, with pressure from the [abolitionist movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism_in_the_United_Kingdom "Abolitionism in the United Kingdom"), Parliament banned the trade in 1807, banned slavery in the British Empire in 1833, and Britain took a leading role in the movement to abolish slavery worldwide through the [blockade of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockade_of_Africa "Blockade of Africa") and pressing other nations to end their trade with a series of treaties.

### United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland

In 1800, the parliaments of Great Britain and Ireland each passed an Act of Union, uniting the two kingdoms and creating the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland on 1 January 1801.

After the defeat of France at the end of the [French Revolutionary Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolutionary_Wars "French Revolutionary Wars") and [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars "Napoleonic Wars") (1792–1815), the United Kingdom emerged as the principal naval and imperial power (with London the [largest city in the world from about 1830](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_cities_throughout_history "List of largest cities throughout history")). [Unchallenged at sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Royal_Navy_(after_1707)#Pax_Britannica,_1815–1914 "History of the Royal Navy (after 1707)"), British dominance was later described as the *[Pax Britannica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pax_Britannica "Pax Britannica")* ("British Peace"), a period of [relative peace amongst the great powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_relations_of_the_Great_Powers_(1814%E2%80%931919) "International relations of the Great Powers (1814–1919)") (1815–1914) during which the British Empire became the global [hegemon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegemon "Hegemon") and adopted the role of [global policeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_policeman "Global policeman"). From 1853 to 1856, Britain took part in the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War "Crimean War"), allied with the [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire "Ottoman Empire") against [Tsarist Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire"). Following the [Indian Rebellion of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857 "Indian Rebellion of 1857") the British government led by [Lord Palmerston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_John_Temple,_3rd_Viscount_Palmerston "Henry John Temple, 3rd Viscount Palmerston") assumed [direct rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_rule "Direct rule") over [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj"). Alongside the formal control it exerted over its own colonies, British dominance of much of world trade meant that it [effectively controlled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informal_empire "Informal empire") the economies of regions such as [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia") and [Latin America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_America "Latin America").

Throughout the [Victorian era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_era "Victorian era") (1837–1901), political attitudes favoured [free trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_trade "Free trade") and *[laissez-faire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laissez-faire "Laissez-faire")* policies. Beginning with the [Great Reform Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Reform_Act "Great Reform Act") in 1832, Parliament gradually [widened the voting franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_Acts "Reform Acts"), with the [1884 Reform Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_of_the_People_Act_1884 "Representation of the People Act 1884") championed by [William Gladstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Ewart_Gladstone "William Ewart Gladstone") granting [suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffrage "Suffrage") to a majority of males for the first time. The British population increased at a dramatic rate, accompanied by rapid [urbanisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanisation "Urbanisation"), causing significant social and economic stresses. By the late 19th century, [Conservative governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK) "Conservative Party (UK)") under [Benjamin Disraeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Disraeli "Benjamin Disraeli") and [Lord Salisbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Gascoyne-Cecil,_3rd_Marquess_of_Salisbury "Robert Gascoyne-Cecil, 3rd Marquess of Salisbury") accelerated [imperial expansion in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scramble_for_Africa "Scramble for Africa"), sustained a foreign policy of "[splendid isolation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Splendid_isolation "Splendid isolation")" from European alliances, and intensified strategic containment of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire") in [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia"), a competition known as the [Great Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Game "Great Game"). During this time, [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada "Canada"), [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia") and [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand "New Zealand") were granted self-governing [dominion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion "Dominion") status. At the turn of the 20th century, Britain's industrial dominance became challenged by the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire "German Empire") and the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States"). The [Edwardian era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwardian_era "Edwardian era") (1901–1910) saw [social reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_welfare_reforms "Liberal welfare reforms") and [home rule for Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Home_Rule_movement "Irish Home Rule movement") become important domestic issues, while the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_(UK) "Labour Party (UK)") emerged from an alliance of [trade unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_unions_in_the_United_Kingdom "Trade unions in the United Kingdom") and small socialist groups in 1900, and [suffragettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffragettes "Suffragettes") campaigned for women's right to vote.

### World wars and partition of Ireland

Britain was one of the principal [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I "Allies of World War I") that defeated the [Central Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Powers "Central Powers") in the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War "First World War") (1914–1918). Alongside their French, Russian and (after 1917) American counterparts, British armed forces were engaged across much of the British Empire and in several regions of Europe, particularly on the [Western Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Front_(World_War_I) "Western Front (World War I)"). The high fatalities of [trench warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trench_warfare "Trench warfare") caused the loss of much of a generation of men, with lasting social effects in the nation and a great disruption in the social order. Britain had suffered 2.5 million casualties and finished the war with a huge national debt.

The consequences of the war persuaded the government to expand the right to vote in national and local elections to all adult men and most adult women with the [Representation of the People Act 1918](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_of_the_People_Act_1918 "Representation of the People Act 1918"). After the war Britain became a permanent member of the Executive Council of the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations") and received [a mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations_mandate "League of Nations mandate") over a number of former German and [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire "Ottoman Empire") colonies. Under the leadership of [David Lloyd George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lloyd_George "David Lloyd George"), the British Empire reached its greatest extent, covering a fifth of the world's land surface and a quarter of its population.

By the mid-1920s, most of the British population could listen to [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") radio programmes. Experimental [television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_in_the_United_Kingdom "Television in the United Kingdom") broadcasts [began in 1929](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_television#United_Kingdom "History of television") and the [first scheduled BBC Television Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_day_of_BBC_television "First day of BBC television") commenced in 1936. The rise of [Irish nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_nationalism "Irish nationalism"), and disputes within Ireland over the terms of [Irish Home Rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Home_Rule "Irish Home Rule"), led eventually to the [partition of the island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_Ireland "Partition of Ireland") in 1921. [A period of conflict in what is now Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Troubles_in_Ulster_(1920%E2%80%931922) "The Troubles in Ulster (1920–1922)") occurred from June 1920 until June 1922. The [Irish Free State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Free_State "Irish Free State") became independent, initially with [Dominion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion "Dominion") status in 1922, and [unambiguously independent in 1931](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_Westminster_1931#Irish_Free_State "Statute of Westminster 1931"). Northern Ireland remained part of the United Kingdom. The [1928 Equal Franchise Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1928_Equal_Franchise_Act "1928 Equal Franchise Act") gave women electoral equality with men in national elections. Strikes in the mid-1920s culminated in the [General Strike of 1926](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Strike_of_1926 "General Strike of 1926"). Still suffering from the long-term economic effects of the First World War, Britain was severely impacted by the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression_in_the_United_Kingdom "Great Depression in the United Kingdom") (1929–1932), which intensified hardship in old industrial areas in traditional sectors, led to mass unemployment peaking near 3 million, and precipitated widespread political instability and social unrest. [A coalition government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Government_(1931) "National Government (1931)") was formed in 1931.

Nonetheless, Britain was described as "a very wealthy country, formidable in arms, ruthless in pursuit of its interests and sitting at the heart of a global production system." After [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany")'s [invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland "Invasion of Poland") in 1939, Britain entered the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War "Second World War"). Despite the defeat of its European allies in the first year, Britain and its empire continued the war against Germany.

In 1940, the [Royal Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Air_Force "Royal Air Force") successfully defended British airspace by repelling the German *[Luftwaffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luftwaffe "Luftwaffe")* in the [Battle of Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Britain "Battle of Britain"). Urban areas suffered heavy bombing during [the Blitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Blitz "The Blitz"). The [Grand Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Alliance_(World_War_II) "Grand Alliance (World War II)") of Britain, the United States and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") formed in 1941, leading the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II "Allies of World War II") against the [Axis powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers "Axis powers"). There were eventual hard-fought victories in the [Battle of the Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Atlantic "Battle of the Atlantic"), the [North Africa campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Africa_campaign "North Africa campaign") and the [Italian campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_campaign_(World_War_II) "Italian campaign (World War II)"). British forces played important roles in the [Normandy landings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normandy_landings "Normandy landings") of 1944 and the [liberation of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_Europe "Liberation of Europe"). The British Army led the [Burma campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma_campaign "Burma campaign") against Japan, and the [British Pacific Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Pacific_Fleet "British Pacific Fleet") fought Japan at sea. [British scientists contributed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_contribution_to_the_Manhattan_Project "British contribution to the Manhattan Project") to the American [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project "Manhattan Project"), whose task was to build a [nuclear weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon "Nuclear weapon").

### Post-war 20th century

The United Kingdom was one of the [Big Three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Three_(World_War_II) "Big Three (World War II)") powers (with the United States and the Soviet Union) who met to plan the [post-war world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aftermath_of_World_War_II "Aftermath of World War II"); it drafted the [Declaration by United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_by_United_Nations "Declaration by United Nations") with the United States and became one of the five permanent members of the [United Nations Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council "United Nations Security Council"). It worked closely with the United States to establish the [International Monetary Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund "International Monetary Fund"), the [World Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank "World Bank") and [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO"). The war left the UK severely weakened and financially dependent on the American [Marshall Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Plan "Marshall Plan"), but it was spared the total war that devastated [eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe "Eastern Europe").

In the immediate post-war years the [Labour government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Government_1945%E2%80%931951 "Labour Government 1945–1951") under [Clement Attlee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clement_Attlee "Clement Attlee") initiated a radical programme of reforms, which significantly affected British society [in the following decades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-war_consensus "Post-war consensus"). Major industries and public utilities were [nationalised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalised "Nationalised"), a [welfare state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_state "Welfare state") was established, and a comprehensive, publicly funded healthcare system, the [National Health Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Health_Service "National Health Service"), was created. The rise of nationalism in the colonies coincided with Britain's much-diminished economic position after [its involvement in the First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_United_Kingdom_during_the_First_World_War "History of the United Kingdom during the First World War") and [in the Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_the_United_Kingdom_during_World_War_II "Military history of the United Kingdom during World War II"), so that a policy of [decolonisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation "Decolonisation") was unavoidable. Independence was granted to India and Pakistan in 1947. Over the next three decades, most colonies of the British Empire gained their independence, and many became members of the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations "Commonwealth of Nations").

The UK was the third country to develop [a nuclear weapons arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapons_and_the_United_Kingdom "Nuclear weapons and the United Kingdom"), with its first atomic bomb test, [Operation Hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Hurricane "Operation Hurricane"), in 1952, although the post-war limitations of Britain's international role were illustrated by the [Suez Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Crisis "Suez Crisis") of 1956. As a result of a shortage of workers in the 1950s, the government [encouraged immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_immigration_to_the_United_Kingdom "Modern immigration to the United Kingdom") from [Commonwealth countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_countries "Commonwealth countries"). In the following decades the UK became a more [multiracial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiracialism "Multiracialism") and [multicultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiculturalism "Multiculturalism") society. Despite rising living standards in the late 1950s and 1960s, the United Kingdom's economic performance was less successful than many of its main competitors such as France, [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany "West Germany") and Japan. In 1969, the United Kingdom was the first democratic nation to [lower its voting age to 18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_of_the_People_Act_1969 "Representation of the People Act 1969").

In the decades-long process of [European integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_integration "European integration") the UK was a founding member of the [Western European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_European_Union "Western European Union"), established with the [London and Paris Conferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_and_Paris_Conferences "London and Paris Conferences") in 1954. In 1960 the UK was one of the seven founding members of the [European Free Trade Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Free_Trade_Association "European Free Trade Association") (EFTA), but in 1973 it left to join the [European Communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Communities "European Communities") (EC). In a [1975 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_United_Kingdom_European_Communities_membership_referendum "1975 United Kingdom European Communities membership referendum") 67 per cent voted to stay in it. When the EC became the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union") (EU) in 1993, the UK was one of the 12 founding member states.

From the late 1960s, Northern Ireland experienced communal and [paramilitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramilitary "Paramilitary") violence, sometimes affecting other parts of the UK, known as [the Troubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Troubles "The Troubles"). It is usually considered to have ended with the 1998 [Belfast "Good Friday" Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belfast_Agreement "Belfast Agreement"). Following a period of widespread economic slowdown and industrial strife in the 1970s, the [Conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK) "Conservative Party (UK)") government of the 1980s led by [Margaret Thatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Thatcher "Margaret Thatcher") initiated a radical policy of [monetarism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monetarism "Monetarism"), [deregulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deregulation "Deregulation"), particularly of the financial sector (for example, the [Big Bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Bang_(financial_markets) "Big Bang (financial markets)") in 1986) and labour markets, the sale of state-owned companies (privatisation), and the withdrawal of subsidies to others.

In 1982, [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina "Argentina") invaded the British territories of [South Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Georgia_and_the_South_Sandwich_Islands "South Georgia and the South Sandwich Islands") and the [Falkland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkland_Islands "Falkland Islands"), leading to the 10-week [Falklands War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_War "Falklands War") in which Argentine forces were defeated. The inhabitants of the islands strongly favour British sovereignty, expressed in a [2013 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Falkland_Islands_sovereignty_referendum "2013 Falkland Islands sovereignty referendum"). From 1984 the British economy was helped by the inflow of substantial [North Sea oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea_oil "North Sea oil") revenues. Another British Overseas Territory, [Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar "Gibraltar"), is [a key military base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Gibraltar "Port of Gibraltar"); a [referendum in 2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Gibraltar_sovereignty_referendum "2002 Gibraltar sovereignty referendum") on shared sovereignty with Spain was rejected.

By the late 20th century, major changes were made to the governance of the UK with the establishment of [devolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution "Devolution") administrations for Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. [The statutory incorporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Act_1998 "Human Rights Act 1998") followed acceptance of the [European Convention on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Convention_on_Human_Rights "European Convention on Human Rights"). The UK remained a [great power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_modern_great_powers#United_Kingdom "List of modern great powers") with global diplomatic and military influence and a leading role in the United Nations and NATO.

### 21st century

The United Kingdom broadly supported the United States' approach to the "[war on terror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_on_terror "War on terror")" in the early 21st century. British troops fought in the [war in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Afghanistan_(2001%E2%80%932021) "War in Afghanistan (2001–2021)"), but controversy surrounded [Britain's military deployment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_involvement_in_the_Iraq_War "British involvement in the Iraq War") in [Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_War "Iraq War"), which saw the [largest protest in British history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15_February_2003_Iraq_War_protests "15 February 2003 Iraq War protests") in opposition to the government led by [Tony Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Blair "Tony Blair").

The [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession "Great Recession") (2007–2010) severely affected the British economy, and was followed by a period of weak growth and stagnation. The [Cameron–Clegg coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron%E2%80%93Clegg_coalition "Cameron–Clegg coalition") government of 2010 introduced [austerity measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_government_austerity_programme "United Kingdom government austerity programme") intended to tackle the substantial public deficits. [A referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Scottish_independence_referendum "2014 Scottish independence referendum") on [Scottish independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_independence "Scottish independence") in 2014 resulted in the Scottish electorate voting by 55.3 to 44.7 per cent to remain part of the United Kingdom.

In 2016, 51.9 per cent of voters in the UK [voted to leave the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_Kingdom_European_Union_membership_referendum "2016 United Kingdom European Union membership referendum") (EU). The [UK left the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brexit "Brexit") in 2020. On 1 May 2021 the [EU–UK Trade and Cooperation Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU%E2%80%93UK_Trade_and_Cooperation_Agreement "EU–UK Trade and Cooperation Agreement"), a free trade agreement between the UK and the EU, came into force.

The [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_United_Kingdom "COVID-19 pandemic in the United Kingdom") had a severe [impact on the British economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_United_Kingdom "Economic impact of the COVID-19 pandemic in the United Kingdom"), caused major [disruptions to education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_on_education_in_the_United_Kingdom "Impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on education in the United Kingdom") and had [far-reaching impacts on society and politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_United_Kingdom "Social impact of the COVID-19 pandemic in the United Kingdom") in 2020 and 2021. The United Kingdom was the first country in the world to use an approved [COVID-19 vaccine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_vaccine "COVID-19 vaccine"). Developing a [vaccine in the UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford%E2%80%93AstraZeneca_COVID-19_vaccine "Oxford–AstraZeneca COVID-19 vaccine") allowed the rollout to be amongst the fastest in the world.

## Geography

The total area of the United Kingdom is approximately 94,354 square miles (244,376 km2), with a land area of 93,723 square miles (242,741 km2). It occupies the major part of the [British Isles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Isles "British Isles") and includes the island of Great Britain, the north-eastern one-sixth of the island of Ireland and [some smaller surrounding islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_the_United_Kingdom "List of islands of the United Kingdom"), meaning it comprises [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England "England"), [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland "Scotland"), [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales "Wales") and [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland "Northern Ireland").

Geographically, the United Kingdom lies between the [North Atlantic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Ocean "North Atlantic Ocean") and the [North Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea "North Sea") with the southeast coast coming within 22 miles (35 km) of the coast of northern France, from which it is separated by the [English Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Channel "English Channel").

The nearby island polities of the [Isle of Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Man "Isle of Man"), [Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jersey "Jersey") and [Guernsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guernsey "Guernsey") are [Crown Dependencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Dependencies "Crown Dependencies"), in union with the [British monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_the_United_Kingdom "Monarchy of the United Kingdom"), but do not strictly form part of the United Kingdom or any of its three jurisdictions or four countries, although the British government retains responsibility for their external affairs. The Isle of Man lies roughly midway between Great Britain and the [island of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland "Ireland") in the [Irish Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Sea "Irish Sea"), while the Channel Islands lie just off the northern French coast.

The [Royal Greenwich Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Observatory,_Greenwich "Royal Observatory, Greenwich") in London was chosen as the defining point of the [Prime Meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Meridian "Prime Meridian") at the [International Meridian Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Meridian_Conference "International Meridian Conference") in 1884.

The UK lies between latitudes [49°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/49th_parallel_north "49th parallel north") and [61° N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/61st_parallel_north "61st parallel north"), and longitudes [9° W](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9th_meridian_west "9th meridian west") and [2° E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_meridian_east "2nd meridian east"). Northern Ireland shares a 310-mile (499 km) land boundary with the Republic of Ireland and has a 404-mile (650 km) coastline. The length of coastline of Great Britain plus its principal islands is about 19,491 miles (31,368 km) long, with the coastline of the main island Great Britain being 11,073 miles (17,820 km) of that, though measurements can vary greatly due to the [coastline paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastline_paradox "Coastline paradox"). It is connected to continental Europe by the [Channel Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_Tunnel "Channel Tunnel"), which at 31 miles (50 km) (24 miles (38 km) underwater) is the longest underwater tunnel in the world.

The UK contains four terrestrial [ecoregions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecoregion "Ecoregion"): [Celtic broadleaf forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_broadleaf_forests "Celtic broadleaf forests"), [English Lowlands beech forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Lowlands_beech_forests "English Lowlands beech forests"), [North Atlantic moist mixed forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_moist_mixed_forests "North Atlantic moist mixed forests"), and [Caledonian conifer forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caledonian_Forest "Caledonian Forest"). The area of woodland in the UK was estimated to be 3.25 million hectares in 2023, which represents 13 per cent of its land area.

### Climate

Most of the United Kingdom has a temperate climate, with generally cool temperatures and plentiful rainfall all year round. The temperature varies with the seasons rarely dropping below 0 [°C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsius "Celsius") (32 [°F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahrenheit "Fahrenheit")) or rising above 30 °C (86 °F). Some parts, away from the coast, of upland England, Wales, Northern Ireland and most of Scotland, experience a [subpolar oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subpolar_oceanic_climate "Subpolar oceanic climate"). Higher elevations in Scotland experience a [continental subarctic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarctic_climate "Subarctic climate") and the mountains experience a [tundra climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra_climate "Tundra climate").

The prevailing wind is from the southwest and bears frequent spells of mild and wet weather from the Atlantic Ocean, although the eastern parts are mostly sheltered from this wind. Since the majority of the rain falls over the western regions, the eastern parts are the driest. Atlantic currents, warmed by the [Gulf Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Stream "Gulf Stream"), bring mild winters, especially in the west where winters are wet and even more so over high ground. Summers are warmest in the southeast of England and coolest in the north. Heavy snowfall can occur in winter and early spring on high ground, and occasionally settles to great depth away from the hills.

The average total annual sunshine in the United Kingdom was 1,339.7 hours between 1971 and 2000. The hours of sunshine vary from 1,200 to about 1,580 hours per year.

Climate change has serious impacts on the country. A third of food price rise in 2023 was attributed to it. In 2024 the United Kingdom ranked 5th out of 180 countries in the [Environmental Performance Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_Performance_Index "Environmental Performance Index"). A law has been passed that [UK greenhouse gas emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions_by_the_United_Kingdom "Greenhouse gas emissions by the United Kingdom") will be [net zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_zero "Net zero") by 2050.

### Topography

[England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_England "Geography of England") accounts for 53 per cent of the UK, covering 50,350 square miles (130,395 km2). Most of the country consists of lowland terrain, with upland and mountainous terrain northwest of the [Tees–Exe line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tees%E2%80%93Exe_line "Tees–Exe line") which roughly divides the UK into [lowland and upland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowland_and_upland "Lowland and upland") areas. Lowland areas include [Cornwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornwall "Cornwall"), the [New Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Forest "New Forest"), the [South Downs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Downs "South Downs") and the [Norfolk Broads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Broads "The Broads"). Upland areas include the [Lake District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_District "Lake District"), the [Pennines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennines "Pennines"), the [Yorkshire Dales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire_Dales "Yorkshire Dales"), [Exmoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exmoor "Exmoor") and [Dartmoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmoor "Dartmoor"). The main rivers and estuaries are the [Thames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Thames "River Thames"), [Severn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Severn "River Severn"), and the [Humber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humber "Humber"). England's highest mountain is [Scafell Pike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scafell_Pike "Scafell Pike"), at 978 metres (3,209 ft) in the Lake District; its largest island is the [Isle of Wight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Wight "Isle of Wight").

[Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Scotland "Geography of Scotland") accounts for 32 per cent of the UK, covering 30,410 square miles (78,772 km2). This includes [nearly 800 islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Scotland "List of islands of Scotland"), notably the [Hebrides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrides "Hebrides"), [Orkney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orkney "Orkney") Islands and [Shetland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shetland_Islands "Shetland Islands"). Scotland is the most mountainous constituent country of the UK. The [Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Highlands "Scottish Highlands") to the north and west are the more rugged region containing the majority of Scotland's mountainous land, including the [Cairngorms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairngorms "Cairngorms"), [Loch Lomond and The Trossachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loch_Lomond_and_The_Trossachs "Loch Lomond and The Trossachs") and [Ben Nevis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Nevis "Ben Nevis") which at 1,345 metres (4,413 ft) is the highest point in the British Isles.

[Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Wales "Geography of Wales") accounts for less than 9 per cent of the UK, covering 8,020 square miles (20,779 km2). It is mostly mountainous, though [South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Wales "South Wales") is less mountainous than [North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Wales "North Wales") and [Mid Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid_Wales "Mid Wales"). The highest mountains in Wales are in [Snowdonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowdonia "Snowdonia") and include [Snowdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowdon "Snowdon") ([Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language "Welsh language"): *Yr Wyddfa*) which, at 1,085 metres (3,560 ft), is the highest peak in Wales. Wales has over 1,680 miles (2,704 kilometres) of coastline including the [Pembrokeshire Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pembrokeshire_Coast "Pembrokeshire Coast"). Several islands lie off the Welsh mainland, the largest of which is [Anglesey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglesey "Anglesey") (*Ynys Môn*).

[Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Ireland "Geography of Ireland"), separated from Great Britain by the [Irish Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Sea "Irish Sea") and [North Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Channel_(Great_Britain_and_Ireland) "North Channel (Great Britain and Ireland)"), has an area of 5,470 square miles (14,160 km2) and is mostly hilly. It includes [Lough Neagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lough_Neagh "Lough Neagh") which, at 150 square miles (388 km2), is the largest lake in the British Isles by area, [Lough Erne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lough_Erne "Lough Erne"), which has over 150 islands, and the [Giant's Causeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant%27s_Causeway "Giant's Causeway"), which is listed by [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO") as a [World Heritage Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Site "World Heritage Site"). The highest peak in Northern Ireland is [Slieve Donard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slieve_Donard "Slieve Donard") in the [Mourne Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mourne_Mountains "Mourne Mountains") at 852 metres (2,795 ft).

## Government and politics

The United Kingdom is a [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy "Constitutional monarchy") and a [parliamentary democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_democracy "Parliamentary democracy") operating under the [Westminster system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_system "Westminster system"), otherwise known as a "democratic parliamentary monarchy". It is a [centralised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralized_government "Centralized government"), [unitary state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state "Unitary state") wherein the [Parliament of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_the_United_Kingdom "Parliament of the United Kingdom") is [sovereign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_sovereignty_in_the_United_Kingdom "Parliamentary sovereignty in the United Kingdom"). Parliament is made up of the elected [House of Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_the_United_Kingdom "House of Commons of the United Kingdom"), the appointed [House of Lords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Lords "House of Lords") and [the Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crown "The Crown") (as personified by [the monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_the_United_Kingdom "Monarchy of the United Kingdom")). The main business of Parliament takes place in the two houses, but [royal assent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_assent "Royal assent") is required for a bill to become an [act of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Parliament_(UK) "Act of Parliament (UK)") (that is, [statute law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_law "Statute law")). As a result of parliamentary sovereignty, the [British constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_United_Kingdom "Constitution of the United Kingdom") is [uncodified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncodified_constitution "Uncodified constitution"), consisting mostly of disparate written sources, including parliamentary [statutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute "Statute"), judge-made [case law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_law "Case law") and international treaties, together with [constitutional conventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Constitution#Conventions "British Constitution"). Nevertheless, the [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_United_Kingdom "Supreme Court of the United Kingdom") recognises a number of principles underlying the British constitution, such as parliamentary sovereignty, the [rule of law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_law "Rule of law"), [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy "Democracy") and upholding [international law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internationalism_(politics) "Internationalism (politics)").

[King Charles III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_III "Charles III") is the [monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarch "Monarch") and [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state "Head of state") of the United Kingdom and 14 other independent [sovereign states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_state "Sovereign state"), referred to as "[Commonwealth realms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_realm "Commonwealth realm")". The monarch is formally vested with all executive authority as the personal embodiment of [the Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crown "The Crown") and is "fundamental to the law and working of government in the UK". The disposition of such powers however, including those belonging to the [royal prerogative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_prerogative_in_the_United_Kingdom "Royal prerogative in the United Kingdom"), is generally exercised only on the [advice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advice_(constitutional_law) "Advice (constitutional law)") of [ministers of the Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministers_of_the_Crown "Ministers of the Crown") responsible to Parliament and thence to the electorate. Nevertheless, in the performance of official duties the monarch has "the right to be consulted, the right to encourage, and the right to warn". In addition the monarch has a number of [reserve powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_power#United_Kingdom "Reserve power") at his disposal to uphold [responsible government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Responsible_government "Responsible government") and prevent [constitutional crises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_crisis "Constitutional crisis").

The prime minister is the [head of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_government "Head of government") in the United Kingdom. Acting under the direction and supervision of a [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_United_Kingdom "Cabinet of the United Kingdom") of senior ministers selected and led by the prime minister, [His Majesty's Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/His_Majesty%27s_Government "His Majesty's Government") serves as the principal instrument for public policymaking, administers public services and, through the [Privy Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom "Privy Council of the United Kingdom"), promulgates [statutory instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statutory_instrument_(UK) "Statutory instrument (UK)") and tenders advice to the monarch. Nearly all prime ministers have served concurrently as [First Lord of the Treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Lord_of_the_Treasury "First Lord of the Treasury") and all prime ministers have continuously served as First Lord of the Treasury since 1905, [Minister for the Civil Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_the_Civil_Service "Minister for the Civil Service") since 1968, and [Minister for the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_the_Union "Minister for the Union") since 2019. While appointed by the monarch, in modern times the prime minister is, by [convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_conventions_of_the_United_Kingdom "Constitutional conventions of the United Kingdom"), an MP, the leader of the political party with the most seats in the House of Commons, and holds office by virtue of their ability to [command the confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence_motions_in_the_United_Kingdom "Confidence motions in the United Kingdom") of the House of Commons. The prime minister as of 20 July 2026 is [Andy Burnham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Burnham "Andy Burnham"), the [leader of the Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Labour_Party_(UK) "Leader of the Labour Party (UK)").

Although not part of the United Kingdom, the three [Crown Dependencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Dependencies "Crown Dependencies") of [Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jersey "Jersey"), [Guernsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guernsey "Guernsey") and the [Isle of Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Man "Isle of Man"), as well as the 14 [British Overseas Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Overseas_Territories "British Overseas Territories"), are subject to the sovereignty of the British Crown.

### Elections

For [general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_elections_in_the_United_Kingdom "General elections in the United Kingdom") (elections to the House of Commons), the United Kingdom is divided into 650 [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_constituencies "United Kingdom constituencies"), each of which is represented by one [member of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament_(United_Kingdom) "Member of Parliament (United Kingdom)") (MP) elected by the [first-past-the-post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-past-the-post_voting "First-past-the-post voting") system. MPs hold office for up to five years and must then stand for re-election if they wish to continue to be an MP. The [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK) "Conservative Party (UK)"), colloquially known as the Tory Party or the Tories, and the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_(UK) "Labour Party (UK)") have been the dominant political parties in the country since the 1920s, leading to the UK being described as a [two-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-party_system "Two-party system"). However, since the 1920s other [political parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_the_United_Kingdom "List of political parties in the United Kingdom") have won seats in the House of Commons, although never more than the Conservatives or Labour. The 2020s marked the erosion of the two-party system and the growth of a multi-party system.

### Administrative divisions

The geographical division of the United Kingdom into counties or [shires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shire "Shire") began in England and Scotland in the [early Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Middle_Ages "Early Middle Ages"), and was completed throughout Great Britain and Ireland by the [early modern period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_period "Early modern period"). Modern [local government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government "Local government") by elected councils, partly based on the ancient counties, was established by separate Acts of Parliament: in England and Wales [in 1888](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1888 "Local Government Act 1888"), Scotland [in 1889](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_(Scotland)_Act_1889 "Local Government (Scotland) Act 1889") and Ireland [in 1898](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_(Ireland)_Act_1898 "Local Government (Ireland) Act 1898"), meaning there is no consistent system of administrative or geographic demarcation across the UK, and [England and Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_law "English law"), [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scots_law "Scots law") and [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Northern_Ireland "Law of Northern Ireland") each have their own distinct jurisdictions. Until the 19th century there was little change to those arrangements, but there has since been a constant evolution of role and function.

[Local government in England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_England "Local government in England") is complex, with the distribution of functions varying according to local arrangements. The upper-tier [subdivisions of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_England "Subdivisions of England") are the nine [regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_England "Regions of England"), used primarily for statistical purposes. One of the regions, [Greater London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_London_Authority "Greater London Authority"), has had a directly elected assembly and mayor since 2000 following popular support for the proposal in a [1998 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Greater_London_Authority_referendum "1998 Greater London Authority referendum").

[Local government in Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Scotland "Local government in Scotland") is divided into [32 council areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Scotland "Subdivisions of Scotland") with a wide variation in size and population. The cities of [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow "Glasgow"), Edinburgh, [Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen "Aberdeen") and [Dundee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dundee "Dundee") are separate council areas, as is the [Highland Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_the_Highland_council_area "Politics of the Highland council area"), which includes a third of Scotland's area but only just over 200,000 people. Local councils are made up of elected councillors, of whom there are 1,223.

[Local government in Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Wales "Local government in Wales") consists of 22 [unitary authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authority "Unitary authority"), each led by a leader and cabinet elected by the council itself. These include the cities of Cardiff, [Swansea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swansea "Swansea") and [Newport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport,_Wales "Newport, Wales"), which are unitary authorities in their own right. Elections are held every four years under the first-past-the-post system.

[Local government in Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Northern_Ireland "Local government in Northern Ireland") since 2015 has been organised into 11 district councils, each elected by single transferable vote. Their powers are limited to services such as waste collection, dog control, and maintaining parks and cemeteries. This system was introduced after a 2008 decision by the executive to replace the previous structure of 26 districts which had been in place since 1973.

### Devolution

In the United Kingdom a process of devolution has transferred various powers from the UK Government to three of the four UK countries—Scotland, Northern Ireland and Wales, which since 1999, have their own governments and parliaments that control various devolved matters. These powers vary and have been moved to the [Scottish Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Government "Scottish Government"), the [Welsh Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Government "Welsh Government"), the [Northern Ireland Executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Executive "Northern Ireland Executive") and (in England) to [strategic authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_authorities "Strategic authorities"): the [Greater London Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_London_Authority "Greater London Authority"), [combined authorities, and combined county authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_authorities_and_combined_county_authorities "Combined authorities and combined county authorities"). Amongst the devolved parliaments across the United Kingdom, the [Scottish Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Parliament "Scottish Parliament") has the most extensive responsibilities for [devolved powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolved,_reserved_and_excepted_matters "Devolved, reserved and excepted matters"), and has been described as "one of the most powerful devolved parliaments in the world".

The United Kingdom has an [uncodified constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncodified_constitution "Uncodified constitution") and constitutional matters are not amongst the powers that have been devolved. Under the doctrine of [parliamentary sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_sovereignty_in_the_United_Kingdom "Parliamentary sovereignty in the United Kingdom"), the UK Parliament could, in theory, therefore, abolish the Scottish Parliament, Senedd or Northern Ireland Assembly. Though in the [Scotland Act 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_Act_2016 "Scotland Act 2016") and the [Wales Act 2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_Act_2017 "Wales Act 2017") it states that the Scottish Government and the Welsh Government "are a permanent part of the United Kingdom's constitutional arrangements".

In practice it would be politically difficult for the UK Parliament to abolish devolution to the Scottish Parliament and the Senedd because these institutions were created by referendums. The political constraints placed upon the UK Parliament's power to interfere with devolution in Northern Ireland are greater still, because devolution in Northern Ireland rests upon an international agreement with the [Government of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Ireland "Government of Ireland"). The UK Parliament restricts the three devolved parliaments' legislative powers in economic policy matters through [an act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_Internal_Market_Act_2020 "United Kingdom Internal Market Act 2020") passed in 2020.

#### England

Unlike Scotland, Northern Ireland and Wales, England does not have a separate devolved government or national parliament, rather a process of devolution of powers from the central government to local authorities has taken place, first in 1998. The [Greater London Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_London_Authority "Greater London Authority") was set up following a [referendum in 1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Greater_London_Authority_referendum "1998 Greater London Authority referendum"). Colloquially known as City Hall, it is the devolved regional government body for Greater London. It consists of two political branches: a directly elected [Mayor of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_London "Mayor of London") and the [London Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Assembly "London Assembly"), which serves as a check and balance on the Mayor.

A [combined authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_authorities_and_combined_county_authorities "Combined authorities and combined county authorities") is a type of local government institution introduced in England outside Greater London by the [Local Democracy, Economic Development and Construction Act 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Democracy,_Economic_Development_and_Construction_Act_2009 "Local Democracy, Economic Development and Construction Act 2009"). CAs allow a group of local authorities to pool appropriate responsibility and receive certain devolved functions from central government to deliver transport and economic policy more effectively over a wider area. A combined county authority is a similar type of local-government institution introduced in England outside Greater London by the [Levelling-up and Regeneration Act 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levelling-up_and_Regeneration_Act_2023 "Levelling-up and Regeneration Act 2023"), but may only be formed by upper-tier authorities: [county councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_council "County council") and [unitary authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authority "Unitary authority").

#### Scotland

Since 1999 Scotland has had a devolved national government and parliament with wide-ranging powers over any matter that has not been specifically [reserved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserved_and_excepted_matters "Reserved and excepted matters") to the UK Parliament. Scotland has the most devolved powers of any of the three devolved parliaments in the United Kingdom, with full legislative control over [education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Scotland "Education in Scotland"), [law and order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scots_law "Scots law"), [the economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Scotland "Economy of Scotland"), [healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Scotland "Health in Scotland"), [elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Scotland "Elections in Scotland"), [Crown Estate Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Estate_Scotland "Crown Estate Scotland"), the [planning system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planning_system_in_Scotland "Planning system in Scotland") and [housing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Housing_in_Scotland "Housing in Scotland").

Additional powers were transferred to the Scottish Parliament via the [Scotland Act 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_Act_2012 "Scotland Act 2012") and the [Scotland Act 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_Act_2016 "Scotland Act 2016"), such as [some taxation powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation_in_Scotland "Taxation in Scotland"), including full control of [income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_tax_in_Scotland "Income tax in Scotland") on income earned through employment, [Land and Buildings Transaction Tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_and_Buildings_Transaction_Tax "Land and Buildings Transaction Tax"), [Landfill Tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Landfill_Tax "Scottish Landfill Tax"), Aggregates Levy, [Air Departure Tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Departure_Tax "Air Departure Tax") and [Revenue Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenue_Scotland "Revenue Scotland"), as well as aspects of the [energy network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_Scotland "Energy in Scotland"), including [renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_in_Scotland "Renewable energy in Scotland"), energy efficiency and onshore [oil and gas licensing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_industry_in_Scotland "Oil industry in Scotland"). Their power over economic issues is significantly constrained by an [act of the UK Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_Internal_Market_Act_2020 "United Kingdom Internal Market Act 2020") passed in 2020.

The [Scottish Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Government "Scottish Government") is a [Scottish National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_National_Party "Scottish National Party") (SNP) [minority government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_government "Minority government"), led by the [first minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Minister_of_Scotland "First Minister of Scotland"), currently [John Swinney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Swinney "John Swinney"), the [leader of the SNP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_SNP "Leader of the SNP"). In 2014 the [Scottish independence referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Scottish_independence_referendum "2014 Scottish independence referendum") was held, with 55.3 per cent voting against independence from the United Kingdom and 44.7 per cent voting in favour, resulting in Scotland staying within the United Kingdom. [Local government in Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Scotland "Local government in Scotland") is divided into [32 council areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Scotland "Subdivisions of Scotland") with a wide variation in size and population. Local councils are made up of elected councillors, of whom there are 1,223.

The Scottish Parliament is separate from the Scottish Government. It is made up of 129 elected [members of the Scottish Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Members_of_the_Scottish_Parliament "Members of the Scottish Parliament") (MSPs). It is the law-making body of Scotland, and thus it scrutinises the work of the incumbent Scottish Government and considers any piece of proposed legislation through parliamentary debates, committees and parliamentary questions.

#### Wales

Since 1999 Wales has had a devolved national government and legislature, known as the [Senedd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senedd "Senedd"). Elections to the Senedd used the [additional member system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Additional_member_system "Additional member system") until 2026 when the voting system was changed to [closed list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_list "Closed list") proportional representation. The number of elected members also increased from 60 to 96, representing 16 [six-member constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senedd_constituencies "Senedd constituencies"). The Senedd has more limited powers than those devolved to Scotland. It can legislate on any matter not specifically reserved to the UK Parliament by [Acts of Senedd Cymru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acts_of_Senedd_Cymru "Acts of Senedd Cymru"). The [Welsh Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Government "Welsh Government") is currently a [Plaid Cymru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaid_Cymru "Plaid Cymru") minority government led by the [first minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Minister_of_Wales "First Minister of Wales"), [Rhun ap Iorwerth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhun_ap_Iorwerth "Rhun ap Iorwerth"). [Local government in Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Wales "Local government in Wales") consists of 22 unitary authorities, each led by a leader and cabinet elected by the council itself.

#### Northern Ireland

The devolved form of government in Northern Ireland is based on the 1998 [Good Friday Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Friday_Agreement "Good Friday Agreement"), which brought to an end a 30-year period of [unionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unionism_in_Ireland "Unionism in Ireland")-[nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_nationalism "Irish nationalism") communal conflict known as [the Troubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Troubles "The Troubles"). The Agreement was [confirmed by referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Northern_Ireland_Good_Friday_Agreement_referendum "1998 Northern Ireland Good Friday Agreement referendum") and implemented later that year. It established [power sharing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_sharing "Power sharing") arrangements for a devolved government and legislature, referred to as the [Northern Ireland Executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Executive "Northern Ireland Executive") and the [Northern Ireland Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Assembly "Northern Ireland Assembly") respectively. Elections to the Assembly use the [single transferable vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_transferable_vote "Single transferable vote") system. The Executive and Assembly have powers similar to those devolved to Scotland. The Executive is led by a [diarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diarchy "Diarchy") representing [unionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Designated_Unionist "Designated Unionist") and [nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Designated_Nationalist "Designated Nationalist") members of the Assembly. The [First Minister and Deputy First Minister of Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Minister_and_Deputy_First_Minister_of_Northern_Ireland "First Minister and Deputy First Minister of Northern Ireland") are the joint heads of government of Northern Ireland. [Local government in Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Northern_Ireland "Local government in Northern Ireland") since 2015 has been divided between 11 councils with limited responsibilities.

### Foreign relations

The United Kingdom is [a permanent member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Five_(United_Nations) "Big Five (United Nations)") of the [United Nations Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council "United Nations Security Council") and a member of [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO"), [AUKUS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AUKUS "AUKUS"), the Commonwealth of Nations, the [G7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G7 "G7"), the [G20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-20_major_economies "G-20 major economies"), the [OECD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_for_Economic_Co-operation_and_Development "Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development"), the [WTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization "World Trade Organization"), the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe "Council of Europe") and the [OSCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_for_Security_and_Co-operation_in_Europe "Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe"). It maintains the [British Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Council "British Council"), an organisation specialising in international cultural and educational opportunities in over 100 countries. The UK remains a [great power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_power "Great power") with considerable political, cultural, economic and military influence.

The United Kingdom is said to have a "[Special Relationship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Relationship "Special Relationship")" with the United States and a close partnership with France – the "[Entente cordiale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entente_cordiale "Entente cordiale")" – and shares nuclear weapons technology with both countries; the [Anglo-Portuguese Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Portuguese_Alliance "Anglo-Portuguese Alliance") is considered to be the oldest binding military alliance in the world. The UK is also closely linked with the Republic of Ireland; the two countries share a [Common Travel Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Travel_Area "Common Travel Area") and co-operate through the [British-Irish Intergovernmental Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British-Irish_Intergovernmental_Conference "British-Irish Intergovernmental Conference") and the [British-Irish Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British-Irish_Council "British-Irish Council"). Britain's global presence and influence is further amplified through its trading relations, foreign investments, [official development assistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_development_assistance "Official development assistance") and military engagements.

### Military

The British Armed Forces consist of three professional service branches: the [Royal Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Navy "Royal Navy") and [Royal Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Marines "Royal Marines") (forming the [Naval Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Service_(United_Kingdom) "Naval Service (United Kingdom)")), the [British Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army "British Army") and the [Royal Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Air_Force "Royal Air Force"). The armed forces of the United Kingdom are managed by the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_(United_Kingdom) "Ministry of Defence (United Kingdom)") and controlled by the [Defence Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom "Defence Council of the United Kingdom"), chaired by the [Secretary of State for Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Defence "Secretary of State for Defence"). The [Commander-in-Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-Chief "Commander-in-Chief") is the [British monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_the_United_Kingdom "Monarchy of the United Kingdom"), to whom members of the forces swear an oath of allegiance.

The armed forces are charged with protecting the United Kingdom and its overseas territories, promoting the UK's global security interests and supporting international peacekeeping efforts. They are active and regular participants in [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO") (including the [Allied Rapid Reaction Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_Rapid_Reaction_Corps "Allied Rapid Reaction Corps")), the [Five Power Defence Arrangements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Power_Defence_Arrangements "Five Power Defence Arrangements"), [RIMPAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIMPAC "RIMPAC") and other worldwide coalition operations. [Overseas garrisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_military_bases_of_the_United_Kingdom "Overseas military bases of the United Kingdom") and facilities are maintained in [Ascension Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Ascension_Island "RAF Ascension Island"), [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mina_Salman "Mina Salman"), [Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_Belize "Military of Belize"), [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Forces_based_in_Brunei "Military Forces based in Brunei"), [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army_Training_Unit_Suffield "British Army Training Unit Suffield"), [Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Forces_Cyprus "British Forces Cyprus"), [Diego Garcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Garcia "Diego Garcia"), the [Falkland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_the_Falkland_Islands "Military of the Falkland Islands"), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Forces_Germany "British Forces Germany"), [Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Forces_Gibraltar "British Forces Gibraltar"), [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army_Training_Unit_Kenya "British Army Training Unit Kenya"), [Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oman "Oman"), [Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Udeid_Air_Base "Al Udeid Air Base") and [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore").

According to the [Stockholm International Peace Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_International_Peace_Research_Institute "Stockholm International Peace Research Institute") and the [International Institute for Strategic Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_for_Strategic_Studies "International Institute for Strategic Studies"), the UK had the world's [sixth- or fifth-highest military expenditures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_military_expenditures "List of countries by military expenditures") in 2025. Total defence spending in 2024 was estimated at 2.3 per cent of [gross domestic product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_domestic_product "Gross domestic product"). Following the end of the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War"), defence policy has a stated assumption that "the most demanding operations" will be undertaken as part of a coalition.

### Law and criminal justice

The United Kingdom does not have a single legal system as the [1706 Treaty of Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Union "Treaty of Union") provided for the continuation of Scotland's separate legal system. The UK has three distinct [systems of law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_systems_of_the_world "Legal systems of the world"): English law, Northern Ireland law and Scots law. A new [Supreme Court of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_United_Kingdom "Supreme Court of the United Kingdom") came into being in October 2009 to replace the [Appellate Committee of the House of Lords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_functions_of_the_House_of_Lords "Judicial functions of the House of Lords"). The [Judicial Committee of the Privy Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_Committee_of_the_Privy_Council "Judicial Committee of the Privy Council"), including the same members as the Supreme Court, is the highest court of appeal for several independent Commonwealth countries, the [British Overseas Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Overseas_Territories "British Overseas Territories") and the [Crown Dependencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Dependencies "Crown Dependencies").

Both English law, which applies in England and Wales, and Northern Ireland law are based on [common law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_law "Common law") (or [case law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_law "Case law")) principles. It originated in England in the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages "Middle Ages") and is the basis for many legal systems around the world. The [courts of England and Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courts_of_England_and_Wales "Courts of England and Wales") are headed by the [Senior Courts of England and Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Courts_of_England_and_Wales "Senior Courts of England and Wales"), consisting of the [Court of Appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Appeal_of_England_and_Wales "Court of Appeal of England and Wales"), the [High Court of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_Justice "High Court of Justice") for civil cases and the [Crown Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Court "Crown Court") for criminal cases. Scots law is a hybrid system based on common-law and [civil-law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_(legal_system) "Civil law (legal system)") principles. The chief courts are the [Court of Session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Session "Court of Session"), for civil cases, and the [High Court of Justiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_Justiciary "High Court of Justiciary"), for criminal cases. The Supreme Court of the United Kingdom serves as the highest court of appeal for civil cases under Scots law. The criminal justice system has been characterised by low [conviction rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conviction_rate "Conviction rate") and [court delays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_delayed_is_justice_denied "Justice delayed is justice denied") for some crime types.

Crime in England and Wales increased between 1981 and 1995. Since that peak there has been an overall fall of 66% in recorded crime from 1995 to 2015, according to [UK crime statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_statistics_in_the_United_Kingdom "Crime statistics in the United Kingdom").

[UK labour laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_labour_law "United Kingdom labour law") establish employment rights including [a minimum wage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Minimum_Wage_Act_1998 "National Minimum Wage Act 1998"), a minimum of 28 days annual holiday, parental leave, statutory sick pay and [a pension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pensions_Act_2008 "Pensions Act 2008"). [Same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage "Same-sex marriage") has been legal in England, Scotland, and Wales since 2014, and in [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland "Northern Ireland") since 2020. [LGBT equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_the_United_Kingdom "LGBT rights in the United Kingdom") in the United Kingdom is considered advanced by modern standards.

Since leaving the [EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union") most disputes under UK-EU agreements are addressed through consultation between the parties. If consultation fails to resolve the issue, either party can request [arbitration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitration "Arbitration"), typically at the [PCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Court_of_Arbitration "Permanent Court of Arbitration") in [The Hague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Palace "Peace Palace"). The [EU–UK Trade and Cooperation Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU%E2%80%93UK_Trade_and_Cooperation_Agreement "EU–UK Trade and Cooperation Agreement") states that the UK and EU have to cooperate and negotiate with each other with 'full mutual respect and good faith', as defined by international law. Under the [Windsor Framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windsor_Framework "Windsor Framework"), [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland "Northern Ireland") matters requiring interpretation of EU law go to the [ECJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Court_of_Justice "European Court of Justice"), though the [Stormont Brake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stormont_Brake "Stormont Brake") can prevent new EU rules from taking effect.

## Economy

The United Kingdom has a highly developed [social market economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_market_economy "Social market economy"). With an estimated nominal [gross domestic product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_domestic_product "Gross domestic product") (GDP) of £2.819 trillion in 2025, it is the [fifth-largest national economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(nominal) "List of countries by GDP (nominal)") in the world and the [second-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states_in_Europe_by_GDP_(nominal) "List of sovereign states in Europe by GDP (nominal)") in Europe. Its currency, the [pound sterling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pound_sterling "Pound sterling"), is the [fourth-most-traded currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_exchange_market#Most_traded_currencies_by_value "Foreign exchange market") in the [foreign exchange market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_exchange_market "Foreign exchange market") and the world's fourth-largest [reserve currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_currency "Reserve currency"), after the [United States dollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar "United States dollar"), the [euro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro "Euro") and the [yen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yen "Yen"). The pound sterling maintains its high nominal value through both its long history of stability and by never undergoing formal [redenomination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redenomination "Redenomination"). Since 2022 the UK has been both the world's [fourth-largest exporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_exports "List of countries by exports") and [fourth-largest importer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_imports "List of countries by imports") of goods and services. Despite having one of the highest levels of [income inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_inequality_in_the_United_Kingdom "Income inequality in the United Kingdom") in the [OECD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OECD "OECD"), the UK has a very high [HDI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index "Human Development Index") ranking, including when [adjusted for inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_inequality-adjusted_Human_Development_Index "List of countries by inequality-adjusted Human Development Index"). As of 2025 the [UK unemployment rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment_in_the_United_Kingdom "Unemployment in the United Kingdom") is 4.7% and the annual [real GDP per head growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_real_GDP_per_capita_growth "List of countries by real GDP per capita growth") was 1.1%.

[HM Treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HM_Treasury "HM Treasury"), led by the [Chancellor of the Exchequer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_of_the_Exchequer "Chancellor of the Exchequer"), is responsible for developing and executing the government's [public finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_finance "Public finance") and [economic policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_policy "Economic policy"). The [Department for Business and Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Business_and_Trade "Department for Business and Trade") is responsible for business, international trade, and enterprise. The [Bank of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_England "Bank of England") is the UK's [central bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_bank "Central bank") and responsible for issuing notes and coins in the pound sterling. Banks in Scotland and Northern Ireland retain the right to issue their own notes, subject to retaining enough Bank of England notes in reserve to cover their issue.

### Industries and services

The [service sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector_of_the_economy "Tertiary sector of the economy") made up around 80 per cent of the UK's [GVA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_value_added "Gross value added") in 2023. As of 2023 it is the world's [second-largest exporter of services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_service_exports "List of countries by service exports") and in 2024 was the world's largest net exporter of [financial services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_services_in_the_United_Kingdom "Financial services in the United Kingdom"). [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London") is the world capital for foreign exchange trading, with a market share of 37.8 per cent in 2022 of the global turnover. It is the largest urban economy in Europe and, alongside [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"), the city in the world most integrated with the [global economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_economy "World economy"). London is also one of the world's leading financial centres, ranking second in the 2025 [Global Financial Centres Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Financial_Centres_Index "Global Financial Centres Index"). [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh "Edinburgh"), the UK's second-largest financial centre, ranks 29th in the world in the same index.

The [UK's manufacturing sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing_in_the_United_Kingdom "Manufacturing in the United Kingdom") in 2024 was the world's [10th-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_manufacturing_output "List of countries by manufacturing output") and Europe's fourth-largest by value output. At the end of 2024 manufacturing in the United Kingdom accounted for 8 per cent of the workforce and 8.6 per cent of national economic output. As reported in 2017 the [East Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Midlands_Manufacturing "East Midlands Manufacturing") and [West Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_Manufacturing "West Midlands Manufacturing") (at 12.6 and 11.8 per cent respectively) were the regions with the highest proportion of employees in manufacturing. [London's manufacturing sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_manufacturing "London manufacturing") had the lowest at 2.8 per cent.

The [country's tourism sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_the_United_Kingdom "Tourism in the United Kingdom") is important to the British economy; The [creative industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_industries "Creative industries") accounted for 5.9 per cent of the UK's GVA in 2019, and contributed £111 billion to the UK economy in 2018. [Lloyd's of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd%27s_of_London "Lloyd's of London"), located in London, is the world's largest [insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance "Insurance") and [reinsurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinsurance "Reinsurance") market. [WPP plc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WPP_plc "WPP plc") is one of the world's biggest advertising companies and also based in London. The UK is one of Europe's leading retail markets and its largest [e-commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-commerce "E-commerce") market. With [consumption expenditures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_spending "Consumer spending") of over US$2 trillion in 2023, it has the [second-largest consumer market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_consumer_markets "List of largest consumer markets") in Europe. [John Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lewis_Partnership "John Lewis Partnership") is the UK's largest employee-owned business.

The [British automotive industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_industry_in_the_United_Kingdom "Automotive industry in the United Kingdom") generates £47 billion of exports (12 per cent of the UK's total exports of goods). In 2024 the UK produced 779,584 passenger vehicles and 125,649 commercial vehicles; 8 out of 10 cars produced in the UK are exported overseas. Britain is known for iconic cars like [Mini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini_Hatch "Mini Hatch") and [Jaguar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar_Cars "Jaguar Cars"), as well as luxury cars such as [Rolls-Royce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolls-Royce_Motor_Cars "Rolls-Royce Motor Cars"), [Bentley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bentley "Bentley") and [Range Rover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Range_Rover "Range Rover"). The UK is a major centre for engine manufacturing: 1.59 million engines were produced in 2024. It was the world's [third-largest exporter of engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_engine_exports "List of countries by engine exports") by value in 2023. The [UK motorsport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorsport_in_the_United_Kingdom "Motorsport in the United Kingdom") industry has an annual turnover of around £10 billion. Seven of the eleven [Formula One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_One "Formula One") teams are based in the UK, with their technology being used in supercars and hypercars from [McLaren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McLaren_Automotive "McLaren Automotive"), [Aston Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aston_Martin "Aston Martin") and [Lotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotus_Cars "Lotus Cars"). The [aerospace industry of the UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_industry_in_the_United_Kingdom "Aerospace industry in the United Kingdom") is the second-largest in the world and has an annual turnover of around £30 billion. The [UK space industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_space_programme "British space programme") was worth £17.5 billion in 2020/21 and employed around 48,800 people. The [UK Space Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Space_Agency "UK Space Agency") has stated in 2023 that it is investing £1.6 billion in space-related projects.

The [British agriculture industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_the_United_Kingdom "Agriculture in the United Kingdom") is intensive, highly mechanised and efficient by European standards, producing approximately 60 per cent of the country's overall food requirements and 73 per cent of its indigenous food needs, using around 0.9 per cent of the labour force (292,000 workers). Around two-thirds of production is devoted to [livestock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livestock "Livestock") and one-third to [arable crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_crops "Arable crops"). The UK retains a significant though much reduced fishing industry, with at least 49 per cent of UK fish sustainably caught in 2020. The UK marine natural capital assets were estimated to have a value of £211 billion in 2021. It is rich in a variety of other natural resources including coal, [petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum "Petroleum"), [natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas "Natural gas"), tin, limestone, iron ore, salt, clay, chalk, [gypsum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gypsum "Gypsum"), lead, and silica and has an abundance of arable land.

### Science and technology

England and Scotland were leading centres of the [Scientific Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_Revolution "Scientific Revolution") from the 17th century. The United Kingdom led the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution "Industrial Revolution") from the 18th century, and has continued to produce scientists and [engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_British_engineers "List of British engineers") credited with [important advances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_British_innovations_and_discoveries "List of British innovations and discoveries"). Major theorists from the 17th and 18th centuries include [Isaac Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Newton "Isaac Newton"), whose [laws of motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton%27s_laws_of_motion "Newton's laws of motion") and illumination of [gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitation "Gravitation") have been seen as a keystone of modern science; from the 19th century [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin "Charles Darwin"), whose theory of [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution "Evolution") by [natural selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_selection "Natural selection") was fundamental to the development of modern biology, and [James Clerk Maxwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Clerk_Maxwell "James Clerk Maxwell"), who formulated classical [electromagnetic theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_theory "Electromagnetic theory"); and more recently [Stephen Hawking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Hawking "Stephen Hawking"), who advanced major theories in the fields of [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology "Cosmology"), [quantum gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_gravity "Quantum gravity") and the investigation of [black holes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_holes "Black holes").

The [Department for Science, Innovation and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Science,_Innovation_and_Technology "Department for Science, Innovation and Technology") (DSIT) is responsible for helping to develop and manage the UK's scientific, research, and technological outputs. Scientific research and development remains important in [British universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universities_in_the_United_Kingdom "Universities in the United Kingdom"), with many establishing [science parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_park "Science park") to facilitate production and co-operation with industry. In 2022, the UK produced [6.0 per cent of the world's scientific research papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_number_of_scientific_and_technical_journal_articles "List of countries by number of scientific and technical journal articles") and had an 8.8 per cent share of scientific citations, the fourth and third highest among G7 countries and Brazil, China, India, Russia, and South Korea. The UK ranked 1st among these countries for Field-Weighted Citation Impact. Scientific journals produced in the UK include publications by the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society"), *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_(journal) "Nature (journal)")*, the *[British Medical Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMJ "BMJ")* and *[The Lancet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lancet "The Lancet")*.

By 2024 the UK tech sector reached a value of US$1.2 trillion surpassing the combined values of the French and German sectors. Cambridge was named the number one university in the world for producing successful technology founders. The [UK's artificial intelligence industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence_industry_in_the_United_Kingdom "Artificial intelligence industry in the United Kingdom") is the largest in Europe by value and the country ranked fifth globally in a 2026 report on artificial intelligence development by [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University"). The UK ranked 6th in the [Global Innovation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Innovation_Index "Global Innovation Index") of 2026 and 2025.

### Transport

In the UK, all traffic [drives on the left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-_and_right-hand_traffic "Left- and right-hand traffic"), with distance and speed being measured in miles, yards and miles per hour (mph). All vehicles manufactured for use in the UK have the steering wheel situated on the right side of the vehicle. A radial road network totals 29,145 miles (46,904 km) of main roads, 2,173 miles (3,497 km) of motorways and 213,750 miles (344,000 km) of paved roads. The [M25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M25_motorway "M25 motorway"), encircling London, is the largest and busiest bypass in the world. In 2024 there were 41.7 million licensed vehicles in the UK.

The UK has an extensive railway network of 10,072 miles (16,209 km). In Great Britain, the [British Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Rail "British Rail") network [was privatised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatisation_of_British_Rail "Privatisation of British Rail") between 1994 and 1997, followed by a rapid rise in passengers. [Great British Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_British_Railways "Great British Railways") is a planned state-owned public body that will oversee [rail transport in Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Great_Britain "Rail transport in Great Britain"). The UK was ranked eighth amongst national European rail systems in the 2017 European Railway Performance Index assessing intensity of use, quality of service and safety.

A train runs directly from London to [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris"). Called the [Eurostar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurostar "Eurostar"), it travels through the [Channel Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_Tunnel "Channel Tunnel") under the [English Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Channel "English Channel"). At 23.5 miles long, it is the world's longest undersea tunnel. There is also a car service through the tunnel to France called [LeShuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeShuttle "LeShuttle"). The [Elizabeth line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_line "Elizabeth line"), a rail link running between [East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_London "East London") and [West London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_London "West London"), was named in honour of [Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II "Queen Elizabeth II") in 2016 and opened in 2022. Another major infrastructure project is [High Speed 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Speed_2 "High Speed 2") (HS2), a high-speed railway under construction since 2019. It will link London with [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham "Birmingham"), with the potential to extend further north and capable of speeds of up to 225 mph.

In 2023 there were 4 billion bus journeys in the UK, 1.8 billion of which were in London. The red [double-decker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-decker_bus "Double-decker bus") bus has entered popular culture as an internationally recognised icon of London and England. The [London bus network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Buses "London Buses") is extensive, with over 6,800 scheduled services every weekday carrying about 6 million passengers on over 700 different routes, making it one of the most extensive bus systems in the world and the largest in Europe.

In 2024, [British airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_the_United_Kingdom_and_the_British_Crown_Dependencies "List of airports in the United Kingdom and the British Crown Dependencies") handled nearly 292.5 million passengers. In that period the three largest airports were [London Heathrow Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Heathrow_Airport "London Heathrow Airport") (83.9 million passengers), [Gatwick Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatwick_Airport "Gatwick Airport") (43.2 million passengers) and [Manchester Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Airport "Manchester Airport") (30.8 million passengers). London Heathrow Airport, located 15 miles (24 km) west of the capital, is the world's [second-busiest airport by international passenger traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_airports_by_passenger_traffic "List of busiest airports by passenger traffic") and has the most international passenger traffic of any airport in the world; it is the hub for the UK flag carrier [British Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Airways "British Airways"), as well as [Virgin Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Atlantic "Virgin Atlantic"). During 2023, 18.3 million passengers travelled internationally by rail and 18.1 million by sea.

### Energy

In 2021 the UK was the world's [14th-largest consumer and 22nd-largest producer of energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_energy_consumption_and_production "List of countries by energy consumption and production"). It is home to [many large energy companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Six_energy_suppliers "Big Six energy suppliers"), including two of the six major oil and gas companies – [BP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BP "BP") and [Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_plc "Shell plc").

Renewable electricity sources provided 51 per cent of the electricity generated in the UK in 2024. [Wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_in_the_United_Kingdom "Wind power in the United Kingdom") was the largest source of electricity in 2024, generating 30 per cent of the UK's total electricity. The UK has the largest offshore wind farm in the world, which is located off the coast of [Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire "Yorkshire").

In 2023 the UK had nine nuclear reactors generating about 15 per cent of the UK's electricity. There are two reactors under construction and more planned. In the late 1990s [nuclear power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_plants_in_the_United_Kingdom "Nuclear power plants in the United Kingdom") contributed around 25 per cent of the total annual electricity generation in the UK, but this has gradually declined as old plants have been shut down. The UK government is investing in [small modular reactors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_modular_reactors "Small modular reactors") that operate via [nuclear fission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fission "Nuclear fission"), as well as in research and development towards commercial [fusion reactors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_reactors "Fusion reactors"). To that end the government entered into a partnership with the US in late 2023 to collaborate on fusion technology, with "a commercial grid-ready fusion reactor by 2040" stated as a goal.

At the end of 2023 it was estimated that there were 1.1 billion [barrels of oil equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrels_of_oil_equivalent "Barrels of oil equivalent") of "proven" and "probable" [gas reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_and_gas_reserves_and_resource_quantification "Oil and gas reserves and resource quantification") and 2.3 billion boe of "proven" and "probable" [oil reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea_oil "North Sea oil") offshore, reducing reliance on imports for energy security and transitioning to renewables. Emissions from UK gas production are roughly four times lower than imported [liquefied natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquefied_natural_gas "Liquefied natural gas") (LNG), according to the [UK's oil and gas regulator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea_Transition_Authority "North Sea Transition Authority").

In September 2024 the last coal power station was closed, making [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal "Coal") no longer a power source in the UK. The UK currently has no [fracking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fracking "Fracking") (hydraulic fracturing) for [shale gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shale_gas "Shale gas") despite a large supply, due to environmental concerns.

### Water supply and sanitation

Access to improved water supply and sanitation in the UK is universal. It was estimated that 96 per cent of households were connected to the sewer network in 2022. According to the [Environment Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environment_Agency "Environment Agency"), total water abstraction for public water supply in the UK was 16,406 [megalitres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalitre "Megalitre") per day in 2007.

In England and Wales, water and sewerage services are provided by 10 private regional water and sewerage companies and 13 mostly smaller private "water only" companies. In Scotland, said services are provided by a single public company, [Scottish Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Water "Scottish Water"). In Northern Ireland, they are also provided by a single public entity, [Northern Ireland Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Water "Northern Ireland Water").

## Demographics

Whereas the censuses of England and Wales and of Northern Ireland went ahead as planned in 2021, the census in Scotland was delayed until 2022 due to the COVID-19 pandemic. The Office for National Statistics has estimated that the total population of the United Kingdom recorded in the census would have been 66,912,637 had it been conducted in all parts of the UK on 21 March 2021. The UK population is the [fourth-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_European_countries_by_population "List of European countries by population") in Europe and the [22nd-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population "List of countries by population") in the world. In 2012 and 2013 births contributed the most to population growth, while in 2014 and 2015 net international migration contributed more. Between 2001 and 2011 the population increased at an average annual rate of 0.7 per cent. The total population is estimated to have been 69.5 million in mid-2025, with the 12 months prior to that experiencing the slowest population growth since 1998, with the exception of 2020, the first year of the COVID-19 pandemic.

The [2011 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_United_Kingdom_census "2011 United Kingdom census") showed that, over the previous 100 years, the proportion of the population aged 0–14 fell from 31 to 18 per cent, and the proportion of people aged 65 and over rose from 5 to 16 per cent. In 2018 the [median age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_age "Median age") of the UK population was 41.7 years. The 2021 census put Scotland's population at 5.48 million, Wales's at 3.1 million and Northern Ireland's at 1.9 million.

### Ethnicity

Historically, indigenous British people were thought to be [descended from ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_history_of_the_British_Isles "Genetic history of the British Isles") that settled there before the 12th century: the [Celts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts "Celts"), Romans, Anglo-Saxons, Norse and the [Normans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normans "Normans"). [Welsh people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_people "Welsh people") could be the oldest ethnic group in the UK. The UK has a history of non-white immigration with [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool "Liverpool") having the oldest black population in the country dating from at least the 1730s, in addition to having the oldest [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Chinese "British Chinese") community in Europe dating from the 19th century.

In 2021, 83.1 per cent of the UK population was classified as white, 8.6 per cent as Asian or Asian British, 3.7 per cent as Black or Black British, 2.7 per cent as mixed ethnicity, and 2.0 per cent other. Ethnic diversity varies significantly across the UK. In 2021, 46.2 per cent of London's population was from a non-white ethnic minority, compared less than 10 per cent in the South West and North East of England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland.

### Language

The [English language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language "English language") is the *[de facto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_facto "De facto")* official and most widely spoken language in the United Kingdom. The UK promotes the language globally to build connections, understanding and trust between people in the UK and countries worldwide.

In the UK, the English language is spoken with distinctive characteristics that collectively form what is known as [British English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English "British English"). The variety of dialects and accents is often noted, with nearby regions frequently having highly distinct variations. [Received Pronunciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Received_Pronunciation "Received Pronunciation") is traditionally associated with educated speakers in southern England. The main national dialects are [Scottish English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_English "Scottish English"), [Welsh English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_English "Welsh English") and [Northern Irish English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Irish_English "Northern Irish English"). Distinctive regional varieties include
[Brummie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brummie_dialect "Brummie dialect"), [Cockney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockney "Cockney"), [Geordie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geordie "Geordie"), [Mancunian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_dialect "Manchester dialect"), [Scouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scouse "Scouse"), [West Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Country_English "West Country English"), [Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire_dialect "Yorkshire dialect") and [MLE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multicultural_London_English "Multicultural London English") (Multicultural London English).

Three indigenous [Celtic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_languages "Celtic languages") are spoken in the UK: [Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language "Welsh language"), [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language "Irish language") and [Scottish Gaelic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Gaelic "Scottish Gaelic"). [Cornish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornish_language "Cornish language"), which became extinct as a first language in the late-18th century, is being revived and has a small group of second-language speakers. In the [2021 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_United_Kingdom_census "2021 United Kingdom census") the [Welsh-speaking population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh-speaking_population "Welsh-speaking population") of Wales aged three or older was 538,300 (17.8 per cent). In addition it is estimated that about 200,000 Welsh-speakers live in England. In the 2021 census in Northern Ireland 12.4 per cent of people had some [Irish language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language_in_Northern_Ireland "Irish language in Northern Ireland") ability and 10.4 per cent had some facility in the [Ulster-Scots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Scots_dialect "Ulster Scots dialect") language.

In 2001, over 92,000 people in Scotland, just under 2 per cent of the population, had some Gaelic language ability, including 72 per cent of those living in the [Outer Hebrides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Hebrides "Outer Hebrides"). The number of children being taught either Welsh or Scottish Gaelic is increasing. [Scots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scots_language "Scots language"), a language descended from early northern [Middle English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_English "Middle English"), has limited [recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Charter_for_Regional_or_Minority_Languages "European Charter for Regional or Minority Languages") alongside its regional variant, [Ulster Scots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Scots_dialects "Ulster Scots dialects") in Northern Ireland, without specific commitments to protection and promotion. As of April 2020 there are around 151,000 users of [British Sign Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Sign_Language "British Sign Language") (BSL), a [sign language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sign_language "Sign language") used by deaf people, in the UK.

In 2013, it was estimated that 95 per cent of the UK's population were [monolingual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monolingual "Monolingual") English speakers. In 2013, over 5 per cent of the population were estimated to speak languages brought to the UK as a result of immigration. [South Asian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_languages "South Asian languages") are the largest group, which includes [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language "Punjabi language"), [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu "Urdu"), [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language "Bengali language"), [Sylheti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylheti_language "Sylheti language"), [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi "Hindi"), [Pahari-Pothwari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahari-Pothwari "Pahari-Pothwari"), [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language "Tamil language") and [Gujarati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_language "Gujarati language"). In the 2011 census [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language "Polish language") was the second-largest language spoken in England, with 546,000 speakers. In 2019 some three-quarters of a million people spoke little or no English.

### Religion

[Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity") has dominated religious life in the United Kingdom for [more than 1,400 years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Christianity_in_Britain "History of Christianity in Britain"). Although a majority of citizens still identify with Christianity in surveys, regular church attendance has fallen dramatically since the middle of the 20th century, while immigration and demographic change have contributed to the growth of Islam and other faiths. This has led some commentators to describe the UK as a multi-faith, [secularised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularised "Secularised"), or [post-Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Christian "Post-Christian") society.

In the 2021 census of England and Wales, 46.2 per cent of respondents reported that they were Christians, with the next largest faiths being [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_the_United_Kingdom "Islam in the United Kingdom") (6.5 per cent), [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_the_United_Kingdom "Hinduism in the United Kingdom") (1.7 per cent), [Sikhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism_in_the_United_Kingdom "Sikhism in the United Kingdom") (0.9 per cent), [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_the_United_Kingdom "Buddhism in the United Kingdom") (0.5 per cent), [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism_in_the_United_Kingdom "Judaism in the United Kingdom") (0.5 per cent), and all other religions (0.6 per cent). Of the respondents, 37.2 per cent stated that they had [no religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion_in_the_United_Kingdom "Irreligion in the United Kingdom") and a further 6.0 per cent did not answer the optional question. In the census of Northern Ireland, 42.3 per cent of the population was recorded as Catholic, 37.7% as Protestant or other Christian, 1.3 per cent as belonging to other religions, 17.4 per cent as having no religion and 1.6 per cent did not state a religion. In Scotland's 2022 census, 51.1 per cent of the population stated that they had no religion, 20.4 per cent were recorded as Church of Scotland, 13.3 per cent as Catholic, 5.1 per cent as other Christian, 2.2 per cent as Muslim and 0.6 per cent as Hindu, with 1.2 per cent belonging to other religions and 6.2 per cent not stating a religion.

A [Tearfund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tearfund "Tearfund") survey in 2007 showed that one Briton in ten attends church weekly. Between the 2001 and 2011 censuses there was a 12 per cent decrease in those who identified as Christian, while the percentage reporting no religious affiliation doubled. This contrasted with growth in the other main religious group categories, with the number of [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims "Muslims") increasing the most to about 5 per cent.

The [Church of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_England "Church of England") is the [established church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_religion "State religion") in England. It retains [representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lords_Spiritual "Lords Spiritual") in the UK Parliament, and the British monarch is its [Supreme Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Governor_of_the_Church_of_England "Supreme Governor of the Church of England"). In [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Scotland "Religion in Scotland") the [Church of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Scotland "Church of Scotland") is the [national church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_church "National church"). It is not subject to state control, and the British monarch is an ordinary member, required to swear an oath to "maintain and preserve the Protestant Religion and [Presbyterian Church Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian_polity "Presbyterian polity")" upon his or her accession. The [Church in Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_in_Wales "Church in Wales") was disestablished in 1920 and, because the [Church of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Ireland "Church of Ireland") was disestablished in 1870 before the [partition of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_Ireland "Partition of Ireland"), there is no established church in Northern Ireland. Although there are no UK-wide data in the 2001 census on adherence to individual Christian denominations, it has been estimated that 62 per cent of Christians are Anglican, 13.5 per cent Catholic, 6 per cent Presbyterian, and 3.4 per cent Methodist, with smaller numbers of other denominations.

### Migration

Immigration is contributing to a rising population in the United Kingdom, with arrivals and UK-born children of migrants accounting for about half of the population increase between 1991 and 2001. According to statistics released in 2015, 27 per cent of UK live births in 2014 were to mothers born outside the UK.

In 2013 approximately 208,000 foreign nationals were naturalised as British citizens, the highest number since 1962. This figure fell to around 125,800 in 2014. Between 2009 and 2013 the average number of British citizenships granted annually was 195,800. The most common origins of those naturalised in 2024 were Pakistani, Indian, [Nigerian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria "Nigeria"), [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines"), [Bangladeshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh"), Italian, Turkish, Romanian and Iranian. The number of grants of settlement, which confer [permanent residence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_residency "Permanent residency") in the UK but not [citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_citizenship "British citizenship"), was approximately 154,700 in 2013, higher than the previous two years. Long-term net migration (the number of people immigrating minus the number emigrating) reached a record of 860,000 in 2023, with immigration at 1.326 million and emigration at 466,000. In comparison, in 2024 net migration was estimated to be 431,000 with immigration at 948,000 and emigration at 517,000. Net migration fell to 222,900 in the 12 months to mid-2025, contributing to the lowest population growth rate (excluding the first year of the COVID-19 pandemic) since 1998.

[Emigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_diaspora "British diaspora") was an important feature of British society in the 19th century. Between 1815 and 1930, around 11.4 million people emigrated from Britain and 7.3 million from Ireland. Estimates show that by the end of the 20th century, some 300 million people of British and Irish descent were permanently settled around the globe. In 2006 at least 5.5 million UK-born people lived abroad, mainly in Australia, Spain, the United States and Canada.

### Education

Education in the United Kingdom is a [devolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution "Devolution") matter, with each country having a separate education system. About 38 per cent of the United Kingdom population has a university or [college degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_degree "College degree"), the highest percentage in Europe and one of the highest in the world. The UK is home to [many universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_the_United_Kingdom "List of universities in the United Kingdom"), including the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford") and [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge"), which often achieve first place on global rankings.

University education has varied [tuition fees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_loans_and_grants_in_the_United_Kingdom "Student loans and grants in the United Kingdom") in different regions of the UK. England and Wales have a fixed maximum annual fee for all UK citizens, contingent on attaining a certain level of income. Only those who reach a certain salary threshold pay this fee through [general taxation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation_in_the_United_Kingdom "Taxation in the United Kingdom"). Northern Ireland and Scotland have a reduced maximum fee or no fee for citizens where it is their home region. Some NHS courses have bursaries which pay the fee and in 2017 it was stated that each doctor gets subsidised by £230,000 during their training.

In 2022 the [Programme for International Student Assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programme_for_International_Student_Assessment "Programme for International Student Assessment") (PISA) ranked the overall knowledge and skills of British 15-year-olds as 14th in the world in reading, mathematics and science. The average British student scored 494, above the [OECD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OECD "OECD") average of 478.

### Health

The modern system of [universal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_service "Universal service") [publicly funded healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publicly_funded_health_care "Publicly funded health care") in the United Kingdom has its origins in the establishment of the [National Health Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Health_Service "National Health Service") (NHS), which began operating in 1948 as the primary healthcare provider in the United Kingdom. The widespread popularity of the NHS has led to it being described as a "national religion". Healthcare in the United Kingdom is a [devolved matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution "Devolution") and each country has its own system of universal publicly funded healthcare ([NHS in England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHS_in_England "NHS in England"), [NHS Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHS_Scotland "NHS Scotland"), [Health and Social Care in Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_and_Social_Care_in_Northern_Ireland "Health and Social Care in Northern Ireland") and [NHS Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHS_Wales "NHS Wales")), although private healthcare is also available. Public healthcare is provided to all [UK permanent residents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_nationality_law "British nationality law") and is mostly free at the point of need, being paid for from [general taxation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation_in_the_United_Kingdom "Taxation in the United Kingdom"). The [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization "World Health Organization"), in 2000, ranked the provision of healthcare in the United Kingdom as fifteenth-best in Europe and eighteenth in the world.

Since 1979 expenditure on healthcare has increased significantly. The 2018 [OECD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OECD "OECD") data, which incorporates in health a chunk of what in the UK is classified as social care, has the UK spending £3,121 per person. In 2017 the UK spent £2,989 per person on healthcare, near the median for members of the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development.

Regulatory bodies are organised on a UK-wide basis such as the [General Medical Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Medical_Council "General Medical Council"), the [Nursing and Midwifery Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nursing_and_Midwifery_Council "Nursing and Midwifery Council") and non-governmental-based, such as the [Royal Colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_royal_college "Medical royal college"). Political and operational responsibility for healthcare lies with four national [governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governments "Governments"); healthcare in England is the responsibility of the UK Government; healthcare in Northern Ireland is the responsibility of the Northern Ireland Executive; healthcare in Scotland is the responsibility of the Scottish Government; and healthcare in Wales is the responsibility of the Welsh Government.

## Culture

The culture of the United Kingdom is influenced by the nation's island status, its history, and being a [political union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_union "Political union") of four countries with each preserving distinctive traditions, customs and symbolism. British influence can be observed in the language, culture and [legal systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_law "Common law") of many of [its former colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_that_have_gained_independence_from_the_United_Kingdom "List of countries that have gained independence from the United Kingdom"), in particular the United States, Australia, Canada, New Zealand and Ireland, a common culture known as the Anglosphere. Most of its former colonies are Commonwealth countries, bonded by a shared British inheritance. A global survey in 2024 ranked the UK third in the 'Most Influential Countries' rankings, behind the US and China.

### Philosophy

The United Kingdom is famous for "[British Empiricism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empiricism "British Empiricism")", a branch of the philosophy that states that only knowledge verified by experience is valid, and 'Scottish Philosophy', sometimes referred to as the '[Scottish School of Common Sense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_School_of_Common_Sense "Scottish School of Common Sense")'. The most famous philosophers of British Empiricism are [John Locke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Locke "John Locke"), [George Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Berkeley "George Berkeley") and [David Hume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hume "David Hume"); while [Dugald Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dugald_Stewart "Dugald Stewart"), [Thomas Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Reid "Thomas Reid") and [William Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_William_Hamilton,_9th_Baronet "Sir William Hamilton, 9th Baronet") were major exponents of the Scottish "common sense" school. Two Britons are also notable for the ethical theory of [utilitarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utilitarianism "Utilitarianism"), a moral philosophy first used by [Jeremy Bentham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Bentham "Jeremy Bentham") and later by [John Stuart Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stuart_Mill "John Stuart Mill") in his short work *[Utilitarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utilitarianism_(book) "Utilitarianism (book)")*.

### Media

The [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC"), founded in 1922, is the United Kingdom's publicly funded radio, television and Internet broadcasting corporation, and is the oldest and largest broadcaster in the world. It operates television and radio stations across the UK and abroad and its domestic services are funded by the [television licence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_licensing_in_the_United_Kingdom "Television licensing in the United Kingdom"). The [BBC World Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_World_Service "BBC World Service") is an [international broadcaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_broadcasting "International broadcasting") owned and operated by the BBC, and the world's largest. It broadcasts radio news, speech and discussions in more than 40 languages.

Other major players in the UK media include [ITV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_plc "ITV plc"), which operates 11 of the 15 regional television broadcasters that make up the [ITV Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_(TV_network) "ITV (TV network)"), and [Sky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_UK "Sky UK"). Newspapers produced in the United Kingdom include the *[Daily Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Mail "Daily Mail")*, *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")*, *[The Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph "The Daily Telegraph")*, *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times "The Times")*, and the *[Financial Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Times "Financial Times")*. Magazines and journals published in the United Kingdom that have achieved worldwide circulation include *[The Spectator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Spectator "The Spectator")*, *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist "The Economist")*, *[New Statesman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Statesman "New Statesman")* and *[Radio Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Times "Radio Times")*.

London dominates the media sector in the UK: national newspapers and television and radio are largely based there, although [MediaCityUK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MediaCityUK "MediaCityUK") in Manchester is also a significant national media centre. Edinburgh and Glasgow, and Cardiff, are important centres of newspaper and broadcasting production in Scotland and Wales, respectively. The UK publishing sector, including books, directories and databases, journals, magazines and business media, newspapers and news agencies, has a combined turnover of around £20 billion and employs 167,000 people. In 2015 the UK published 2,710 book titles per million inhabitants, more than any other country, with much of this exported to other [Anglophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglophone "Anglophone") countries.

In 2010, 82.5 per cent of the UK population were Internet users, the [highest proportion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_number_of_Internet_users "List of countries by number of Internet users") amongst the 20 countries with the largest total number of users in that year. The [British video game industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_games_in_the_United_Kingdom "Video games in the United Kingdom") is the largest in Europe, and since 2022 the UK has the [largest video game market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_video_games_markets_by_country "List of video games markets by country") in Europe by sales, surpassing [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_games_in_Germany "Video games in Germany"). It is the world's third-largest producer of video games after [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_games_in_Japan "Video games in Japan") and the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_games_in_the_United_States "Video games in the United States").[*[failed verification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability "Wikipedia:Verifiability")*]

### Visual art

Major British artists include the [Romantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanticism "Romanticism") artists [William Blake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Blake "William Blake"), [John Constable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Constable "John Constable"), [Samuel Palmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Palmer "Samuel Palmer"), and [J. During the late 1980s and 1990s the [Saatchi Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saatchi_Gallery "Saatchi Gallery") in London helped to bring to public attention a group of multi-genre artists who would become known as the "[Young British Artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_British_Artists "Young British Artists")": [Damien Hirst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damien_Hirst "Damien Hirst"), [Chris Ofili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Ofili "Chris Ofili"), [Rachel Whiteread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Whiteread "Rachel Whiteread"), [Tracey Emin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracey_Emin "Tracey Emin"), [Mark Wallinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Wallinger "Mark Wallinger"), [Steve McQueen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_McQueen_(director) "Steve McQueen (director)"), [Sam Taylor-Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Taylor-Wood "Sam Taylor-Wood") and the [Chapman Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_and_Dinos_Chapman "Jake and Dinos Chapman") are amongst the better-known members of this loosely affiliated movement.

The [Royal Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Academy "Royal Academy") in London is a key organisation for the promotion of the visual arts in the United Kingdom. Major schools of art in the UK include: the six-school [University of the Arts London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Arts_London "University of the Arts London"), which includes the [Central Saint Martins College of Art and Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Saint_Martins_College_of_Art_and_Design "Central Saint Martins College of Art and Design") and [Chelsea College of Art and Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_College_of_Art_and_Design "Chelsea College of Art and Design"); [Goldsmiths, University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldsmiths,_University_of_London "Goldsmiths, University of London"); the [Slade School of Fine Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slade_School_of_Fine_Art "Slade School of Fine Art") (part of [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London "University College London")); the [Glasgow School of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow_School_of_Art "Glasgow School of Art"); the [Royal College of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Art "Royal College of Art"); and [The Ruskin School of Drawing and Fine Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ruskin_School_of_Drawing_and_Fine_Art "The Ruskin School of Drawing and Fine Art") (part of the University of Oxford). ### Music

Various styles of music have become popular in the UK, including the [folk music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") of [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music_of_England "Folk music of England"), [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music_of_Scotland "Folk music of Scotland"), [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Wales#Folk_music "Music of Wales") and [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music_of_Northern_Ireland "Folk music of Northern Ireland"). English folk features melodic ballads with strong lyrics and music for country dancing often using accordion and fiddles. Scottish folk features bagpipes and fiddles playing traditional dance tunes with bouncy tempos. Welsh folk has harps and vocal harmonies often sung in Welsh. Northern Irish folk blends fiddles with flutes merging Scottish and Irish influences.

Historically, there has been [Renaissance music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_music "Renaissance music") from the [Tudor period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudor_period "Tudor period"), with [masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_(music) "Mass (music)"), [madrigals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrigal "Madrigal") and [lute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lute "Lute") music by [Thomas Tallis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Tallis "Thomas Tallis"), [John Taverner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Taverner "John Taverner"), [William Byrd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Byrd "William Byrd"), [Orlando Gibbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Gibbons "Orlando Gibbons") and [John Dowland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dowland "John Dowland"). After the [Stuart Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Restoration "Stuart Restoration"), an English tradition of dramatic [masques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masque "Masque"), anthems and airs became established, led by [Henry Purcell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Purcell "Henry Purcell"), followed by [Thomas Arne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Arne "Thomas Arne") and others. [George Frideric Handel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Frideric_Handel "George Frideric Handel") composed the anthem *[Zadok the Priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zadok_the_Priest "Zadok the Priest")* for the [coronation of George II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation_of_George_II "Coronation of George II"); it became the traditional ceremonial music for anointing all future monarchs. Handel's many oratorios, such as his famous *[Messiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messiah_(Handel) "Messiah (Handel)")*, were written in English.

In the second half of the 19th century, [Arthur Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Sullivan "Arthur Sullivan") and his librettist [W. S. Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._S._Gilbert "W. S. Gilbert") wrote their popular [Savoy operas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoy_opera "Savoy opera"), and [Edward Elgar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Elgar "Edward Elgar") composed a wide range of music. Increasingly, composers became inspired by the English countryside and its [folk music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music"), notably [Gustav Holst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Holst "Gustav Holst"), [Ralph Vaughan Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Vaughan_Williams "Ralph Vaughan Williams") and [Benjamin Britten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Britten "Benjamin Britten"), a pioneer of modern British opera. Amongst the many post-war composers, some of the most notable have made their own personal choice of musical identity: [Peter Maxwell Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Maxwell_Davies "Peter Maxwell Davies") (Orkney), [Harrison Birtwistle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrison_Birtwistle "Harrison Birtwistle") (mythological), and [John Tavener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tavener "John Tavener") (religious). Recent classical singers include [Alfie Boe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfie_Boe "Alfie Boe"), [Bryn Terfel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryn_Terfel "Bryn Terfel"), [Katherine Jenkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katherine_Jenkins "Katherine Jenkins"), [Michael Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Ball "Michael Ball"), [Roderick Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roderick_Williams "Roderick Williams"), [Russell Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Watson "Russell Watson") and [Sarah Brightman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Brightman "Sarah Brightman"), while [Nicola Benedetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicola_Benedetti "Nicola Benedetti") and [Nigel Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Kennedy "Nigel Kennedy") are renowned for their violin ability.

According to *[The New Grove Dictionary of Music and Musicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Grove_Dictionary_of_Music_and_Musicians "The New Grove Dictionary of Music and Musicians")* the term "[pop music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_music "Pop music")" originated in Britain in the mid-1950s to describe [rock and roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_roll "Rock and roll")'s fusion with the "new youth music". *The Oxford Dictionary of Music* states that artists such as [the Beatles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beatles "The Beatles") and [the Rolling Stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rolling_Stones "The Rolling Stones") drove pop music to the forefront of popular music in the early 1960s. Birmingham became known as the birthplace of [heavy metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metal_music "Heavy metal music"), with the band [Black Sabbath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sabbath "Black Sabbath") starting there in the 1960s. In the following years, Britain took part in the development of [rock music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_music "Rock music"), with British acts pioneering [hard rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_rock "Hard rock"), [raga rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raga_rock "Raga rock"), heavy metal, [space rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_rock "Space rock"), [glam rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glam_rock "Glam rock"), [Gothic rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_rock "Gothic rock"), [psychedelic rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychedelic_rock "Psychedelic rock") and [punk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock "Punk rock"). British acts also developed [neo soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo_soul "Neo soul") and created [dubstep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubstep "Dubstep"). The UK has produced some of the most prominent English-speaking [rappers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rappers "Rappers") along with the United States, including [Stormzy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stormzy "Stormzy"), [Kano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kano_(rapper) "Kano (rapper)"), [Yxng Bane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yxng_Bane "Yxng Bane"), [Ramz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramz_(rapper) "Ramz (rapper)"), [Little Simz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Simz "Little Simz") and [Skepta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skepta "Skepta").

The Beatles have international sales of over 1 billion units and are the [biggest-selling band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_best-selling_music_artists "List of best-selling music artists"), in addition to being [widely regarded as the most influential band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_impact_of_the_Beatles "Cultural impact of the Beatles") in the history of popular music. Other prominent British contributors to popular music over the last 50 years include the Rolling Stones, [Pink Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_Floyd "Pink Floyd"), [Queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_(band) "Queen (band)"), [Led Zeppelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Led_Zeppelin "Led Zeppelin"), the [Bee Gees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bee_Gees "Bee Gees") and [Elton John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elton_John "Elton John"), all of whom have worldwide record sales of 200 million or more. The [Brit Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brit_Awards "Brit Awards") are the [BPI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Phonographic_Industry "British Phonographic Industry")'s annual music awards, and some of the British recipients of the [Outstanding Contribution to Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brit_Award_for_Outstanding_Contribution_to_Music "Brit Award for Outstanding Contribution to Music") award include [the Who](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Who "The Who"), [David Bowie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bowie "David Bowie"), [Eric Clapton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Clapton "Eric Clapton"), [Rod Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_Stewart "Rod Stewart"), [the Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Police "The Police") and [Fleetwood Mac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleetwood_Mac "Fleetwood Mac") (who are a British-American band). More recent UK music acts that have had international success include [George Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Michael "George Michael"), [Oasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oasis_(band) "Oasis (band)"), [Spice Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice_Girls "Spice Girls"), [Radiohead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiohead "Radiohead"), [Coldplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coldplay "Coldplay"), [Arctic Monkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Monkeys "Arctic Monkeys"), [Robbie Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Williams "Robbie Williams"), [Amy Winehouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Winehouse "Amy Winehouse"), [Susan Boyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Boyle "Susan Boyle"), [Adele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adele "Adele"), [Ed Sheeran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Sheeran "Ed Sheeran"), [Lewis Capaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Capaldi "Lewis Capaldi"), [One Direction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Direction "One Direction"), [Harry Styles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Styles "Harry Styles") and [Dua Lipa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dua_Lipa "Dua Lipa"). As of 2024 the UK is the world's [third-largest music market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_music_markets "List of largest music markets") behind the US and Japan, and in 2021 was the second-largest exporter of music behind the US.

Many British cities are known for their music. Acts from Liverpool have had 54 UK chart number 1 hit singles, more per capita than any other city worldwide. Glasgow's contribution was recognised in 2008 when it was named a [City of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Music_(UNESCO) "City of Music (UNESCO)") by [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO"). Manchester played a role in the spread of dance music such as [acid house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid_house "Acid house"), and from the mid-1990s, [Britpop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britpop "Britpop"). London and Bristol are closely associated with the origins of [electronic music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_music "Electronic music") sub-genres such as [drum and bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drum_and_bass "Drum and bass") and [trip hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trip_hop "Trip hop").

UK dance music traces its roots back to the black British [Sound System Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_system_(Jamaican) "Sound system (Jamaican)") and the [New Age Traveller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Age_travellers "New Age travellers") movement of the 60s and 70s, it also has influences from the [Chicago House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_music "House music") and [Detroit techno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_techno "Detroit techno") scenes. Influential UK dance acts past and present include [808 State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/808_State "808 State"), [Orbital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_(band) "Orbital (band)"), [the Prodigy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prodigy "The Prodigy"), [Underworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underworld_(band) "Underworld (band)"), [Roni Size](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roni_Size "Roni Size"), [Leftfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leftfield "Leftfield"), [Massive Attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massive_Attack "Massive Attack"), [Groove Armada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groove_Armada "Groove Armada"), [Fatboy Slim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatboy_Slim "Fatboy Slim"), [Faithless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faithless "Faithless"), [Basement Jaxx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basement_Jaxx "Basement Jaxx"), [Chemical Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_Brothers "Chemical Brothers"), [Disclosure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disclosure_(band) "Disclosure (band)"), [Calvin Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Harris "Calvin Harris"), and [Fred Again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Again "Fred Again"). Other influential UK DJs include [Judge Jules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judge_Jules "Judge Jules"), [Pete Tong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Tong "Pete Tong"), [Carl Cox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Cox "Carl Cox"), [Paul Oakenfold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Oakenfold "Paul Oakenfold"), [John Digweed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Digweed "John Digweed") and [Sasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasha_(dj) "Sasha (dj)").

### Cinema

The United Kingdom has had a considerable influence on the history of the cinema. The British directors [Alfred Hitchcock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Hitchcock "Alfred Hitchcock"), whose film *[Vertigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertigo_(film) "Vertigo (film)")* is considered by some critics as the [best film of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_films_considered_the_best "List of films considered the best"), and [David Lean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lean "David Lean"), who directed *[Lawrence of Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_of_Arabia_(film) "Lawrence of Arabia (film)")*, are amongst the most critically acclaimed directors ever. Recent popular directors include [Christopher Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan "Christopher Nolan"), [Sam Mendes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Mendes "Sam Mendes"), [Steve McQueen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_McQueen_(director) "Steve McQueen (director)"), [Richard Curtis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Curtis "Richard Curtis"), [Danny Boyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Boyle "Danny Boyle"), [Tony Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Scott "Tony Scott") and [Ridley Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridley_Scott "Ridley Scott"). Many British actors have achieved international fame and critical success. Some of the most commercially successful films have been produced in the United Kingdom, including two of the [highest-grossing film franchises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_film_series "List of highest-grossing film series") (*[Harry Potter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Potter_(film_series) "Harry Potter (film series)")* and *[James Bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bond_(film_series) "James Bond (film series)")*).

2019 was a particularly good year for British films which grossed around £10.3 billion globally, accounting for 28.7 per cent of global box office revenue. UK box-office takings totalled £1.25 billion in 2019, with around 176 million admissions. In 2023 UK film and television studio stage space stood at 6.9 million sq ft, with 1 million sq ft added in the past year with more in development. The annual [BAFTA Film Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Awards "British Academy Film Awards") are hosted by the [British Academy of Film and Television Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_of_Film_and_Television_Arts "British Academy of Film and Television Arts").

### Cuisine

British cuisine developed from influences reflective of its land, settlements, [arrivals of new settlers and immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_the_United_Kingdom "Immigration to the United Kingdom"), trade and colonialism. The food of England has historically been characterised by simplicity of approach and a reliance on the high quality of natural produce. The traditional [Sunday roast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunday_roast "Sunday roast") is one example, featuring a [roast joint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roasting "Roasting") (usually of beef), lamb, chicken, or pork, often [free range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_range "Free range") (and generally grass-fed, in the case of beef). Roasts are served with either roasted or boiled vegetables, [Yorkshire pudding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire_pudding "Yorkshire pudding") and [gravy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravy "Gravy"). Other traditional meals include [meat pies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meat_pie "Meat pie") and [stews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stew "Stew"). A poll by [YouGov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouGov "YouGov") in 2019 rated classic British food, with more than 80 per cent liking the Sunday roast, Yorkshire pudding, [fish and chips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_and_chips "Fish and chips"), [crumpets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crumpet "Crumpet") and the [full English breakfast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_English_breakfast "Full English breakfast").

The UK is home to a large selection of [fine dining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_dining "Fine dining"). The nation hosts multiple restaurant guides such as *[The Good Food Guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Good_Food_Guide "The Good Food Guide")* and *[Harden's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harden%27s "Harden's")*. In 2025 there were 197 restaurants with a [Michelin Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelin_Star "Michelin Star"); [ten of which have received a three-star rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Michelin_3-star_restaurants_in_the_United_Kingdom "List of Michelin 3-star restaurants in the United Kingdom"). Sweet foods are common within British cuisine, and there is a long list of [British desserts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_British_desserts "List of British desserts"). Afternoon tea is a light afternoon meal served with tea in tea rooms and hotels around the United Kingdom, with the tradition dating back to around 1840. A poll from July 2024 revealed that 3 per cent of the UK population follow a [vegan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegan "Vegan") diet, 6 per cent are [vegetarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetarian "Vegetarian"), and 13 per cent identify as [flexitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flexitarian "Flexitarian") (following a mainly vegetarian diet). The British Empire facilitated knowledge of [Indian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_cuisine "Indian cuisine") with its "strong, penetrating spices and herbs". British cuisine has absorbed the cultural influence of those who have settled in Britain, producing hybrid dishes, such as [chicken tikka masala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_tikka_masala "Chicken tikka masala").

The UK has many [gastropubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastropub "Gastropub") and is the birthplace of many alcoholic drinks including several beer styles such as [pale ale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pale_ale "Pale ale"), [India pale ale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_pale_ale "India pale ale"), [bitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitter_(beer) "Bitter (beer)"), [brown ale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_ale "Brown ale"), [porter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porter_(beer) "Porter (beer)"), and [stout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stout "Stout"). The number of [craft beers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craft_beer "Craft beer") and microbreweries has expanded rapidly in the last two decades. Other popular alcoholic drinks produced in the UK include [Scotch whisky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotch_whisky "Scotch whisky"), [English wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine_from_the_United_Kingdom "Wine from the United Kingdom"), [gin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gin "Gin"), [perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perry "Perry") and [cider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cider "Cider").

### Sport

[Association football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football "Association football"), [tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis "Tennis"), [table tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_tennis "Table tennis"), [badminton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton "Badminton"), [rugby union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_union "Rugby union"), [rugby league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league "Rugby league"), [rugby sevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_sevens "Rugby sevens"), [golf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golf "Golf"), [boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxing "Boxing"), [netball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netball "Netball"), [water polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_polo "Water polo"), [field hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_hockey "Field hockey"), [billiards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_billiards "English billiards"), [darts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darts "Darts"), [rowing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowing_(sport) "Rowing (sport)"), [rounders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rounders "Rounders") and [cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket "Cricket") originated or were substantially developed in the UK, with the rules and codes of many modern sports invented and codified in the Victorian era.

A poll in 2003 found that football is the most popular sport in the UK. England is recognised by [FIFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA "FIFA") as the birthplace of club football, and the [Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Association "Football Association") is the oldest of its kind, with the [rules of football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laws_of_the_Game_(association_football) "Laws of the Game (association football)") first drafted in 1863 by [Ebenezer Cobb Morley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebenezer_Cobb_Morley "Ebenezer Cobb Morley"). Each of the [Home Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Nations "Home Nations") (England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland) has its own football association, national team and [league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_system "League system"), and each is individually a governing member of the [International Football Association Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Football_Association_Board "International Football Association Board") alongside FIFA. The English top division, the [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_League "Premier League"), is the most watched football league in the world. The first international football match was contested by [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_national_football_team "England national football team") and [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team "Scotland national football team") on 30 November 1872. England, Scotland, [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_national_football_team "Wales national football team") and [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_national_football_team "Northern Ireland national football team") usually compete as separate countries in international competitions.

In 2003 rugby union was ranked the second-most-popular sport in the UK. The sport was created in [Rugby School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_School "Rugby School"), Warwickshire, and the [first rugby international](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1871_England_versus_Scotland_rugby_union_match "1871 England versus Scotland rugby union match") took place on 27 March 1871 between [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_national_rugby_union_team "England national rugby union team") and [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_rugby_union_team "Scotland national rugby union team"). England, Scotland, [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_national_rugby_union_team "Wales national rugby union team"), [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland_national_rugby_union_team "Ireland national rugby union team"), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_national_rugby_union_team "France national rugby union team") and [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_national_rugby_union_team "Italy national rugby union team") compete in the [Six Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Nations_Championship "Six Nations Championship"), the premier international rugby union tournament in the northern hemisphere. [Sports governing bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_governing_bodies "Sports governing bodies") in [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_union_in_England "Rugby union in England"), [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_union_in_Scotland "Rugby union in Scotland"), [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_union_in_Wales "Rugby union in Wales") and [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_union_in_Ireland "Rugby union in Ireland") organise and regulate the game separately. Every four years the Home Nations make a combined team known as the [British and Irish Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_and_Irish_Lions "British and Irish Lions") which tours Australia, New Zealand and South Africa.

The United Kingdom hosted the [Summer Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_Olympic_Games "Summer Olympic Games") in [1908](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1908_Summer_Olympics "1908 Summer Olympics"), [1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Summer_Olympics "1948 Summer Olympics") and [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics "2012 Summer Olympics"), with London acting as the host city on all three occasions. Birmingham hosted the [2022 Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Commonwealth_Games "2022 Commonwealth Games"), the seventh time a country of the United Kingdom hosted the [Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Games "Commonwealth Games") (England, Scotland and Wales have each hosted the Commonwealth Games at least once).

### Symbols

The [flag of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_the_United_Kingdom "Flag of the United Kingdom") is the [Union Flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Flag "Union Flag"), which is also referred to as the Union Jack. It was created in 1606 by the superimposition of the [flag of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_England "Flag of England"), representing [Saint George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_George "Saint George"), on the [flag of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Scotland "Flag of Scotland"), representing [Saint Andrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Andrew "Saint Andrew"), and was updated in 1801 with the addition of [Saint Patrick's Flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Patrick%27s_Flag "Saint Patrick's Flag"). Wales is not represented in the Union Flag, as Wales had been conquered and [annexed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexed "Annexed") to England prior to the formation of the United Kingdom. The possibility of redesigning the Union Flag to include representation of Wales was discussed in 2007. The [national anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_anthem "National anthem") of the United Kingdom is "[God Save the King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_Save_the_King "God Save the King")", with "King" replaced with "Queen" in the lyrics whenever the monarch is a woman.

[England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_symbols_of_England "National symbols of England"), [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_symbols_of_Wales "National symbols of Wales") and [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_symbols_of_Scotland "National symbols of Scotland") each have their own national symbols, including their national flags. [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_symbols_of_Northern_Ireland "National symbols of Northern Ireland") also has symbols, [many of which are shared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_symbols_of_Ireland,_the_Republic_of_Ireland_and_Northern_Ireland "National symbols of Ireland, the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland") with the [Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland "Republic of Ireland"), but has no official flag. Beside [the Lion and the Unicorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lion_and_the_Unicorn "The Lion and the Unicorn") and the [Welsh Dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Dragon "Welsh Dragon") of heraldry, the [bulldog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulldog "Bulldog") is also used and commonly represented with the Union Flag. Great Britain, rather than the United Kingdom as a whole, also has [personifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_personification "National personification") representing it, two of which are [Britannia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britannia "Britannia") and [John Bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bull "John Bull").
