# James Talarico

**James Dell Talarico** ([/ˌtæləˈriːkoʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*TAL-ə-REE-koh*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"), born May 17, 1989) is an American politician, Presbyterian [seminarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminarian "Seminarian"), and educator serving since 2018 as a member of the [Texas House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_House_of_Representatives "Texas House of Representatives"). He is the [Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)") nominee in the [2026 U.S. Senate election in Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Texas "2026 United States Senate election in Texas").

Born in [Round Rock, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Round_Rock,_Texas "Round Rock, Texas"), Talarico graduated from the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin "University of Texas at Austin") with a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") in [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government "Government") in 2011, and from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") with a [Master of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Education "Master of Education") degree in [education policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_policy "Education policy") in 2016. Before entering politics, Talarico was a middle school English teacher and an executive director for an education nonprofit. He is serving his fourth term in the Texas House of Representatives, having first been elected in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Texas_House_of_Representatives_election "2018 Texas House of Representatives election"). While serving in the Texas House, Talarico started to pursue a [Master of Divinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Divinity "Master of Divinity") at [Austin Presbyterian Theological Seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Presbyterian_Theological_Seminary "Austin Presbyterian Theological Seminary").

In 2025, he announced his candidacy for the United States Senate in [2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Texas "2026 United States Senate election in Texas"). He defeated U.S. representative [Jasmine Crockett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasmine_Crockett "Jasmine Crockett") in the Democratic primary and will face the Republican nominee, Texas attorney general [Ken Paxton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Paxton "Ken Paxton"), in [the general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Texas "2026 United States Senate election in Texas").

## Early life and education

James Talarico was born in [Round Rock, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Round_Rock,_Texas "Round Rock, Texas"), on May 17, 1989, to Tamara Causey, who separated from his biological father when he was seven weeks old. A few months later Tamara married Mark Talarico, who adopted James and gave him his surname. Talarico attended [Round Rock Independent School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Round_Rock_Independent_School_District "Round Rock Independent School District") schools and graduated from [McNeil High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McNeil_High_School "McNeil High School") in [Williamson County, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamson_County,_Texas "Williamson County, Texas"), where he competed in speech and debate. He also acted in the school's drama productions, including playing Danny Zuko in *[Grease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grease_(musical) "Grease (musical)")*. Talarico's maternal grandfather, Jimmy Causey, was a [Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptists_in_the_United_States "Baptists in the United States") preacher at several churches in [South Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Texas "South Texas").

Before earning his Bachelor of Arts degree in [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science "Political science") from the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin "University of Texas at Austin"), he organized students for tuition relief. Talarico later earned a Master of Education degree in education policy from the [Harvard Graduate School of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Graduate_School_of_Education "Harvard Graduate School of Education"), and went on to receive a [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts "Master of Arts") in theological studies at [Austin Presbyterian Theological Seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Presbyterian_Theological_Seminary "Austin Presbyterian Theological Seminary") but as of June 2026 had not yet completed his [Master of Divinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Divinity "Master of Divinity") there.

## Early career

In 2011, Talarico joined [Teach For America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teach_For_America "Teach For America") and taught [sixth-grade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth-grade "Sixth-grade") English language arts at Rhodes Middle School on the west side of [San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio "San Antonio"). Talarico has said his experience teaching [at-risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/At-risk_students "At-risk students") and low-income students shaped his understanding of Texas's [socioeconomic disparities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socioeconomic_status "Socioeconomic status") and his advocacy for increased school funding and student well-being. After two years of teaching, he became [Central Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Texas "Central Texas") executive director for [Reasoning Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reasoning_Mind "Reasoning Mind"), a nonprofit that implements computer-based math lessons in classrooms.

## Texas House of Representatives

Talarico was first elected to the [Texas House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_House_of_Representatives "Texas House of Representatives") in 2018 at age 29, becoming the legislature's youngest member during the 2019 session. During his four terms in the Republican-majority legislature, Talarico served as lead author of nearly 200 bills, 16 of which became law. Eight of the enacted bills addressed education, childcare, or youth workforce development.

### First term (2018–2019)

Talarico launched his campaign for the Texas House shortly after incumbent state legislator [Larry Gonzales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Gonzales_(politician) "Larry Gonzales (politician)") chose not to run for reelection. He won the concurrent [special](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/By-election "By-election") and general elections against Republican nominee Cynthia Flores, garnering media attention for walking the full length of the district. He was sworn into the Texas House of Representatives on November 20, 2018, and appointed to the Public Education and Juvenile Justice Committees.

In the [86th Texas Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/86th_Texas_Legislature "86th Texas Legislature"), Talarico filed the Whole Student Agenda, a 24-bill public education policy legislative package. The legislature passed two bills from this list. The first, HB 3012, required that students suspended from school have an alternative means to receive coursework. HB 455, also sponsored by Talarico, was written to provide a standardized school recess policy. Governor [Greg Abbott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Abbott "Greg Abbott") vetoed the bill.

Talarico was a coauthor of House Bill 3, the school-finance legislation enacted in 2019. The law increased pre-student funding, provided funding for teacher pay increases, and funded full-day prekindergarten for eligible four-year-olds.

### Second term (2020–2021)

Talarico defeated former [Hutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutto,_Texas "Hutto, Texas") city council member Lucio Valdez with 51.5% of the vote. In the [87th Texas Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/87th_Texas_Legislature "87th Texas Legislature"), he was reappointed to the Public Education and Juvenile Justice Committees and appointed to the Calendars Committee. During the 87th legislative session, Talarico filed HB 54, also known as Javier Ambler's Law, and the legislature passed it. It prohibits state law enforcement agencies, except [game wardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_wardens "Game wardens"), from entering into contracts with reality TV shows that film them in the line of duty. This was in response to the role *[Live PD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_PD "Live PD")* is alleged to have played in the [killing of Javier Ambler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Javier_Ambler "Killing of Javier Ambler") by [Williamson County, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamson_County,_Texas "Williamson County, Texas"), police. Talarico had previously criticized Sheriff Robert Chody's handling of the incident, calling for his resignation.

Talarico was the primary author of HB 30, which provides a path for minors in the criminal justice system who have been adjudicated as adults or who are eligible under the [Individuals with Disabilities Education Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individuals_with_Disabilities_Education_Act "Individuals with Disabilities Education Act") to earn a high school diploma instead of pursuing a high school equivalency.[*[non-primary source needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:No_original_research#Primary,_secondary_and_tertiary_sources "Wikipedia:No original research")*]

Talarico introduced House Bill 41 to establish class-size limits for public school prekindergarten programs. Similar legislation, Senate Bill 2081, became law in 2021, establishing a maximum of 22 students in covered prekindergarten classes, subject to exceptions and waivers.

In 2018, Talarico was diagnosed with [type 1 diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_1_diabetes "Type 1 diabetes") during a five-day stint in the ICU after a campaign event where he walked 25 miles (40 km) across his district. After his diagnosis, he paid $684 for his first 30-day supply of insulin. Talarico was the House joint sponsor helping to pass House Bill 82, capping [insulin costs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medication_costs "Medication costs") at $25 per month. In 2021, Talarico was a joint author of House Bill 82 and one of the House sponsors of its Senate companion, Senate Bill 827. SB 827 became law, [capping out-of-pocket copays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prescription_drug_prices_in_the_United_States "Prescription drug prices in the United States") for a 30-day supply of insulin at $25 for people covered by state-regulated health plans.

In the summer of 2021, Democrats in the Texas House of Representatives, including Talarico, organized a [quorum break](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quorum-busting "Quorum-busting") in an attempt to stop the passage of the Election Integrity Protection Act of 2021. They flew to [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C."), to lobby the Senate to pass the [John Lewis Voting Rights Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lewis_Voting_Rights_Act "John Lewis Voting Rights Act") and the [For the People Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_the_People_Act "For the People Act"), which would have superseded parts of the state legislation. Talarico was one of the first Democrats to return to Texas as the quorum break progressed, arguing that the effort had achieved its goals, that Democrats needed to reduce the harm of Republican legislation, and that an indefinite quorum break was unsustainable. Some representatives who remained in D.C. strongly criticized him for this. Quorum was eventually reestablished and the legislation passed. At the end of the legislative session, *[Texas Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Monthly "Texas Monthly")* magazine named Talarico one of the Top 10 Best Legislators.

### Third term (2022–2023)

After his district was made significantly more Republican during the 2020 redistricting process, Talarico announced he would run in neighboring House District 50, a safe Democratic seat being vacated by [Celia Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celia_Israel "Celia Israel"). His previous district was a swing district. Talarico won the primary election with 78.5% of the vote and the general election with 76.8%. During the [88th Texas Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/88th_Texas_Legislature "88th Texas Legislature"), he was the primary author of House Bill 25, which would create the Texas Wholesale Prescription Drug Importation Program and allow Texas to import lower-cost Canadian medications approved by the [U.S. Food and Drug Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Food_and_Drug_Administration "U.S. Food and Drug Administration").

Talarico was an outspoken critic of legislation that would have required the display of the [Ten Commandments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Commandments "Ten Commandments") in all elementary and secondary classrooms, on the constitutional grounds of [separation of church and state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_church_and_state_in_the_United_States "Separation of church and state in the United States"). He called the measure "un-American" and "un-Christian". The bill did not pass the state house, and therefore failed.

Talarico was a House sponsor of Senate Bill 1145, which allowed cities and counties to offer property-tax exemptions to qualifying [childcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_care "Child care") facilities. The measure was intended to reduce providers' operating costs amid concerns about the high cost of [daycare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daycare "Daycare"). Eligible facilities must participate in the Texas Rising Star quality-rating program and have at least 20% of enrolled children receiving state childcare subsidies.

### Fourth term (2024–2025)

Talarico defeated Nathan Boynton in the Democratic primary and was unopposed in the general election. In 2025, he continued to oppose placing the [Ten Commandments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Commandments "Ten Commandments") in Texas public schools. The specific legislation, SB 10, required every classroom to visibly display a poster containing the Ten Commandments, sized at least 16 by 20 inches. When the bill was first brought to the floor of the Texas House, Talarico called a [point of order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_of_order "Point of order") that delayed its passage. The bill ultimately passed the legislature, but videos of his remarks against it went viral and led to an appearance on *[The Joe Rogan Experience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Joe_Rogan_Experience "The Joe Rogan Experience")*.

Talarico was a joint author of House Bill 3133, enacted in 2025, which established reporting and removal requirements for explicit [deepfake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepfake "Deepfake") material on social media platforms. The law required platforms to acknowledge reports, remove reported material and known identical copies, and take measures to prevent material determined to be explicit deepfake content from being reposted.

During the legislative debate over [private school vouchers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_school_vouchers "Private school vouchers"), Talarico, who opposed the legislation, attempted to amend the bill to have a statewide referendum determine whether the program would go into effect. The legislation passed and was signed into law without the amendment. In August 2025, Talarico was one of 51 Democratic Texas House members who broke quorum to delay the passage of mid-decade [new congressional maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Texas_redistricting "2025 Texas redistricting"). While he was absent from the state, Attorney General [Ken Paxton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Paxton "Ken Paxton") filed a lawsuit to expel Talarico and 12 other representatives from the House by declaring their seats vacant. Talarico and the others named in the suit returned to the state before the [Texas Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Supreme_Court "Texas Supreme Court") ruled in the case. In May 2026, the court determined it did not have jurisdiction to resolve a legislative dispute and dismissed the case.

## 2026 U.S. Senate campaign

On September 9, 2025, Talarico announced his candidacy for the [2026 U.S. Senate election in Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Texas "2026 United States Senate election in Texas"). He and U.S. Representative [Jasmine Crockett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasmine_Crockett "Jasmine Crockett") vied for the Democratic nomination. In February 2026, the *[Houston Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Chronicle "Houston Chronicle")*, the *[Austin American-Statesman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_American-Statesman "Austin American-Statesman")*, *[The Dallas Morning News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dallas_Morning_News "The Dallas Morning News")*, and the *[Fort Worth Star-Telegram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Worth_Star-Telegram "Fort Worth Star-Telegram")* endorsed Talarico. Later in February, the FCC opened an investigation into Talarico's appearance on *[The View](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_View "The View")*, citing a potential violation of the [equal-time rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal-time_rule "Equal-time rule"). Late-night and talk shows were exempt from the requirement until an FCC rule change in January 2026.

On February 16, 2026, Talarico was scheduled to appear on *[The Late Show with Stephen Colbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Late_Show_with_Stephen_Colbert "The Late Show with Stephen Colbert")*. Colbert said [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS "CBS") canceled the interview because of the [Trump administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_policy_of_the_second_Trump_administration "Domestic policy of the second Trump administration")'s "intensifying pressure against broadcast TV networks". He said the network's lawyers had instructed him not to have Talarico on the show or mention the cancellation. Against their advice, he interviewed Talarico and spoke publicly about the cancellation. Colbert accused CBS of censorship and posted the interview to the show's [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube") page instead, where it had gained 7.3 million views by February 18, making it the most viewed interview segment on *The Late Show*'s channel in a year.

On March 3, Talarico won the Democratic primary, securing the party's nomination in the November general election. In the general election, he faces Texas Attorney General [Ken Paxton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Paxton "Ken Paxton"), who won the Republican primary [runoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Texas#Runoff "2026 United States Senate election in Texas") on May 26. Former President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") appeared at an event with Talarico in May 2026.In September 2026, Talarico was interviewed on *[Jimmy Kimmel Live!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Kimmel_Live! "Jimmy Kimmel Live!")*; after the [FCC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FCC "FCC") threatened to apply the equal-time rule, the segment was not aired on television, but was uploaded to [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube").On the campaign trail, Talarico often tells a story from when he was a middle school teacher about a student who was kicked out of fifth grade for bringing a knife to school and threatening his teacher. Talarico later had the boy as a student and said that his behavior improved before he had a fight and was expelled from school again. Talarico blamed the events on the Texas legislature cutting [public school funding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_school_funding_in_the_United_States "Public school funding in the United States"), which he said led to the school's therapist being laid off.

Since Talarico won the nomination, his campaign has been noted for its record-breaking fundraising. In the first quarter of 2026, it raised over $27 million, more than any Senate campaign ever for that period, and more than double the totals raised by the two previous Democratic nominees' campaigns in Texas during the same period, with [Colin Allred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Allred "Colin Allred") raising $9.5 million in the first quarter of [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_Senate_election_in_Texas "2024 United States Senate election in Texas"), and [Beto O'Rourke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beto_O%27Rourke "Beto O'Rourke") raising $6.7 million in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_United_States_Senate_election_in_Texas "2018 United States Senate election in Texas"). The campaign again set a record for fundraising in the second quarter of 2026 by raising over $30 million, which again surpassed the approximately $10 million raised by both O'Rourke and Allred in the same period. The pattern continued into the third quarter as the campaign raised a $64 million, the largest quarterly total by a US Senate candidate ever, surpassing the $57 million record that had been set by [Jaime Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaime_Harrison "Jaime Harrison") in the [2020 South Carolina Senate Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_Senate_election_in_South_Carolina "2020 United States Senate election in South Carolina").

## Political positions

Talarico has been described as a [populist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populist "Populist").

### Cannabis

In March 2021, Talarico introduced House Bill 4089 to legalize adult cannabis use and provide for the expungement of past convictions. During the 2025 legislative session, he opposed Senate Bill 3, which sought to ban most hemp-derived THC products, arguing that the ban would undermine the state industry and shift consumers toward unregulated markets.

### Economic policy

In August 2026, Talarico announced a "New American Dream" economic policy agenda. The plan includes repealing tax cuts for the top 1% of earners; closing "billionaire tax loopholes"; providing universal childcare and paid family leave; providing down payment assistance for first-time homebuyers; cutting regulations on home building; providing grants to small businesses; an education investment resembling the [G.I. Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G.I._Bill "G.I. Bill"); and capping credit card interest rates. He has criticized [supply-side economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply-side_economics "Supply-side economics") as [trickle-down economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trickle-down_economics "Trickle-down economics") and a "false gospel" and has blamed affordability issues on corruption.

### Energy policy

In August 2026, Talarico released an energy plan which proposed investments in oil and gas, [nuclear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power "Nuclear power"), [wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power "Wind power"), solar, and [geothermal energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_energy "Geothermal energy") and was described as an "all-of-the-above" policy.

In August 2026, Talarico announced a "Hold Data Centers Accountable Plan", which included ending tax incentives for [data centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_center "Data center"), requiring data centers to pay for infrastructure and grid interconnection costs, requiring data centers to use closed-loop water systems, and allowing communities to approve or reject data center projects in their area. His proposals also included repealing Texas's data center sales tax exemption and establishing minimum federal standards under new development.

He proposed a plan to suspend [federal gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_gas_tax "Federal gas tax") and diesel taxes after prices rose due to the [2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war "2026 Iran war").

### Foreign policy

Talarico supports a [two-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-state_solution "Two-state solution") to the [Israeli–Palestinian conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_conflict "Israeli–Palestinian conflict"). He has condemned what he called Israel's "[atrocities in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_genocide "Gaza genocide")" and "[war crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_war_crimes_in_the_Gaza_war "Israeli war crimes in the Gaza war")", and has criticized its role in the [Gaza Strip famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Strip_famine "Gaza Strip famine"). He opposes offensive [U.S. aid to Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._aid_to_Israel "U.S. aid to Israel"). He supports banning the sale of offensive weapons to Israel. Talarico is a critic of the [pro-Israel lobby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-Israel_lobby_in_the_United_States "Pro-Israel lobby in the United States") group [AIPAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIPAC "AIPAC"). In 2025, he criticized Democrats for supporting the [Gaza war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_war "Gaza war") during the [2024 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_presidential_election "2024 presidential election"). He called the war "the moral test of our time".

Talarico has criticized the [2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war "2026 Iran war"). After the initial strikes on February 28, he wrote, "No more forever wars" on [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_(social_network) "X (social network)"). In an interview on *[The Briefing with Jen Psaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Briefing_with_Jen_Psaki "The Briefing with Jen Psaki")*, Talarico said the U.S. could prevent Iran from obtaining nuclear weapons without "bombing innocent schoolchildren", referring to the [2026 Minab school attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Minab_school_attack "2026 Minab school attack"). He also called for "immediate negotiations" to end the war.

### Healthcare

Talarico supports [healthcare reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_reform_in_the_United_States "Healthcare reform in the United States") and [universal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_healthcare "Universal healthcare") [healthcare as a human right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_as_a_human_right "Healthcare as a human right"). He supports making buy-in [Medicare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicare_(United_States) "Medicare (United States)") or a [public health insurance option](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health_insurance_option "Public health insurance option") available to every American, calling his healthcare plan "Medicare for Y'all".

In August 2026, Talarico unveiled a [healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_the_United_States "Healthcare in the United States") plan focused on breaking up vertically integrated healthcare companies, including pharmacy benefit managers, insurance companies, and hospital networks. The proposal included measures to increase transparency in pharmacy benefit manager pricing, encourage the development and approval of generic drugs, and cap out-of-pocket healthcare costs.

### Immigration

In February 2026, Talarico said he supported the [Dignity Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DIGNIDAD_Act "DIGNIDAD Act"), a proposed bipartisan immigration reform bill. In a January 2026 debate, he called for the increase in funding for [ICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Immigration_and_Customs_Enforcement "United States Immigration and Customs Enforcement") during the [Trump administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_presidency_of_Donald_Trump "Second presidency of Donald Trump") to be diverted to other services. Talarico has described himself as a "border security Democrat" and criticized former President [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden")'s border policy, saying it "created chaos in our border communities".

### Institutional reform

During his 2026 Senate campaign, Talarico has pledged to reject corporate [political action committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_action_committee "Political action committee") contributions and advocated overturning *[Citizens United v. FEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens_United_v._FEC "Citizens United v. FEC")*, banning [super PACs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_PAC "Super PAC") and the use of [dark money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_money "Dark money") in elections.

Talarico has proposed institutional reforms including [term limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Term_limits "Term limits") for members of U.S. Congress, banning congressional stock trading, and banning [gerrymandering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerrymandering_in_the_United_States "Gerrymandering in the United States"). He supports eliminating the [filibuster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filibuster_in_the_United_States_Senate "Filibuster in the United States Senate") under the current rules in the U.S. Senate, but supports the [talking filibuster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filibuster_in_the_United_States_Senate#Talking_filibuster "Filibuster in the United States Senate"). He has advocated for [reform of the U.S. Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_reform_in_the_United_States "Supreme Court reform in the United States") including through term limits. He supports an enforceable [code of ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_of_ethics "Code of ethics") for the court's justices, "for recusals, financial disclosures, and limitations on accepting gifts." He said he is "open" to increasing the number of justices.

### Social issues

In 2022, Talarico proposed a constitutional amendment that would prevent the Texas Legislature from further restricting abortion access. In 2025, he proposed an amendment prohibiting legislative restrictions on abortion and related healthcare people choose in consultation with licensed physicians.

Talarico supports legal abortion. In a 2025 interview, he said there is no "set Christian orthodoxy" on abortion and cited Mary's consent in the [Annunciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annunciation "Annunciation") in explaining his view that "creation has to be done with consent" and freedom. Talarico stated in 2025 that he would support the [Equality Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equality_Act_(United_States) "Equality Act (United States)") if elected, and as a representative, Talarico voted against a 2021 Texas bill to ban [transgender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender "Transgender") [student-athletes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender_people_in_sports "Transgender people in sports") from teams that do not match their sex assigned at birth, and against bills targeting [gender-affirming care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender_health_care "Transgender health care") for minors.

In a 2026 interview with [Fox News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_News "Fox News"), he said he opposed trans student-athletes in girls' sports "if it endangers safety or fairness", and said he had voted against the 2021 bill because the legislature did not consider amendments to reduce its scope. Following the interview, Talarico faced scrutiny from [LGBTQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_people "LGBTQ people") rights supporters, who believed that his stances were ambiguous, and that the campaign was distancing itself from his previous pro-LGBTQ sentiment; Talarico's campaign told the *Washington Post* that he had "not shifted any of his stances". At a fundraising event backed by [Equality Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equality_Texas "Equality Texas") and [Human Rights Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Campaign "Human Rights Campaign") that the interview had been presented without full context, that he supported trans student-athletes in girls' sports if it did not interfere with safety or fairness, and that politics should not interfere with the decisions of doctors. However, he has also aired campaign advertising stating that he was opposed to [gender-affirming surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender-affirming_surgery "Gender-affirming surgery") for minors.

#### Religion

Talarico has been described as a [progressive Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Christianity "Progressive Christianity"), but has objected to the term, saying he believes not in "a progressive or conservative Christianity" but in a "Biblical Christianity." Talarico has cited his faith and the [teachings of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teachings_of_Jesus "Teachings of Jesus") as the reason for launching his political career. He said he was following the commandments to [love God and one's neighbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Commandment "Great Commandment"). He said politics is "another word for how we treat our neighbors".

Talarico is a critic of [Christian nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_nationalism "Christian nationalism") and has called it "a cancer on our religion". He said, "there's nothing Christian about Christian nationalism". In a 2023 guest sermon, Talarico called Christian nationalism "the worship of power—social power, economic power, political power, in the name of Christ". He has said Christian nationalists have turned Jesus "into a gun-toting, gay-bashing, science-denying, money-loving, fear-mongering fascist" and that it is "incumbent on all Christians to confront it and denounce it".

## Personal life

Talarico is a member of the [Presbyterian Church (USA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian_Church_(USA) "Presbyterian Church (USA)"). He is active in St. Andrew's Presbyterian Church in [Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin,_Texas "Austin, Texas"). As of May 2026, Talarico has been in a four‑year relationship with Brianna Menard, who previously served as his chief of staff in his Texas House office; she left the position after they developed feelings for each other.
