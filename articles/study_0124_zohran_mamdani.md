# Zohran Mamdani

**Zohran Kwame Mamdani** (born October 18, 1991) is an American politician who has served since 2026 as the 112th [mayor of New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_New_York_City "Mayor of New York City"). A member of the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)") and the [Democratic Socialists of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Socialists_of_America "Democratic Socialists of America"), he represented the [36th district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York%27s_36th_State_Assembly_district "New York's 36th State Assembly district") in the [New York State Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Assembly "New York State Assembly") from 2021 to 2025. Mamdani is New York City's first [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim "Muslim") and first [Asian American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_American "Asian American") mayor.

Born in [Kampala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampala "Kampala"), Uganda, to parents of [Indian descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_people "Indian people"), Mamdani moved to [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") at seven years old and graduated from [Bowdoin College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowdoin_College "Bowdoin College") in 2014 with a bachelor's degree in [Africana studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africana_studies "Africana studies"). After graduation, he worked as a housing counselor and [rapper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapper "Rapper"), and entered [New York City politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_New_York_City "Politics of New York City") as a campaign manager for [Khader El-Yateem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khader_El-Yateem "Khader El-Yateem") and [Ross Barkan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Barkan "Ross Barkan"). He was first elected to the [New York State Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Assembly "New York State Assembly") in 2020, defeating five-term incumbent [Aravella Simotas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aravella_Simotas "Aravella Simotas") in the Democratic primary. Representing [Astoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astoria,_Queens "Astoria, Queens") and [Long Island City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island_City "Long Island City"), he was reelected without opposition in 2022 and 2024.

In October 2024, Mamdani announced his candidacy for mayor of New York City in the [2025 mayoral election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_New_York_City_mayoral_election "2025 New York City mayoral election"). A [democratic socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_socialist "Democratic socialist"), Mamdani campaigned on a [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism_in_the_United_States "Progressivism in the United States"), affordability-focused platform, supporting [fare-free city buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fare-free_city_buses "Fare-free city buses"), universal [child care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_care "Child care"), city-owned grocery stores, a [rent freeze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rent_freeze "Rent freeze") on [rent-stabilized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rent-stabilized "Rent-stabilized") units, additional [affordable housing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affordable_housing "Affordable housing") units, and a $30 [minimum wage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_wage_in_the_United_States "Minimum wage in the United States") by 2030. He also expressed support for [LGBTQ rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_rights_in_the_United_States "LGBTQ rights in the United States"), comprehensive [public safety reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_reform_in_the_United_States "Police reform in the United States"), and tax increases on corporations and those earning above [$1 million annually](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-net-worth_individual "High-net-worth individual"). He won [the Democratic primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_New_York_City_Democratic_mayoral_primary "2025 New York City Democratic mayoral primary") in June 2025, defeating former governor [Andrew Cuomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Cuomo "Andrew Cuomo") in an [upset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upset_(competition) "Upset (competition)"), and was elected mayor in the November general election.

## Early life and education

Zohran Kwame Mamdani was born on October 18, 1991, at [Nsambya Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nsambya_Hospital "Nsambya Hospital") in [Kampala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampala "Kampala"), Uganda, the only child of postcolonialist academic [Mahmood Mamdani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmood_Mamdani "Mahmood Mamdani") and filmmaker [Mira Nair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mira_Nair "Mira Nair"). He was given his middle name, [Kwame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwame "Kwame"), by his father in honor of [Kwame Nkrumah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwame_Nkrumah "Kwame Nkrumah"), the first [president of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Ghana "President of Ghana"). Both his parents are of [Indian descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_people "Indian people"). His father is a [Gujarati Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_Muslim "Gujarati Muslim") who was born in [Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai "Mumbai") and raised in Uganda. His mother is a [Punjabi Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_Hindu "Punjabi Hindu"). His paternal grandparents were born in present-day [Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania "Tanzania"), and his father's family was part of the [Indian diaspora in Southeast Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_diaspora_in_Southeast_Africa "Indian diaspora in Southeast Africa"). The Mamdani family belongs to the [Khoja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoja_(community)#Twelver_Khojas "Khoja (community)") [Twelver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelver_Shi%27ism "Twelver Shi'ism") community.

Mamdani lived in Kampala until the age of five, when his family moved to [Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town "Cape Town"), South Africa, after his father was appointed head of [African studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_studies "African studies") at the [University of Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cape_Town "University of Cape Town"). He attended [St. George's Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._George%27s_Grammar_School_(Cape_Town) "St. George's Grammar School (Cape Town)") in [Mowbray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mowbray,_Cape_Town "Mowbray, Cape Town") from 1996 to 1998, during the early [post-apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-apartheid "Post-apartheid") years. He later said that the experience of living in Cape Town "taught me what inequality looks like up close ... [and] that justice has to be more than an idea; it has to be material".

The family moved to the United States and settled in New York City when Mamdani was seven, and he was raised in Manhattan's [Morningside Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morningside_Heights "Morningside Heights") neighborhood. He has called his upbringing "privileged", saying, "I never had to want for something, and yet I knew that was not in any way the reality for most New Yorkers." As a child, he was often present on his mother's film sets, where film crew members variously called him "Z", "Zoru", "Fadoose", and "Nonstop Mamdani".

### Education

Mamdani attended the [Bank Street School for Children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_Street_College_of_Education#Bank_Street_School_for_Children "Bank Street College of Education") on the [Upper West Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_West_Side "Upper West Side") of Manhattan, where he successfully ran as the independent candidate in a middle school [mock election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mock_election "Mock election"), adopting a platform of "equal rights, anti-war policies that proposed spending money on education rather than the military". In 2003, he returned to Kampala for a year and attended school during his father's [sabbatical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabbatical "Sabbatical") there; his paternal grandparents and aunt still lived there and helped take care of him while his father was working on the book *Good Muslim, Bad Muslim*.

In 2010, Mamdani graduated from the [Bronx High School of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_High_School_of_Science "Bronx High School of Science") in [Kingsbridge Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsbridge_Heights "Kingsbridge Heights"), [Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx "Bronx"), where he co-founded the school's first [cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket "Cricket") team and unsuccessfully ran for student body vice president. He also played soccer with the West Side Soccer League.

Mamdani attended [Bowdoin College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowdoin_College "Bowdoin College") in [Brunswick, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunswick,_Maine "Brunswick, Maine"), where he co-founded the school's chapter of [Students for Justice in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Students_for_Justice_in_Palestine "Students for Justice in Palestine"). He was a regular contributor to the campus newspaper *[The Bowdoin Orient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bowdoin_Orient "The Bowdoin Orient")*, covering politics, culture, and sports in his columns, "Kwame's Kolumnalu" and "Talk of the Quad". In January 2014, he co-authored an [op-ed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op-ed "Op-ed") in the *[Bangor Daily News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangor_Daily_News "Bangor Daily News")* urging Bowdoin to join the [American Studies Association's boycott of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Studies_Association%27s_boycott_of_Israel "American Studies Association's boycott of Israel") and criticizing the college's president, [Barry Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Mills_(college_president) "Barry Mills (college president)").

Mamdani graduated from Bowdoin in 2014 with a bachelor's degree in [Africana studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africana_studies "Africana studies").

## Early career

### 2015–2019: Hip-hop and rap

Mamdani is a fan of [hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip-hop "Hip-hop") and has composed, performed, and produced rap music. Under the moniker Young Cardamom, he collaborated with his best friend, Ugandan rapper HAB (Abdul Bar Hussein), whose origins are in [South Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Sudan "South Sudan"), as Young Cardamom & HAB. Their first song, "Kanda [Chap Chap]", was about [chapati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapati "Chapati"), an [Indian flatbread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_flatbread "Indian flatbread"). They performed tracks from their 2016 [EP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EP "EP") *Sidda Mukyaalo* ("No going back to the village") at the [Nyege Nyege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyege_Nyege "Nyege Nyege") festival. The pair rapped in languages including [Nubi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubi "Nubi"), [Luganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luganda "Luganda"), [Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili "Swahili"), and English, partly to create a unique Ugandan style of rap rather than imitating American rap, and partly to convey that Ugandan residents with roots in other countries are all Ugandan. The chapati was chosen as a symbol because it originates in the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent") but has become a Ugandan staple. In their music, they addressed social issues in Uganda, such as corruption and "black and brown relations", as well as [colonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonisation_of_Africa "Colonisation of Africa"). Young Cardamom & HAB were nominated for "Rookie of the Year" at the inaugural Ugandan (UG) Hip Hop Awards.

Mamdani curated and produced [the soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_of_Katwe_(Original_Motion_Picture_Soundtrack) "Queen of Katwe (Original Motion Picture Soundtrack)") for his mother Mira Nair's 2016 film *[Queen of Katwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_of_Katwe "Queen of Katwe")*, for which he was nominated for a 2017 [Guild of Music Supervisors Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guild_of_Music_Supervisors_Awards "Guild of Music Supervisors Awards"); he co-wrote the song "#1 Spice" with HAB for the film. Mamdani also appears as an extra in the film, and is credited as [third assistant director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_assistant_director "Third assistant director"). Nair subsequently offered him parts in the stage musical adaptation of her film *[Monsoon Wedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon_Wedding "Monsoon Wedding")*, after he took part in [stage readings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stage_reading "Stage reading") for the show, and her [television adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Suitable_Boy_(TV_series) "A Suitable Boy (TV series)") of the [Vikram Seth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikram_Seth "Vikram Seth") novel *[A Suitable Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Suitable_Boy "A Suitable Boy")*, but he declined.

In 2017, Mamdani released the song "Salaam" under the name Zohran Kwame. In the song, Mamdani expressed support for the Holy Land Five, five people connected to the [Holy Land Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Land_Foundation_for_Relief_and_Development "Holy Land Foundation for Relief and Development") charity. In April 2019, under the moniker Mr. Cardamom, he released the single "Nani", an homage to his grandmother. Actress and food writer [Madhur Jaffrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhur_Jaffrey "Madhur Jaffrey") portrays Mamdani's grandmother in the video, which pays tribute to Jaffrey and [New York's South Asian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asians_in_New_York_City "Asians in New York City").

### 2015–2019: Political involvement

Mamdani entered [New York City politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_politics "New York City politics") as a volunteer for Ali Najmi's campaign in the [2015 special election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City%27s_23rd_City_Council_district#2015_special "New York City's 23rd City Council district") for the [23rd district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City%27s_23rd_City_Council_district "New York City's 23rd City Council district") of the City Council. Mamdani was inspired to join Najmi's campaign after learning that he was supported by [Heems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heems "Heems"), a New York rapper of Indian descent and co-founder of alternative hip-hop group [Das Racist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Das_Racist "Das Racist"). Specifically, Mamdani attributes his involvement in local politics to a 2015 *[The Village Voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Village_Voice "The Village Voice")* article about Najmi and Heems, whom he described as one of his favorite rappers.

In 2017, Mamdani joined the New York City chapter of the Democratic Socialists of America (DSA) and worked for the campaign of [New York City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Council "New York City Council") candidate [Khader El-Yateem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khader_El-Yateem "Khader El-Yateem"), a [Palestinian Lutheran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Christians "Palestinian Christians") [minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_(Christianity) "Minister (Christianity)") and democratic socialist from [Bay Ridge, Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_Ridge,_Brooklyn "Bay Ridge, Brooklyn"). Part of Mamdani's motivation for joining the DSA was its pro-Palestine stance, which aligned with his own prior activism. Mamdani served as the campaign manager for [Ross Barkan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Barkan "Ross Barkan")'s 2018 unsuccessful bid for the [New York State Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Senate "New York State Senate") and was also a field organizer for fellow democratic socialist [Tiffany Cabán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiffany_Cab%C3%A1n "Tiffany Cabán")'s close-run but also unsuccessful 2019 campaign for [Queens County district attorney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_County_district_attorney "Queens County district attorney").

Starting in 2018, Mamdani worked as a [foreclosure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreclosure "Foreclosure") prevention and housing counselor. There, he assisted lower-income immigrant homeowners in [Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens "Queens") with [eviction notices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eviction_notice "Eviction notice") and efforts to prevent them from being evicted from their homes. He said the experience motivated him to run for office to address the housing and affordability crisis.

## New York State Assembly (2020–2025)

In October 2019, Mamdani announced his campaign to represent New York's 36th State Assembly district, which encompasses Astoria and [Long Island City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island_City "Long Island City") in Queens. He was endorsed by the DSA, running on a platform of [housing reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_housing "Right to housing"), [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_reform_in_the_United_States "Police reform in the United States") and [prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner_rights_in_the_United_States "Prisoner rights in the United States") reform, and [public ownership of utilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_utility "Public utility"). Mamdani's June 2020 [primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_election "Primary election") victory over five-term Democratic incumbent [Aravella Simotas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aravella_Simotas "Aravella Simotas") took almost a month to call, and he won the general election with no [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States) "Republican Party (United States)") opposition in November. Mamdani was reelected without opposition in 2022 and 2024.

Mamdani was a member of the DSA's nine-member ["State Socialists in Office" bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Socialists_in_Office "New York State Socialists in Office") in New York and a member of the Muslim Democratic Club of New York. He was the keynote speaker at the 2023 DSA convention, saying, "We are special as DSA electeds not because of ourselves; we are special because of our organization".

By January 2025, Mamdani was a member of nine Assembly committees: the Committee on Aging; the Committee on Cities; the Committee on Election Law; the Committee on Energy; the Committee on Real Property Taxation; the Black, Puerto Rican, Hispanic & Asian Legislative Caucus; the Puerto Rican/Hispanic Task Force; the Asian Pacific American Task Force; and the Task Force on New Americans.

By May 2025, Mamdani had been the primary sponsor of 20 bills in the Assembly—three of which became law—and the co-sponsor of 238 bills. As a member of the Assembly, he helped launch a successful fare-free bus pilot program and participated in a [hunger strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunger_strike "Hunger strike") alongside taxi drivers. Due to his mayoral campaign, Mamdani attended two thirds of the 2025 sessions in Albany, with perfect attendance until late April.

## 2025 New York City mayoral election

### 2025 campaign

On October 23, 2024, Mamdani announced his candidacy for [mayor of New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_New_York_City "Mayor of New York City"). His platform included support for free city buses and a rent freeze in rent-stabilized housing. Mamdani also wanted the city government to operate five [grocery stores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grocery_store "Grocery store")—one in each borough—to drive down grocery prices. His platform included support for universal child care and the construction of 200,000 new affordable housing units. He also supported [public safety reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_reform_in_the_United_States "Police reform in the United States") and a $30 [minimum wage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_wage_in_the_United_States "Minimum wage in the United States") by 2030. His platform called for tax increases on corporations and on those earning above $1 million annually.

For most of the primary campaign, Mamdani trailed former New York governor [Andrew Cuomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Cuomo "Andrew Cuomo") in polling. He and Cuomo raised similar amounts of money, but his donor base was considerably larger than Cuomo's. A poll taken shortly before the June 24, 2025, [primary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_election "Primary election") showed that Mamdani had caught up to Cuomo. [First-choice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranked_voting "Ranked voting") results on election night showed Mamdani had a large lead over Cuomo, who conceded the race that evening. His polling margin was increased by [ranked choice voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instant-runoff_voting "Instant-runoff voting"), particularly because he and third-favored candidate [Brad Lander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Lander "Brad Lander") cross-endorsed each other by asking their voters to rank the other candidate second. Mamdani and [Michael Blake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Blake_(politician) "Michael Blake (politician)") also cross-endorsed each other a few days later. On June 16, *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* editorial board advised voters not to rank Mamdani while criticizing Cuomo.

On July 1, after the New York City Board of Elections released its ranked-choice ballot tabulation, the Associated Press announced Mamdani had won the primary. It was considered a major [upset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upset_(competition) "Upset (competition)"). Mamdani's campaign launch drew media attention for its use of [Bollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bollywood "Bollywood") songs and cultural references, which aimed to engage younger and immigrant voters.

During the primary, Mamdani's campaign was supported by the *New Yorkers for Lower Costs* [super PAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_PAC "Super PAC"), which spent approximately $1.3 million supporting him and opposing [Cuomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Cuomo "Andrew Cuomo") before the primary and raised an additional $1 million afterward. The super PAC received $100,000 in contributions from the Unity and Justice Fund in May and June 2025. Cuomo accused Mamdani of accepting "dirty money", saying the Unity and Justice Fund was tied to the [Council on American-Islamic Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_on_American-Islamic_Relations "Council on American-Islamic Relations").

After Mamdani won the primary, criticisms of and attacks against him used racist, [xenophobic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenophobic "Xenophobic"), and [Islamophobic tropes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamophobic_tropes "Islamophobic tropes"), particularly references to the [9/11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9/11_attacks "9/11 attacks") and terrorism. These criticisms and attacks came from across the political spectrum, and sparked concern and debate over [Islamophobia in mainstream American politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamophobia_in_mainstream_American_politics "Islamophobia in mainstream American politics"). The success of Mamdani's campaign was attributed in part to his use of social media to reach potential voters, especially younger voters.

In early November, President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") threatened to withdraw federal funding for New York City should Mamdani be elected mayor. Mamdani was elected mayor on November 4, 2025, becoming the city's youngest mayor since [Hugh J. Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_J._Grant "Hugh J. Grant") in 1892, its first [Asian American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Americans "Asian Americans") and Muslim mayor, and its first foreign-born mayor since [Abraham Beame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Beame "Abraham Beame"), who was born in the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom").

### Mayor-elect and transition

On November 5, 2025, the day after the election, Mamdani announced the first members of his mayoral transition team, with Elana Leopold as executive director, and co-chairs [Maria Torres-Springer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Torres-Springer "Maria Torres-Springer"), the former first deputy mayor; [Lina Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lina_Khan "Lina Khan"), the former chair of the [Federal Trade Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Trade_Commission "Federal Trade Commission"); and nonprofit executives [Melanie Hartzog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanie_Hartzog "Melanie Hartzog") and Grace Bonilla. On November 10, he announced his selection of former first deputy mayor [Dean Fuleihan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Fuleihan "Dean Fuleihan") as his first deputy, and his former Assembly chief of staff and campaign manager [Elle Bisgaard-Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elle_Bisgaard-Church "Elle Bisgaard-Church") as his chief of staff. He faced criticism for selecting Catherine Almonte Da Costa as director of appointments after past antisemitic comments of hers resurfaced; Da Costa resigned one day after her appointment.

Mamdani met with President Trump at the [White House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House "White House") on November 21. A spokesperson said the discussion would focus on public safety, economic security, and affordability. After the meeting, Trump praised Mamdani and said they "agree on a lot more than I would have thought", following months of mutual criticism.

Mamdani's mayoralty was expected to be the city's 111th, but a December 2025 review of city archives established that the enumeration of New York City mayors had omitted [Matthias Nicoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Nicoll "Matthias Nicoll")'s second mayoralty, and that Mamdani would therefore be the city's 112th mayor.

## Mayor of New York City (2026–present)

### Inauguration

Mamdani's mayoralty began on January 1, 2026. New York Attorney General [Letitia James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letitia_James "Letitia James") administered the oath of office in the abandoned [City Hall subway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Hall_station_(IRT_Lexington_Avenue_Line) "City Hall station (IRT Lexington Avenue Line)") just after midnight. Mamdani took the oath on the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran "Quran"), making him the first mayor of the city to do so. He used two copies of the book, his grandfather's and [Arturo Alfonso Schomburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arturo_Alfonso_Schomburg "Arturo Alfonso Schomburg")'s. A public inauguration was held in the afternoon, at which Mamdani was sworn in by [Bernie Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Sanders "Bernie Sanders"). Speakers and performers at the inauguration included Sanders, [Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria_Ocasio-Cortez "Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez"), [Javier Muñoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_Mu%C3%B1oz_(actor) "Javier Muñoz (actor)"), [Lucy Dacus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucy_Dacus "Lucy Dacus"), and [Mandy Patinkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandy_Patinkin "Mandy Patinkin").

Mamdani's first act as mayor, immediately after taking the oath of office, was to appoint transportation consultant, educator, and former [NYC Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Department_of_Transportation "New York City Department of Transportation") director of capital planning and project management [Mike Flynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Flynn_(transportation_official) "Mike Flynn (transportation official)") as his Department of Transportation commissioner. His first [executive order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_order "Executive order") was to revoke all executive orders his predecessor [Eric Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Adams "Eric Adams") made after he was [indicted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigations_into_the_Eric_Adams_administration#Indictment "Investigations into the Eric Adams administration") on bribery charges on September 25, 2024.

### Administration

One week after his inauguration, Mamdani and Governor [Kathy Hochul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathy_Hochul "Kathy Hochul") announced a plan to expand free and low-cost childcare in New York City, with the aim to eventually provide universal free childcare. One of his first major challenges as mayor was managing the effects of a  [January 2026 winter storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_23%E2%80%9327,_2026_North_American_winter_storm "January 23–27, 2026 North American winter storm"), which killed 14 people in New York City.

In February 2026, Mamdani's administration secured a $5.2 million labor rights settlement against [Uber Eats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uber_Eats "Uber Eats"), HungryPanda, and [Fantuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantuan_Delivery "Fantuan Delivery"), paying workers against whom the companies had committed [wage theft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wage_theft "Wage theft"). Uber Eats also agreed to reinstate 10,000 workers who were wrongfully terminated between 2023 and 2024. Later that year, his administration reached a similar $131 million settlement with [DoorDash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DoorDash "DoorDash"), finding that they failed to pay their workers minimum wage. Per the settlement, DoorDash will pay $115 million to 260,000 workers and $16 million in fines.

On April 14, 2026, Mamdani announced [La Marqueta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Marqueta "La Marqueta") marketplace in Manhattan's [East Harlem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Harlem "East Harlem") neighborhood as the first identified site of his planned [city-run grocery stores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_grocery_store "Public grocery store"). He plans to spend roughly $30 million to build the store in the predominantly Latino neighborhood. Five stores are planned, one in each borough. Each will offer groceries at prices 30% below the market rate, as a subsidy from the city budget. The stores are expected to progressively open from 2027 through 2029.

On April 15, 2026, Mamdani and Governor [Kathy Hochul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathy_Hochul "Kathy Hochul") announced a [pied-à-terre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pied-%C3%A0-terre "Pied-à-terre") tax, taxing second homes of owners who live outside New York City for single-family homes valued above $5 million or condominiums above $1 million. The plan passed the [New York State Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Assembly "New York State Assembly") in May 2026.

In May 2026, Mamdani announced his housing plan "Block by Block", to build 200,000 new affordable housing units over the next decade, preserve 200,000 others, and expand the city's affordable housing programs. On June 25, the New York City Rent Guidelines Board approved Mamdani's proposal to freeze the rent of rent-stabilized apartments, which make up 40% of New York City's apartments, for two years. Ahead of the vote, board member Christina Smyth, who represents landlords as an attorney, resigned in protest over the proposal.

In July 2026, Mamdani said he would not accept a salary increase if the New York City Council decided to raise all citywide elected officials' salaries. Later that month, his administration introduced a rule banning [junk fees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junk_fees "Junk fees"), making New York City the first city in the United States to do so.

On September 8, 2026, to commemorate the 25th anniversary of the [September 11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_attacks "September 11 attacks"), Mamdani launched a public portal containing about 170,000 pages of information on the attacks. The documents reveal that New York City leaders at the time knew that the air quality was unsafe in the fallout area, while telling the public otherwise.

On September 25, 2026, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu spoke at the United Nations General Assembly. In his speech, he said: "To all those spreading lies about my country, whether they sit in this hall or in the office of the antisemitic mayor of New York, I say this: shame on you." He also said: "Mr. Mamdani, you tried to stop me from coming here. You tried to silence me. Well, you can't silence me." Netanyahu denied that Israel has committed genocide in Gaza. Mamdani had previously said he would push for Netanyahu's arrest during his visit to New York. Mamdani called Netanyahu's remarks about him "baseless lies" meant to "sanitize his genocide of Palestinians."

Later in September, Mamdani released a report that outlined a strategy to fight antisemitism in New York City, in part by using millions of dollars already pledged to fight hate crimes. The report also promised to support programs celebrating Jewish traditions and expand Holocaust education programs in city schools. Some New York City elected officials, as well as some Jewish organizations, criticized the report for not defining antisemitism or discussing any link between it and [anti-Zionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Zionism "Anti-Zionism"). While praising the strategy to increase security funding and expand some programs, several Jewish organizations, including the UJA-Federation of New York, said in a joint statement that the plan "fails to address a central issue that the Mayor himself has perpetuated: rhetoric that demonizes and delegitimizes Israel inevitably leads to the targeting of Jewish New Yorkers."

### Gracie Mansion bombing attempt

On March 7, 2026, a [homemade explosive device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Improvised_explosive_device "Improvised explosive device") was thrown into the crowd of an anti-Islam protest organized by far-right activist and [January 6 United States Capitol attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_6_United_States_Capitol_attack "January 6 United States Capitol attack") rioter [Jake Lang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Lang "Jake Lang") outside [Gracie Mansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gracie_Mansion "Gracie Mansion"), Mamdani's official residence as [Mayor of New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_New_York_City "Mayor of New York City"). Police investigated the attack as an "act of [ISIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISIS "ISIS")-inspired terrorism". Two men, Emir Balat and Ibrahim Kayumi, were taken into custody, and the [U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Attorney_for_the_Southern_District_of_New_York "U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of New York") brought federal charges against them. Mamdani condemned the protest, calling it "appalling" and a display of anti-Muslim bigotry rooted in racism. He further added that the "right to peaceful protest is sacred" and "does not belong only to those we agree with", calling the bombing attempt "heinous".

## Political positions

Mamdani identifies as a [democratic socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_socialist "Democratic socialist") and is a member of the [Democratic Socialists of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Socialists_of_America "Democratic Socialists of America"). Described as a [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism_in_the_United_States "Progressivism in the United States") and [left-wing populist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-wing_populist "Left-wing populist"), he has advanced policies that have been characterized as [center-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-left_politics "Centre-left politics") and [left-wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-wing "Left-wing"). Mamdani has maintained that the "struggle for [Palestinian liberation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_liberation "Palestinian liberation")" has been at "the core of [his] politics" throughout his political life. His economic platform centers on equity and affordability—supporting debt relief for [taxi medallion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxi_medallion "Taxi medallion") owners, stronger [rent control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rent_control "Rent control"), tenant protections, and a Social Housing Development Agency to build 200,000 affordable units. In [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"), he advocates raising the local [minimum wage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_wage "Minimum wage") to $30 by 2030, implementing higher taxes on corporations and high-income earners to fund free tuition at [CUNY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CUNY "CUNY") and [SUNY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SUNY "SUNY"), universal childcare, [city-owned grocery stores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City-owned_grocery_stores "City-owned grocery stores"), and [free public transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_public_transit "Free public transit"), while cutting taxes for [outer-borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boroughs_of_New_York_City#Background "Boroughs of New York City") homeowners and reforming New York's property tax system. Mamdani has cited [sewer socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewer_socialism "Sewer socialism"), an early-20th-century democratic socialist movement centered in [Milwaukee, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee,_Wisconsin "Milwaukee, Wisconsin"), as an inspiration for improving public infrastructure in New York City.

Mamdani supported [Proposal 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_New_York_Proposal_1 "2024 New York Proposal 1")—a successful 2024 ballot measure that banned discrimination based on ethnicity, gender identity, disability, and reproductive rights—and backs [single-payer healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-payer_healthcare "Single-payer healthcare") through the [New York Health Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Health_Act "New York Health Act"). He argues that "dignified work, economic stability, and well-resourced neighborhoods" prevent harm better than policing, proposing a civilian Department of Community Safety to handle mental health crises and community outreach. He had voiced support for [defunding the NYPD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defunding_the_police "Defunding the police") and accused the department of [racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_racism "Police racism"), stances for which he later apologized. As a mayoral candidate, he instead emphasized building a cooperative relationship with the department on violent crime prevention while reducing the involvement of police in other matters.

Mamdani has condemned dictatorships in [Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuela "Venezuela") and [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba "Cuba") while criticizing US sanctions on those countries. After [the U.S. captured Nicolas Maduro and his wife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_strikes_in_Venezuela "2026 United States strikes in Venezuela"), Mamdani called the action "a violation of federal and international law". In January 2026, Mamdani criticized the [Iranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran") regime's [violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_massacres "2026 Iran massacres") against [anti-government protesters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%932026_Iranian_protests "2025–2026 Iranian protests"). He has denounced Indian prime minister [Narendra Modi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narendra_Modi "Narendra Modi") as a "war criminal" and remains a critic of Israel and the [Gaza genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_genocide "Gaza genocide"), describing its policies as [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_apartheid "Israeli apartheid") and supporting the movement to [boycott, divest from, and sanction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boycott,_Divestment_and_Sanctions "Boycott, Divestment and Sanctions") the country. He co-sponsored the "Not on our dime!" bill to stop New York charities from funding [Israeli settler violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_settler_violence "Israeli settler violence"). After the [October 7 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_7_attacks "October 7 attacks"), Mamdani mourned all victims, condemned [Hamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamas "Hamas")'s attacks as war crimes, and called for both sides to "lay down their arms" while advocating a permanent [ceasefire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calls_for_a_ceasefire_during_the_Gaza_war "Calls for a ceasefire during the Gaza war"), the end of [Israeli occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_occupation "Israeli occupation"), and the end of [ethnocratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnocratic "Ethnocratic") policy in favor of "equal rights for all". He said he would arrest [Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_arrest_warrants_for_Israeli_leaders "International Criminal Court arrest warrants for Israeli leaders") and [Russian president Vladimir Putin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_arrest_warrants_for_Russian_leaders "International Criminal Court arrest warrants for Russian leaders") if they visited New York City to honor the [International Criminal Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court "International Criminal Court")'s arrest warrants. In a July 21 statement, Mamdani said the [municipality of New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_New_York_City "Government of New York City") does not have independent legal authority to execute the ICC's arrest warrant but that the US federal government does, calling on the federal government to execute the arrest.

Mamdani connects environmental justice to social equity. He opposed expansion of the gas-fired [Astoria Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Poletti_Power_Project "Charles Poletti Power Project") power plant, supports the All-Electric Buildings Act and [congestion pricing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congestion_pricing_in_New_York_City "Congestion pricing in New York City"), and proposed a "Green Schools for a Healthier New York City" plan to retrofit schools with [solar panels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_panels "Solar panels"), create green schoolyards, and establish resilience hubs. His platform includes universal [pre-K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-K "Pre-K"), [baby baskets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternity_package "Maternity package") for new families, defending Hasidic [yeshivas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshivas "Yeshivas")' autonomy, expanding sanctuary protections for immigrants, making New York City an [LGBTQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ "LGBTQ")+ sanctuary city with an Office of LGBTQIA+ Affairs, and eliminating bus fares through expanded [MTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Transit_Authority_(New_York) "Metropolitan Transit Authority (New York)") funding and free transit initiatives.

After the January 2026 deaths of [Renée Good](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9e_Good "Renée Good") and [Alex Pretti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Alex_Pretti "Killing of Alex Pretti"), who were both civilians killed by federal border patrol agents during [Operation Metro Surge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Metro_Surge "Operation Metro Surge"), Mamdani took a strong stance against [ICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICE "ICE"). He called to [abolish ICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolish_ICE "Abolish ICE"), described Good's and Pretti's killings as murders, and blamed [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump")'s "year of cruelty" for Good's death. In February 2026, Mamdani signed an executive order requiring ICE agents to get judicial warrants in New York City.

## Personal life

Mamdani is a [dual citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_citizen "Dual citizen") of Uganda and the United States ([naturalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalization#United_States "Naturalization") in 2018). He follows the [Twelver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelver_Shi%27ism "Twelver Shi'ism") branch of [Shia Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Islam "Shia Islam"). In 2021, Mamdani met animator and illustrator [Rama Duwaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rama_Duwaji "Rama Duwaji") through [Hinge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinge_(app) "Hinge (app)"), an online dating application. They became engaged in October 2024, held a private [*nikah* ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage_in_Islam#Nikah_ceremony "Marriage in Islam") two months later in [Dubai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai "Dubai"), and were married in February 2025 in a [civil ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_ceremony "Civil ceremony") at [New York City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Hall "New York City Hall"); they also had a ceremony in Uganda in July 2025. The couple resided in Mamdani's legislative district of [Astoria, Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astoria,_Queens "Astoria, Queens"), near [Steinway Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steinway_Street "Steinway Street"), until they moved into [Gracie Mansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gracie_Mansion "Gracie Mansion").

Besides English, Mamdani can speak at least five other languages with varying degrees of proficiency: [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi "Hindi"), [Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili "Swahili"), [Luganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luganda "Luganda"), Spanish, and Arabic. He is a [fan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_F.C._supporters "Arsenal F.C. supporters") of the [English football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_League "Premier League") team [Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_F.C. "Arsenal F.C."), the [New York Knicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Knicks "New York Knicks") of the [NBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Basketball_Association "National Basketball Association"), whose games he has attended as part of past campaigns, the [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Giants "New York Giants") of the [NFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League "National Football League"), and the [New York Mets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Mets "New York Mets") of [MLB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Baseball "Major League Baseball"). He also follows [cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket "Cricket") and [professional wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_wrestling "Professional wrestling"). He is a shareholder of the Spanish soccer team [Real Oviedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Oviedo "Real Oviedo"), having been one of 20,000 people to purchase shares in the club in 2012 as part of a fundraising drive to stave off bankruptcy. His favorite [anime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anime "Anime") series is *[Digimon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digimon "Digimon")*.
