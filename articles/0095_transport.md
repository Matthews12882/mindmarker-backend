# Transport

**Transport** (in [British English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English "British English")) or **transportation** (in [American English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English "American English")) is the intentional [movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion "Motion") of humans, animals, and [goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo "Cargo") from one location to another. [Modes of transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mode_of_transport "Mode of transport") include [air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation "Aviation"), [land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_transport "Land transport") ([rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport "Rail transport") and [road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_transport "Road transport")), [water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_transport "Ship transport"), [cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_transport "Cable transport"), [pipelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipeline_transport "Pipeline transport"), and [space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_transport "Space transport"). The field can be divided into [infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrastructure "Infrastructure"), [vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle "Vehicle"), and operations. Transport enables human [trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade "Trade"), which is essential for the development of [civilizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization "Civilization").

Transport infrastructure consists of fixed installations, including [roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road "Road"), railways, [airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airway_(aviation) "Airway (aviation)"), [waterways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterway "Waterway"), [canals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal "Canal"), and pipelines, as well as terminals such as [airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport "Airport"), [railway stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train_station "Train station"), [bus stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_station "Bus station"), [warehouses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warehouse "Warehouse"), trucking terminals, refueling depots (including fuel docks and [fuel stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_station "Fuel station")), and [seaports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaport "Seaport"). Terminals may be used both for the interchange of passengers and cargo and for maintenance.

[Means of transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Means_of_transport "Means of transport") are any of the different kinds of transport facilities used to carry people or cargo. They may include vehicles, [riding animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riding_animal "Riding animal"), and [pack animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pack_animals "Pack animals"). Vehicles may include [wagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagons "Wagons"), [automobiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobiles "Automobiles"), [bicycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycles "Bicycles"), [buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buses "Buses"), [trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train "Train"), [trucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truck "Truck"), [helicopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopter "Helicopter"), [watercraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watercraft "Watercraft"), [spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacecraft "Spacecraft"), and [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft "Aircraft").

**Mobility** is concerned with the experience around transportation services, including aspects such as availability, information, usability, accessibility, and affordability (as in [right to mobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_mobility "Right to mobility")).

## Modes

A **mode of transport** is a method or way of travelling, or of transporting people or cargo. The transport of a person or of cargo may involve one mode or several of the modes, with the latter case being called inter-modal or multi-modal transport. Each mode has its own advantages and disadvantages, and will be chosen on the basis of cost, capability, and route. The different modes of transport include [air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation "Aviation"), [water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_transport "Ship transport"), and [land transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_transport "Land transport"), which includes [rails or railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport "Rail transport"), [road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_transport "Road transport") and [off-road transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-road_transport "Off-road transport"). Other modes of transport also exist, including [pipelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipeline_transport "Pipeline transport"), [cable transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_transport "Cable transport"), and [space transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_transport "Space transport"). [Human-powered transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human-powered_transport "Human-powered transport") and [animal-powered transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal-powered_transport "Animal-powered transport") are sometimes regarded as distinct modes, but they may lie in other categories such as land or water transport. In general, *[transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation "Transportation")* refers to the moving of people, animals, and other goods from one place to another, and *[means of transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Means_of_transport "Means of transport")* refers to the transport facilities used to carry people or cargo according to the chosen mode. Examples of the means of transport include automobile, airplane, ship, truck, and train. Each mode of transport has a fundamentally different set of technological solutions. Each mode has its own [infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrastructure "Infrastructure"), [vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle "Vehicle"), transport operators and operations.

Governments regulate the way the vehicles are operated, and the procedures set for this purpose, including financing, legalities, and policies. In the transport industry, operations and ownership of infrastructure can be [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_ownership "State ownership"), [private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_property "Private property"), or a [partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public%E2%80%93private_partnership "Public–private partnership") between the two, depending on the country and mode. Transport modes can be a mix of the two ownership systems, such as privately owned cars and government-owned [urban transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_transport "Urban transport") in cities. Many international [airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airlines "Airlines") have a mixed public-private ownership.

Passenger transport may be [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport "Public transport"), where operators provide scheduled services, or [private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_transport "Private transport"). Freight transport has become focused on [containerization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Containerization "Containerization"), although [bulk transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulk_material_handling "Bulk material handling") is used for large volumes of durable items. Transport plays an important part in economic growth and [globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization "Globalization"), but machine-propelled forms cause [air pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_pollution "Air pollution") and [use large amounts of land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_use "Land use"). While it[*[vague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Vagueness "Wikipedia:Vagueness")*] is heavily subsidized by governments, good planning of transport is essential to make [traffic flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_flow "Traffic flow") and restrain [urban sprawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_sprawl "Urban sprawl").

### Human-powered

Human-powered transport, a form of [sustainable transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_transport "Sustainable transport"), is the transport of people or goods using [human](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human "Human") muscle-power, in the form of [walking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walking "Walking"), [running](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running "Running"), and [swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_swimming "Human swimming"). [Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology "Technology") has allowed [machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine "Machine") to improve the [energy efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_efficiency_in_transport "Energy efficiency in transport") of human mobility on relatively smooth terrain. Human-powered transport remains popular for reasons of cost-saving, [leisure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leisure "Leisure"), [physical exercise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_exercise "Physical exercise"), and [environmentalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentalism "Environmentalism"); it is sometimes the only type available, especially in underdeveloped or inaccessible regions.

Although humans are able to walk without infrastructure, the accessibility can be enhanced through the use of roads, [sidewalks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidewalks "Sidewalks"), and [shared-use paths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shared-use_path "Shared-use path"), especially when using the human power with vehicles, such as [bicycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle "Bicycle"), [inline skates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inline_skates "Inline skates"), and [wheelchairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheelchair "Wheelchair"). Human-powered vehicles have been developed for difficult environments, such as snow and water, by [watercraft rowing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watercraft_rowing "Watercraft rowing") and [skiing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skiing "Skiing"); even the air can be flown through with [human-powered aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human-powered_aircraft "Human-powered aircraft"). [Personal transporters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_transporter "Personal transporter"), a form of hybrid human-electric powered vehicle, have emerged in the 21st century as a form of multi-model urban transport.

### Animal-powered

Animal-powered transport is the use of [working animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_animal "Working animal") for the movement of people and commodities. Humans may ride some of the animals directly, use them as [pack animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pack_animal "Pack animal") for carrying goods, or harness them, alone or in [teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team "Team"), to pull [sleds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sled "Sled") or wheeled [vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle "Vehicle"). They remain useful in rough terrain that is not readily accessible by automotive-based transportation.

### Air

A [fixed-wing aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed-wing_aircraft "Fixed-wing aircraft"), commonly called an [airplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airplane "Airplane"), is a heavier-than-air craft where movement of the air in relation to the wings is used to generate lift. The term is used to distinguish this from [rotary-wing aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotorcraft "Rotorcraft"), where the movement of the lift surfaces relative to the air generates lift. For short distances or in places without runways, [helicopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopters "Helicopters") can be operable. A [gyroplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyroplane "Gyroplane") is both fixed-wing and rotary wing.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] Fixed-wing aircraft range from small trainers and recreational aircraft to large [airliners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airliner "Airliner") and military cargo aircraft.

Two things necessary for [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft "Aircraft") are air flow over the wings for [lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lift_(soaring) "Lift (soaring)") and an apparatus for [landing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing "Landing"). The majority of aircraft require an [airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport "Airport") with the infrastructure for maintenance, restocking, and refueling and for the loading and unloading of crew, cargo, and passengers. Many [aerodromes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerodrome "Aerodrome") have takeoff and landing restrictions on weight and runway length, and so are not able to handle all types of aircraft. While the vast majority of fixed-wing aircraft land and take off on land, some are capable of take-off and landing on ice, snow, and calm water.

Autonomous or remotely-piloted airplanes are known as [unmanned aerial vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmanned_aerial_vehicle "Unmanned aerial vehicle"), or UAV. These drones can range in size from less than a metre across to a full-sized airplane. They are capable of carrying a payload, and are being used for package delivery.

The aircraft is the second fastest method of transport, after the [rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket "Rocket"). Commercial jets reach speeds of up to 955 kilometres per hour (593 mph) and a considerably higher ground speed if there is a [jet stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_stream "Jet stream") tailwind, while piston-powered general aviation aircraft may reach up to 555 kilometres per hour (345 mph) or more. This celerity comes with higher cost and energy use, and [aviation's impacts to the environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_aviation "Environmental impact of aviation") and particularly the global climate require consideration when comparing modes of transportation. The [Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intergovernmental_Panel_on_Climate_Change "Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change") (IPCC) estimates a commercial jet's flight to have some 2-4 times the effect on the climate than if the same CO2 emissions were made at ground level, because of different atmospheric chemistry and [radiative forcing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiative_forcing "Radiative forcing") effects at the higher altitude. U.S. airlines alone burned about 16.2 billion gallons of fuel during the twelve months between October 2013 and September 2014. WHO estimates that globally as many as 500,000 people at a time are on planes. The global trend has been for increasing numbers of people to travel by air, and individually to do so with increasing frequency and over longer distances, a dilemma that has the attention of climate scientists and other researchers, along with the press. The issue of impacts from frequent travel, particularly by air because of the long distances that are easily covered in one or a few days, is called [hypermobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypermobility_(travel) "Hypermobility (travel)") and has been a topic of research and governmental concern for many years.

An [aerostat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerostat "Aerostat") is a class of lighter-than-air aircraft that gains its lift by containing a volume of gas that has a lower [density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density_of_air "Density of air") than the surrounding atmosphere. These include [balloons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balloon_(aeronautics) "Balloon (aeronautics)") and [rigid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigid_airship "Rigid airship"), [semi-rigid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-rigid_airship "Semi-rigid airship"), or non-rigid [airships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airship "Airship"); the last is called a [blimp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blimp "Blimp"). The [lifting gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifting_gas "Lifting gas") is typically helium, as hydrogen is highly flammable. Alternatively, heated air is used in [hot air balloons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_air_balloon "Hot air balloon") and [thermal airships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_airship "Thermal airship"). Aerostats can transport passengers and a payload over long distances. For example, [zeppelins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeppelin "Zeppelin") were used on long-ranged bombing raids during [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I").

### Land

Land transport covers all land-based transport systems that provide for the movement of people, goods, and services. Land transport plays a vital role in linking communities to each other. Land transport is a key factor in [urban planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning "Urban planning"). It consists of two kinds, rail and road.

#### Rail

[Rail transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport "Rail transport") consists of wheeled vehicles running on tracks, which usually consist of two parallel [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel "Steel") rails, known as a [railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_track "Railway track") or railroad. The rails are anchored [perpendicular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpendicular "Perpendicular") to [ties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railroad_tie "Railroad tie") (or sleepers) of timber, concrete, or steel, to maintain a consistent distance apart, or [gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_gauge "Rail gauge"). The rails and perpendicular beams are placed on a foundation made of concrete or compressed [earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil "Soil") and [gravel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravel "Gravel") in a [bed of ballast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Track_ballast "Track ballast"). Alternative methods include [monorail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monorail "Monorail"), [maglev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maglev_(transport) "Maglev (transport)"), and [hyperloop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperloop "Hyperloop"). [Dual gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_gauge "Dual gauge") railways have three or four rails, allowing use by trains with two or three [track gauges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Track_gauge "Track gauge"). For [steep grades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steep_grade_railway "Steep grade railway"), a railway can use an additional toothed [rack rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rack_railway "Rack railway") for traction.

A train consists of one or more connected vehicles that operate on the rails, known as [rolling stock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_stock "Rolling stock"). Propulsion is commonly provided by a [locomotive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locomotive "Locomotive"), which hauls a series of unpowered cars that carry passengers or freight. The locomotive can be powered by [steam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_locomotive "Steam locomotive"), [diesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_locomotive "Diesel locomotive"), [gas turbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_turbine "Gas turbine"), or else [electricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_locomotive "Electric locomotive") supplied by [trackside systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_electrification_system "Railway electrification system"). Some or all the cars can be powered, known as a [multiple unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_unit "Multiple unit"). A [tram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tram "Tram") is similar to a train, but is generally smaller, travels shorter distances, and runs on rails that are integrated into the streets. Typically a tram is electric-powered, but they have also been propelled by [horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horsecar "Horsecar"), [cables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funicular "Funicular"), [gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity "Gravity"),[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] or [pneumatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumatics "Pneumatics"). Railed vehicles move with much less friction than rubber tires on paved roads, making trains more [energy efficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_conversion_efficiency "Energy conversion efficiency"), though not as efficient as ships.

[Intercity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-city_rail "Inter-city rail") trains are long-haul services connecting cities; modern [high-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail "High-speed rail") is capable of speeds up to 350 km/h (220 mph), but this requires specially built track. [Commercial maglev transport in Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_maglev_train "Shanghai maglev train") runs at 460 km/h (290 mph). [Regional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_rail "Regional rail") and [commuter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commuter_rail "Commuter rail") trains feed cities from suburbs and surrounding areas, while intra-urban transport is performed by high-capacity [tramways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tram "Tram") and [rapid transits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit "Rapid transit"), in many cases making up the backbone of a city's public transport.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] [Freight trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freight_trains "Freight trains") traditionally used [box cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box_car "Box car"), requiring manual loading and unloading of the [cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo "Cargo"). Since the 1980s, [container trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Container_train "Container train") have become the dominant solution for general freight, while large quantities of bulk are transported by dedicated rolling stock. An example of the latter are specially designed [tank cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank_car "Tank car") for the transport of [hazardous materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazardous_material "Hazardous material").

#### Road

A road is an identifiable [route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Route_number "Route number"), way, or [path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trail "Trail") between two or more [places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Location_(geography) "Location (geography)"). Roads are typically smoothed, [paved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavement_(material) "Pavement (material)"), or otherwise prepared to allow easy travel; though they need not be, and historically many roads were simply recognizable routes without any formal [construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction "Construction") or [maintenance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maintenance,_repair_and_operations "Maintenance, repair and operations"). In [urban areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area "Urban area"), roads may pass through a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City "City") or [village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village "Village") and be named as [streets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street "Street"), serving a dual function as urban space [easement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easement "Easement") and route.

The most common road vehicle is the automobile, a light duty [wheeled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel "Wheel") passenger vehicle that carries its own [motor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car_engine "Car engine"). Other users of roads include [buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus "Bus"), [trucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truck "Truck"), [motorcycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorcycles "Motorcycles"), [bicycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycles "Bicycles"), and [pedestrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedestrians "Pedestrians"). As of 2015, there were 950 million passenger cars worldwide, with a projected total of 2.5 billion in 2050. Road transport offers road users the flexibility to transfer the vehicle from one lane to the other and from one road to another according to the need and convenience. This combination of changes in location, direction, speed, and timings of travel is not available to other motorized modes of transport. It is possible to provide efficient intracity door-to-door service only by road transport.

Some drawbacks are that road systems consume large amounts of space, are costly to build and maintain (including vehicles), lead to urban congestion, and have only limited ability to achieve [economies of scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economies_of_scale "Economies of scale"). Automobiles provide high flexibility with low capacity, but require high energy and area use, and are the main source of harmful [noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_pollution "Noise pollution") and [air pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_pollution "Air pollution") in cities; buses allow for more efficient travel at the cost of reduced flexibility. Road transport by truck is often the initial and [final stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_mile_(transportation) "Last mile (transportation)") of freight transport.

### Water

Water transport is movement by means of a [watercraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watercraft "Watercraft")—such as a [barge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barge "Barge"), [boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boat "Boat"), [ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship "Ship"), or [sailboat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailboat "Sailboat")—over a body of water, such as a [sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea "Sea"), [ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean "Ocean"), [lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake "Lake"), [canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal "Canal"), or [river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River "River"). The need for buoyancy is common to watercraft, making the [hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hull_(watercraft) "Hull (watercraft)") a critical aspect of its construction, maintenance, and appearance.

In the 19th century, the first [steam ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_ships "Steam ships") were developed, using a [steam engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_engine "Steam engine") to drive a [paddle wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddle_wheel "Paddle wheel") or [propeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propeller "Propeller") to move the ship. The [steam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam "Steam") was produced in a [boiler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boiler "Boiler") using wood or [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal "Coal") and fed through a steam [external combustion engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/External_combustion_engine "External combustion engine"). Now most commercial ships have an [internal combustion engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_combustion_engine "Internal combustion engine") using a slightly refined type of [petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum "Petroleum") called [bunker fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunker_fuel "Bunker fuel"). Some ships, such as [submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine "Submarine"), use [nuclear marine propulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_marine_propulsion "Nuclear marine propulsion") with heat from a [nuclear reactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_reactor "Nuclear reactor") generating the steam. [Recreational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreation "Recreation") or [educational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education "Education") craft still use wind power or oars, while some smaller craft use [internal combustion engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_combustion_engines "Internal combustion engines") to drive one or more [propellers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propeller "Propeller") or, in the case of jet boats, an inboard water jet. In shallow draft areas, [hovercraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hovercraft "Hovercraft") are propelled by large pusher-prop fans. (See [Marine propulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_propulsion "Marine propulsion").)

Although it is slow compared to other transport, modern [sea transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_transport "Sea transport") is a highly efficient method of transporting large quantities of goods. [Commercial vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_vessel "Commercial vessel"), nearly 35,000 in number, carried 7.4 billion tons of cargo in 2007. Transport by water is significantly less costly than air transport for transcontinental [shipping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipping "Shipping"); [short sea shipping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_sea_shipping "Short sea shipping") and [ferries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferry "Ferry") remain viable in coastal areas.

### Other modes

[Micromobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micromobility "Micromobility") is the collective name for small electric powered vehicles.

[Pipeline transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipeline_transport "Pipeline transport") sends goods through a [pipe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipe_(material) "Pipe (material)"); most commonly, chemically stable liquids, vapors, and gases can be sent, while a [slurry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slurry "Slurry") can be used to transport solids. [Pneumatic tubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumatic_tube "Pneumatic tube") can send solid capsules using compressed air. Short-distance systems exist for [sewage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewage "Sewage"), [slurry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slurry "Slurry"), [water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water "Water"), and [beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer "Beer"), while long-distance networks are used for [freshwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater "Freshwater"), [petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum "Petroleum"), and [natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas "Natural gas").

[Cable transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_transport "Cable transport") is a broad mode where vehicles are pulled by [cables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wire_rope "Wire rope") instead of an internal power source. It is most commonly used at steep [gradient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gradient "Gradient"). Typical solutions include [aerial tramways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_tramway "Aerial tramway"), [funiculars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funicular "Funicular"), [elevators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elevator "Elevator"), [material ropeways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Material_ropeway "Material ropeway"), and [ski lifts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ski_lift "Ski lift"); some of these are also categorized as [conveyor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conveyor_belt "Conveyor belt") transport.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] A variant is the [zip line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zip_line "Zip line"), which uses gravity for propulsion.

[Unmanned aerial vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmanned_aerial_vehicle "Unmanned aerial vehicle") transport (drone transport) is being used for medicine transportation in least developed countries with inadequate infrastructure. [Amazon.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon.com "Amazon.com") and other transportation companies are currently testing the use of unmanned aerial vehicles in parcel delivery. This method will allow short-range small-parcel delivery in a short time frame.

[Spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_transport "Space transport") is transport outside Earth's atmosphere by means of a [spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacecraft "Spacecraft"). It is most frequently used for [satellites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite "Satellite") placed in Earth orbit. However, [human spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_spaceflight "Human spaceflight") mission have landed on the Moon and are occasionally used to rotate crew-members to [space stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_station "Space station"). [Uncrewed spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncrewed_spacecraft "Uncrewed spacecraft") have been sent to all the planets of the Solar System.

[Suborbital spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suborbital_spaceflight "Suborbital spaceflight") is the fastest of the existing and planned transport systems from a place on Earth to a distant "other place" on Earth. These rocket-propelled systems could potentially do global point-to-point transport delivery of passengers or cargo in less than 90 minutes.

Vertical transport devices perform displacement in a [vertical direction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_direction "Vertical direction"), although as a side effect they may also transport in a horizontal direction. These include:

* [Cable cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_car_(railway) "Cable car (railway)"), [inlined planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_railway "Cable railway"), [funiculars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funicular_railway "Funicular railway") and [rack railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rack_railway "Rack railway")
* [Chair lifts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chair_lift "Chair lift"), [detachable chairlifts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detachable_chairlift "Detachable chairlift"), [funitels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funitel "Funitel"), [gondola lifts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondola_lift "Gondola lift") and [ski tows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ski_tow "Ski tow")
* [Elevators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elevator "Elevator") (classical lifts)
* [Hot Air Balloons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Air_Balloon "Hot Air Balloon")
* [Escalators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escalator "Escalator") and [inclined travelators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moving_sidewalk "Moving sidewalk")
* [Locks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lock_(water_transport) "Lock (water transport)"), [boat lifts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boat_lift "Boat lift") and [inclined planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal_inclined_plane "Canal inclined plane") (on canals and rivers)
* [Skyhooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyhook_(structure) "Skyhook (structure)") and [space elevators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_elevator "Space elevator") (hypothetical)

## Elements

### Infrastructure

Infrastructure is the fixed installations that allow a vehicle to operate. It consists of a transport network, a terminal, and facilities for parking and maintenance. For rail, pipeline, road, and cable transport, the entire way the vehicle travels must be constructed. Air and watercraft are able to avoid this, since the [airway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airway_(aviation) "Airway (aviation)") and [seaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_lane "Sea lane") do not need to be constructed. However, they require fixed infrastructure at terminals.

Terminals such as airports, ports, and stations, are locations where passengers and freight can be transferred from one vehicle or mode to another. For passenger transport, terminals are integrating different modes to allow riders, who are interchanging between modes, to take advantage of each mode's benefits. For instance, [airport rail links](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport_rail_link "Airport rail link") connect airports to the city centres and suburbs. The terminals for automobiles are [parking lots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parking_lot "Parking lot"), while buses and coaches can operate from simple stops. For freight, terminals act as [transshipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transshipment "Transshipment") points, though some cargo is transported directly from the point of production to the point of use.

The [financing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_finance "Transport finance") of infrastructure can either be [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government "Government") or [private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_enterprise "Private enterprise"). Transport is often a [natural monopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_monopoly "Natural monopoly") and a necessity for the public; roads, and in some countries railways and airports, are funded through [taxation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation "Taxation"). New infrastructure projects can have high costs and are often financed through [debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debt "Debt"). Many infrastructure owners, therefore, impose usage fees,[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] such as [landing fees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_fees "Landing fees") at airports or [toll plazas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toll_plaza "Toll plaza") on roads. Independent of this, authorities may impose [taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax "Tax") on the purchase or use of vehicles. Because of poor forecasting and overestimation of passenger numbers by planners, there is frequently a benefits shortfall for transport infrastructure projects.

### Means of transport

#### Animals

Animals used in transportation include [pack animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pack_animals "Pack animals") and [riding animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riding_animal "Riding animal"). These include various [bovids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bovids "Bovids"), [equids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equids "Equids"), and [camelids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camelids "Camelids"); animal families noted for their muscular strength. Other species employed for various forms of transport include the dog, elephant, ostrich, sheep, and even the [dolphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_marine_mammal "Military marine mammal").[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

#### Vehicles

A vehicle is a non-living device that is used to move people and goods. Unlike the infrastructure, the vehicle moves along with the cargo and riders. Unless being pulled/pushed by a cable or muscle-power, the vehicle must provide its own propulsion; this is most commonly done through a [steam engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_engine "Steam engine"), [combustion engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_combustion_engine "Internal combustion engine"), [electric motor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_motor "Electric motor"), [jet engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_engine "Jet engine"), or [rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket "Rocket"), though other means of propulsion also exist such as sail power or [compressed air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compressed-air_car "Compressed-air car"). Vehicles also need a system of converting the energy into movement; this is most commonly done through [wheels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel "Wheel"), [propellers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propeller "Propeller"), and [air pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_pressure "Air pressure").

Vehicles are commonly staffed by a [driver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driving "Driving"). However, some systems, such as [people movers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_mover "People mover") and some rapid transits, are fully [automated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automation "Automation"). For [passenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passenger "Passenger") transport, the vehicle must have a compartment, seat, or platform for the passengers. Simple vehicles, such as automobiles, bicycles, or simple aircraft, may have one of the passengers as a driver. Since 2016, progress related to the [Fourth Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Industrial_Revolution "Fourth Industrial Revolution") has brought a lot of new emerging technologies for transportation and automotive fields such as [connected vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_car "Connected car") and [autonomous vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicular_automation "Vehicular automation"). These innovations are said to form future mobility, but concerns remain on safety and cybersecurity, particularly concerning connected and autonomous mobility.

### Operation

Private transport is only subject to the owner of the vehicle, who operates the vehicle themselves. For public transport and freight transport, operations are done through [private enterprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_enterprise "Private enterprise"), [governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government "Government"), or a partnership between the two. The infrastructure and vehicles may be owned and operated by the same company, or they may be operated by different entities. Traditionally, many countries have had a [national airline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_carrier "Flag carrier") and [national railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_company "Railway company"). Since the 1980s, many of these have been [privatized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatization "Privatization"). International shipping remains a highly competitive industry with little regulation, but ports can be public-owned.

### Policy

As the [population of the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_population "World population") increases, [cities grow in size](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization "Urbanization") and population—according to the United Nations, 55% of the world's population live in cities, and by 2050 this number is expected to rise to 68%. [Public transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport "Public transport") policy must evolve to meet the changing priorities of the urban world. The institution of policy enforces a degree of order in transport, which is by nature chaotic as people attempt to travel from one place to another as rapidly as possible. This policy helps to reduce accidents and save lives.

## Functions

Relocation of travelers and cargo are the most common uses of transport. However, other uses exist, such as the transfer of mobile construction and emergency equipment, or the strategic and tactical [relocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_logistics "Military logistics") of [armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_force "Armed force") during [warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warfare "Warfare").

### Passenger

Passenger transport, or travel, is divided into [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport "Public transport") and [private transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_transport "Private transport"). Public transport is scheduled services on fixed routes, while private service can be scheduled (e.g. commercial airlines) or chartered (e.g. [shipping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chartering_(shipping) "Chartering (shipping)")) or can provide ad hoc services at the riders desire (e.g. taxi). The latter offers better flexibility, but has lower capacity and a higher environmental impact. Travel may be as part of daily [commuting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commuting "Commuting") or for [business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_travel "Business travel"), leisure, or [migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_migration "Human migration").

Short-haul transport is dominated by the automobile and mass transit. The latter consists of [buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus "Bus") in rural and small cities, supplemented with commuter rail, trams, and [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit "Rapid transit") in larger cities. Long-haul transport involves the use of the automobile, trains, ships, [coaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coach_(vehicle) "Coach (vehicle)"), and aircraft, the last of which have become predominantly used for the longest, including intercontinental, travel. [Intermodal passenger transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermodal_passenger_transport "Intermodal passenger transport") is where a journey is performed through the use of several modes of transport; since all human transport normally starts and ends with walking, all passenger transport can be considered intermodal. Public transport may also involve the intermediate change of vehicle, within or across modes, at a [transport hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_hub "Transport hub"), such as a [bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_station "Bus station") or [railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_station "Railway station").

Taxis and buses can be found on both ends of the public transport spectrum. Buses are the cheapest mode of transport but are not necessarily flexible, and taxis are very flexible but more expensive. In the middle is [demand-responsive transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demand-responsive_transport "Demand-responsive transport"), offering flexibility whilst remaining affordable.

[International travel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_travel "International travel") may be restricted for some individuals due to legislation and [visa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_(document) "Visa (document)") requirements.

### Medical

An ambulance is a vehicle used to transport people from or between places of treatment, and in some instances will also provide out-of-hospital medical care to the patient. The word is often associated with road-going "emergency ambulances", which form part of [emergency medical services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_medical_service "Emergency medical service"), administering [emergency care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_medicine "Emergency medicine") to those with acute medical problems.

*Air medical services* is a comprehensive term covering the use of air transport to move patients to and from healthcare facilities and accident scenes. Personnel provide comprehensive prehospital and emergency and critical care to all types of patients during aeromedical evacuation or rescue operations, aboard helicopters, propeller aircraft, or jet aircraft.

### Freight

Freight transport, or shipping, is a key in the [value chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_chain "Value chain") in manufacturing. With increased specialization and [globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization "Globalization"), production is being located further away from consumption, rapidly increasing the demand for transport. Transport creates place utility by moving the goods from the place of production to the place of consumption. While all modes of transport are used for cargo transport, there is high differentiation between the nature of the cargo transport, in which mode is chosen. [Logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics "Logistics") refers to the entire process of transferring products from producer to consumer, including storage, transport, transshipment, warehousing, material-handling, and packaging, with associated exchange of information. [Incoterm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incoterm "Incoterm") deals with the handling of payment and responsibility of [risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk "Risk") during transport.

[Containerization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Containerization "Containerization"), with the standardization of [ISO containers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_container "ISO container") on all vehicles and at all ports, has revolutionized [international](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_trade "International trade") and [domestic trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_trade "Domestic trade"), offering a huge reduction in [transshipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transshipment "Transshipment") costs. Traditionally, all cargo had to be manually loaded and unloaded into the haul of any ship or car; containerization allows for automated handling and transfer between modes, and the standardized sizes allow for gains in [economy of scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_scale "Economy of scale") in vehicle operation. This has been one of the key driving factors in international trade and globalization since the 1950s.

[Bulk transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulk_material_handling "Bulk material handling") is common with cargo that can be handled roughly without significant deterioration; typical examples are [ore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ore "Ore"), coal, [cereals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cereals "Cereals"), and [petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum "Petroleum"). Because of the uniformity of the product, mechanical handling can allow enormous quantities to be handled quickly and efficiently. The low value of the cargo combined with high volume also means that [economies of scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economies_of_scale "Economies of scale") become essential in transport, and gigantic ships and whole trains are commonly used to transport bulk. Liquid products with sufficient volume may also be transported by pipeline.

[Air freight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo_airline "Cargo airline") has become more common for products of high value; while less than one percent of world transport by volume is by airline, it amounts to forty percent of the value. Time has become especially important in regards to principles such as [postponement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postponement "Postponement") and [just-in-time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_in_time_(business) "Just in time (business)") within the value chain, resulting in a high willingness to pay for quick delivery of key components or items of high value-to-weight ratio. In addition to mail, common items sent by air include [electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics "Electronics") and [fashion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion "Fashion") clothing.

## Industry

The [transport/transportation and logistics industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_industry "Transport industry") is a category of companies that provide services to transport people or goods. The [Global Industry Classification Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Industry_Classification_Standard "Global Industry Classification Standard") (GICS) lists transport below the [industrials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrials "Industrials") sector. The sector consists of several industries including [logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics "Logistics") and [air freight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_freight "Air freight") or airlines, marine, road and rail, and their respective [infrastructures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_infrastructure "Transportation infrastructure"). Entire stock market indexes focus on the sector, like the [Dow Jones Transportation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dow_Jones_Transportation_Index "Dow Jones Transportation Index") (DJTA).

In the EU, the transport industry directly employs around 10 million people and accounts for about 5% of the [gross domestic product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_domestic_product "Gross domestic product") (GDP). Logistics account for 10–15% of the cost of a finished product for European companies. On average 13.2% of every household's budget is spent on transport, which still depends heavily on [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuels "Fossil fuels") and represents an important source of [CO2 emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions "Greenhouse gas emissions"). Emissions from road freight transport have risen by more than 20% since 1995, counterweighting the increased [energy efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_efficiency_in_transport "Energy efficiency in transport") of vehicles.

Logistics and transport as the basis of [global trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_trade "Global trade") is worth over 5.7 trillion Euros.

Digitalisation and the expansion of e-commerce are gradually changing the transport and logistics industry. Modern logistics networks are now characterised by [real-time tracking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTTVP "RTTVP"), data-oriented route optimisation and cross-border fulfilment to complement traditional freight and passenger services .

## Impact

In the [three-sector model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-sector_model "Three-sector model") of [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics "Economics"), transportation is a component of the [tertiary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector "Tertiary sector") that provides services for a functioning economy. Thus, the inefficiency and malfunctioning of transport creates an economic impact. Even when functioning effectively, the operation of a transportation network can have an adverse effect on the environment and human safety. For example, road traffic accidents are one of the leading causes of death world-wide, killing or injuring nearly 1.35 million people every year. The planning, design, maintenance, and operation of facilities for different transport modes is performed through transportation engineering. Their goal is to provide for the safe, efficient, rapid, comfortable, convenient, economical, and environmentally compatible movement of people and goods transport.

### Economic

Transport is a key necessity for [specialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departmentalization "Departmentalization")—allowing production and consumption of products to occur at different locations. Throughout history, transport has been a spur to expansion; better transport allows more [trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade "Trade") and a greater spread of people. [Economic growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_growth "Economic growth") has always been dependent on increasing the capacity and rationality of transport. But the infrastructure and operation of transport have a great impact on the land, and transport is the largest drainer of energy, making [transport sustainability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_sustainability "Transport sustainability") a major issue.

Due to the way modern cities and communities are planned and operated, a physical distinction between home and work is usually created, forcing people to transport themselves to places of work, study, or leisure, as well as to temporarily relocate for other daily activities. Passenger transport is the essence of [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism "Tourism"), a major part of [recreational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreation "Recreation") transport. Commerce requires the transport of people to conduct business, either to allow face-to-face communication for important decisions or to move specialists from their regular place of work to sites where they are needed.

In [lean thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lean_thinking "Lean thinking"), transporting materials or [work in process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Work_in_process "Work in process") from one location to another is seen as one of the seven wastes (Japanese term: *[muda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muda_(Japanese_term) "Muda (Japanese term)")*) which do not add value to a product.

### Planning

Transport planning allows for high use and less impact regarding new infrastructure. Using models of [transport forecasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_forecasting "Transport forecasting"), planners are able to predict future transport patterns. On the operative level, logistics allows owners of cargo to plan transport as part of the [supply chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_chain "Supply chain"). Transport as a field is studied through [transport economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_economics "Transport economics"), a component for the creation of regulation policy by authorities.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] [Transport engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_engineering "Transport engineering"), a sub-discipline of [civil engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_engineering "Civil engineering"), must take into account [trip generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trip_generation "Trip generation"), [trip distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trip_distribution "Trip distribution"), [mode choice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mode_choice "Mode choice"), and [route assignment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Route_assignment "Route assignment"), while the operative level is handled through [traffic engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_engineering_(transportation) "Traffic engineering (transportation)").

Because of the negative impacts incurred, transport often becomes the subject of controversy related to choice of mode, as well as increased capacity. Automotive transport can be seen as a [tragedy of the commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tragedy_of_the_commons "Tragedy of the commons"), where the flexibility and comfort for the individual deteriorate the natural and urban environment for all. [Density of development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_use "Land use") depends on mode of transport, with public transport allowing for better spatial use. Good land use keeps common activities close to people's homes and places higher-density development closer to transport lines and hubs, to minimize the need for transport. There are [economies of agglomeration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economies_of_agglomeration "Economies of agglomeration"). Beyond transport, some land uses are more efficient when clustered. Transport facilities consume land, and in cities pavement (devoted to streets and parking) can easily exceed 20 percent of the total land use. An efficient transport system can reduce land waste.

Too much infrastructure and too much smoothing for maximum vehicle throughput mean that in many cities there is too much traffic and many—if not all—of the negative impacts that come with it.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] It is only in recent years[*[when?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")*] that traditional practices have started to be questioned in many places; as a result of new types of analysis which bring in a much broader range of skills than those traditionally relied on—spanning such areas as environmental impact analysis, public health, sociology, and economics—the viability of the old mobility solutions is increasingly being questioned.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Safety

The energy levels involved in a transport accident can pose a significant risk for crew and passengers, making safety an issue of importance to governments. Significant accidents involve a review by law enforcement and independent investigators from a safety board, such as the [NTSB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transportation_Safety_Board "National Transportation Safety Board") in the U.S. Measures and methods have been implemented to improve the safety of [roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_safety "Road safety"), [automobiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_safety "Automotive safety"), [motorcycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorcycle_safety "Motorcycle safety"), [bicycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_safety "Bicycle safety"), railways, ships, and [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_safety "Aviation safety"). There are [emergency medical services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_medical_services "Emergency medical services") and [sea rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-sea_rescue "Air-sea rescue") measures for rapid response to transport emergencies. Statistics are gathered from accidents, then analyzed and used to determine safety measures to lower the casualty rate.

### Environment

Transport is a major use of [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_(society) "Energy (society)") and burns most of the world's [petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum "Petroleum"). This creates air pollution, including [nitrous oxides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrous_oxide "Nitrous oxide") and [particulates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particulate "Particulate"), and is a significant contributor to [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming "Global warming") through emission of [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide "Carbon dioxide"), for which transport is the fastest-growing emission sector. According to the International Energy Agency (IEA), the transportation sector accounts for more than one-third of CO2 emissions globally in the early 2020ies. By sub-sector, road transport is the largest contributor to global warming. [Environmental regulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_regulation "Environmental regulation") in developed countries have reduced individual vehicles' emissions; however, this has been offset by increases in the numbers of vehicles and in the use of each vehicle. Some pathways to reduce the carbon emissions of road vehicles considerably have been studied. Energy use and emissions vary largely between modes, causing [environmentalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentalism "Environmentalism") to call for a transition from air and road to rail and human-powered transport, as well as increased [transport electrification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_electrification "Transport electrification") and [energy efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efficient_energy_use "Efficient energy use").

Other environmental impacts of transport systems include [traffic congestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_congestion "Traffic congestion") and automobile-oriented [urban sprawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_sprawl "Urban sprawl"), which can consume natural habitat and agricultural lands. By reducing transport emissions globally, it is predicted that there will be significant positive effects on Earth's [air quality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_quality "Air quality"), [acid rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid_rain "Acid rain"), [smog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smog "Smog"), and climate change.

While [electric cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_car "Electric car") are being built to cut down [CO2 emission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CO2_emission "CO2 emission") at the point of use, an approach that is becoming popular among cities worldwide is to prioritize public transport, bicycles, and [pedestrian movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedestrian "Pedestrian"). Redirecting vehicle movement to create 20-minute neighbourhoods that promotes [exercise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise "Exercise") while greatly reducing vehicle dependency and pollution. Some policies are levying a congestion charge to cars for travelling within congested areas during peak time.

[Airplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airplane "Airplane") emissions change depending on the flight distance. It takes a lot of energy to take off and land, so longer flights are more efficient per mile traveled. However, longer flights naturally use more fuel in total. Short flights produce the most CO2 per passenger mile, while long flights produce slightly less. Things get worse when planes fly high in the [atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmosphere "Atmosphere"). Their emissions trap much more heat than those released at ground level. This is not just because of CO2, but a mix of other greenhouse gases in the exhaust. In 2022 global CO2 emissions from the transport sector grew by more than 250 Mt CO2 to nearly 8 Gt CO2, which represent more than 3% compared to 2021. Aviation was responsible for a significant part of that increase.

City buses produce about 0.3 kg of CO2 for every mile traveled per passenger. For long-distance bus trips (over 20 miles), that pollution drops to about 0.08 kg of CO2 per passenger mile. On average, commuter trains produce around 0.17 kg of CO2 for each mile traveled per passenger. Long-distance trains are slightly higher at about 0.19 kg of CO2 per passenger mile. The fleet emission average for delivery vans, trucks and big rigs is 10.17 kg (22.4 lb) CO2 per gallon of diesel consumed. Delivery vans and trucks average about 7.8 mpg (or 1.3 kg of CO2 per mile) while big rigs average about 5.3 mpg (or 1.92 kg of CO2 per mile).

### Sustainable development

The United Nations first formally recognized the role of transport in [sustainable development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_development "Sustainable development") in the 1992 [United Nations Earth summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Earth_Summit_2012 "United Nations Earth Summit 2012"). In the 2012 United Nations World Conference, global leaders unanimously recognized that transport and mobility are central to achieving the sustainability targets. Since then, data has been collected to show that the transport sector contributes to a quarter of the global [greenhouse gas emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas "Greenhouse gas"), and therefore [sustainable transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_transport "Sustainable transport") has been mainstreamed across several of the [2030 Sustainable Development Goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Development_Goals "Sustainable Development Goals"), especially those related to food, security, health, energy, economic growth, infrastructure, and cities and human settlements. Meeting sustainable transport targets is said to be particularly important to achieving the [Paris Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Agreement "Paris Agreement").

There are various [Sustainable Development Goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Development_Goals "Sustainable Development Goals") (SDGs) that are promoting sustainable transport to meet the defined goals. These include [SDG 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Development_Goal_3 "Sustainable Development Goal 3") on health (increased road safety), [SDG 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Development_Goal_7 "Sustainable Development Goal 7") on energy, [SDG 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Development_Goal_8 "Sustainable Development Goal 8") on decent work and economic growth, [SDG 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Development_Goal_9 "Sustainable Development Goal 9") on resilient infrastructure, [SDG 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Development_Goal_11 "Sustainable Development Goal 11") on sustainable cities (access to transport and expanded public transport), [SDG 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Development_Goal_12 "Sustainable Development Goal 12") on sustainable consumption and production (ending [fossil fuel subsidies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel_subsidies "Fossil fuel subsidies")), and SDG 14 on oceans, seas, and marine resources.

Contemporary development studies recognise transportation networks as a key element of economic development, socio-economic well-being and poverty reduction. However, road network development has not always fulfilled its original intentions and has contributed significantly to environmental degradation and, in some cases, led to the loss of cultural traditions and the marginalisation of indigenous peoples. Compared to roads, the development of air links (helicopters and planes) has had an even more devastating impact. What is more, helicopters used for tourist activities are subject to considerable criticism from a perspective of environmental protection as well as sports ethics.

## History

### Natural

Humans' first ways to move included walking, running, and swimming. The [domestication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestication "Domestication") of animals introduced a new way to lay the burden of transport on more powerful creatures, allowing the hauling of heavier loads, or humans riding animals for greater speed and duration. Inventions such as the wheel and the sled (U.K. sledge) helped make animal transport more efficient through the introduction of [vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle "Vehicle").

The first forms of [road transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_transport "Road transport") involved animals, such as [horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse "Horse") ([domesticated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestication_of_the_horse "Domestication of the horse") in the 4th or the 3rd millennium BCE), [oxen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ox "Ox") (from about 8000 BCE), or humans carrying goods over [dirt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirt "Dirt") tracks that often followed [game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_(food) "Game (food)") [trails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trail "Trail").

### Water transport

Water transport, including rowed and sailed vessels, dates back to [time immemorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_immemorial "Time immemorial") and was the only efficient way to transport large quantities or over large distances prior to the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution "Industrial Revolution"). The first watercraft were [canoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canoe "Canoe") either cut out from [tree trunks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_trunk "Tree trunk") or made of animal hides. Early deep water transport was accomplished with ships that were either rowed or used the [wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind "Wind") for propulsion, or a combination of the two. The importance of water has led to most cities that grew up as sites for trading being located on rivers or on the sea-shore, often at the intersection of two bodies of water.

### Mechanical

Until the Industrial Revolution, transport remained slow and costly, and production and consumption gravitated as close to each other as feasible.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] The [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution "Industrial Revolution") in the 19th century saw several inventions fundamentally change transport. With the [optical telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_telegraph "Optical telegraph"), communication became rapid and independent of the transport of physical objects. The invention of the [steam engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_engine "Steam engine"), closely followed by its application in [rail transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport "Rail transport"), made land transport independent of human or animal muscles. Both speed and capacity increased, allowing specialization through manufacturing being located independently of natural resources. The 19th century also saw the development of the [steam ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_ship "Steam ship"), which sped up global transport.

With the development of the [combustion engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_combustion_engine "Internal combustion engine") and the automobile around 1900, road transport became more competitive again, and mechanical private transport originated. The first "modern" highways were constructed during the 19th century with [macadam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macadam "Macadam"). Later, [tarmac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarmacadam "Tarmacadam") and [concrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concrete "Concrete") became the dominant paving materials.

In 1903 the [Wright brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_brothers "Wright brothers") demonstrated the first successful controllable [airplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airplane "Airplane"), and after World War I (1914–1918) aircraft became a fast way to transport people and express goods over long distances.

After World War II (1939–1945) the automobile and airlines took higher shares of transport, reducing rail and water to freight and short-haul passenger services. Scientific spaceflight began in the 1950s, with rapid growth until the 1970s, when interest dwindled. In the 1950s the introduction of [containerization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Containerization "Containerization") gave massive efficiency gains in freight transport, fostering [globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization "Globalization"). International air travel became much more accessible in the 1960s with the commercialization of the [jet engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_engine "Jet engine"). Along with the growth in automobiles and motorways, rail and water transport declined in relative importance. After the introduction of the [Shinkansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinkansen "Shinkansen") in Japan in 1964, high-speed rail in Asia and Europe started attracting passengers on long-haul routes away from the airlines.

In the U.S. during the 19th century, [private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privately_held_company "Privately held company") [joint-stock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint-stock "Joint-stock") [corporations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporations "Corporations") owned most [aqueducts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigable_aqueduct "Navigable aqueduct"), [bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridge "Bridge"), [canals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canals "Canals"), [railroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railroads "Railroads"), [roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roads "Roads"), and [tunnels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunnels "Tunnels"). Most such transport infrastructure came under government control in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, culminating in the [nationalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalization "Nationalization") of inter-city passenger rail-service with the establishment of [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak "Amtrak"). However, as recently as 2010, a movement to privatize roads and other infrastructure has gained ground and adherents.
