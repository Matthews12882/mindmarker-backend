# Pakistan

**Pakistan**, officially the **Islamic Republic of Pakistan**, is a country in [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia"). It is the [fifth-most populous country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population "List of countries and dependencies by population"), with a population of over 241.5 million, having the [second-largest Muslim population in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_by_country#Countries "Islam by country") as of 2023. [Islamabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad "Islamabad") is the nation's capital, while [Karachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi "Karachi") is [its largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Pakistan_by_population "List of cities in Pakistan by population") and [financial centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_centre "Financial centre"). Pakistan is the [33rd-largest country by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area "List of countries and dependencies by area"). Bounded by the [Arabian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Sea "Arabian Sea") on the south, the [Gulf of Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Oman "Gulf of Oman") on the southwest, and the [Sir Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Creek "Sir Creek") on the southeast, it shares land borders with [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") to the east; [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan") to the west; [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran") to the southwest; and [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") to the northeast. It shares a maritime border with [Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oman "Oman") in the Gulf of Oman, and is separated from [Tajikistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajikistan "Tajikistan") in the northwest by Afghanistan's narrow [Wakhan Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakhan_Corridor "Wakhan Corridor").

Pakistan is the site of [several ancient cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Pakistan "History of Pakistan"), including the 8,500-year-old [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic "Neolithic") site of [Mehrgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehrgarh "Mehrgarh") in [Balochistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochistan,_Pakistan "Balochistan, Pakistan"), the [Indus Valley Civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation "Indus Valley Civilisation") of the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age "Bronze Age"), and the ancient [Gandhara civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandhara_civilisation "Gandhara civilisation"). The regions that compose the modern state of Pakistan were the realm of multiple empires and dynasties, including the [Achaemenid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid "Achaemenid"), the [Maurya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya "Maurya"), the [Kushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushan_Empire "Kushan Empire"), the [Gupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire "Gupta Empire"); the [Umayyad Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate "Umayyad Caliphate") in its southern regions, the [Hindu Shahis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Shahis "Hindu Shahis"), the [Ghaznavids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaznavids "Ghaznavids"), the [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate "Delhi Sultanate"), the [Samma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samma_dynasty "Samma dynasty"), the [Shah Miris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Miris "Shah Miris"), the [Mughals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughals "Mughals"), and finally, the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj") from 1858 to 1947.

Spurred by the [Pakistan Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Movement "Pakistan Movement"), which sought a homeland for the Muslims of [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidencies_and_provinces_of_British_India "Presidencies and provinces of British India"), and election victories in 1946 by the [All-India Muslim League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-India_Muslim_League "All-India Muslim League"), Pakistan gained independence in 1947 after the [partition of British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_British_India "Partition of British India"), which awarded separate statehood to its Muslim-majority regions and was accompanied by an unparalleled mass migration and loss of life. Initially [a dominion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Pakistan "Dominion of Pakistan") of the [British Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Commonwealth "British Commonwealth"), Pakistan [adopted a republican constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan_(1956%E2%80%931958) "Constitution of Pakistan (1956–1958)") in 1956 and became an [Islamic republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_republic "Islamic republic") with two geographically separate provinces, [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan "East Pakistan") and [West Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pakistan "West Pakistan"). East Pakistan seceded as the new country of [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh") in 1971 after a [nine-month-long civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War "Bangladesh Liberation War"). In the following four decades, Pakistan has been ruled by governments that alternated between civilian and military, democratic and authoritarian, relatively [secular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularism_in_Pakistan "Secularism in Pakistan") and Islamist.

Pakistan is considered a [middle power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_power "Middle power") and is a declared [nuclear-weapon state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear-weapon_state "Nuclear-weapon state"), with the world's [seventh-largest standing armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_number_of_military_and_paramilitary_personnel "List of countries by number of military and paramilitary personnel"). The [Pakistani economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_economy "Pakistani economy") is ranked amongst the world's emerging and growth-leading economies, with a large and rapidly growing middle class. Pakistan's political history since independence has been characterised by periods of significant economic and military growth as well as those of political and economic instability. It is an [ethnically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Pakistan "Ethnic groups in Pakistan") and [linguistically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Pakistan "Languages of Pakistan") diverse country, with similarly diverse [geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Pakistan "Geography of Pakistan") and [wildlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_of_Pakistan "Wildlife of Pakistan"). The country continues to face [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_Pakistan "Poverty in Pakistan"), [illiteracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literate_Pakistan_Foundation "Literate Pakistan Foundation"), [corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_Pakistan "Corruption in Pakistan"), and [terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_in_Pakistan "Terrorism in Pakistan"). Pakistan is a member of the United Nations, the [Shanghai Cooperation Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_Cooperation_Organisation "Shanghai Cooperation Organisation"), the [Organisation of Islamic Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_Islamic_Cooperation "Organisation of Islamic Cooperation"), the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations "Commonwealth of Nations"), the [South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Association_for_Regional_Cooperation "South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation"), and the [Islamic Military Counter-Terrorism Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Military_Counter_Terrorism_Coalition "Islamic Military Counter Terrorism Coalition"), and is designated as a [major non-NATO ally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_non-NATO_ally "Major non-NATO ally") by the United States.

## Etymology

The name *Pakistan* was coined by [Choudhry Rahmat Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choudhry_Rahmat_Ali "Choudhry Rahmat Ali"), a [Pakistan Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Movement "Pakistan Movement") activist, who in January 1933 first published it (originally as "Pakstan") in a pamphlet *[Now or Never](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Declaration "Pakistan Declaration")*, using it as an [acronym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acronym "Acronym"). Rahmat Ali explained: "It is composed of letters taken from the names of all our homelands, Indian and Asian, [**P**anjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Province_(British_India) "Punjab Province (British India)"), [**A**fghania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-West_Frontier_Province_(1901%E2%80%932010) "North-West Frontier Province (1901–2010)"), [**K**ashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_(princely_state) "Jammu and Kashmir (princely state)"), [**S**indh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sind_Division "Sind Division"), and [Baluchis**tan**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baluchistan_Agency "Baluchistan Agency")." He added, "Pakistan is both a [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language "Persian language") and [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu "Urdu") word... It means the land of the Paks, the spiritually pure and clean." Etymologists note that [پاک](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D9%BE%D8%A7%DA%A9#Persian "wikt:پاک") *pāk*, is 'pure' in Persian and [Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto "Pashto") and the Persian suffix [ـستان](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D9%80%D8%B3%D8%AA%D8%A7%D9%86#Persian "wikt:ـستان") *[-stan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/-stan "-stan")* means 'land' or 'place of'.

Rahmat Ali's concept of Pakistan only related to the northwestern area of the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent"). He also proposed the name "Banglastan" for the Muslim areas of [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal "Bengal") and "Osmanistan" for [Hyderabad State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad_State "Hyderabad State"), as well as a political federation between the three.

## History

### Prehistory and antiquity

Some of the earliest ancient human civilisations in [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia") originated from areas encompassing present-day Pakistan. The earliest known stone tools in the region, dating to the [Lower Palaeolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Palaeolithic "Lower Palaeolithic") (~2 million years ago), were discovered in the [Soan Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soan_Valley "Soan Valley") of [northern Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Pakistan "Northern Pakistan"). The [Indus plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_plain "Indus plain") region, which covers most of the present-day Pakistan, was the site of several successive ancient cultures including the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic "Neolithic") (7000–4300 BCE) site of [Mehrgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehrgarh "Mehrgarh"), and the 5,000-year history of urban life in South Asia to the various sites of the [Indus Valley Civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation "Indus Valley Civilisation"), including [Mohenjo-daro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohenjo-daro "Mohenjo-daro") and [Harappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harappa "Harappa").

Following the decline of the Indus Valley Civilisation, semi-nomadic [Indo-European Aryans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryans "Indo-Aryans") migrated into the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent") around 2000 BCE, perhaps by way of the [Khyber Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khyber_Pass "Khyber Pass"). They fused with the indigenous Harappan culture of the Indus Valley, and elements of the pre-Aryan spiritual traditions were assimilated into the developing Vedic tradition. This cultural milieu shaped the [Gandhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandhara "Gandhara") Civilisation, which flourished at the crossroads of India, Central Asia, and the Middle East, connecting [trade routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road "Silk Road") and absorbing cultural influences from diverse civilisations. By the early Vedic period, parts of the Indus region in present-day Pakistan were populated by numerous tribes that were beginning to coalesce into chieftain-led clans and early kingdoms. During this period, the [Vedas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedas "Vedas"), the oldest [scriptures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scriptures "Scriptures") of [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism"), were composed.

### Classical period

The western regions of Pakistan [became a part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_conquest_of_the_Indus_Valley "Achaemenid conquest of the Indus Valley") of the [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire "Achaemenid Empire") around 517 BCE. In 326 BCE, [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great "Alexander the Great") conquered the region by defeating various local rulers, most notably, the King [Porus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porus "Porus"), at [Jhelum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Hydaspes "Battle of Hydaspes"). Among the major powers that ruled the region were the [Mauryas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauryas "Mauryas") (322–185 BCE), during which [Ashoka the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka_the_Great "Ashoka the Great") extended the empire. The [Indo-Greek Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Greek_Kingdom "Indo-Greek Kingdom") founded by [Demetrius of Bactria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demetrius_I_of_Bactria "Demetrius I of Bactria") (180–165 BCE) included Gandhara and [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab "Punjab") and reached its greatest extent under [Menander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menander_I "Menander I") (165–150 BCE), allowing the [Greco-Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Buddhist "Greco-Buddhist") culture in the region to prosper. [Taxila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxila "Taxila") had one of the earliest universities and centres of higher education in the world, which was established during the late Vedic period in the 6th century BCE. [The ancient university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_ancient_Taxila "University of ancient Taxila") was documented by the invading forces of Alexander the Great and recorded by Chinese pilgrims in the 4th or 5th century CE. At its zenith, the [Rai dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rai_dynasty "Rai dynasty") (489–632 CE) ruled [Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindh "Sindh") and the surrounding territories.

### Medieval period

[Makran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makran "Makran") came under Muslim rule in the 7th century CE when it was conquered and a garrison was established under [Mu'awiya I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mu%27awiya_I "Mu'awiya I") (r. 661–680). The Arab general [Muhammad ibn Qasim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_ibn_Qasim "Muhammad ibn Qasim") conquered Sindh and some regions of Punjab in 711 CE. The Pakistan government's official chronology claims this as the time when the foundation of Pakistan was laid. The early medieval period (642–1219 CE) witnessed the spread of Islam in the region. Before the arrival of Islam beginning in the 8th century, the region of Pakistan was home to a diverse plethora of faiths, including Hinduism, [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"), [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism "Jainism") and [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism "Zoroastrianism"). During this period, [Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufi "Sufi") [missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawah "Dawah") played a pivotal role in converting a majority of the regional population to Islam. Upon the defeat of the [Turk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turk_Shahis "Turk Shahis") and [Hindu Shahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Shahi "Hindu Shahi") dynasties which governed the [Kabul Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_Valley "Kabul Valley"), Gandhara, and western Punjab in the 7th to 11th centuries CE, [several successive Muslim empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_in_the_Indian_subcontinent "Muslim conquest in the Indian subcontinent") ruled over the region, including the [Ghaznavid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaznavid_Empire "Ghaznavid Empire") (975–1187 CE), the [Ghorid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghorid_Empire "Ghorid Empire"), and the [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate "Delhi Sultanate") (1206–1526 CE). The [Lodi dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lodi_dynasty "Lodi dynasty"), the last of the Delhi Sultanate, was replaced by the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire "Mughal Empire") (1526–1857 CE).

The Mughals strengthened [Persianate culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persianate_culture "Persianate culture") through literature, book production, illustration, and architecture. In the region of modern-day Pakistan, key urban centres during the Mughal period were [Multan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multan "Multan"), [Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore "Lahore"), [Peshawar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshawar "Peshawar") and [Thatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thatta "Thatta"), which were chosen as the site of impressive [Mughal buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_architecture "Mughal architecture"). In the early 16th century, the region remained under the Mughal Empire. In the 18th century, the slow disintegration of the Mughal Empire was hastened by the emergence of the rival powers like the [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire "Maratha Empire") and later the [Sikh Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_Empire "Sikh Empire"), as well as invasions by [Nader Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah "Nader Shah") from Iran in 1739, and the [Durrani Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durrani_Empire "Durrani Empire") of Afghanistan in 1748. The growing political power of the British in Bengal had not yet reached the territories of modern Pakistan.

### Colonial rule

None of modern Pakistan was under British rule until 1839 when [Karachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi "Karachi"), a small fishing village governed by [Talpurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talpur_dynasty "Talpur dynasty") of Sindh with a mud fort guarding the harbour, was [taken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Karachi#Company_rule "History of Karachi"), and used as an enclave with a port and [military base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_Cantonment "Karachi Cantonment") for the [First Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Afghan_War "First Afghan War") that ensued. The remainder of Sindh was acquired in 1843, and subsequently, through a series of wars and treaties, the [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company "East India Company"), and later, after the [Sepoy Mutiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepoy_Mutiny "Sepoy Mutiny") (1857–1858), direct rule by [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria "Queen Victoria") of the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire"), acquired most of the region. Key conflicts included those against the [Baloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baloch_people "Baloch people") Talpur dynasty, resolved by the [Battle of Miani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Miani "Battle of Miani") (1843) in Sindh, the [Anglo-Sikh Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Sikh_War_(disambiguation) "Anglo-Sikh War (disambiguation)") (1845–1849), and the [Anglo-Afghan Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Afghan_Wars "Anglo-Afghan Wars") (1839–1919). By 1893, modern Pakistan was part of the [British Indian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Empire "British Indian Empire"), and remained so until independence in 1947.

Under British rule, modern Pakistan was primarily divided into the [Sind Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sind_Division "Sind Division"), [Punjab Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Province_(British_India) "Punjab Province (British India)"), and the [Baluchistan Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baluchistan_Agency "Baluchistan Agency"). The region also included various [princely states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_state "Princely state"), with the largest being [Bahawalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahawalpur_(princely_state) "Bahawalpur (princely state)").

An important uprising against the British in the region was the [Indian Rebellion of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857 "Indian Rebellion of 1857"), known at the time as the [Sepoy Mutiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepoy_Mutiny "Sepoy Mutiny"). Divergence in the [relationship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu%E2%80%93Islamic_relations "Hindu–Islamic relations") between [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism") and Islam resulted in significant tension in [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj"), leading to religious violence. The [language controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi%E2%80%93Urdu_controversy "Hindi–Urdu controversy") further exacerbated tensions between Hindus and Muslims. A [Muslim intellectual movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aligarh_Movement "Aligarh Movement"), led by Sir [Syed Ahmed Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syed_Ahmed_Khan "Syed Ahmed Khan") to counter the [Hindu renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_renaissance "Hindu renaissance"), advocated for the [two-nation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-nation_theory "Two-nation theory") and led to the establishment of the [All-India Muslim League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-India_Muslim_League "All-India Muslim League") in 1906.

In March 1929, in response to the [Nehru Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nehru_Report "Nehru Report"), [Muhammad Ali Jinnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_Jinnah "Muhammad Ali Jinnah"), the founder of Pakistan, issued his [fourteen points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourteen_Points_of_Jinnah "Fourteen Points of Jinnah"), which included proposals to safeguard the interests of the Muslim minority in a united India. These proposals were rejected. In his 29 December 1930 address, [Allama Iqbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allama_Iqbal "Allama Iqbal") advocated the amalgamation of Muslim-majority states in [North-West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_India_(pre-1947) "Northwest India (pre-1947)") India, including [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Province_(British_India) "Punjab Province (British India)"), [North-West Frontier Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-West_Frontier_Province_(1901%E2%80%9355) "North-West Frontier Province (1901–55)"), [Sind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sind_Province_(1936%E2%80%9355) "Sind Province (1936–55)"), and [Baluchistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baluchistan_(Chief_Commissioner%27s_Province) "Baluchistan (Chief Commissioner's Province)"). The perception that Congress-led British [provincial governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1937_Indian_provincial_elections "1937 Indian provincial elections") neglected the Muslim League from 1937 to 1939 motivated Jinnah and other Muslim League leaders to embrace the two-nation theory. This led to the adoption of the [Lahore Resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Resolution "Lahore Resolution") of 1940, presented by [Sher-e-Bangla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sher-e-Bangla "Sher-e-Bangla") [A.K. Fazlul Haque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.K._Fazlul_Haque "A.K. Fazlul Haque"), also known as the Pakistan Resolution.

By 1942, Britain faced considerable strain during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), with India directly threatened by Japanese forces. Britain had pledged voluntary independence for India in exchange for support during the war. However, this pledge included a clause stating that no part of British India would be compelled to join the resulting dominion, which could be interpreted as support for an independent Muslim nation. Congress under the leadership of [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi "Mahatma Gandhi") launched the [Quit India Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quit_India_Movement "Quit India Movement"), demanding an immediate end to British rule. In contrast, the Muslim League chose to support the [UK's war efforts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_in_World_War_II "United Kingdom in World War II"), thereby nurturing the possibility of establishing a Muslim nation.

### Independence

The [1946 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1946_Indian_provincial_elections "1946 Indian provincial elections") saw the Muslim League secure 90 percent of the Muslim seats, supported by the landowners of Sindh and Punjab. This forced the Indian National Congress, initially skeptical of the League's representation of Indian Muslims, to acknowledge its significance. Jinnah's emergence as the voice of the Indian Muslims, compelled the British to consider their stance, despite their [reluctance to partition India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposition_to_the_partition_of_India "Opposition to the partition of India"). In a final attempt to prevent partition, they proposed the [Cabinet Mission Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Mission_Plan "Cabinet Mission Plan").

As the Cabinet Mission failed, the British announced their intention to end rule by June 1948. Following rigorous discussions involving [Viceroy of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy_of_India "Viceroy of India"), [Lord Mountbatten of Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Mountbatten_of_Burma "Lord Mountbatten of Burma"), [Muhammad Ali Jinnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_Jinnah "Muhammad Ali Jinnah") of the [All-India Muslim League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-India_Muslim_League "All-India Muslim League"), and [Jawaharlal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru "Jawaharlal Nehru") of Congress, the formal declaration to partition British India into two independent dominions—namely Pakistan and India—was issued by Mountbatten on the evening of 3 June 1947. In Mountbatten's oval office, the prime ministers of around a dozen major princely states gathered to receive their copies of the plan before its worldwide broadcast. At 7:00 P.M., [All India Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Radio "All India Radio") transmitted the public announcement, starting with the viceroy's address, followed by individual speeches from Nehru, and Jinnah. The founder of Pakistan [Muhammad Ali Jinnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_Jinnah "Muhammad Ali Jinnah") concluded his address with the slogan *Pakistan Zindabad* (Long Live Pakistan).

As the United Kingdom agreed to the [partitioning of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitioning_of_India "Partitioning of India"), the modern state of Pakistan was established on [14 August 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_(Pakistan) "Independence Day (Pakistan)") (27th of [Ramadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan "Ramadan") in 1366 of the [Islamic calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_calendar "Islamic calendar"), [a holy time regarded as auspicious and noted for its religious importance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_of_Power "Night of Power")). This new nation amalgamated the [Muslim-majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_India "Islam in India") eastern and [northwestern regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_India_(pre-1947) "Northwest India (pre-1947)") of [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India "British India"), comprising the provinces of [Balochistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochistan_(Pakistan) "Balochistan (Pakistan)"), [East Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bengal "East Bengal"), the [North-West Frontier Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-West_Frontier_Province_(1901%E2%80%9355) "North-West Frontier Province (1901–55)"), [West Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab,_Pakistan "Punjab, Pakistan"), and Sindh.

In the riots that accompanied the partition in Punjab Province, between 200,000 and 2,000,000 people were killed in what some have described as a retributive genocide between the religions. Around 50,000 Muslim women were [abducted and raped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence_against_women_during_the_partition_of_India "Violence against women during the partition of India") by Hindu and Sikh men, while 33,000 Hindu and Sikh women experienced the same fate at the hands of Muslims. Around 6.5 million Muslims moved from India to West Pakistan and 4.7 million Hindus and Sikhs moved from West Pakistan to India. It was the largest mass migration in human history. A subsequent dispute over the [princely state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_state "Princely state") of [Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_(princely_state) "Jammu and Kashmir (princely state)") eventually sparked the [Indo-Pakistani War of 1947–1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1947%E2%80%931948 "Indo-Pakistani War of 1947–1948").

### Post-independence

After [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creation_of_Pakistan "Creation of Pakistan") in 1947, Jinnah, the [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_chair "Party chair") of the Muslim League, became Pakistan's first [governor-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_Pakistan "Governor-General of Pakistan") and the first [President-Speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_National_Assembly_of_Pakistan "Speaker of the National Assembly of Pakistan") of the [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Pakistan "Parliament of Pakistan"), but he died of tuberculosis on 11 September 1948. Meanwhile, Pakistan's founding fathers agreed to appoint [Liaquat Ali Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liaquat_Ali_Khan "Liaquat Ali Khan"), the [secretary-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary-general "Secretary-general") of the [party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-India_Muslim_League "All-India Muslim League"), the nation's [first prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Prime_Ministers_of_Pakistan "List of Prime Ministers of Pakistan"). From 1947 to 1956, [Pakistan was a monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_Pakistan "Monarchy of Pakistan") within the Commonwealth of Nations, and had two monarchs before it became a republic.

The creation of Pakistan was never fully accepted by many British leaders, including [Lord Mountbatten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Mountbatten "Lord Mountbatten"). Mountbatten expressed his lack of support and faith in the Muslim League's idea of Pakistan. Jinnah refused Mountbatten's offer to serve as Governor-General of Pakistan. When Mountbatten was asked by [Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Collins_(writer) "Larry Collins (writer)") and [Lapierre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominique_Lapierre "Dominique Lapierre") if he would have sabotaged Pakistan had he known that Jinnah was dying of tuberculosis, he replied 'most probably'.

> "You are free; you are free to go to your temples, you are free to go to your mosques or to any other place of worship in this State of Pakistan. You may belong to any religion or caste or creed – that has nothing to do with the business of the State."

— [Muhammad Ali Jinnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_Jinnah "Muhammad Ali Jinnah")'s first speech to the Constituent Assembly of Pakistan.

Maulana [Shabbir Ahmad Usmani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shabbir_Ahmad_Usmani "Shabbir Ahmad Usmani"), a respected Deobandi *alim* (scholar) who held the position of Shaykh al-Islam in Pakistan in 1949, and [Maulana Mawdudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maulana_Mawdudi "Maulana Mawdudi") of [Jamaat-i-Islami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaat-i-Islami "Jamaat-i-Islami") played key roles in advocating for an Islamic constitution. Mawdudi insisted that the Constituent Assembly declare the "supreme sovereignty of God" and the supremacy of the *shariah* in Pakistan.

The efforts of Jamaat-i-Islami and the *[ulama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulama "Ulama")* led to the passage of the [Objectives Resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Objectives_Resolution "Objectives Resolution") in March 1949. This resolution, described by Liaquat Ali Khan as the second most significant step in Pakistan's history, affirmed that "sovereignty over the entire universe belongs to God Almighty alone and the authority which He has delegated to the State of Pakistan through its people for being exercised within the limits prescribed by Him is a sacred trust". It was later included as a preamble to the constitutions of 1956, 1962, and 1973.

[Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_in_Pakistan "Democracy in Pakistan") faced setbacks due to the [martial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_Pakistani_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat "1958 Pakistani coup d'état") imposed by President [Iskander Mirza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iskander_Mirza "Iskander Mirza"), who was succeeded by General [Ayub Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayub_Khan "Ayub Khan"). After adopting a [presidential system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan_of_1962 "Constitution of Pakistan of 1962") in 1962, Pakistan witnessed significant growth until the [second war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1965 "Indo-Pakistani War of 1965") with India in 1965, resulting in an economic downturn and widespread public discontent in 1967. In 1969, President [Yahya Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahya_Khan "Yahya Khan") consolidated control, but faced a devastating [cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_Bhola_cyclone "1970 Bhola cyclone") in East Pakistan resulting in 500,000 deaths.

In 1970, Pakistan conducted its [first democratic elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_Pakistani_general_election "1970 Pakistani general election") since independence, intending to transition from [military rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_Framework_Order,_1970 "Legal Framework Order, 1970") to democracy. However, after the East Pakistani [Awami League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awami_League "Awami League") emerged victorious over the [Pakistan Peoples Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Peoples_Party "Pakistan Peoples Party") (PPP), Yahya Khan and the military refused to transfer power. This led to [Operation Searchlight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Searchlight "Operation Searchlight"), a military crackdown, and eventually sparked the [war of liberation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War "Bangladesh Liberation War") by Bengali [Mukti Bahini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukti_Bahini "Mukti Bahini") forces in East Pakistan, described in West Pakistan as a civil war rather than a liberation struggle.

Independent researchers estimate that between 300,000 and 500,000 civilians died during this period while the Bangladesh government puts the number of dead at three million, a figure now nearly universally regarded as excessively inflated. Some academics such as [Rudolph Rummel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolph_Rummel "Rudolph Rummel") and [Rounaq Jahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rounaq_Jahan "Rounaq Jahan") say both sides committed genocide; others such as [Richard Sisson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Richard_Sisson "John Richard Sisson") and Leo E. Rose believe there was no genocide. In response to India's support for the insurgency in East Pakistan, [preemptive strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preemptive_strikes "Preemptive strikes") on India by Pakistan's [air force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Air_Force "Pakistan Air Force"), [navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Navy "Pakistan Navy"), and [marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Marines "Pakistan Marines") sparked [a conventional war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1971 "Indo-Pakistani War of 1971") in 1971 that resulted in an Indian victory and East Pakistan gaining [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Bangladesh "Independence of Bangladesh") as [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh").

Yahya Khan was replaced by [Zulfikar Ali Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulfikar_Ali_Bhutto "Zulfikar Ali Bhutto") as president; the country worked towards promulgating [its constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan "Constitution of Pakistan") and putting the country on the road to democracy. In 1972 Pakistan embarked on an ambitious plan to develop its [nuclear deterrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_and_its_Nuclear_Deterrent_Program "Pakistan and its Nuclear Deterrent Program") capability with [the goal of preventing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutually_assured_destruction "Mutually assured destruction") any [foreign invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_interventionism "Foreign interventionism"); the country's [first](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KANUPP "KANUPP") [nuclear power plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_plant "Nuclear power plant") was inaugurated in that same year. India's [first nuclear test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Smiling_Buddha "Operation Smiling Buddha") in 1974 gave Pakistan additional justification to accelerate its [nuclear program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_and_Nuclear_Weapons "Pakistan and Nuclear Weapons").

Democracy ended with a [military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Fair_Play "Operation Fair Play") in 1977 against the [leftist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism_in_Pakistan "Socialism in Pakistan") PPP, which saw [General Zia-ul-Haq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Zia-ul-Haq "General Zia-ul-Haq") become the president in 1978. From 1977 to 1988, President Zia's [corporatisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_sector_of_Pakistan "Corporate sector of Pakistan") and [economic Islamisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamization_of_Economy "Islamization of Economy") initiatives led to Pakistan becoming one of the fastest-growing economies in South Asia. While building up the country's [nuclear program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_and_its_Nuclear_Deterrent_Program "Pakistan and its Nuclear Deterrent Program"), increasing [Islamisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zia-ul-Haq%27s_Islamization "Zia-ul-Haq's Islamization"), and the rise of a homegrown [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_Pakistan "Conservatism in Pakistan") philosophy, Pakistan helped subsidise and distribute US [resources to factions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Cyclone "Operation Cyclone") of the [mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_mujahideen "Afghan mujahideen") against [the USSR's intervention in communist Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War "Soviet–Afghan War"). Pakistan's [North-West Frontier Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khyber_Pakhtunkhwa "Khyber Pakhtunkhwa") became a base for the anti-Soviet Afghan fighters, with the province's influential [Deobandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deobandi "Deobandi") ulama playing a significant role in encouraging and organising the 'jihad'.

[President Zia died](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Muhammad_Zia-ul-Haq "Death of Muhammad Zia-ul-Haq") in a plane crash in 1988, and [Benazir Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benazir_Bhutto "Benazir Bhutto"), daughter of Zulfikar Ali Bhutto, was elected as the country's [first female](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_Muslim_societies "Women in Muslim societies") Prime Minister. The PPP was followed by conservative [Pakistan Muslim League (N)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Muslim_League_(N) "Pakistan Muslim League (N)") (PML (N)), and over the next decade the leaders of the two parties fought for power, alternating in office. This period is marked by prolonged [stagflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periods_of_stagflation_in_Pakistan "Periods of stagflation in Pakistan"), political instability, [corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_Pakistan "Corruption in Pakistan"), misgovernment, [geopolitical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geostrategy "Geostrategy") rivalry with India, and the clash between [left-wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism_in_Pakistan "Socialism in Pakistan") and [right-wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_Pakistan "Conservatism in Pakistan") ideologies. As PML (N) secured a [supermajority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermajority "Supermajority") in [elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Pakistani_general_election "1997 Pakistani general election") in 1997, [Nawaz Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawaz_Sharif "Nawaz Sharif") authorised [nuclear testings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_nuclear_weapons_tests_of_Pakistan "List of nuclear weapons tests of Pakistan"), as a [retaliation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tit-for-tat "Tit-for-tat") to the [second nuclear tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokhran-II "Pokhran-II") conducted by India in May 1998.

Military tension between the two countries in the [Kargil district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil_district "Kargil district") led to the [Kargil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil_War "Kargil War") of 1999, and turmoil in [civil-military relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil-military_relations "Civil-military relations") allowed General [Pervez Musharraf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pervez_Musharraf "Pervez Musharraf") to take over through a [bloodless coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Pakistani_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat "1999 Pakistani coup d'état"). Musharraf governed Pakistan as [chief executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_government "Head of government") from 1999 to 2002 and as president from 2001 to 2008—a period of [enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlightened_moderation "Enlightened moderation"), social [liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_islam "Liberal islam"), extensive [economic reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_reforms_in_Pakistan "Economic reforms in Pakistan"), and [direct involvement in the US-led war on terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan%27s_role_in_the_War_on_Terror "Pakistan's role in the War on Terror"). By its own financial calculations, Pakistan's involvement in the war on terrorism has cost up to $118 billion, over [eighty one thousand casualties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_terrorist_incidents_in_Pakistan_since_2001 "List of terrorist incidents in Pakistan since 2001"), and more than 1.8 million displaced civilians.

The [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_(Pakistan) "National Assembly (Pakistan)") historically completed its first full five-year term on 15 November 2007. After the [assassination of Benazir Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Benazir_Bhutto "Assassination of Benazir Bhutto") in 2007, the PPP secured the [most votes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority "Majority") in the [elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Pakistani_general_election "2008 Pakistani general election") of 2008, appointing party member [Yusuf Raza Gilani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusuf_Raza_Gilani "Yusuf Raza Gilani") as Prime Minister. Threatened with [impeachment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_to_impeach_Pervez_Musharraf "Movement to impeach Pervez Musharraf"), President Musharraf resigned on 18 August 2008, and was succeeded by [Asif Ali Zardari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asif_Ali_Zardari "Asif Ali Zardari"). Clashes with the [judicature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_of_Pakistan "Judiciary of Pakistan") prompted Gilani's disqualification from the [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Pakistan "Parliament of Pakistan") and as the prime minister in June 2012. The [general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Pakistani_general_election "2013 Pakistani general election") held in 2013 saw the PML (N) achieve victory, following which Nawaz Sharif was elected as Prime Minister for the third time. In 2018, [PTI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Tehreek-e-Insaf "Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf") won the [general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Pakistan_general_election "2018 Pakistan general election") and [Imran Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imran_Khan "Imran Khan") became the 22nd Prime Minister. In April 2022, [Shehbaz Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shehbaz_Sharif "Shehbaz Sharif") was elected as prime minister, after Imran Khan lost a no-confidence vote. During [2024 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Pakistani_general_election "2024 Pakistani general election"), PTI-backed independents became the largest bloc, but Shehbaz Sharif was elected prime minister for a second term, as a result of a coalition between PML (N) and PPPP.

## Geography

Pakistan's diverse [geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Pakistan "Geography of Pakistan") and [climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Pakistan "Climate of Pakistan") host a wide array of [wildlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_of_Pakistan "Wildlife of Pakistan"). Covering 881,913 km2 (340,509 sq mi), Pakistan ranks as the [33rd-largest nation by total area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_area "List of countries by area"), but this varies based on Kashmir's disputed status. Pakistan boasts a 1,046 km (650 mi) coastline along the Arabian Sea and the Gulf of Oman, and shares land borders totalling 6,774 km (4,209 mi), including 2,430 km (1,510 mi) [with Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durand_Line "Durand Line"), 523 km (325 mi) [with China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Pakistan_border "China–Pakistan border"), 2,912 km (1,809 mi) [with India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India%E2%80%93Pakistan_border "India–Pakistan border"), and 909 km (565 mi) [with Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Pakistan_border "Iran–Pakistan border"). It has a [maritime border with Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oman%E2%80%93Pakistan_maritime_boundary "Oman–Pakistan maritime boundary") and is separated from Tajikistan via the narrow strip of the [Wakhan Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakhan_Corridor "Wakhan Corridor"). Situated at the crossroads of South Asia, the Middle East, and Central Asia, Pakistan's location is geopolitically significant. Geologically, Pakistan lies at the interaction of the [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Plate "Indian Plate") and [Eurasian tectonic plates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Plate "Eurasian Plate") within the Indus–Tsangpo Suture Zone, a region of high seismic activity. The collision between these two plates occurs in [northwest Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Pakistan "Northwest Pakistan"), particularly in [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khyber_Pakhtunkhwa "Khyber Pakhtunkhwa"). Sindh and Punjab are located on the north-western corner of the Indian Plate, while Balochistan and much of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa lie along its north-western margin. [Northern areas of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Pakistan "Northern Pakistan"), including Azad Kashmir, lie along the northern edge of the Indian Plate and are [prone to powerful earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_earthquakes_in_Pakistan "List of earthquakes in Pakistan").

Pakistan's landscapes vary from coastal plains to glaciated mountains, offering deserts, forests, hills, and plateaus. Pakistan is divided into three major geographic areas: the northern highlands, the Indus River plain, and the Balochistan Plateau. The northern highlands feature the [Karakoram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakoram "Karakoram"), [Hindu Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush "Hindu Kush"), and [Pamir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamir_Mountains "Pamir Mountains") mountain ranges, hosting some of the world's highest peaks, including five of the fourteen [eight-thousanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight-thousanders "Eight-thousanders") (mountain peaks over 8,000 metres or 26,250 feet), notably [K2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K2 "K2") (8,611 m or 28,251 ft) and [Nanga Parbat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanga_Parbat "Nanga Parbat") (8,126 m or 26,660 ft). The Balochistan Plateau lies in the west and the [Thar Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thar_Desert "Thar Desert") in the east. The 1,609 km (1,000 mi) Indus River and its tributaries traverse the nation from Kashmir to the Arabian Sea, sustaining alluvial plains along the Punjab and Sindh regions.

The climate varies from tropical to temperate, with arid conditions in the coastal south. There is a monsoon season with frequent flooding due to heavy rainfall, and a dry season with significantly less rainfall or none at all. Pakistan experiences four distinct seasons: a cool, dry winter from December through February; a hot, dry spring from March through May; the summer rainy season, or southwest monsoon period, from June through September; and the retreating monsoon period of October and November. Rainfall varies greatly from year to year, with patterns of alternate flooding and drought common.

### Flora and fauna

The diverse landscape and climate in Pakistan support a wide range of trees and plants. From coniferous [alpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_plant "Alpine plant") and [subalpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subalpine "Subalpine") trees like [spruce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spruce "Spruce"), [pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine "Pine"), and [deodar cedar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deodar_cedar "Deodar cedar") in the northern mountains to [deciduous trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deciduous_trees "Deciduous trees") like [shisham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shisham "Shisham") in the [Sulaiman Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulaiman_Mountains "Sulaiman Mountains"), and palms such as [coconut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut "Coconut") and [date](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_dactylifera "Phoenix dactylifera") in the southern regions. The western hills boast [juniper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juniper "Juniper"), [tamarisk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamarisk "Tamarisk"), coarse grasses, and scrub plants. [Mangrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove "Mangrove") forests dominate the coastal wetlands in the south. Coniferous forests span altitudes from 1,000 to 4,000 metres (3,300 to 13,100 feet) in most northern and northwestern highlands. In Balochistan's xeric regions, date palms and *[Ephedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephedra_(genus) "Ephedra (genus)")* are prevalent. In Punjab and Sindh's Indus plains, tropical and subtropical dry and moist broadleaf forests as well as tropical and xeric shrublands thrive. Approximately 4.8% or 36,845.6 square kilometres (3,684,560 ha) of Pakistan was forested in 2021.

Pakistan's fauna mirrors its diverse climate. The country boasts around 668 bird species, including [crows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crow "Crow"), [sparrows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World_sparrow "Old World sparrow"), [mynas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acridotheres "Acridotheres"), [hawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawk "Hawk"), [falcons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon "Falcon"), and [eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle "Eagle"). [Palas, Kohistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palas,_Kohistan "Palas, Kohistan"), is home to the [western tragopan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_tragopan "Western tragopan"), with many migratory birds visiting from Europe, Central Asia, and India. The southern plains harbor [mongooses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongoose "Mongoose"), [small Indian civet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_Indian_civet "Small Indian civet"), hares, the [Asiatic jackal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asiatic_jackal "Asiatic jackal"), the [Indian pangolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_pangolin "Indian pangolin"), the [jungle cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungle_cat "Jungle cat"), and the [sand cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand_cat "Sand cat"). Indus is home to [mugger crocodiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mugger_crocodile "Mugger crocodile"), while surrounding areas host [wild boars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_boar "Wild boar"), deer, and [porcupines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_porcupine "Indian porcupine"). Central Pakistan's sandy scrublands shelter Asiatic jackals, [striped hyenas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striped_hyena "Striped hyena"), wildcats, and [leopards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopard "Leopard"). The mountainous north hosts a variety of animals like the [Marco Polo sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Polo_sheep "Marco Polo sheep"), [urial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urial "Urial"), [markhor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markhor "Markhor") goat, [ibex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibex "Ibex") goat, [Asian black bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_black_bear "Asian black bear"), and [Himalayan brown bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayan_brown_bear "Himalayan brown bear").

The lack of vegetative cover, severe climate, and grazing impact on deserts have endangered wild animals. The [chinkara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinkara "Chinkara") is the only animal found in significant numbers in [Cholistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholistan "Cholistan"), with a few [nilgai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilgai "Nilgai") along the Pakistan–India border and in some parts of Cholistan. Rare animals include the [snow leopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow_leopard "Snow leopard") and the blind [Indus river dolphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_river_dolphin "Indus river dolphin"), of which there are believed to be about 1,816 remaining, protected at the [Indus Dolphin Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Dolphin_Reserve "Indus Dolphin Reserve") in Sindh. In total, 174 species of mammals, 177 species of reptiles, 22 species of amphibians, 198 species of freshwater fish, 668 species of birds, over 5,000 species of insects, and over 5,700 species of plants have been recorded in Pakistan. Pakistan faces deforestation, hunting, and pollution, with a 2019 [Forest Landscape Integrity Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Landscape_Integrity_Index "Forest Landscape Integrity Index") mean score of 7.42/10, ranking 41st globally out of 172 countries.

## Government and politics

Pakistan operates as a democratic [parliamentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_republic "Parliamentary republic") [federal republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_republic "Federal republic"), with Islam designated as the [state religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_religion "State religion"). The military's influence in mainstream politics has been significant throughout Pakistan's history. Initially adopting a [constitution in 1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan_of_1956 "Constitution of Pakistan of 1956"), Pakistan saw it suspended by Ayub Khan in 1958, replaced by a [second constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan_of_1962 "Constitution of Pakistan of 1962") in 1962. Under the leadership of [Zulfikar Ali Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulfikar_Ali_Bhutto "Zulfikar Ali Bhutto"), the [1973 constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan "Constitution of Pakistan") established a parliamentary system, but it was suspended under [Zia-ul-Haq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zia-ul-Haq "Zia-ul-Haq")'s martial law in 1977 and restored, with amendments, in 1985. The eras of 1958–1971, [1977–1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Pakistani_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat "1977 Pakistani coup d'état"), and 1999–2008 witnessed [military coups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_coups_in_Pakistan "Military coups in Pakistan"), leading to [martial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_in_Pakistan "Martial law in Pakistan") and military leaders governing de facto as presidents. Presently, Pakistan operates a [multi-party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party "Multi-party") [parliamentary system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system "Parliamentary system"), with distinct [checks and balances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checks_and_balances "Checks and balances") among government branches. The first successful [democratic transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Pakistani_general_election "2013 Pakistani general election") occurred in May 2013. Pakistani politics revolves around a blend of [socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism_in_Pakistan "Socialism in Pakistan"), [conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_Pakistan "Conservatism in Pakistan"), and the [third way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_way "Third way"), with the three main political parties being the conservative [Pakistan Muslim League (N)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Muslim_League_(N) "Pakistan Muslim League (N)"), socialist [Pakistani People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_People%27s_Party "Pakistani People's Party"), and centrist [Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Tehreek-e-Insaf "Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf"). Constitutional amendments in 2010 curtailed presidential powers, enhancing the role of the prime minister.

* [Head of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_State "Head of State"): The ceremonial head of the state and civilian [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief "Commander-in-chief") of the [Pakistan Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Armed_Forces "Pakistan Armed Forces") is the [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Pakistan "President of Pakistan"), elected by an [Electoral College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_College_of_Pakistan "Electoral College of Pakistan"). The [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Pakistan "Prime Minister of Pakistan") advises the president on key appointments, including military and judicial positions, and the president is constitutionally bound to act on this advice. The president also holds powers to pardon and grant clemency.
* [Legislative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative "Legislative"): The [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral "Bicameral") legislature includes a 96-member [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Pakistan "Senate of Pakistan") ([upper house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_house "Upper house")) and a 336-member [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Pakistan "National Assembly of Pakistan") ([lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house "Lower house")). [National Assembly members](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_National_Assembly_of_Pakistan "Member of the National Assembly of Pakistan") are elected via [first-past-the-post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-past-the-post "First-past-the-post") under [universal adult suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_adult_suffrage "Universal adult suffrage"), representing National Assembly [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituencies_of_Pakistan "Constituencies of Pakistan"). The constitution reserves 70 seats for women and religious minorities, allocated to political parties based on proportional representation. Senate members are elected by provincial legislators, ensuring equal representation across all provinces.

* Executive: The prime minister, typically the leader of the [majority rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority_rule "Majority rule") party or coalition in the National Assembly (the lower house), serves as the country's chief executive and head of government. Responsibilities include forming a [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Pakistan "Cabinet of Pakistan"), making executive decisions, and appointing senior civil servants, subject to executive confirmation.
* [Provincial governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_provincial_governments_of_Pakistan "List of provincial governments of Pakistan"): Each of the [four provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Provinces_(Pakistan) "Four Provinces (Pakistan)") follows a similar governance system, with a directly elected choosing the [Chief Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_(Pakistan) "Chief Minister (Pakistan)"), usually from the largest party or coalition. Chief Ministers lead the provincial cabinet and oversee provincial governance. The [Chief Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Secretary_(Pakistan) "Chief Secretary (Pakistan)"), appointed by the prime minister, heads the provincial bureaucracy. Provincial assemblies legislate and approve the provincial budget, typically presented by the provincial finance minister annually. Ceremonial heads of provinces, the Provincial Governors, are appointed by the president based on the binding advice of the prime minister.

* [Judicature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_of_Pakistan "Judiciary of Pakistan"): The judiciary in Pakistan has two classes: the superior and subordinate judiciary. The superior judiciary includes the [Supreme Court of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Pakistan "Supreme Court of Pakistan"), [Federal Shariat Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Shariat_Court "Federal Shariat Court"), and five [high courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_courts_of_Pakistan "High courts of Pakistan"), with the Supreme Court at the top. It's responsible for safeguarding the constitution. Azad Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan have their own court systems.

### Role of Islam

Pakistan, the only country established in the name of Islam, had overwhelming support among Muslims, especially in provinces like the [United Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Provinces_(1937%E2%80%9350) "United Provinces (1937–50)"), where Muslims were a minority. This idea, articulated by the Muslim League, the [Islamic clergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_clergy "Islamic clergy"), and Jinnah, envisioned an [Islamic state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_state "Islamic state"). Jinnah, closely associated with the *ulama*, was described upon his death by [Maulana Shabbir Ahmad Usmani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maulana_Shabbir_Ahmad_Usmani "Maulana Shabbir Ahmad Usmani") as the greatest Muslim after [Aurangzeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurangzeb "Aurangzeb"), aspiring to unite Muslims worldwide under Islam.

The Objectives Resolution of March 1949 marked the initial step towards this goal, affirming God as the sole sovereign. Muslim League leader [Chaudhry Khaliquzzaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaudhry_Khaliquzzaman "Chaudhry Khaliquzzaman") asserted that Pakistan could only truly become an Islamic state after bringing all believers of Islam into a single political unit. Keith Callard observed that Pakistanis believed in the essential unity of purpose and outlook in the Muslim world, expecting similar views on religion and nationality from Muslims worldwide.

Pakistan's desire for a united Islamic bloc, called Islamistan, wasn't supported by other Muslim governments, though figures like the Grand Mufti of Palestine, [Al-Haj Amin al-Husseini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amin_al-Husseini "Amin al-Husseini"), and leaders of the [Muslim Brotherhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_Brotherhood "Muslim Brotherhood") were drawn to the country. Pakistan's desire for an international organisation of Muslim countries was fulfilled in the 1970s when the [Organisation of Islamic Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_Islamic_Conference "Organisation of Islamic Conference") (OIC) was formed. In East Pakistan, Bengali Muslims increasingly prioritised Bengali ethno-nationalism over religious nationalism. The Islamist party [Jamaat-e-Islami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaat-e-Islami "Jamaat-e-Islami") backed an Islamic state and opposed [Bengali nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_nationalism "Bengali nationalism").

After the 1970 general elections, the Parliament crafted the [1973 Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan "Constitution of Pakistan"). It declared Pakistan an Islamic Republic, with Islam as the state religion, and mandated laws to comply with Islamic teachings laid down in the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran "Quran") and [Sunnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnah "Sunnah") and that no law repugnant to such injunctions could be enacted. Additionally, it established institutions like the Shariat Court and the [Council of Islamic Ideology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Islamic_Ideology "Council of Islamic Ideology") to interpret and apply Islam.

Zulfikar Ali Bhutto faced opposition under the banner of *Nizam-e-Mustafa* ("Rule of the [Prophet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad "Muhammad")"), advocating an Islamic state. Bhutto conceded to some Islamist demands before being ousted in a coup.

General Zia-ul-Haq, after seizing power, committed to establishing an Islamic state and enforcing *[sharia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharia "Sharia")* law. He instituted Shariat judicial courts, and court benches, to adjudicate using Islamic doctrine. Zia aligned with Deobandi institutions, exacerbating sectarian tensions with anti-Shia policies.

Most Pakistanis, according to a [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center "Pew Research Center") (PEW) poll, favour Sharia law as the official law, and 94 percent of them identify more with religion than nationality compared to Muslims in other nations.

### Foreign relations

Since independence, Pakistan has aimed to maintain an independent foreign policy. Pakistan's [foreign policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy_of_Pakistan "Foreign policy of Pakistan") and [geostrategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_geostrategy "Pakistani geostrategy") focus on the economy, security, [national identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism_in_Pakistan "Nationalism in Pakistan"), and territorial integrity, as well as building close ties with other Muslim nations. According to foreign policy expert [Hasan Askari Rizvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasan_Askari_Rizvi "Hasan Askari Rizvi"), "Pakistan highlights sovereign equality of states, bilateralism, mutuality of interests, and non-interference in each other's domestic affairs as the cardinal features of its foreign policy."

The [Kashmir conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir_conflict "Kashmir conflict") remains a major issue between Pakistan and India, with three of their [four wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_wars_and_conflicts "Indo-Pakistani wars and conflicts") fought over it. Due partly to strained relations with India, Pakistan has close ties with Turkey and Iran, both focal points in its foreign policy. Saudi Arabia also holds importance in Pakistan's foreign relations.

As a non-signatory of the [Treaty on Nuclear Non-Proliferation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Non-Proliferation_of_Nuclear_Weapons "Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons"), Pakistan holds influence in the [IAEA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAEA "IAEA"). For years, Pakistan has blocked an [international treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fissile_Material_Cut-off_Treaty "Fissile Material Cut-off Treaty") to limit [fissile material](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fissile_material "Fissile material"), arguing that its stockpile does not meet its long-term needs. Pakistan's nuclear program in the 20th century aimed to counter India's nuclear ambitions in the [region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia"), and [reciprocal nuclear tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan%27s_nuclear_testing_series "Pakistan's nuclear testing series") ensued after India's [nuclear tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokhran-II "Pokhran-II"), solidifying Pakistan as a [nuclear power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_in_Pakistan "Nuclear power in Pakistan"). Pakistan maintains a policy of [Full spectrum deterrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_spectrum_deterrence "Full spectrum deterrence"), considering its nuclear program vital for [deterring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_and_its_Nuclear_Deterrent_Program "Pakistan and its Nuclear Deterrent Program") foreign aggression.

Located strategically in the world's major maritime oil supply lines and communication [fiber optic corridors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_fiber "Optical fiber"), Pakistan also enjoys proximity to the natural resources of Central Asian countries. Pakistan actively participates in the United Nations with a [Permanent Representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Representative_of_Pakistan_to_the_United_Nations "Permanent Representative of Pakistan to the United Nations") representing its positions in international politics. It has advocated for the concept of "[enlightened moderation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlightened_moderation "Enlightened moderation")" in the Muslim world. Pakistan is a member of the Commonwealth of Nations, [SAARC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAARC "SAARC"), [ECO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Cooperation_Organization "Economic Cooperation Organization"), and the [G20 developing nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G20_developing_nations "G20 developing nations").

Pakistan is designated as an "Iron Brother" by China, emphasising the significance of their close and supportive [relationship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Pakistan_relations "China–Pakistan relations"). In the 1950s, Pakistan opposed the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") for geopolitical reasons. During the [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War "Soviet–Afghan War") in the 1980s, it was a close ally of the United States. Relations with Russia have improved since the end of the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War"), but Pakistan's relationship with the United States has been "on-and-off". Initially a close ally during the Cold War, Pakistan's relations with the US soured in the 1990s due to [sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressler_amendment "Pressler amendment") over its secretive nuclear program. Since [9/11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9/11 "9/11"), Pakistan has been a US ally on [counterterrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterterrorism "Counterterrorism"), but their relationship has been strained due to diverging interests and mistrust during the [20-year war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Afghanistan_(2001%E2%80%932021) "War in Afghanistan (2001–2021)") and terrorism issues. Although Pakistan was granted [major non-NATO ally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_non-NATO_ally "Major non-NATO ally") status by the U.S. in 2004, it faced accusations of supporting the [Taliban insurgents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taliban_insurgents "Taliban insurgents") in Afghanistan.

Pakistan does not have formal [diplomatic relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan-Israel_relations "Pakistan-Israel relations") with [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel"); nonetheless, an exchange occurred between the two countries in 2005, with Turkey acting as an intermediary.

#### Relations with China

Pakistan was among the first nations to establish formal diplomatic ties with China, forging a strong relationship since China's [1962 conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Indian_War "Sino-Indian War") with India, culminating in a special bond. During the 1970s, Pakistan acted as an intermediary in U.S.-China rapprochement, facilitating US President [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon "Richard Nixon")'s [historic visit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Nixon_visit_to_China "1972 Nixon visit to China") to China. Despite changes in Pakistani governance and regional/global dynamics, China's influence in Pakistan remains paramount. In reciprocation, China stands as Pakistan's largest trading partner, with substantial investment in Pakistani infrastructure, notably the [Gwadar port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwadar_port "Gwadar port"). In 2015 alone, they inked 51 agreements and Memorandums of Understanding (MoUs) for cooperative efforts. Both nations signed a [Free Trade Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Pakistan_Free_Trade_Agreement "China–Pakistan Free Trade Agreement") in 2006, with China making its largest investment in Pakistan's history through [CPEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPEC "CPEC"). Pakistan acts as China's liaison to the Muslim world, and both nations support each other on sensitive issues like Kashmir, Taiwan, Xinjiang, and more.

#### Relations with the Muslim world

After Independence, Pakistan vigorously pursued bilateral relations with other Muslim countries. The [Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maulana_Muhammad_Ali_Jauhar "Maulana Muhammad Ali Jauhar") brothers sought to project Pakistan as the natural leader of the Islamic world, partly due to its significant manpower and military strength. [Khaliquzzaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaudhry_Khaliquzzaman "Chaudhry Khaliquzzaman"), a prominent [Muslim League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Muslim_League "All India Muslim League") leader, declared Pakistan's ambition to unite all Muslim countries into [Islamistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamistan "Islamistan"), a pan-Islamic entity.

These developments, alongside Pakistan's creation, didn't receive approval from the United States, with British Prime Minister [Clement Attlee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clement_Attlee "Clement Attlee") expressing a hope for India and Pakistan to reunite. However, due to a nationalist awakening in the Arab world at that time, there was little interest in Pakistan's Pan-Islamic aspirations. Some Arab countries perceived the 'Islamistan' project as Pakistan's bid to dominate other Muslim states.

Pakistan's founder, [Muhammad Ali Jinnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_Jinnah "Muhammad Ali Jinnah"), consistently advocated for the [Palestinian cause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_cause "Palestinian cause"), shaping Pakistan's foreign policy to support Palestinian rights within the broader framework of Muslim solidarity. During the [1967 Arab-Israel war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six-Day_War "Six-Day War"), Pakistan supported the Arab states and played a key role in securing Iran's backing for the Arab cause both within the U.N. and beyond.

Pakistan's relations with Iran have been strained by sectarian tensions, with both Iran and Saudi Arabia using Pakistan as a battleground for their proxy sectarian war. Since the early days of the [Iran–Iraq war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_war "Iran–Iraq war"), President [Zia-ul-Haq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zia-ul-Haq "Zia-ul-Haq") played an important mediatory role, with Pakistan actively engaging in efforts to end the conflict. Pakistan provided support to Saudi Arabia during the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War "Gulf War"). Pakistan chose to remain neutral during [Operation Decisive Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Decisive_Storm "Operation Decisive Storm"), refraining from sending military support to Saudi Arabia in its offensive against [Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemen "Yemen"). Instead, Pakistan aimed to play a proactive diplomatic role in resolving the crisis, which led to tensions between the two countries. In 2016, Pakistan mediated between Saudi Arabia and Iran following the execution of Shia cleric [Nimr al-Nimr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimr_al-Nimr "Nimr al-Nimr"), with visits to both countries by then Prime Minister [Nawaz Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawaz_Sharif "Nawaz Sharif") and the Chief of Army Staff, [Raheel Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raheel_Sharif "Raheel Sharif").

Pakistan provided refuge to millions of displaced Afghans after the [Soviet invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War "Soviet–Afghan War") and supported the [Afghan mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_mujahideen "Afghan mujahideen") in their efforts to expel Soviet forces from Afghanistan. After the Soviets withdrew, [infighting erupted among Mujahideen factions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Civil_War_(1989%E2%80%931992) "Afghan Civil War (1989–1992)") over control of Afghanistan. Pakistan facilitated [peace talks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshawar_Accord "Peshawar Accord") to help end the conflict. After four years of unresolved conflict between rival Mujahideen groups, Pakistan helped establish the Taliban as a stabilising force. Pakistan's support for the Sunni Taliban in Afghanistan challenged Shia-led Iran, which opposed a Taliban-controlled Afghanistan.

Pakistan vigorously advocated for self-determination among Muslims globally. Its efforts in supporting independence movements in countries like Indonesia, Algeria, Tunisia, Morocco, and [Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrea "Eritrea") fostered strong ties. Due to its support for [Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan "Azerbaijan") in the [Nagorno-Karabakh conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagorno-Karabakh_conflict "Nagorno-Karabakh conflict"), Pakistan has not established [diplomatic relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia%E2%80%93Pakistan_relations "Armenia–Pakistan relations") with [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia "Armenia").

Pakistan and Bangladesh have experienced strained relations, particularly under the [Awami League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awami_League "Awami League") governments led by [Sheikh Hasina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Hasina "Sheikh Hasina"), driven by her pro-India stance and historical grievances.

Pakistan, a prominent member of the [Organisation of Islamic Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_Islamic_Cooperation "Organisation of Islamic Cooperation") (OIC), prioritises maintaining cultural, political, social, and economic relations with Arab and other Muslim-majority nations in its foreign policy.

In September 2025, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia signed a [Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Mutual_Defence_Agreement "Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement") under which any aggression against one country is considered an aggression against both, formalising a defensive commitment that also extends Pakistan's nuclear umbrella to Saudi Arabia.

### Kashmir conflict

[Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir "Kashmir"), a Himalayan region at the northern tip of the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent"), was governed as the autonomous [princely state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_state "Princely state") of [Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_(princely_state) "Jammu and Kashmir (princely state)") during the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj") before the [Partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India "Partition of India") in August 1947. This sparked a [major territorial dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir_conflict "Kashmir conflict") between India and Pakistan, resulting in [several conflicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_wars_and_conflicts "Indo-Pakistani wars and conflicts") over the region. India controls about 45.1% of Kashmir, including [Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_(union_territory) "Jammu and Kashmir (union territory)") and [Ladakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladakh "Ladakh"), while Pakistan controls roughly 38.2%, comprising [Azad Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azad_Jammu_and_Kashmir "Azad Jammu and Kashmir") and [Gilgit−Baltistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilgit-Baltistan "Gilgit-Baltistan"). Additionally, about 20% of the region, known as [Aksai Chin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aksai_Chin "Aksai Chin") and the [Shaksgam Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Karakoram_Tract "Trans-Karakoram Tract"), is under Chinese control. India claims the entire Kashmir region based on the [Instrument of Accession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_of_Accession_(Jammu_and_Kashmir) "Instrument of Accession (Jammu and Kashmir)") signed by the princely state's ruler, Maharaja Hari Singh, while Pakistan argues for its Muslim-majority population, and geographical proximity to Pakistan. The United Nations was involved in resolving the conflict, leading to a ceasefire in 1949 and the establishment of the [Line of Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_of_Control "Line of Control") (LoC) as a *de facto* border. India, fearing Kashmir's secession, did not hold the promised plebiscite, as it believed Kashmiris would vote to join Pakistan.

Pakistan claims that its position is for the right of the [Kashmiri people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiri_people "Kashmiri people") to determine their future through impartial elections as mandated by the United Nations, while India has stated that Kashmir is an "[integral part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhand_Bharat "Akhand Bharat")" of India, referring to the [1972 Simla Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simla_Agreement "Simla Agreement") and to the fact that [regional elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Jammu_and_Kashmir "Elections in Jammu and Kashmir") take place regularly. Certain Kashmiri independence groups believe that Kashmir should be independent of both India and Pakistan.

### Military

Pakistan is considered a [middle power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_power "Middle power") nation, with the world's [seventh-largest standing armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_number_of_military_and_paramilitary_personnel "List of countries by number of military and paramilitary personnel") in terms of personnel size, comprising approximately 660,000 active-duty troops and 291,000 paramilitary personnel as of 2024. [Established in 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_Pakistan "Military history of Pakistan"), the [armed forces of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_forces_of_Pakistan "Armed forces of Pakistan") wielded significant influence over [national politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Pakistan "Politics of Pakistan"). The main branches include the [Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Army "Pakistan Army"), [Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Navy "Pakistan Navy"), and [Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Air_Force "Pakistan Air Force"), supported by numerous [paramilitaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Armed_Forces "Civil Armed Forces").

The [Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_of_the_Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff_Committee "Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff Committee") (CJCSC) is the highest-ranking military officer, advising the civilian government. However, they lack direct command over the branches and serve as intermediaries, ensuring communication between the military and civilian leadership. Overseeing the [Joint Staff Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Staff_Headquarters_(Pakistan) "Joint Staff Headquarters (Pakistan)"), they coordinate inter-service cooperation and joint military missions.

[Command and control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_command_and_control "Nuclear command and control") over Pakistan's [strategic arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan%27s_nuclear_testing_series "Pakistan's nuclear testing series") development and employment is vested in the [National Command Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Command_Authority_(Pakistan) "National Command Authority (Pakistan)"), overseeing work on [nuclear doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_Doctrine_of_Pakistan "Nuclear Doctrine of Pakistan") to maintain [Full spectrum deterrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_spectrum_deterrence "Full spectrum deterrence").

The United States, Turkey, and China maintain close military relations with [Pakistan Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Armed_Forces "Pakistan Armed Forces"), regularly exporting military equipment and [technology transfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology_transfer "Technology transfer"). Pakistan was the 5th-largest recipient and importer of arms between 2019 and 2023.

#### Military history

Since 1947, Pakistan has been involved in [four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_wars_and_conflicts "Indo-Pakistani wars and conflicts") [conventional wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conventional_warfare "Conventional warfare") with India. The [first conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pak_war_of_1947 "Indo-Pak war of 1947") took place in Kashmir and ended in a United Nations-mediated ceasefire, with Pakistan gaining control of one-third of the region. Territorial disputes led to [another war in 1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1965 "Indo-Pakistani War of 1965"). In 1971, India and Pakistan fought [another war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1971 "Indo-Pakistani War of 1971") over [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan "East Pakistan"), with Indian forces aiding its independence, leading to the creation of [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh"). Tensions in Kargil brought the two countries to the [brink of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1999 "Indo-Pakistani War of 1999").

Pakistan's primary intelligence agency, the Inter-Services Intelligence (ISI), was established within a year of Pakistan's independence in 1947. During the [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War "Soviet–Afghan War"), Pakistan's intelligence community, mostly the [ISI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-Services_Intelligence "Inter-Services Intelligence"), [coordinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Cyclone "Operation Cyclone") [US resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_involvement_in_regime_change "United States involvement in regime change") to support [Afghan mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_mujahideen "Afghan mujahideen") and [foreign fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Arabs "Afghan Arabs") against Soviet presence. The [PAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Air_Force "Pakistan Air Force") engaged with [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Air_Forces "Soviet Air Forces") and [Afghan Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Air_Force "Afghan Air Force") during the [conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_war_in_afghanistan "Russian war in afghanistan"). Pakistan has been an active [participant in UN peacekeeping missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_peacekeeping_missions_involving_Pakistan "United Nations peacekeeping missions involving Pakistan"), playing a major role in operations like the rescue mission in [Mogadishu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogadishu "Mogadishu"), Somalia, in 1993. According to a 2023 UN report, the Pakistani military was the fifth largest troop contributor to UN peacekeeping missions.

Pakistan has [deployed its military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Armed_Forces_deployments "Pakistan Armed Forces deployments") in some Arab countries, providing defense, training, and advisory roles. The [PAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Air_Force "Pakistan Air Force")'s fighter pilots participated in missions against Israel during the [Six-Day War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six-Day_War "Six-Day War") and the [Yom Kippur War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur_War "Yom Kippur War"). Pakistani [special forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Service_Group "Special Service Group") assisted [Saudi forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_forces "Saudi forces") in [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca "Mecca") during the [Grand Mosque Seizure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Mosque_Seizure "Grand Mosque Seizure"). Pakistan also sent 5,000 troops as part of a US-led coalition for the defense of Saudi Arabia during the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War "Gulf War").

Despite the UN arms embargo on [Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia "Bosnia"), the ISI under General [Javed Nasir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javed_Nasir "Javed Nasir") airlifted anti-tank weapons and missiles to Bosnian mujahideen, shifting the tide in favour of Bosnian Muslims. ISI, under Nasir's leadership, supported Chinese Muslims in [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang "Xinjiang"), rebel groups in the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines"), and religious groups in Central Asia.

Since 2001, the Pakistan military has been engaged in [counterinsurgency and internal security operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_North-West_Pakistan "War in North-West Pakistan") in the [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khyber_Pakhtunkhwa "Khyber Pakhtunkhwa") province, primarily targeting [Tehrik-e-Taliban Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehrik-e-Taliban_Pakistan "Tehrik-e-Taliban Pakistan") and associated militant groups. Major military operations conducted during this period include [Operation Enduring Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom "Operation Enduring Freedom"), [Operation al-Mizan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_al-Mizan "Operation al-Mizan"), [Operation Zalzala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Zalzala "Operation Zalzala"), [Operation Sherdil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Sherdil "Operation Sherdil"), [Operation Rah-e-Haq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Rah-e-Haq "Operation Rah-e-Haq"), [Operation Rah-e-Rast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Rah-e-Rast "Operation Rah-e-Rast"), and [Operation Rah-e-Nijat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Rah-e-Nijat "Operation Rah-e-Nijat").

### Law enforcement

[Law enforcement in Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_in_Pakistan "Law enforcement in Pakistan") consists of federal and provincial police agencies. Each of the [four provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_units_of_Pakistan "Administrative units of Pakistan") ([Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab,_Pakistan "Punjab, Pakistan"), [Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindh "Sindh"), [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khyber_Pakhtunkhwa "Khyber Pakhtunkhwa"), and [Balochistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochistan,_Pakistan "Balochistan, Pakistan")) has its own police force, while the [Islamabad Capital Territory (ICT)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad_Capital_Territory "Islamabad Capital Territory") has the Islamabad Police. Provincial police forces are led by an Inspector-General of Police (IGP), who is appointed from the federally recruited and trained [Police Service of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_Service_of_Pakistan "Police Service of Pakistan") (PSP) through a consultative process between the federal and provincial governments. All positions above the Assistant Superintendent level are filled from the PSP, ensuring national standards across provincial forces.

**Specialised Units:**

* [National Highways & Motorway Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highways_%26_Motorway_Police "National Highways & Motorway Police") (NHMP): Enforces traffic laws and ensures safety on Pakistan's inter-provincial motorway network.
* Specialised Rapid Response Units: Specialised counter-terrorism units, such as the [Punjab Elite Police Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elite_Police "Elite Police"), have been trained by army commandos and exist in every province to respond to hostage situations and neutralise armed groups.

The [Civil Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Armed_Forces "Civil Armed Forces") (CAF) assist local law enforcement agencies and participate in border security and internal security operations, particularly in conflict-affected regions.

In 2021, the [National Intelligence Coordination Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Intelligence_Coordination_Committee_(Pakistan) "National Intelligence Coordination Committee (Pakistan)") was established to improve coordination among Pakistan's intelligence agencies. The inaugural meeting was attended by the heads of the [ISI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-Services_Intelligence "Inter-Services Intelligence"), [IB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_Bureau_(Pakistan) "Intelligence Bureau (Pakistan)"), and [FIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Investigation_Agency "Federal Investigation Agency").

### Crime

According to the 2025 [Global Organized Crime Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Organized_Crime_Index "Global Organized Crime Index"), Pakistan had a criminality score of 6.32 out of 10, ranking 45th among 193 countries, and a resilience score ranking 131st among 193 countries, with the latter measuring the country's capacity to withstand and respond to organized crime.

According to the [Federal Investigation Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Investigation_Agency "Federal Investigation Agency")'s annual report, the agency received more than 73,000 cybercrime complaints in 2024, of which 1,604 were registered as cases, with financial fraud accounting for nearly half of all complaints. In 2025, the [National Cyber Crimes Investigation Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Cyber_Crimes_Investigation_Agency "National Cyber Crimes Investigation Agency") estimated that cybercrime had increased by about 35% compared with the previous year. A 2025 survey conducted on behalf of [Visa Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_Inc. "Visa Inc.") found that 55% of respondents in Pakistan had fallen victim to an [online scam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_scam "Online scam") in 2024, while 20% reported having been victims more than once.

Pakistan scored 27 out of 100 on the [Corruption Perceptions Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_Perceptions_Index "Corruption Perceptions Index") in 2024, ranking 135th out of 180 countries in the assessment of perceived public-sector corruption.

### Human rights

In Pakistan, all sexual activity outside of marriage is illegal. The punishment for sex outside marriage (*[zina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zina "Zina")*) ranges from up to five years' imprisonment for minors to 100 lashes for unmarried adults and [stoning to death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoning_to_death "Stoning to death") for married adults, depending on marital status, age, sanity, and whether strict evidentiary requirements for a *[hadd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadd "Hadd")* punishment—such as four adult male Muslim witnesses or a confession—are met; however, no one has been stoned to death under the law to date. Male [homosexuality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosexuality "Homosexuality") is illegal in Pakistan and is punishable by up to ten years in prison, [corporal punishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporal_punishment "Corporal punishment") such as [whipping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whipping "Whipping"), and, under [Islamic law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_law "Islamic law") since 1990, potentially even stoning.

[Honour killings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honour_killings "Honour killings"), locally referred to as *[karo-kari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karo-kari "Karo-kari")* in parts of Pakistan, involve the killing of women and, less frequently, men by family or community members over alleged extramarital relationships or perceived violations of honour. Human rights organisations report that hundreds of women are killed each year, with many cases unreported. Pakistan also has a high number of reported [acid attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid_attacks "Acid attacks"), with about 200 incidents annually as of 2023, most victims being women. According to [Masarrat Misbah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masarrat_Misbah "Masarrat Misbah"), founder of the [Depilex Smileagain Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depilex_Smileagain_Foundation "Depilex Smileagain Foundation"), women have been targeted for rejecting marriage proposals, giving birth to daughters instead of sons, or insufficient dowry. The 2011 Acid and Burn Crime Bill criminalised acid attacks, making them punishable by lengthy imprisonment and fines.

In parts of Pakistan, girls are given in marriage to settle disputes or crimes between families in a customary practice called *[Vani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vani_(custom) "Vani (custom)")*. Girls married through this practice are often treated as compensation for a dispute, which places them in a vulnerable social position within the family they marry into. In 2021, the [Federal Shariat Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Shariat_Court "Federal Shariat Court") declared the practice un-Islamic and unconstitutional, reaffirming earlier Supreme Court jurisprudence.

[UN Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Women "UN Women") reported in 2024 that around one in six girls in Pakistan are married during childhood, with the country home to nearly 19 million child brides. In 2025, Pakistan enacted a law setting the minimum legal age for marriage at 18 and introducing penalties for arranging underage marriages; the law is limited to the [Islamabad Capital Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad_Capital_Territory "Islamabad Capital Territory") and does not apply nationwide.

Pakistan's economy [ranks 27th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(PPP) "List of countries by GDP (PPP)") globally by [purchasing power parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity "Purchasing power parity") (PPP) and [42nd by nominal GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(nominal) "List of countries by GDP (nominal)"). Historically, Pakistan was part of the wealthiest [region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent") in the first millennium CE, but lost ground to regions like China and Western Europe by the 18th century. Pakistan is a [developing country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_country "Developing country"), and part of the [Next Eleven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Eleven "Next Eleven"), poised to become one of the world's largest economies in the 21st century, alongside the [BRIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRIC_(economics_term) "BRIC (economics term)") countries. The semi-industrialised economy is heavily dependent on agriculture, and industrial growth in Pakistan benefits significantly from agricultural expansion. Pakistan's economy has shifted from [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Pakistan "Agriculture in Pakistan") to trade and services, with agriculture contributing about one-fifth of GDP and manufacturing one-sixth, while trade and services form the largest share. In 2023, Pakistan was the 66th-largest export economy, with a trade deficit of US$21.3 billion.

As of 2025[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pakistan&action=edit), Pakistan's [nominal GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nominal_GDP "Nominal GDP") stood at US$407.79 billion, while GDP ([PPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity "Purchasing power parity")) was US$1.8 trillion in 2026. The nominal per capita GDP was US$1,700, and the [GDP (PPP)/capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP_(PPP)_per_capita "GDP (PPP) per capita") amounted to US$6,950 ([international dollars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_dollar "International dollar")). Around 44.7% of the population live below the international poverty line of US$4.20 a day, 16.5% live in extreme poverty on less than US$3 a day, 8% of the total labour force is unemployed, and [CPI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPI "CPI") inflation in 2025 was 4.5%. Pakistan ranks near the bottom globally on the [Global Gender Gap Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Gender_Gap_Index "Global Gender Gap Index")'s economic participation and opportunity sub-index for women, reflecting very low female labour force participation and limited representation of women in senior economic roles. Pakistan's middle class is estimated to be around 40–50 million people, representing 15–20 per cent of the population. Among [South Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia") countries, Pakistan's GDP growth rate of 2.5% in 2024 was the second lowest after Afghanistan.

Pakistan has strong potential to produce diverse food commodities due to its vast natural resource base across varied ecological and climatic zones. It has the world's 7th-largest [labour force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_in_Pakistan "Labour in Pakistan"), and its [diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_diaspora "Pakistani diaspora") of around 10 million, sent US$38.3 billion in remittances in fiscal year 2024–25, as reported by the [Special Investment Facilitation Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Investment_Facilitation_Council "Special Investment Facilitation Council"). Pakistan's share of global exports averaged 0.14% between 1990 and 2023 and was 0.12% in 2023.

### Services

As of 2025–26, the services sector was the largest contributor to Pakistan's GDP, accounting for 58.42% of GDP. The sector accounted for 41.6% of total employment in 2024–25. In the [International Telecommunication Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Telecommunication_Union "International Telecommunication Union")'s [ICT Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICT_Development_Index "ICT Development Index"), Pakistan's score increased from 56.4 in 2025 to 67.7 in 2026, an increase of 20% from 2025. Approximately 57% of Pakistan's population used the Internet in 2024. In fiscal year 2023–24, Pakistan's ICT exports rose to a record $2.6 billion. Pakistan ranks among the world's top five freelancing markets, with an estimated three million freelancers. During the first ten months of fiscal year 2025–26, freelancers earned more than US$950 million in foreign exchange.

### Agriculture

The agriculture sector employs 43.5% of the labour force and is a major source of foreign exchange. Pakistan produced 28.98 million metric tons of wheat in 2025/2026 (3% of global output), ranking 7th globally. Pakistan ranks fourth in cotton production, fifth in sugar production, and is the fourth-largest milk producer globally. Though land and water resources haven't increased proportionately, productivity gains, especially from the [Green Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Revolution "Green Revolution") in the late 1960s and 1970s, significantly boosted wheat and rice yields. Private tube wells and High Yielding Varieties (HYVs) further augmented crop yields. Meat industry accounts for 1.4 percent of overall GDP.

### Industry

Industry, constituting 19.74% of GDP and 24% of total employment, is the second-largest sector. Large-scale manufacturing (LSM) dominates, representing 12.2% of GDP, with cement production thriving due to demand from Afghanistan and the domestic real estate sector. In 2013, Pakistan exported 7,708,557 metric tons of cement, with an installed capacity of 44,768,250 metric tons. The [textile industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile_industry_in_Pakistan "Textile industry in Pakistan"), a key player in Pakistan's manufacturing, contributes 9.5% to GDP and employs around 15 million people. As of 2022, Pakistan ranks seventh globally in cotton production, with substantial spinning capacity, making it a major exporter of textile products in Asia. China has been a significant buyer of Pakistani textiles, importing US$1.527 billion worth of textiles in 2012.

### Tourism

Pakistan drew around 6.6 million foreign tourists in 2018. Pakistan boasts attractions from [mangroves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River_Delta-Arabian_Sea_mangroves "Indus River Delta-Arabian Sea mangroves") in the south to Himalayan [hill stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_hill_stations_of_Pakistan "List of hill stations of Pakistan") in the northeast, including ancient Buddhist ruins of [Takht-i-Bahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takht-i-Bahi "Takht-i-Bahi") and [Taxila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxila "Taxila"), the 5,000-year-old [Indus Valley civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_civilisation "Indus Valley civilisation") sites such as [Mohenjo-daro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohenjo-daro "Mohenjo-daro") and [Harappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harappa "Harappa"), and numerous [mountain peaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mountains_in_Pakistan "List of mountains in Pakistan") over 7,000 metres (23,000 feet). The northern part of Pakistan boasts numerous old fortresses, showcasing ancient architecture. It encompasses the [Hunza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunza_Valley "Hunza Valley") and [Chitral valleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitral_valley "Chitral valley"), where the small pre-Islamic [Kalasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalash_people "Kalash people") community resides, claiming descent from Alexander the Great. [Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore "Lahore"), Pakistan's cultural capital, contains lots of [Mughal architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_architecture "Mughal architecture"), including the [Badshahi Masjid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badshahi_Masjid "Badshahi Masjid"), the [Shalimar Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalimar_Gardens_(Lahore) "Shalimar Gardens (Lahore)"), the [Tomb of Jahangir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Jahangir "Tomb of Jahangir"), and the [Lahore Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Fort "Lahore Fort"). Following the [2005 Kashmir earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Kashmir_earthquake "2005 Kashmir earthquake"), *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* highlighted "The top five tourist sites in Pakistan" to boost tourism, featuring destinations like Taxila, Lahore, the [Karakoram Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakoram_Highway "Karakoram Highway"), [Karimabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karimabad_(Hunza) "Karimabad (Hunza)"), and [Lake Saiful Muluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Saiful_Muluk "Lake Saiful Muluk"). Festivals and government initiatives aim to promote Pakistan's cultural heritage. In 2015, the [World Economic Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Economic_Forum "World Economic Forum") ranked Pakistan 125th out of 141 countries in its Travel & Tourism Competitiveness Report.

### Natural resources

Pakistan's untapped coal reserves have been estimated as the fifth largest in the world and equivalent to about 400 billion barrels of oil, exceeding the combined oil reserves of Saudi Arabia and Iran. [Daniel Fitzgerald Runde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Fitzgerald_Runde "Daniel Fitzgerald Runde") wrote in *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes "Forbes")* in 2016 that Pakistan "could be the 'Saudi Arabia of Coal'."

The [Reko Diq Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reko_Diq_Mine "Reko Diq Mine") has one of the world's largest undeveloped [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper "Copper") deposits, with an estimated 15 million tonnes in reserves, and is expected to become the world's fifth largest copper mine upon completion; a feasibility study also estimates that it could produce approximately 17.9 million ounces of gold over its projected 37-year mine life, with total copper and gold reserves valued at over $60 billion at prevailing prices.

## Infrastructure

Pakistan was lauded as the top nation for infrastructure development in South Asia during the 2016 annual meetings of the IMF and World Bank.

### Power and energy

As of May 2021, Pakistan operates six licensed commercial [nuclear power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_plants "Nuclear power plants"). The [Pakistan Atomic Energy Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Atomic_Energy_Commission "Pakistan Atomic Energy Commission") (PAEC) oversees these plants, while the [Pakistan Nuclear Regulatory Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Nuclear_Regulatory_Authority "Pakistan Nuclear Regulatory Authority") ensures their safe operation. These plants contribute approximately 5.8% to Pakistan's electricity supply, while fossil fuels (crude oil and natural gas) provide 64.2%, [hydroelectric power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric_power "Hydroelectric power") provides 29.9%, and [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_power_plant "Coal power plant") contributes 0.1%. The [KANUPP-I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_Nuclear_Power_Plant "Karachi Nuclear Power Plant"), Pakistan's first commercial nuclear power plant, was supplied by Canada in 1971. Sino-Pakistani nuclear cooperation began in the 1980s, leading to the establishment of CHASNUPP-I. In 2005, both countries proposed a joint energy security plan, aiming for a generation capacity exceeding 160,000 [MWe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megawatt "Megawatt") by 2030. Pakistan's Nuclear Energy Vision 2050 targets a capacity of 40,000 MWe, with 8,900 MWe expected by 2030.

In June 2008, the nuclear complex at [Chashma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chashma_Nuclear_Power_Plant "Chashma Nuclear Power Plant") in Punjab Province expanded with the installation of Chashma-III and Chashma–IV reactors, each with 325–340 MWe, costing [₨](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_rupee "Pakistani rupee")129 billion, with ₨80 billion from international sources, mainly China. Another agreement for China's assistance was signed in October 2008, seen as a response to the [US–India agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India%E2%80%93United_States_Civil_Nuclear_Agreement "India–United States Civil Nuclear Agreement"). The project's cost was then US$1.7 billion, with a foreign loan of US$1.07 billion. In 2013, Pakistan established a second [nuclear complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_Nuclear_Power_Complex "Karachi Nuclear Power Complex") in Karachi with plans for additional reactors, similar to Chashma. [Electrical energy in Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_in_Pakistan "Electricity in Pakistan") is generated by [various corporations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_distribution_companies_of_Pakistan "Electricity distribution companies of Pakistan") and distributed evenly among the [four provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Provinces_(Pakistan) "Four Provinces (Pakistan)") by the [National Electric Power Regulatory Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Electric_Power_Regulatory_Authority "National Electric Power Regulatory Authority") (NEPRA). However, Karachi-based [K-Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-Electric "K-Electric") and [Water and Power Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_and_Power_Development_Authority "Water and Power Development Authority") (WAPDA) generate much of the electricity used in Pakistan and collect revenue nationwide. In 2023, Pakistan's installed [electricity generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_generation "Electricity generation") capacity was ~45,885 MWt. Pakistan produced 1,135 megawatts of [renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy "Renewable energy") for the month of October 2016. Pakistan expects to produce 10,000 megawatts of renewable energy by 2025.

### Transport

Pakistan boasts 2567 km of motorways and approximately 263,942 km of highways, which handle 92% of passengers and 96% of freight traffic. Despite constituting only 4.6% of the total road length, these north–south links manage 85% of the nation's traffic. They connect southern seaports such as [Karachi port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_port "Karachi port") and [Port Qasim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Qasim "Port Qasim") in Sindh, along with [Gwadar Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwadar_Port "Gwadar Port") and [Port of Pasni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Pasni "Port of Pasni") in Balochistan, to populous provinces like Punjab and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa domestically, and neighbouring countries like Afghanistan, [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia"), and China through the [China Pakistan Economic Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Pakistan_Economic_Corridor "China Pakistan Economic Corridor"). According to the [WEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WEF "WEF")'s Global Competitiveness Report, Pakistan's port infrastructure quality ratings rose from 3.7 to 4.1 between 2007 and 2016. The railway's share of inland traffic is reduced to below 8% for passengers and 4% for freight. This shift led to a decrease in total rail track from 8,775 kilometres (5,453 miles) in 1990–91 to 7,791 kilometres (4,841 miles) in 2011.

The transport landscape of Pakistan features various [modern transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit "Rapid transit") systems. The [Orange Line Metro Train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Line_(Lahore_Metro) "Orange Line (Lahore Metro)") in Lahore, inaugurated in 2020, spans 27.1 km (16.8 mi), and includes both elevated and underground sections, accommodating over 250,000 passengers daily. Lahore also boasts the [Lahore Metrobus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Metrobus "Lahore Metrobus"), the first of its kind in Pakistan, operational since February 2013. The [Rawalpindi-Islamabad Metrobus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi-Islamabad_Metrobus "Rawalpindi-Islamabad Metrobus"), stretching 48.1 km, commenced its first phase in June 2015, with subsequent extensions, and employs e-ticketing and an Intelligent Transportation System. [Multan Metrobus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multan_Metrobus "Multan Metrobus"), inaugurated in January 2017, serves [Multan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multan "Multan") with its rapid transit services. [Peshawar's Bus Rapid Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshawar_Metrobus "Peshawar Metrobus"), inaugurated in August 2020, marks the fourth BRT system in Pakistan. Karachi's [Green Line Metrobus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Line_-_Karachi_Metrobus "Green Line - Karachi Metrobus"), operational since December 2021, is part of a larger metrobus project financed by the Government of Pakistan and initiated in February 2016. Meanwhile, [Faisalabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisalabad "Faisalabad") awaits its proposed rapid transit project, the [Faisalabad Metrobus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisalabad_Metrobus "Faisalabad Metrobus"). [Karachi Circular Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_Circular_Railway "Karachi Circular Railway"), partially revived in November 2020, offers public transit services in the Karachi metropolitan area. Additionally, plans are underway to resurrect Karachi's [tramway service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamedali_Tramways_Company "Mohamedali Tramways Company"), which ceased operations in 1975, in collaboration with Austrian experts.

As of 2013, Pakistan boasts approximately 151 airports and airfields, encompassing both military and [civilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Civil_Aviation_Authority "Pakistan Civil Aviation Authority") installations. Despite [Jinnah International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinnah_International_Airport "Jinnah International Airport") serving as the primary international gateway, significant international traffic also flows through [Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allama_Iqbal_International_Airport "Allama Iqbal International Airport"), [Islamabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad_International_Airport "Islamabad International Airport"), [Peshawar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshawar_International_Airport "Peshawar International Airport"), [Quetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quetta_International_Airport "Quetta International Airport"), [Faisalabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisalabad_International_Airport "Faisalabad International Airport"), [Sialkot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sialkot_International_Airport "Sialkot International Airport"), and [Multan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multan_International_Airport "Multan International Airport") airports. The civil aviation industry, deregulated in 1993, operates with a blend of [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalisation_in_Pakistan "Nationalisation in Pakistan") and [private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatisation_in_Pakistan "Privatisation in Pakistan") entities while [state-owned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_owned_enterprises "State owned enterprises") [Pakistan International Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_International_Airlines "Pakistan International Airlines") (PIA) dominates, carrying 73% of domestic passengers and all domestic freight.

### Science and technology

Developments in [science and technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_technology_in_Pakistan "Science and technology in Pakistan") have played a significant role in Pakistan's infrastructure, linking the nation to the global community. Each year, the [Pakistan Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Academy_of_Sciences "Pakistan Academy of Sciences") and the government invite scientists worldwide to the [International Nathiagali Summer College on Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Nathiagali_Summer_College_on_Physics "International Nathiagali Summer College on Physics"). In 2005, Pakistan hosted an international seminar on "Physics in Developing Countries" for the International Year of Physics. Pakistani theoretical physicist [Abdus Salam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdus_Salam "Abdus Salam") won a [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for his work on the [electroweak interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electroweak_interaction "Electroweak interaction"). Pakistani scientists have made notable contributions in mathematics, biology, economics, [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science "Computer science"), and [genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics "Genetics").

In [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry"), [Salimuzzaman Siddiqui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salimuzzaman_Siddiqui "Salimuzzaman Siddiqui") identified the medicinal properties of the [neem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neem "Neem") tree's components. [Ayub K. Ommaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayub_K._Ommaya "Ayub K. Ommaya") developed the [Ommaya reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ommaya_reservoir "Ommaya reservoir") for treating brain conditions. Scientific research is integral to [Pakistani universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_universities "Pakistani universities"), national laboratories, [science parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_park "Science park"), and the [industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industry_of_Pakistan "Industry of Pakistan"). [Abdul Qadeer Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Qadeer_Khan "Abdul Qadeer Khan") spearheaded Pakistan's [HEU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enriched_uranium "Enriched uranium")-based [gas-centrifuge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zippe-type_centrifuge "Zippe-type centrifuge") [uranium enrichment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium_enrichment "Uranium enrichment") program for its [atomic bomb project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_and_weapons_of_mass_destruction "Pakistan and weapons of mass destruction"). He established the [Kahuta Research Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahuta_Research_Laboratories "Kahuta Research Laboratories") (KRL) in 1976, serving as both its senior scientist and the Director-General until his retirement in 2001. Besides atomic bomb project, he made significant contributions in [molecular morphology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphology_(biology) "Morphology (biology)"), physical [martensite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martensite "Martensite"), and their applications in [condensed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter_physics "Condensed matter physics") and [material physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Material_physics "Material physics").

In 2023, Pakistan ranked 26th globally in published scientific papers. The influential [Pakistan Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Academy_of_Sciences "Pakistan Academy of Sciences") guides the government on science policies. Pakistan was ranked 99th in the [Global Innovation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Innovation_Index "Global Innovation Index") by 2025.

The 1960s marked the rise of Pakistan's space program, led by [SUPARCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SUPARCO "SUPARCO"), yielding advancements in [rocketry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket "Rocket"), [electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics_Engineering "Electronics Engineering"), and [aeronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeronomy "Aeronomy"). Notably, Pakistan launched its [first rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehbar-I "Rehbar-I") into space, pioneering South Asia's space exploration. In 1990, it successfully launched its [first satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badr-1 "Badr-1"), becoming the first Muslim nation and second in South Asia to achieve this milestone.

> Pakistan witnessed a fourfold increase in its scientific productivity in the past decade surging from approximately 2,000 articles per year in 2006 to more than 9,000 articles in 2015. Making Pakistan's cited article's higher than the [BRIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRIC_(economics_term) "BRIC (economics term)") countries put together.

— [Thomson Reuters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomson_Reuters "Thomson Reuters")'s Another BRIC in the Wall 2016 report

Following the [1971 war with India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1971 "Indo-Pakistani War of 1971"), Pakistan hastily developed [atomic weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_weapons "Atomic weapons") to deter foreign intervention and entered the [atomic age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_age "Atomic age"). Tensions with India led to Pakistan's [1998 underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_nuclear_weapons_tests_of_Pakistan "List of nuclear weapons tests of Pakistan") [nuclear tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagai-I "Chagai-I"), making it the [seventh country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_club "Nuclear club") to possess such weapons.

Pakistan is the sole Muslim nation active in Antarctica research, maintaining its [Jinnah Antarctic Research Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Antarctic_Programme "Pakistan Antarctic Programme") since 1992. The government invests heavily in [information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology_in_Pakistan "Information technology in Pakistan") projects, focusing on e-government and infrastructure.

## Demographics

Pakistan had a population of 247,541,947 according to the final results of the [2023 Pakistani census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Pakistani_census "2023 Pakistani census"). Pakistan is the world's [fifth–most populous country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population "List of countries and dependencies by population") and has the [second-largest Muslim population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_by_country#Countries "Islam by country") as of 2023. Between 1951 and 2023, Pakistan's population expanded over sevenfold, going from 33.7 million to 247.5 million. The country has a relatively high, although declining, growth rate supported by high birth rates and low death rates. Between 1998 and 2017, the average annual population growth rate stood at +2.40%.

Dramatic social changes have led to [urbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanisation_in_Pakistan "Urbanisation in Pakistan") and the emergence of two [megacities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megacity "Megacity"): [Karachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi "Karachi") and [Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore "Lahore"). The country's urban population more than tripled between 1981 and 2017 (from 23.8 million to 75.7 million), as Pakistan's [urbanisation rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization "Urbanization") rose from 28.2% to 36.4%. Even with this, the nation's urbanisation rate remains one of [the lowest in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization_by_sovereign_state "Urbanization by sovereign state"), and in 2017, over 130 million Pakistanis (making up nearly 65% of the population) lived in rural areas.

Due to a high [fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate "Total fertility rate"), which was estimated at 3.5 in 2022, Pakistan has one of the world's [youngest populations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_median_age "List of countries by median age"). The 2017 census recorded that 40.3% of the country's population was under the age of 15, while only 3.7% of Pakistanis were aged 65 or more. The [median age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_age "Median age") of the country was 19, while its [sex ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_ratio "Sex ratio") was recorded to be 105 males per 100 females. Pakistan ranked last (148th of 148 countries) on the [Global Gender Gap Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Gender_Gap_Report "Global Gender Gap Report"), with a gender parity score of 56.7%, reflecting one of the lowest levels of gender equality globally.

The [demographic history of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_history_of_Pakistan "Demographic history of Pakistan") from the ancient [Indus Valley civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation "Indus Valley Civilisation") to the modern era includes the arrival and settlement of many cultures and ethnic groups in the modern region of Pakistan from [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia "Eurasia") and the nearby [Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East "Middle East"). Because of this, Pakistan has a multicultural, multilinguistic, and multiethnic society. Pakistan is also thought to have the world's fourth-largest [refugee population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states_by_refugee_population "List of sovereign states by refugee population"), estimated at 1.4 million in mid-2021 by the [UNHCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_High_Commissioner_for_Refugees "United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees").

### Urbanisation

Since [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Pakistan "Independence of Pakistan") due to the [partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India "Partition of India"), [urbanisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanisation_in_Pakistan "Urbanisation in Pakistan") has surged for various reasons. In the south, [Karachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi "Karachi") stands as the most populous commercial hub along the [Indus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River "Indus River"). In the east, west, and north, a dense population arc spans cities like [Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore "Lahore"), [Faisalabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisalabad "Faisalabad"), [Rawalpindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi "Rawalpindi"), [Islamabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad "Islamabad"), [Sargodha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sargodha "Sargodha"), [Gujranwala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujranwala "Gujranwala"), [Sialkot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sialkot "Sialkot"), [Gujrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujrat_city "Gujrat city"), [Jhelum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhelum "Jhelum"), [Sheikhupura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikhupura "Sheikhupura"), [Nowshera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowshera,_Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa "Nowshera, Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa"), [Mardan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mardan "Mardan"), and [Peshawar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshawar "Peshawar"). By 1990–2008, city dwellers constituted 36% of Pakistan's population, making it South Asia's most urbanised nation, with over 50% living in towns of 5,000+ inhabitants. [Immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Pakistan "Immigration to Pakistan"), both domestic and international, significantly fuels urban growth. Migration from India, especially to Karachi, the largest metropolis, and from nearby countries, accelerates urbanisation, posing new political and socio-economic challenges. Economic shifts like the green revolution and political developments also play crucial roles.

### Ethnicity and languages

Pakistan is a diverse society with estimates suggesting it has between 75 and 85 languages. Urdu and English serve as the official languages, with Urdu being the country's [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca "Lingua franca") and a unifying force among over 75% of Pakistanis. According to the [2023 national census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Pakistani_census "2023 Pakistani census"), the largest [ethnolinguistic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnolinguistic_group "Ethnolinguistic group") include the [Punjabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabis "Punjabis") (36.98%), [Pashtuns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns "Pashtuns") (18.15%), [Sindhis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhis "Sindhis") (14.31%), [Saraikis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraikis "Saraikis") (12%), [Urdu speaking people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu_speaking_people "Urdu speaking people") (9.25%), [Balochs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochs "Balochs") (3.38%), [Hindkowans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindkowans "Hindkowans")/[Hazarewals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazarewal "Hazarewal") (2.32%), and [Brahuis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahuis "Brahuis") (1.16%). The remaining population consists of various ethnic minorities such as [Kashmiris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiris "Kashmiris"), [Paharis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahari_people_(Kashmir) "Pahari people (Kashmir)"), [Chitralis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitralis "Chitralis"), various peoples of [Gilgit-Baltistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilgit-Baltistan "Gilgit-Baltistan"), [Kohistanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohistanis "Kohistanis"), [Torwalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torwalis "Torwalis"), [Meos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meo_(ethnic_group) "Meo (ethnic group)"), [Hazaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazaras "Hazaras"), [Kalash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalash_people "Kalash people") and [Siddis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siddi "Siddi"). The Pakistani diaspora, numbering over seven million, is the sixth largest in the world.

### Immigration

Even after the 1947 partition, the Muslims from India kept migrating to Pakistan, especially Karachi and Sindh province. Wars in neighbouring Afghanistan in the 1980s and 1990s pushed millions of [Afghan refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_refugees "Afghan refugees") into Pakistan, mainly in Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa and [tribal areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federally_Administered_Tribal_Areas "Federally Administered Tribal Areas"), with some in Karachi and Quetta. Pakistan hosts one of the world's largest refugee populations. Additionally, around 2 million [Bengalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengalis_in_Pakistan "Bengalis in Pakistan") and half a million undocumented individuals, purportedly from [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar"), reside in Pakistan. Pakistan ordered the [mass expulsion of undocumented Afghans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportation_of_undocumented_Afghans_from_Pakistan "Deportation of undocumented Afghans from Pakistan") in October 2023, citing national security risks.

Migration of Bengalis and [Rohingya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya "Rohingya") to Pakistan started in the 1980s and continued till 1998. Karachi hosts a significant number of Bengali settlements, and large Rohingya migration made it one of their largest populations outside Myanmar. Karachi's [Burmese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_people "Burmese people") community resides in various slums across the city.

According to [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_World_Service "BBC World Service"), thousands of [Uyghur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uyghurs "Uyghurs") Muslims live in Gilgit-Baltistan, some left Xinjiang, China and the thriving trading town of Kashgar in 1949, while others are later arrivals, claiming to escape political oppression. Since 1989, thousands of [Kashmiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiris "Kashmiris") Muslim refugees fled to Pakistan, alleging rape and forced displacement by Indian soldiers.

### Diaspora

According to the UN Department of Economic and Social Affairs, Pakistan has the sixth-largest diaspora globally. Approximately 7 million Pakistanis reside abroad, mainly in the Middle East, Europe, and North America. Pakistan ranks 10th globally for remittances sent home. Saudi Arabia is the largest source of remittances, contributing $5.9 billion as of 2016[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pakistan&action=edit). The term *[Overseas Pakistani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Pakistani "Overseas Pakistani")* is officially recognised by the Government of Pakistan, with the [Ministry of Overseas Pakistanis and Human Resource Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Overseas_Pakistanis_and_Human_Resource_Development "Ministry of Overseas Pakistanis and Human Resource Development") addressing their needs, welfare, and issues. Overseas Pakistanis constitute the second-largest source of foreign exchange remittances to Pakistan, with remittances increasing by over 100% from US$8.9 billion in 2009–10 to US$19.9 billion in 2015–16.

[Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Pakistan "Islam in Pakistan") is the state religion, with freedom of religion guaranteed by the [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan "Constitution of Pakistan"). The majority are Muslims (96.35%), followed by [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Pakistan "Hinduism in Pakistan") (2.17%) and [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Pakistan "Christianity in Pakistan") (1.37%). Other minorities include [Sikhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism_in_Pakistan "Sikhism in Pakistan"), [Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_Pakistan "Buddhism in Pakistan"), [Jains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism_in_Pakistan "Jainism in Pakistan"), [Zoroastrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrians "Zoroastrians") ([Parsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parsi "Parsi")), and the unique [Kalash people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalash_people "Kalash people") who practice [animism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animism "Animism"). In 2012, 2% of the population identified as atheist in a [Gallup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallup_Pakistan "Gallup Pakistan") survey.

#### Islam

Islam dominates in Pakistan, with about 96.35% of the population being Muslim. Pakistan ranks second globally in Muslim population, and is home to 10.5% of the world's Muslims. Karachi is the largest Muslim city in the world.

The majority follow [Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam "Sunni Islam"), with a significant presence of [Sufism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism_in_Pakistan "Sufism in Pakistan"), while Shia Muslims constitute a minority. Shias represent between 5–25%. The Shia population in Pakistan was estimated at 42 million in 2019. As of 2012[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pakistan&action=edit), 12% of Pakistani Muslims self-identify as [non-denominational Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-denominational_Muslims "Non-denominational Muslims").

The [Ahmadis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmadiyya_in_Pakistan "Ahmadiyya in Pakistan") are a minority, officially considered non-Muslims. Ahmadis face persecution, banned from calling themselves Muslims since 1974.

#### Hinduism

[Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Pakistan "Hinduism in Pakistan") is the second-largest religion, followed by 2.17% of the population according to the census in 2023. Pakistan had the fifth-largest Hindu population globally in 2010. In 2023, Hindus numbered 5,217,216. They reside across Pakistan but are concentrated in [Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindh "Sindh"), where they make up 8.81% of the population. [Umerkot district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umerkot_district "Umerkot district") of the province is the only Hindu majority area. [Tharparkar district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tharparkar_district "Tharparkar district") hosts the largest Hindu population. Four districts – Umerkot, Tharparkar, [Mirpurkhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirpurkhas_District "Mirpurkhas District"), and [Sanghar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanghar_District "Sanghar District") – have over half of Pakistan's Hindus.

At Pakistan's inception, the 'hostage theory' suggested fair treatment of Hindus to safeguard Muslims in India. However, some Pakistani Hindus felt marginalised, leading to emigration to India. In the aftermath of the [Babri Masjid demolition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babri_Masjid_demolition "Babri Masjid demolition") they faced violence.

#### Christianity and other religions

Christians are the next largest religious minority after Hindus, constituting 1.37% of the population. They are concentrated in [Lahore District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_District "Lahore District") (5%) and [Islamabad Capital Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad_Capital_Territory "Islamabad Capital Territory") (over 4%). Karachi hosts a historic [Roman Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism_in_Pakistan "Roman Catholicism in Pakistan") community established by [Goan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goa "Goa") and [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_people "Tamil people") migrants during British colonial rule.

Following Christianity, the [Bahá'í Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%27%C3%AD_Faith_in_Pakistan "Bahá'í Faith in Pakistan") had 30,000 followers in 2008, followed by Sikhism, Buddhism, and [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism "Zoroastrianism"), each with around 20,000 adherents in 2008, alongside a small [Jain community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism_in_Pakistan "Jainism in Pakistan").

### Education

Pakistan's [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan "Constitution of Pakistan") mandates [free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_education "Free education") primary and secondary education, with public universities established in each province, including [Punjab University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Punjab "University of the Punjab"), [Sindh University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindh_University "Sindh University"), [Peshawar University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshawar_University "Peshawar University"), [Karachi University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_University "Karachi University"), and [Balochistan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochistan_University "Balochistan University"). The country's educational landscape encompasses both [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_university "Public university") and [private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_university "Private university") universities, fostering collaboration to enhance research and [higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education_in_Pakistan "Higher education in Pakistan") opportunities, albeit with concerns regarding teaching quality in newer institutions. [Technical and vocational institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocational_education "Vocational education") in Pakistan number approximately 3,193, complemented by *[madrassahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrassas_in_Pakistan "Madrassas in Pakistan")* providing free Islamic education to students, with government efforts to [regulate and monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_reform "Education reform") their quality amidst concerns over extremists recruitment. [Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Pakistan "Education in Pakistan") is divided into six main levels, including nursery, primary, [middle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_school "Middle school"), [matriculation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_school_(upper_secondary) "High school (upper secondary)"), [intermediate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_college "Community college"), and university programs. Additionally, [private schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schools_in_Pakistan "Schools in Pakistan") offer a parallel secondary education system based on the curriculum set by the [Cambridge International Examinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_International_Examinations "Cambridge International Examinations"), with 439 international schools reported in Pakistan.

Initiatives since 2007 made [English medium education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_medium_education "English medium education") mandatory nationwide. Following a 2012 attack on activist [Malala Yousafzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malala_Yousafzai "Malala Yousafzai") by the [Taliban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehrik-i_Taliban_Pakistan "Tehrik-i Taliban Pakistan"), she became the youngest Nobel laureate for her education advocacy. Reforms in 2013 mandated Chinese language courses in Sindh, reflecting China's growing influence. As of 2018, Pakistan's literacy rate stands at 62.3%, with significant regional and gender disparities. Government initiatives, including computer literacy since 1995, aim to eradicate illiteracy, targeting 100% enrollment among primary school-age children and an ~86% literacy rate by 2015. Pakistan allocates 2.3% of its GDP to education, among the lowest in South Asia.

## Culture

[Civil society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Pakistan "Culture of Pakistan") in Pakistan is hierarchical, emphasising [local cultural etiquette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etiquette_in_Pakistan "Etiquette in Pakistan") and traditional Islamic values. The primary family unit is the [extended family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_family "Extended family"), but there's a rising trend towards [nuclear families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_families "Nuclear families") due to socio-economic factors. Both men and women typically wear *[Shalwar Kameez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalwar_Kameez "Shalwar Kameez")*; men also favour trousers, jeans, and shirts. The middle class has grown to about 35 million, with another 17 million in the upper and upper-middle classes, leading to a shift in power from rural landowners to urban elites. Festivals like *[Eid ul-Fitr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eid_ul-Fitr "Eid ul-Fitr")*, *[Eid ul-Azha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eid_ul-Azha "Eid ul-Azha")*, [Ramadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan "Ramadan"), [Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas "Christmas"), [Easter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter "Easter"), [Holi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holi "Holi"), and [Diwali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diwali "Diwali") are primarily religious. Pakistan ranked 56th on the 2006 [A.T. Kearney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.T._Kearney "A.T. Kearney")/FP [Globalization Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_Index "Globalization Index") due to increasing globalisation.

### Architecture

Four periods define Pakistani architecture: [pre-Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Pakistan#Early_history "History of Pakistan"), [Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Pakistan#Muslim_period "History of Pakistan"), [colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Pakistan#Colonial_era "History of Pakistan"), and [post-colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Pakistan#Independence "History of Pakistan"). The onset of the [Indus civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_civilisation "Indus civilisation") around the mid-3rd millennium BCE heralded an urban culture, evidenced by surviving large structures. Notable pre-Islamic settlements include [Mohenjo-daro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohenjo-daro "Mohenjo-daro"), [Harappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harappa "Harappa"), and [Kot Diji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kot_Diji "Kot Diji"). The fusion of Buddhism and [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_campaign_of_Alexander_the_Great "Indian campaign of Alexander the Great") influences birthed a distinctive [Greco-Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Buddhist "Greco-Buddhist") style from the 1st century CE, exemplified by the renowned [Gandhara style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandara_art "Gandara art"). Notable [Buddhist architectural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_architecture "Buddhist architecture") remnants include the [Takht-i-Bahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takht-i-Bahi "Takht-i-Bahi") monastery in [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khyber_Pakhtunkhwa "Khyber Pakhtunkhwa").

The advent of Islam in present-day Pakistan marked the cessation of Buddhist architecture, ushering in [Islamic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_architecture "Islamic architecture"). The notable [Indo-Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Islamic_architecture "Indo-Islamic architecture") structure, the [tomb of Shah Rukn-i-Alam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rukn-e-Alam "Rukn-e-Alam") in Multan, remains significant. During the Mughal era, Persian-Islamic design merged with Hindustani art, seen in Lahore's architectural gems like the [Badshahi Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badshahi_Mosque "Badshahi Mosque") and the [Lahore Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Fort "Lahore Fort") with the iconic [Alamgiri Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamgiri_Gate "Alamgiri Gate"). Lahore also boasts the vibrant [Wazir Khan Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wazir_Khan_Mosque "Wazir Khan Mosque"), and the lush [Shalimar Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalimar_Gardens_(Lahore) "Shalimar Gardens (Lahore)"). In the British colonial period, Indo-European buildings emerged, blending European and Indian-Islamic styles. Post-colonial identity shines through modern landmarks like the [Faisal Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisal_Mosque "Faisal Mosque"), [Minar-e-Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minar-e-Pakistan "Minar-e-Pakistan"), and [Mazar-e-Quaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazar-e-Quaid "Mazar-e-Quaid"). [British architectural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_the_United_Kingdom "Architecture of the United Kingdom") influence persists in structures across Lahore, Peshawar, and Karachi.

### Clothing, arts, and fashion

The *[Shalwar kameez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalwar_kameez "Shalwar kameez")* is Pakistan's [national dress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_dress "National dress"), worn in all provinces: Punjab, Sindh, Balochistan, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and Azad Kashmir. Each province has its own style. Pakistanis wear a variety of fabrics like silk, [chiffon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiffon_(fabric) "Chiffon (fabric)"), and cotton. In addition to the national dress, men often wear domestically tailored suits and [neckties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necktie "Necktie"), especially in offices, schools, and social gatherings.

Pakistan's [fashion industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_clothing "Pakistani clothing") has thrived, blending traditional and modern styles to create a unique cultural identity. Regional and traditional dress remain significant symbols of native tradition, evolving into both modern and purer forms. Organisations like the Pakistan Fashion Design Council in [Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore "Lahore") and the Fashion Pakistan Council in [Karachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi "Karachi") host events like [PFDC Fashion Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PFDC_Fashion_Week "PFDC Fashion Week") and [Fashion Pakistan Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_Pakistan_Week "Fashion Pakistan Week"). Pakistan's inaugural fashion week took place in November 2009.

### Literature and philosophy

Pakistan boasts literature in various languages including [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu_literature "Urdu literature"), [Sindhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhi_literature "Sindhi literature"), [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_literature "Punjabi literature"), [Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto_literature_and_poetry "Pashto literature and poetry"), [Baluchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochi_Academy "Balochi Academy"), [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_literature "Persian literature"), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_English_literature "Pakistani English literature"), and more. The [Pakistan Academy of Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Academy_of_Letters "Pakistan Academy of Letters") actively promotes literature and poetry both domestically and internationally. [National Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Library_of_Pakistan "National Library of Pakistan") contributes to literary dissemination. Historically, Pakistani literature consisted mainly of [lyric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyric_poetry "Lyric poetry"), [religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufi_poetry "Sufi poetry"), and [folkloric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_folklore "Pakistani folklore") works, later diversifying under colonial influence into prose fiction, now widely embraced.

The [national poet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_poet "National poet") of Pakistan, [Muhammad Iqbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Iqbal "Muhammad Iqbal"), wrote influential poetry in Urdu and Persian, advocating for Islamic civilisational revival. Notable figures in contemporary Urdu literature include [Josh Malihabadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Malihabadi "Josh Malihabadi"), [Faiz Ahmed Faiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faiz_Ahmed_Faiz "Faiz Ahmed Faiz"), and [Saadat Hasan Manto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saadat_Hasan_Manto "Saadat Hasan Manto"). Popular Sufi poets like [Shah Abdul Latif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Abdul_Latif "Shah Abdul Latif") and [Bulleh Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulleh_Shah "Bulleh Shah") are revered. [Mirza Kalich Beg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirza_Kalich_Beg "Mirza Kalich Beg") is hailed as the father of modern Sindhi prose. Pakistani philosophy has been shaped by influences from British and American philosophy, with notable figures like [M. M. Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._M._Sharif "M. M. Sharif") contributing to its development. After 1971, Marxist thought gained prominence in Pakistani philosophy through figures like [Jalaludin Abdur Rahim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalaludin_Abdur_Rahim "Jalaludin Abdur Rahim").

### Media and entertainment

The private [print media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_newspapers_in_Pakistan "List of newspapers in Pakistan"), state-owned [Pakistan Television Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Television_Corporation "Pakistan Television Corporation") (PTV), and [Pakistan Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Broadcasting_Corporation "Pakistan Broadcasting Corporation") (PBC) dominated media until the 21st century. Pakistan now boasts a vast network of domestic, privately owned [24-hour electronic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24-hour_news_cycle "24-hour news cycle") [news media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_television_channels_in_Pakistan "List of television channels in Pakistan") and [cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_television "Cable television") [television channels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_in_Pakistan "Television in Pakistan"). The [Reporters Without Borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reporters_Without_Borders "Reporters Without Borders") has indicated pressure faced by Pakistani reporters, particularly when reporting against the military or government. The BBC describes Pakistani media as "among the most outspoken in South Asia". Pakistani media has been instrumental in exposing corruption. In 2025, Pakistan ranked 158 out of 180 countries in the [Press Freedom Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Press_Freedom_Index "Press Freedom Index") by [Reporters Without Borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reporters_Without_Borders "Reporters Without Borders"), highlighting restrictions on [freedom of the press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_the_press "Freedom of the press"). According to the journalist [Raza Rumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raza_Rumi "Raza Rumi"), in 2023, TV channels in Pakistan faced suspensions and legal threats for airing content critical of the government or military, while online platforms also experienced temporary takedowns. According to a 2025 report, some newspapers have faced financial pressure—such as withdrawal of government advertisements—for publishing content critical of government policies. Both military and civilian governments have historically used such tactics. [Freedom House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_House "Freedom House") rated Pakistan's Internet freedom as "Not Free" in 2024.

The [Lollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lollywood "Lollywood"), Punjabi, and [Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto_cinema "Pashto cinema") film industry is centered in Karachi, Lahore, and Peshawar. Although Bollywood films were banned from public cinemas from 1965 to 2008, they remained influential in Pakistani popular culture. However, in 2019, the screening of Bollywood movies faced an indefinite ban. Despite challenges faced by the Pakistani film industry, Urdu [televised dramas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_dramas "Pakistani dramas") and theatrical performances remain popular, frequently broadcast by many entertainment media outlets. Urdu dramas dominate the television entertainment industry, renowned for their quality since the 1990s. Pakistani music encompasses diverse forms, from provincial folk music and traditional styles like [Qawwali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qawwali "Qawwali") and [Ghazal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazal "Ghazal") Gayaki to modern fusions of traditional and western music. Pakistan boasts numerous renowned folk singers, and the arrival of Afghan refugees in western provinces has sparked interest in Pashto music, despite occasional intolerance.

### Cuisine

Pakistani cuisine, rooted in the royal kitchens of 16th-century Mughal emperors, blends influences from [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_cuisine "British cuisine"), [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_cuisine "Indian cuisine"), [Central Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asian_cuisine "Central Asian cuisine"), and [Middle Eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Eastern_cuisine "Middle Eastern cuisine") culinary traditions. Unlike Middle Eastern fare, Pakistani dishes are heavily spiced with garlic, ginger, turmeric, [chilli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chili_powder "Chili powder"), and [garam masala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garam_masala "Garam masala"). [Roti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roti "Roti"), a wheat-based flatbread, accompanies most meals, alongside [curry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curry "Curry"), meat, vegetables, and lentils. Rice is also common, served plain, spiced, or in sweet dishes. [Lassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lassi "Lassi"), a traditional drink from the [Punjab region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_region "Punjab region"), and [black tea with milk and sugar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_tea_culture "Pakistani tea culture") are popular beverages enjoyed nationwide. [Sohan halwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sohan_halwa "Sohan halwa"), a beloved sweet dish from southern Punjab, is savoured across Pakistan.

### Sports

[Cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket "Cricket") is the most popular sport in Pakistan, followed by [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football "Association football"). [Field hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_hockey "Field hockey") is the national sport. Other sports like [squash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squash_(sport) "Squash (sport)"), [polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polo "Polo"), and [traditional games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_games_of_Pakistan "Traditional games of Pakistan") are also enjoyed.

In cricket, Pakistan holds victories in all major [ICC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cricket_Council "International Cricket Council") tournaments, including the [ICC Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICC_Cricket_World_Cup "ICC Cricket World Cup"), [ICC World Twenty20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICC_World_Twenty20 "ICC World Twenty20"), and [ICC Champions Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICC_Champions_Trophy "ICC Champions Trophy"). The [Pakistan Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Super_League "Pakistan Super League") ranks among the top [T20 leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_T20_cricket_competitions "List of T20 cricket competitions") globally.

In football, Pakistan established the [Pakistan Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Football_Federation "Pakistan Football Federation") soon after its creation, and it is known for producing [FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup "FIFA World Cup") balls.

In field hockey, Pakistan holds four [Hockey World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockey_World_Cup "Hockey World Cup") wins, eight [Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_Asian_Games "Field hockey at the Asian Games") gold medals, and three [Olympic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_Summer_Olympics "Field hockey at the Summer Olympics") gold medals. Squash player [Jahangir Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahangir_Khan "Jahangir Khan") holds the record for the longest winning streak in professional sport history, winning 555 consecutive matches. Pakistan has hosted various international events, including Cricket and Hockey World Cups and Asian Games.
