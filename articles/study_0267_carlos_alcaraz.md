# Carlos Alcaraz

**Carlos Alcaraz Garfia** (born 5 May 2003) is a Spanish professional [tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis "Tennis") player. He has been ranked [world No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ATP_number_1_ranked_singles_tennis_players "List of ATP number 1 ranked singles tennis players") in men's singles by the [Association of Tennis Professionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Tennis_Professionals "Association of Tennis Professionals") (ATP) for 66 weeks, and finished as the [year-end No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ATP_number_1_ranked_singles_tennis_players#Year-end_No._1_players "List of ATP number 1 ranked singles tennis players") in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_ATP_Tour "2022 ATP Tour") and [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_ATP_Tour "2025 ATP Tour"). Alcaraz has won 27 [ATP Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Tour "ATP Tour")–level singles titles, including seven [majors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis)#Tournaments "Grand Slam (tennis)") and eight [ATP Masters 1000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Masters_1000_tournaments "ATP Masters 1000 tournaments") titles. He is the ninth and youngest man in history to complete the [career Grand Slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Grand_Slam_men%27s_singles_champions#Career_Grand_Slam "List of Grand Slam men's singles champions") in singles.

A teen phenomenon, Alcaraz won the [Next Gen ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Next_Gen_ATP_Finals "2021 Next Gen ATP Finals") and was the 2020 [ATP Newcomer of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Newcomer_of_the_Year "ATP Newcomer of the Year"). In 2022, Alcaraz won his first major title at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 US Open – Men's singles"). He became the youngest man in the [Open Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Era "Open Era") to be ranked world No. 1, at 19 years, 130 days old, and finished the year as the youngest year-end No. 1 in [ATP rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_rankings "ATP rankings") history. Alcaraz won his second major title at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles") in 2023, defeating seven-time champion [Novak Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic") in [an epic final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles_final "2023 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles final").

In 2024, Alcaraz won his first [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 French Open – Men's singles") and defended his title at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles"), becoming the youngest man to win the "[Channel Slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_Slam "Channel Slam")". In 2025, he defeated [Jannik Sinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jannik_Sinner "Jannik Sinner") in [a historic final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles_final "2025 French Open – Men's singles final") to win the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 French Open – Men's singles"), as well as the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 US Open – Men's singles"), and finished as the year-end world No. 1 for a second time. In 2026, Alcaraz won his seventh major at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2026 Australian Open – Men's singles"), and became the youngest man to complete the career Grand Slam, at 22 years, 272 days old.

## Early and personal life

Carlos Alcaraz Garfia was born on 5 May 2003, in [El Palmar, Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Palmar,_Murcia "El Palmar, Murcia"), Spain, to parents Carlos Alcaraz González and Virginia Garfia Escandón. Alcaraz has one older brother, Álvaro, and two younger brothers, Sergio and Jaime.

Alcaraz started playing tennis at the age of four at the Real Sociedad Club de Campo de Murcia, where his father was a tennis coach and club administrator. His mother worked as a sales assistant at [IKEA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IKEA "IKEA"). Alcaraz's father had played tennis but stopped pursuing a professional career as a teenager, as he could not afford it. In 2013, at age 10, Alcaraz signed his first contract with [Babolat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babolat "Babolat"). After watching him compete at age 11, [IMG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMG_(company) "IMG (company)") agent Albert Molina recognized Carlos as a standout talent and sought to persuade his parents to work with him; his father ultimately agreed when Carlos was 12.

Alcaraz is frequently accompanied to tournaments by his father and by his brother Álvaro, who works as his [hitting partner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitting_partner "Hitting partner") and assistant coach. During the tennis off-season, he lives at his parents' home in [Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murcia "Murcia"). His friends and family call him "Carlitos" or "Charly". He is [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church "Catholic Church"), and has received blessings from priests before important tournaments.

## Junior career

At the age of 10, Alcaraz competed outside Spain for the first time at the under-10 World Championship in Croatia, where he lost in the final. That same year, he became an official member of the [Babolat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babolat "Babolat") team, later joining the brand's international roster at age 13.

In 2015, Alcaraz won the under-12 division of the Rafa Nadal Tour Masters, and the following year captured the competition's under-14 title.

In 2017, Alcaraz enjoyed a breakthrough season at the under-14 level. He won the XIV Taça Internacional Maia Jovem, defeating [Daniel Rincón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Rinc%C3%B3n_(tennis) "Daniel Rincón (tennis)") in the final, and later captured the Babolat Cup. That summer, he helped Spain win the 14-and-under European Summer Cup and was part of the Spanish team that finished runner-up to Switzerland at the [ITF World Junior Tennis Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITF_World_Junior_Tennis_Finals "ITF World Junior Tennis Finals").

Alcaraz continued his rise in 2018. In July, he won the Dutch Junior Open, defeating Filip Kolasinski in the final, and weeks later captured the [European 16-and-under Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_European_Junior_Championships#European_Junior_Championships_16_&_Under "Tennis European Junior Championships") by defeating [Elmer Møller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmer_M%C3%B8ller "Elmer Møller"). Later that year at the [Junior Davis Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_Davis_Cup_and_Junior_Billie_Jean_King_Cup "Junior Davis Cup and Junior Billie Jean King Cup") in Budapest, he played a key role in Spain's title run, saving match point while trailing against [Harold Mayot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Mayot "Harold Mayot") in singles before returning to win the decisive doubles match after France had taken an early lead.

In March 2019, Alcaraz won the J300 Villena, defeating [Illya Beloborodko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illya_Beloborodko "Illya Beloborodko") in the final, and was ranked No. 1 on the [Tennis Europe Junior Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_Europe_Junior_Tour "Tennis Europe Junior Tour") during the year. As a junior, he achieved a career-high world ranking of No. 22.

## Professional career

### 2018–2020: Pro debut

In February 2018, aged 14, Alcaraz played his first professional tennis event as a qualifier at the Spain F5 [ITF Futures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITF_Men%27s_World_Tennis_Tour "ITF Men's World Tennis Tour") in Murcia. He knocked out second seed and world No. 292 [Federico Gaio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_Gaio "Federico Gaio") in the first round, and ultimately reached the quarterfinals. He collected his first two ATP points from this tournament, and entered the rankings as world No. 1414 on 26 February 2018.

In April 2019, aged 15, Alcaraz made his [Challenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Challenger_Tour "ATP Challenger Tour") debut at the [JC Ferrero Challenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JC_Ferrero_Challenger_Open "JC Ferrero Challenger Open") in Villena. He won his first round match against a 17-year old [Jannik Sinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jannik_Sinner "Jannik Sinner"), who was on a sixteen-match winning streak. With this victory, Alcaraz became the first player born in 2003 to win a Challenger match. He was defeated by eighth seed [Lukáš Rosol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luk%C3%A1%C5%A1_Rosol "Lukáš Rosol") in the second round. One week later at the [Murcia Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Murcia_Open "2019 Murcia Open"), he defeated fifth seed and world No. 140 [Pedro Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Mart%C3%ADnez_(tennis) "Pedro Martínez (tennis)") in the second round. Alcaraz went on to win four Challenger titles, three of them before the age of 18. He was the first player born in 2003 to reach a Challenger title match.

In February 2020, aged 16, Alcaraz made his ATP main-draw debut at the [Rio Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Rio_Open "2020 Rio Open") after receiving a wild card for the singles main draw. He defeated world No. 41 [Albert Ramos Viñolas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Ramos_Vi%C3%B1olas "Albert Ramos Viñolas") in a marathon three-setter that lasted three hours and 37 minutes. Alcaraz became the first player born in 2003 to win an ATP Tour match, and also achieved this feat before anyone born in 2002. He was defeated in the second round by [Federico Coria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_Coria "Federico Coria").

In September 2020, aged 17, Alcaraz played his first qualifying round at a major tournament at the postponed [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles_qualifying "2020 French Open – Men's singles qualifying"). He held two match points to defeat [Aleksandar Vukic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandar_Vukic "Aleksandar Vukic") in straight sets, but ended up losing in three.

### 2021: First ATP title, Next Gen champion

Alcaraz made his debut in the [main draw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_draw_(tennis) "Main draw (tennis)") of a major as a [qualifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qualifier_(tennis) "Qualifier (tennis)") at the [2021 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Australian_Open "2021 Australian Open"). He was the youngest man to qualify for the tournament since [Novak Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic") in 2005. As the main draw was postponed due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"), Alcaraz played the [Great Ocean Road Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Great_Ocean_Road_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 Great Ocean Road Open – Singles") in early February, where he was defeated by [Thiago Monteiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiago_Monteiro_(tennis) "Thiago Monteiro (tennis)"). In his first main draw match at the Australian Open, Alcaraz defeated fellow qualifier [Botic van de Zandschulp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botic_van_de_Zandschulp "Botic van de Zandschulp") in straight sets. He was the only teenager in the draw to progress past the first round, and the first person born in 2003 to win a match at a major. In the second round, Alcaraz was defeated by [Mikael Ymer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikael_Ymer "Mikael Ymer").

Alcaraz played a mix of Challenger and ATP Tour-level tournaments throughout the spring, including the [Gran Canaria Challenger II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Gran_Canaria_Challenger_II_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 Gran Canaria Challenger II – Singles") and the [Open Occitanie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Open_Sud_de_France_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 Open Sud de France – Singles"). He received [wildcards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_card_(tennis) "Wild card (tennis)") at the [Mexican Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Abierto_Mexicano_Telcel_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 Abierto Mexicano Telcel – Singles"), where he lost to eventual champion [Alexander Zverev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Zverev "Alexander Zverev") in the first round; at the [Barcelona Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Barcelona_Open_Banc_Sabadell_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 Barcelona Open Banc Sabadell – Singles"), where he lost to [Frances Tiafoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Tiafoe "Frances Tiafoe") in the first round; and at the [Andalucía Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Andaluc%C3%ADa_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 Andalucía Open – Singles"), where he defeated [Nikola Milojevic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikola_Milojevi%C4%87_(tennis) "Nikola Milojević (tennis)"), [Feliciano Lopez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feliciano_L%C3%B3pez "Feliciano López"), and third seed [Casper Ruud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casper_Ruud "Casper Ruud") to make the deepest ATP tournament run of his career. He lost to compatriot [Jaume Munar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaume_Munar "Jaume Munar") in the semifinal.

Alcaraz received a wildcard at the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2021 Miami Open – Men's singles"), where he lost in the first round to [Emil Ruusuvuori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Ruusuvuori "Emil Ruusuvuori"). This was his first appearance at a ATP Masters 1000 tournament. He was given another ATP 1000 wildcard at the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2021 Mutua Madrid Open – Men's singles"). Alcaraz became the youngest match winner in tournament history after his first round defeat of [Adrian Mannarino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Mannarino "Adrian Mannarino"), breaking a record set by [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal "Rafael Nadal") in 2004. He then met Nadal for their first career meeting, and lost to him in two short sets on the day of his eighteenth birthday. Alcaraz entered the [Challenger Oeiras III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Open_de_Oeiras_III_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 Open de Oeiras III – Singles") as an unseeded player, and defeated [Facundo Bagnis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facundo_Bagnis "Facundo Bagnis") in the final to claim the fourth Challenger title of his career. On 24 May 2021, he rose from 114th to 94th in the ATP rankings. He became the youngest player inside the top 100, as well as the youngest inside the top 500.

Alcaraz easily qualified for the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2021 French Open – Men's singles") and reached the third round, where he was defeated by [Jan-Lennard Struff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan-Lennard_Struff "Jan-Lennard Struff"). He was the youngest man to reach the third round at Roland Garros in 29 years, and the youngest to do so at any major since Nadal in 2004. Alcaraz then made his debut at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2021 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles") via a wildcard. In the first round, he defeated [Yasutaka Uchiyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasutaka_Uchiyama "Yasutaka Uchiyama") in the first five-set match of his career. He was defeated in the second round in straight sets by world No. 2 [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev "Daniil Medvedev"). In July, Alcaraz won the [Umag Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Croatia_Open_Umag_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 Croatia Open Umag – Singles"), defeating [Richard Gasquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Gasquet "Richard Gasquet") to claim the first ATP title of his career. He was the youngest ATP champion since [Kei Nishikori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kei_Nishikori "Kei Nishikori") in 2008.

In the third round of the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2021 US Open – Men's singles"), Alcaraz defeated world No. 3 [Stefanos Tsitsipas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefanos_Tsitsipas "Stefanos Tsitsipas") in a dramatic fifth-set tiebreak on [Arthur Ashe Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Ashe_Stadium "Arthur Ashe Stadium"). At 18 years and four months, Alcaraz became the youngest man to beat a top 3 ranked-player in singles at the US Open since the [ATP rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_rankings "ATP rankings") began in 1973. He also became the youngest man in a major fourth round since 17-year-old [Andrei Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Medvedev_(tennis) "Andrei Medvedev (tennis)") at the [1992 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "1992 French Open – Men's singles"), and the youngest man in the US Open fourth round since 17-year-old [Michael Chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Chang "Michael Chang") in [1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "1989 US Open – Men's singles"). Alcaraz then reached the quarterfinals by defeating qualifier [Peter Gojowczyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Gojowczyk "Peter Gojowczyk"). He became the youngest US Open men's quarterfinalist in the [Open Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Era "Open Era"), the youngest all-time since [Thomaz Koch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomaz_Koch "Thomaz Koch") in [1963](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_U.S._National_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "1963 U.S. National Championships – Men's singles"), and the youngest at any major since Michael Chang at the [1990 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "1990 French Open – Men's singles"). He retired against [Félix Auger-Aliassime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Auger-Aliassime "Félix Auger-Aliassime") in the quarterfinals after injuring his leg.

At [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2021 BNP Paribas Open – Men's singles"), Alcaraz was seeded (30th) for the first time at an ATP Masters 1000 event, but lost in the first round to [Andy Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Murray "Andy Murray"). At the [Vienna Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Erste_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 Erste Bank Open – Singles") two weeks later, Alcaraz met Murray for a rematch and defeated him. He then defeated world No. 7 [Matteo Berrettini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Berrettini "Matteo Berrettini") to claim a second career top 10 win, and entered the top 35 on 1 November. He lost to [Alexander Zverev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Zverev "Alexander Zverev") in the semifinal. At the [Paris Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Rolex_Paris_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2021 Rolex Paris Masters – Singles"), Alcaraz took a third top 10 win against eighth seed [Jannik Sinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jannik_Sinner "Jannik Sinner") in their first ATP meeting. However, in the round of 16, Alcaraz was unable to cope with a raucous home crowd backing Frenchman [Hugo Gaston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Gaston "Hugo Gaston") and lost in straight sets. Alcaraz ended his season at the [Next Gen ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Next_Generation_ATP_Finals "2021 Next Generation ATP Finals"); he had qualified as third seed behind Sinner and Auger-Aliassime, but became first seed after their withdrawals. He dominated the draw to win the title, dropping just one set.

### 2022: US Open champion, world No. 1

Alcaraz was seeded for the first time in a major at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 Australian Open – Men's singles"). He reached the third round, where he was defeated in a fifth set tiebreak by [Matteo Berrettini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Berrettini "Matteo Berrettini"). This was the only five set match Alcaraz would lose until the [2026 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2026 US Open – Men's singles"). At the [Rio Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Rio_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 Rio Open – Singles"), Alcaraz won his first [ATP 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_500_tournaments "ATP 500 tournaments") title by defeating [Diego Schwartzman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Schwartzman "Diego Schwartzman") in the final. He became the youngest winner of an ATP 500 event since the category was created in 2009. He entered the ATP top 20 for the first time on 21 February 2022.

At [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 BNP Paribas Open – Men's singles"), Alcaraz defeated defending champion [Cameron Norrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Norrie "Cameron Norrie") to reach his first ATP Masters 1000 quarterfinal and semifinal, which he lost to [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal "Rafael Nadal"). Two weeks later, at the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 Miami Open – Men's singles"), Alcaraz defeated [Casper Ruud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casper_Ruud "Casper Ruud") to win his first 1000 title. He was the youngest men's singles winner in tournament history, as well as the youngest men's ATP Masters 1000 champion since Rafael Nadal at [Monte-Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Monte_Carlo_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2005 Monte Carlo Masters – Singles") in 2005. He was also the first Spaniard to win Miami. At [Monte-Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Monte-Carlo_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 Monte-Carlo Masters – Singles"), Alcaraz was upset by [Sebastian Korda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Korda "Sebastian Korda"). Alcaraz beat top seed [Stefanos Tsitsipas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefanos_Tsitsipas "Stefanos Tsitsipas") in the quarterfinals of the [Barcelona Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Barcelona_Open_Banc_Sabadell_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 Barcelona Open Banc Sabadell – Singles") to enter the world top 10 for the first time on 25 April 2022. He became the youngest man to break into the top 10 since Nadal on 25 April 2005, exactly 17 years earlier. Alcaraz defeated [Pablo Carreño Busta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Carre%C3%B1o_Busta "Pablo Carreño Busta") to win the Barcelona title.

The day after his 19th birthday, Alcaraz defeated five-time champion, world No. 4 and third seed [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal "Rafael Nadal") in the quarterfinals of the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 Mutua Madrid Open – Men's singles"). This was Alcaraz's first victory against Nadal, and their final Tour-level meeting. The following day, Alcaraz faced world No. 1 [Novak Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic") for their first meeting. After three hours and thirty-six minutes, Alcaraz prevailed in a tight third set tiebreak. He became the youngest male player to win a match against a world No. 1 since Nadal in 2004, and the only player ever to defeat Nadal and Djokovic back-to-back on clay. In the final, Alcaraz dismantled defending champion and world No. 3 [Alexander Zverev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Zverev "Alexander Zverev") in an hour and four minutes. He became the youngest champion in the tournament's history at 19 years, 3 days. On 9 May 2022, Alcaraz climbed to a career-high ranking of world No. 6.

Alcaraz was widely projected to be one of the favorites for the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 French Open – Men's singles") title. In his second round match against [Albert Ramos Viñolas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Ramos_Vi%C3%B1olas "Albert Ramos Viñolas"), he was taken to five sets and forced to save match point. He lost to [Alexander Zverev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Zverev "Alexander Zverev") in the quarterfinal. At [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles"), Alcaraz lost to [Jannik Sinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jannik_Sinner "Jannik Sinner") in the fourth round. At the [Hamburg Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Hamburg_European_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 Hamburg European Open – Men's singles"), Alcaraz recorded his first-ever defeat in an ATP final against [Lorenzo Musetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Musetti "Lorenzo Musetti"). Nevertheless, this result took him to a new career-high ranking of world No. 5 on July 25; he became the youngest male player to enter the top 5 since Nadal in 2005. Alcaraz reached and lost a second consecutive final at the [Croatia Open Umag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Croatia_Open_Umag_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 Croatia Open Umag – Singles"), in another defeat by Jannik Sinner. He became world No. 4 on 1 August.

Alcaraz's North American swing did not start well: he was upset by [Tommy Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Paul_(tennis) "Tommy Paul (tennis)") in his debut at the [Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 National Bank Open – Men's singles"), and [Cameron Norrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Norrie "Cameron Norrie") in the quarterfinals of the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Western_%26_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 Western & Southern Open – Men's singles"). Prior to the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_US_Open_(tennis) "2022 US Open (tennis)"), Alcaraz, [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal "Rafael Nadal"), [Casper Ruud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casper_Ruud "Casper Ruud"), [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev "Daniil Medvedev"), and [Stefanos Tsitsipas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefanos_Tsitsipas "Stefanos Tsitsipas") were all in contention to take over the [world No. 1 ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_rankings "ATP rankings"). In the fourth round, Alcaraz defeated former champion [Marin Čilić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marin_%C4%8Cili%C4%87 "Marin Čilić") in five sets, and became the youngest man to reach back-to-back US Open quarterfinals in the [Open Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Era "Open Era"). In the quarterfinals, Alcaraz saved a match point in the fourth set before recovering to win in five sets against [Jannik Sinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jannik_Sinner "Jannik Sinner"). The match lasted five hours and fifteen minutes, and recorded the latest finish in the history of the tournament at 2:50AM EST. Alcaraz played a third consecutive five-setter to defeat [Frances Tiafoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Tiafoe "Frances Tiafoe").

Alcaraz then met fifth seed [Casper Ruud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casper_Ruud "Casper Ruud") for his first major final, a match which doubled as a shoot-out for the world No. 1 ranking. Alcaraz won the match in four sets to claim his first major title. He became the youngest No. 1 in the history of the [ATP Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Rankings "ATP Rankings") at 19 years, 130 days, breaking [Lleyton Hewitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lleyton_Hewitt "Lleyton Hewitt")'s record, and the [second youngest all-time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-time_tennis_records_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "All-time tennis records – Men's singles") behind [Lew Hoad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lew_Hoad "Lew Hoad"). He also became the youngest men's major champion since Nadal at the [2005 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2005 French Open – Men's singles"), the youngest [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Open_(tennis) "US Open (tennis)") champion since [Pete Sampras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Sampras "Pete Sampras") in [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "1990 US Open – Men's singles"), and the first man born in the 2000s to win a major singles title.

Alcaraz's season ended more flatly. He lost his first singles match as world No. 1 to [Félix Auger-Aliassime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Auger-Aliassime "Félix Auger-Aliassime") at the [2022 Davis Cup Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Davis_Cup_Finals "2022 Davis Cup Finals"). He lost to [David Goffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Goffin "David Goffin") in the first round of the [Astana Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Astana_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 Astana Open – Singles"), and lost again to Auger-Aliassime in [Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Swiss_Indoors_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 Swiss Indoors – Singles"). Alcaraz reached the quarterfinals of the [Paris Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Rolex_Paris_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2022 Rolex Paris Masters – Singles"), but retired down a set to [Holger Rune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holger_Rune "Holger Rune"). A day later, he announced he had suffered an abdominal tear requiring a six-week layoff, forcing him to end his season early. At 19 years and 214 days, Alcaraz ended the year as youngest and first teenage world No. 1 in the ATP era, and [second youngest of all-time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-time_tennis_records_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "All-time tennis records – Men's singles") behind Hoad.

### 2023: Wimbledon champion

On January 7, Alcaraz announced his withdrawal from the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Australian_Open "2023 Australian Open") due to a right hamstring injury sustained while training. He had held the world No. 1 ranking for 20 weeks, but lost it after the tournament to champion [Novak Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic"). Alcaraz began his season at the [Argentina Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Argentina_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 Argentina Open – Singles"), where he was playing for the first time. He defeated [Cameron Norrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Norrie "Cameron Norrie") in the final in straight sets to claim his first title since the [2022 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 US Open – Men's singles"). The next week, Alcaraz reached the final of the [Rio Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Rio_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 Rio Open – Singles"), where he was defending champion, also against Norrie; however, he aggravated his leg injury during the match and lost in three sets. Alcaraz withdrew from the [Mexican Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Open_(tennis) "Mexican Open (tennis)"), citing the same injury.

At [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_BNP_Paribas_Open "2023 BNP Paribas Open"), Alcaraz recorded his 100th career win against 31st seed [Tallon Griekspoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallon_Griekspoor "Tallon Griekspoor") in the third round, making him the second fastest player to reach this milestone after [John McEnroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McEnroe "John McEnroe"). He defeated [Jannik Sinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jannik_Sinner "Jannik Sinner") in the semifinal and [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev "Daniil Medvedev") in the final to lift the trophy. He became the first male player to win Indian Wells without dropping a set since [Roger Federer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Federer "Roger Federer") in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2017 BNP Paribas Open – Men's singles"), and the youngest man to have won both legs of the [Sunshine Double](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunshine_Double "Sunshine Double"). As a result, he regained the world No. 1 ranking on 20 March 2023. Alcaraz hoped to complete the Sunshine Double in [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 Miami Open – Men's singles"), where he was the defending champion. He reached the semifinals without dropping a set, where he lost to Jannik Sinner in three sets. With the loss of his title points, Alcaraz dropped back to No. 2 in the world rankings.

Alcaraz was scheduled to play at the [Monte-Carlo Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Monte-Carlo_Masters "2023 Monte-Carlo Masters") the following week, but withdrew due to post-traumatic arthritis in his left hand and muscular discomfort in his spine. He defeated [Stefanos Tsitsipas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefanos_Tsitsipas "Stefanos Tsitsipas") in the final of the [Barcelona Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Barcelona_Open_Banc_Sabadell_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 Barcelona Open Banc Sabadell – Singles") to defend his title without dropping a set. He also defended his title at the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 Mutua Madrid Open – Men's singles"), defeating [Jan-Lennard Struff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan-Lennard_Struff "Jan-Lennard Struff") to claim his tenth career title and cement his dominance on Spanish clay. At the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 Italian Open – Men's singles"), Alcaraz recovered the No. 1 ranking from Djokovic just by playing his second round match. However, he was upset in the third round by world No. 135 [Fabian Marozsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabian_Marozsan "Fabian Marozsan") in straight sets, in what [Jon Wertheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Wertheim "Jon Wertheim") labelled the biggest upset of the year.

Alcaraz only dropped one set before the semifinals of [Roland-Garros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 French Open – Men's singles"), where he met [Novak Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic") for their second career meeting. This was a highly anticipated showdown, as the two men had not played a match in over a year despite their ongoing fight over the world No. 1 ranking. The match was competitive until Alcaraz faltered at the start of the third set due to cramps from mental pressure and physical intensity; Djokovic won in four sets, going on to win the tournament and regain the No. 1 ranking.

At the [Queen's Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Queen%27s_Club_Championships_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 Queen's Club Championships – Singles"), Alcaraz claimed his first title on grass in just his third Tour-level grass tournament, beating [Alex de Minaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_de_Minaur "Alex de Minaur") in the final. This victory also restored him as world No. 1. Despite relatively muted expectations for Alcaraz at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles"), he only dropped two sets en route to the final, where he faced off against seven-time champion and four-time defending champion Djokovic. Alcaraz was [breadsticked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breadstick "Breadstick") in the first set but held his nerve to win the match in five sets. It lasted four hours and forty-two minutes, and was instantly acclaimed as a modern epic. This was his first Wimbledon title, and his second major title overall. Alcaraz also became the only man outside the [Big Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Three_(tennis)#Big_Four_era "Big Three (tennis)") to claim the Wimbledon singles title since 2002, before he himself had been born.

Alcaraz lost in the quarterfinals of [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 National Bank Open – Men's singles") to [Tommy Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Paul_(tennis) "Tommy Paul (tennis)"). He then met Djokovic again for the [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Western_%26_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 Western & Southern Open – Men's singles") final. Alcaraz lost in three sets, despite having a championship point in the second-set tiebreak. It was the longest ever best-of-three-sets ATP Masters 1000 final and the longest match in the tournament's history, at 3 hours and 49 minutes; Djokovic praised it as "one of the toughest matches of [his] life". Alcaraz entered the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 US Open – Men's singles") as defending champion, and reached the semifinals for the loss of just one set. However, he lost there to [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2021 US Open – Men's singles") champion [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev "Daniil Medvedev") in four sets. Following the tournament, Alcaraz lost the No. 1 ranking once again to eventual champion Djokovic, who had not been defending any points.

Alcaraz entered the autumn-winter hardcourt season with the goal of seizing the year-end No. 1 ranking. However, just like 2022, he experienced an end-of-year slump as the tour moved back to fast [hardcourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcourt "Hardcourt"). He lost to [Jannik Sinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jannik_Sinner "Jannik Sinner") in the semifinals of [Beijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_China_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 China Open – Men's singles"), and then to [Grigor Dimitrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigor_Dimitrov "Grigor Dimitrov") in the round of 16 at [Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Rolex_Shanghai_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 Rolex Shanghai Masters – Singles"). He withdrew from [Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Swiss_Indoors "2023 Swiss Indoors") due to injury, and lost in his opening round at the [Paris Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Rolex_Paris_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 Rolex Paris Masters – Singles") to [Roman Safiullin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Safiullin "Roman Safiullin"). At the [ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_ATP_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 ATP Finals – Singles") in [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin "Turin"), Alcaraz lost to [Alexander Zverev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Zverev "Alexander Zverev") but defeated [Andrey Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Rublev "Andrey Rublev") and [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev "Daniil Medvedev") in the round robin stage. He qualified for the semifinals, where he lost decisively to eventual champion Djokovic. Ultimately, Alcaraz ended the year ranked No. 2.

### 2024: Channel Slam, Olympic silver

Alcaraz began his 2024 campaign at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 Australian Open – Men's singles"). He produced a dominant performance against [Miomir Kecmanovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miomir_Kecmanovi%C4%87 "Miomir Kecmanović") to reach the quarterfinals for the first time in his career; however, he lost there to [Alexander Zverev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Zverev "Alexander Zverev") in four sets. In February, Alcaraz chose to play on South American clay courts for the third year in a row. He entered the [Argentina Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Argentina_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2024 Argentina Open – Singles") as defending champion, but lost to [Nicolás Jarry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Jarry "Nicolás Jarry") in the semifinal. The following week, he retired from the [Rio Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Rio_Open "2024 Rio Open") two games into his first match after twisting his right ankle. He announced that he had suffered a lateral sprain, but that he would miss just "a few days" of training before [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 BNP Paribas Open – Men's singles").

As defending champion on some of his favorite courts in Indian Wells, Alcaraz came back into form. In the semifinals, he defeated [Jannik Sinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jannik_Sinner "Jannik Sinner") to end his 19-match winning streak. He then defeated [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev "Daniil Medvedev") in the final, earning him his first title in eight months. In [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 Miami Open – Men's singles"), Alcaraz reached the quarterfinals without dropping a set but lost there to [Grigor Dimitrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigor_Dimitrov "Grigor Dimitrov"). Following the tournament, he dropped to world No. 3 behind [Novak Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic") and Miami champion Sinner.

Alcaraz's clay season was disrupted by injury. He withdrew from the [Monte-Carlo Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Monte-Carlo_Masters "2024 Monte-Carlo Masters") and the [Barcelona Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Barcelona_Open_Banc_Sabadell "2024 Barcelona Open Banc Sabadell") due to a problem with his right forearm. In [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_Open_(tennis) "Madrid Open (tennis)"), Alcaraz continued to struggle with the injury, which hampered his ability to hit his signature forehand at full power. He lost in the quarterfinals to eventual champion [Andrey Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Rublev "Andrey Rublev"), and withdrew from [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Italian_Open_(tennis) "2024 Italian Open (tennis)"). However, Alcaraz recovered in time to play the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 French Open – Men's singles") in June. Despite some shaky early matches he reached the semi-final, where he defeated second seed [Jannik Sinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jannik_Sinner "Jannik Sinner") in five gruelling sets to advance to his first Roland Garros final. Alcaraz defeated [Alexander Zverev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Zverev "Alexander Zverev") in another five-setter to claim his first Roland Garros title. At 21 years and 35 days, he became the youngest male player in history to win a major title on three different surfaces.

In July, Alcaraz defended his [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Wimbledon_Championships "2024 Wimbledon Championships") title by defeating [Novak Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic") in straight sets in a rematch of their [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Wimbledon_Championships "2023 Wimbledon Championships") final. At 21 years and 70 days, Alcaraz became the youngest male player in the Open Era to complete the [Channel Slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_Slam "Channel Slam") (winning the French Open and Wimbledon in the same season). Alcaraz then entered the [Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "Tennis at the 2024 Summer Olympics – Men's singles") in both doubles and singles. Partnering Rafael Nadal, he reached the quarterfinals in doubles. In singles, Alcaraz reached the final without dropping a set. He was the heavy favorite for the gold medal; however, he lost to Djokovic in two tightly contested sets during which neither man dropped serve. He became the youngest-ever silver medalist in men's singles.

Alcaraz skipped the [2024 Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 National Bank Open – Men's singles"), citing fatigue. He played at the [2024 Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Cincinnati_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 Cincinnati Open – Men's singles"), but was upset by [Gaël Monfils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ga%C3%ABl_Monfils "Gaël Monfils") in the opening round. This match marked the first time Alcaraz broke a racket on court, which generated significant media coverage and drove him to deliver a public apology. He next played the [2024 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 US Open – Men's singles"), where he suffered a shocking second-round defeat in straight sets to [Botic van de Zandschulp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botic_van_de_Zandschulp "Botic van de Zandschulp"). This was his earliest loss in a major since Wimbledon 2021.

Alcaraz recovered his morale with successes at the [Davis Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Davis_Cup "2024 Davis Cup") and [Laver Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Laver_Cup "2024 Laver Cup") team events. Afterwards, he defeated [Jannik Sinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jannik_Sinner "Jannik Sinner") in the final of the [China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_China_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 China Open – Men's singles"). This was Alcaraz's third encounter with Sinner in 2024, and his third victory. With this win, Alcaraz reclaimed the world No. 2 ranking from [Alexander Zverev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Zverev "Alexander Zverev"). He also became the first player in ATP Tour history to win an [ATP 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_500 "ATP 500") singles title on every surface – [clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_court "Clay court"), [grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grass_court "Grass court") and [hardcourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcourt "Hardcourt"). However, he delivered an otherwise shaky autumn for the third year in a row. He lost in the quarterfinals of the [Shanghai Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Shanghai_Masters_(tennis) "2024 Shanghai Masters (tennis)") to [Tomáš Macháč](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Mach%C3%A1%C4%8D "Tomáš Macháč"), and in the round of 16 at the [Paris Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Rolex_Paris_Masters "2024 Rolex Paris Masters") to [Ugo Humbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugo_Humbert "Ugo Humbert"). He was unwell during the [ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_ATP_Finals "2024 ATP Finals") but did not withdraw, beating [Andrey Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Rublev "Andrey Rublev") but losing to [Casper Ruud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casper_Ruud "Casper Ruud") and Alexander Zverev in straight sets to exit the tournament at the round robin stage. Alcaraz reunited with the [Spanish Davis Cup team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_Davis_Cup_team "Spain Davis Cup team") in [Málaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1laga "Málaga") for the quarterfinals. They were knocked out by the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Davis_Cup_team "Netherlands Davis Cup team"), marking the end of [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal "Rafael Nadal")'s career. Alcaraz became the first man to finish a season ranked as low as world No. 3 after winning two majors.

### 2025: French and US Open titles, return to No. 1

Alcaraz began his 2025 season at the [2025 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 Australian Open – Men's singles"), where he was vying to become the youngest man to complete a [Career Grand Slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Grand_Slam_men%27s_singles_champions#Career_Grand_Slam "List of Grand Slam men's singles champions"). However, he was defeated by [Novak Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic") in the quarterfinals, in what was widely analysed as a nervous match from his side of the net. Alcaraz opted to play on European [hardcourts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcourt "Hardcourt") in February for the first time in his career. He won the first indoor hardcourt title of his career at the [Rotterdam Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_ABN_AMRO_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 ABN AMRO Open – Singles"), where he defeated [Alex de Minaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_de_Minaur "Alex de Minaur") in the final. At the [Qatar Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Qatar_ExxonMobil_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 Qatar ExxonMobil Open – Singles"), Alcaraz lost in the quarterfinals to [Jiří Lehečka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ji%C5%99%C3%AD_Lehe%C4%8Dka "Jiří Lehečka"), despite breaking Lehečka's serve first in every set. As the two-time defending champion at [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_BNP_Paribas_Open "2025 BNP Paribas Open"), Alcaraz lost to [Jack Draper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Draper "Jack Draper") in the semifinals despite [bagelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagel_(tennis) "Bagel (tennis)") him in the second set. This was Alcaraz's first loss beyond the quarterfinal stage of a tournament in over a year. In dire form, Alcaraz then lost his first match in [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Miami_Open "2025 Miami Open") to world No. 55 [David Goffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Goffin "David Goffin"), marking just his third career opening-round defeat at an ATP 1000 tournament.

Alcaraz's clay season was much more successful. He began it at the [Monte-Carlo Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Monte-Carlo_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 Monte-Carlo Masters – Singles"), a tournament where he had previously never won a match. Still initially in shaky form, Alcaraz fought through a tight three-setter with [Arthur Fils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Fils "Arthur Fils") in the quarterfinals. In the final, he defeated [Lorenzo Musetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Musetti "Lorenzo Musetti") to claim his first ATP Masters 1000 title in thirteen months. Alcaraz comfortably reached the final of the [Barcelona Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Barcelona_Open_Banc_Sabadell_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 Barcelona Open Banc Sabadell – Singles"), where he lost to [Holger Rune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holger_Rune "Holger Rune"). Following the final, he announced his withdrawal from the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 Mutua Madrid Open – Men's singles"), with injuries to both legs. Alcaraz defeated world No. 1 [Jannik Sinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jannik_Sinner "Jannik Sinner") to claim his first [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 Italian Open – Men's singles") title. He became the third man to win every modern big title on clay (French Open, Monte-Carlo, Madrid, and Rome) after [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal "Rafael Nadal") and [Novak Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic").

Alcaraz met Sinner for a second consecutive big [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles_final "2025 French Open – Men's singles final") at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 French Open – Men's singles"). After winning the first two sets, Sinner had three [championship points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_tennis_terms#Match_point "Glossary of tennis terms") on Alcaraz's serve in the fourth set. Alcaraz held from 0–40 and then broke Sinner's own serve to win the set. This forced a fifth set, which Alcaraz won with a dominant [super tiebreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_tiebreak "Super tiebreak"), thereby claiming his fifth major title. At 5 hours 29 minutes, it was the longest French Open final ever played, and the second longest major final of all time. It was also the first time that Alcaraz had rallied from two sets to love down, and he became the ninth male player to achieve this feat to win a major singles final.. Several writers and sports analysts declared it one of the greatest matches of all time.

Alcaraz began his grass season at the [Queen's Club Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Queen%27s_Club_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 Queen's Club Championships – Men's singles"). His semifinal win against [Roberto Bautista Agut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Bautista_Agut "Roberto Bautista Agut") was his 250th ATP victory; he became the third-fastest man to achieve this milestone in the Open Era. Alcaraz defeated [Jiří Lehečka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ji%C5%99%C3%AD_Lehe%C4%8Dka "Jiří Lehečka") in the final to claim his third consecutive title, and fifth title of the season. In the first round of [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles"), Alcaraz was unexpectedly taken to five sets by [Fabio Fognini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabio_Fognini "Fabio Fognini"), who had already announced his retirement and had not won a match all season. Alcaraz dropped a set each to [Jan-Lennard Struff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan-Lennard_Struff "Jan-Lennard Struff"), [Andrey Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Rublev "Andrey Rublev"), and [Taylor Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Fritz "Taylor Fritz"), but ultimately still reached his sixth consecutive final. He lost to [Jannik Sinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jannik_Sinner "Jannik Sinner") in four sets, his first loss in a slam final and his first loss to Sinner since 2023. This also ended Alcaraz's 24-match win streak, the longest of his career.

Alcaraz and Sinner faced off in yet another big final at the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Cincinnati_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 Cincinnati Open – Men's singles"). Sinner struggled physically, and retired while Alcaraz was leading 5–0 in the first set. Alcaraz therefore claimed his third ATP 1000 title of the season. At the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 US Open – Men's singles"), Alcaraz swept through the first six rounds of competition, [bagelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagel_(tennis) "Bagel (tennis)") both [Mattia Bellucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattia_Bellucci "Mattia Bellucci") and [Luciano Darderi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luciano_Darderi "Luciano Darderi") on the way. In the semifinal, he recorded his first win over [Novak Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic") on [hardcourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcourt "Hardcourt"). Alcaraz reached the final without dropping a single set, the first man to do so at the US Open since [Roger Federer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Federer "Roger Federer") in 2015. This was his eighth consecutive tour-level final, and for a third consecutive slam final, he faced [Jannik Sinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jannik_Sinner "Jannik Sinner"). Alcaraz defeated him in four sets to claim his sixth major in one of the most dominant runs of all time. He became the fourth and youngest man in the [Open Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Era "Open Era") to win multiple slam titles on each surface. With this victory, Alcaraz also regained the [world No. 1 ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_rankings "ATP rankings") for the first time in two years.

Alcaraz then made his debut at the [Japan Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Japan_Open_Tennis_Championships_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 Japan Open Tennis Championships – Singles"). In the first set of his first round match against [Sebastián Báez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A1n_B%C3%A1ez "Sebastián Báez"), he twisted his ankle. Despite the injury, he kept playing to reach his ninth consecutive final, where he faced [Taylor Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Fritz "Taylor Fritz"). Having lost to Fritz at the [Laver Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Laver_Cup "2025 Laver Cup") just 10 days prior, Alcaraz swept past him to secure his eighth title of the year. Alcaraz then announced his withdrawal from the [Shanghai Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Rolex_Shanghai_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 Rolex Shanghai Masters – Singles") due to injury. He lost his first match at the [Paris Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Rolex_Paris_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 Rolex Paris Masters – Singles") to [Cameron Norrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Norrie "Cameron Norrie") in three sets, ending his streak of consecutive finals. At the [ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_ATP_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 ATP Finals – Singles"), Alcaraz defeated [Alex de Minaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_de_Minaur "Alex de Minaur"), Taylor Fritz, and [Lorenzo Musetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Musetti "Lorenzo Musetti") to top his round robin group. With the win against Musetti, he clinched the year-end No. 1 ranking for the second time. Alcaraz defeated [Félix Auger-Aliassime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Auger-Aliassime "Félix Auger-Aliassime") in the semifinal, making only three unforced errors in the first set. He progressed to yet another final against [Jannik Sinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jannik_Sinner "Jannik Sinner"), which he lost in straight sets. Alcaraz then announced his withdrawal from the [Davis Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Davis_Cup_Finals "2025 Davis Cup Finals") due to a hamstring injury, ending his season.

### 2026: Australia title, Career Grand Slam, injury

During the December preseason, Alcaraz parted ways with longtime coach [Juan Carlos Ferrero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Carlos_Ferrero "Juan Carlos Ferrero"). Alcaraz began his 2026 season at the [2026 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2026 Australian Open – Men's singles"), where he reached the semifinals without dropping a set. In the semifinal against [Alexander Zverev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Zverev "Alexander Zverev"), he won the first two sets before struggling due to cramps. After dropping the third and fourth sets in a pair of tiebreaks, he resurged to win the decider. At 5 hours and 27 minutes, this was the longest Australian Open semifinal and third-longest overall match in tournament history. Alcaraz became the youngest man in the [Open Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_tennis#Open_Era "History of tennis") to reach the final of all four majors, breaking the record set by [Jim Courier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Courier "Jim Courier") in 1993. He then defeated [Novak Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic") to clinch his first Australian Open title, becoming the youngest player in history to complete the [career Grand Slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis)#Career_Grand_Slam "Grand Slam (tennis)") in men's singles.

Alcaraz withdrew from the [Rotterdam Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_ABN_AMRO_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2026 ABN AMRO Open – Singles"), citing tiredness, and resumed his season at the [Qatar Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Qatar_ExxonMobil_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles "2026 Qatar ExxonMobil Open – Singles"). He dropped just one set, to [Karen Khachanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Khachanov "Karen Khachanov") in the quarterfinal, on his way to the final. He demolished [Arthur Fils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Fils "Arthur Fils") in 50 minutes, the fastest completed match of his career to date, to claim his second title of the year. Alcaraz then returned to [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2026 BNP Paribas Open – Men's singles"). His quarterfinal victory against [Cameron Norrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Norrie "Cameron Norrie") was his 34th consecutive win on outdoor [hardcourts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcourt "Hardcourt") - the third longest-such streak in the Open Era, tied with [Pete Sampras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Sampras "Pete Sampras"). In the semifinal, Alcaraz met [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev "Daniil Medvedev") for a rematch of the 2023 and 2024 finals, where he lost in straight sets. At the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2026 Miami Open – Men's singles"), Alcaraz was upset by [Sebastian Korda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Korda "Sebastian Korda") in the third round, concluding a disappointing performance at the Sunshine Double tournaments.

Alcaraz reached the final at his first clay court tournament of the year in [Monte Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Monte-Carlo_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2026 Monte-Carlo Masters – Singles"), where he lost the match and the world No. 1 ranking to rival [Jannik Sinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jannik_Sinner "Jannik Sinner"). A few days later he played his first match at the [Barcelona Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Barcelona_Open_Banc_Sabadell_%E2%80%93_Singles "2026 Barcelona Open Banc Sabadell – Singles"), during which he received a medical timeout to treat his forearm. After the match he withdrew from the tournament, citing an unspecified wrist injury later identified as [tenosynovitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenosynovitis "Tenosynovitis"). He also withdrew from the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2026 Mutua Madrid Open – Men's singles"). At the end of April, he announced his withdrawal from the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Italian_Open "2026 Italian Open") and the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_French_Open "2026 French Open"), both tournaments where he was defending champion. In May, he announced his withdrawal from the [Queen's Club Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Queen%27s_Club_Championships "2026 Queen's Club Championships") and [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Wimbledon_Championships "2026 Wimbledon Championships"), where he was defending champion and runner-up, respectively. He did not enter the [Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2026 National Bank Open – Men's singles"), and withdrew from the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Cincinnati_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2026 Cincinnati Open – Men's singles"), where he was defending champion.

On August 20, Alcaraz announced he would return to competition at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_US_Open_(tennis) "2026 US Open (tennis)"), where he was also defending champion. He entered the mixed doubles draw in a high-profile partnership with [Serena Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serena_Williams "Serena Williams"); they lost in the second round. Alcaraz played his first singles match in five months against [Roman Safiullin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Safiullin "Roman Safiullin") and won in straight sets, although his average serve speed was noticeably reduced. Alcaraz reached his fifth career quarterfinals at the US Open, losing one set on the way, to [Jaime Faria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaime_Faria "Jaime Faria"). He extended his winning streak at the majors to a personal best 18 matches. Alcaraz then lost to [Ben Shelton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Shelton "Ben Shelton") in the quarterfinals in a fifth set [super tiebreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_tiebreak "Super tiebreak"), with Shelton becoming the second player after [Matteo Berrettini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Berrettini "Matteo Berrettini") to defeat Alcaraz in five sets.

After helping Team Europe win the [Laver Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Laver_Cup "2026 Laver Cup"), Alcaraz returned to the [Japan Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Japan_Open_Tennis_Championships "2026 Japan Open Tennis Championships"). Despite a persistent rustiness on serve, he defeated [Jiří Lehečka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ji%C5%99%C3%AD_Lehe%C4%8Dka "Jiří Lehečka") in straight sets to defend his title. He finished the tournament with a 10–0 career record in Tokyo. It was his tenth [ATP 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_500 "ATP 500") title, making him the fourth man to reach that total, after [Roger Federer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Federer "Roger Federer"), [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal "Rafael Nadal") and [Novak Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic").

## Rivalries

### Jannik Sinner

Carlos Alcaraz and [Jannik Sinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jannik_Sinner "Jannik Sinner") have faced each other 17 times on the ATP Tour, with Alcaraz leading 10–7.

Notable early matches include the [2022 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 US Open – Men's singles") quarterfinal, which Alcaraz won en route to the title in five sets after [saving a match point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Grand_Slam_and_related_tennis_records#Won_a_title_after_saving_match_points "List of Grand Slam and related tennis records"). The pair met three times in 2024, with Alcaraz winning all three matches, including the tightly contested final of the [China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_China_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 China Open – Men's singles"). Sinner lost as many matches to Alcaraz in 2024 as he did to all other players on tour combined.

In 2025, Alcaraz and Sinner met in three consecutive major finals. Alcaraz won their [first major final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles_final "2025 French Open – Men's singles final") at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open "2025 French Open"), despite Sinner holding three [championship points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_tennis_terms#Match_point "Glossary of tennis terms") in the fourth set. *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* acclaimed it as "one of the greatest finals ever played, in any sport." They met again at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles"), where Sinner won in four sets, dethroning defending champion Alcaraz; and at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 US Open – Men's singles"), where Alcaraz won in four sets, dethroning defending champion Sinner. For a second consecutive year, they split the season's slams at two apiece.

They also contested two ATP Masters 1000 finals in 2025, at the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 Italian Open – Men's singles") and the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Cincinnati_Open "2025 Cincinnati Open"), both won by Alcaraz; and the final of the [2025 ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_ATP_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 ATP Finals – Singles"), where Sinner defeated Alcaraz in straight sets. Sinner defeated Alcaraz in straight sets to claim the title at the [Monte-Carlo Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Monte-Carlo_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2026 Monte-Carlo Masters – Singles") in their only meeting in 2026. Their rivalry has been described as "potentially era-defining".

### Alexander Zverev

Carlos Alcaraz and [Alexander Zverev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Zverev "Alexander Zverev") have faced off thirteen times, with Alcaraz leading 7–6.

They met twice in 2021, with Zverev dismantling a 17–18 year old Alcaraz in both matches. Their first notable match was the final of the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 Mutua Madrid Open – Men's singles") in 2022, where Alcaraz swept past defending champion Zverev in one hour and four minutes. A few weeks later they had their first major meeting in the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 French Open – Men's singles") quarterfinals, where Zverev defeated Alcaraz in four sets. They had further high profile encounters in the quarterfinals of the [2023 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 US Open – Men's singles"), won by Alcaraz, and the quarterfinals of the [2024 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 Australian Open – Men's singles"), won by Zverev.

In 2024 they met for the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 French Open – Men's singles") final, where Zverev was hunting for his first major title. Zverev took a two-sets-to-one lead, but Alcaraz outlasted him to win in five sets and claim his first French Open title.

In the semifinals of the [2026 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2026 Australian Open – Men's singles"), Alcaraz took a two-set lead before struggling due to cramps. Zverev won the next two sets in a pair of tiebreaks, but Alcaraz recovered in time to win the fifth set, proceeding to his maiden Australian Open final. At 5 hours and 27 minutes, this was the longest Australian Open semifinal in history.

### Novak Djokovic

Carlos Alcaraz and [Novak Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic") have faced off ten times; despite a 16-year age gap, their rivalry has remained competitive, with the head-to-head tied at 5–5. Nine of their ten matches have been in the semifinals or finals of "big" tournaments: at all four majors, two ATP Masters 1000, the ATP Finals and the Olympics.

Their first meeting was at the [2022 Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2022 Mutua Madrid Open – Men's singles") semifinals, where Alcaraz prevailed in a deciding set tiebreaker, becoming the only man ever to secure back-to-back victories against Nadal and Djokovic on clay. Despite their ongoing fight over the world No. 1 ranking, they did not meet again for over a year. They finally faced off in the semifinals of the [2023 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 French Open – Men's singles"), a highly anticipated showdown heavily promoted by the media. Djokovic won in four sets, with the match competitive until Alcaraz faltered at the start of the third set due to cramps.

The duo soon met for a rematch in the [2023 Wimbledon final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles_final "2023 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles final"), in which Alcaraz defeated four-time defending champion Djokovic in an epic five-setter that lasted 4 hours and 42 minutes. One month later, they fought another epic at the [2023 Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Western_%26_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2023 Western & Southern Open – Men's singles") final, with Djokovic prevailing in three tightly contested sets after saving a championship point. At 3 hours and 49 minutes, this was the longest best-of-three-sets [ATP Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Tour "ATP Tour") final in history, the longest match in the tournament's history, and was immediately heralded as one of the best matches ever. They then played a relatively uncompetitive match at the [ATP Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_ATP_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles "2023 ATP Finals – Singles"), where Djokovic overpowered Alcaraz in straight sets.

Djokovic and Alcaraz met twice in the summer of 2024, both in high-profile finals. On July 14, Alcaraz cruised to victory in straight sets against Djokovic in [a rematch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles_final "2024 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles final") of the previous year's Wimbledon final. Three weeks later, on August 4, Djokovic defeated Alcaraz in [the Olympics final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "Tennis at the 2024 Summer Olympics – Men's singles") on [Court Philippe Chatrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_Philippe_Chatrier "Court Philippe Chatrier"). Neither player was broken on serve across two sets, with Djokovic winning both tiebreaks. Sports historian [Steve Flink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Flink "Steve Flink") called it "the best two-set match I have ever seen". *[Tennis.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_(magazine) "Tennis (magazine)")* listed it as its ATP match of the year for 2024.

Djokovic and Alcaraz met twice in 2025. In their first quarterfinal-level meeting at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Australian_Open "2025 Australian Open"), Djokovic prevailed in four sets to retain his unbeaten record against Alcaraz on hardcourt and deny his hopes of achieving the [Career Grand Slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis)#Career_Grand_Slam "Grand Slam (tennis)"). They met for a rematch in the semifinals of the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2025 US Open – Men's singles"), where Alcaraz took revenge in straight sets to record his first win over Djokovic on hardcourt. Tickets to the match were the most expensive in US Open history. With this match, Alcaraz and Djokovic completed a set of meetings at each of the four majors, something that the [Federer–Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federer%E2%80%93Nadal_rivalry "Federer–Nadal rivalry") rivalry never achieved.

Djokovic and Alcaraz have met once in 2026, in the final of the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2026 Australian Open – Men's singles"), which Alcaraz won in four sets to end Djokovic's undefeated 10–0 record in Australian Open finals and become the youngest male player to complete the Career Grand Slam. Notably, Alcaraz (along with [Stan Wawrinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stan_Wawrinka "Stan Wawrinka")) is one of two tennis players to hold a perfect major final record against Djokovic.

## Playing style

Alcaraz is an [all-court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-court_player "All-court player") tennis player, but primarily employs an aggressive baseline style of play. He is known for his exceptional court coverage and defensive retrieval ability, often turning defensive positions into offensive opportunities during rallies. His straight-armed [forehand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forehand "Forehand") is typically his most potent shot: he can either hit it flat and fast for winners from every court position, or add a great amount of topspin and margin over the net. He plays with a flatter and lower two-handed [backhand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backhand "Backhand"). Due to his aggression, Alcaraz typically generates a high count of both [winners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_tennis_terms#winner "Glossary of tennis terms") and [unforced errors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_tennis_terms#unforced_error "Glossary of tennis terms") off his groundstrokes, particularly his forehand.

Alcaraz has a powerful first serve for his height; commonly around 115 to 120 mph and as high as 135 mph, but hit with average placement. Alcaraz often adds topspin to his second serve in order to generate a high bounce, which either pushes his opponents back or forces a weak return. This serve typically reaches 150 to 170 km/h (93 to 106 mph). Due mostly to its lack of targeted placement, Alcaraz's serve is often described as a relative weak spot in his game. He is elite as a returner, particularly of first serves. In 2024, he won nearly a third of his opponents' service games.

Alcaraz has an impressive net-game with excellent drop volleys and drive volleys, and frequently serves-and-volleys on crucial points. His greatly disguised [drop shot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drop_shot "Drop shot") is key to his game. The heaviness of his groundstrokes pushes his opponents to the back of the court, which then makes his drop shots difficult to reach. His slight preference for forehand over backhand drop shots has been noted as unusual. [Andy Roddick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Roddick "Andy Roddick") has said that the Alcaraz drop shot is "the best in the history of tennis". Some players and commentators have credited Alcaraz as responsible for rising drop shot usage across both professional tours in recent years.

Alcaraz has earned acclaim for his athletic and physical traits. His direct sprints, counterattacking abilities, and an extremely high peak [footspeed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Footspeed "Footspeed") have garnered him comparisons to [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal "Rafael Nadal"). He has been compared to [Novak Djokovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novak_Djokovic "Novak Djokovic") for his assured lateral movement and court-coverage, aided by physical splits and sliding through the court on defense, particularly on the backhand side. Like [Roger Federer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Federer "Roger Federer"), exceptional touch and shotmaking have been crucial to his success. Djokovic has said Alcaraz combines "the best of all three worlds" from his game, Federer's, and Nadal's.

Alcaraz is frequently lauded for his on-court creativity and showmanship. He has been described many times as a "human highlight reel". In 2023, he said, "Obviously, I want to win every match that I play, but at the same time, I want to have fun, try different things, make the people enjoy watching tennis and watching my matches. Sometimes I talk to myself about what is most important: if I win or doing great things."

## Coaches and team

Alcaraz's childhood coaches were Kiko Navarro and Carlos Santos. In September 2018, fifteen-year-old Alcaraz moved to [Villena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villena "Villena") to begin training at the [Ferrero Tennis Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrero_Tennis_Academy "Ferrero Tennis Academy") under the direction of [Juan Carlos Ferrero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Carlos_Ferrero "Juan Carlos Ferrero"), who became his personal coach. Ferrero turned down several coaching requests from top players in order to coach Alcaraz. Their partnership was widely praised: Ferrero won the [ATP Coach of the Year Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Awards "ATP Awards") in 2022 and 2025. In December 2025, Alcaraz unexpectedly announced that he and Ferrero had parted ways after seven years.

In December 2024, it was announced that [Samuel López](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_L%C3%B3pez_(tennis_coach) "Samuel López (tennis coach)"), a former member of Ferrero's own coaching team, would be joining Alcaraz's team to work under Ferrero as a second coach. Following Ferrero's departure, he became Alcaraz's head coach for the 2026 season.

Alcaraz has maintained largely the same staff since he arrived on tour. His team includes physical trainer Alberto Lledó, physiotherapist Juanjo Moreno, doctor Juanjo López, and agent Albert Molina. His brother Álvaro works as his longtime [hitting partner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitting_partner "Hitting partner") and, as of 2026, assistant coach. In 2025, Alcaraz appointed childhood friend Fran Rubio as an additional physiotherapist. Alcaraz also works with sports psychologist Isabel Balaguer.

## Off the court

Alcaraz is sponsored by [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc. "Nike, Inc.") for clothing and shoes, and by [Babolat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babolat "Babolat") for racquets. In February 2026, Alcaraz's longtime sponsor Babolat announced a commercial range of "Carlitos Junior" racquets and bags for young tennis players. ### In the media and popular culture

Alcaraz made appearances in both seasons of Netflix's *[Break Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Break_Point_(2023_TV_series) "Break Point (2023 TV series)")* docuseries, and played against [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal "Rafael Nadal") in *The Netflix Slam*, a live-streamed tennis match hosted by MGM Resorts International on March 3, 2024. Alcaraz was the subject of the [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") show *[Carlos Alcaraz: My Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Alcaraz:_My_Way "Carlos Alcaraz: My Way")*, which documented his 2024 season and was released April 23, 2025. The three-part series focused on conflict between Alcaraz and his team as he attempted to balance his ambition to make tennis history with the normal life of a 21-year-old. It included appearances by Rafael Nadal, [Roger Federer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Federer "Roger Federer"), [Garbiñe Muguruza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garbi%C3%B1e_Muguruza "Garbiñe Muguruza"), [Martina Navratilova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martina_Navratilova "Martina Navratilova"), [Andre Agassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andre_Agassi "Andre Agassi"), [Björn Borg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B6rn_Borg "Björn Borg"), and [John McEnroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McEnroe "John McEnroe") alongside other tennis greats.

In April 2025, Barney Ronay for *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* described Alcaraz as "arguably the most interesting athlete in the world right now". He is known for his wide smile and charisma on the court. He has received significant media attention off the court for his personal life, particularly his trips to [Ibiza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibiza "Ibiza"). Alcaraz's style choices, particularly his unplanned [buzz cut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzz_cut "Buzz cut") at the [2025 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_US_Open_(tennis) "2025 US Open (tennis)") and his subsequent blonde dye job, have also generated outsize media attention. He is known for receiving tattoos to commemorate his slam victories. The *[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* selected him as one of its "most stylish people" of 2025, and *[GQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GQ "GQ")* as one of the most stylish male tennis players of all time. At the [2026 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_US_Open_(tennis) "2026 US Open (tennis)"), [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc. "Nike, Inc.") launched the [Cactus Jack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cactus_Jack_Records "Cactus Jack Records") x Carlos Alcaraz Performance Collection in collaboration with Alcaraz and rapper [Travis Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_Scott "Travis Scott"), which Alcaraz wore on court.

In 2024, a media controversy arose when Alcaraz publicly broke a racket for the first time during a match in [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Cincinnati_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 Cincinnati Open – Men's singles"). He afterwards delivered a public apology. Alcaraz is also known for his willingness to discuss mental health with the media. After his loss in the [2024 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles "2024 US Open – Men's singles"), he stated, "I've taken steps backwards, like I'm not doing well mentally, I'm not strong. I don't know how to control myself when faced with problems and I don't know how to handle it." Following a loss at [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_BNP_Paribas_Open "2025 BNP Paribas Open") in 2025, he admitted to having felt "nervous the whole day", and following a win in [Monte Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Monte-Carlo_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles "2025 Monte-Carlo Masters – Singles") a month later, that he had been "struggling stepping on the court" and had considered taking an extended break from tennis. Various commentators, including [Andy Roddick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Roddick "Andy Roddick"), [Boris Becker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Becker "Boris Becker"), and [Andrea Petkovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Petkovic "Andrea Petkovic"), have highlighted his unusual candidness about mental health.

Alcaraz has often advocated for professional tennis players' welfare. In 2024, he spoke out several times against the length of the tennis calendar, stressing the frequency of injuries and joking that "probably they are going to kill us in some way". In 2025, he supported fellow Spanish player [Sara Sorribes Tormo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Sorribes_Tormo "Sara Sorribes Tormo")'s decision to take a break from the sport for her health. He also signed two letters alongside other top men's and women's tennis players asking that the four major tournaments distribute a greater share of their revenue to all competing players. In 2026, after returning from injury, he once again criticised the tennis schedule and announced his intention to take one or two month breaks during future seasons.

Alcaraz has often been compared to his compatriot Rafael Nadal, particularly in their native Spain. As a teenager, he was dubbed "baby Rafa" or "young Nadal". However, he and Nadal have both dismissed these comparisons at length.

Alcaraz was featured on the [Time 100 Next](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100_Next "Time 100 Next") list in 2022. In December 2025, he was included on the *50 Names in the [Boardroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boardroom_(company) "Boardroom (company)")* list.

### Philanthropy

In April 2024, Alcaraz announced the launch of the Carlos Alcaraz Garfia Foundation, with the aim of improving the lives of disadvantaged children. It is headquartered in [El Palmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Palmar,_Murcia "El Palmar, Murcia") and operates in partnership with local schools. Alcaraz's foundation provided buses to transport volunteers after the [flash floods in Valencia in 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Spanish_floods "2024 Spanish floods"), during which Alcaraz and [Juan Carlos Ferrero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Carlos_Ferrero "Juan Carlos Ferrero") also ran a charity auction to raise funds for those affected. On December 12, 2024, Alcaraz inaugurated his foundation's exhibition "Los Pies en la Tierra" in Murcia, highlighting the importance of equal opportunities for children. The exhibition travelled to other areas of Spain, including Madrid and Seville, in 2025 and 2026.
