# Monster: The Lizzie Borden Story

***Monster: The Lizzie Borden Story*** is the fourth season of the American [crime drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_drama "Crime drama") television series [*Monster*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monster_(American_TV_series) "Monster (American TV series)"), created by [Ian Brennan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Brennan_(writer) "Ian Brennan (writer)") for [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix"). The season centers on murder suspect [Lizzie Borden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lizzie_Borden "Lizzie Borden") ([Ella Beatty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ella_Beatty "Ella Beatty")) who was tried and acquitted of the 1892 axe murders of her father and stepmother in [Fall River, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_River,_Massachusetts "Fall River, Massachusetts"). It also stars [Vicky Krieps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicky_Krieps "Vicky Krieps"), [Charlie Hunnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Hunnam "Charlie Hunnam"), and [Rebecca Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_Hall "Rebecca Hall"), alongside [Jessica Barden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Barden "Jessica Barden"), [Joey Pollari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_Pollari "Joey Pollari"), [Billie Lourd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billie_Lourd "Billie Lourd"), and [Sarah Paulson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Paulson "Sarah Paulson") in recurring roles.

It is the fourth installment in the *Monster* [anthology series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthology_series "Anthology series"), following *[Monster: The Jeffrey Dahmer Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monster:_The_Jeffrey_Dahmer_Story "Monster: The Jeffrey Dahmer Story")* (2022), *[Monsters: The Lyle and Erik Menendez Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsters:_The_Lyle_and_Erik_Menendez_Story "Monsters: The Lyle and Erik Menendez Story")* (2024), and *[Monster: The Ed Gein Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monster:_The_Ed_Gein_Story "Monster: The Ed Gein Story")* (2025). The season is the first in the anthology to center on a female protagonist, a person who was never convicted of the crimes they were accused of, and a story not primarily set in the 20th century. Brennan, who also served as the writer of all eight episodes, said that the season's focus on Borden provided an opportunity to explore themes of [sexism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexism "Sexism") and gender inequality.

Upon its premiere on September 17, 2026, the season received mixed to negative reviews from critics, who praised the performances and production design but criticized its historical liberties and fictionalized portrayals of Borden and other historical figures.

## Premise

On August 4, 1892, the Borden spouses, Andrew and Abby, are found hacked to death with a hatchet in their home in [Fall River, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_River,_Massachusetts "Fall River, Massachusetts"). Their daughter/stepdaughter, [Lizzie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lizzie_Borden "Lizzie Borden"), became the prime suspect in the murders.

## Production

The season was first announced to be in development on July 8, 2025. [Ella Beatty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ella_Beatty "Ella Beatty") was cast as Lizzie Borden later that month, alongside [Rebecca Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_Hall "Rebecca Hall") and [Vicky Krieps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicky_Krieps "Vicky Krieps"), while [Charlie Hunnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Hunnam "Charlie Hunnam") joined the cast in September. Principal photography began in Los Angeles in September 2025 and wrapped in March 2026.

According to creator [Ian Brennan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Brennan_(writer) "Ian Brennan (writer)"), the season explores misogyny, gender roles, and societal expectations placed on women in the [Victorian era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_era "Victorian era"). It also examines class and sexuality themes through the relationship between Borden and Bridget Sullivan.

## Release

The season was released globally on [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix"), on September 17, 2026.

## Reception

### Audience viewership

*The Lizzie Borden Story* became the third season of the anthology series to debut at number one on Netflix's global weekly chart, with 12.1 million views (or 80.1 million hours viewed) within four days of its release, although it was the lowest debut in the anthology. It garnered 14.5 million views (or 95.9 million hours viewed) in its second week, representing a 20% increase in viewership from the previous week, but still remaining below the second-week viewership of its predecessors.

### Critical response

The [review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator "Review aggregator") website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes") reported a 38% approval rating, based on 29 critic reviews, with an average rating of 5.0/10. The website's consensus reads: "[Ella Beatty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ella_Beatty "Ella Beatty"), [Sarah Paulson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Paulson "Sarah Paulson"), and [Rebecca Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_Hall "Rebecca Hall") remain trapped in an ill-fitted, uninspiring new rendition of *Monster* that doesn't bother to give *The Lizzie Borden Story* anything interesting to say." [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), which uses a weighted average, gave a score of 47 out of 100, based on 15 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.

In her review for *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")*, [Lucy Mangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucy_Mangan "Lucy Mangan") gave the series 2 out of 5 stars, criticizing its focus on [menstruation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menstruation "Menstruation"), sexuality and [graphic violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphic_violence "Graphic violence") while arguing that it failed to explore [Lizzie Borden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lizzie_Borden "Lizzie Borden"), the social conditions of the period and the circumstances surrounding her acquittal. She praised the performances, particularly Rebecca Hall's portrayal of [Abby Borden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abby_Borden "Abby Borden"). Henri Enoh Tchato of *Critical Registrar* gave the series 2.5 out of 5, praising the performances and production design, but criticizing its reliance on invented drama and sensationalism. In a similar review, Kaiya Shunyata of [RogerEbert.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RogerEbert.com "RogerEbert.com") praised the series' performances, [production design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_designer "Production designer") and its stories involving [Elizabeth Báthory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_B%C3%A1thory "Elizabeth Báthory") and [Aileen Wuornos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aileen_Wuornos "Aileen Wuornos"), but criticized its portrayal of Lizzie Borden and its fictionalized treatment of the historical case. However, Matthew Creith of *[TheWrap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TheWrap "TheWrap")* described the series as a highly stylized mixture of gothic horror, sexual melodrama and dark comedy, while criticizing the series for its historical liberties and its inclusion of Aileen Wuornos.
