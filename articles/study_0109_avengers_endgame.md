# Avengers: Endgame

***Avengers: Endgame*** is a 2019 American [superhero film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superhero_film "Superhero film") based on the [Marvel Comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Comics "Marvel Comics") superhero team the [Avengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers_(comics) "Avengers (comics)"). Produced by [Marvel Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Studios "Marvel Studios") and distributed by [Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Studios_Motion_Pictures "Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures"), it is the [direct sequel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_sequel "Direct sequel") to *[Avengers: Infinity War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers:_Infinity_War "Avengers: Infinity War")* (2018) and [the 22nd film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Marvel_Cinematic_Universe_films "List of Marvel Cinematic Universe films") in the [Marvel Cinematic Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe "Marvel Cinematic Universe") (MCU). Directed by [Anthony and Joe Russo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_and_Joe_Russo "Anthony and Joe Russo") and written by [Christopher Markus and Stephen McFeely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Markus_and_Stephen_McFeely "Christopher Markus and Stephen McFeely"), the film features an [ensemble cast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensemble_cast "Ensemble cast") that includes [Robert Downey Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Downey_Jr. "Robert Downey Jr."), [Chris Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Evans_(actor) "Chris Evans (actor)"), [Mark Ruffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Ruffalo "Mark Ruffalo"), [Chris Hemsworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Hemsworth "Chris Hemsworth"), [Scarlett Johansson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarlett_Johansson "Scarlett Johansson"), [Jeremy Renner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Renner "Jeremy Renner"), [Don Cheadle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Cheadle "Don Cheadle"), [Paul Rudd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Rudd "Paul Rudd"), [Brie Larson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brie_Larson "Brie Larson"), [Karen Gillan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Gillan "Karen Gillan"), [Danai Gurira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danai_Gurira "Danai Gurira"), [Benedict Wong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedict_Wong "Benedict Wong"), [Jon Favreau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Favreau "Jon Favreau"), [Bradley Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradley_Cooper "Bradley Cooper"), [Gwyneth Paltrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwyneth_Paltrow "Gwyneth Paltrow"), and [Josh Brolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Brolin "Josh Brolin"). In the film, the surviving members of the [Avengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Avengers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") and their allies attempt to reverse [Thanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanos_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Thanos (Marvel Cinematic Universe)")'s actions in *Infinity War*, which erased half of all life in the universe.

The film was announced in October 2014 as *Avengers: Infinity War – Part 2*, but Marvel Studios later removed this title. The Russo brothers joined as directors in April 2015, with Markus and McFeely signing on to write the script a month later. It is a conclusion to the story of the MCU up to that point, ending several main characters' story arcs. The plot revisits moments from earlier films, bringing back actors and settings from throughout the franchise. Filming began in August 2017 at [Pinewood Atlanta Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinewood_Atlanta_Studios "Pinewood Atlanta Studios") in [Fayette County, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fayette_County,_Georgia "Fayette County, Georgia"), shooting [back-to-back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back-to-back_film_production "Back-to-back film production") with *Infinity War*, and ended in January 2018. Additional filming took place in the [Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Atlanta "Metro Atlanta") and [downtown Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Atlanta "Downtown Atlanta") areas, [New York state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_state "New York state"), [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland "Scotland"), and [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England "England"). The official title was announced in December 2018. With an estimated budget range of $356–400 million, *Endgame* is one of the [most expensive films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_expensive_films "List of most expensive films") ever produced.

*Avengers: Endgame* premiered at the [Los Angeles Convention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Convention_Center "Los Angeles Convention Center") on April 22, 2019, and was released in the United States on April 26 as part of [Phase Three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe:_Phase_Three "Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Three") of the MCU. The film received positive reviews from critics, with praise for its direction, acting, musical score, action scenes, visual effects, and emotional weight, with critics lauding its culmination of the 22-film story. It grossed $2.8 billion worldwide during its initial theatrical run, surpassing *Infinity War*'s entire theatrical run in eleven days, setting several [box-office records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_box_office_records_set_by_Avengers:_Endgame "List of box office records set by Avengers: Endgame") and becoming the [highest-grossing film of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_films "List of highest-grossing films") between 2019 and 2021, overtaking and later surpassed by *[Avatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avatar_(2009_film) "Avatar (2009 film)")* (2009). *Endgame* was nominated for [Best Visual Effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Visual_Effects "Academy Award for Best Visual Effects") at the [92nd Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/92nd_Academy_Awards "92nd Academy Awards"), among [numerous other accolades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_accolades_received_by_Avengers:_Endgame "List of accolades received by Avengers: Endgame"). An extended version, subtitled *[Encore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers_Endgame:_Encore "Avengers Endgame: Encore")*, was released in theaters in September 2026 in anticipation of the film's two sequels, *[Avengers: Doomsday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers:_Doomsday "Avengers: Doomsday")* and *[Avengers: Secret Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers:_Secret_Wars "Avengers: Secret Wars")*, which are respectively scheduled for release in December 2026 and 2027. The box office from *Encore* helped the film to once again become the highest-grossing film of all time, with a total gross of $2.938 billion.

## Plot

In 2018, 23 days after [Thanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanos_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Thanos (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") erased half of all life in the universe, [Carol Danvers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_Danvers_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Carol Danvers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") rescues [Tony Stark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Stark_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Tony Stark (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") and [Nebula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebula_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Nebula (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") from deep space. They reunite with the remaining [Avengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Avengers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)")—[Bruce Banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Banner_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Bruce Banner (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"), [Steve Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Rogers_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Steve Rogers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"), [Thor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thor_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Thor (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"), [Natasha Romanoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natasha_Romanoff_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Natasha Romanoff (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"), and [James Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Rhodes_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "James Rhodes (Marvel Cinematic Universe)")—and [Rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Rocket (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") on Earth. Finding Thanos on [an uninhabited planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet_0259-S "Planet 0259-S"), they plan to use the [Infinity Stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_Stones "Infinity Stones") to reverse his actions but learn that Thanos has destroyed them. Enraged, Thor decapitates Thanos.

Five years later, [Scott Lang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Lang_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Scott Lang (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") escapes from the [Quantum Realm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_Realm "Quantum Realm"). Reaching the [Avengers Compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers_Compound_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Avengers Compound (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"), he informs Rogers and Romanoff that he experienced only five hours while trapped. Theorizing that the Quantum Realm allows [time travel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_travel "Time travel"), they ask a reluctant Stark to help them retrieve the Stones from the past to reverse Thanos's present actions. Stark, Rocket, and Banner, who has merged his intelligence with the Hulk's strength, build a time machine. Banner theorizes that altering the past will not affect the present, since any changes will create [alternate realities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_universes_in_fiction "Parallel universes in fiction"). Banner and Rocket travel to [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway "Norway"), where they visit the [Asgardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asgardians_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Asgardians (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") refugee settlement of [New Asgard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Asgard "New Asgard") and recruit an overweight, despondent Thor. In Tokyo, Romanoff recruits [Clint Barton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clint_Barton_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Clint Barton (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"), who became a [vigilante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigilantism "Vigilantism") after his family was erased.

Banner, Lang, Rogers, and Stark time-travel to New York City during [Loki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loki_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Loki (Marvel Cinematic Universe)")'s attack in 2012. At the [Sanctum Sanctorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Sanctum "New York Sanctum"), Banner convinces the [Ancient One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_One_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Ancient One (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") to give him the [Time Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Stone "Time Stone") after promising to return the Infinity Stones to their proper points in time. At [Stark Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stark_Tower_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Stark Tower (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"), Rogers retrieves the [Mind Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind_Stone "Mind Stone") from [Hydra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydra_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Hydra (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") sleeper agents, but Stark and Lang's attempt to steal the [Space Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Stone "Space Stone") fails, allowing 2012-Loki to escape with it. Rogers and Stark travel to [Camp Lehigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Lehigh "Camp Lehigh") in 1970, where Stark obtains an earlier version of the Space Stone and encounters his father, [Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Stark_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Howard Stark (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"). Rogers steals [Pym Particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pym_Particles_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Pym Particles (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") from [Hank Pym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hank_Pym_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Hank Pym (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") to return to the present, and sees his lost love, [Peggy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peggy_Carter_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Peggy Carter (Marvel Cinematic Universe)").

Rocket and Thor travel to [Asgard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asgard_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Asgard (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") in 2013. Rocket extracts the [Reality Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reality_Stone "Reality Stone") from [Jane Foster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Foster_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Jane Foster (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"), while Thor is encouraged by his mother, [Frigga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frigga_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Frigga (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"), and retrieves his old hammer, [Mjolnir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mjolnir_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Mjolnir (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"). Barton, Romanoff, Nebula, and Rhodes travel to 2014. Nebula and Rhodes go to [Morag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morag_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Morag (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") and steal the [Power Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_Stone_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Power Stone (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") before [Peter Quill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Quill_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Peter Quill (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") can, while Barton and Romanoff travel to [Vormir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vormir_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Vormir (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"). The [Soul Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_Stone "Soul Stone")'s keeper, [Red Skull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Skull_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Red Skull (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"), says that it can be acquired only by sacrificing a loved one. Romanoff sacrifices herself, allowing Barton to get the Stone. Rhodes and Nebula attempt to return to their own time, but Nebula is incapacitated when her cybernetic implants link with her past self, allowing 2014-Thanos to learn about his future self's success and the Avengers' attempt to undo it. 2014-Thanos captures Nebula and sends 2014-Nebula forward in time to prepare for his arrival.

Reuniting in the present, the Avengers place the Stones in a gauntlet that Stark, Banner, and Rocket have built. Banner, who has the most resistance to radiation, uses the gauntlet to undo Thanos's disintegrations. Meanwhile, 2014-Nebula (impersonating her future self) uses the time machine to transport 2014-Thanos and [his warship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctuary_II_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Sanctuary II (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") to the present, where he destroys the Avengers Compound. Present-day Nebula convinces 2014-[Gamora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamora_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Gamora (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") to betray Thanos, but is unable to convince 2014-Nebula and kills her. Thanos overpowers Stark, Thor, and a Mjolnir-wielding Rogers. He summons his army to retrieve the Stones, intent on using them to destroy the universe and create a new one. A restored [Stephen Strange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Strange_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Stephen Strange (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") arrives with [other sorcerers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masters_of_the_Mystic_Arts "Masters of the Mystic Arts"), the restored Avengers, the [Guardians of the Galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardians_of_the_Galaxy_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Guardians of the Galaxy (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"), the [Ravagers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravagers_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Ravagers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"), and the armies of [Wakanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakanda_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Wakanda (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") and Asgard to fight Thanos's army. Danvers also arrives and destroys Thanos's warship, but Thanos overpowers her and seizes the gauntlet. Stark steals the Stones and uses them to disintegrate Thanos and his army, sacrificing himself.

After Stark's funeral, Thor appoints [Valkyrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valkyrie_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Valkyrie (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") as the new king of New Asgard and joins the Guardians. Rogers returns the Stones and Mjolnir to their proper timelines and remains in the past to live with Carter. In the present, an elderly Rogers passes [his shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_America%27s_shield_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Captain America's shield (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") to [Sam Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Wilson_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Sam Wilson (Marvel Cinematic Universe)").

## Cast

* [Robert Downey Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Downey_Jr. "Robert Downey Jr.") as [Tony Stark / Iron Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Stark_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Tony Stark (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"):
  The benefactor of the [Avengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Avengers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") who describes himself as a "genius, billionaire, playboy, and philanthropist" with [electromechanical suits of armor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Man%27s_armor_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Iron Man's armor (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") he made. According to directors [Joe and Anthony Russo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo_brothers "Russo brothers"), Downey was the only actor to receive the entire screenplay for the film. Screenwriters [Christopher Markus and Stephen McFeely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Markus_and_Stephen_McFeely "Christopher Markus and Stephen McFeely") knew that Stark's death was inevitable as a "[move] to selflessness" and as an end to the "chapter" Stark began. They felt that his death was earned after granting him "the perfect retirement life ... That's the life he's been striving for [...] They got married, they had a kid, it was great. It's a good death. It doesn't feel like a tragedy. It feels like a heroic, finished life." Joe Russo said that Stark "always knew he was going to die because he could never reconcile that notion in himself of not protecting the universe," and was the most defiant of the Avengers: "Stark is the most formidable of all of them [...] because of his heart." The Russos sought Downey's approval for Stark's arc, which they had developed since *[Captain America: Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_America:_Civil_War "Captain America: Civil War")* (2016).
* [Chris Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Evans_(actor) "Chris Evans (actor)") as [Steve Rogers / Captain America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Rogers_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Steve Rogers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"):
  The leader of the Avengers, [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") veteran and former fugitive, he was enhanced to the peak of human physicality by an experimental serum and frozen in suspended animation before waking up in the modern world. Markus described Rogers as someone who is "moving toward some sort of [enlightened self-interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlightened_self-interest "Enlightened self-interest")". McFeely knew that Rogers "was going to get his dance" that he promised [Peggy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peggy_Carter_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Peggy Carter (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") in *[Captain America: The First Avenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_America:_The_First_Avenger "Captain America: The First Avenger")* (2011). He initially planned to complete Rogers's story arc by killing him off, given the character's long life for his occupation, before deciding to have Rogers bequeath [his shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_America%27s_shield_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Captain America's shield (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") to Sam Wilson. [Patrick Gorman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Gorman_(American_actor) "Patrick Gorman (American actor)") provided an on-set reference for an elderly Steve Rogers.
* [Mark Ruffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Ruffalo "Mark Ruffalo") as [Bruce Banner / Hulk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Banner_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Bruce Banner (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"):
  An Avenger and genius scientist who, because of exposure to [gamma radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_radiation "Gamma radiation"), transforms into a monster when enraged or agitated. Banner balances his two sides with gamma experimentation in this film, enabling him to combine his intelligence with the Hulk's strength and physical stature based on his "Professor Hulk" comic-book identity. Compared to other heroes who were demotivated by their loss to Thanos, Banner is the only character to remain hopeful; according to Anthony Russo, "Banner is the sole character who is actually forging into a bright new future, trying to build something totally new and find something completely new [...] Banner is the one who is most heroic in a sense that he maintains his will to keep trying." This concludes a character arc that was established in *[Thor: Ragnarok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thor:_Ragnarok "Thor: Ragnarok")* (2017) and continued in *[Avengers: Infinity War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers:_Infinity_War "Avengers: Infinity War")* (2018).
* [Chris Hemsworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Hemsworth "Chris Hemsworth") as [Thor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thor_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Thor (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"):
  An Avenger and the king of [Asgard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asgard_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Asgard (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"), based on the [Norse mythological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norse_mythology "Norse mythology") deity [of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thor "Thor"). Thor now wields a mystical axe known as [Stormbreaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stormbreaker_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Stormbreaker (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") after the destruction of his hammer, [Mjolnir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mjolnir_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Mjolnir (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"), in *Thor: Ragnarok*. In the film, Thor has become an overweight, drunken ruler of Asgard's refugees in [Tønsberg, Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%B8nsberg "Tønsberg"). Hemsworth said about this drastic character change, "I just had an opinion. I wanted to do something different this time. Each film I've wanted to, in particular, the last couple, and they were on board," and added, "We shot for many hours and days and discussed how far could we push (Thor) and what we could do different." According to Anthony Russo, "Even though there's a lot of fun to be had in the movie with his physical condition, it's not a gag. It's a manifestation of where he is on a character level, and we think it's one of the most relatable aspects of him. I mean, it's a very common sort of response to depression and pain." Thor's story was Russo's favorite arc: "Part of Chris' magic as a comedic actor is his dedication to the depth of the character on a very earnest level [...] It's so devious and subversive when comedy is coming from a place of complete commitment and emotional complexity." Hemsworth underwent around three hours of hair and makeup work for the transformation, which also required him to wear a large silicone prosthetic suit; he called himself "[Lebowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebowski "Lebowski") Thor" on set. Thor was initially supposed to revert to his "old chiseled self" in the middle of *Endgame*, but Hemsworth wanted to retain Thor's new physique.
* [Scarlett Johansson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarlett_Johansson "Scarlett Johansson") as [Natasha Romanoff / Black Widow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natasha_Romanoff_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Natasha Romanoff (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"):
  An Avenger and a highly trained spy and former agent of [S.H.I.E.L.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.H.I.E.L.D._(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "S.H.I.E.L.D. (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") Romanoff continues to command several teams from around the galaxy in the Avengers headquarters at the beginning of the film, which Joe Russo said stemmed from her inability to move on from their failure to stop Thanos: "[S]he's doing everything she can to try and hold the community together [...] She's the watcher on the wall still." On the decision for Romanoff to sacrifice herself for Barton to acquire the Soul Stone to bring everyone back, Joe Russo said that it was part of a larger theme of the desire to sacrifice or protect in *Infinity War*: "When she gets to that [Soul Stone] scene, I think she understands that the only way to bring the community back is for her to sacrifice herself." McFeely said, "Her journey, in our minds, had come to an end if she could get the Avengers back. She comes from such an abusive, terrible, mind-control background, so when she gets to Vormir and she has a chance to get the family back, that's a thing she would trade for." To prepare for the film, Johansson adopted an intense workout regimen which included [plyometrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plyometrics "Plyometrics"), [Olympic weightlifting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_weightlifting "Olympic weightlifting") and gymnastics and a time-restricted diet. All were supervised by her longtime trainer, Eric Johnson, with whom she had worked since *[Iron Man 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Man_2 "Iron Man 2")* (2010) – the film which introduced her character.
* [Jeremy Renner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Renner "Jeremy Renner") as [Clint Barton / Hawkeye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clint_Barton_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Clint Barton (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"):
  A master archer and a former Avenger and agent of S.H.I.E.L.D. who was recently under house arrest. McFeely described Barton's dark turn as "a good example of people who had much stronger stories after [the Snap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Snap "The Snap")". The film's cold open, with the disintegration of Barton's family, was initially supposed to be in *Infinity War* after Thanos snapped; it was moved to *Endgame*, however, with Markus saying that it was "going to blunt the brutality of what [Thanos] did." Joe Russo called it "a very tragic scene to open the movie with. It's one of the few scenes in the movie that actually makes me tear up when I watch it, because I think about my own family [...] And then you think about what would happen to you, as a father. You'd become very self-destructive."
* [Don Cheadle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Cheadle "Don Cheadle") as [James "Rhodey" Rhodes / War Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Rhodes_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "James Rhodes (Marvel Cinematic Universe)"):

## Production

In October 2014, [Marvel Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Studios "Marvel Studios") announced a two-part sequel of *[Avengers: Age of Ultron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers:_Age_of_Ultron "Avengers: Age of Ultron")* (2015) entitled *Avengers: Infinity War*. *Part 1* was scheduled to be released on May 4, 2018, and *Part 2* was scheduled for May 3, 2019. Marvel's plan was to film both parts of *Infinity War* [back-to-back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back-to-back_film_production "Back-to-back film production"). [Anthony and Joe Russo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo_brothers "Russo brothers") were hired to direct the films in April 2015. The next month, [Christopher Markus and Stephen McFeely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Markus_and_Stephen_McFeely "Christopher Markus and Stephen McFeely") signed on to write the screenplays for both parts of the film. Producer [Kevin Feige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Feige "Kevin Feige") said they would be "two distinct" projects with shared elements, not [one story split across two films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_films_split_into_multiple_parts "List of films split into multiple parts"). The Russos decided to rename them to remove this misconception in May 2016, and Marvel left the second film untitled that July. Feige and the Russo brothers indicated that the title was being withheld to avoid giving away plot details.

[Principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography "Principal photography") began on August 10, 2017, under the [working title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_title "Working title") *Mary Lou 2* at [Pinewood Atlanta Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinewood_Atlanta_Studios "Pinewood Atlanta Studios") in [Fayette County, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fayette_County,_Georgia "Fayette County, Georgia"), with [Trent Opaloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trent_Opaloch "Trent Opaloch") the [director of photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_of_photography "Director of photography"). The film and *Infinity War* were shot with ARRI Alexa [IMAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAX "IMAX") 2D cameras, the first time a [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_the_United_States "Cinema of the United States") feature film was shot entirely with IMAX digital cameras. Filming was done that month in [the Gulch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gulch_(Atlanta) "The Gulch (Atlanta)") area of [downtown Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Atlanta "Downtown Atlanta"), near the [Five Points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Points_station "Five Points station") [MARTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Atlanta_Rapid_Transit_Authority "Metropolitan Atlanta Rapid Transit Authority") station, and in [Piedmont Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_Park "Piedmont Park"). Feige said that the films were originally scheduled to be filmed simultaneously but were ultimately shot back-to-back: "It became too complicated to cross-board them like that, and we found ourselves—again, something would always pay the price. We wanted to be able to focus and shoot one movie and then focus and shoot another movie." Anthony Russo originally felt that it made more sense to shoot the films simultaneously for financial and logistical reasons (including the large number of cast members), and had suggested that "some days we'll be shooting the first movie and some days we'll be shooting the second movie. Just jumping back and forth." The 2013 [Asgard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asgard_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Asgard (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") scenes were shot at [Durham Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_Cathedral "Durham Cathedral") in [Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham,_England "Durham, England"), England during production of *Infinity War* in early May 2017. Production [wrapped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrap_(filmmaking) "Wrap (filmmaking)") on January 11, 2018, although additional filming took place in [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_(state) "New York (state)")'s [Dutchess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutchess_County,_New_York "Dutchess County, New York") and [Ulster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_County,_New_York "Ulster County, New York") counties in June 2018. Re-shoots began by September 7, 2018, and ended on October 12. More re-shoots were done in January 2019. Location shooting also took place in [St Abbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Abbs "St Abbs"), Scotland, which doubled for [New Asgard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Asgard "New Asgard") in Norway. Evans and Hemsworth earned $15 million each for the film.

The film's official title, *Avengers: Endgame*, and final U.S. release date of April 26, 2019, were revealed with the film's first trailer in December 2018. Visual effects for the film were created by [Industrial Light & Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Light_%26_Magic "Industrial Light & Magic"), [Weta Digital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weta_Digital "Weta Digital"), [DNEG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNEG "DNEG"), [Framestore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framestore "Framestore"), [Cinesite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinesite "Cinesite"), [Digital Domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Domain "Digital Domain"), Rise, [Lola VFX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lola_VFX "Lola VFX"), [Cantina Creative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantina_Creative "Cantina Creative"), Capital T, Technicolor VFX, and [Territory Studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_Studio "Territory Studio"). Like previous MCU films, Lola worked on the de-aging scenes; the film has 200 de-aging and aging shots. Downey, Evans, Ruffalo, Hemsworth, Johansson, and Renner were de-aged to their 2012 appearances for scenes recreated from *[The Avengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Avengers_(2012_film) "The Avengers (2012 film)")* (2012). [Michael Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Douglas "Michael Douglas"), [John Slattery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Slattery "John Slattery"), and [Stan Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stan_Lee "Stan Lee") were also de-aged for the 1970 New Jersey scene, and Douglas's appearance in *[The Streets of San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Streets_of_San_Francisco "The Streets of San Francisco")* was referenced. Lola aged Evans for the final scene, where he is an elderly man, with makeup and a stand-in. [Jeffrey Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Ford_(film_editor) "Jeffrey Ford (film editor)") and [Matthew Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Schmidt "Matthew Schmidt") were the film's editors.

## Music

[Alan Silvestri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Silvestri "Alan Silvestri"), who composed the score for *[The Avengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Avengers_(2012_film) "The Avengers (2012 film)")*, was announced in June 2016 to be returning to score *Infinity War* and *Endgame*. The Russos began working with Silvestri on the *Endgame* score in early November 2018, and it was completed in late March 2019. A [soundtrack album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers:_Endgame_(soundtrack) "Avengers: Endgame (soundtrack)") with Silvestri's score was released digitally by [Hollywood Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Records "Hollywood Records") on April 26, 2019, with a physical release on May 24. A video for the track "Portals", composed for the climactic ["Avengers assemble" scene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers_assemble_scene "Avengers assemble scene") in which reinforcements arrive for the Avengers, was released on June 13.

Silvestri described the score as having the franchise's most versatile tone, ranging from "thunderous percussion and powerful brass" for the action scenes to minimalist, jazz-inspired music for Ant-Man and the Quantum Realm. He reprised his themes from the previous *Avengers* films and *Captain America: The First Avenger*, including material he wrote for Thanos and the Infinity Stones in *Infinity War*. Silvestri found writing the music to end Captain America's story poignant, since he had "been on this journey with him since the beginning". The film also used the *[Ant-Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant-Man_(film) "Ant-Man (film)")* (2015) theme by [Christophe Beck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christophe_Beck "Christophe Beck"), the *[Doctor Strange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Strange_(2016_film) "Doctor Strange (2016 film)")* (2016) theme by [Michael Giacchino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Giacchino "Michael Giacchino"), and the *[Captain Marvel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Marvel_(film) "Captain Marvel (film)")* theme by [Pinar Toprak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinar_Toprak "Pinar Toprak"). The songs "[Come and Get Your Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Come_and_Get_Your_Love "Come and Get Your Love")" by [Redbone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redbone_(band) "Redbone (band)") and "[It's Been a Long, Long Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%27s_Been_a_Long,_Long_Time "It's Been a Long, Long Time")" by [Jule Styne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jule_Styne "Jule Styne") and [Sammy Cahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sammy_Cahn "Sammy Cahn") were also used after previously being heard in *Guardians of the Galaxy* and *[Captain America: The Winter Soldier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_America:_The_Winter_Soldier "Captain America: The Winter Soldier")* (2014), respectively.

## Marketing

The marketing campaign for *Endgame* cost over $200 million, the most for any Marvel Studios film. Promotional partners included [Stand Up to Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand_Up_to_Cancer "Stand Up to Cancer"), [Mastercard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mastercard "Mastercard"), [Ulta Beauty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulta_Beauty "Ulta Beauty"), the [Audi e-tron GT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audi_e-tron_GT "Audi e-tron GT") concept car (which appears in the film), [McDonald's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonald%27s "McDonald's"), [GEICO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GEICO "GEICO"), [Coca-Cola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca-Cola "Coca-Cola"), [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google "Google"), [General Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Mills "General Mills"), [Hertz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hertz_Corporation "The Hertz Corporation"), [Ziploc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziploc "Ziploc"), [Oppo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppo "Oppo"), and [Synchrony Financial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synchrony_Financial "Synchrony Financial"). A year before the film's release, Germain Lussier of *[io9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Io9 "Io9")* spoke on the approach Marvel might have to take in marketing the film, given the end of *Infinity War* where many established characters die. He questioned if those characters would appear on posters and in toy campaigns, and if the actors playing them would participate in press events leading up to the film's release. Lussier felt that Disney and Marvel could focus on the original Avengers team members (most of the living characters), but noted that it would be more beneficial to show the return of the dead characters; this would create a "mystery and curiosity about *how* they come back" and a "whole new level of interest" for the film "while having all the stars front and center". Feige said in June 2018 that the dead characters would not be featured in the film's marketing. He presented a behind-the-scenes video from the film at [CineEurope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CineEurope "CineEurope"), and said that its marketing campaign would begin at the end of 2018. In early December 2018, before the first trailer's release, Graeme McMillan of *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")* spoke about the "fevered anticipation" surrounding it and felt it "remarkable", mostly "fan-created, without [the] noticeable direction from Marvel or the filmmakers involved"; knowledge about the film, without promotion, was "a kind of brand awareness" that was unusual. McMillan urged Marvel not to release any trailers for the film, since "the advanced level of enthusiasm [...] [was] likely to build" before its release; a trailer would take away the "[Schrödinger's cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schr%C3%B6dinger%27s_cat "Schrödinger's cat")-esque position", as it was "almost guaranteed" to disappoint fans.

The first trailer for the film was released on December 7, 2018. Dustin Sandoval, vice president of [digital marketing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_marketing "Digital marketing") for Marvel Studios, said that the marketing team decided to omit the "title or hashtag" for the film in its trailer posts, allowing fans to see the trailer without spoiling it by watching "at the end". Richard Newby, also of *The Hollywood Reporter*, felt that although little new material was revealed in the trailer, it offered a "somber glimpse of a universe made unrecognizable" and let the viewer consider "the ending of *Avengers: Infinity War* and our questions of loss". Newby noted that the trailer highlighted the characters' "humble beginnings" with its visual language, and left viewers with "just as many questions as we had before". Austen Goslin of *[Polygon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygon_(website) "Polygon (website)")* said that the title references a line by Doctor Strange to Tony Stark in *Infinity War* and a line by Stark in *Age of Ultron*. Goslin said, "The scene surrounding this line in *Age of Ultron* is one of the most important ones in the movie. Things look dark, and the group of heroes face an enemy they don't think they can defeat." The *Endgame* trailer "mirrors this perfectly", and "shows us that the Avengers' two most prominent characters are who they've always been: Iron Man, a pessimist who keeps fighting no matter how hopeless things look, and Captain America, an optimist who believes that nothing is hopeless when the world's heroes fight together." The trailer was viewed 289 million times in its first 24 hours and was the most-viewed trailer in that time period, surpassing the record of *Avengers: Infinity War* (230 million views). It set a record for [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter") conversation for a film trailer in the first 24 hours, generating 549,000 mentions. By January 3, 2019, *[BoxOffice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BoxOffice "BoxOffice")*'s "Trailer Impact" metric service indicated that 77 to 78 percent of people surveyed who viewed the *Endgame* trailer in the past three weeks had expressed interest in seeing the film. In the three weeks it was measured by the service, the trailer was number one for all and had the top two percent of respondents express interest in seeing the film since the service's introduction in March 2018.

The second trailer for the film and its theatrical release poster were released on March 14, 2019. All 13 actors on the poster received top billing except for Danai Gurira, whose name appeared in the poster's bottom [billing block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billing_block "Billing block") with Benedict Wong, Jon Favreau, and Gwyneth Paltrow (none of whom appeared on the poster). Despite this, her exclusion from the top billing prompted criticism from some fans. Petrana Radulovic of *Polygon* said that how an actor is credited on a poster "is a complex process" based on "dealing with agents, fees, and movie star demands." Marvel Studios released an updated poster later that day, with Gurira in the top billing. The second trailer was viewed 268 million times in the first 24 hours, becoming the second-most-viewed trailer in that time period (behind the first trailer).

## Release

### Theatrical

*Avengers: Endgame* premiered at the [Los Angeles Convention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Convention_Center "Los Angeles Convention Center") on April 22, 2019. Disney converted the convention center's Hall K for the premiere, working with [Dolby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolby "Dolby") and [QSC Audio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QSC_Audio "QSC Audio") to install a 70-foot (21-metre) screen, [Dolby Vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolby_Vision "Dolby Vision") projectors, and a [Dolby Atmos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolby_Atmos "Dolby Atmos") sound system. The convention center also held the red carpet arrival and after-party for the premiere. The film was released in Australia, China, and other parts of Asia and Europe on April 24, in the United Kingdom on April 25, in the United States and India on April 26, and in Russia on April 29, in [IMAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAX "IMAX") and [3D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_film "3D film"). It was originally scheduled to be released in the U.S. on May 3. [Radio Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Liberty "Radio Liberty") alleged that the [Russian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_government "Russian government") postponed the film's release in that country to promote Russian-produced films. *Endgame* is part of [Phase Three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe:_Phase_Three "Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Three") of the MCU. *Endgame* was the first film to feature [4DX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4DX "4DX")'s "Signature Character Motions" unique to the ten lead characters.

Following the release of the second trailer for the MCU film *[Spider-Man: Far From Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man:_Far_From_Home "Spider-Man: Far From Home")* (2019) on May 6, it was added to the end of *Endgame* screenings with a message before the film from *Far From Home* star Tom Holland telling the audience to stay until after the credits to see the trailer. In June, Feige announced that *Endgame* would be re-released in theaters with seven minutes of new post-credits footage, including a Stan Lee tribute, an unfinished deleted scene, and the opening scene of *Far From Home*. A limited-edition poster was given out at select theaters. The re-release began on June 28 in 1,040 theaters in the U.S.

Mobius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobius_M._Mobius_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Mobius M. Mobius (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") ([Owen Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Wilson "Owen Wilson")), [Ouroboros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouroboros_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Ouroboros (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") ([Ke Huy Quan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ke_Huy_Quan "Ke Huy Quan")), [Casey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casey_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Casey (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") ([Eugene Cordero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Cordero "Eugene Cordero")), and [Ralph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Ralph (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") (James Dryden) detect numerous "incursions" between universes as the [multiverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiverse_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Multiverse (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") begins to collapse; [Tara Strong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tara_Strong "Tara Strong") also voices [Miss Minutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_Minutes "Miss Minutes") in the scene; and a re-worked post-credit sound, which was originally taken from *[Iron Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Man_(2008_film) "Iron Man (2008 film)")* (2008) when Stark is forging his initial Iron Man mask, instead shown to be Doom forging his mask.

### Home media

The film was released for [digital download](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_distribution "Digital distribution") by [Walt Disney Studios Home Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Studios_Home_Entertainment "Walt Disney Studios Home Entertainment") on July 30, 2019, and on [Ultra HD Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_HD_Blu-ray "Ultra HD Blu-ray"), [Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blu-ray "Blu-ray"), and [DVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD-Video "DVD-Video") on August 13. The IMAX Enhanced version of the film was made available on Disney+ on November 12, 2021. The film earned $107.8 million from DVD and Blu-ray sales in the U.S.

## Reception

### Box office

*Avengers: Endgame* originally grossed $858.4 million in the United States and Canada and $1.941 billion in other territories for a worldwide total of $2.799 billion. It was the [highest-grossing film of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_films#Highest-grossing_films "List of highest-grossing films"), until it was surpassed by *[Avatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avatar_(2009_film) "Avatar (2009 film)")* (2009) due to the 2021 re-release in China, and was the [second-highest-grossing film of all time in the United States and Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_films_in_the_United_States_and_Canada#Not_adjusted_for_inflation "List of highest-grossing films in the United States and Canada"). When adjusted for inflation, *[Gone with the Wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gone_with_the_Wind_(film) "Gone with the Wind (film)")* (1939) remains the highest-grossing film of all-time worldwide, while *Avengers: Endgame* is the [fifth-highest-grossing film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_films "List of highest-grossing films").

The film had a worldwide opening of $1.2 billion, the [biggest of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_openings_for_films#Biggest_worldwide_openings_on_record "List of highest-grossing openings for films"), and nearly double *Infinity War*'s previous record of $640 million. It is the fastest film to reach the $1 billion and $1.5 billion marks, doing so in five and eight days, respectively. *[Deadline Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadline_Hollywood "Deadline Hollywood")* estimated that the film would [break even](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Break_even "Break even") five days after release, "unheard of for a major studio tentpole during its opening weekend". The website calculated *Avengers: Endgame*'s final net profit as $890 million, accounting for production budgets, marketing, talent participation, and other costs; box-office grosses and home-media revenue placed it first on its list of 2019's "Most Valuable Blockbusters".

On May 4, the film's global box-office earnings surpassed the theatrical run of *Infinity War*. It was the fastest film to gross $2 billion worldwide, reaching that threshold in 11 days; *Avatar* did so in 47 days. *Avengers: Endgame* was the fifth film to surpass this threshold (after *Avatar*, *[Titanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic_(1997_film) "Titanic (1997 film)")* (1997), *[Star Wars: The Force Awakens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars:_The_Force_Awakens "Star Wars: The Force Awakens")* (2015), and *Infinity War*), and reached the $2.5 billion mark in 20 days; *Avatar* did so in 72. As of October 16, 2026[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Avengers:_Endgame&action=edit), *Encore* has added an additional $138.9 million to its box office, bringing its gross to $898.2 million in the United States and Canada and $2.04 billion in other territories, for a worldwide total of $2.938 billion, making the film the highest-grossing film of all time once again, surpassing the $2.923 billion gross of *Avatar*.

#### Pre-sale records

*Endgame* was one of 2019's most anticipated films in late December 2018, ranked second by [IMDb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb "IMDb") and first by [Fandango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fandango_Media "Fandango Media") and [Atom Tickets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom_Tickets "Atom Tickets"). Due to high demand when pre-sale tickets became available in the U.S. on April 2, 2019, customers on Atom Tickets and Fandango experienced long wait times and system delays; [AMC Theatres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMC_Theatres "AMC Theatres")' website and app [crashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crash_(computing) "Crash (computing)") for several hours. Fandango announced that day that the film was its top-selling pre-sale film for the first 24 hours, breaking *Star Wars: The Force Awakens*'s record in six hours. Atom said that the film was the website's bestselling first-day film (outselling *[Aquaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquaman_(film) "Aquaman (film)")* (2018) by four times), and [Regal Cinemas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regal_Cinemas "Regal Cinemas") reported that *Endgame* had sold more tickets in its first eight hours than *Infinity War* did in its first week. The film grossed $120–140 million in pre-sales. Fandango announced the day before release that *Endgame* was its biggest pre-selling film of all time, surpassing *The Force Awakens* with over 8,000 sold-out showtimes across the US.

In India, the film sold one million tickets in one day for its English, Hindi, Tamil, and Telugu-language screenings; 18 tickets were sold every second. In China, pre-sale tickets became available on April 12; a record one million were sold in six hours, surpassing *Infinity War*'s first-24-hours total in the first hour and earning $114.5 million ([RMB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMB "RMB") 770 million) in pre-sales.

#### United States and Canada

On April 4, industry tracking projected that the film would gross $200–250 million domestically during its opening weekend; some insiders saw those figures as conservative, and expected a $260–300 million debut. By the week of its release, domestic estimates had risen to $260–270 million, and some insiders suggested that a $300 million debut was possible. The film played in 4,662 theaters, 410 of which were in IMAX; it was the widest release ever, surpassing the record of *[Despicable Me 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Despicable_Me_3 "Despicable Me 3")*'s (2017) 4,529 theaters. *Avengers: Endgame* [earned $357.1 million in its opening weekend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_openings_for_films "List of highest-grossing openings for films"), breaking *Infinity War*'s record by nearly $100 million. It set records for Friday ($157.5 million, including $60 million from Thursday night previews), Saturday ($109.3 million), and Sunday ($90.4 million) totals, and had a higher total weekend gross than the previous box-office high of all films combined ($314 million). The film earned an average of $76,601 per theatre, breaking records for highest per-theatre average for a nationwide release ($59,982 for *The Force Awakens*) and the inflation-adjusted record held by *[Return of the Jedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_of_the_Jedi "Return of the Jedi")* (approximately $66,400, set in 1983). It earned $36.9 million the following Monday and $33.1 million on Tuesday, both the third-highest of all time. In its second weekend, the film made $147.4 million (the second-best second weekend ever) for a 10-day total of $621.3 million. It was the fastest film to pass the $600 million mark, ahead of *The Force Awakens*'s 12 days and less than half the 26 days it took *Infinity War*. The film grossed $64.8 million the following week, the fourth-best third weekend ever. It passed the $700 million mark, tying *The Force Awakens*'s record of 16 days. *Endgame* was finally dethroned in its fourth weekend by newcomer *[John Wick: Chapter 3 – Parabellum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wick:_Chapter_3_%E2%80%93_Parabellum "John Wick: Chapter 3 – Parabellum")* (2019). It earned $17.2 million the following weekend (a total of $22.3 million over the four-day Memorial Day weekend), crossing the $800 million threshold domestically. The film was added to 1,040 theaters during its tenth-weekend re-release and earned $6.1 million, an increase of 207 percent from the previous weekend. It earned $1.2 million in its thirteenth weekend, breaking *Avatar*'s all-time record.

*Encore* earned $3.8 million from previews, surpassing the $3.4 million from the 2025 re-release of *[Star Wars: Episode III – Revenge of the Sith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars:_Episode_III_%E2%80%93_Revenge_of_the_Sith "Star Wars: Episode III – Revenge of the Sith")* (2005), and earned $26 million in its opening weekend, taking the top spot at the box office. It was the third-best opening for a re-release, after the $35.9 million for the 1997 re-release of *[Star Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars_(film) "Star Wars (film)")* (1977) and the $30.2 million for the 2011 3D re-release of *[The Lion King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lion_King "The Lion King")* (1994). IMAX and Infinity Vision screenings accounted for 60% of the weekend gross. Its second weekend saw a gross of $4.4 million, an 83% drop, which *Deadline Hollywood* reported was attributed to the film losing most of its Infinity Vision screens after the first week, and that the appeal for the re-release was "clearly very front-loaded".

#### Other territories

*Endgame* was projected to gross around $680 million internationally over its first five days, for a global debut of $850–950 million. The film was initially projected to gross $250–280 million in China in its opening weekend, but earned a record $107.5 million (RMB 719 million) in the country on its first day; this included $28.2 million (RMB 189 million) from midnight, 3 am and 6 am screenings, eclipsing *[The Fate of the Furious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fate_of_the_Furious "The Fate of the Furious")*'s (2017) record of $9.1 million. Due to the record-breaking first day and word of mouth (with a 9.1 on local review aggregator [Douban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douban "Douban") and a 9.3 on ticket website [Maoyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maoyan "Maoyan")), debut projections were increased to over $300 million. The film made $169 million on its first day, the highest total of all time. Its largest markets after China were India ($9 million), South Korea ($8.4 million, the largest non-holiday single-day gross ever), Australia ($7.1 million), France ($6 million), and Italy ($5.8 million). The film over-performed, opening with $866 million overseas. Its largest markets, setting records for the best-ever opening in each country, were China ($330.5 million, RMB 2.22 billion), the United Kingdom ($53.8 million), South Korea ($47.4 million), Mexico ($33.1 million), Australia ($30.8 million), Brazil ($26 million), Spain ($13.3 million), Japan ($13 million), and Vietnam ($10 million). The film earned $21.6 million over its first four days in Russia after a government-caused delay of its premiere.

In its first week, the film's top five largest international markets were China ($459.4 million), the United Kingdom ($68.2 million), South Korea ($60.3 million), Mexico ($48.6 million), and India ($40.9 million). A week after its release, it became the highest-grossing foreign film of all time in China and India. The film's running total passed $1.569 billion from international markets in its second weekend, passing *Titanic* as the second-highest film overseas of all time. By January 2021, its top international markets were China ($632 million), the United Kingdom ($115 million), South Korea ($105 million), Brazil ($86 million), and Mexico ($78 million).

*Encore* earned $60 million its opening weekend internationally, the second-highest opening for a re-release behind the $67 million for the 2012 3D re-release of *[Titanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic_(1997_film) "Titanic (1997 film)")* (1997). The re-release opened as the top film at the box-office in over 30 markets and the top non-local film in at least 7 markets, while Disney estimated that *Encore* was the highest opening weekend ever for a re-release in over 30 markets. China was its top grossing market with $21.3 million, with other notable markets including Mexico ($6.1 million) and Brazil ($3.1 million). *Encore* became the highest-grossing re-release across the Latin America region as well as Malaysia, India, and Indonesia.

### Critical response

[Review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator "Review aggregator") [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes") reported an approval rating of 94%, with an average score of 8.2/10 based on 551 reviews. The website's critical consensus reads, "Exciting, entertaining, and emotionally impactful, *Avengers: Endgame* does whatever it takes to deliver a satisfying finale to Marvel's epic [Infinity Saga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_Saga "Infinity Saga")." [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic") (which uses a [weighted average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_arithmetic_mean "Weighted arithmetic mean")) gave the film a score of 78 out of 100 based on 57 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews. Audiences polled by [CinemaScore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CinemaScore "CinemaScore") gave the film an A+ grade on an A+ to F scale, the third Marvel film to receive the grade after *[The Avengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Avengers_(2012_film) "The Avengers (2012 film)")* and *[Black Panther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Panther_(film) "Black Panther (film)")*. [PostTrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PostTrak "PostTrak") audiences gave the film five stars out of five and an 85% "definite recommend". *Encore* received a 91% positive score and an 81% "definite recommend" from PostTrak audiences.

Writing for [NPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR "NPR"), [Glen Weldon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glen_Weldon "Glen Weldon") gave the film a positive review and found it a worthy sequel to its predecessor: "The Russos' decision to stick close to the experiences of the remaining Avengers proves a rewarding one, as they've expressly constructed the film as an extended victory lap for the Marvel Cinematic Universe writ large. Got a favorite character from any Marvel movie over the past decade, no matter how obscure? Prepare to get serviced, fan." In his *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")* review, [Peter Travers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Travers "Peter Travers") gave the film four stars out of five: "You don't have to make jokes about the clichéd time travel plot – the film is ready, willing and able to make its own, with *[Back to the Future](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_the_Future "Back to the Future")* coming in for a serious ribbing."

Peter Debruge of *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* wrote, "After the must-see showdown that was *Infinity War*, the Russo brothers deliver a more fan-facing three-hour follow-up, rewarding loyalty to Marvel Cinematic Universe." J. R. Kinnard of *[PopMatters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PopMatters "PopMatters")* wrote, "Big budget action filmmaking doesn't get much better than this." [Todd McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_McCarthy "Todd McCarthy") of *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")* said, "[W]hat comes across most strongly here, oddly enough for an effects-driven comic-book-derived film, is the character acting, especially from Downey, Ruffalo, Evans, Hemsworth, Brolin, and Paul Rudd". [Richard Roeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Roeper "Richard Roeper"), writing for the *[Chicago Sun-Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Sun-Times "Chicago Sun-Times")*, gave the film four stars and praised its "emotional punch" and the "funny, well-paced, smart, expertly rendered screenplay by Christopher Markus and Stephen McFeely, crisp direction from Anthony Russo and Joe Russo, [...] and the universally stellar performances".

*[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* reviewer [A. O. Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._O._Scott "A. O. Scott") gave the film a positive-but-guarded review: "*Endgame* is a monument to adequacy, a fitting capstone to an enterprise that figured out how to be good enough for enough people enough of the time. Not that it's really over, of course: Disney and Marvel are still working out new wrinkles in the time-money continuum. But the Russos do provide the sense of an ending, a chance to appreciate what has been done before the timelines reset and we all get back to work." [Justin Chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Chang "Justin Chang") of the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")* wrote that "*Avengers: Endgame* achieves and earns its climactic surge of feeling, even as it falls just short of real catharsis". Some called the film a notable improvement over its predecessor, *Avengers: Infinity War*, including Brian Tallerico of *[RogerEbert.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RogerEbert.com "RogerEbert.com")*: *Endgame* is "a more patient, focused film [than *Infinity War*], even as its plot draws in elements of a dozen other movies." [Matt Zoller Seitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Zoller_Seitz "Matt Zoller Seitz"), also of *RogerEbert.com*, compared the film positively to *Infinity War*, which he considered "too crowded, too rushed and yet too long". Seitz called *Endgame* "a heartfelt and satisfying experience" and a "surprisingly relaxed, character-driven, self-aware yet sincere comedy [for] two-thirds of [the film]. Much of the script suggests a laid-back [Richard Linklater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Linklater "Richard Linklater") movie with superheroes". [Joe Morgenstern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Morgenstern "Joe Morgenstern") of *[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")* cited *Avengers: Endgame* as the conclusion of the Infinity Saga, calling the final battle "inevitably unwieldy [...] but thrilling all the same, and followed by a delicate [coda](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/coda "wikt:coda"). So many stories. So many adventures. So much to sort out before the next cycle starts."

[Richard Brody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Brody "Richard Brody") was more critical of the film in *[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker "The New Yorker")*, saying that its good acting was not matched by comparable skill from its directors: "The Russos have peculiarly little sense of visual pleasure, little sense of beauty, little sense of metaphor, little aptitude for texture or composition; their spectacular conceit is purely one of scale, which is why their finest moments are quiet and dramatic ones". [Anthony Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Lane "Anthony Lane") of *The New Yorker* gave the film a negative review, finding it overwrought: "The one thing you do need to know about *Avengers: Endgame* is that it runs for a little over three hours, and that you can easily duck out during the middle hour, do some shopping, and slip back into your seat for the climax. You won't have missed a thing."

### Accolades

At the [92nd Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/92nd_Academy_Awards "92nd Academy Awards"), *Avengers: Endgame* received a nomination for [Best Visual Effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Visual_Effects "Academy Award for Best Visual Effects"). The film's other nominations include an [Annie Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_Award "Annie Award") (which it won), a [British Academy Film Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Award "British Academy Film Award"), and three [Critics' Choice Movie Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Movie_Awards "Critics' Choice Movie Awards") (winning two). Its [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia "Wikipedia") page was [the most-viewed article of 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:2019_Top_50_Report "Wikipedia:2019 Top 50 Report"), with 44.2 million views. *Endgame* ranked 41st in a March 2020 *[Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_(magazine) "Empire (magazine)")* magazine poll of the 100 greatest films of the 21st century. The [Avengers' assembling moment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers_assemble_scene "Avengers assemble scene") was one of five finalists for an Oscars Cheer Moment in the "[Oscars Fan Favorite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscars_Fan_Favorite "Oscars Fan Favorite")" contest for the [94th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/94th_Academy_Awards "94th Academy Awards") in February 2022, despite the film's ineligibility for other Academy Awards that year.

## Sequels

In May 2018, [the Walt Disney Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company "The Walt Disney Company") CEO [Bob Iger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Iger "Bob Iger") said Marvel Studios was focusing on new characters and franchises for the MCU following *Endgame*, but another *[Avengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers_(film_series) "Avengers (film series)")* film could happen eventually, given the popularity of the previous films. Shortly after the film's premiere, the Russo brothers said that they were not opposed to returning to the MCU in the future due to their positive relationship with Marvel Studios, but did not plan to do so at that time. Feige said in January 2021 that another *Avengers* film would happen eventually.

At the 2022 [San Diego Comic-Con](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Comic-Con "San Diego Comic-Con"), Feige announced *Avengers: The Kang Dynasty* and *[Avengers: Secret Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers:_Secret_Wars "Avengers: Secret Wars")*. The films would conclude [Phase Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe:_Phase_Six "Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Six") of the MCU. Feige later said that the Russos would not return to direct the films. [Destin Daniel Cretton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destin_Daniel_Cretton "Destin Daniel Cretton"), who directed *[Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang-Chi_and_the_Legend_of_the_Ten_Rings "Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings")* (2021), was set to direct *The Kang Dynasty*, with a script from *[Ant-Man and the Wasp: Quantumania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant-Man_and_the_Wasp:_Quantumania "Ant-Man and the Wasp: Quantumania")* (2023) writer [Jeff Loveness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Loveness "Jeff Loveness"). *[Loki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loki_(TV_series) "Loki (TV series)")* [season one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loki_season_1 "Loki season 1") (2021) [head writer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_writer "Head writer") and *[Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Strange_in_the_Multiverse_of_Madness "Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness")* (2022) writer [Michael Waldron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Waldron "Michael Waldron") was set to write the screenplay for *Secret Wars*. By November 2023, Cretton had stepped down as director of *The Kang Dynasty*, when Waldron was hired to replace Loveness as the writer for the film. [Jonathan Majors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Majors "Jonathan Majors"), who portrayed [Kang the Conqueror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kang_the_Conqueror_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Kang the Conqueror (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") and was expected to appear in both films, was fired by Disney and Marvel Studios in December 2023 [after he was convicted of assault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Majors#2023_assault "Jonathan Majors"). By then, Marvel was referring to *The Kang Dynasty* internally as *Avengers 5*.

At the 2024 San Diego Comic-Con, the Russo brothers were announced to be directing the next two *Avengers* films. They revealed that *Avengers 5* was now titled *[Avengers: Doomsday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers:_Doomsday "Avengers: Doomsday")* and would feature [Doctor Doom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_von_Doom_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) "Victor von Doom (Marvel Cinematic Universe)") as the main antagonist rather than Kang. Downey was revealed to have been cast as Doom for both films, and McFeely had joined as a co-writer. Frequent MCU film writers [Chris McKenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_McKenna_(writer) "Chris McKenna (writer)") and [Erik Sommers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Sommers "Erik Sommers") also worked on the scripts for both films. Numerous MCU cast members reprise their roles in *Doomsday*, along with several actors who starred in [20th Century Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Century_Fox "20th Century Fox")'s [*X-Men* film series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-Men_(film_series) "X-Men (film series)"). Evans also reprises his role as Rogers, despite his "definitive ending" in *Endgame*, with the Russos calling him "the character that changed our lives... it was always going to come back" to his story. Feige described *Doomsday* as "picking up" where *Endgame* ended. *Doomsday* is scheduled to be released on December 18, 2026, with *Secret Wars* following on December 17, 2027.
