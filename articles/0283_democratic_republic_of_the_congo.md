# Democratic Republic of the Congo

The **Democratic Republic of the Congo** (**DRC**), also known as the **DR Congo**, **Congo-Kinshasa**, or simply the **Congo**, and formerly named **[Zaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaire "Zaire")**, is a country in [Central Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Africa "Central Africa"). By land area, it is the [second-largest country in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_African_countries_by_area "List of African countries by area") and the [eleventh-largest in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area "List of countries and dependencies by area"). With a population of about 124 million people, the Democratic Republic of the Congo is the [fourth-most populous country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_African_countries_by_population "List of African countries by population") in Africa and the most populous [Francophone country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_territories_where_French_is_an_official_language "List of countries and territories where French is an official language") in the world. [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_French "Belgian French") is the official and most widely spoken language. There are [over 200 indigenous languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Languages of the Democratic Republic of the Congo"), of which [Lingala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingala "Lingala") is the most widely spoken. The capital, largest city, and economic center is [Kinshasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinshasa "Kinshasa"). The DRC is bordered by the [Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_the_Congo "Republic of the Congo"), the [Cabinda exclave of Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinda_Province "Cabinda Province"), and the [South Atlantic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic_Ocean "South Atlantic Ocean") to the west; the [Central African Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_African_Republic "Central African Republic") and [South Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Sudan "South Sudan") to the north; [Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda "Uganda"), [Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwanda "Rwanda"), [Burundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burundi "Burundi"), and [Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania "Tanzania") (across [Lake Tanganyika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Tanganyika "Lake Tanganyika")) to the east; and [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia "Zambia") and [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola "Angola") to the south. Centered on the [Congo Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Basin "Congo Basin"), most of the DRC's [terrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Geography of the Democratic Republic of the Congo") is covered by [dense rainforests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congolian_rainforests "Congolian rainforests") and is crossed by many rivers. The east and southeast are mountainous.

The territory of the Congo was first inhabited by [Central African foragers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Pygmies "African Pygmies") around 90,000 years ago and was settled in the [Bantu expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_expansion "Bantu expansion") about 2,000 to 3,000 years ago. In the west, the [Kingdom of Kongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kongo "Kingdom of Kongo") ruled around the mouth of the [Congo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_River "Congo River") from the 14th to the 19th century. In the center and east, the empires of [Mwene Muji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mwene_Muji "Mwene Muji"), [Luba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Luba "Kingdom of Luba"), and [Lunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Lunda "Kingdom of Lunda") ruled between the 15th and 19th centuries. These kingdoms were broken up by Europeans during the [colonization of the Congo Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonization_of_the_Congo_Basin "Colonization of the Congo Basin"). King [Leopold II of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_II_of_Belgium "Leopold II of Belgium") acquired rights to the Congo territory in 1885 and called it the [Congo Free State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Free_State "Congo Free State"). In 1908, Leopold [ceded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_II_of_Belgium#Relinquishment_of_the_Congo "Leopold II of Belgium") the territory after international pressure in response to [widespread atrocities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atrocities_in_the_Congo_Free_State "Atrocities in the Congo Free State"), and it became a [Belgian colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_Congo "Belgian Congo"). Congo achieved [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_the_Congo_(L%C3%A9opoldville) "Republic of the Congo (Léopoldville)") from [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium "Belgium") in 1960 and was immediately confronted by [secessionist movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Crisis "Congo Crisis"), the assassination of Prime Minister [Patrice Lumumba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrice_Lumumba "Patrice Lumumba"), and the [seizure of power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat "1965 Democratic Republic of the Congo coup d'état") by [Mobutu Sese Seko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobutu_Sese_Seko "Mobutu Sese Seko") in 1965. Mobutu renamed the country [Zaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaire "Zaire") in 1971 and imposed a [personalist dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personalist_dictatorship "Personalist dictatorship").

Instability caused by an influx of refugees from the [Rwandan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_Civil_War "Rwandan Civil War") led to the [First Congo War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Congo_War "First Congo War"), from 1996 to 1997, which ended with Mobutu's overthrow. The country's name was changed, and it experienced the [Second Congo War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Congo_War "Second Congo War") from 1998 to 2003, which resulted in an estimated several million deaths and the assassination of President [Laurent-Désiré Kabila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent-D%C3%A9sir%C3%A9_Kabila "Laurent-Désiré Kabila"). The war ended under President [Joseph Kabila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Kabila "Joseph Kabila"), who restored relative stability. [Human rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Human rights in the Democratic Republic of the Congo") remained poor, and there were frequent abuses, such as [forced disappearances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enforced_disappearance "Enforced disappearance"), torture, arbitrary imprisonment and restrictions on civil liberties. Kabila stepped down in 2019 after [Félix Tshisekedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Tshisekedi "Félix Tshisekedi") won the contentious [2018 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo_general_election "2018 Democratic Republic of the Congo general election"), marking the country's first [peaceful transition of power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peaceful_transition_of_power "Peaceful transition of power") since independence. Over [120 armed groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_armed_groups_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "List of armed groups in the Democratic Republic of the Congo") remain active, concentrated in the east. One of the region's largest cities, [Goma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goma "Goma"), was occupied by the [M23 rebels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_23_Movement "March 23 Movement") [in 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M23_rebellion_(2012%E2%80%932013) "M23 rebellion (2012–2013)") and again [since 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Goma_offensive "2025 Goma offensive"). The [M23 uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M23_campaign_(2022%E2%80%93present) "M23 campaign (2022–present)") escalated in 2025 with military support from [Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwanda "Rwanda"), which has caused a [conflict between the two countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo%E2%80%93Rwanda_conflict_(2022%E2%80%93present) "Democratic Republic of the Congo–Rwanda conflict (2022–present)").

Despite being rich in [natural resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_resource "Natural resource"), the DRC is one of the world's poorest and [least developed countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_developed_countries "Least developed countries"), having suffered from political instability, lack of infrastructure, rampant corruption, and centuries of commercial and colonial extraction and exploitation. The nation is a prominent example of the "[resource curse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resource_curse "Resource curse")". Aside from Kinshasa, the next largest cities, [Lubumbashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubumbashi "Lubumbashi") and [Mbuji-Mayi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbuji-Mayi "Mbuji-Mayi"), are [mining communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining_community "Mining community"). DRC's main exports are raw [minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral "Mineral") and [metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal "Metal"), which accounted for 80% of exports in 2023, with [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") being its largest trade partner. The DRC's level of human development was ranked 171 out of 193 countries by the [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index "Human Development Index") in 2023. As of 2022[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo&action=edit), following two decades of [civil wars](#Continental_and_civil_wars_(1996–2007)) and [internal conflicts](#Continued_conflicts_(2008–2018)), around one million Congolese refugees were still living in neighbouring countries. Two million children are at risk of starvation, and the fighting has displaced 7 million people. The DRC is a member of the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations"), [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement "Non-Aligned Movement"), [African Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union "African Union"), [COMESA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COMESA "COMESA"), [Southern African Development Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_African_Development_Community "Southern African Development Community"), *[Organisation internationale de la Francophonie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_internationale_de_la_Francophonie "Organisation internationale de la Francophonie")*, and [Economic Community of Central African States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Community_of_Central_African_States "Economic Community of Central African States").

## Etymology

The Democratic Republic of the Congo is named after the [Congo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_River "Congo River"), which flows through the country. The Congo River is the world's deepest river and the world's third-largest river [by discharge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rivers_by_discharge "List of rivers by discharge"). The *Comité d'études du haut Congo* ("Committee for the Study of the Upper Congo"), established by King [Leopold II of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_II_of_Belgium "Leopold II of Belgium") in 1876, and the [International Association of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Association_of_the_Congo "International Association of the Congo"), established by him in 1879, were also named after the river.

The Congo River was named by early European sailors after the [Kingdom of Kongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kongo "Kingdom of Kongo") and its [Bantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_peoples "Bantu peoples") inhabitants, the [Kongo people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_people "Kongo people"), when they encountered them in the 16th century. The word *Kongo* comes from the [Kongo language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_language "Kongo language"), also called *Kikongo*. According to American writer Samuel Henry Nelson: "It is probable that the word 'Kongo' itself implies a public gathering and that it is based on the root *konga*, 'to gather' (trans[itive])." The modern name of the Kongo people, *Bakongo*, was introduced in the early 20th century.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The Democratic Republic of the Congo has been known in the past as, in chronological order, the [Congo Free State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Free_State "Congo Free State"), [Belgian Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_Congo "Belgian Congo"), the [Republic of the Congo-Léopoldville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_the_Congo_(L%C3%A9opoldville) "Republic of the Congo (Léopoldville)"), the Democratic Republic of the Congo and the [Republic of Zaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Zaire "Republic of Zaire"), before returning to its current name, the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

At the time of independence, the country was named the Republic of the Congo-Léopoldville to distinguish it from its neighbour the [Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_the_Congo "Republic of the Congo"). With the promulgation of the [Luluabourg Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luluabourg_Constitution "Luluabourg Constitution") in August 1964, the country became the DRC. It was renamed [Zaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaire "Zaire"), a past name for the Congo River, in October 1971 by President [Mobutu Sese Seko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobutu_Sese_Seko "Mobutu Sese Seko") as part of his *[Authenticité](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authenticit%C3%A9_(Zaire) "Authenticité (Zaire)")* initiative.

The word *Zaire* is from a [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language "Portuguese language") adaptation of a Kikongo word *nzadi* ("river"), a truncation of *nzadi o nzere* ("river swallowing rivers"). The river was known as *Zaire* during the 16th and 17th centuries. *Congo* seems to have replaced *Zaire* gradually in English usage during the 18th century. *Congo* is the preferred English name in 19th-century literature, although references to *Zaire* as the name used by the natives (i.e., derived from Portuguese usage) remained common.

In 1992, the Sovereign National Conference voted to change the name of the country to the "Democratic Republic of the Congo", but the change was not made. The country's name was later restored by President [Laurent-Désiré Kabila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent-D%C3%A9sir%C3%A9_Kabila "Laurent-Désiré Kabila") when he overthrew Mobutu in 1997. To distinguish it from the neighboring Republic of the Congo, it is sometimes referred to as *Congo (Kinshasa)* or *Congo-Kinshasa*. Its name is sometimes also abbreviated as *Congo DR*, *DR Congo*, *DRC*, *the DROC*, and *RDC* (in French).

## History

### Early history

The area of the present-day Democratic Republic of the Congo was inhabited as early as 90,000 years ago, and later underwent major demographic and technological change with the expansion of [Bantu peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_peoples "Bantu peoples") during the first millennium BC. From this process emerged organised states and empires, including early federated states around Pool Malebo and later the [Kingdom of Kongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kongo "Kingdom of Kongo"), as well many others such as the [Luba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luba_Empire "Luba Empire") and [Lunda Empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunda_Empire "Lunda Empire").[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Congo Free State (1877–1908)

From the 1870s to 1908, King [Leopold II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_II_of_Belgium "Leopold II of Belgium") of Belgium established and ruled the [Congo Free State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Free_State "Congo Free State") as his personal possession, using forced labour and concessionary companies to extract rubber, while violence, disease, and exploitation caused catastrophic population loss. International exposure of the abuses, notably through the 1904 [Casement Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casement_Report "Casement Report") and growing diplomatic pressure, led to Leopold's loss of control and the annexation of the territory as the Belgian Congo in 1908.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Belgian Congo (1908–1960)

The Belgian Congo was governed directly from [Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels "Brussels"), and although this change in administration ended the most extreme abuses of the Congo Free State, Belgian rule largely continued earlier patterns of direct control, racial segregation, forced labour, and repression, while prioritising economic exploitation through close cooperation between the state, missionaries, and private companies. After [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), urbanisation, development programmes, and limited social improvements fostered a small African middle class, but political exclusion persisted, contributing to the rise of nationalist movements and escalating demands for independence in the late 1950s.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Independence and political crisis (1960–1965)

In May 1960, a growing nationalist movement, the [Congolese National Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congolese_National_Movement "Congolese National Movement") led by [Patrice Lumumba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrice_Lumumba "Patrice Lumumba"), won the [parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_Belgian_Congo_general_election "1960 Belgian Congo general election"). Lumumba became the first [prime minister of the Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Prime Minister of the Democratic Republic of the Congo"), on 24 June 1960. The parliament elected [Joseph Kasa-Vubu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Kasa-Vubu "Joseph Kasa-Vubu") of the [ABAKO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABAKO "ABAKO") party as [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "President of the Democratic Republic of the Congo"). Other parties that emerged included the [African Solidarity Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parti_Solidaire_Africain "Parti Solidaire Africain") led by [Antoine Gizenga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine_Gizenga "Antoine Gizenga"), and the National Party of the People led by Albert Delvaux and [Laurent Mbariko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Mbariko "Laurent Mbariko").

The Belgian Congo achieved independence on 30 June 1960 under the name "Republic of the Congo". Shortly after independence the *[Force Publique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_Publique "Force Publique")* mutinied, and on 11 July the [province of Katanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katanga_Province "Katanga Province"), led by [Moïse Tshombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mo%C3%AFse_Tshombe "Moïse Tshombe"), and [South Kasai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Kasai "South Kasai") engaged in [secessionist uprisings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Crisis "Congo Crisis") against the new leadership. Most of the 100,000 [Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_people_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "White people in the Democratic Republic of the Congo") who had remained behind after independence fled the country. After the United Nations rejected Lumumba's call for help to put down the secessionist movements, Lumumba asked for assistance from the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"), who accepted and sent military supplies and advisers. On 23 August, the Congolese armed forces [invaded South Kasai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_South_Kasai "Invasion of South Kasai").

Lumumba was dismissed from office on 5 September 1960 by Kasa-Vubu, who publicly blamed him for massacres by the armed forces in South Kasai and for involving Soviets in the country. On 7 September, Lumumba made a speech to the Congolese House of Representatives, arguing his dismissal was illegal under the nation's laws. Congolese law gave parliament, not the president, the authority to dismiss a government minister. The House and Senate both rejected the dismissal of Lumumba, but the removal proceeded unconstitutionally.

On 14 September, Colonel [Joseph Mobutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobutu_Sese_Seko "Mobutu Sese Seko"), with the backing of the US and Belgium, removed Lumumba from office. On 17 January 1961, Lumumba was handed over to Katangan authorities and executed by Belgian-led Katangan troops. A 2001 investigation by Belgium's Parliament found Belgium "morally responsible" for the murder of Lumumba, and the country has since officially apologised for its role in his death.

Amidst widespread confusion and chaos, a temporary government was created led by educated technocrats, the [College of Commissioners-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Commissioners-General "College of Commissioners-General"). Katangan secession ended in January 1963 with the [assistance of UN forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Operation_in_the_Congo "United Nations Operation in the Congo"). Several short-lived governments took over in quick succession, led by [Joseph Ileo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Il%C3%A9o "Joseph Iléo"), [Cyrille Adoula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrille_Adoula "Cyrille Adoula"), and then Tshombe.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Meanwhile, in the east of the country, Soviet and Cuban-backed rebels called the [Simbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simba_rebellion "Simba rebellion") rose up, taking a significant amount of territory and proclaiming a communist "People's Republic of the Congo" in Stanleyville. The Simbas were pushed out of Stanleyville in November 1964 during [Operation Dragon Rouge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Dragon_Rouge "Operation Dragon Rouge"), a military operation conducted by Belgian and U.S. forces to rescue hundreds of hostages. Congolese government forces fully defeated the Simba rebels by November 1965.

Lumumba had previously appointed Mobutu chief of staff of the new [Congolese National Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Armed Forces of the Democratic Republic of the Congo"). Taking advantage of the leadership crisis between Kasavubu and Tshombe, Mobutu garnered enough support within the army to launch a coup. A [constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Republic_of_the_Congo_(L%C3%A9opoldville)_constitutional_referendum "1964 Republic of the Congo (Léopoldville) constitutional referendum") the year before [Mobutu's coup of 1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Mobutu_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat "Second Mobutu coup d'état") resulted in the country's official name being changed to the "Democratic Republic of the Congo". In 1971 Mobutu would change the name of the country again, this time to "Republic of Zaire".

### Mobutu autocracy and Zaire (1965–1997)

Mobutu had the staunch support of the United States because of his [opposition to communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-communism "Anti-communism"); the U.S. believed that his administration would serve as an effective counter to communist movements in Africa. A [one-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-party_state "One-party state") was established, and Mobutu declared himself [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_heads_of_state_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "List of heads of state of the Democratic Republic of the Congo"). He periodically held elections in which he was the only candidate. Although relative peace and stability were achieved, Mobutu's government was marked by severe [human rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights "Human rights") violations, [political repression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_repression "Political repression"), a [cult of personality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult_of_personality "Cult of personality"), and [corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Corruption in the Democratic Republic of the Congo").

By late 1967, Mobutu had successfully neutralized his political opponents and rivals, either through co-opting them into his regime, arresting them, or rendering them otherwise politically impotent. Throughout the late 1960s, Mobutu continued to shuffle his governments and cycle officials in and out of the office to maintain control. Joseph Kasa-Vubu's death in April 1969 ensured that no person with credentials from the First Republic could challenge his rule. By the early 1970s, Mobutu was attempting to assert Zaire as a leading African nation. He traveled frequently across the continent while the government became more vocal about African issues, particularly those relating to the southern region. Zaire established semi-clientelist relationships with several smaller African states, especially [Burundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burundi "Burundi"), [Chad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad "Chad"), and [Togo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Togo "Togo").

Corruption became so common the term "*le mal Zairois*", meaning "Zairian sickness", meaning gross corruption, theft and mismanagement, was coined, reportedly by Mobutu. Mobutu accumulated a large personal fortune through the diversion of state funds, foreign aid and revenue from natural resources, while Zaire remained impoverished and heavily indebted. Zaire became a [kleptocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kleptocracy "Kleptocracy").

In a campaign to identify himself with [African nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_nationalism "African nationalism"), starting on 1 June 1966, Mobutu renamed the nation's cities: Léopoldville became Kinshasa (the country was known as Congo-Léopoldville), Stanleyville became [Kisangani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisangani "Kisangani"), Elisabethville became [Lubumbashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubumbashi "Lubumbashi"), and Coquilhatville became [Mbandaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbandaka "Mbandaka"). In 1971, Mobutu renamed the country the "Republic of Zaire", its fourth name change in eleven years and its sixth overall. The [Congo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_River "Congo River") was renamed the Zaire River.

In the 1970s and 1980s, Mobutu was invited to visit the U.S. on several occasions, meeting with U.S. presidents [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon "Richard Nixon"), [Ronald Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan "Ronald Reagan") and [George H. W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush "George H. W. Bush"). Following the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union "Dissolution of the Soviet Union"), U.S. relations with Mobutu cooled, as he was no longer deemed necessary as a [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War") ally. Opponents within Zaire stepped up demands for reform. This atmosphere contributed to Mobutu's declaring the Third Republic in 1990, whose constitution was supposed to pave the way for democratic reform. The reforms turned out to be largely cosmetic. Mobutu continued his rule until the armed forces forced him to flee into exile in 1997. From 1990 to 1993, the United States supported Mobutu's efforts to obstruct political transition, and later also backed [Laurent-Désiré Kabila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent-D%C3%A9sir%C3%A9_Kabila "Laurent-Désiré Kabila")'s [rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Congo_War "First Congo War"), which brought down Mobutu's regime."

In September 1997, Mobutu died in exile in [Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco "Morocco").

### Continental and civil wars (1996–2007)

By 1996, following the [Rwandan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_Civil_War "Rwandan Civil War") and [genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_genocide "Rwandan genocide"), and the ascension of a [Tutsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutsi "Tutsi")-led government in Rwanda, Rwandan [Hutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutu "Hutu") militia forces ([Interahamwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interahamwe "Interahamwe")) fled to eastern Zaire and used refugee camps as bases for incursions against Rwanda. They allied with the Zairian Armed Forces to launch a campaign against Congolese ethnic Tutsis in eastern Zaire.

A coalition of Rwandan and Ugandan armies invaded Zaire to overthrow the government of Mobutu, launching the [First Congo War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Congo_War "First Congo War"). The coalition allied with some opposition figures, led by [Laurent-Désiré Kabila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent-D%C3%A9sir%C3%A9_Kabila "Laurent-Désiré Kabila"), becoming the [Alliance of Democratic Forces for the Liberation of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_of_Democratic_Forces_for_the_Liberation_of_the_Congo "Alliance of Democratic Forces for the Liberation of the Congo"). In 1997 Mobutu fled the country and Kabila marched into Kinshasa, naming himself president and reverting the name of the country to the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

Kabila later requested that foreign military forces return to their own countries. Rwandan troops retreated to [Goma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goma "Goma") and launched a new Tutsi-led rebel military movement called the [Rally for Congolese Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rally_for_Congolese_Democracy "Rally for Congolese Democracy") to fight Kabila, while Uganda instigated the creation of a rebel movement called the [Movement for the Liberation of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_for_the_Liberation_of_the_Congo "Movement for the Liberation of the Congo"), led by Congolese warlord [Jean-Pierre Bemba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Bemba "Jean-Pierre Bemba"). The two rebel movements, along with Rwandan and Ugandan troops, started the [Second Congo War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Congo_War "Second Congo War") by attacking the Congolese army in 1998. Angolan, Zimbabwean, and Namibian troops entered the war on the side of the Congolese government.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Kabila was assassinated in 2001. His son [Joseph Kabila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Kabila "Joseph Kabila") succeeded him and called for multilateral peace talks. UN peacekeepers, then known as MONUC and now known as [MONUSCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MONUSCO "MONUSCO"), arrived in April 2001. From 2002 to 2003, Bemba intervened in the [Central African Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_African_Republic "Central African Republic") on behalf of its then president, [Ange-Félix Patassé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ange-F%C3%A9lix_Patass%C3%A9 "Ange-Félix Patassé"). Talks led to a peace accord under which Kabila would share power with former rebels, implemented between 2002 and 2003. The most widely reported estimate for the death toll during the Second Congo War is 5.4 million people, though other estimates put it at 3 million. It has been described as the deadliest conflict since [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II").

By June 2003 all foreign armies except those of Rwanda had pulled out of the Congo. A [transitional government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitional_Government_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Transitional Government of the Democratic Republic of the Congo") was set up until an election was held. A constitution was approved by voters; further, on 30 July 2006, DRC held its [first multi-party elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo_general_election "2006 Democratic Republic of the Congo general election"). These were the first free national elections since 1960, which many believed would mark the end to violence in the region. However, an election-result dispute between Kabila and Bemba turned into a skirmish between their supporters in Kinshasa. MONUC took control of the city. The second round of the election took place in October 2006, which Kabila won, and in December 2006 he was sworn in as president.

### Continued conflicts (2008–2018)

[Laurent Nkunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Nkunda "Laurent Nkunda"), a member of [Rally for Congolese Democracy–Goma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rally_for_Congolese_Democracy%E2%80%93Goma "Rally for Congolese Democracy–Goma"), defected along with troops loyal to him and formed the [National Congress for the Defence of the People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Congress_for_the_Defence_of_the_People "National Congress for the Defence of the People") (CNDP), which began an armed rebellion against the government. In March 2009, after a deal between the DRC and Rwanda, Rwandan troops entered the DRC and arrested Nkunda and were allowed to pursue FDLR militants. The CNDP signed a peace treaty with the government in which it agreed to become a political party and to have its soldiers integrated into the national army in exchange for the release of its imprisoned members. In 2012 [Bosco Ntaganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosco_Ntaganda "Bosco Ntaganda"), the leader of the CNDP, and troops loyal to him, [mutinied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M23_rebellion_(2012%E2%80%932013) "M23 rebellion (2012–2013)") and briefly captured the provincial capital of Goma in November 2012.

Neighboring countries have been accused of arming rebel groups and using them as proxies to gain control of the resource-rich country. In March 2013, the [United Nations Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council "United Nations Security Council") authorized the [United Nations Force Intervention Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Force_Intervention_Brigade "United Nations Force Intervention Brigade") to neutralize armed groups. In 2013, the [Mai-Mai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mai-Mai "Mai-Mai") created by Laurent Kabila briefly invaded the provincial capital of [Lubumbashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubumbashi "Lubumbashi") and 400,000 people are displaced in the province as of 2013[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo&action=edit). On and off fighting in the [Ituri conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ituri_conflict "Ituri conflict") occurred between the [Nationalist and Integrationist Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_and_Integrationist_Front "Nationalist and Integrationist Front") and the [Union of Congolese Patriots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Congolese_Patriots "Union of Congolese Patriots"). In the northeast, [Joseph Kony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Kony "Joseph Kony")'s [Lord's Resistance Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord%27s_Resistance_Army "Lord's Resistance Army") moved from their original bases in Uganda and South Sudan to DR Congo in 2005 and set up camps in the [Garamba National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garamba_National_Park "Garamba National Park").

In 2015, [major protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19_January_2015_DRC_protests "19 January 2015 DRC protests") broke out across the country and protesters demanded that Kabila step down as president. On 27 November 2016 Congolese foreign minister [Raymond Tshibanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Tshibanda "Raymond Tshibanda") told the press no elections would be held in 2016: "it has been decided that the voter registration operation will end on July 31, 2017, and that election will take place in April 2018". [Protests broke out in the country on 20 December](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/December_2016_Congolese_protests "December 2016 Congolese protests") when Kabila's term in office ended. Across the country, dozens of protesters were killed and hundreds were arrested.

Human Rights Watch said in 2017 that Kabila recruited former 23 March Movement fighters to put down country-wide protests over his refusal to step down from office at the end of his term. "M23 fighters patrolled the streets of the Congo's main cities, firing on or arresting protesters or anyone else deemed to be a threat to the president", they said. Fierce fighting has erupted in Masisi between government forces and a powerful local warlord, General Delta. The United Nations mission in the DRC is its largest and most expensive peacekeeping effort, but it shut down five UN bases near Masisi in 2017, after the U.S. led a push to cut costs.

### 2018 election and Tshisekedi presidency (2018–present)

On 30 December 2018, a [general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo_general_election "2018 Democratic Republic of the Congo general election") was held. The election was originally scheduled for 23 December, but was postponed after a warehouse fire destroyed several electronic voting machines, prompting concerns about potential tampering. Polling eventually opened on 30 December across most of the country, though voting remained suspended in certain areas—a measure officials said was intended to protect residents from an ongoing Ebola outbreak in eastern provinces.

After polls closed, the government imposed an internet blackout in some regions, announcing that service would be restored following the release of results, which had been slated for 6 January.

On 10 January 2019, the electoral commission announced opposition candidate [Félix Tshisekedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Tshisekedi "Félix Tshisekedi") as the winner of the presidential vote, and he was officially sworn in as president on 24 January. However, there were widespread suspicions that the results were rigged and that a deal had been made between Tshisekedi and Kabila, as professional election polls carried out on election day found that Martin Fayulu, the leading opposition candidate, was expected to take 47% of the vote, compared to 23% for Felix Tshisekedi and 19% for Emmanuel Shadary, Kabila's hand-picked successor.

In August 2019, six months after the inauguration of Félix Tshisekedi, a coalition government was announced. Tshisekedi succeeded in strengthening his hold on power, gaining the support of almost 400 out of 500 members of the National Assembly, the pro-Kabila speakers of both houses of parliament being forced out. In April 2021, the [new government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lukonde_cabinet "Lukonde cabinet") was formed without the supporters of Kabila.

A [major measles outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%932020_measles_outbreak_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "2019–2020 measles outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo") in the country left nearly 5,000 dead in 2019. The [Ebola outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_%C3%89quateur_province_Ebola_outbreak "2018 Équateur province Ebola outbreak") ended in June 2020, after causing 2,280 deaths over two years. The [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic") also [reached the DRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "COVID-19 pandemic in the Democratic Republic of the Congo") in March 2020, with a [vaccination campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_vaccination_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "COVID-19 vaccination in the Democratic Republic of the Congo") beginning on 19 April 2021. The [Italian ambassador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ambassadors_of_Italy_to_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "List of ambassadors of Italy to the Democratic Republic of the Congo") to the DRC, [Luca Attanasio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luca_Attanasio "Luca Attanasio"), and his bodyguard were [killed in North Kivu on 22 February 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Luca_Attanasio "Killing of Luca Attanasio"). On 22 April 2021, meetings between Kenyan President [Uhuru Kenyatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uhuru_Kenyatta "Uhuru Kenyatta") and Tshisekedi resulted in new agreements increasing international trade and security (counterterrorism, immigration, cyber security, and customs) between the two countries. In February 2022, [allegations of a coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat_allegations "2022 Democratic Republic of the Congo coup d'état allegations") in the country led to uncertainty, but the coup attempt failed. In December 2023, President Felix Tshisekedi won [reelection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo_general_election "2023 Democratic Republic of the Congo general election") with more than 70% of the vote.

The M23 Movement had a [resurgence in 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M23_campaign_(2022%E2%80%93present) "M23 campaign (2022–present)") and [received military assistance from Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo%E2%80%93Rwanda_conflict_(2022%E2%80%93present) "Democratic Republic of the Congo–Rwanda conflict (2022–present)"), which accused the Congolese government of supporting the [FDLR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Forces_for_the_Liberation_of_Rwanda "Democratic Forces for the Liberation of Rwanda") Hutu militia. In early 2025, the conflict escalated with the M23 capture of [Goma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Goma_offensive "2025 Goma offensive") and [Bukavu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Bukavu_offensive "2025 Bukavu offensive"), involving thousands of Rwandan troops. Peace negotiations between Rwanda and the DRC brokered by the United States took place in the spring of 2025, and culminated in the signing of a peace deal on 27 June 2025. The [peace agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo%E2%80%93Rwanda_peace_agreement "2025 Democratic Republic of the Congo–Rwanda peace agreement") calls for the withdrawal of Rwandan troops from eastern Congo within 90 days and the end of the DRC's support for the FDLR, along with the creation of a regional economic integration framework for the two countries.

Despite the treaty, the [United Nations Humans Rights Office (UNHRO)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Human_Rights_Office "United Nations Human Rights Office") found that hostilities in the provinces where Goma and Bukavu are located, increased during the period from January to July 2025. UNHRO found that M23, along with the occupying [Rwandan Defense Force (RDF)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_Defence_Force "Rwandan Defence Force") soldiers allegedly massacred hundreds of civilians in four villages within [Rutshuru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutshuru "Rutshuru"), a region within [North Kivu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Kivu "North Kivu") province. In July 2025, a Declaration of Principles was signed in [Doha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha "Doha") as a first agreement on shared objectives for a ceasefire, and to limit future violence in the conflict. In October 2025, former president Joseph Kabila was sentenced to death in absentia for alleged collaboration with the rebel group M23.

## Geography

The DRC is located in central [sub-Saharan Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-Saharan_Africa "Sub-Saharan Africa"), bordered to the northwest by the [Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_the_Congo "Republic of the Congo"), to the north by the [Central African Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_African_Republic "Central African Republic"), to the northeast by [South Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Sudan "South Sudan"), to the east by [Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda "Uganda"), [Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwanda "Rwanda") and [Burundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burundi "Burundi"), and by [Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania "Tanzania") (across Lake Tanganyika), to the south and southeast by [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia "Zambia"), to the southwest by [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola "Angola"), and to the west by the South Atlantic Ocean and the [Cabinda exclave of Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinda_Province "Cabinda Province"). The DRC lies between latitudes [6°N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_parallel_north "6th parallel north") and [14°S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_parallel_south "14th parallel south"), and longitudes [12°E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th_meridian_east "12th meridian east") and [32°E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_meridian_east "32nd meridian east"). It straddles the [Equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator "Equator"), with one-third to the north and two-thirds to the south. With an area of 2,345,408 square kilometres (905,567 sq mi), it is the second-largest country in Africa by area, after [Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeria "Algeria").[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

As a result of its equatorial location, the DRC has high precipitation and has the highest frequency of thunderstorms in the world. The annual rainfall can total upwards of 2,000 millimetres (80 in) in some places. The DRC sustains the [Congo rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congolian_rainforests "Congolian rainforests"), the second-largest rainforest in the world after the [Amazon rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_rainforest "Amazon rainforest"). This massive expanse of lush jungle covers most of the vast, low-lying central [basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin "Drainage basin") of the river, which slopes toward the Atlantic Ocean in the west. This area is surrounded by [plateaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plateau "Plateau") merging into [savannas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savanna "Savanna") in the south and southwest, by mountainous terraces in the west, and dense [grasslands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grassland "Grassland") extending beyond the Congo River in the north. The glaciated [Rwenzori Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwenzori_Mountains "Rwenzori Mountains") are found in the extreme eastern region.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The [tropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_climate "Tropical climate") produced the Congo River system which dominates the region topographically along with the rainforest it flows through. The [Congo Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Basin "Congo Basin") occupies nearly the entire country and an area of nearly 1,000,000 km2 (390,000 sq mi). The river and its tributaries form the backbone of Congolese economics and transportation. Major tributaries include the [Kasai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasai_River "Kasai River"), [Sangha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangha_River "Sangha River"), [Ubangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubangi_River "Ubangi River"), [Ruzizi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruzizi_River "Ruzizi River"), [Aruwimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aruwimi_River "Aruwimi River"), and [Lulonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lulonga_River "Lulonga River").[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The Congo River has the second-largest flow and the second-largest [watershed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin "Drainage basin") of any river in the world, behind the [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_River "Amazon River") in both respects. The sources of the Congo River are in the [Albertine Rift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albertine_Rift "Albertine Rift") Mountains that flank the western branch of the [East African Rift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_Rift "East African Rift"), as well as [Lake Tanganyika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Tanganyika "Lake Tanganyika") and [Lake Mweru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Mweru "Lake Mweru"). The river flows generally west from Kisangani just below [Boyoma Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyoma_Falls "Boyoma Falls"), then gradually bends southwest, passing by [Mbandaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbandaka "Mbandaka"), joining with the Ubangi River, and running into the [Pool Malebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pool_Malebo "Pool Malebo") (Stanley Pool). Kinshasa and [Brazzaville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazzaville "Brazzaville") are on opposite sides of the river at the Pool. Then the river narrows and falls through a number of cataracts in deep canyons, collectively known as the [Livingstone Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livingstone_Falls "Livingstone Falls"), and runs past [Boma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boma,_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Boma, Democratic Republic of the Congo") into the Atlantic Ocean. The river and a 37-kilometre-wide (23 mi) strip of coastline on its north bank provide the country's only outlet to the Atlantic.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The Albertine Rift plays a key role in shaping the Congo's geography. The northeastern section of the country much more mountainous. Tectonic movement results in [volcanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanism "Volcanism") activity, occasionally with loss of life. The geologic activity in this area created the [African Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Great_Lakes "African Great Lakes"), four of which lie on the Congo's eastern frontier: [Lake Albert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Albert_(Africa) "Lake Albert (Africa)"), [Lake Kivu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Kivu "Lake Kivu"), [Lake Edward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Edward "Lake Edward"), and Lake Tanganyika.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The [rift valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rift_valley "Rift valley") has exposed an enormous amount of mineral wealth throughout the south and east of the Congo, making it accessible to mining. [Cobalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobalt "Cobalt"), copper, [cadmium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadmium "Cadmium"), industrial and gem-quality [diamonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamonds "Diamonds"), gold, silver, [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc "Zinc"), [manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese "Manganese"), [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin "Tin"), [germanium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanium "Germanium"), [uranium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium "Uranium"), [radium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radium "Radium"), [bauxite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite "Bauxite"), iron ore, and coal are all found in plentiful supply, especially in the Congo's southeastern Katanga region. The gold production in 2015 was 37 metric tonnes.

On 17 January 2002, [Mount Nyiragongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Nyiragongo "Mount Nyiragongo") [erupted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_eruption "Volcanic eruption"), with three streams of extremely fluid [lava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magma "Magma") running out at 64 km/h (40 mph) and 46 m (50 yd) wide. One of the three streams flowed directly through Goma, killing 45 people and leaving 120,000 homeless. Over 400,000 people were evacuated from the city during the eruption. The lava flowed into and poisoned the water of [Lake Kivu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Kivu "Lake Kivu") killing its plants, animals and fish. Only two planes left the local airport because of the possibility of the explosion of stored petrol. The lava flowed through and past the airport, destroying a runway and trapping several parked airplanes. Six months after the event, nearby [Mount Nyamuragira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyamuragira "Nyamuragira") also erupted. The mountain erupted again in 2006, and in January 2010.

### Climate change

As of 2023, the DRC is the 18th highest emitting nation of [greenhouse gases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas "Greenhouse gas") and the highest emitter on the African continent. The majority of these emissions arise from [land use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_use "Land use") change and [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation "Deforestation"), as the country uses little [fossil fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel "Fossil fuel") and only one-fifth of the population has access to electricity. The DRC is one of the most vulnerable nations to [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change "Climate change") and the least prepared to deal with its impacts. Climate change in the DRC is predicted to cause more frequent and intense floods, droughts and exacerbated disease epidemics. Sectors vulnerable to climate change include agriculture, forestry and energy.

### Biodiversity and conservation

The rainforests of the Democratic Republic of the Congo are the second-largest in the world, spanning 155 million hectares, and contain great [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity "Biodiversity"). There are over 10,000 animal species, including many rare and [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism "Endemism") ones, such as the common [chimpanzee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimpanzee "Chimpanzee") and the [bonobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonobo "Bonobo"), the [African forest elephant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_forest_elephant "African forest elephant"), [mountain gorilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_gorilla "Mountain gorilla"), [okapi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okapi "Okapi"), [forest buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_buffalo "Forest buffalo"), [leopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_leopard "African leopard") and, further south in the country, the [southern white rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_white_rhinoceros "Southern white rhinoceros"). Five of the country's national parks are listed as [World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Site "World Heritage Site"): the [Garumba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garamba_National_Park "Garamba National Park"), [Kahuzi-Biega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahuzi-Bi%C3%A9ga_National_Park "Kahuzi-Biéga National Park"), [Salonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salonga_National_Park "Salonga National Park") and [Virunga National Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virunga_National_Park "Virunga National Park"), and the [Okapi Wildlife Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okapi_Wildlife_Reserve "Okapi Wildlife Reserve"). the Democratic Republic of the Congo is one of 17 [Megadiverse countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megadiverse_countries "Megadiverse countries") and is the most biodiverse African country.

Conservationists have particularly worried about [primates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primate "Primate"). The Congo is inhabited by several [great ape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominidae "Hominidae") species: the common chimpanzee (*Pan troglodytes*), the bonobo (*Pan paniscus*), the [eastern gorilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_gorilla "Eastern gorilla") (*Gorilla beringei*), and possibly a population of the [western gorilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_gorilla "Western gorilla") (*Gorilla gorilla*). It is the only country in the world in which bonobos are found in the wild. Much concern has been raised about great ape extinction. Because of hunting and habitat destruction, the numbers of chimpanzee, bonobo and gorilla (each of whose populations once numbered in the millions) have now dwindled down to only about 200,000 gorillas, 100,000 chimpanzees and possibly only about 10,000 bonobos. The gorillas, chimpanzee, bonobo, and okapi are all classified as [endangered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_status "Conservation status") by the [World Conservation Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Union_for_Conservation_of_Nature "International Union for Conservation of Nature").

Major environmental issues in DRC include [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation "Deforestation"), [poaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poaching "Poaching"), which threatens wildlife populations, water pollution and mining. From 2015 to 2019, the rate of [deforestation in the Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Deforestation in the Democratic Republic of the Congo") doubled. In 2021, deforestation of the [Congolian rainforests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congolian_rainforests "Congolian rainforests") increased by 5%. In 2023, the rate of deforestation in the DRC was the highest in Africa. The causes include [slash-and-burn agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slash-and-burn_agriculture "Slash-and-burn agriculture") and illegal [logging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logging "Logging").

In January 2025, the DRC government established the [Kivu-Kinshasa Green Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kivu-Kinshasa_Green_Corridor "Kivu-Kinshasa Green Corridor") an initiative whose stated aim was to protect a large portion of the [Congo Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Basin "Congo Basin") forest while supporting sustainable economic development.

## Government and politics

The Democratic Republic of the Congo is, constitutionally, a [unitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state "Unitary state") and [semi-presidential republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-presidential_republic "Semi-presidential republic") in which the [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "President of the Democratic Republic of the Congo") is the head of state, elected by [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage "Universal suffrage") to a term of five years. The [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Prime Minister of the Democratic Republic of the Congo") is the head of government appointed by the president with the support of the [parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Parliament of the Democratic Republic of the Congo"). The prime minister and the [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Cabinet of the Democratic Republic of the Congo"), known as the government, are responsible to the legislature rather than to the president. The parliament is a [bicameral legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral_legislature "Bicameral legislature") consisting of the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Senate of the Democratic Republic of the Congo") and the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "National Assembly of the Democratic Republic of the Congo").

Deputies of the National Assembly are directly elected for a term of five years. Senators are elected indirectly by provincial legislatures, also for a term of five years. The president is the [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief "Commander-in-chief") of the armed forces and appoints government and military officials. The [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Constitution of the Democratic Republic of the Congo") is the basis for an independent judiciary, consisting of the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Constitutional Court of the Democratic Republic of the Congo") to evaluate the constitutionality of laws and to settle disputes regarding elections or the separation of powers; the [Court of Cassation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Cassation_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Court of Cassation of the Democratic Republic of the Congo") as the [court of last resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_last_resort "Court of last resort") in the civil and military judicial system; and the [Council of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_State_(Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo) "Council of State (Democratic Republic of the Congo)") as the highest administrative court.

The DRC's political system is considered to be [authoritarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism "Authoritarianism") and scores low on rankings of democracy, political rights, and civil liberties. Despite the constitution providing for the separation of executive, legislative, and judicial powers, in practice the president and his associates have dominated the political system. [Freedom House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_House "Freedom House") rates the country as "not free" in 2025, citing endemic corruption, the manipulation of the political system, and human rights abuses, including by security forces. *[The Economist Democracy Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist_Democracy_Index "The Economist Democracy Index")* described the DRC as an "authoritarian regime" in 2024, ranking it 156 out of the 167 countries that it evaluated.

The DRC was ranked 163 out of 180 countries in the [Corruption Perceptions Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_Perceptions_Index "Corruption Perceptions Index") for 2024. Administrative institutions remain weak, especially in rural areas, undermining the ability of government to provide basic services to the population. The state has also struggled to extend its administration over the country's entire territory. The [Fragile States Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fragile_States_Index "Fragile States Index") ranked the DRC the 5th most fragile country in the world, out of 179 countries, as of 2024.

During the presidency of [Joseph Kabila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Kabila "Joseph Kabila") from 2001 to 2019, the government was marked by corruption, a lack of transparency, and human rights violations. Kabila remained in office beyond his constitutional mandate, which ended in 2016, by delaying an election. After it was [held in late 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo_general_election "2018 Democratic Republic of the Congo general election"), Kabila was succeeded by [Félix Tshisekedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Tshisekedi "Félix Tshisekedi") in the DRC's first [peaceful transition of power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peaceful_transition_of_power "Peaceful transition of power") since independence. The election is widely seen as having been rigged in a deal made between Kabila and Tshisekedi. Tshisekedi was [reelected in late 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo_general_election "2023 Democratic Republic of the Congo general election"), and after negotiations appointed [Judith Suminwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judith_Suminwa "Judith Suminwa") as prime minister in 2024, the first woman to hold the post.

The current political system is known as the Third Republic, declared by [Laurent-Désiré Kabila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent-D%C3%A9sir%C3%A9_Kabila "Laurent-Désiré Kabila") at his presidential inauguration in May 1997. Apart from changing the country's name from Zaire back to the DR Congo and restoring its previous symbols, Kabila's government made few changes from the dictatorship of [Mobutu Sese Seko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobutu_Sese_Seko "Mobutu Sese Seko"). After the Second Congo War (1998–2003), during which Laurent-Désiré Kabila was assassinated, a transitional constitution was adopted in April 2003 as part of the peace process. It was replaced by the current constitution, the Constitution of the Third Republic, which went into effect in February 2006. The current [flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Flag of the Democratic Republic of the Congo") was also adopted.

The [Transitional Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitional_Government_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Transitional Government of the Democratic Republic of the Congo"), led by Joseph Kabila, oversaw the creation of the current system: the first cabinet took office in June 2003, the two chambers of the parliament were organized in July 2003, a constitution was adopted by a [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo_constitutional_referendum "2005 Democratic Republic of the Congo constitutional referendum") in December 2005, and the country's [first free election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo_general_election "2006 Democratic Republic of the Congo general election") in over four decades was held in July 2006. Since the process of democratization began in 1990, more than 600 registered political parties emerged in the DRC. Four of them—the [UDPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_Democracy_and_Social_Progress_(Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo) "Union for Democracy and Social Progress (Democratic Republic of the Congo)"), [PPRD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Party_for_Reconstruction_and_Democracy "People's Party for Reconstruction and Democracy"), [UNC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_the_Congolese_Nation "Union for the Congolese Nation"), and [MLC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_for_the_Liberation_of_the_Congo "Movement for the Liberation of the Congo")—successfully operate across the entire country.

### Foreign relations

There are economic and strategic incentives (for external countries) to bring more "security" to the Congo, which is rich in natural resources such as [cobalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobalt "Cobalt"), a metal used in many industrial and military applications. The country possesses 80% of the world's cobalt reserves.

It is thought that due to the importance of cobalt for batteries for electric vehicles and stabilization of electric grids with large proportions of intermittent renewables in the electricity mix, the DRC could become an object of increased geopolitical competition.

In the 21st century, [Chinese investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo_relations#Present_economic_relations "China–Democratic Republic of the Congo relations") in the DRC and Congolese exports to China have grown rapidly. In July 2019, UN ambassadors of 37 countries, including DRC, have signed a joint letter to the UNHRC defending China's [treatment of Uyghurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang_re-education_camps "Xinjiang re-education camps") and other Muslim ethnic minorities. In 2021, President [Félix Tshisekedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Tshisekedi "Félix Tshisekedi") called for a review of mining contracts signed with [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") by his predecessor [Joseph Kabila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Kabila "Joseph Kabila"), in particular the Sicomines multibillion ['minerals-for-infrastructure' deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo_relations#Sicomines "China–Democratic Republic of the Congo relations").

Although located in the Central African [UN subregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_geoscheme "United Nations geoscheme"), the nation is also economically and regionally affiliated with Southern Africa as a member of the [Southern African Development Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_African_Development_Community "Southern African Development Community") (SADC).

### Military

The military of the Democratic Republic of the Congo, known as the FARDC, consists of the [Land Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_Forces_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Land Forces of the Democratic Republic of the Congo"), the [Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Air Force of the Democratic Republic of the Congo"), and the [Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Navy of the Democratic Republic of the Congo"). There is also a separate [Republican Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Guard_(Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo) "Republican Guard (Democratic Republic of the Congo)") that is outside of the FARDC command structure, being directly responsible to the president. In 2023 there were 103,000 soldiers in the Land Forces, 6,700 in the Navy, 2,550 in the Air Force, 14,000 in the central command, and 8,000 in the Republican Guard. All services together numbered 134,250 personnel, nominally making the FARDC the largest military in Central Africa. However, the FARDC has been undermined by low levels of professionalism, training, morale, pay, and equipment, along with rampant corruption. Its lack of vehicles and aircraft makes it difficult to move troops across the country's large territory.

The FARDC was established in 2003 after the end of the Second Congo War with the integration of many former rebel groups into its ranks, and it still works with pro-government militias. Since then it has been a diverse combination of the former Zairian armed forces, rebel groups from the Congo Wars, and other militias that were integrated more recently. President Félix Tshisekedi announced the beginning of military reforms in 2022 to create a more cohesive national army. This effort included replacing most of the high command with younger officers that had a record of success and providing more military spending for the years between 2022 and 2024. Despite this, the FARDC continues to be disorganized and has systematic corruption, preventing it from fulfilling its mission to effectively defend the country's entire territory.

The armed forces are organized into geographic joint commands known as defense zones, one each for the western, south-central, and eastern DRC, which are further divided into military regions. The Land Forces consist of brigades. In 2011, those in Eastern Congo were reorganized into regiments. In 2023, it was estimated that there are nine brigades, 27 regiments, one artillery battalion, and one military police battalion. Many units are reported to be at half of their official strength or less because of combat losses and desertions. The FARDC has spent decades fighting against over 100 armed groups in Eastern Congo and the [Kasaï region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasa%C3%AF_region "Kasaï region"), including local [Mai-Mai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mai-Mai "Mai-Mai") militias, the Rwandan-backed 23 March Movement (M23), [Nduma Defense of Congo-Renovated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nduma_Defense_of_Congo-Renovated "Nduma Defense of Congo-Renovated") (NDC-R), the [Allied Democratic Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_Democratic_Forces "Allied Democratic Forces") (which has become part of the [Islamic State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State "Islamic State")), and the [Lord's Resistance Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord%27s_Resistance_Army "Lord's Resistance Army").

### Law enforcement, crime, and corruption

The [Congolese National Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congolese_National_Police "Congolese National Police"), known as the PNC, are the primary police force in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. The DRC has a [civil law system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_system "Civil law system") primarily based on [Belgian law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_law "Belgian law"), but also customary and tribal law. The country accepts the jurisdiction of the [International Criminal Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court "International Criminal Court"), having ratified the [Rome Statute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome_Statute "Rome Statute") on 11 April 2002. The Criminal Code, the Military Criminal Code, and the Code of Criminal Procedure were modified by the DRC's parliament on 31 December 2015 to be in compliance with the Rome Statute.

A relative of Mobutu explained how the government illicitly collected revenue during his rule: "Mobutu would ask one of us to go to the bank and take out a million. We'd go to an intermediary and tell him to get five million. He would go to the bank with Mobutu's authority and take out ten. Mobutu got one, and we took the other nine." Mobutu institutionalized corruption to prevent political rivals from challenging his control, leading to an [economic collapse in 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo#1990s "Economy of the Democratic Republic of the Congo").

Mobutu allegedly amassed between US$50 million and $125 million during his rule. He was not the first corrupt Congolese leader by any means: "Government as a system of organized theft goes back to King Leopold II", noted [Adam Hochschild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Hochschild "Adam Hochschild") in 2009. In July 2009, a Swiss court determined that the [statute of limitations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_limitations "Statute of limitations") had run out on an [international asset recovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_asset_recovery "International asset recovery") case of about $6.7 million of deposits of Mobutu's in a Swiss bank, and therefore the assets should be returned to Mobutu's family.

President Kabila established the Commission of Repression of Economic Crimes upon his ascension to power in 2001. However, in 2016 the [Enough Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enough_Project "Enough Project") issued a report claiming that the Congo is run as a violent [kleptocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kleptocracy "Kleptocracy").

In June 2020, a court in the Democratic Republic of the Congo found President Tshisekedi's chief of staff [Vital Kamerhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vital_Kamerhe "Vital Kamerhe") guilty of corruption. He was sentenced to 20 years' hard labour, after facing charges of embezzling almost $50m (£39m) of public funds. He was the most high-profile figure to be convicted of corruption in the DRC. However, Kamerhe was released already in December 2021.

In November 2021, a judicial investigation targeting Kabila and his associates was opened in Kinshasa after revelations of alleged embezzlement of $138 million.

### Human rights

The [International Criminal Court investigation in the Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_investigation_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "International Criminal Court investigation in the Democratic Republic of the Congo") was initiated by Kabila in April 2004. The International Criminal Court prosecutor opened the case in June 2004. [Child soldiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children_in_the_military "Children in the military") have been used on a large scale in DRC, and in 2011 it was estimated that 30,000 children were still operating with armed groups. Instances of child labor and [forced labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labour "Forced labour") have been observed and reported in the [U.S. Department of Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Labor "United States Department of Labor")'s *Findings on the Worst Forms of Child Labor* in the DRC in 2013 and six goods produced by the country's mining industry appear on the department's December 2014 *[List of Goods Produced by Child Labor or Forced Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Goods_Produced_by_Child_Labor_or_Forced_Labor "List of Goods Produced by Child Labor or Forced Labor")*.

The Democratic Republic of the Congo has prohibited [same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage "Same-sex marriage") since 2006, and attitudes towards the [LGBT community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "LGBT rights in the Democratic Republic of the Congo") are generally negative throughout the nation.
[Violence against women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence_against_women "Violence against women") is perceived by large sectors of society to be normal.
The United Nations [Committee on the Elimination of Discrimination against Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_on_the_Elimination_of_All_Forms_of_Discrimination_Against_Women "Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women") in 2006 expressed concern that in the post-war transition period, the promotion of women's human rights and gender equality is not seen as a priority.

Mass rapes, sexual violence and sexual slavery are used as a weapon of war by the Armed Forces of the Democratic Republic of the Congo and armed groups in the eastern part of the country. The eastern part of the country in particular has been described as the "rape capital of the world" and the prevalence of sexual violence there described as the worst in the world. The prevalence of [Female genital mutilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Female_genital_mutilation "Female genital mutilation") is estimated at 5% of women and is illegal.

## Economy

The economy of the DR Congo has grown from US$9.02 billion at the end of the Second Congo War in 2003 to US$72.48 billion in 2024 by [nominal GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(nominal) "List of countries by GDP (nominal)"), and from [$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_dollar "International dollar")29.23 billion to $190.13 billion by [PPP-adjusted GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(PPP) "List of countries by GDP (PPP)") during the same time period. Minerals and metal, specifically [cobalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobalt "Cobalt") and [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper "Copper"), made up 80% of the country's exports in 2023. Its largest trade partner by a significant margin is [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China"), which represented 41% of its exports in 2024, followed by [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia "Zambia"), [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa "South Africa"), [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore"), and the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates "United Arab Emirates").

The DRC's economy, especially its mining sector, received significant foreign investment, and after the early 2000s it experienced high GDP growth rates that were above the average for sub-Saharan Africa. This led to improvements in infrastructure, but the growth has not alleviated poverty for the population, the majority of which (65%) is involved in [subsistence agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsistence_agriculture "Subsistence agriculture"). Most jobs are informal and there is also very high youth unemployment.

An estimated 73.5% of people in the DRC lived below the [international poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_poverty_line "International poverty line") of US$2.15 per day as of 2024, making it among the five poorest countries in the world. The DRC had the lowest [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index "Human Development Index") of the 187 ranked countries in 2011. Agriculture is a large sector of the economy. Its output has declined over the past several decades with the DRC dependent upon food imports. In 2023, [food-price inflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_price_inflation "Food price inflation") reached 173%.

At independence in 1960, the DRC was the second-most-industrialized country in Africa after South Africa; it boasted a thriving mining sector and a relatively productive agriculture sector. It is widely considered one of the world's richest countries in natural resources; its untapped deposits of raw minerals are estimated to be worth in excess of US$24 trillion. Despite such vast mineral wealth, the economy of the DRC has declined drastically since the mid-1980s. The country generated up to 70% of its export revenue from minerals in the 1970s and 1980s and was particularly hit when resource prices deteriorated at that time. Due to the fall in commodity prices, widespread corruption, and the collapse of [the currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaire_(currency) "Zaire (currency)") from [hyperinflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperinflation "Hyperinflation"), by the early 1990s the formal economy had nearly ceased to exist, with much of the population depending on subsistence agriculture or informal bartering.

The new government tried to implement a currency reform in 1997 after the First Congo War, but was disrupted by outbreak of the Second Congo War in 1998, which worsened all of the problems, along with infrastructure breakdown and the lack of a clear legal system. Economic stability improved in 2003 with assistance from international donors following the withdrawal of foreign troops, though continuing conflicts and the resulting humanitarian crisis still undermine economic development.

In the 2020s, the DRC has taken measures to move away from its traditional [state-dominated economic model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_capitalism "State capitalism"), with state monopolies and elite control in key sectors, towards a [market economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_economy "Market economy"), with partial privatization in the mining and telecommunications sectors. It also prioritized economic liberalization and integration, having trade agreements with over 50 countries, and has taken steps to develop e-commerce. The DRC joined the [African Continental Free Trade Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Continental_Free_Trade_Area "African Continental Free Trade Area") in 2022, and aligned itself with EAC and SADC legal standards with regard to anti-competitive practices in 2023. These efforts have been stalled by government corruption, patronage networks, limited enforcement, and trade policies inconsistent with regional frameworks.

### Mining

The mining sector has been responsible for much of the DRC's economic growth since the Second Congo War. The DRC is the world's largest producer of cobalt ore, accounting for 70% of global production in 2023, and a major producer of copper and [diamonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond "Diamond"). It has 70% of the world's [coltan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coltan "Coltan"), a third of its cobalt, more than 30% of its diamond reserves, and a tenth of its copper. In February 2018, global asset management firm [AllianceBernstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllianceBernstein "AllianceBernstein") defined the DRC as economically "the Saudi Arabia of the electric vehicle age", because of its cobalt resources, cobalt being essential in the production of the [lithium-ion batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium-ion_battery "Lithium-ion battery") that power many [electric vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_vehicle "Electric vehicle").

Diamonds come from [Kasaï Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasa%C3%AF_Province "Kasaï Province") in the central DRC. By far the largest mines are located in southern [Katanga Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katanga_Province "Katanga Province") and are highly mechanized, with a capacity of several million tons per year of copper and cobalt ore, and refining capability for metal ore. The DRC is the second-largest diamond-producing nation in the world. Artisanal and small-scale miners account for most of its production.

Smaller-scale economic activity from [artisanal mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artisanal_mining "Artisanal mining") occurs in the [informal sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informal_economy "Informal economy") and is not reflected in GDP data. A third of the DRC's diamonds are believed to be smuggled out of the country, making it difficult to quantify diamond production levels. In 2002, tin was discovered in the east of the country but to date has only been mined on a small scale. Smuggling of [conflict minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_resource "Conflict resource") such as coltan and [cassiterite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassiterite "Cassiterite"), ores of [tantalum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tantalum "Tantalum") and tin, respectively, helped to fuel the war in the eastern Congo. Open-pit cobalt mining has led to [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Deforestation in the Democratic Republic of the Congo") and habitat destruction.

[Katanga Mining Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katanga_Mining "Katanga Mining"), a Swiss-owned company, owns the [Luilu](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Luilu&action=edit&redlink=1 "Luilu (page does not exist)") Metallurgical Plant, which has a capacity of 175,000 tonnes of copper and 8,000 tonnes of cobalt per year, making it the largest cobalt refinery in the world. After a major rehabilitation program, the company resumed copper production operations in December 2007 and cobalt production in May 2008.

In 2007–08, Joseph Kabila's administration entered a 'resources-for-infrastructure' deal with China, creating the [joint venture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_venture "Joint venture") [Sicomines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicomines "Sicomines") (*Sino-Congolais des Mines*), with the majority of the shares owned by the [China Railway Engineering Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Railway_Engineering_Corporation "China Railway Engineering Corporation") (CREC) while the DRC's [Gécamines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9camines "Gécamines") owned the rest. The company received mining rights in exchange for investing US$3 billion into building infrastructure. Sicomines began production in 2015. The deal has received criticism for terms that appeared to be disproportionately favorable to China at the expense of the DRC. Félix Tshisekedi's administration ordered an investigation into the deal, which concluded that less than US$1 billion had been spent on infrastructure. Tshisekedi renegotiated the agreement to add new terms, and in 2024 this led to the infrastructure investment being increased to US$7 billion.

In April 2013, anti-corruption NGOs revealed that Congolese tax authorities had failed to account for $88 million from the mining sector, despite booming production and positive industrial performance. The missing funds date from 2010 and tax bodies should have paid them into the central bank. Later in 2013, the [Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extractive_Industries_Transparency_Initiative "Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative") suspended the country's candidacy for membership due to insufficient reporting, monitoring and independent audits, but in July 2013 the country improved its accounting and transparency practices to the point where the EITI gave the country full membership.

### Transportation

The DRC has 152,373 kilometres (94,680 mi) of roads, out of which only 3,047 kilometres (1,893 mi) are paved. It also has 4,007 kilometres (2,490 mi) of railways, with most being [narrow-gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrow-gauge_railway "Narrow-gauge railway"). The infrastructure is in a state of disrepair, and the national highway system is very limited; reaching the capital by road is not possible from many parts of the country. Since the early 2000s there have been improvements to the road network, but the dense forests and numerous rivers in the DRC make construction and maintenance difficult. Air and river transportation have an important role due to the terrain and the poor state of the road and rail networks.

Air travel has seen an increase since the early 2000s, with 24 city pairs having airline service as of 2007, although it has a poor safety record. All air carriers certified by the DRC have been banned from European Union airports because of inadequate safety standards. Despite this, airlines are seen as the most reliable form of domestic travel. There are eight airlines in the country, including the [flag carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_carrier "Flag carrier") [Congo Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Airways "Congo Airways"), and several international airlines service [Kinshasa's international airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%27djili_Airport "N'djili Airport"). Besides Kinshasa there are three other international airports in the DRC, which are at [Lubumbashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubumbashi_International_Airport "Lubumbashi International Airport"), [Kisangani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisangani_Bangoka_International_Airport "Kisangani Bangoka International Airport"), and [Goma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goma_International_Airport "Goma International Airport").

The DRC has about 15,000 kilometres (9,300 mi) of [navigable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigable "Navigable") [waterways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterway "Waterway"), with the Congo River serving as the spine. Water transport has traditionally been the dominant means of moving around in the DRC and is also used to fill gaps between roads. Around two million tons of cargo pass through the port of Kinshasa on the Congo River every year, more than triple the volume moved by the national railroad company, the [SNCC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soci%C3%A9t%C3%A9_nationale_des_chemins_de_fer_du_Congo "Société nationale des chemins de fer du Congo"). River transports are owned by many private operators. The DRC's three economic hubs—Kinshasa in the west, Lubumbashi in the south, and Kinsangani in the northeast—are not connected by roads or rail.

The rail system is concentrated in the southeast. Kinshasa is connected by river ferry to [Ilebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilebo "Ilebo"), where the rail line to Lubumbashi begins. This line is also critical for the movement of metal and minerals from the southern DRC to ports in Angola or South Africa (via Zambia) to be exported overseas. There is an electrified line between Kinshasa and the Atlantic seaport of Matadi. The track and [rolling stock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_stock "Rolling stock") of the SNCC system is in poor condition, though the more recently built Matadi–Kinshasa line has better track.

There are 44 [national roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_roads_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "List of roads in the Democratic Republic of the Congo") with a total of 58,358 kilometres (36,262 mi). Three of them are considered the most important. [National Road No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Road_1_(Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo) "National Road 1 (Democratic Republic of the Congo)") (RN1) is the main highway of the road system, connecting seaports in Kongo Central with Kinshasa and cities in the interior, such as Lubumbashi. RN1 reaches the border with Zambia in the south. [National Road 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Road_2_(Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo) "National Road 2 (Democratic Republic of the Congo)") (RN2) connects the central city of Mbuji-Mayi with Goma in the east, with most of it outside of the Kivu region being in a bad condition, and [National Road 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Road_3_(Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo) "National Road 3 (Democratic Republic of the Congo)") (RN3) connects Goma to Kisangani, from where a river boat can be taken to Kinshasa.

### Energy

The generation and distribution of electricity is controlled by [Société Nationale d'Électricité](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soci%C3%A9t%C3%A9_Nationale_d%27%C3%89lectricit%C3%A9 "Société Nationale d'Électricité") (SNEL), but only 15% of the country has access to electricity. The DRC has the infrastructure for hydro-electricity from the Congo River at the [Inga dams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inga_dams "Inga dams"). Both coal and crude oil resources were mainly used domestically up to 2008.

In 2010, the DRC had 2,400 [megawatts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megawatt "Megawatt") of [installed capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Installed_capacity "Installed capacity"), with only 1,000 MW functioning. The only interconnected part of the power grid is the high voltage transmission line between the Inga dams in Kongo Central and the southern DRC. This line runs to the Zambian border and is also used to export electricity to Zambia, Zimbabwe, and South Africa. Almost half of companies in the DRC have their own generators due to blackouts and the limitations of the power grid.

The DRC has a river system that could provide hydro-electric power to the entire continent, according to a UN report on the country's strategic significance and its potential role as an economic power in central Africa. The DRC's electricity generation potential from hydro-power is estimated at 100,000 MW. The DRC is a member of three electrical power pools. These are Southern African Power Pool, East African Power Pool, and Central African Power Pool.

Because of abundant sunlight, the potential for solar power is very high in the DRC. There are already about 836 solar power systems in the DRC, with a total power of 83 MW, located in [Équateur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_%C3%89quateur "Province of Équateur") (167), Katanga (159), Nord-Kivu (170), the two Kasaï provinces (170), and Bas-Congo (170). Also, the 148 Caritas network system has a total power of 6.31 MW.

## Demographics

The DRC has a high annual population growth rate, and the population increased from 51.9 million in 2000 to 105 million in 2021. Between 1950 and 2000, the country's population nearly quadrupled from 12.2 million to 46.9 million.

In 2026, 52% of the population had access to water services (29% in rural areas). 31% have access to sanitation services (21% in rural areas). The DRC also lacks state-sponsored social safety nets, with 12% of formal workers, mostly government employees, being enrolled in the National Social Security Fund.

### Ethnic groups

Over 250 ethnic groups and 450 tribes (ethnic subgroups) populate the DRC. They are in the [Bantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_languages "Bantu languages"), [Sudanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanic_languages "Sudanic languages"), [Nilotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilotic_languages "Nilotic languages"), [Ubangian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adamawa%E2%80%93Ubangi_languages "Adamawa–Ubangi languages") and [Pygmy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classification_of_Pygmy_languages "Classification of Pygmy languages") linguistic groups. Because of this diversity, there is no dominant ethnic group in the Congo, however the following ethnic groups account for 51.5% of the population:

* [Luba-Kasaï](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luba-Kasai_language "Luba-Kasai language")
* [Kongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_people "Kongo people")
* [Mongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongo_people "Mongo people")
* [Lubakat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luba-Katanga_language "Luba-Katanga language")

In 2021, the UN estimated the country's population to be 96 million, a rapid increase from 39.1 million in 1992 despite the ongoing war. As many as 250 ethnic groups have been identified and named. About 600,000 [Pygmies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Pygmies "Congo Pygmies") live in the DRC.

### Migration

Given the often unstable situation in the country and the condition of state structures, it is extremely difficult to obtain reliable migration data. However, evidence suggests that DRC continues to be a destination country for immigrants, in spite of recent declines in their numbers. Immigration is very diverse in nature; refugees and asylum-seekers – products of the numerous and violent conflicts in the [Great Lakes Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Great_Lakes "African Great Lakes") – constitute an important subset of the population. The country's large mine operations attract migrant workers from Africa and beyond. There is also considerable migration for commercial activities from other African countries and the rest of the world, but these movements are not well studied. Transit migration towards South Africa and Europe also plays a role.

Immigration to the DRC has decreased steadily over the past two decades, most likely as a result of the armed violence that the country has experienced. The number of immigrants in the DRC has fallen from just over one million in 1960, to 754,000 in 1990, to 480,000 in 2005, to an estimated 445,000 in 2010. Official figures are unavailable, partly due to the predominance of the [informal economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informal_economy "Informal economy") in the DRC. Data is lacking on irregular immigrants, however given neighbouring countries' ethnic links to DRC nationals, irregular migration is assumed to be a significant phenomenon.

Figures for Congolese nationals abroad vary greatly depending on the source; sources believe there are between three and six million Congolese living abroad. This discrepancy is due to a lack of official, reliable data. Emigrants from the DRC are above all long-term emigrants, the majority of whom live in Africa and to a lesser extent in Europe; 79.7% and 15.3% respectively, in 2000.

New destination countries include South Africa and various points en route to Europe. The DRC has produced a considerable number of [refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugees "Refugees") and [asylum-seekers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asylum-seekers "Asylum-seekers") located in the region and beyond. These numbers peaked in 2004 when there were more than 460,000 refugees from the DRC; in 2008, Congolese refugees numbered 367,995 in total, 68% of whom were living in other African countries.

Since 2003, more than 400,000 Congolese migrants have been expelled from [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola "Angola").

[Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_people_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "White people in the Democratic Republic of the Congo") and Asians makeup a significant part of the Democratic Republic of the Congo's migrant population. Most Europeans and Asians went to the country for temporary employment.

#### Forced displacement and refugees

The DRC has around 6.9 million people displaced within its borders, more than five million of whom are in the eastern provinces of North Kivu, South Kivu and Ituri. In addition, over 990,000 Congolese refugees and asylum seekers live elsewhere on the African continent. The internal displacement crisis in the DRC is the most severe in Africa.

### Languages

[French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language "French language") is the [official language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language "Official language") of the Democratic Republic of the Congo. It is culturally accepted as the *[lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca "Lingua franca")*, facilitating communication among the many different ethnic groups of the Congo. In 2018, 49 million Congolese people (51% of the population) could read and write French. A 2021 survey found that 74% of the population could speak French, making it the most widely spoken language in the country.

In Kinshasa, 67% of the population in 2014 could read and write French, and 68.5% could speak and understand it.

In 2024, there were around 12 million native French speakers in the country.

Approximately 242 languages are spoken in the DRC, of which four have the status of national languages: [Kituba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kituba_language "Kituba language") (Kikongo), [Lingala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingala "Lingala"), [Tshiluba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luba-Kasai_language "Luba-Kasai language"), and [Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_language "Swahili language") ([Congo Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Swahili "Congo Swahili")). Although some limited number of people speak these as first languages, most of the population speak them as a second language, after the native language of their own ethnic group. Lingala was the official language of the *Force Publique* under Belgian colonial rule and remains to this day the predominant language of the armed forces. Since the recent rebellions, a good part of the army in the east also uses Swahili, where it competes to be the regional *lingua franca*.

Under [Belgian rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_colonial_empire "Belgian colonial empire"), the Belgians instituted the teaching and use of the four Bantu languages in primary schools, making it one of the few African nations to have literacy in local languages during the European colonial period. This trend was reversed after independence, when French became the sole language of education at all levels. Since 1975, the four national languages have been reintroduced in the first two years of primary education, with French becoming the sole language of education from the third year onward, but in practice many primary schools in urban areas solely use French from the first year of school onward.

[Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language "Portuguese language") is taught in public schools as a foreign language due to the [Angolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola "Angola"), and to a lesser extent [Mozambican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique "Mozambique") immigrant and refugee communities. Brazil has also been promoting the language in Africa. The [lexical similarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexical_similarity "Lexical similarity") and comparable phonology of French to Portuguese makes it a relatively easy language for people to learn. Many of the roughly 175,000 Portuguese speakers in the DRC are Angolans who fled violence during the [Angolan War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_War_of_Independence "Angolan War of Independence") and [Angolan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_Civil_War "Angolan Civil War") of the late 20th century. Most of them have been repatriated back since the war ended in 2002.

### Religion

Christianity is the predominant religion of the DRC. In 2013, Christians constituted 93.7% of the population, with Catholics making up 29.7%, Protestants 26.8%, and other Christians 37.2%. A new Christian religious movement, [Kimbanguism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimbanguism "Kimbanguism"), had the adherence of 2.8%, while Muslims made up 1%. Other recent estimates have found Christianity the majority religion, followed by 95.8% of the population according to a 2010 [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center "Pew Research Center") estimate. The [CIA World Factbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA_World_Factbook "CIA World Factbook") reports this figure to be 95.9%. The proportion of followers of Islam is estimated from 1% to 12%.

In 2014, there were about 35 million Catholics in the country with six [archdioceses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese "Archdiocese") and 41 [dioceses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioceses "Dioceses"). The impact of the Catholic Church is difficult to overestimate. Schatzberg has called it the country's "only truly national institution apart from the state". Its schools have educated over 60% of the nation's primary school students and more than 40% of its secondary students. The church owns and manages an extensive network of hospitals, schools, and clinics, as well as many diocesan economic enterprises, including farms, ranches, stores, and artisans' shops.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Sixty-two Protestant denominations are federated under the umbrella of the [Church of Christ in the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Christ_in_the_Congo "Church of Christ in the Congo"). It is often referred to as *the Protestant Church*, since it covers most of the DRC Protestants. With more than 25 million members, it constitutes [one of the largest Protestant bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_largest_Protestant_denominations "List of the largest Protestant denominations") in the world.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Kimbanguism was seen as a threat to the colonial regime and was banned by the Belgians. Kimbanguism, officially "the church of Christ on Earth by the prophet Simon Kimbangu", has about three million members, primarily among the Bakongo of [Kongo Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_Central "Kongo Central") and Kinshasa.

Islam has been present in the Democratic Republic of the Congo since the 18th century, brought by Arab traders. Today, Muslims constitute approximately 1% of the Congolese population according to the Pew Research Center. The majority are [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam "Sunni Islam") Muslims.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The first members of the [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith "Baháʼí Faith") to live in the country came from Uganda in 1953. Four years later, the first local administrative council was elected. In 1970, the National Spiritual Assembly (national administrative council) was first elected. Though the religion was banned in the 1970s and 1980s, due to misrepresentations of foreign governments, the ban was lifted by the end of the 1980s. In 2012, plans were announced to build a national [Baháʼí House of Worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_House_of_Worship "Baháʼí House of Worship") in the country.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Traditional religions embody such concepts as [monotheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotheism "Monotheism"), [animism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animism "Animism"), [vitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitalism "Vitalism"), [spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_worship "Spirit worship") and [ancestor worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestor_worship "Ancestor worship"), [witchcraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witchcraft "Witchcraft"), and sorcery and vary widely among ethnic groups. The syncretic sects often merge elements of Christianity with traditional beliefs and rituals and are not recognized by mainstream churches as part of Christianity. New variants of ancient beliefs have become widespread, led by US-inspired Pentecostal churches which have been in the forefront of witchcraft accusations, particularly against children and the elderly.[*[clarification needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")*]

Children accused of witchcraft are sent away from homes and family, often to live on the street, which can lead to physical violence against these children.[*[clarification needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")*] There are charities supporting street children such as the [Congo Children Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Children_Trust "Congo Children Trust"). The Congo Children Trust's flagship project is Kimbilio, which works to reunite street children in [Lubumbashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubumbashi "Lubumbashi").

The usual term for these children is *enfants sorciers* (child witches) or *enfants dits sorciers* (children accused of witchcraft). Non-denominational church organizations have been formed to capitalize on this belief by charging exorbitant fees for [exorcisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exorcisms "Exorcisms"). Though recently outlawed, children have been subjected in these exorcisms to often-violent abuse at the hands of self-proclaimed prophets and priests.

### Education

In 2014, the [literacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy "Literacy") rate for the population between the ages of 15 and 49 was estimated to be 75.9% (88.1% male and 63.8% female). The education system is governed by three government ministries: the *Ministère de l'Enseignement Primaire, Secondaire et Professionnel (MEPSP*), the *Ministère de l'Enseignement Supérieur et Universitaire (MESU)* and the *Ministère des Affaires Sociales (MAS)*. Primary education is neither free nor compulsory,[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] even though the Congolese constitution says it should be (Article 43 of the 2005 Congolese Constitution).

As a result of the [First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Congo_War "First Congo War") and [Second Congo Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Congo_War "Second Congo War") in the late 1990s—early 2000s, over 5.2 million children in the country did not receive any education. Since the end of the civil war, the situation has improved tremendously, with the number of children enrolled in primary schools rising from 5.5 million in 2002 to 16.8 million in 2018, and the number of children enrolled in secondary schools rising from 2.8 million in 2007 to 4.6 million in 2015.

Actual school attendance has also improved greatly in recent years, with primary school net attendance estimated to be 82.4% in 2014 (82.4% of children ages 6–11 attended school; 83.4% for boys, 80.6% for girls).

### Health

The DRC faces persistent healthcare challenges, including outbreaks of [Ebola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebola "Ebola"), [measles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measles "Measles"), and [malaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaria "Malaria"), and its healthcare system is under-resourced. Inadequate infrastructure limits emergency response capability. The result is difficulty in providing transportation for medical treatment and supplies. Financial inequalities prevent access to services. Medical supplies and equipment have also been looted from healthcare facilities. In of 2024, the DRC spent 3.1% of its GDP on healthcare, and 75% of Congolese rely on out-of-pocket payments for medical treatment. [Hospitals in the Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_hospitals_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "List of hospitals in the Democratic Republic of the Congo") include the [General Hospital of Kinshasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinshasa_General_Hospital "Kinshasa General Hospital").

The DRC has the world's second-highest rate of [infant mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality "Infant mortality") (after Chad). In April 2011, through aid from [Global Alliance for Vaccines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAVI "GAVI"), a new vaccine to prevent [pneumococcal disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streptococcus_pneumoniae "Streptococcus pneumoniae") was introduced around Kinshasa. In 2012, it was estimated that about 1.1% of adults aged 15–49 were living with [HIV/AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS "HIV/AIDS"). [Malaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaria "Malaria") and [yellow fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_fever "Yellow fever") are problems.

In early 2025, there was a 50% drop in children under the age of five years old visiting medical facilities, compared to a year earlier. The number of vaccinated children dropped from 67,000 to 29,000 when comparing the first quarter of 2023 to that of 2025.

In May 2026, the World Health Organization declared the [Ebola outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Ebola_epidemic "2026 Ebola epidemic") caused by the [Bundibugyo virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundibugyo_virus "Bundibugyo virus") in the Democratic Republic of the Congo a [public health emergency of international concern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health_emergency_of_international_concern "Public health emergency of international concern") (PHEIC).

The incidence of yellow fever-related fatalities in DRC is relatively low. In 2021, only two individuals died due to yellow fever in DRC.

In 2016, 26,529 people died on the roads in DRC due to traffic accidents.

[Maternal health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternal_health "Maternal health") is poor in DRC. In 2010, the DRC had the 17th highest [maternal mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternal_death "Maternal death") rate in the world. In 2015, 43.5% of children under five were [stunted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stunted_growth "Stunted growth").

In 2020, the United Nations emergency food relief agency warned that amid the escalating conflict and worsening situation following [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronavirus_disease_2019 "Coronavirus disease 2019") in the DRC, millions of lives were at risk as they could die of hunger. In 2020, four in ten people in Congo lacked food security and about 15.6 million were facing a potential hunger crisis.

[Air pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_pollution "Air pollution") levels in the Democratic Republic of the Congo are very unhealthy. In 2020, annual average air pollution in the DRC stood at 34.2 μg/m3, which is almost 6.8 times the [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization "World Health Organization") PM2.5 guideline (5 μg/m3: set in September 2021). These pollution levels are estimated to reduce the [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy "Life expectancy") of an average citizen of the DRC by almost 2.9 years. Currently, the DRC does not have a national ambient air quality standard.

## Culture

The culture of the Democratic Republic of the Congo reflects the diversity of its numerous ethnic groups and their differing ways of life throughout the country—from the mouth of the River Congo on the coast, upriver through the rainforest and savanna in its centre, to the more densely populated mountains in the far east. Since the late 19th century, traditional ways of life have undergone changes brought about by colonialism, the struggle for independence, the stagnation of the Mobutu era, and most recently, the First and Second Congo Wars. Despite these pressures, the customs and cultures of the Congo have retained much of their individuality. The country's 81 million inhabitants (2016) are mainly rural. The 30% who live in urban areas have been the most open to [Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_culture "Western culture") influences.

### Literature

French-language Congolese literature developed particularly from the end of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), during the period of the [Belgian Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_Congo "Belgian Congo"). Early figures included [Antoine-Roger Bolamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine-Roger_Bolamba "Antoine-Roger Bolamba") and [Paul Lomami-Tshibamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Lomami-Tshibamba "Paul Lomami-Tshibamba").

After independence, Congolese literature diversified, with [V. Y. Mudimbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V._Y._Mudimbe "V. Y. Mudimbe") becoming a prominent writer and scholar of the postcolonial period. Contemporary Congolese literature includes writers such as [In Koli Jean Bofane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Koli_Jean_Bofane "In Koli Jean Bofane"), [Fiston Mwanza Mujila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiston_Mwanza_Mujila "Fiston Mwanza Mujila"), [Sinzo Aanza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinzo_Aanza "Sinzo Aanza") and [Blaise Ndala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaise_Ndala "Blaise Ndala").

### Music

Congo has a rich musical heritage, rooted in traditional rhythms. The earliest known form of popular [partnered dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partner_dance "Partner dance") music in Congo was *Maringa*, denoting a [Kongolese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_people "Kongo people") dance practised within the former [Kingdom of Loango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Loango "Kingdom of Loango"), encompassing parts of the present-day Republic of the Congo, southern Gabon and [Cabinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinda_Province "Cabinda Province"). The style gained popularity in the 1920s–1930s, introducing the "bar-dancing" culture in [Léopoldville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinshasa "Kinshasa") (now Kinshasa), incorporating unique elements like a [bass drum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bass_drum "Bass drum"), a bottle as a [triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangle_(musical_instrument) "Triangle (musical instrument)"), and an [accordion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accordion "Accordion").

In the 1940s and 1950s, the influence of [Cuban son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_cubano "Son cubano") bands transformed *Maringa* into "[Congolese rumba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congolese_rumba "Congolese rumba")". Imported records by Sexteto Habanero and Trio Matamoros, often mislabeled as "rumba", played a significant role. Artists such as Antoine Kasongo, Paul Kamba, [Henri Bowane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Bowane "Henri Bowane"), [Antoine Wendo Kolosoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendo_Kolosoy "Wendo Kolosoy"), [Franco Luambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Luambo "Franco Luambo"), [Le Grand Kallé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Grand_Kall%C3%A9 "Le Grand Kallé"), [Vicky Longomba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicky_Longomba "Vicky Longomba"), [Nico Kasanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nico_Kasanda "Nico Kasanda"), [Tabu Ley Rochereau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabu_Ley_Rochereau "Tabu Ley Rochereau"), and [Papa Noël Nedule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papa_No%C3%ABl_Nedule "Papa Noël Nedule") authentically popularized the style and made significant contributions to it in the 1940s and 1950s.

The 1960s and 1970s saw the emergence of [Zaïko Langa Langa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Za%C3%AFko_Langa_Langa "Zaïko Langa Langa"), one of the most innovative groups of the genre, which has crossed generations and is considered part of Congolese heritage and pop culture, as well as [soukous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soukous "Soukous"), an [urban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_contemporary_music "Urban contemporary music") [dance music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance_music "Dance music") style that evolved from Congolese rumba. Soukous led to diverse offshoots, such as *ekonda saccadé*, reflecting the [Mongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongo_people "Mongo people") rhythmic influence, and *mokonyonyon*, emulating [pelvic thrust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelvic_thrust "Pelvic thrust") dance movements from the [Otetela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetela_people "Tetela people") ethnic background. The same soukous, under the guidance of "*[le sapeur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Sape "La Sape")*", [Papa Wemba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papa_Wemba "Papa Wemba"), have set the tone for a generation of young men always dressed up in exorbitant designer clothes. They came to be known as the fourth generation of Congolese music and mostly come from the former prominent band [Wenge Musica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenge_Musica "Wenge Musica").

Political and economic challenges under Mobutu prompted a mass exodus of musicians to Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Zambia, [Sierra Leone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Leone "Sierra Leone"), [Liberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberia "Liberia"), [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe "Europe") and [Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia "Asia"), expanding the spread of Congolese urban music. Notably, the quartet Ry-Co Jazz played a crucial role in globalizing Congolese music, touring [West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Africa "West Africa"), the [Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean "Caribbean"), and France. By the 1980s, numerous Congolese musicians were based in Europe, facilitating the global dissemination of their musical prowess. Congolese lead guitarists became a sought-after commodity, attracting bands worldwide eager to infuse a Congolese flavor into their compositions or learn the intricate art of Congolese guitar dexterity.

### Media

Newspapers of the DRC include *[L'Avenir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%27Avenir_(Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo_newspaper) "L'Avenir (Democratic Republic of the Congo newspaper)")*, *Radion Télévision Mwangaza*, *[La Conscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=La_Conscience&action=edit&redlink=1 "La Conscience (page does not exist)") [[fr](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Conscience "fr:La Conscience")]*, *[L'Observateur](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=L%27Observateur&action=edit&redlink=1 "L'Observateur (page does not exist)") [[fr](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%27Observateur_(RDC) "fr:L'Observateur (RDC)")]*, *Le Phare*, *[Le Potentiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Potentiel "Le Potentiel")*, *Le Soft* and *LeCongolais.CD*, a web-based daily. ### Cuisine

The [cuisine of the Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congolese_cuisine "Congolese cuisine") varies widely, representing the food of indigenous people. [Cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava "Cassava"), *[fufu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fufu "Fufu")*, rice, plantain and potatoes are generally the staple foods.

### Sports

Many sports are played in the DRC, including [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Football in the Democratic Republic of the Congo"), basketball, baseball, and [rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_union_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Rugby union in the Democratic Republic of the Congo"). The sports are played in numerous stadiums throughout the country, including the [Stade Frederic Kibassa Maliba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Frederic_Kibassa_Maliba "Stade Frederic Kibassa Maliba").

As Zaire, they participated in the [1974 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup "1974 FIFA World Cup"), where they would be drawn into a group containing Scotland, Yugoslavia, and Brazil. The team would concede fourteen goals and fail to score, finishing bottom of the group.

In the [2026 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup "2026 FIFA World Cup") the DRC drew with [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal_national_football_team "Portugal national football team") on the way to finishing third in their group and progressing to the knockout stage, where they put up a magnificent performance against [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_national_football_team "England national football team") in the round of 32 but lost 2-1.

Internationally, the country is especially famous for its professional basketball [NBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Basketball_Association "National Basketball Association") and football players. [Dikembe Mutombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dikembe_Mutombo "Dikembe Mutombo") is one of the best African basketball players to ever play the game. Mutombo is well known for humanitarian projects in his home country. [Bismack Biyombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bismack_Biyombo "Bismack Biyombo"), [Christian Eyenga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Eyenga "Christian Eyenga"), [Jonathan Kuminga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Kuminga "Jonathan Kuminga"), and [Emmanuel Mudiay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Mudiay "Emmanuel Mudiay") are others who gained significant international attention in basketball. Several Congolese players and players of Congolese descent—including [Romelu Lukaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romelu_Lukaku "Romelu Lukaku"), [Yannick Bolasie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yannick_Bolasie "Yannick Bolasie"), [Yoane Wissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoane_Wissa "Yoane Wissa"), and [Dieumerci Mbokani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieumerci_Mbokani "Dieumerci Mbokani")—have gained prominence in world football. DR Congo has twice won the [African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa_Cup_of_Nations "Africa Cup of Nations") football tournament.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

DR Congo's women's national volleyball team lastly qualified for the [2021 Women's African Nations Volleyball Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Women%27s_African_Nations_Volleyball_Championship "2021 Women's African Nations Volleyball Championship"). The country featured a national team in [beach volleyball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beach_volleyball "Beach volleyball") that competed at the [2018–2020 CAVB Beach Volleyball Continental Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%932020_CAVB_Beach_Volleyball_Continental_Cup "2018–2020 CAVB Beach Volleyball Continental Cup") in both the women's and the men's section.
