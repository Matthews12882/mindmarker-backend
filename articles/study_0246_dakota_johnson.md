# Dakota Johnson

**Dakota Mayi Johnson** (born October 4, 1989) is an American actress. [Her accolades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Dakota_Johnson "List of awards and nominations received by Dakota Johnson") include nominations for a [British Academy Film Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award "BAFTA Award") and a [Critics' Choice Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Award "Critics' Choice Award"). She was featured in the [*Forbes* 30 Under 30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_30_Under_30 "Forbes 30 Under 30") list in 2016.

The daughter of actors [Don Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Johnson "Don Johnson") and [Melanie Griffith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanie_Griffith "Melanie Griffith"), she began acting at a young age. Johnson's early credits include a supporting role in the biographical drama film *[The Social Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Social_Network "The Social Network")* (2010), before landing a starring role in the [Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company "Fox Broadcasting Company") sitcom *[Ben and Kate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_and_Kate "Ben and Kate")* (2012–2013). Her [breakthrough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_role "Breakthrough role") came as Anastasia Steele in the [*Fifty Shades* film trilogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifty_Shades_(film_series) "Fifty Shades (film series)") (2015–2018), which collectively grossed over $1 billion worldwide and established her as a [sex symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_symbol "Sex symbol"). She has since starred in major-studio films such as *[How to Be Single](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_to_Be_Single "How to Be Single")* (2016), *[Bad Times at the El Royale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Times_at_the_El_Royale "Bad Times at the El Royale")* (2018), *[Madame Web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madame_Web_(film) "Madame Web (film)")* (2024), and *[Materialists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materialists_(film) "Materialists (film)")* (2025), and earned praise for her performances in *[The Peanut Butter Falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Peanut_Butter_Falcon "The Peanut Butter Falcon")* (2019), *[Cha Cha Real Smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cha_Cha_Real_Smooth "Cha Cha Real Smooth")* (2022), and *[Am I OK?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am_I_OK%3F "Am I OK?")* (2022).

## Early life and background

Dakota Mayi Johnson was born on October 4, 1989, in [Austin, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin,_Texas "Austin, Texas"), to actors [Don Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Johnson "Don Johnson") and [Melanie Griffith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanie_Griffith "Melanie Griffith"). Her father was shooting the film *[The Hot Spot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hot_Spot "The Hot Spot")* (1990) in Texas when she was born. Her maternal grandparents are advertising executive and former child actor Peter Griffith, and actress [Tippi Hedren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tippi_Hedren "Tippi Hedren"). She is a half-niece of actress [Tracy Griffith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracy_Griffith "Tracy Griffith") and [production designer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_designer "Production designer") Clay A. Griffith. Actor [Antonio Banderas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Banderas "Antonio Banderas") is her former stepfather. Her maternal ancestry is Swedish and Norwegian.

Johnson has a complex family, as each of her parents had children by three different people. Her six half-siblings are: [Jesse Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Johnson_(actor) "Jesse Johnson (actor)") (b. 1982), from her father's relationship with [Patti D'Arbanville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patti_D%27Arbanville "Patti D'Arbanville"); Alexander Bauer (b. 1985), from her mother's marriage to [Steven Bauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Bauer "Steven Bauer"); Stella Banderas (b. 1996), from her mother's marriage to Banderas; Grace (b. 1999), Jasper (b. 2002), and Deacon Johnson (b. 2006), from her father's marriage to Kelley Phleger.

Owing to her parents' occupations, Johnson spent much of her childhood in their various filming locations and accompanying them to premieres. She also spent time in [Aspen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspen,_Colorado "Aspen, Colorado") and [Woody Creek, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Creek,_Colorado "Woody Creek, Colorado"), where, in her teen years, she worked summers at the local market. In Woody Creek, [Hunter S. Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_S._Thompson "Hunter S. Thompson") was one of her neighbors. She also attended the Aspen Community School for a time. "I was so consistently unmoored and discombobulated, I didn't have an anchor anywhere," Johnson recalled. She attended the Santa Catalina School in [Monterey, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monterey,_California "Monterey, California"), for her freshman year of high school before transferring to the private New Roads School in [Santa Monica, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica,_California "Santa Monica, California").

Johnson became interested in modeling at age 12 after participating in a photo shoot with other celebrities' children for *[Teen Vogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teen_Vogue "Teen Vogue").* She subsequently earned an income modeling while attending high school in Santa Monica. She has struggled with [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_depressive_disorder "Major depressive disorder") since around age 14 and checked into rehabilitation. Even as a child, Johnson was interested in acting, having spent significant time on film sets with her parents, but they discouraged her from pursuing the profession until she graduated from high school. After reaching that milestone, she applied to [Juilliard School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juilliard_School "Juilliard School"), performing in her audition monologues by [Shakespeare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakespeare "Shakespeare") and [Steve Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Martin "Steve Martin"), but was not accepted.

## Career

### 1999–2014: Beginnings

In 1999, Johnson made her film debut in *[Crazy in Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crazy_in_Alabama "Crazy in Alabama")*, where she and her half-sister Stella Banderas played daughters to their real-life mother, Griffith. The film was directed by Griffith's then-husband Antonio Banderas and was based on the 1993 novel by [Mark Childress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Childress "Mark Childress"). In 2006, she was chosen as [Miss Golden Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Ambassador "Golden Globe Ambassador"), where she served as the first second-generation Miss Golden Globe in the Globes' history. That same year, Johnson signed with [IMG Models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMG_Models "IMG Models"). Though acting is her primary work, she has since modeled for [MANGO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mango_(retailer) "Mango (retailer)") brand's jeans line in 2009 and shot the "Rising Star" campaign for Australian fashion label Wish in 2011.

After graduating from high school, Johnson took [acting classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama_school "Drama school") with teacher Tom Todoroff until 2008. She signed with the [William Morris Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Morris_Agency "William Morris Agency") and started her acting career. She had a minor role in [David Fincher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Fincher "David Fincher")'s [biographical film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biographical_film "Biographical film") *[The Social Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Social_Network "The Social Network")* (2010) and also had a small role in the fantasy film *[Beastly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beastly_(film) "Beastly (film)")* (2011). Johnson starred in [So Yong Kim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/So_Yong_Kim "So Yong Kim")'s drama *[For Ellen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Ellen "For Ellen")* (2012), alongside [Paul Dano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Dano "Paul Dano") and [Jon Heder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Heder "Jon Heder"). The film is about a struggling musician in the midst of a custody battle. Also in 2012, she played a student at a prep school in [Christopher Neil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Neil "Christopher Neil")'s independent comedy *[Goats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goats_(film) "Goats (film)")*. She also starred in [Nicholas Stoller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Stoller "Nicholas Stoller")'s romance film *[The Five-Year Engagement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Five-Year_Engagement "The Five-Year Engagement")* and the comedy *[21 Jump Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21_Jump_Street_(film) "21 Jump Street (film)")*. She had a leading role for Chris Nelson's film *[Date and Switch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_and_Switch "Date and Switch")*.

In March 2012, Johnson had a leading role in the [Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company "Fox Broadcasting Company") comedy television series *[Ben and Kate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_and_Kate "Ben and Kate")*. It was canceled on January 25, 2013, after one season. Johnson continued acting in films, with a small role in *[Need for Speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Need_for_Speed_(film) "Need for Speed (film)")* (2014). In 2013, she had a role as one of the new hires on the [series finale episode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finale_(The_Office) "Finale (The Office)") of the [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC "NBC") comedy series *[The Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Office_(American_TV_series) "The Office (American TV series)")*.

### 2015–2019: Breakthrough

Johnson's breakthrough came with her leading role as Anastasia "Ana" Steele in the erotic romantic drama film *[Fifty Shades of Grey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifty_Shades_of_Grey_(film) "Fifty Shades of Grey (film)")*, which was released in February 2015 and brought her international recognition. Johnson won the role over [Lucy Hale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucy_Hale "Lucy Hale"), [Felicity Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felicity_Jones "Felicity Jones"), [Elizabeth Olsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Olsen "Elizabeth Olsen"), [Danielle Panabaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danielle_Panabaker "Danielle Panabaker"), and [Shailene Woodley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shailene_Woodley "Shailene Woodley"). In response to questions regarding her stance on gender rights concerning her character in the *[Fifty Shades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifty_Shades_(film_series) "Fifty Shades (film series)")* film series, Johnson stated: "I'm proud of [the film]. I completely disagree with people who think Ana's weak. I think she's actually stronger than *he* is. Everything she does is her choice. And if I can be an advocate for women to do what they want to do with their bodies and not be ashamed of what they want, then I'm all for that." While the trilogy was widely criticized, Johnson received praise for often being the standout performer. However, her role in the first film won her first [Golden Raspberry Award for Worst Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Raspberry_Award_for_Worst_Actress "Golden Raspberry Award for Worst Actress"), as well as an [Award for Worst Screen Combo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Raspberry_Award_for_Worst_Screen_Combo "Golden Raspberry Award for Worst Screen Combo"), which she shared alongside [Jamie Dornan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Dornan "Jamie Dornan"). She received nominations in the same categories for *[Fifty Shades Darker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifty_Shades_Darker_(film) "Fifty Shades Darker (film)")* (2017), though she did not win. She reprised her role for the trilogy's final film, *[Fifty Shades Freed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifty_Shades_Freed_(film) "Fifty Shades Freed (film)")* (2018).

On February 15, 2015, Johnson appeared on *[Saturday Night Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live "Saturday Night Live")*'s 40th anniversary special and hosted *SNL* on February 28, 2015, making her the second daughter of a former *SNL* host (after [Gwyneth Paltrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwyneth_Paltrow "Gwyneth Paltrow"), whose mother [Blythe Danner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blythe_Danner "Blythe Danner") hosted during the show's seventh season in 1982) to host the show. Also in 2015, she reunited with her *21 Jump Street* cast member [Johnny Depp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Depp "Johnny Depp"), playing the mother of his character's child in the feature film *[Black Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Mass_(film) "Black Mass (film)")*. Jessica Kiang of [IndieWire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndieWire "IndieWire") said that she "makes something of nothing" in her role. In 2015, Johnson starred in [Luca Guadagnino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luca_Guadagnino "Luca Guadagnino")'s thriller *[A Bigger Splash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Bigger_Splash_(2015_film) "A Bigger Splash (2015 film)")*, alongside [Tilda Swinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilda_Swinton "Tilda Swinton"), [Matthias Schoenaerts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Schoenaerts "Matthias Schoenaerts"), and [Ralph Fiennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Fiennes "Ralph Fiennes"). Writing for *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")*, [Peter Travers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Travers "Peter Travers") stated that Johnson showed that her character "has more on her mind than slithering seductively". According to [Christy Lemire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christy_Lemire "Christy Lemire") from *[RogerEbert.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RogerEbert.com "RogerEbert.com")*: "*A Bigger Splash* allows Johnson to be both funnier and sexier than she was in *Fifty Shades of Grey*". The same year saw the release of *[Cymbeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cymbeline_(film) "Cymbeline (film)")*, a modern film adaptation of the [William Shakespeare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Shakespeare "William Shakespeare") play, in which she starred alongside [Ethan Hawke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethan_Hawke "Ethan Hawke") and [Ed Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Harris "Ed Harris"). She also played a lead in the 2016 comedy *[How to Be Single](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_to_Be_Single "How to Be Single")*, with [Leslie Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Mann "Leslie Mann") and her *Date and Switch* co-star [Nicholas Braun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Braun "Nicholas Braun"). She performed a cover of the [Frankie Valli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankie_Valli "Frankie Valli") song "[Can't Take My Eyes Off of You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Can%27t_Take_My_Eyes_Off_of_You "Can't Take My Eyes Off of You")", alongside Zani Jones Mbayise, Vanessa Rubio and [Damon Wayans Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damon_Wayans_Jr. "Damon Wayans Jr.") for the soundtrack of the film. Johnson was invited to join the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences "Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences") in June 2016.

Johnson starred in [Luca Guadagnino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luca_Guadagnino "Luca Guadagnino")'s supernatural horror film *[Suspiria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suspiria_(2018_film) "Suspiria (2018 film)")* (2018), a remake of the [1977 film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suspiria "Suspiria"), in which she played an American dancer in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin "Berlin") who enrolls in an academy run by a [coven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coven "Coven") of witches. [David Ehrlich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ehrlich "David Ehrlich") of [IndieWire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndieWire "IndieWire") described Johnson's performance in the film as "thrillingly unrepentant". Also in 2018, she starred in [Drew Goddard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drew_Goddard "Drew Goddard")'s neo-noir thriller *[Bad Times at the El Royale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Times_at_the_El_Royale "Bad Times at the El Royale")*, with [Jeff Bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Bridges "Jeff Bridges"), [Jon Hamm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Hamm "Jon Hamm"), [Cynthia Erivo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cynthia_Erivo "Cynthia Erivo"), and [Chris Hemsworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Hemsworth "Chris Hemsworth"). In the film, she played a hippie staying at a resort on the California-[Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada "Nevada") border where the lives of various people with suspicious pasts intersect. *[Screen Rant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Rant "Screen Rant")* ranked Johnson's performance as the fourth-best in the film and stated that "she brings a reserved, under-the-surface power to her role".

In 2019, Johnson starred opposite [Armie Hammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armie_Hammer "Armie Hammer") in [Babak Anvari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babak_Anvari "Babak Anvari")'s psychological horror film *[Wounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wounds_(film) "Wounds (film)")*, which is based on [Nathan Ballingrud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Ballingrud "Nathan Ballingrud")'s horror novella *The Visible Filth*. It had its world premiere at the [Sundance Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundance_Film_Festival "Sundance Film Festival") on January 26, 2019. Johnson starred in the well-reviewed independent adventure film *[The Peanut Butter Falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Peanut_Butter_Falcon "The Peanut Butter Falcon")*, alongside [Shia LaBeouf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_LaBeouf "Shia LaBeouf"), [Zack Gottsagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zack_Gottsagen "Zack Gottsagen"), and [Bruce Dern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Dern "Bruce Dern"). The film premiered at [South by Southwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_by_Southwest "South by Southwest") on March 9, 2019. It became the highest grossing independent film of the year. While promoting the film on *[The Ellen DeGeneres Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ellen_DeGeneres_Show "The Ellen DeGeneres Show")*, Johnson had a notably tense interaction with the show's host [Ellen DeGeneres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellen_DeGeneres "Ellen DeGeneres") regarding a birthday party invite – leading to Johnson saying the now-famous phrase, "[that's not the truth, Ellen."](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_Johnson%27s_interview_on_The_Ellen_DeGeneres_Show "Dakota Johnson's interview on The Ellen DeGeneres Show") She starred in [Gabriela Cowperthwaite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriela_Cowperthwaite "Gabriela Cowperthwaite")'s drama film *[Our Friend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Friend "Our Friend")*, alongside [Casey Affleck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casey_Affleck "Casey Affleck") and [Jason Segel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Segel "Jason Segel"). The film is based upon real life couple Nicole and Matthew Teague, faced with Nicole's impending death, see their best friend move in with them to help them out. Johnson sang again during the film, performing a cover of "If I Had the World to Give" by the [Grateful Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grateful_Dead "Grateful Dead"). [Joe Morgenstern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Morgenstern "Joe Morgenstern") of *[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")* wrote that the "intimacy of Ms. Johnson's performance is extraordinary. She is the least assertive of movie stars, yet the courage, despair and fury she finds in Nicole will lift you up and spin you around". While Gary Goldstein from the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")* stated that Johnson "impresses with affecting range — from flirty, ebullient and adoring to stalwart, enraged and resigned; it's a lovely performance".

### 2020–present: Recent work

Johnson founded the production company TeaTime Pictures, alongside former [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") development executive Ro Donnelly, to develop films and television series. In 2020, Johnson made her directorial debut, co-directing (with Cory Bailey) the music video for [Coldplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coldplay "Coldplay")'s "Cry Cry Cry", which featured her then boyfriend [Chris Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Martin "Chris Martin"). Johnson starred alongside [Tracee Ellis Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracee_Ellis_Ross "Tracee Ellis Ross") in the [dramedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comedy_drama "Comedy drama") film *[The High Note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_High_Note "The High Note")*, which was released on May 29, 2020. [Ann Hornaday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Hornaday "Ann Hornaday") of *[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")* wrote that "she's lovely to look at and can never be accused of overacting, but in terms of conveying single-minded drive, Johnson is no match for [Tracee Ellis] Ross's carefully calibrated tonal swings between imperiousness, self-awareness, isolation and down-to-earth intimacy." Conversely [Richard Roeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Roeper "Richard Roeper") of the *[Chicago Sun Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Sun_Times "Chicago Sun Times")* saw it as "maybe her best and certainly most lovable performance."

In 2021, she co-starred in *[The Lost Daughter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lost_Daughter_(film) "The Lost Daughter (film)")*, directed by [Maggie Gyllenhaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maggie_Gyllenhaal "Maggie Gyllenhaal") and based on [Elena Ferrante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elena_Ferrante "Elena Ferrante")'s novel of the same name. In December 2021, [Boat Rocker Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boat_Rocker_Media "Boat Rocker Media") acquired a minority interest for TeaTime Pictures. In 2022, she starred in the [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") film adaptation of author [Jane Austen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Austen "Jane Austen")'s *[Persuasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persuasion_(2022_film) "Persuasion (2022 film)")*. Johnson co-produced and starred in several films. She played a lesbian in *[Am I OK?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am_I_OK%3F "Am I OK?")*, which was directed by [Stephanie Allynne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephanie_Allynne "Stephanie Allynne") and [Tig Notaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tig_Notaro "Tig Notaro"). She played a young mother with an autistic child in *[Cha Cha Real Smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cha_Cha_Real_Smooth "Cha Cha Real Smooth")*, in which she starred opposite [Cooper Raiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_Raiff "Cooper Raiff"). Johnson played a cab passenger who has an emotionally profound interaction with a cabbie in the film *[Daddio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daddio_(film) "Daddio (film)")*, in which she starred opposite [Sean Penn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Penn "Sean Penn"). Her performances in *The Lost Daughter*, *Persuasion*, *Cha Cha Real Smooth*, and *Daddio* were met with positive reception from critics and audiences.

In November 2023, Johnson departed [WME](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WME_(talent_agency) "WME (talent agency)") and signed a representation deal with [Creative Artists Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Artists_Agency "Creative Artists Agency"). She played [the title role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madame_Web "Madame Web") in the superhero film *[Madame Web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madame_Web_(film) "Madame Web (film)")* (2024), set in [Sony's Spider-Man Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony%27s_Spider-Man_Universe "Sony's Spider-Man Universe"). It was produced on a budget of $80 million and was critically and commercially unsuccessful, earning her a second [Golden Raspberry Award for Worst Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Raspberry_Award_for_Worst_Actress "Golden Raspberry Award for Worst Actress"). Johnson said about the film: "We don't have control over how something turns out. (I) signed on to a movie that, by the end of shooting it, (was) a completely different script than what I attached to, and that is a crazy journey to go on as an artist. Because you're like, 'OK, I'm doing something with my actual body and my actual mind and my heart and my emotions,' and it's just being taken and fucked with, but you can't do anything about it. Like, what am I going to do, fucking cry about *Madame Web*? No. I'm gonna laugh."

In 2025, Johnson had a supporting role in the comedy film *[Splitsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Splitsville_(film) "Splitsville (film)")* which she also served as a producer. That same year, she had a leading role in [Celine Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celine_Song "Celine Song")'s romance film *[Materialists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materialists_(film) "Materialists (film)")* (2025), alongside [Chris Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Evans_(actor) "Chris Evans (actor)") and [Pedro Pascal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Pascal "Pedro Pascal"). Johnson starred with [Anne Hathaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hathaway "Anne Hathaway") and [Josh Hartnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Hartnett "Josh Hartnett") in [Michael Showalter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Showalter "Michael Showalter")'s film adaptation of [Colleen Hoover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colleen_Hoover "Colleen Hoover")'s [novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verity_(novel) "Verity (novel)") *[Verity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verity_(film) "Verity (film)")* (2026). Johnson is set to make her feature-length directorial debut with the film *A Tree is Blue*, written by Vanessa Burghardt who is her co-star in *Cha Cha Real Smooth*. The film began production in late 2025, but a release date has not yet been confirmed.

## Public image

Johnson's role in the *Fifty Shades* film series established her as a [sex symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_symbol "Sex symbol") early in her career. Since then, her performances have diversified, ranging from supporting roles in [blockbusters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockbuster_(entertainment) "Blockbuster (entertainment)") and [art films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_film "Art film") to leading roles in [prestige films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prestige_picture "Prestige picture"). In 2024, she said that she does not “discriminate against movie genres” when choosing acting projects. She has also said she is willing to accept smaller roles, provided the character interests her, and that she uses each one as an opportunity to hone her craft.

Johnson's acting has often drawn a polarized response from critics. While some reviewers have criticized her performances as wooden, emotionally detached, or lacking range, others have praised her subtle, understated, and emotionally nuanced work, as well as her willingness to take creative risks. Tom W. Clarke of *[The Sydney Morning Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sydney_Morning_Herald "The Sydney Morning Herald")* labeled her "the most unfairly maligned actor in Hollywood", while Louis Chilton of *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent "The Independent")* and Sonia Rao of *[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")* argued that few of her films fully capitalize on her unusual, enigmatic strengths. Miriam Handel of *[The Daily Beast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Beast "The Daily Beast")* and Coleman Spilde of *[Salon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salon.com "Salon.com")* agreed that Johnson frequently elevates the material she is given, and Clarke said she excels at "playing complex characters on journeys of [self-discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-discovery "Self-discovery")". [Luca Guadagnino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luca_Guadagnino "Luca Guadagnino"), who directed her in *[Suspiria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suspiria_(2018_film) "Suspiria (2018 film)")*, described her as a bold, bombastic, and committed performer.

Johnson has been described as refreshingly candid and relatable in her off-screen appearances, with her interview clips and quotes consistently going [viral online](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_video "Viral video"). *[Texas Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Monthly "Texas Monthly")*’s Meher Yeda observed that her press appearances often overshadow the films she is promoting. Her press tour for *Madame Web* was particularly noted by the media, with her remarks and demeanor receiving significant news coverage due to her [self-awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-referential_humor "Self-referential humor") and apparent indifference towards the film’s reception. Regarding her press tour for *Materialists*, Taryn Ryder of [Yahoo! Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahoo!_Entertainment "Yahoo! Entertainment") said the actress "reminded us why she’s one of the most magnetic, self-aware stars working today ... effortlessly chic, disarmingly unfiltered and entirely in control of the narrative". Stephanie McNeal of *[Glamour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glamour_(magazine) "Glamour (magazine)")* described Johnson’s public persona as “acerbic, witty, [and] [deadpan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadpan "Deadpan")”, noting that she has, in her own way, become something of an “[America’s sweetheart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America%27s_Sweetheart "America's Sweetheart")”. In 2024, film critic [Stephanie Zacharek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephanie_Zacharek "Stephanie Zacharek") said the actress had endeared herself to the general public through a combination of unfiltered responses, intelligence, dry wit, and commitment to diverse performances.

As a child of well-known actors, Johnson’s career and the extent to which her family background influenced her opportunities have been discussed in the media. While she has acknowledged benefiting from a degree of privilege, she has also described the broader discourse surrounding [nepo babies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepo_baby "Nepo baby") as “boring”, particularly among journalists. She has also poked fun at the label in her appearances on *[Saturday Night Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live "Saturday Night Live")*.

## Personal life

Johnson was previously involved in long-term relationships with musician Noah Gersh and actor [Jordan Masterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Masterson "Jordan Masterson"). She dated [Matthew Hitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Hitt "Matthew Hitt"), the lead vocalist of Welsh indie rock band [Drowners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drowners "Drowners"), intermittently for almost two years until 2016. She and [Coldplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coldplay "Coldplay")'s vocalist [Chris Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Martin "Chris Martin") started dating in October 2017. They resided together in [Malibu, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malibu,_California "Malibu, California") and were engaged. It was reported in June 2025 that the couple split up after eight years of dating. She then briefly dated singer-songwriter [Role Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Role_Model_(singer) "Role Model (singer)") in 2026.

Johnson has been named a [brand ambassador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_ambassador "Brand ambassador") for luxury fashion brand [Gucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gucci "Gucci"). In 2018, she collaborated with 300 women in Hollywood to set up the [Time's Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time%27s_Up_(organization) "Time's Up (organization)") initiative to protect women from harassment and discrimination. In November 2020, it was announced that she had become an investor and co-creative director of Maude, a sexual wellness brand.

## Awards and nominations

Among her accolades, Johnson received the [Robert Altman Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Spirit_Robert_Altman_Award "Independent Spirit Robert Altman Award") from the [Independent Spirit Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Spirit_Awards "Independent Spirit Awards") as a part of the ensemble for *[Suspiria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suspiria_(2018_film) "Suspiria (2018 film)")*. For her role in the *[Fifty Shades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifty_Shades_(film_series) "Fifty Shades (film series)")* film series, she received a [People's Choice Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Choice_Awards "People's Choice Awards"), as well as nomination for two [MTV Movie & TV Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Movie_%26_TV_Awards "MTV Movie & TV Awards"). She was nominated for [BAFTA Rising Star Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Rising_Star_Award "BAFTA Rising Star Award") in 2016.
