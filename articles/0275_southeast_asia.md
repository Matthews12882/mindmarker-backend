# Southeast Asia

**Southeast Asia** is the geographical [south-eastern subregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_geoscheme#South-eastern_Asia "United Nations geoscheme") of [Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia "Asia"), consisting of the regions that are situated south of China, east of the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent"), and northwest of [mainland Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Australia "Mainland Australia"), which is a part of [Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania "Oceania"). Southeast Asia is bordered to the north by [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia"), to the west by [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia") and the [Bay of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Bengal "Bay of Bengal"), to the east by Oceania and the [Pacific Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean "Pacific Ocean"), and to the south by [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_(continent) "Australia (continent)") and the [Indian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean "Indian Ocean"). Apart from the [British Indian Ocean Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Ocean_Territory "British Indian Ocean Territory") and two out of [26 atolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atolls_of_the_Maldives "Atolls of the Maldives") of [Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldives "Maldives") in South Asia, [Maritime Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Southeast_Asia "Maritime Southeast Asia") is the only other subregion of Asia that lies partly within the [Southern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Hemisphere "Southern Hemisphere"). [Mainland Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Southeast_Asia "Mainland Southeast Asia") is entirely in the [Northern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere "Northern Hemisphere"). [Timor-Leste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timor-Leste "Timor-Leste") and the southern portion of Indonesia are the parts of Southeast Asia that are located south of the [Equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator "Equator").

The region lies near the intersection of [geological plates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plate_tectonics "Plate tectonics"), with both heavy seismic and volcanic activities. The [Sunda plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_plate "Sunda plate") is the main plate of the region, featuring almost all Southeast Asian countries except [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar"), northern [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand"), northern [Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laos "Laos"), northern [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam "Vietnam"), and northern [Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzon "Luzon") of the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines"), while the Sunda plate only includes western Indonesia to as far east as the Indonesian province of [Bali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bali "Bali"). The mountain ranges in Myanmar, Thailand, [Peninsular Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Malaysia "Peninsular Malaysia"), and the Indonesian islands of [Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatra "Sumatra"), [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java "Java"), [Bali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bali "Bali"), [Lesser Sunda Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Sunda_Islands "Lesser Sunda Islands"), and [Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timor "Timor") are part of the [Alpide belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpide_belt "Alpide belt"), while the islands of the Philippines and Indonesia as well as [Timor-Leste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timor-Leste "Timor-Leste") are part of the Pacific [Ring of Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_Fire "Ring of Fire"). Both seismic belts meet in Indonesia, causing the region to have relatively high occurrences of earthquakes and volcanic eruptions, particularly in the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_earthquakes_in_the_Philippines "List of earthquakes in the Philippines") and [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_earthquakes_in_Indonesia "List of earthquakes in Indonesia").

It covers about 4,500,000 km2 (1,700,000 sq mi), which is 9.80% of [Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia "Asia"), 8% of [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia "Eurasia") and 3% of Earth's total land area. Its total population is about 676 million, about 8.5% of the world's population. It is the third most populous geographical region in Asia after South Asia and East Asia. The region is culturally and ethnically diverse, with hundreds of languages spoken by different ethnic groups. The eleven countries in the region are members of the [Association of Southeast Asian Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN "ASEAN") (ASEAN), a regional organisation established for economic, political, military, educational, and cultural integration among its members.

Southeast Asia is one of the most culturally diverse regions of the world. There are many different languages and ethnicities in the region. Historically, Southeast Asia was significantly influenced by [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_India "Greater India"), [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_cultural_sphere "East Asian cultural sphere"), [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Southeast_Asia "Islam in Southeast Asia"), and [colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonisation_of_Southeast_Asia "European colonisation of Southeast Asia") cultures, which became core components of the region's cultural and political institutions. Most modern Southeast Asian countries were colonised by European powers. European colonisation exploited natural resources and labour from the lands they conquered, and attempted to spread European institutions to the region. Several Southeast Asian countries were also briefly occupied by the [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan "Empire of Japan") during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). The [aftermath of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aftermath_of_World_War_II "Aftermath of World War II") saw most of the region decolonised. Today, Southeast Asia is predominantly governed by independent states.

## Definition

The region, together with part of South Asia, was well known by Europeans as the [East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Indies "East Indies") or simply the Indies until the 20th century. Chinese sources referred to the region as *[Nanyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanyang_(region) "Nanyang (region)")* ("[南洋](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%8D%97%E6%B4%8B#Chinese "wikt:南洋")"), which literally means the "Southern Ocean". The mainland section of Southeast Asia was referred to as [Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Southeast_Asia "Mainland Southeast Asia") by European geographers due to its location between China and the Indian subcontinent and its having cultural influences from both neighbouring regions. In the 20th century, however, the term became more restricted to territories of the former [French Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina "French Indochina") (Cambodia, Laos, and Vietnam). The maritime section of Southeast Asia is also known as the [Malay Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Archipelago "Malay Archipelago"), a term derived from the European concept of a [Malay race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_race "Malay race"). Another term for [Maritime Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Southeast_Asia "Maritime Southeast Asia") is *[Insulindia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Archipelago#Insulindia_and_East_Indies "Malay Archipelago")* (Indian Islands), used to describe the region between Indochina and [Australasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australasia "Australasia").

The term "Southeast Asia" was first used in 1839 by American pastor Howard Malcolm in his book *Travels in South-Eastern Asia*. Malcolm only included the Mainland section and excluded the Maritime section in his definition of Southeast Asia. The term was officially used in the midst of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") by the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II "Allies of World War II"), through the formation of [South East Asia Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_East_Asia_Command "South East Asia Command") (SEAC) in 1943. SEAC popularised the use of the term "Southeast Asia", although what constituted Southeast Asia was not fixed; for example, SEAC excluded the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines") and a large part of [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia") while including [Ceylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka"). However, by the late 1970s, a roughly standard usage of the term "Southeast Asia" and the territories it encompasses had emerged. Although from a cultural or linguistic perspective the definitions of "Southeast Asia" may vary, the most common definitions nowadays include the area represented by the countries (sovereign states and dependent territories) listed below.

All eleven countries of Southeast Asia are members of the [Association of Southeast Asian Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN "ASEAN") (ASEAN). [Timor-Leste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timor-Leste "Timor-Leste") is the latest country to join this association (2025). [Papua New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papua_New_Guinea "Papua New Guinea") has stated that it might join ASEAN, and is currently an observer. [Sovereignty issues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_disputes_in_the_South_China_Sea "Territorial disputes in the South China Sea") exist over some [islands in the South China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_Sea_Islands "South China Sea Islands").

### Geographical divisions

Southeast Asia is geographically divided into two subregions, namely [Mainland Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Southeast_Asia "Mainland Southeast Asia") (or the Indochinese Peninsula) and [Maritime Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Southeast_Asia "Maritime Southeast Asia").

**Mainland Southeast Asia includes**:

* [Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodia "Cambodia")
* [Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laos "Laos")
* [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar")
* [Peninsular Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Malaysia "Peninsular Malaysia")
* [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand")
* [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam "Vietnam")

**Maritime Southeast Asia includes**:

* [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei "Brunei")
* [East Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Malaysia "East Malaysia")
* [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia")
* [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines")
* [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore")
* [Timor-Leste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timor-Leste "Timor-Leste")

While [Peninsular Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Malaysia "Peninsular Malaysia") is geographically situated in [Mainland Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Southeast_Asia "Mainland Southeast Asia"), it shares many similar cultural and ecological affinities with surrounding islands, thus it is often grouped with them as part of [Maritime Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Southeast_Asia "Maritime Southeast Asia"). Geographically, the [Andaman and Nicobar Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andaman_and_Nicobar_Islands "Andaman and Nicobar Islands") of India is also considered a part of Maritime Southeast Asia.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] Eastern [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh") and [Northeast India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_India "Northeast India") have strong cultural ties with Mainland Southeast Asia and are sometimes considered transregional areas between South Asia and Southeast Asia (see also: [Eastern South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_South_Asia "Eastern South Asia") and [Southeast Asian relations with Northeast India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_relations_with_Northeast_India#Southeast_Asia "Asian relations with Northeast India")). To the east, [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong "Hong Kong") is sometimes regarded as part of Southeast Asia.[*[excessive citations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources#Bundling_citations "Wikipedia:Citing sources")*] Similarly, [Christmas Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_Island "Christmas Island") and the [Cocos (Keeling) Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocos_(Keeling)_Islands "Cocos (Keeling) Islands") have strong cultural ties with Maritime Southeast Asia and are sometimes considered transregional areas between Southeast Asia and Australia/Oceania. On some occasions, [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka") has been considered a part of Southeast Asia because of its cultural and religious ties to Mainland Southeast Asia. The eastern half of the island of [New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Guinea "New Guinea"), which is not a part of Indonesia, namely, Papua New Guinea, is sometimes included as a part of Maritime Southeast Asia, and so are [Guam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guam "Guam"), the [Northern Mariana Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Mariana_Islands "Northern Mariana Islands"), and [Palau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau "Palau"), which were all parts of the [Spanish East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_East_Indies "Spanish East Indies") with strong cultural and linguistic ties to the region, specifically, the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines").

Timor-Leste and the eastern half of Indonesia (east of the [Wallace Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_Line "Wallace Line") in the region of [Wallacea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallacea "Wallacea")) are considered to be geographically associated with Oceania due to their distinctive faunal features. Geologically, the island of [New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Guinea "New Guinea") and its surrounding islands are considered as parts of the [Australian continent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_(continent) "Australia (continent)"), connected via the [Sahul Shelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahul_Shelf "Sahul Shelf"). Both [Christmas Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_Island "Christmas Island") and the [Cocos (Keeling) Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocos_(Keeling)_Islands "Cocos (Keeling) Islands") are located on the [Australian plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_plate "Australian plate"), south of the [Sunda Trench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_Trench "Sunda Trench"). Even though they are geographically closer to Maritime Southeast Asia than [mainland Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Australia "Mainland Australia"), these two [Australian external territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_and_territories_of_Australia "States and territories of Australia") are not geologically associated with Asia as none of them is actually on the [Sunda plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_plate "Sunda plate"). The [UN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations") [Statistics Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Statistics_Division "United Nations Statistics Division")'s [geoscheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_geoscheme "United Nations geoscheme"), which is a UN [political geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_geography "Political geography") tool created specifically for statistical purposes, has classified both island territories as parts of [Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania "Oceania"), under the [UNSD subregion "Australia and New Zealand"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_geoscheme_for_Oceania#UNSD_subregions "United Nations geoscheme for Oceania") ([Australasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australasia "Australasia")).

Some definitions of Southeast Asia may include [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan"). Taiwan has sometimes been included in Southeast Asia as well as East Asia but is not a member of [ASEAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN "ASEAN"). Likewise, a similar argument could be applied to some [southern parts of mainland China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China "South China"), as well as [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong "Hong Kong") and [Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macau "Macau"), may also considered as part of Southeast Asia as well as East Asia but are not members of [ASEAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN "ASEAN").

## History

### Prehistory

The region was already inhabited by *[Homo erectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_erectus "Homo erectus")* from approximately 1,500,000 years ago during the [Middle Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Pleistocene "Middle Pleistocene") age. Distinct *[Homo sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human "Human")* groups, ancestral to Eastern non-African (related to East Asians as well as Papuans) populations, reached the region by between 50,000BC to 70,000BC, with some arguing earlier. [Rock art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_art "Rock art") (parietal art) dating from 40,000 to 60,000 years ago (which is currently the world's oldest) has been discovered in the caves of [Sulawesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulawesi "Sulawesi") and [Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo "Borneo") ([Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalimantan "Kalimantan")). *[Homo floresiensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_floresiensis "Homo floresiensis")* also lived in the area up until at least 50,000 years ago, after which they became extinct. During much of this time the present-day islands of [Western Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Indonesia "Western Indonesia") were joined into a single landmass with the [Malay Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula "Malay Peninsula") known as [Sundaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundaland "Sundaland") due to much lower sea levels. The [Gulf of Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Thailand "Gulf of Thailand") was dry land which connected Sundaland with [Mainland Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Southeast_Asia "Mainland Southeast Asia").

Distinctive [Basal-East Eurasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_East_Eurasians "Ancient East Eurasians") (Eastern non-African) ancestry was recently found to have originated in Mainland Southeast Asia at ~50,000BC, and expanded through multiple migration waves southwards and northwards respectively, giving rise to both Oceanian (Papuan related) and basal East Asian ([Onge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onge "Onge") and [Tianyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianyuan_man "Tianyuan man") related) lineages.

Ancient remains of hunter-gatherers in Maritime Southeast Asia, such as one Holocene hunter-gatherer from [South Sulawesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Sulawesi "South Sulawesi"), had ancestry from both the Oceanian-related and East Asian-related branches of the Eastern non-African lineage. The hunter-gatherer individual had approximately ~50% "Basal-East Asian" ancestry, modeled as [Onge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onge "Onge") or [Tianyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianyuan_man "Tianyuan man")-like ancestry, and was positioned in between the Andamanese Onge and the Papuans of Oceania. The authors concluded that the presence of this ancestry in the Holocene hunter-gatherer suggests that East Asian-related admixture from [Mainland Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Southeast_Asia "Mainland Southeast Asia") into [Maritime Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Southeast_Asia "Maritime Southeast Asia") may have taken place long before the expansion of [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples "Austronesian peoples") societies. Geneflow of East Asian-related ancestry into [Maritime Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Southeast_Asia "Maritime Southeast Asia") and [Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania "Oceania") could be estimated to ~25,000BC (possibly even earlier).

The pre-Neolithic Oceanian-related populations of Maritime Southeast Asia were largely replaced by the expansion of various [East Asian-related populations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_history_of_East_Asians "Genetic history of East Asians"), beginning about 50,000 to 25,000 years ago from Mainland Southeast Asia. East Asian-related ancestry was already widespread across Southeast Asia by 15,000BC, predating the expansion of [Austroasiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austroasiatic_languages "Austroasiatic languages") and [Austronesian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples "Austronesian peoples").

Samples dated to c. 10,000–2000 BCE from the [Hoabinhian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoabinhian "Hoabinhian") hunter-gatherer lithic techno-complex in Mainland Southeast Asia, which predated the Austronesian and Austroasiatic expansions, display the closest genetic affinities to basal East Asian lineages related to the Upper Paleolithic [Tianyuan man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianyuan_man "Tianyuan man") from northern China, as well as the prehistoric [Jōmon peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Dmon_people "Jōmon people") of Japan. Compared to modern populations, they share the closest affinities to the [Andamanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andamanese_peoples "Andamanese peoples") [Onge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onge "Onge") and [Jarawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarawas_(Andaman_Islands) "Jarawas (Andaman Islands)"), and the [Semang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semang "Semang") (also known as "Malaysian [Negritos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negritos "Negritos")") and [Maniq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maniq_people "Maniq people") in the interior of the Malay Peninsula.

In the late [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic "Neolithic"), the [Austronesian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_people "Austronesian people"), who form the majority of the modern population in Brunei, Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, and Timor-Leste, migrated to Southeast Asia from [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan") in the first seaborne human migration known as the [Austronesian Expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_expansion "Austronesian expansion"). They arrived in the northern Philippines between 7,000 BC to 2,200 BC and rapidly spread further into the [Northern Mariana Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Mariana_Islands "Northern Mariana Islands") and [Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo "Borneo") by 1500 BC; [Island Melanesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_Melanesia "Island Melanesia") by 1300 BC; and to the rest of [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia"), [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia"), southern [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam "Vietnam"), and [Palau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau "Palau") by 1000 BC. They often settled along coastal areas, replacing and assimilating the diverse preexisting peoples. The remainders of these preexisting populations, known as Negritos, form small minority groups in geographically isolated regions.

The [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples "Austronesian peoples") peoples of Southeast Asia have been [seafarers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailor "Sailor") for thousands of years. They spread eastwards to [Micronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronesia "Micronesia") and [Polynesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesia "Polynesia"), as well as westwards to [Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar "Madagascar"), becoming the ancestors of modern-day [Malagasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagasy_peoples "Malagasy peoples"), [Micronesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronesian_people "Micronesian people"), [Melanesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanesians "Melanesians"), and [Polynesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesians "Polynesians"). Passage through the Indian Ocean aided the colonisation of Madagascar, as well as commerce between Western Asia, eastern coast of India and Chinese southern coast. Gold from [Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatra "Sumatra") is thought to have reached as far west as Rome. [Pliny the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder "Pliny the Elder") wrote in his *[Natural History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_History_(Pliny) "Natural History (Pliny)")* about [Chryse and Argyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chryse_and_Argyre "Chryse and Argyre"), two legendary islands rich in gold and silver, located in the Indian Ocean. Their vessels, such as the [vinta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinta "Vinta"), were capable to sail across the ocean. [Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Magellan "Ferdinand Magellan")'s voyage records how much more manoeuvrable their vessels were, as compared to the European ships. A [slave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrique_of_Malacca "Enrique of Malacca") from the [Sulu Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Sea "Sulu Sea") was believed to have been used in the [Magellan expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magellan_expedition "Magellan expedition") as a translator.

Studies presented by the [Human Genome Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Genome_Organisation "Human Genome Organisation") (HUGO) through genetic studies of the various peoples of Asia show empirically that there was a single migration event from Africa, whereby the early people travelled along the south coast of Asia, first entered the Malay Peninsula 50,000–90,000 years ago. The Orang Asli, in particular the [Semang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semang "Semang") who show Negrito characteristics, are the direct descendants of these earliest settlers of Southeast Asia. These early people diversified and travelled slowly northwards to China, and the populations of Southeast Asia show greater genetic diversity than the younger population of China.

Solheim and others have shown evidence for a *Nusantao* maritime trading network ranging from [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam "Vietnam") to the rest of the archipelago as early as 5000 BC to 1 AD. The [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age "Bronze Age") [Dong Son culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dong_Son_culture "Dong Son culture") flourished in [Northern Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Vietnam "Northern Vietnam") from about 1000 BC to 1 BC. Its influence spread to other parts Southeast Asia. The region entered the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age "Iron Age") era in 500 BC, when iron was forged also in northern Vietnam still under Dong Son, due to its frequent interactions with neighbouring [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China").

Most Southeast Asian people were originally [animist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animism "Animism"), engaged in ancestors, nature, and spirits worship. These belief systems were later supplanted by [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism") and Buddhism after the region, especially coastal areas, came under contact with [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent") during the first century. Indian Brahmins and traders brought Hinduism to the region and made contacts with local courts. Local rulers converted to Hinduism or Buddhism and adopted Indian religious traditions to reinforce their legitimacy, elevate ritual status above their fellow chief counterparts and facilitate trade with South Asian states. They periodically invited Indian Brahmins into their realms and began a gradual process of [Indianisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianized_kingdom "Indianized kingdom") in the region. [Shaivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaivism "Shaivism") was the dominant religious tradition of many southern Indian Hindu kingdoms during the first century. It then spread into Southeast Asia via the [Bay of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Bengal "Bay of Bengal"), Indochina, then Malay Archipelago, leading to thousands of Shiva temples on the islands of Indonesia as well as Cambodia and Vietnam, co-evolving with [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism") in the region. [Theravada Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theravada_Buddhism "Theravada Buddhism") entered the region during the third century, via maritime trade routes between the region and [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka"). Buddhism later established a strong presence in [Funan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funan "Funan") region in the fifth century. In present-day mainland Southeast Asia, Theravada is still the dominant branch of Buddhism, practised by the Thai, Burmese, and [Cambodian Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_Cambodia "Buddhism in Cambodia"). This branch was fused with the Hindu-influenced Khmer culture. [Mahayana Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahayana "Mahayana") established presence in Maritime Southeast Asia, brought by Chinese monks during their transit in the region en route to [Nalanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nalanda "Nalanda"). It is still the dominant branch of Buddhism practised by Indonesian and [Malaysian Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_Malaysia "Buddhism in Malaysia").

The spread of these two Indian religions confined the adherents of Southeast Asian indigenous beliefs into remote inland areas. The [Maluku Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maluku_Islands "Maluku Islands") and New Guinea were never Indianised and its native people were predominantly animists until the 15th century when [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam") began to spread in those areas. While in Vietnam, Buddhism never managed to develop strong institutional networks due to strong Chinese influence. In present-day Southeast Asia, Vietnam is the only country where [its folk religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_folk_religion "Vietnamese folk religion") makes up the plurality. Recently, Vietnamese folk religion is undergoing a revival with the support of the government. Elsewhere, there are [ethnic groups in Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Southeast_Asia "Ethnic groups in Southeast Asia") that resisted conversion and still retain their original animist beliefs, such as the [Dayaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayak_people "Dayak people") in [Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalimantan "Kalimantan"), the [Igorots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igorot_people "Igorot people") in Luzon, and the [Shans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_people "Shan people") in eastern Myanmar.

### Hindu and Buddhist kingdoms era

After the region came under contact with the Indian subcontinent c. 400 BCE, it began a gradual process of [Indianisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_India "Greater India") where Indian ideas such as religions, cultures, architectures, and political administrations were brought by traders and religious figures and adopted by local rulers. In turn, Indian Brahmins and monks were invited by local rulers to live in their realms and help transforming local polities to become more Indianised, blending Indian and indigenous traditions. [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit "Sanskrit") and [Pali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pali "Pali") became the elite language of the region, which effectively made Southeast Asia part of the [Indosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indosphere "Indosphere"). Most of the region had been Indianised during the first centuries, while the Philippines later Indianised c. ninth century when the [Kingdom of Tondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Tondo "Kingdom of Tondo") was established in Luzon. Vietnam, especially its northern part, was never fully Indianised due to the many periods of [Chinese domination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_under_Chinese_rule "Vietnam under Chinese rule") it experienced.

The first Indian-influenced polities established in the region were the [Pyu city-states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyu_city-states "Pyu city-states") that already existed circa second century BCE, located in inland Myanmar. It served as an overland trading hub between India and China. Theravada Buddhism was the predominant religion of these city states, while the presence of other Indian religions such as Mahayana Buddhism and Hinduism were also widespread. In the first century, the [Funan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funan "Funan") states centered in the [Mekong Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong_Delta "Mekong Delta") were established, encompassed modern-day Cambodia, southern Vietnam, Laos, and eastern Thailand. It became the dominant trading power in mainland Southeast Asia for about five centuries, provided passage for Indian and Chinese goods and assumed authority over the flow of commerce through Southeast Asia. In maritime Southeast Asia, the first recorded Indianised kingdom was [Salakanagara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salakanagara "Salakanagara"), established in western Java circa second century CE. This Hindu kingdom was known by the Greeks as *Argyre* (Land of Silver).

By the fifth century CE, trade networking between East and West was concentrated in the maritime route. Foreign traders were starting to use new routes such as the [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca "Strait of Malacca") and [Sunda Straits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_Strait "Sunda Strait") due to the development of maritime Southeast Asia. This change resulted in the decline of Funan, while new maritime powers such as [Srivijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivijaya "Srivijaya"), [Tarumanagara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarumanagara "Tarumanagara"), and [Mataram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mataram_Kingdom "Mataram Kingdom") emerged. Srivijaya especially became the dominant maritime power for more than 5 centuries, controlling both [Strait of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca "Strait of Malacca") and [Sunda Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_Strait "Sunda Strait"). This dominance started to decline when Srivijaya were [invaded by Chola Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola_invasion_of_Srivijaya "Chola invasion of Srivijaya"), a dominant maritime power of Indian subcontinent, in 1025. The invasion reshaped power and trade in the region, resulted in the rise of new regional powers such as the [Khmer Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_Empire "Khmer Empire") and [Kahuripan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahuripan "Kahuripan"). Continued commercial contacts with the [Chinese Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Imperial_China "Mid-Imperial China") enabled the Cholas to influence the local cultures. Many of the surviving examples of the [Hindu cultural influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Southeast_Asia "Hinduism in Southeast Asia") found today throughout Southeast Asia are the result of the Chola expeditions.

As Srivijaya influence in the region declined, The Hindu Khmer Empire experienced a golden age during the 11th to 13th century CE. The empire's capital [Angkor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angkor "Angkor") hosts majestic monuments—such as [Angkor Wat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angkor_Wat "Angkor Wat") and [Bayon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayon "Bayon"). Satellite imaging has revealed that Angkor, during its peak, was the largest pre-industrial urban centre in the world. The [Champa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champa "Champa") civilisation was located in what is today central Vietnam, and was a highly Indianised Hindu kingdom. The [Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_people "Vietnamese people") launched a massive conquest against the [Cham people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cham_(Asia) "Cham (Asia)") during the [1471 Vietnamese invasion of Champa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1471_Vietnamese_invasion_of_Champa "1471 Vietnamese invasion of Champa"), ransacking and burning Champa, slaughtering thousands of Cham people, and forcibly assimilating them into Vietnamese culture.

During the 13th century CE, the region experienced [Mongol invasions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasions_and_conquests "Mongol invasions and conquests"), affecting areas such as the Vietnamese coast, inland Burma (Myanmar) and [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java "Java"). In 1258, 1285 and 1287, the Mongols attempted to invade [Đại Việt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90%E1%BA%A1i_Vi%E1%BB%87t "Đại Việt") and [Champa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champa "Champa"). The invasions were unsuccessful, yet both Dai Viet and Champa agreed to become tributary states to [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty "Yuan dynasty") to avoid further conflicts. The Mongols also invaded [Pagan Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagan_Kingdom "Pagan Kingdom") in Burma from 1277 to 1287, resulted in fragmentation of the Kingdom and rise of smaller [Shan States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_States "Shan States") ruled by local chieftains nominally submitted to Yuan dynasty. However, in 1297, a new local power emerged. [Myinsaing Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myinsaing_Kingdom "Myinsaing Kingdom") became the real ruler of Central Burma and challenged the Mongol rule. This resulted in the second Mongol invasion of Burma in 1300, which was repulsed by Myinsaing. The Mongols would later in 1303 withdrawn from Burma. In 1292, the Mongols sent envoys to the [Singhasari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singhasari "Singhasari") kingdom in Java to ask for submission to Mongol rule. Singhasari rejected the proposal and injured the envoys, enraged the Mongols and made them sent a large invasion fleet to Java. Unbeknownst to them, Singhasari collapsed in 1293 due to a revolt by [Kadiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kediri_(historical_kingdom) "Kediri (historical kingdom)"), one of its vassals. When the Mongols arrived in Java, a local prince named [Raden Wijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raden_Wijaya "Raden Wijaya") offered his service to assist the Mongols in punishing Kadiri. After Kadiri was defeated, Wijaya turned on his Mongol allies, ambushed their invasion fleet and forced them to immediately leave Java.

After the departure of the Mongols, Wijaya established the [Majapahit Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majapahit_Empire "Majapahit Empire") in eastern Java in 1293. Majapahit would soon grow into a regional power. Its greatest ruler was [Hayam Wuruk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayam_Wuruk "Hayam Wuruk"), whose reign from 1350 to 1389 marked the empire's peak when other kingdoms in the southern [Malay Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula "Malay Peninsula"), [Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo "Borneo"), [Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatra "Sumatra"), and [Bali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bali "Bali") came under its influence. Various sources such as the Nagarakertagama also mention that its influence spanned over parts of [Sulawesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulawesi "Sulawesi"), [Maluku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maluku_Islands "Maluku Islands"), and some areas of [western New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_New_Guinea "Western New Guinea") and southern [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines"), making it one of the largest empire to ever exist in Southeast Asian history. By the 15th century CE however, Majapahit's influence began to wane due to many war of successions it experienced and the rise of new Islamic states such as [Samudera Pasai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samudera_Pasai_Sultanate "Samudera Pasai Sultanate") and [Malacca Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca_Sultanate "Malacca Sultanate") around the strategic [Strait of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca "Strait of Malacca"). Majapahit then collapsed around 1500. It was the last major Hindu kingdom and the last regional power in the region before the arrival of the [Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europeans "Europeans").

### Spread of Islam

[Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam") began to make contacts with Southeast Asia in the eighth-century CE, when the [Umayyads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate "Umayyad Caliphate") established trade with the region via sea routes. However, it only began to spread into the region centuries later. In the 11th century, a turbulent period occurred in the history of [Maritime Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Southeast_Asia "Maritime Southeast Asia"). The Indian [Chola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola "Chola") navy crossed the ocean and attacked the [Srivijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivijaya "Srivijaya") kingdom of Sangrama Vijayatungavarman in Kadaram ([Kedah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kedah_Kingdom "Kedah Kingdom")); the capital of the powerful maritime kingdom was sacked and the king was taken captive. Along with Kadaram, Pannai in present-day [Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatra "Sumatra") and Malaiyur and the Malayan peninsula were attacked too. Soon after that, the king of Kedah Phra Ong Mahawangsa became the first ruler to abandon the traditional [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu "Hindu") faith, and converted to Islam with the [Sultanate of Kedah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Kedah "Sultanate of Kedah") established in 1136. [Samudera Pasai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samudera_Pasai "Samudera Pasai") converted to Islam in 1267, the King of Malacca [Parameswara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parameswara_(sultan) "Parameswara (sultan)") married the princess of Pasai, and the son became the first sultan of Malacca. Soon, Malacca became the center of Islamic study and maritime trade, and other rulers followed suit. [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia") religious leader and Islamic scholar [Hamka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamka "Hamka") (1908–1981) wrote in 1961: "The development of Islam in Indonesia and [Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Malaya "Federation of Malaya") is intimately related to a Chinese Muslim, Admiral [Zheng He](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zheng_He "Zheng He")." Muslims as far as Ottoman Turkey reached Southeast Asia that Melchor Davalos a Spanish officer in Manila, remarked:

> Persians and Arabs and Egyptians and Turks brought [Muhammad's] veneration and evil sect here, and even Moors from Tunis and Granada came here, sometimes in the armadas of Campson [Kait Bey], former Sultan of Cairo and King of Egypt... Thus it seems to me that these Moros of the Philippine Islands [are] mainly those who, as had been said, come from Egypt and Arabia and Mecca, and are their relatives, disciples and members, and every year they say that Turks come to Sumatra and Borneo, and to Ternate, where there are now some of those defeated in the famous battle which Señor Don Juan de Austria won.

— Melchor Davalos

There are several theories to the [Islamization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamization "Islamization") process in Southeast Asia. Another theory is trade. The expansion of trade among West Asia, India, and Southeast Asia helped the spread of the religion as Muslim traders from South Arabia ([Hadhramaut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadhramaut "Hadhramaut")) brought Islam to the region with their large volume of trade. Many settled in [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia"), [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore"), and [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia"). This is evident in the Arab-Indonesian, [Arab-Singaporean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Singaporeans "Arab Singaporeans"), and Arab-Malay populations who were at one time very prominent in each of their countries. Finally, the ruling classes embraced Islam and that further aided the permeation of the religion throughout the region. The ruler of the region's most important port, [Malacca Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca_Sultanate "Malacca Sultanate"), embraced Islam in the 15th century, heralding a period of accelerated conversion of Islam throughout the region as Islam provided a positive force among the ruling and trading classes. [Gujarati Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_Muslims "Gujarati Muslims") played a pivotal role in establishing Islam in Southeast Asia.

### Trade and colonization

Trade among Southeast Asian countries has a long tradition. The consequences of colonial rule, struggle for independence, and in some cases war influenced the economic attitudes and policies of each country.

#### Chinese

From 111 BC to 938 AD, northern Vietnam was under Chinese rule. Vietnam was successfully governed by a series of Chinese dynasties including the [Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty "Han dynasty"), [Eastern Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Han "Eastern Han"), [Eastern Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Wu "Eastern Wu"), [Cao Wei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cao_Wei "Cao Wei"), [Jin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_dynasty_(266%E2%80%93420) "Jin dynasty (266–420)"), [Liu Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Song "Liu Song"), [Southern Qi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Qi "Southern Qi"), [Liang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liang_dynasty "Liang dynasty"), [Sui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_dynasty "Sui dynasty"), [Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty "Tang dynasty"), and [Southern Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Han "Southern Han").

Records from Magellan's voyage show that [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei "Brunei") possessed more [cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannon "Cannon") than European ships, so the Chinese must have been trading with them.

Malaysian legend has it that a Chinese Ming emperor sent a princess, [Hang Li Po](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hang_Li_Po "Hang Li Po"), to Malacca, with a retinue of 500, to marry [Sultan Mansur Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansur_Shah_of_Malacca "Mansur Shah of Malacca") after the emperor was impressed by the wisdom of the sultan. [Hang Li Poh's Well](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hang_Li_Poh%27s_Well "Hang Li Poh's Well") (constructed 1459) is now a tourist attraction there, as is [Bukit Cina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukit_Cina "Bukit Cina"), where her retinue settled.

The strategic value of the [Strait of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca "Strait of Malacca"), which was controlled by [Sultanate of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Malacca "Sultanate of Malacca") in the 15th and early 16th century, did not go unnoticed by Portuguese writer [Tomé Pires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A9_Pires "Tomé Pires"), who wrote in the *Suma Oriental*: "Whoever is lord of Malacca has his hand on the throat of [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice "Venice")." (Venice was a major European trading partner, and goods were transported there via the Strait.)

#### European

Western influence started to enter in the 16th century, with the arrival of the Portuguese in Malacca, [Maluku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maluku_Islands "Maluku Islands"), and the Philippines, the latter being settled by the Spaniards years later, which they used to [trade between Asia and Latin America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Galleons "Manila Galleons"). Throughout the 17th and 18th centuries, the Dutch established the [Dutch East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_Indies "Dutch East Indies"); the [French Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina "French Indochina"); and the British [Strait Settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straits_Settlements "Straits Settlements"). By the 19th century, all Southeast Asian countries were colonised except for [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand").

[European explorers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronology_of_European_exploration_of_Asia "Chronology of European exploration of Asia") were reaching Southeast Asia from the west and from the east. Regular trade between the ships sailing east from the Indian Ocean and south from mainland Asia provided goods in return for natural products, such as [honey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey "Honey") and [hornbill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hornbill "Hornbill") beaks from the islands of the archipelago. Before the 18th and 19th centuries, the Europeans mostly were interested in expanding trade links. For the majority of the populations in each country, there was comparatively little interaction with Europeans and traditional social routines and relationships continued. For most, a life with subsistence-level agriculture, fishing and, in less developed civilisations, hunting and gathering was still hard.

Europeans brought [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity") allowing Christian missionaries to become widespread. Spain, although a European power, sent the [most colonists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Filipinos "Spanish Filipinos") and have sourced a significant portion of them from the [Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_Asian "Latin American Asian"). This was so, that, Casimiro Díaz once wrote:

> "He (Governor Don Sebastían Hurtado de Corcuera) brought a great reënforcements of soldiers, many of them from Perú, as he made his voyage to Acapulco from that kingdom."

— Casimiro Díaz

Since then the popularity of Christianity grew to about 89 percent of the Filipino population in 2021.

Thailand also allowed Western scientists to enter its country to develop its own education system as well as start sending royal members and Thai scholars to get higher education from [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe "Europe") and [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire").

#### Japanese

During World War II, [Imperial Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan "Empire of Japan") invaded most of the former western colonies under the concept of "[Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_East_Asia_Co-Prosperity_Sphere "Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere")". However, the [Shōwa occupation regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dwa_period "Shōwa period") committed violent actions against civilians such as live human experimentation, sexual slavery under the brutal "[comfort women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comfort_women "Comfort women")" system, the [Manila massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_massacre "Manila massacre") and the implementation of a system of [forced labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfree_labour "Unfree labour"), such as the one involving four to ten million *[romusha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romusha "Romusha")* in Indonesia. A later UN report stated that four million people died in Indonesia as a result of famine and forced labour during the Japanese occupation. The Allied powers who then defeated Japan (and other allies of [Axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers "Axis powers")) in the [South-East Asian theatre of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South-East_Asian_theatre_of_World_War_II "South-East Asian theatre of World War II") then contended with nationalists to whom the occupation authorities had granted independence.

#### Indian

[Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat "Gujarat"), India had a flourishing trade relationship with Southeast Asia in the 15th and 16th centuries. The trade relationship with Gujarat declined after the Portuguese invasion of Southeast Asia in the 17th century.

#### American

The United States took the Philippines from Spain in 1898. Internal autonomy was granted in 1934, and independence in 1946.

### Contemporary history

Most countries in the region maintain national autonomy. [Democratic forms of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy "Democracy") are practised in most Southeast Asian countries and human rights is recognised but dependent on each nation state. [Socialist countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_state "Communist state") in Southeast Asia include Vietnam and Laos. [ASEAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN "ASEAN") provides a framework for the integration of commerce and regional responses to international concerns.

China has asserted broad claims over the [South China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_Sea "South China Sea"), based on its [nine-dash line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine-dash_line "Nine-dash line"), and has [built artificial islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Wall_of_Sand "Great Wall of Sand") in an attempt to bolster its claims. China also has asserted an [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone "Exclusive economic zone") based on the [Spratly Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spratly_Islands "Spratly Islands"). The [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines") challenged China in the [Permanent Court of Arbitration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Court_of_Arbitration "Permanent Court of Arbitration") in [The Hague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hague "The Hague") in 2013, and in *[Philippines v. China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_v._China "Philippines v. China")* (2016), the Court ruled in favour of the Philippines and rejected China's claims.

## Geography

Southeast Asia stretches from the margins of the [Tibetan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Plateau "Tibetan Plateau") in Northern Myanmar southward across the Malay Peninsula to the islands of the Indonesian and Philippine archipelagos.

Indonesia is the largest country in Southeast Asia and also the [largest archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_island_countries "List of island countries") in the world by size. Geologically, the [Indonesian Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Indonesia "List of islands of Indonesia") is one of the most [volcanically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanology "Volcanology") active regions in the world. Geological [uplifts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tectonic_uplift "Tectonic uplift") in the region have also produced some impressive mountains, culminating in [Puncak Jaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puncak_Jaya "Puncak Jaya") in [Papua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papua_(Indonesian_province) "Papua (Indonesian province)"), Indonesia at 5,030 metres (16,503 feet), on the island of [New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Guinea "New Guinea"); it is the only place where ice glaciers can be found in Southeast Asia. The highest mountain in Southeast Asia is [Hkakabo Razi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hkakabo_Razi "Hkakabo Razi") at 5,967 metres (19,577 feet) in northern [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar"), sharing the same range of its parent peak, [Mount Everest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Everest "Mount Everest").

The regional seas that make up the vast maritime landscape are: the [South China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_Sea "South China Sea"), [Java Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java_Sea "Java Sea"), [Sulu Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Sea "Sulu Sea"), [Celebes Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebes_Sea "Celebes Sea"), and the [Andaman Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andaman_Sea "Andaman Sea"). The [South China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_Sea "South China Sea") is the largest regional sea within Southeast Asia. The Philippines, Vietnam, Malaysia, Brunei, Indonesia, and Singapore, have integral rivers that flow into the South China Sea.

[Tonlé Sap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonl%C3%A9_Sap "Tonlé Sap") in [Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodia "Cambodia") is the [largest freshwater lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_lakes_by_area "List of lakes by area") in Southeast Asia with a maximum surface area of 16,000 km2 (6,200 mi2).

The [Gulf of Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Thailand "Gulf of Thailand") is the [largest gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_gulfs "List of gulfs") in SEA with a surface area of 320,000 km2 (120,000 sq mi). It has a mean depth of 45 meters, and a maximum depth of 80 meters.

The [Mekong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong "Mekong") is 4,900 km long and the longest river in Southeast Asia. It flows from its source in the [Tibetan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Plateau "Tibetan Plateau") through multiple countries in SEA including Myanmar, Laos, Thailand, Cambodia, and Vietnam. The river basin drains a total land area of 795,000 km2 and it has the world's 10th largest mean annual discharge of 475 km3.

[Mayon Volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayon_Volcano "Mayon Volcano"), despite being dangerously [active](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_active_volcanoes_in_the_Philippines "List of active volcanoes in the Philippines"), holds the record of the world's most perfect cone which is built from past and continuous [eruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Types_of_volcanic_eruptions "Types of volcanic eruptions").

### Boundaries

Geographically, Southeast Asia borders [Mainland China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_China "Mainland China") to the north, the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent") to the west, [Micronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronesia "Micronesia") to the east, and the [Australian continent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_(continent) "Australia (continent)") to the south. The boundary between SEA and Australia is often considered to run through [Wallacea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallacea "Wallacea").
Geopolitically, the northern boundary is with [Mainland China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_China "Mainland China") and [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan"). The western boundary is with [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") and [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh"). The eastern boundary is with the islands of [Micronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronesia "Micronesia"). The southern boundary is with [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia"). It lies between [Papua New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papua_New_Guinea "Papua New Guinea") and the [Indonesian region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Indonesia "Regions of Indonesia") of [Western New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_New_Guinea "Western New Guinea") (Papua and [West Papua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Papua_(province) "West Papua (province)")). Both countries share the island of [New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Guinea "New Guinea").

The islands of [Micronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronesia "Micronesia") are not biogeographically, geologically or historically linked to mainland Asia. They are considered part of [Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania "Oceania") by the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations"), *[The World Factbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World_Factbook "The World Factbook")*, and other organisations. The Oceania region is politically represented through the [Pacific Islands Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islands_Forum "Pacific Islands Forum"), a governing body which, up until 2022, included Australia, [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand "New Zealand") and all independent territories in [Melanesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanesia "Melanesia"), Micronesia, and [Polynesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesia "Polynesia"). Several countries of [Maritime Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Southeast_Asia "Maritime Southeast Asia"), such as Indonesia and the Philippines, are dialogue partners of the [Pacific Islands Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islands_Forum "Pacific Islands Forum"), but none have full membership.

Maritime Southeast Asia was often grouped with Australia and Oceania in the mid to late 1800s, rather than with mainland Asia. The term Oceania came into usage at the beginning of the 1800s, and the earlier definitions predated the advent of concepts such as Wallacea.

The non-continental Australian external territories of [Christmas Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_Island "Christmas Island") and [Cocos (Keeling) Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocos_(Keeling)_Islands "Cocos (Keeling) Islands") are sometimes considered part of Maritime Southeast Asia, as they lie in much closer proximity to western Indonesia than they do to mainland Australia. They have a [multicultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiculturalism "Multiculturalism") mix of inhabitants with Asian and [European Australian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Australian "European Australian") ancestry, and were uninhabited when discovered by the British during the 17th century. The islands lie within the bounds of the [Australian Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Plate "Australian Plate"), and are defined by *The World Factbook* as the westernmost extent of Oceania. The United Nations also include these islands in their definition of Oceania, under the same sub-region as Australia and New Zealand.

### Climate

Most of Southeast Asia has a [tropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_climate "Tropical climate") that is hot and humid all year round with plentiful rainfall. The majority of Southeast Asia has a wet and dry season caused by seasonal shifts in winds or [monsoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon "Monsoon"). The [tropical rain belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rain_belt "Tropical rain belt") causes additional rainfall during the monsoon season. The rainforest is the second largest on Earth[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] (with the [Amazon rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_rainforest "Amazon rainforest") being the largest). Exceptions to the typical tropical climate and forest vegetation are:

1. Places such as [Northern Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Vietnam "Northern Vietnam") with a [subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical_climate "Subtropical climate") that is sometimes influenced by [cold waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_waves "Cold waves") which move from the northeast and the [Siberian High](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_High "Siberian High")
2. mountain areas in the northern region and the higher islands, where high altitudes lead to milder temperatures
3. the ["dry zone"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_Zone_(Myanmar) "Dry Zone (Myanmar)") of central Myanmar in the [rain shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_shadow "Rain shadow") of the [Arakan Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakan_Mountains "Arakan Mountains"), where annual rainfall can be as low as 600 millimetres or 24 inches, which under the hot temperatures that prevail is dry enough to qualify as [semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate "Semi-arid climate").
4. Southern areas in [South Central Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Central_Coast "South Central Coast") of Vietnam is marked with hot [semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate "Semi-arid climate") due to weak monsoon activities and high temperature throughout the year. Annual rainfall of this region varies between 400 millimetres or 16 inches to 800 millimetres or 31 inches, with an 8-month dry season.

#### Climate change

Southeast Asia lags behind on [mitigation measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_mitigation "Climate change mitigation"), even though it is one of the most [vulnerable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_vulnerability "Climate change vulnerability") regions to [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change "Climate change") in the world. Climate change has already caused an increase in *heavy* [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation "Precipitation") events (defined as 400 mm or more in a day) and greater increases are expected in this region. Changes in rainfall and runoff will also affect the quality of water supply used by the [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation "Irrigation") systems. Under a high-warming scenario, heat-related deaths in the region could increase by 12.7% by 2100. Among the elderly in [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia"), annual heat-related deaths may go from less than 1 per 100,000 to 45 per 100,000.

[Sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise "Sea level rise") is a serious threat. Along Philippine coasts, it occurs three times faster than the global average, while 199 out of 514 cities and districts in [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia") could be affected by tidal flooding by 2050. [Bangkok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangkok "Bangkok"), [Ho Chi Minh City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_City "Ho Chi Minh City") and [Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta "Jakarta") are amongst the 20 coastal cities which would have the world's highest annual flood losses in the year 2050. Due to [land subsidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_subsidence "Land subsidence"), Jakarta is [sinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flooding_in_Jakarta "Flooding in Jakarta") so much (up to 28 cm (11 in) per year between 1982 and 2010 in some areas) that by 2019, the government had committed to relocate the [capital of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_of_Indonesia "Capital of Indonesia") to another city.

Climate change is also likely to pose a serious threat to the region's fisheries:
3.35 million fishers in the Southeast Asia are reliant on [coral reefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_reef "Coral reef"), and yet those reefs are highly vulnerable to even low-emission climate change and will likely be lost if global warming exceeds 1.5 °C (2.7 °F) By 2050–2070, around 30% of the region's aquaculture area and 10–20% of aquaculture production may be lost.

### Environment

The vast majority of Southeast Asia falls within the warm, humid tropics, and its climate generally can be characterized as monsoonal. The animals of Southeast Asia are diverse; on the islands of [Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo "Borneo") and [Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatra "Sumatra"), the [orangutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orangutan "Orangutan"), the [Asian elephant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_elephant "Asian elephant"), the [Malayan tapir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayan_tapir "Malayan tapir"), the [Sumatran rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_rhinoceros "Sumatran rhinoceros"), and the [Bornean clouded leopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bornean_clouded_leopard "Bornean clouded leopard") can also be found. Six subspecies of the [binturong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binturong "Binturong") or *bearcat* exist in the region, though the one endemic to the island of [Palawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palawan "Palawan") is now classed as vulnerable. Tigers of three different subspecies are found on the island of Sumatra (the [Sumatran tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_tiger "Sumatran tiger")), in peninsular Malaysia (the [Malayan tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayan_tiger "Malayan tiger")), and in Indochina (the [Indochinese tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indochinese_tiger "Indochinese tiger")); all of which are endangered species. The [Komodo dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komodo_dragon "Komodo dragon") is the largest living species of lizard and inhabits the islands of [Komodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komodo_(island) "Komodo (island)"), [Rinca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rinca "Rinca"), [Flores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flores "Flores"), and [Gili Motang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gili_Motang "Gili Motang") in Indonesia. The [Philippine eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_eagle "Philippine eagle") is the [national bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_bird "National bird") of the Philippines. It is considered by scientists as the largest eagle in the world, and is endemic to the Philippines' forests. The [wild water buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_water_buffalo "Wild water buffalo"), and on various islands related dwarf species of *[Bubalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubalus "Bubalus")* such as [anoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anoa "Anoa") were once widespread in Southeast Asia; nowadays the [domestic Asian water buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_Asian_water_buffalo "Domestic Asian water buffalo") is common across the region, but its remaining relatives are rare and endangered. The [mouse deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mouse_deer "Mouse deer"), a small tusked deer as large as a toy dog or cat, mostly can be found on Sumatra, Borneo (Indonesia), and in Palawan (Philippines). The [gaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaur "Gaur"), a gigantic wild ox larger than even wild water buffalo, is found mainly in Indochina. There is very little scientific information available regarding Southeast Asian amphibians.

Birds such as the [green peafowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_peafowl "Green peafowl") and [drongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drongo "Drongo") live in this subregion as far east as Indonesia. The [babirusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babirusa "Babirusa"), a four-tusked pig, can be found in Indonesia as well. The [hornbill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hornbill "Hornbill") was prized for its beak and used in trade with China. The horn of the rhinoceros, not part of its skull, was prized in China as well.

The [Indonesian Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Indonesia "List of islands of Indonesia") is split by the [Wallace Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_Line "Wallace Line"). This line runs along what is now known to be a tectonic plate boundary, and separates Asian (Western) species from Australasian (Eastern) species. The islands between Java/Borneo and Papua form a mixed zone, where both types occur, known as Wallacea. As the pace of development accelerates and populations continue to expand in Southeast Asia, concern has increased regarding the impact of human activity on the region's environment. A significant portion of Southeast Asia, however, has not changed greatly and remains an unaltered home to wildlife. The nations of the region, with only a few exceptions, have become aware of the need to maintain forest cover not only to prevent [soil erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_erosion "Soil erosion") but to preserve the diversity of flora and fauna. Indonesia, for example, has created an extensive system of national parks and preserves for this purpose. Even so, such species as the [Javan rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javan_rhinoceros "Javan rhinoceros") face extinction, with only a handful of the animals remaining in western Java.

The shallow waters of the [Southeast Asian coral reefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_coral_reefs "Southeast Asian coral reefs") have the highest levels of [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity "Biodiversity") for the world's marine ecosystems, where coral, fish, and molluscs abound. According to Conservation International, marine surveys suggest that the marine life diversity in the [Raja Ampat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ampat "Raja Ampat") (Indonesia) is the highest recorded on Earth. Diversity is considerably greater than any other area sampled in the Coral Triangle composed of Indonesia, the Philippines, and Papua New Guinea. The Coral Triangle is the heart of the world's coral reef biodiversity, the Verde Passage is dubbed by Conservation International as the world's "center of the center of marine shore fish biodiversity". The [whale shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whale_shark "Whale shark"), the world's largest species of fish and 6 species of [sea turtles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_turtle "Sea turtle") can also be found in the [South China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_Sea "South China Sea") and the Pacific Ocean territories of the Philippines.

The trees and other plants of the region are tropical; in some countries where the mountains are tall enough, temperate-climate vegetation can be found. These rainforest areas are currently being logged-over, especially in Borneo.

While Southeast Asia is rich in flora and fauna, [Southeast Asia is facing severe deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation_in_Southeast_Asia "Deforestation in Southeast Asia") which causes habitat loss for various [endangered species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endangered_species "Endangered species") such as orangutan and the [Sumatran tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_tiger "Sumatran tiger"). Predictions have been made that more than 40% of the animal and [plant species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora "Flora") in Southeast Asia could be wiped out in the 21st century. At the same time, [haze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haze "Haze") has been a regular occurrence. The two worst regional hazes were in [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Southeast_Asian_haze "1997 Southeast Asian haze") and [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Southeast_Asian_haze "2006 Southeast Asian haze") in which multiple countries were covered with thick haze, mostly caused by "[slash and burn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slash_and_burn "Slash and burn")" activities in Sumatra and Borneo. In reaction, several countries in Southeast Asia signed the [ASEAN Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN_Agreement_on_Transboundary_Haze_Pollution "ASEAN Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution") to combat haze pollution.

The [2013 Southeast Asian haze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Southeast_Asian_haze "2013 Southeast Asian haze") saw API levels reach a hazardous level in some countries. Muar experienced the highest [API](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_pollution_index "Air pollution index") level of 746 on 23 June 2013 at around 7 am.

## Economy

Even prior to the penetration of European interests, Southeast Asia was a critical part of the world trading system. A wide range of commodities originated in the region, but especially important were spices such as pepper, ginger, cloves, and nutmeg. The spice trade initially was developed by Indian and Arab merchants, but it also brought Europeans to the region. First, [Spaniards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain "Spain") ([Manila galleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_galleon "Manila galleon")) who sailed from the Americas and [Kingdom of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Portugal "Kingdom of Portugal"), then the Dutch, and finally the British and French became involved in this enterprise in various countries. The penetration of European commercial interests gradually evolved into annexation of territories, as traders lobbied for an extension of control to protect and expand their activities. As a result, the Dutch moved into Indonesia, the British into [Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula "Malay Peninsula") and parts of Borneo, the French into [Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indochina "Indochina"), and the Spanish and the US into the Philippines. An economic effect of this imperialism was the shift in the production of commodities. For example, the rubber plantations of Malaysia, Java, Vietnam, and Cambodia, the tin mining of Malaya, the rice fields of the Mekong Delta in Vietnam, and the Irrawaddy River delta in Myanmar, were a response to the powerful market demands.

The [overseas Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese "Overseas Chinese") community has played a large role in the development of the economies in the region. The origins of Chinese influence can be traced to the 16th century, when Chinese migrants from [southern China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_China "Southern China") settled in Indonesia, Thailand, and other Southeast Asian countries. Chinese populations in the region saw a rapid increase following the establishment of the PRC in 1949, which forced many refugees to emigrate outside of China. In 2022, Malaysian petroleum industry through its oil and gas company, [Petronas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petronas "Petronas"), was ranked eighth in the world by the Brandirectory. Seventeen telecommunications companies contracted to build the [Asia-America Gateway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-America_Gateway "Asia-America Gateway") submarine cable to connect Southeast Asia to the US This is to avoid disruption of the kind caused by the cutting of the undersea cable from Taiwan to the US in the [2006 Hengchun earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Hengchun_earthquakes "2006 Hengchun earthquakes").

Tourism has been a key factor in economic development for many Southeast Asian countries, especially Cambodia. According to UNESCO, "tourism, if correctly conceived, can be a tremendous development tool and an effective means of preserving the cultural diversity of our planet." Since the early 1990s, "even the non-ASEAN nations such as Cambodia, Laos, Vietnam and Burma, where the income derived from tourism is low, are attempting to expand their own tourism industries." In 1995, Singapore was the regional leader in tourism receipts relative to GDP at over 8%. By 1998, those receipts had dropped to less than 6% of GDP while Thailand and Lao PDR increased receipts to over 7%. Since 2000, Cambodia has surpassed all other ASEAN countries and generated almost 15% of its GDP from tourism in 2006. Furthermore, Vietnam is considered as a growing [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_(international_relations) "Power (international relations)") in Southeast Asia due to its large foreign investment opportunities and the booming tourism sector.

By the early 21st century, [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia") had grown to an [emerging market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerging_markets "Emerging markets") economy, becoming the [largest economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Indonesia "Economy of Indonesia") in the region. It was classified a [newly industrialised country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newly_industrialised_country "Newly industrialised country") and is the region's singular member of the [G-20 major economies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G20 "G20"). Indonesia's estimated gross domestic product (GDP) for 2020 was US$1,088.8 billion (nominal) or $3,328.3 billion (PPP) with per capita GDP of US$4,038 (nominal) or $12,345 (PPP). By GDP per capita in 2023, Singapore is the leading nation in the region with US$84,500 (nominal) or US$140,280 (PPP), followed by Brunei with US$41,713 (nominal) or US$79,408 (PPP) and Malaysia with US$13,942 (nominal) or US$33,353 (PPP). Besides that, Malaysia has the lowest cost of living in the region, followed by Brunei and Vietnam. On the contrary, Singapore is the costliest country in the region, followed by Thailand and the Philippines.

Stock markets in Southeast Asia have performed better than other bourses in the Asia-Pacific region in 2010, with the Philippines' [PSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSE_Composite_Index "PSE Composite Index") leading the way with 22 per cent growth, followed by Thailand's [SET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SET_Index "SET Index") with 21 per cent and Indonesia's [JKSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JSX_Composite "JSX Composite") with 19 per cent.

Southeast Asia's GDP per capita is US$4,685 according to a 2020 [International Monetary Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund "International Monetary Fund") estimates, which is comparable to [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa "South Africa"), [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq "Iraq"), and [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_(country) "Georgia (country)").

## Energy

Energy demand in South East Asia more than doubled from 458 TWh in 2003 to 1,258 TWh in 2023, driven by economic growth and rising [electric vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_vehicle "Electric vehicle") use and development of [data centres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_center "Data center"). By 2023, 97% of the population had access to electricity and 81% to clean cooking, placing the region on track for near-universal electrification by 2040.

[Fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel "Fossil fuel") dominated the energy mix in 2023, accounting for 83.5% of total primary energy supply. [Coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal "Coal") continues to play a central role in power generation and demand is projected to increase by 2030. Despite commitments to reduce coal use, [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_in_Indonesia "Coal in Indonesia"), Vietnam, and the Philippines still plan substantial new coal capacity. [ASEAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN "ASEAN") countries are net importers of oil but exporters of [natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas "Natural gas"), supported by extensive refining and [LNG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquefied_natural_gas "Liquefied natural gas") infrastructure. Oil is mainly used in transport (powering 91.2% of the transport sector), while natural gas provides about one-fifth of the [energy mix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_mix "Energy mix").

[Renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy "Renewable energy") is expanding but remains underdeveloped relative to potential. Under the ASEAN Plan of Action for Energy Cooperation 2026–2030, member states aim for a 30% renewable share in [TPES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_energy "Primary energy") and 45% in installed capacity by 2030. Growth is expected to be led by [solar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_power "Solar power") and [wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power "Wind power"), supported by [energy storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_storage "Energy storage") and grid integration. Although the region has significant renewable resources, installed capacity (around 30.5 GW of solar and 8.3 GW of wind) remains far below (less than 1%) theoretical potential. [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_in_Thailand "Renewable energy in Thailand"), [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_Myanmar "Energy in Myanmar") and [Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_Cambodia "Energy in Cambodia") are the top three countries with the largest solar potentials, while Myanmar, [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_in_Vietnam "Renewable energy in Vietnam") and Thailand rank as the top three for prospective wind capacity among ASEAN countries.

## Demographics

Southeast Asia has an area of approximately 4,500,000 square kilometres (1,700,000 mi2). As of 2021, around 676 million people live in the region, more than a fifth live (143 million) on the Indonesian island of [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java "Java"), the most densely populated large island in the world. [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia") is the most populous country with 274 million people (~40% of South East Asia), and also the fourth most populous country in the world. The distribution of the religions and people is diverse in Southeast Asia and varies by country. Some 30 million [overseas Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese "Overseas Chinese") also live in Southeast Asia, most prominently in [Christmas Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_Island "Christmas Island"), Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, and Thailand, and also as the [Hoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoa_people "Hoa people") in Vietnam. People of Southeast Asian origins are known as Southeast Asians or Aseanites.

### Ethnic groups

The peoples of Southeast Asia are mainly divided into four major ethnolinguistic groups: the [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples "Austronesian peoples"), Austroasiatic (or Mon-Khmers), [Tai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_peoples "Tai peoples") (part of the wider [Kra-Dai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kra-Dai "Kra-Dai") family) and Tibeto-Burman (part of greater [Sino-Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Tibetan "Sino-Tibetan") language family) peoples. There is also a smaller but significant number of [Hmong–Mien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmong%E2%80%93Mien "Hmong–Mien"), [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese "Han Chinese"), [Dravidians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravidian_peoples "Dravidian peoples"), [Indo-Aryans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_peoples "Indo-Aryan peoples"), Eurasians and Papuans, which also contributes to the diversity of peoples in the region.

The [Aslians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orang_Asli "Orang Asli") and [Negritos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negrito "Negrito") were believed to be one of the earliest inhabitants in the region. They are genetically related to [Papuans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_people_of_New_Guinea "Indigenous people of New Guinea") in Eastern Indonesia, Timor-Leste and [Aboriginal Australians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Aborigines "Australian Aborigines"). In modern times, the [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people "Javanese people") are the largest ethnic group in Southeast Asia, with more than 100 million people, mostly concentrated in [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java "Java"), Indonesia. The second-largest ethnic group in Southeast Asia are the [Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_people "Vietnamese people") (Kinh people) with around 86 million people, mainly inhabiting Vietnam but also forming a significant minority in neighbouring Cambodia and Laos. The [Thais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_people "Thai people") are the third largest with around 59 million people, forming the majority in Thailand.

[Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia") is politically and culturally dominated by the [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people "Javanese people") and [Sundanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundanese_people "Sundanese people") ethnic groups (both native to Java), but the country also has hundreds of ethnic groups scattered throughout the archipelago, such as the [Madurese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madurese_people "Madurese people"), [Minangkabau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minangkabau_people "Minangkabau people"), [Acehnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acehnese_people "Acehnese people"), [Bugis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugis "Bugis"), [Balinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balinese_people "Balinese people"), [Makassarese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makassar_people "Makassar people"), [Dayak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayak_people "Dayak people"), [Minahasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minahasan_people "Minahasan people"), [Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak_(Indonesia) "Batak (Indonesia)"), [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Indonesian "Malay Indonesian"), [Betawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betawi_people "Betawi people"), [Torajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torajan_people "Torajan people") and [Ambonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambonese_people "Ambonese people") peoples.

In Malaysia, the country is demographically divided into [Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Malays "Malaysian Malays"), who make up more than half of the country's population; the [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Chinese "Malaysian Chinese"), at around 22%; other [Bumiputeras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bumiputera_(Malaysia) "Bumiputera (Malaysia)"), at 12%; and [Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians "Malaysian Indians"), at around 6%. In [East Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Malaysia "East Malaysia"), the [Dayaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayaks "Dayaks") (mainly [Ibans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iban_people "Iban people") and [Bidayuhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bidayuh "Bidayuh")) make up the majority in the state of [Sarawak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarawak "Sarawak"), while the [Kadazan-Dusuns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadazan-Dusun "Kadazan-Dusun") make up the majority in [Sabah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabah "Sabah"). In [Labuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labuan "Labuan"), the [Bruneian Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruneian_Malay_people "Bruneian Malay people") and [Kedayans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kedayan "Kedayan") are the largest groups. Overall, the [Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_Malay "Ethnic Malay") are the majority in Malaysia and Brunei and form a significant minority in Indonesia, [Southern Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_Malays "Thai Malays"), [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_Malays "Burmese Malays"), and [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Singaporean "Malay Singaporean"). In Singapore, the demographics of the country is similar to that of its West Malaysian counterparts but instead of Malays, it is the [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Singaporean "Chinese Singaporean") that are the majority, while the Malays are the second largest group and [Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Singaporean "Indian Singaporean") third largest.

Within the Philippines, the country has no majority ethnic groups; but the four largest ethnolinguistic groups in the country are the [Visayans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visayan_people "Visayan people") (mainly [Cebuanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_people "Cebuano people"), [Warays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waray_people "Waray people") and [Hiligaynons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_people "Hiligaynon people")), [Tagalogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_people "Tagalog people"), [Ilocanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_people "Ilocano people") and [Bicolanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicolano_people "Bicolano people"). Besides the major four, there are also the [Moro peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_people "Moro people") of [Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindanao "Mindanao"), consisting of the [Tausug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tausug_people "Tausug people"), [Maranao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maranao_people "Maranao people"), [Yakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakan_people "Yakan people") and [Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_people "Maguindanao people"). Other regional groups in the country are the [Kapampangans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_people "Kapampangan people"), [Pangasinans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_people "Pangasinan people"), [Surigaonons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigaonon_people "Surigaonon people"), [Ifugao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ifugao_people "Ifugao people"), [Kalinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinga_people "Kalinga people"), [Kamayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamayo_people "Kamayo people"), [Cuyonon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyonon_people "Cuyonon people") and [Ivatan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivatan_people "Ivatan people").

In mainland Southeast Asia, the [Burmese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamar "Bamar") accounts for more than two-thirds of the population in Myanmar, but the country also has several regional ethnic groups which mainly live in [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_Divisions_of_Myanmar#States "Administrative Divisions of Myanmar") that are specifically formed for ethnic minorities. The major regional ethnic groups in Myanmar are the Tai-speaking [Shan people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_people "Shan people"), [Karen people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_people "Karen people"), [Rakhine people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhine_people "Rakhine people"), [Chin people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chin_people "Chin people"), [Kayah people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayah_people "Kayah people") and Indo-Aryan-speaking [Rohingya people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya_people "Rohingya people") living on the westernmost part of the country near the border with [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh"). In neighbouring Thailand, the Thais are the largest ethnic group in the country but is divided into several regional Tai groups such as Central Thais, [Northern Thais or Lanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Thai_people "Northern Thai people"), [Southern Thais or Pak Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Thai "Southern Thai"), and Northeastern Thai or [Isan people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isan_people "Isan people") (which is ethnically more closely related to [Lao people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lao_people "Lao people") than to Central Thais), each have their own unique dialects, history and culture. Besides the Thais, Thailand is also home to more than 70 ethnolinguistic groups of which the largest being Patani Malays, [Northern Khmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Khmers "Northern Khmers"), Karen, [Hmongs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmong_people "Hmong people") and [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_Chinese "Thai Chinese").

Cambodia is one of the most homogeneous countries in the area, with [Khmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_people "Khmer people") forming more than 90% of the population but the country also has a large number of ethnic [Chams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cham_people "Cham people"), [Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_Cambodians "Vietnamese Cambodians") and various inland tribes categorised under the term [Khmer Loeu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_Loeu "Khmer Loeu") (Hill Khmers).

### Religion

Countries in Southeast Asia practice many different religions and the region is home to many world religions including Abrahamic, Indian, East Asian and Iranian religions. By population, [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam") is the most practised faith with approximately 240 million adherents, or about 40% of the entire population, concentrated in [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia"), [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei "Brunei"), [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia"), [Southern Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Thailand "Southern Thailand"), and in the [Southern Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Philippines "Southern Philippines"). Indonesia is the most populous Muslim-majority country in the world. Meanwhile, Islam is constitutionally the official religion in Malaysia and Brunei.

There are approximately 190–205 million Buddhists in Southeast Asia, making it the second-largest religion in the region. Approximately 28 to 35% of the world's Buddhists reside in Southeast Asia. [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism") is predominant in Vietnam, Thailand, Laos, Cambodia, Myanmar and Singapore, and adherents may come from Theravada or Mahayana schools. Ancestor worship and [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism "Confucianism") are also widely practised in Vietnam and Singapore. Taoism and Chinese folk religions such as Mazuism are also widely practised by the overseas Chinese community in Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand. In certain cases, they may include Chinese or local deities in their worshipping practises such as Tua Pek Kong, Datuk Keramat and many more.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

[Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity") is predominant in the Philippines, eastern Indonesia, East Malaysia, and Timor-Leste. The Philippines has the largest Roman Catholic population in Asia. Timor-Leste is also predominantly Roman Catholic due to a history of [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia") and Portuguese rule. In October 2019, the number of Christians, both Catholic and Protestant, in Southeast Asia reached 156 million, of which 97 million came from the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines"), 29 million from [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia"), 11 million from [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam "Vietnam"), and the rest from [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia"), [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar"), [Timor-Leste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timor-Leste "Timor-Leste"), [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore"), [Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laos "Laos"), [Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodia "Cambodia") and [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei "Brunei"). In addition, Eastern Orthodox Christianity can also be found in the region. In addition, Judaism is practised in certain countries such as in the Philippines, Singapore and Indonesia due to the presence of Jewish diaspora. There is a small population of Parsis in Singapore who practise [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism "Zoroastrianism"), and [Baháʼí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith "Baháʼí Faith") is also practised by very small population in Malaysia, Vietnam, Singapore and Thailand.

No individual Southeast Asian country is religiously homogeneous. Some groups are protected *de facto* by their isolation from the rest of the world. In the world's most populous Muslim nation, Indonesia, Hinduism is dominant on islands such as [Bali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bali "Bali"). Christianity also predominates in the rest of the part of the Philippines, [New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Guinea "New Guinea"), [Flores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flores "Flores"), and [Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timor "Timor"). Pockets of Hindu population can also be found around Southeast Asia in Singapore, Malaysia, etc. [Garuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garuda "Garuda"), the [phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_(mythology) "Phoenix (mythology)") who is the mount (*vahanam*) of [Vishnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnu "Vishnu"), is a national symbol in both Thailand and Indonesia; in the Philippines, gold images of Garuda have been found on [Palawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palawan "Palawan"); gold images of other Hindu gods and goddesses have also been found on [Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindanao "Mindanao"). [Balinese Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balinese_Hinduism "Balinese Hinduism") is somewhat different from Hinduism practised elsewhere, as [animism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animism "Animism") and local culture is incorporated into it. Meanwhile, Hindu community in Malaysia and Singapore are mostly South Indian diaspora, hence the practices are closely related to the Indian Hinduism. Additionally, Sikhism is also practised by significant population especially in Malaysia and Singapore by North Indian diaspora specifically from Punjab region. Small population of the Indian diaspora in the region are Jains and can be found in Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia. Christians can also be found throughout Southeast Asia; they are in the majority in Timor-Leste and the Philippines, Asia's largest Christian nation. In addition, there are also older tribal religious practices in remote areas of [Sarawak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarawak "Sarawak") in East Malaysia, Highland Philippines, and Papua in eastern Indonesia. In Myanmar, Sakka ([Indra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indra "Indra")) is revered as a *[Nat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nat_(spirit) "Nat (spirit)")*. In Vietnam, Mahayana Buddhism is practised, which is influenced by native animism but with a strong emphasis on [ancestor worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veneration_of_the_dead "Veneration of the dead"). Vietnamese folk religions are practised by majority of population in Vietnam. Caodaism, a monotheistic syncretic new religious movement, is also practised by less than one percent of the population in Vietnam. Due to the presence of Japanese diaspora in the region, the practice of Shinto has growingly made appearance in certain countries such as in Thailand.

## Culture

The culture in Southeast Asia is diverse: on mainland Southeast Asia, the culture is a mix of Burmese, Cambodian, Laotian, Thai ([Indic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_India "Greater India")) and Vietnamese ([Sinic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_cultural_sphere "East Asian cultural sphere")) cultures. While in Indonesia, the Philippines, Singapore, and Malaysia the culture is a mix of indigenous [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_people "Austronesian people"), Indian, Islamic, Western, and Chinese cultures. In addition, Brunei shows a strong influence from Arabia. Vietnam and Singapore show more Chinese influence in that Singapore, although being geographically a Southeast Asian nation, is home to a large Chinese majority and Vietnam was in [China's sphere of influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_cultural_sphere "East Asian cultural sphere") for much of its history. Indian influence in Singapore is most prominently evident through the Tamil migrants, which influenced, to some extent, the [cuisine of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Singapore "Cuisine of Singapore"). Throughout Vietnam's history, it has had no direct influence from India – only through contact with the Thai, Khmer and Cham peoples. Moreover, Vietnam is also categorised under the [East Asian cultural sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_cultural_sphere "East Asian cultural sphere") along with China, Korea, and Japan due to a large amount of Chinese influence embedded in their culture and lifestyle.

[Rice paddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddy_field "Paddy field") agriculture has existed in Southeast Asia for millennia, ranging across the subregion. Some dramatic examples of these rice paddies populate the [Banaue Rice Terraces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banaue_Rice_Terraces "Banaue Rice Terraces") in the mountains of Luzon in the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines"). Maintenance of these paddies is very labour-intensive. The rice paddies are well-suited to the monsoon climate of the region.

[Stilt houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stilt_house "Stilt house") can be found all over Southeast Asia, from Thailand and Vietnam to Borneo, to Luzon in the Philippines, to Papua New Guinea. The region has diverse metalworking, especially in Indonesia. This includes weaponry, such as the distinctive [kris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kris "Kris"), and musical instruments, such as the [gamelan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamelan "Gamelan").

### Influences

The region's chief cultural influences have been from some combination of [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam"), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_India "Culture of India"), and [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_China "Culture of China"). Diverse cultural influence is pronounced in the Philippines, derived particularly from the period of Spanish and American rule, contact with Indian-influenced cultures, and the Chinese and Japanese trading era.

As a rule of thumb, the peoples who ate with their fingers were more likely influenced by the culture of India, for example, than the culture of China, where the peoples ate with [chopsticks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chopstick "Chopstick"); tea, as a beverage, can be found across the region. The [fish sauces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_sauce "Fish sauce") distinctive to the region tend to vary.

### Arts

The [arts of Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_arts "Southeast Asian arts") have an affinity with the arts of other areas. Dance in much of Southeast Asia includes movement of the hands as well as the feet, to express the dance's emotion and meaning of the story that the ballerina is going to tell the audience. Most of Southeast Asia introduced dance into their court; in particular, [Cambodian royal ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_classical_dance "Khmer classical dance") represented them in the early seventh century before the [Khmer Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_Empire "Khmer Empire"), which was highly influenced by Indian Hinduism. The [Apsara Dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apsara_Dance "Apsara Dance"), famous for strong hand and feet movement, is a great example of Hindu symbolic dance.

Puppetry and shadow plays were also a favoured form of entertainment in past centuries, a famous one being the *[wayang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayang "Wayang")* from Indonesia. The arts and literature in some of Southeast Asia are quite influenced by Hinduism, which was brought to them centuries ago. Indonesia, despite large-scale conversion to Islam which opposes certain forms of art, has retained many forms of Hindu-influenced practices, culture, art, and literature. An example is the *[wayang kulit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayang_kulit "Wayang kulit")* (shadow puppet) and literature like the *[Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana "Ramayana")*. The *wayang kulit* show has been recognised by [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO") on 7 November 2003 as a [Masterpiece of Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masterpiece_of_Oral_and_Intangible_Heritage_of_Humanity "Masterpiece of Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity").

It has been pointed out that Khmer and Indonesian classical arts were concerned with depicting the life of the gods, but to the Southeast Asian mind, the life of the gods was the life of the peoples themselves—joyous, earthy, yet divine. The [Tai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_peoples "Tai peoples"), coming late into Southeast Asia, brought with them some Chinese artistic traditions, but they soon shed them in favour of the Khmer and [Mon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mon_people "Mon people") traditions, and the only indications of their earlier contact with [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_China "Culture of China") arts were in the style of their temples, especially the tapering roof, and in their [lacquerware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacquerware "Lacquerware").

#### Music

Traditional music in Southeast Asia is as varied as its many ethnic and cultural divisions. The main styles of traditional music include court music, folk music, music styles of smaller ethnic groups, and music influenced by genres outside the geographic region.

Of the court and folk genres, [gong chime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gong_chime "Gong chime") ensembles and orchestras make up the majority (the exception being lowland areas of Vietnam). *[Gamelan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamelan "Gamelan")* and *[angklung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angklung "Angklung")* orchestras from *[Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia")*; *[piphat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piphat "Piphat")* and *[pinpeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinpeat "Pinpeat")* ensembles of Thailand and Cambodia; and the *[kulintang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulintang "Kulintang")* ensembles of the southern Philippines, [Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo "Borneo"), [Sulawesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulawesi "Sulawesi") and [Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timor "Timor") are the three main distinct styles of musical genres that have influenced other traditional musical styles in the region. String instruments are also popular in the region.

On 18 November 2010, UNESCO officially recognised the *angklung* as a *Masterpiece of Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity*, and encouraged the Indonesian people and government to safeguard, transmit, promote performances and to encourage the craftsmanship of *angklung* making.

#### Writing

The history of Southeast Asia has led to a wealth of different authors, from both within and without writing about the region.

Some of the earliest writing systems of Southeast Asia stem from those of [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_India "Culture of India"). This is shown through [Brahmic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmic_family "Brahmic family") forms of writing present in the region, such as the [Balinese script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balinese_script "Balinese script") shown on split palm leaves called *lontar* (see image to the left – magnify the image to see the writing on the flat side, and the decoration on the reverse side).

The antiquity of this form of writing extends before the invention of paper around the year 100 in China. Note each palm leaf section was only several lines, written longitudinally across the leaf, and bound by twine to the other sections. The outer portion was decorated. The alphabets of Southeast Asia tended to be [abugidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abugida "Abugida"), until the arrival of the Europeans, who used words that also ended in consonants, not just vowels. Other forms of official documents, which did not use paper, included Javanese copperplate scrolls. This material would have been more durable than paper in the tropical climate of Southeast Asia.

In Malaysia, Brunei, and Singapore, the Malay language is now generally written in the Latin script. The same phenomenon is present in Indonesian, although different spelling standards are utilised (e.g. 'Teksi' in Malay and 'Taksi' in Indonesian for the word 'Taxi').

The use of Chinese characters, in the past and present, is only evident in Vietnam and more recently, Singapore and Malaysia. The adoption of [chữ Hán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%E1%BB%AF_H%C3%A1n "Chữ Hán") in Vietnam dates back to around 111 BC when it was occupied by the Chinese. A Vietnamese script called [chữ Nôm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%E1%BB%AF_N%C3%B4m "Chữ Nôm") used modified chữ Hán to express the Vietnamese language. Both chữ Hán and chữ Nôm were used up until the early 20th century.

[Rapa Nui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapa_Nui_language "Rapa Nui language") is an Austronesian language like those of Indonesian, Tagalog, and many other Southeast Asian languages. [Rongorongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rongorongo "Rongorongo") is presumed to be the script of Rapa Nui and if proven so, would place it as one of very few [inventions of writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inventions_of_writing "Inventions of writing") in human history.

#### Sports

[Association football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football "Association football") is the most popular sport in the region, with the [ASEAN Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN_Football_Federation "ASEAN Football Federation"), the region's primary regulatory body, formed on 31 January 1984, in [Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta "Jakarta"), Indonesia. The [AFF Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFF_Championship "AFF Championship") is the largest football competition in the region since its inaugural in [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_AFF_Championship "1996 AFF Championship"), with [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand_national_football_team "Thailand national football team") holding the most titles in the competition with seven titles. The current reigning winner is [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_national_football_team "Vietnam national football team"), who defeated Thailand in the [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_ASEAN_Championship "2024 ASEAN Championship") final. Thailand has had the most numerous appearances in the [AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Asian_Cup "AFC Asian Cup") with 7 while the highest-ranked result in the Asian Cup for a Southeast Asian team is second place in the [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_AFC_Asian_Cup "1968 AFC Asian Cup") by [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_national_football_team "Myanmar national football team") in [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran"). [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_Indies_national_football_team_results "Dutch East Indies national football team results") is the only Southeast Asian team to have played in the [1938 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup "1938 FIFA World Cup") as the [Dutch East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_Indies_national_football_team_results "Dutch East Indies national football team results").

ASEAN has also committed to preserving [traditional sports and games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_sports_and_games "Traditional sports and games") (TSG) in the region.
