# Katie Taylor

**Katie Taylor** (born 2 July 1986) is an Irish former [professional boxer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_boxing "Professional boxing") and [footballer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_association_football "Women's association football"). She is the former [three-time undisputed champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undisputed_championship_(boxing) "Undisputed championship (boxing)"), having previously been the undisputed and undefeated [lineal world lightweight champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lineal_championship "Lineal championship") from 2019 to 2024, before moving up and becoming the undisputed and lineal world [super lightweight champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_welterweight "Light welterweight") on two separate occasions between 2023 and 2026. Credited with raising the profile of [women's boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_boxing "Women's boxing") in Ireland and around the world, Taylor has been described as one of Ireland's greatest ever athletes and is considered by many to be one of the greatest female boxers of all time.

As an [amateur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_boxing "Amateur boxing"), Taylor was involved in the first ever officially sanctioned female boxing bout in Ireland on October 31, 2001 at the age of 15 years old before eventually going on to win five consecutive gold medals at the [Women's World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIBA_Women%27s_World_Boxing_Championships "AIBA Women's World Boxing Championships"), gold six times at the [European Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Amateur_Boxing_Championships "European Amateur Boxing Championships"), and gold five times at the [European Union Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Amateur_Boxing_Championships "European Union Amateur Boxing Championships"). She was also pivotal in getting women’s boxing entered into the Olympics and was the [flag bearer for Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_flag_bearers_for_Ireland_at_the_Olympics "List of flag bearers for Ireland at the Olympics") at the [2012 London Olympics opening ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony "2012 Summer Olympics opening ceremony") before going on to win [the gold medal in the lightweight division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxing_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_lightweight "Boxing at the 2012 Summer Olympics – Women's lightweight").

Taylor turned professional in November 2016 under promoter [Eddie Hearn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Hearn "Eddie Hearn")'s [Matchroom Boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matchroom_Boxing "Matchroom Boxing"). In her professional debut she secured a third round [TKO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TKO "TKO") victory over Poland’s Karina Kopińska, she followed that up with 3 more victories over the next 4 months before getting the opportunity to face undefeated German contender and future world champion [Nina Meinke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Meinke "Nina Meinke") on the undercard of [Anthony Joshua vs Wladimir Klitschko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Joshua_vs_Wladimir_Klitschko "Anthony Joshua vs Wladimir Klitschko") at [Wembley Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley_Stadium "Wembley Stadium") in a final eliminator for the [WBA lightweight title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Boxing_Association "World Boxing Association"), with Taylor winning by seventh round TKO. Later that year only 11 months after making her pro debut she won her first world title by defeating [three-weight world champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weight_class_(boxing) "Weight class (boxing)") [Anahí Ester Sánchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anah%C3%AD_Ester_S%C3%A1nchez "Anahí Ester Sánchez") for the [WBA lightweight title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Boxing_Association "World Boxing Association") by [unanimous decision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unanimous_decision "Unanimous decision"), before later adding the [IBF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Boxing_Federation "International Boxing Federation") and [WBO titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Boxing_Organization "World Boxing Organization") to her collection with wins over [Victoria Bustos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Bustos "Victoria Bustos") and [Rose Volante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Volante "Rose Volante") respectively.

After her TKO victory over [Volante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Volante "Rose Volante"), Taylor called out [WBC champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Boxing_Council "World Boxing Council") [Delfine Persoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delfine_Persoon "Delfine Persoon") and the two met three months later at [Madison Square Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden "Madison Square Garden") to crown the first ever [undisputed lightweight champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undisputed_championship_(boxing) "Undisputed championship (boxing)") as well as the [inaugural *The Ring* lightweight champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_The_Ring_female_world_champions#Lightweight "List of The Ring female world champions"). Taylor came out victorious ending Persoon's 8-year reign and in the process becoming only the seventh boxer in history (female or male) to hold all four major world titles in boxing—WBA, IBF, WBO and WBC—simultaneously. She repeated this feat four years later, at 37 years of age, when she defeated [Chantelle Cameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chantelle_Cameron "Chantelle Cameron") in [Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin "Dublin") for the WBA, IBF, WBO, WBC and IBO titles, regaining the WBO title she had initially won from [Christina Linardatou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christina_Linardatou "Christina Linardatou") back in 2019.

On 5 September 2026, at the age of 40 years and 65 days, she again became [undisputed champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undisputed_championship_(boxing) "Undisputed championship (boxing)") after defeating the undefeated [IBO champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Boxing_Organization "International Boxing Organization") [Flora Pili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora_Pili "Flora Pili") in front of 83,000 at [Croke Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croke_Park "Croke Park"), the largest crowd ever assembled for an individual female sporting event as well as being the [eleventh highest attendance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_boxing_attendance_records "List of boxing attendance records") overall and sixth highest attendance for a post-war boxing event.

With her win over Pili, Taylor became only the fifth boxer (female or male) to become undisputed three times and became the oldest boxer to become [undisputed champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undisputed_championship_(boxing) "Undisputed championship (boxing)"), surpassing [Bernard Hopkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Hopkins "Bernard Hopkins") who achieved the feat at 39 years and 247 days.

Taylor ended her career ranked as the best female boxer, [pound-for-pound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pound_for_pound "Pound for pound"), by *[The Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ring_(magazine) "The Ring (magazine)")* and [BoxRec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BoxRec "BoxRec"). She was known for her fast-paced, aggressive and exciting boxing style.

## Early life

Katie Taylor was born on 2 July 1986 in [Bray, County Wicklow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bray,_County_Wicklow "Bray, County Wicklow"), the daughter of Irish mother Bridget (*née* Cranley), and English-born father, [Pete Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Taylor_(boxing_coach) "Pete Taylor (boxing coach)"). She has an older sister, Sarah, and two older brothers, Lee and Peter; the latter is a mathematics professor at [Dublin City University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_City_University "Dublin City University"). Her father, who was born near [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds "Leeds") and grew up in [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham "Birmingham"), first visited Bray to work with his own father in the amusement arcades on the seafront. After meeting and marrying Bridget, he decided to settle in Bray. In 1986, he became the [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Amateur_Boxing_Association "Irish Amateur Boxing Association") senior light heavyweight champion. Originally an electrician, he later became Taylor's full-time boxing coach. He also coached [Adam Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Nolan "Adam Nolan") who, like Taylor, represented [Ireland at the 2012 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics "Ireland at the 2012 Summer Olympics"). Bridget also developed an interest in boxing and became one of the earliest female referees and judges in Ireland.

Between 1999 and 2005, Taylor attended St. Kilian's Community School in Bray. Her three older siblings had also attended the school. As well as [boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxing "Boxing") and playing [association football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football "Association football"), she played [ladies' Gaelic football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladies%27_Gaelic_football "Ladies' Gaelic football") and [camogie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camogie "Camogie") with her local [GAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaelic_Athletic_Association "Gaelic Athletic Association") clubs, [Bray Emmets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bray_Emmets_GAA "Bray Emmets GAA") and Fergal Ógs. She was a member of Bray Runners, a local [athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_of_athletics "Sport of athletics") club, and several American colleges reportedly offered her sports scholarships while she was still studying at St Killian's. She instead chose to attend [University College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Dublin "University College Dublin"), known for its sports scholarship programme, which she qualified for through her [Leaving Cert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaving_Certificate_(Ireland) "Leaving Certificate (Ireland)") results. As her sporting career began to progress, she decided not to complete her studies at UCD.

Taylor is a cousin of former [UFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_Fighting_Championship "Ultimate Fighting Championship") [flyweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flyweight_(MMA) "Flyweight (MMA)") [Molly McCann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molly_McCann "Molly McCann").

## Amateur career

Taylor first began boxing in 1996, aged 10. Her father coached her and her two older brothers, Lee and Peter, at St Fergal's Boxing Club, which operated out of a former boathouse in [Bray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bray,_County_Wicklow "Bray, County Wicklow"). At 15, in 2001, she fought in the first officially sanctioned female boxing match in Ireland, at the [National Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Stadium_(Ireland) "National Stadium (Ireland)"), and defeated Alanna Audley from Belfast.

### List of title fights

2005 European Amateur Boxing Championship

Taylor's first noteworthy success was at the 2005 [European Amateur Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Amateur_Boxing_Championships "European Amateur Boxing Championships"), in [Tønsberg, Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%B8nsberg,_Norway "Tønsberg, Norway"). She won the gold medal, defeating [Eva Wahlström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eva_Wahlstr%C3%B6m "Eva Wahlström") of Finland in the final of the 60 kg lightweight class.

2005 AIBA Women's World Boxing Championship

Later in 2005, at the [World Amateur Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Amateur_Boxing_Championships "World Amateur Boxing Championships") in [Podolsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podolsk "Podolsk"), [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia "Russia"), Taylor advanced to the quarter-finals in the 60 kg weight class, where she lost to Kang Kum-Hui.

2006 European Amateur Boxing Championship

At the 2006 European Amateur Championships in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw "Warsaw"), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland "Poland"), Taylor won her second successive gold medal by stopping reigning world champion Tatiana Chalaya of Russia, also collecting the tournament's Best Boxer award.

2006 AIBA Women's World Boxing Championship

At the [2006 World Women's Boxing Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_World_Women%27s_Boxing_Championship "2006 World Women's Boxing Championship"), contested in [New Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Delhi "New Delhi"), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India"), Taylor became Ireland's first World Champion, defeating Chalaya again in the semi-final and then [Erica Farias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erica_Farias "Erica Farias") of Argentina in the 60 kg final.

2007 European Amateur Boxing Championship

In 2007, she won her third successive European Championship title in Denmark.

2008 Women's European Union Amateur Boxing Championships

2008 saw Taylor win her first European Union gold medal, contested in August in [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool "Liverpool"), [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England "England"). Here she defeated Cindy Orain of France.

2008 AIBA Women's World Boxing Championship

Taylor went on to claim her second World title at the [2008 AIBA Women's World Boxing Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_AIBA_Women%27s_World_Boxing_Championship "2008 AIBA Women's World Boxing Championship"), contested in November at [Ningbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ningbo "Ningbo"), [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China"). In the 60 kg weight class, she defeated China's Cheng Dong in the final match which was her 100th bout.

2009

On 21 March 2009 at [The Dublin O2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3Arena_(Dublin) "3Arena (Dublin)"), Taylor won a 27–3 win over three-time Pan-American champion Caroline Barry of the United States on the undercard of a pro WBA super bantamweight world title fight between [Bernard Dunne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Dunne "Bernard Dunne") of Ireland and [Ricardo Cordoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Cordoba "Ricardo Cordoba") of Panama. Speaking after the fight, Taylor, who had stopped Barry in the final of the 2006 World Championships in New Delhi, said she was stunned by the welcome she received from Irish boxing fans. She said: "I couldn't believe the reception I got – it was an amazing experience for me. I knew it was going to be a tough fight and well done to her for never backing off."

2009 Women's European Union Amateur Boxing Championships

Taylor defended her European Union title in 2009. She beat home favourite, Bulgaria's Denitsa Elisayeva, in the July tournament hosted in [Pazardzhik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pazardzhik "Pazardzhik").

2010 AIBA Women's World Boxing Championship

On 18 September 2010, Taylor went on to claim her third successive World title at the [2010 AIBA Women's World Boxing Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_AIBA_Women%27s_World_Boxing_Championship "2010 AIBA Women's World Boxing Championship"), in [Barbados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbados "Barbados"). In the 60 kg weight class, she again defeated China's Cheng Dong in the final match. This was Taylor's 100th career win.

2011 EU Women Boxing Championships

Taylor won the gold medal at the EU Women Boxing Championships in Katowice, Poland in 2011.

2012 AIBA Women's World Boxing Championship

On 19 May 2012, Taylor won her fourth successive World title at the [2012 AIBA Women's World Boxing Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_AIBA_Women%27s_World_Boxing_Championship "2012 AIBA Women's World Boxing Championship"), in [Qinhuangdao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qinhuangdao "Qinhuangdao") China. In the 60 kg weight class, she defeated Russian southpaw [Sofya Ochigava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofya_Ochigava "Sofya Ochigava").

2012 Summer Olympics

Taylor qualified for the [2012 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics "2012 Summer Olympics") in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London"), the first time women's boxing had been considered for inclusion. Crowds gathered on the streets of her hometown Bray to watch her progress on giant screens erected especially for the occasion. Coddle released a single called "Katie Taylor Ireland's Boxing Legend". The song reached number 42 in the Irish Charts.

Taylor's first appearance at the 2012 Summer Olympics came on 6 August, after a first round bye. She achieved an impressive 26–15 victory (R1: 5–2, R2: 5–5, R3: 9–4, R4: 7–4) over Great Britain's [Natasha Jonas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natasha_Jonas "Natasha Jonas"), booking her place in the semi-final and guaranteeing her, at least, an Olympic bronze medal. Fans of Taylor produced record noise levels at the Olympics.

In the semi-final on 8 August 2012, she proved far too good for Tajikistan's [Mavzuna Chorieva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mavzuna_Chorieva "Mavzuna Chorieva") and won in a 17–9 victory (R1: 3–1, R2: 4–2, R3: 6–3, R4: 4–3), booking her place in the final and guaranteeing her, at least, an Olympic silver medal.

Taylor defeated Russia's [Sofya Ochigava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofya_Ochigava "Sofya Ochigava") in the final bout by 10–8 (R1: 2–2, R2: 1–2, R3: 4–1, R4: 3–3) on 9 August 2012, winning an Olympic gold medal, and becoming the first ever Olympic female [lightweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxing_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_lightweight "Boxing at the 2012 Summer Olympics – Women's lightweight") champion.

On her return to Dublin with the rest of the Olympic squad she got into the cockpit of the plane and leaned out the window waving an Irish flag.

2014 AIBA Women's World Boxing Championships

On 24 November 2014, Taylor won her fifth straight [lightweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_AIBA_Women%27s_World_Boxing_Championships_%E2%80%93_Lightweight "2014 AIBA Women's World Boxing Championships – Lightweight") title in South Korea at the [2014 AIBA Women's World Boxing Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_AIBA_Women%27s_World_Boxing_Championships "2014 AIBA Women's World Boxing Championships"), defeating [Yana Alekseevna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yana_Alekseevna "Yana Alekseevna") of Azerbaijan. The final scoreline was 40–36, 39–37, and 39–37 in her favour.

2015 European Games

On 27 June 2015, Taylor won the lightweight title in Azerbaijan at the inaugural [European Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxing_at_the_2015_European_Games#Women's_events "Boxing at the 2015 European Games"), defeating [Estelle Mossely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estelle_Mossely "Estelle Mossely") of France. The final scoreline was 40–36, 40–36, and 39–37 in her favour.

2016 AIBA Women's World Boxing Championships

On 24 May 2016, Taylor qualified for the [2016 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Summer_Olympics "2016 Summer Olympics") after defeating Victoria Torres in the quarter-finals of the [lightweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_AIBA_Women%27s_World_Boxing_Championships_%E2%80%93_Lightweight "2016 AIBA Women's World Boxing Championships – Lightweight") division at the [2016 AIBA Women's World Boxing Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_AIBA_Women%27s_World_Boxing_Championships "2016 AIBA Women's World Boxing Championships"). Two days later, Taylor lost to Estelle Mossely in the semi-finals which ended her quest for a sixth World title in a row.

2016 Summer Olympics

On 15 August 2016, Taylor lost 2–1 in the quarter-finals to [Mira Potkonen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mira_Potkonen "Mira Potkonen") of [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland "Finland") and did not advance.

## Professional career

### Debut and early career

After a successful 15 year amateur career spanning 189 fights Taylor reached out to promoter [Eddie Hearn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Hearn "Eddie Hearn") and soon after officially sign with [Matchroom Boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matchroom_Boxing "Matchroom Boxing") becoming the first female boxer to sign with the promotion.

Taylor made her professional debut on 26 November 2016, scoring a third-round [technical knockout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_knockout "Technical knockout") (TKO) over Karina Kopińska at the [Wembley Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley_Arena "Wembley Arena") in London.

In her second professional bout she faced [Viviane Obenauf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viviane_Obenauf "Viviane Obenauf") on 10 December 2016 at the [Manchester Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Arena "Manchester Arena"), [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester "Manchester"). The fight was televised live on [Sky Sports Box Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Sports_Box_Office "Sky Sports Box Office") as part of the undercard for the [Anthony Joshua vs. Éric Molina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Joshua_vs._%C3%89ric_Molina "Anthony Joshua vs. Éric Molina") heavyweight world title fight. In a bout which saw Taylor score a knockdown in the second round after Obenauf appeared to slip, and the latter suffer a cut to her left eye following an apparent clash of heads, Taylor won by a shutout six-round [points decision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Points_decision "Points decision") (PTS) with the referee scoring the bout 60–53.

She scored two more wins in March 2017—against Monica Gentili via fifth-round TKO and Milena Koliva via PTS over eight rounds—before facing [Nina Meinke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Meinke "Nina Meinke") for the vacant [WBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Boxing_Association "World Boxing Association") International female [lightweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightweight "Lightweight") title on 29 April 2017 at the Wembley Stadium. The bout served as a final eliminator for the [WBA female lightweight title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_WBA_female_world_champions#Lightweight "List of WBA female world champions") and was televised live on Sky Sports Box Office as part of the undercard for the [Anthony Joshua vs. Wladimir Klitschko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Joshua_vs._Wladimir_Klitschko "Anthony Joshua vs. Wladimir Klitschko") world title fight. Taylor controlled the contest from the opening bell with her speed and combination punches. Meinke suffered a cut in the fifth round from an accidental clash of heads. With a badly swollen right eye, and after being on the receiving end of a flurry of punches, referee Howard Foster called a halt to the contest in the seventh round to save Meinke from further punishment, handing Taylor a TKO victory.

Three months later Taylor made her American debut when she faced Jasmine Clarkson at the [Barclays Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barclays_Center "Barclays Center") in New York City. Taylor secured a third-round stoppage victory in the fight via [corner retirement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corner_retirement "Corner retirement") (RTD), taking Taylor's record to 6-0.

### WBA lightweight champion

#### Taylor vs. Sánchez

Following her win over Clarkson in July Taylor finally got her shot to win her first world title as she faced former two-weight world champion [Anahí Ester Sánchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anah%C3%AD_Ester_S%C3%A1nchez "Anahí Ester Sánchez") for the vacant WBA female lightweight title on 28 October 2017 at the [Principality Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_Stadium "Principality Stadium") in [Cardiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff "Cardiff"), Wales. Sánchez was set to defend the title on the night, however, she failed to make weight for the fight and was subsequently stripped by the WBA, with the title on the line for Taylor only. The bout was televised live on Sky Sports Box Office as part of the undercard for [Anthony Joshua vs. Carlos Takam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Joshua_vs._Carlos_Takam "Anthony Joshua vs. Carlos Takam"). After controlling the first round with her speed and power, Taylor dropped the former champion to the canvas with a body shot in the second. Sánchez was hurt again in the next round after a right hand from Taylor caused her to stagger. Sánchez had some success in the fifth, landing right hands to force Taylor onto the back foot. Taylor regained control for the rest of the fight to secure a wide [unanimous decision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unanimous_decision "Unanimous decision") (UD) victory, with all three judges scoring the bout 99–90 to hand Taylor her first world title.

#### Taylor vs. McCaskill

The first defence of her title came against future [undisputed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undisputed_championship_(boxing) "Undisputed championship (boxing)") welterweight champion [Jessica McCaskill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_McCaskill "Jessica McCaskill") on 13 December at the [York Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_Hall "York Hall") in London. McCaskill had spent months talking trash about Taylor and now had the chance to back it up in the ring. After a closely contested first round, Taylor began to take control with her speed and power before McCaskill found momentary success in the sixth round. Taylor received a controversial point deduction in the seventh, but took back control keeping McCaskill at a distance and fighting at range for the next three rounds to secure a UD victory. One judge scored the bout 98–91 and the other two scored it 97–92.

### WBA & IBF lightweight champion

#### Taylor vs. Bustos

A unification bout against reigning [IBF female lightweight champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_IBF_female_world_champions#Lightweight "List of IBF female world champions") [Victoria Bustos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Bustos "Victoria Bustos") took place on 28 April 2018 at the [Barclays Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barclays_Center "Barclays Center") in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"). Taylor controlled the majority of the fight with her speed from a distance, only encountering difficulty in rounds seven and eight after engaging Bustos at close range. Taylor secured the win with a wide UD to add the IBF title to her growing collection, with two judges scoring the bout 99–91 and the third scoring it 98–92.

### Title defences

After defeating Bustos, Taylor made three more successful defences of her titles in 2018, taking her record to 12-0. She started with a dominant win over [Kimberly Connor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimberly_Connor "Kimberly Connor") via third-round TKO in July. After that she defeated former [WBO female featherweight champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_WBO_female_world_champions#Featherweight "List of WBO female world champions") [Cindy Serrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cindy_Serrano "Cindy Serrano") by UD in October; To close out 2018 Taylor faced 22-0 [WBC female super-featherweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_WBC_female_world_champions#Super_featherweight "List of WBC female world champions") champion Eva Wahlström in December at [Madison Square Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden "Madison Square Garden") on the undercard of [Canelo Álvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canelo_%C3%81lvarez "Canelo Álvarez") vs. [Rocky Fielding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Fielding "Rocky Fielding"). Taylor won 100-90 on all three judges scorecards. After the fight Taylor was asked who she might want to fight next; she named [Delfine Persoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delfine_Persoon "Delfine Persoon"), [Rose Volante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Volante "Rose Volante") and [Amanda Serrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Serrano "Amanda Serrano").

### Unified champion

#### Taylor vs. Volante

Taylor's first fight of 2019 was another unification bout, facing [WBO female lightweight champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_WBO_female_world_champions#Lightweight "List of WBO female world champions") [Rose Volante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Volante "Rose Volante") on 15 March at the [Liacouras Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liacouras_Center "Liacouras Center") in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia"), [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania "Pennsylvania"). Taylor started the fight strong, using her combination punching and movement to take control, dropping Volante to the canvas with a right hand in the opening round. The second round saw Taylor stay behind the jab, keeping her opponent at range as Volante attempted to close the distance and fight at close quarters. Volante switched tactics in the third round, staying in the centre of the ring and waiting for Taylor to press the action. Taylor obliged, but the Irish champion's footwork allowed her to get in-and-out before Volante could land any meaningful punches. After much of the same in the fourth, Taylor began to pile on the pressure in the fifth, switching her attacks from head to body to find an opening with a powerful body shot to hurt Volante. Round six saw Taylor pierce the guard of Volante with straight punches, leaving her opponent with a bloodied nose. Volante had moments of success in the seventh round with heavy punches to the body of Taylor, before the Irish woman resumed her attacks to regain control. Taylor continued her success in the eighth, landing an overhand right followed by a rapid six-punch combination. After Volante was on the receiving end of a flurry of punches, and with a cut over her nose which appeared to be the result of a clash of heads, referee Benjy Esteves stepped in and called a halt to the contest to hand Taylor a ninth-round TKO victory and her third world title.

In the post-fight interview, Taylor declared her intentions of becoming the [undisputed female lightweight champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_undisputed_world_boxing_champions#Lightweight "List of undisputed world boxing champions"), saying; "Now we can start talking about that fight, [Persoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delfine_Persoon "Delfine Persoon"). That name's been coming up over the last two years. I've got the three belts, she's got the [WBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Boxing_Council "World Boxing Council") belt, so we have to get that fight on next."

### Undisputed champion

#### Taylor vs. Persoon I

In December 2018, Irish journalist [Evanne Ní Chuilinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evanne_N%C3%AD_Chuilinn "Evanne Ní Chuilinn") stated that Taylor accused Delfine Persoon of turning down a "life-changing" amount of money to fight her. Later Persoon claimed to have offered $100,000 to Taylor for a fight. Taylor's manager stated that Taylor wouldn't even get out of bed for this sum. On 15 April 2019, Taylor and Persoon agreed to a lightweight title unification bout, to take place on 1 June at [Madison Square Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden "Madison Square Garden") as undercard for the [Anthony Joshua vs. Andy Ruiz Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Joshua_vs._Andy_Ruiz_Jr. "Anthony Joshua vs. Andy Ruiz Jr.") heavyweight title fight. In addition to all four sanctioning body's titles being on the line, [*The Ring*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ring_(magazine) "The Ring (magazine)") magazine belt was also at stake.

Persoon's management labelled Taylor's team "totally disrespectful" and claimed "psychological warfare" after a series of demands late in the fight week. This included a claim of forcing Persoon to switch hotels on the grounds that Taylor was staying in the same hotel, and demands for an additional blood test and to prove Persoon has exercise asthma. Taylor's team denied expelling Persoon's team from the hotel, but claimed that there was a clause in the contract allowing them to do so which Persoon's manager should have been aware of. Although, in a different interview Taylor's team stated it was not done by them, but by the organizers and that they had no idea about this forced move out of the hotel. Taylor's team accused Persoon's manager of purposefully attempting to stir controversy by booking his fighter into the hotel Taylor was staying at.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

From the opening bell, Taylor came under relentless pressure from Persoon, forcing the Irish champion to fight off the back foot and rely on counter punching. After having success in the first round, Taylor began to have difficulties with Persoon's pressure in the second and third, with the Belgian champion employing roughhouse tactics in the clinch. Taylor suffered a cut to her forehead in the fourth round. In the fifth, with Taylor ahead on all three judges' scorecards, she spent the majority of the round with her back against the ropes, still engaging with Persoon and having success. Taylor found more success in the sixth, scoring with counter punches while making Persoon miss as the Belgian began to tire. The tables turned in the eighth as Taylor also looked to be tiring, with the highlight of the round being a solid right hand from Persoon to stop Taylor in her tracks. The Irish champion rallied back in the ninth to land her trademark rapid-fire combinations. The tenth and final round was a back-and-forth affair as both fighters traded punches to try and close the show as the stronger fighter. After the final bell rang Taylor secured the win via [majority decision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority_decision "Majority decision") (MD), with two judges scoring the bout in favour of Taylor at 96–94 while the third judge scored it a draw at 95–95, crowning Taylor the first undisputed female lightweight champion. According to [CompuBox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CompuBox "CompuBox") stats, Taylor landed 103 of 410 (25.1%) punches, while Persoon landed 116 of 586 (19.8%).

Controversy ensued regarding the decision, with some ringside media outlets and boxers feeling Persoon did enough to get the win. Former two-weight world champion [Carl Frampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Frampton "Carl Frampton") said, "In my opinion, the judges have got it wrong", and described the decision as "disgraceful". Former two-weight world champion [David Haye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Haye "David Haye") said, "That is not the sight you want to see where someone has given everything in the gym but they do not get the decision because of the political power. Delfine Persoon is going to be heartbroken by that." [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN "ESPN")'s unofficial scorecard also had Persoon winning by a small margin. [DAZN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DAZN "DAZN") commentator [Brian Kenny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Kenny_(sportscaster) "Brian Kenny (sportscaster)") also thought Persoon had won, but explained that the fight was close and did not dispute the judges' decision. Michael Woods, writing for *The Ring* magazine, argued the decision "was no robbery".

### Two-weight world champion

#### Taylor vs. Linardatou

For her next fight, Taylor elected to move up in weight, facing [WBO female junior-welterweight champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_WBO_female_world_champions#Junior_welterweight "List of WBO female world champions") [Christina Linardatou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christina_Linardatou "Christina Linardatou") on 2 November 2019 at the Manchester Arena. The champion started the fight as the aggressor, forcing Taylor to fight off the back foot. The Irish challenger found success at the end of the round with two solid right hands as the bell rang. Taylor started to find her rhythm in the second round, scoring punches while using her fast footwork to make her opponent miss. The third round saw more of the same with Taylor staying behind the jab and landing left hooks. Linardatou found success in the fourth and fifth rounds, landing multiple right hands and leaving Taylor with swelling above her right eye. Taylor began regaining control in the sixth, using her footwork and landing her signature rapid combinations. Round eight saw Taylor plant her feet and trade punches, landing a combination of power punches before taking a solid right hand from the champion that stopped Taylor in her tracks. After critical comments from her corner for taking too many punches in the eighth round, Taylor got back to her boxing for the next two rounds, coasting to a UD victory with the scorecards reading 97–93, 97–93, and 96–94. With the win Taylor became only the third boxer from Ireland, after [Steve Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Collins "Steve Collins") and Carl Frampton, to capture world titles in two weight divisions.

### Undisputed lightweight title defences

#### Taylor vs. Persoon II

Taylor was scheduled to defend her lightweight titles against seven-division world champion [Amanda Serrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Serrano "Amanda Serrano") on 2 May 2020 at the Manchester Arena. However, due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_England "COVID-19 pandemic in England") the bout was postponed and rescheduled to 4 July, with the venue changing to the [Matchroom Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matchroom_Sport "Matchroom Sport") headquarters in [Brentwood, Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brentwood,_Essex "Brentwood, Essex"). The bout was once again postponed, with a new proposed date of 22 August. After negotiations began to break down, Taylor's promoter, [Eddie Hearn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Hearn "Eddie Hearn"), revealed he was in talks with Delfine Persoon's team to secure a rematch for the 22 August date. On 9 July, it was confirmed that the Taylor vs. Persoon rematch would take place on 22 August at the Matchroom Sport headquarters in Brentwood, Essex, airing live on Sky Sports Box Office as part of the undercard for [Dillian Whyte vs. Alexander Povetkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dillian_Whyte_vs._Alexander_Povetkin "Dillian Whyte vs. Alexander Povetkin").

The match went to Taylor by unanimous points decision, 98–93, 96–94, 96–94, and Persoon conceded that Taylor had clearly won, and deserved to retain the slate of titles held - "she is so good. I have no problem with this result". Two-time world heavyweight champion [Anthony Joshua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Joshua "Anthony Joshua") described it as "another fantastic fight between these two great boxers".

#### Taylor vs. Gutierrez

Taylor defended her titles on 14 November 2020 at The SSE Arena in London against [Miriam Gutiérrez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miriam_Guti%C3%A9rrez "Miriam Gutiérrez"). Taylor dropped her opponent to the canvas with a left hook at the end of the fourth round en route to a unanimous decision victory, with the judges' scorecards reading 100–89, 100–90, and 99–91. During the post-fight interview, Eddie Hearn listed potential opponents for Taylor's next fight, which included Amanda Serrano, a rematch with undisputed welterweight champion Jessica McCaskill, the former undisputed welterweight champion [Cecilia Braekhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecilia_Braekhus "Cecilia Braekhus"), or a crossover fight with [MMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MMA "MMA") fighter [Cris Cyborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cris_Cyborg "Cris Cyborg").

#### Taylor vs. Jonas

Taylor was next scheduled to defend her undisputed lightweight titles at the [AO Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AO_Arena "AO Arena") against future two-weight champion [Natasha Jonas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natasha_Jonas "Natasha Jonas"), an opponent she had previously defeated in the quarter-finals at the 2012 London Olympics. The bout took place on 1 May 2021 on the undercard of [Derek Chisora vs. Joseph Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_Chisora_vs._Joseph_Parker "Derek Chisora vs. Joseph Parker"). Following a highly competitive fight, Taylor won by a narrow unanimous decision (96–94, 95–94, 95–94) to retain her undisputed lightweight titles for the third time. This was the final Taylor fight to be broadcast live on [Sky Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Sports "Sky Sports").

#### Taylor vs. Han

Taylor's next defence was against [Jennifer Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Han "Jennifer Han") in front of a crowd of 20,000 at the [Headingley Rugby League Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headingley_Rugby_League_Stadium "Headingley Rugby League Stadium") in Leeds, England. Taylor won decisively on all three scorecards (100–89) after flooring Han with a short right hook in the eighth round to retain her lightweight titles.

During her post-fight interview, Taylor said "I want the megafights and to be involved in the biggest fights out there. I'm confident I will end my career as unbeaten and undisputed world champion."

#### Taylor vs. Sharipova

Taylor was next announced to be fighting [Firuza Sharipova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firuza_Sharipova "Firuza Sharipova") on 11 December 2021 at the [M&S Bank Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%26S_Bank_Arena "M&S Bank Arena"), Liverpool. Taylor defeated Sharipova via unanimous decision (98–92, 97–92, 96–93) to successfully defend her undisputed lightweight titles for the fifth time.

#### Taylor vs. Serrano

Taylor defended her undisputed lightweight titles against [seven-division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septuple_champion "Septuple champion") world champion [Amanda Serrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Serrano "Amanda Serrano") on 30 April 2022 at [Madison Square Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden "Madison Square Garden"). The fight was the first women's boxing match to headline Madison Square Garden, and was described as the 'biggest women's fight of all time' in the build up. Despite being badly hurt in the fifth round, Taylor rallied to retain her titles with a split decision victory (94–96, 97–93, and 96–93). The bout was named Fight of the Year by [Sports Illustrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_Illustrated "Sports Illustrated") and Event of the Year by *The Ring*.

#### Taylor vs. Carabajal

Taylor successfully defended her undisputed lightweight titles for the seventh and final time against undefeated [Argentine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina "Argentina") boxer, Karen Carabajal, on 29 October 2022 at the [OVO Arena, Wembley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OVO_Arena_Wembley "OVO Arena Wembley") winning by unanimous decision (100–91, 98–92, and 99–91). In her post-fight interview, Taylor called for her next fight to be held in the 82,300 capacity [Croke Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croke_Park "Croke Park") stadium in [Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin "Dublin").

### Return to super-lightweight

#### Taylor vs. Cameron

Taylor was scheduled to defend her lightweight titles in a rematch against [Amanda Serrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Serrano "Amanda Serrano") on May 20 in [Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin "Dublin"), following Serrano becoming the undisputed featherweight champion with a victory over [Erika Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erika_Cruz "Erika Cruz"). However, in late February 2023 it was announced that Serrano had suffered an injury and the bout would not take place. On March 2, Taylor took to [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram "Instagram") and publicly challenged the undisputed super-lightweight champion [Chantelle Cameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chantelle_Cameron "Chantelle Cameron") to fill the vacancy left by Serrano. Cameron accepted, and the bout was subsequently announced by Eddie Hearn on March 11 to be taking place at the [3Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3Arena_(Dublin) "3Arena (Dublin)") for Cameron's undisputed super-lightweight titles. The bout was Taylor's first professional fight in her home country of [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland "Republic of Ireland") and saw her attempt to become a two-weight undisputed world champion. The fight went the distance but Taylor lost by majority decision, 94–96, 94–96, 95–95.

#### Taylor vs. Cameron II

Following her first professional loss, Taylor invoked the rematch clause in her contract to secure a second fight with Cameron on 25 November 2023, again at the 3Arena. Despite going down in the first round, which was ultimately ruled as a slip, Taylor emerged victorious via a majority decision (95–95, 98–92, 96-94) after 10 rounds to hand Cameron her first professional loss. In doing so, she became the undisputed super-lightweight champion and a two-weight undisputed champion. The win also made Taylor the second female boxer, and the first Irish female boxer, to be an undisputed champion in two weight classes; the first was the American female boxer [Claressa Shields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claressa_Shields "Claressa Shields"). Post-fight, Taylor stated a willingness to fight Cameron again, preferably at Croke Park.

#### Taylor vs. Serrano II

Taylor was scheduled to make the first defense of her undisputed super lightweight title in a rescheduled rematch against [Amanda Serrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Serrano "Amanda Serrano") at [AT&T Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT%26T_Stadium "AT&T Stadium") in [Arlington, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington,_Texas "Arlington, Texas"). The bout was to serve as the co-main event to [Jake Paul vs. Mike Tyson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Paul_vs._Mike_Tyson "Jake Paul vs. Mike Tyson"), originally on July 20, 2024. However, due to [Mike Tyson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Tyson "Mike Tyson") having an ulcer flare up, the event was postponed and took place at the same stadium on November 15, 2024. Following another gruelling fight, Taylor retained her titles by unanimous decision, winning 95–94 on all three scorecards. Netflix reported that the bout had an "average minute audience" (AMA) of 47 million in the United States, making it the most-watched women's professional sporting event in U.S. history.

### Trilogy

#### Taylor vs. Serrano III

A trilogy fight against [Amanda Serrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Serrano "Amanda Serrano") at [Madison Square Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden "Madison Square Garden") in New York occurred on 11 July 2025. The bout headlined the first all-women's boxing card at Madison Square Garden, live on Netflix.

Taylor successfully defended her undisputed super lightweight title by majority decision (95–95, 97–93, 97–93) closing out the epic trilogy and going 3–0 against her longtime rival to cement herself as the No.1 P4P fighter.

### Three-times undisputed & retirement

#### Taylor vs. Pili

On 11 July 2025, after beating [Amanda Serrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Serrano "Amanda Serrano") for the third and final time, Taylor at 39 years of age was considering retirement, with her promoter [Eddie Hearn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Hearn "Eddie Hearn") claiming that the only thing he could see making her reconsider retiring would be her finally getting to headline [Croke Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croke_Park "Croke Park"), something that had been discussed for years but had never materialised.

On 17 September 2025, after Taylor had made the decision to take some time away from boxing to consider her future, the [WBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Boxing_Council "World Boxing Council") made her champion in recess. The WBC also named her [Champion Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WBC_Emeritus "WBC Emeritus"). Interim WBC champion [Chantelle Cameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chantelle_Cameron "Chantelle Cameron"), who Taylor had previously beaten to become undisputed champion in 2023, was elevated to full champion status.
Cameron was ordered to fight former world champion and long time rival [Sandy Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandy_Ryan "Sandy Ryan") for the WBC title. Cameron refused to fight Ryan and instead, only a month after being elevated to full champion, vacated the title. Ryan was instead ordered to fight Karla Ramos Zamora for the vacant WBC title on 21 February 2026. Despite suffering a broken hand early in the fight, Ryan pulled off the win via majority decision. After the fight, Ryan declared an interest in an undisputed title fight against Taylor.

Ryan vacated the title in June 2026 without making any defences, after announcing she was pregnant, and the WBC designated her as champion in recess. On 3 June 2026, the WBC ordered a fight for the vacant title between Taylor and undefeated 12–0 IBO female super-lightweight champion, [Flora Pili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora_Pili "Flora Pili"), who was ranked No.1 by both the WBC and IBF. On 5 June 2026, at a [Matchroom Boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matchroom_Boxing "Matchroom Boxing") press conference, it was announced that Taylor vs Pili would happen on 5 September 2026 at [Croke Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croke_Park "Croke Park"), with Taylor defending her IBF, WBO and WBA titles and the vacant WBC title also being on the line.

Taylor beat [Pili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora_Pili "Flora Pili") via [unanimous decision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unanimous_decision "Unanimous decision") with the three judges scoring the fight (100–90, 100–90, 99–91) in front of 83,000 fans before leaving her gloves and titles in the ring as she retired from the sport after 30 years.

## Association football

### Club career

As a schoolgirl, Taylor played [association football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football "Association football") for St Fergal's and Newtown Juniors in the [Wicklow and District Schoolboys League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wicklow_and_District_Schoolboys_League "Wicklow and District Schoolboys League") and Ballyfermot United in the Dublin and District Schoolboys league. Taylor played in teams with boys. She also represented the league in the Kennedy Cup, an inter–league competition, and won the league's Player of the Year award. At senior club level, Taylor played in the [Dublin Women's Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_Women%27s_Soccer_League "Dublin Women's Soccer League") for Lourdes Celtic, [St James's Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_James%27s_Gate_F.C. "St James's Gate F.C."), [St Catherine's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Catherine%27s_L.F.C. "St Catherine's L.F.C.") and [Peamount United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peamount_United_F.C. "Peamount United F.C.").
Taylor played for St James's Gate in the 2003 [DWSL Premier Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DWSL_Premier_Cup "DWSL Premier Cup") final against [Dundalk City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dundalk_City_L.F.C. "Dundalk City L.F.C."). Gate lost 3–2 after extra time. Taylor also played for Peamount United in the 2005 [FAI Women's Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAI_Women%27s_Cup "FAI Women's Cup") final. However Peamount lost 1–0 to Dundalk City. In 2009, Taylor was a member of the St Catherine's squad that reached the FAI Women's Cup final. However, her boxing commitments saw her miss out on the final itself. In 2010, together with [Nicola Sinnott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicola_Sinnott "Nicola Sinnott"), [Áine O'Gorman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81ine_O%27Gorman "Áine O'Gorman"), [Sara Lawlor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Lawlor "Sara Lawlor") and [Louise Quinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louise_Quinn "Louise Quinn"), Taylor was a member of the Peamount United squad that won a treble, winning the Dublin Women's Soccer League, the DWSL Premier Cup and the FAI Women's Cup.

### International

Taylor represented the [Republic of Ireland women's national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland_women%27s_national_football_team "Republic of Ireland women's national football team") at [under-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland_women%27s_national_under-17_football_team "Republic of Ireland women's national under-17 football team"), [under-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland_women%27s_national_under-19_football_team "Republic of Ireland women's national under-19 football team") and senior levels. She was 14 when she played for the under-17s and 15 when she started playing for the under-19s. According to Taylor, her boxing training helped her football career because it made her physically strong enough to bridge the age gaps. On 25 September 2003, Taylor scored four goals for the Republic of Ireland under-19s in a [2004 UEFA Women's Under-19 Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_UEFA_Women%27s_Under-19_Championship "2004 UEFA Women's Under-19 Championship") qualifier against Macedonia.

Between 2006 and 2009, Taylor made 11 appearances and scored 2 goals for the senior team. She made her senior international debut on 22 April 2006 against [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland_women%27s_national_football_team "Switzerland women's national football team") in a [2007 FIFA Women's World Cup qualifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup_qualification_(UEFA) "2007 FIFA Women's World Cup qualification (UEFA)") at [Richmond Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond_Park_(football_ground) "Richmond Park (football ground)"). The Republic of Ireland won 2–0. In [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_in_American_soccer "2006 in American soccer") Taylor also played for the Republic of Ireland in a prestige away friendly against the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_women%27s_national_soccer_team "United States women's national soccer team"). Taylor scored her first senior international goal on 1 April 2007 in a [UEFA Women's Euro 2009 qualifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Women%27s_Euro_2009_qualifying "UEFA Women's Euro 2009 qualifying") against [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_women%27s_national_football_team "Hungary women's national football team") at [Tolka Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolka_Park "Tolka Park"). Taylor's goal helped the Republic of Ireland win 2–1. She scored her second goal in the same qualifying campaign in a 4–1 defeat against in [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_women%27s_national_football_team "Italy women's national football team") on 16 February 2008. She was also sent off in the same game. She made her final international appearance on 24 September 2009 when she came on as substitute in a [2011 FIFA Women's World Cup qualifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_6 "2011 FIFA Women's World Cup qualification – UEFA Group 6") against [Kazakhstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakhstan_women%27s_national_football_team "Kazakhstan women's national football team").

## Personal life

Taylor and her family attend [St. Mark's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mark%27s_Church,_Dublin "St. Mark's Church, Dublin") in [Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin "Dublin"), which is part of the [Assemblies of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assemblies_of_God "Assemblies of God"), the world's largest [Pentecostal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostal "Pentecostal") denomination.

In 2002, at the age of 15, Taylor appeared on RTÉ's *Sport Stream* and discussed her aim of one day appearing at the Olympics. She was a coach on RTÉ's *[Charity Lords of the Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charity_Lords_of_the_Ring "Charity Lords of the Ring")* in 2009, and appeared in a [Lucozade Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucozade_Sport "Lucozade Sport") advertisement in 2011 alongside English rapper [Tinie Tempah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinie_Tempah "Tinie Tempah") and American musician [Travis Barker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_Barker "Travis Barker"). She became the brand ambassador for Its4women, an online insurance company aimed at young women, in 2016. She appeared in advertisements for the company on television and YouTube.

*My Olympic Dream*, Taylor's "illustrated memoir" written with assistance from *[The Irish Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Irish_Times "The Irish Times")* sports writer Johnny Watterson, was published by [Simon & Schuster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_%26_Schuster "Simon & Schuster") in 2012. In 2018, the documentary *Katie* was released, chronicling her early life and her route to her first title shot. She has a fondness for the [Irish language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language "Irish language") and once appeared on [Bernard Dunne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Dunne "Bernard Dunne")'s show *Bród Club* to promote it.

On 15 June 2018, Taylor's father, Pete, was a victim in the [Bray boxing club shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bray_boxing_club_shooting "Bray boxing club shooting"). He was shot in the chest and arm but survived.

In 2025, Taylor appeared with her new husband, American real-estate agent Sean McCavanagh. As of November 2025[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Katie_Taylor&action=edit), they live in Connecticut, where Taylor is stepmother to McCavanagh's five children from his late wife.
