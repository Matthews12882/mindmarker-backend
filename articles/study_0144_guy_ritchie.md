# Guy Ritchie

**Guy Stuart Ritchie** (born 10 September 1968) is an English filmmaker known primarily for British comedy [gangster films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangster_film "Gangster film") and large-scale action-adventure films.

Ritchie left school at the age of 15, and worked in entry-level jobs in the film industry before going on to direct television commercials. In 1995, he directed a short film, *The Hard Case*, followed by the crime comedy *[Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lock,_Stock_and_Two_Smoking_Barrels "Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels")* (1998), his feature-length [directorial debut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_directorial_debuts "List of directorial debuts"). He gained recognition with his second film, *[Snatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snatch_(film) "Snatch (film)")* (2000), which found critical and commercial success. Following *Snatch*, Ritchie directed *[Swept Away](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swept_Away_(2002_film) "Swept Away (2002 film)")* (2002), a critically panned [box-office bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box-office_bomb "Box-office bomb") starring [Madonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna "Madonna"), to whom Ritchie was married between 2000 and 2008. He went on to direct *[Revolver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolver_(2005_film) "Revolver (2005 film)")* (2005) and *[RocknRolla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RocknRolla "RocknRolla")* (2008), which were less successful and received mixed reviews. In 2009 and 2011, he directed the box-office hits *[Sherlock Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes_(2009_film) "Sherlock Holmes (2009 film)")* and its sequel, *[Sherlock Holmes: A Game of Shadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes:_A_Game_of_Shadows "Sherlock Holmes: A Game of Shadows")* starring [Robert Downey Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Downey_Jr. "Robert Downey Jr.") as the title character. The former movie was nominated for [Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards") in [Best Original Score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Score "Academy Award for Best Original Score") and [Best Art Direction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Production_Design "Academy Award for Best Production Design").

His other directed films include *[The Man from U.N.C.L.E.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Man_from_U.N.C.L.E._(film) "The Man from U.N.C.L.E. (film)")* (2015), based on the [1960s television series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Man_From_U.N.C.L.E. "The Man From U.N.C.L.E."), *[King Arthur: Legend of the Sword](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Arthur:_Legend_of_the_Sword "King Arthur: Legend of the Sword")* (2017), and *[Aladdin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aladdin_(2019_film) "Aladdin (2019 film)")* (2019), [Disney's live-action adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_remakes_and_adaptations_of_Disney_animated_films "List of remakes and adaptations of Disney animated films") of their [1992 animated film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aladdin_(1992_Disney_film) "Aladdin (1992 Disney film)"), which grossed over $1 billion worldwide, becoming one of the [highest-grossing films in 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_in_film#Highest-grossing_films "2019 in film") and the highest-grossing film of Ritchie's career. In 2019, he returned to crime comedy with *[The Gentlemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gentlemen_(2019_film) "The Gentlemen (2019 film)")* (2019), which was mostly well received and a commercial success. He subsequently reteamed with [Jason Statham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Statham "Jason Statham") on the action films *[Wrath of Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrath_of_Man "Wrath of Man")* (2021) and *[Operation Fortune: Ruse de Guerre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Fortune:_Ruse_de_Guerre "Operation Fortune: Ruse de Guerre")* (2023). His second film of 2023, *[The Covenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Ritchie%27s_The_Covenant "Guy Ritchie's The Covenant")*, received generally positive reviews.

## Life and career

### 1968–1997: Early life and career beginnings

Ritchie was born in [Hatfield, Hertfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatfield,_Hertfordshire "Hatfield, Hertfordshire"), the second of two children of Amber (*[née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name")* Parkinson) and [Captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_(British_Army_and_Royal_Marines) "Captain (British Army and Royal Marines)") John Vivian Ritchie, a former [Seaforth Highlanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaforth_Highlanders "Seaforth Highlanders") officer and an advertising executive. He has an elder sister, and a half-brother who was born to Amber Parkinson and placed for adoption.

Both of Ritchie's parents remarried. His father's second marriage was to [Shireen Ritchie, Baroness Ritchie of Brompton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shireen_Ritchie,_Baroness_Ritchie_of_Brompton "Shireen Ritchie, Baroness Ritchie of Brompton"), a former model and later [Conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK) "Conservative Party (UK)") politician and [life peer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_peer "Life peer"). Between 1973 and 1980, Ritchie's mother was married to [Sir Michael Leighton, 11th Baronet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leighton_baronets "Leighton baronets") of [Loton Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loton_Park "Loton Park"). As a divorcée, she is styled as Amber, Lady Leighton.

Ritchie is [dyslexic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyslexic "Dyslexic"), and attended [Windlesham House School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windlesham_House_School "Windlesham House School") in [West Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Sussex "West Sussex") and [Stanbridge Earls School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanbridge_Earls "Stanbridge Earls") in [Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampshire "Hampshire"). He was expelled from school at the age of 15. He has claimed that drug use was the reason for expulsion, although Ritchie's father said he was "cutting class and entertaining a girl in his room".

### 1998–2002: Breakthrough

After working on a short film, *The Hard Case* (1995), in 1998, Ritchie met [Matthew Vaughn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Vaughn "Matthew Vaughn"), who had been working in Los Angeles and expressed interest in producing Ritchie's directorial debut, *[Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lock,_Stock_and_Two_Smoking_Barrels "Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels")* (1998). It took 15 months to secure financial backing. [Trudie Styler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trudie_Styler "Trudie Styler") served as an executive producer—she said "I've always liked bad-boy movies".

> There's a perennial pub debate that poses the question: Which is better, *[Snatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snatch_(film) "Snatch (film)")* or *[Lock Stock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lock,_Stock_and_Two_Smoking_Barrels "Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels")*? *Snatch* apologists talk a good game, but the correct answer is, of course, Guy Ritchie's jaw-dropping debut. After all, this is a movie that brought the world '[The Stath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Statham "Jason Statham")', [Vinnie Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinnie_Jones "Vinnie Jones") hammering someone's skull with a car door, and the knowledge that a big purple dildo can be used as an offensive weapon.

— *[Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_(film_magazine) "Empire (film magazine)")* magazine's entry for *Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels* on their list of the 100 best British films

The production of comedy [heist film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heist_film "Heist film") *Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels* was completed in about eight months. Starring [Nick Moran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Moran "Nick Moran"), [Jason Statham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Statham "Jason Statham"), [Jason Flemyng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Flemyng "Jason Flemyng") and [Dexter Fletcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dexter_Fletcher "Dexter Fletcher"), the film exposed them to worldwide audiences, and launched the acting career of former footballer [Vinnie Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinnie_Jones "Vinnie Jones"). It was released in the United Kingdom on 28 August 1998 to critical and commercial success, with [Janet Maslin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Maslin "Janet Maslin") of *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* praising Ritchie's "brash, ebullient direction" and "punchy little flourishes that load this English gangster film". The feature earned $28.1 million at the worldwide box office. At the 1999 [British Academy Film Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Awards "British Academy Film Awards") (BAFTAs), *Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels* was nominated for three awards: [Outstanding British Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Outstanding_British_Film "BAFTA Award for Outstanding British Film"), [Best Editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Editing "BAFTA Award for Best Editing") and [Outstanding Debut by a British Writer, Director or Producer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Outstanding_Debut_by_a_British_Writer,_Director_or_Producer "BAFTA Award for Outstanding Debut by a British Writer, Director or Producer") for Vaughn. The film won a BAFTA for Film of the Year. In response to the film's success, Ritchie created a spin-off television series called *[Lock, Stock...](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lock,_Stock... "Lock, Stock...")*. in 2000.

Ritchie's next film was *[Snatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snatch_(film) "Snatch (film)")* (2000), another crime-comedy about a group of criminals searching for a stolen diamond which starred an ensemble cast including [Benicio del Toro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benicio_del_Toro "Benicio del Toro"), [Dennis Farina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Farina "Dennis Farina"), Flemyng, Jones, [Brad Pitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Pitt "Brad Pitt"), [Rade Šerbedžija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rade_%C5%A0erbed%C5%BEija "Rade Šerbedžija") and Statham. Similar to *Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels*, the film depicted events from different characters' perspectives; a [narrative device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plot_device "Plot device") which he would use in later films. *Snatch* was released on 23 August 2000 to a commercial success at the box office, grossing more than $83 million worldwide. [Mick LaSalle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mick_LaSalle "Mick LaSalle"), writing for *[San Francisco Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Chronicle "San Francisco Chronicle")*, was impressed with Ritchie's directing and "sequences that discharge with energy", but felt the writing could have been better. Film critic [Roger Ebert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ebert "Roger Ebert") describes Ritchie as a "zany, high-energy director" but felt the film's plot "doesn't build and it doesn't arrive anywhere".

In 2001, Ritchie directed a music video for "[What It Feels Like for a Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_It_Feels_Like_for_a_Girl "What It Feels Like for a Girl")", a song performed by [Madonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna "Madonna"), to whom Ritchie was married at the time. In the video, she commits criminal and violent acts towards men; music channels [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV "MTV") and [VH1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VH1 "VH1") banned the video from their rotation, opting to play it only once on the release date. Ritchie directed a short film starring Madonna and [Clive Owen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clive_Owen "Clive Owen"), titled *Star* for season one of *[The Hire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hire "The Hire")*, a 2001 online series to promote [BMW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW "BMW") automobiles. Ritchie's next film, starring Madonna and [Adriano Giannini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adriano_Giannini "Adriano Giannini"), was *[Swept Away](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swept_Away_(2002_film) "Swept Away (2002 film)")* (2002), a remake of [Lina Wertmüller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lina_Wertm%C3%BCller "Lina Wertmüller")'s 1974 Italian film of the [same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swept_Away_(1974_film) "Swept Away (1974 film)"). It is a romantic comedy about a wealthy socialite who is shipwrecked on a deserted island with a Communist sailor. The film was a critical and commercial failure, with an average rating of 5% on film [review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator "Review aggregator") [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes"). Almar Haflidason of the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") was critical of the lead actors, writing, "[Madonna] has neither good comedic sense nor any warmth [...] as for Giannini, he spends the first half of the movie endlessly complaining like some old fishwife". The feature won five awards at the [2002 Golden Raspberry Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23rd_Golden_Raspberry_Awards "23rd Golden Raspberry Awards") for [Worst Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Raspberry_Award_for_Worst_Picture "Golden Raspberry Award for Worst Picture"), [Worst Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Raspberry_Award_for_Worst_Actress "Golden Raspberry Award for Worst Actress"), [Worst Screen Couple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Raspberry_Award_for_Worst_Screen_Couple/Ensemble "Golden Raspberry Award for Worst Screen Couple/Ensemble"), [Worst Remake or Sequel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Raspberry_Award_for_Worst_Prequel,_Remake,_Rip-off_or_Sequel "Golden Raspberry Award for Worst Prequel, Remake, Rip-off or Sequel") and [Worst Director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Raspberry_Award_for_Worst_Director "Golden Raspberry Award for Worst Director").

In 2002, Ritchie conceived a prank show titled *[Swag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swag_(TV_series) "Swag (TV series)")*, for [Channel 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_5_(UK) "Channel 5 (UK)") in the United Kingdom.

### 2003–2015: Critical disappointments and *Sherlock Holmes*

Ritchie's next heist film *[Revolver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolver_(2005_film) "Revolver (2005 film)")* (2005) starred Statham, [Ray Liotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Liotta "Ray Liotta"), [Vincent Pastore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Pastore "Vincent Pastore"), and [André Benjamin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_3000 "André 3000"). The story is about gambler Jake Green (Statham) who is released from prison and seeks revenge on those who stole his money. Filming was completed in late 2004 and the film premiered at the 2005 [Toronto International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_International_Film_Festival "Toronto International Film Festival"). *Revolver* was released in the United Kingdom on 22 September 2005, but it was critically panned and a commercial failure. Simon Guerrier of *FilmFocus* gave it one out of five stars, calling it, "tedious, humourless, pretentious and nasty". [Adrian Hennigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Hennigan "Adrian Hennigan") from the BBC wrote, "the cheeky charm has been replaced by plodding pretentiousness in a film that's illuminated by great action set-pieces and some powerful performances, but not redeemed". Ritchie responded to the criticism by stating, "I don't think anything went wrong with *Revolver*. By its very nature it's an esoteric movie. It's not designed for the masses". The film was budgeted at $27 million but earned $7.1 million at the worldwide box office. In 2007, *Revolver* was re-edited and released for the United States.

In 2008, Ritchie directed *[RocknRolla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RocknRolla "RocknRolla")*, for which he also wrote the screenplay. It stars [Gerard Butler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_Butler "Gerard Butler"), [Tom Wilkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Wilkinson "Tom Wilkinson"), [Thandiwe Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thandiwe_Newton "Thandiwe Newton"), [Mark Strong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Strong "Mark Strong"), [Idris Elba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idris_Elba "Idris Elba"), [Tom Hardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Hardy "Tom Hardy"), and [Toby Kebbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toby_Kebbell "Toby Kebbell"). *RocknRolla* was released on 5 September 2008 in the United Kingdom, reaching number one at the UK box office in its first week of release. Rotten Tomatoes gave the film a 60% rating, stating, "Mixed reviews for Guy Ritchie's return to his London-based cockney wideboy gangster movie roots, but most agree, it's a step in the right direction following two major turkeys". In that same year, Ritchie directed a television commercial for [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc. "Nike, Inc.") called "Take It To The Next Level". The commercial includes appearances from [Cristiano Ronaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristiano_Ronaldo "Cristiano Ronaldo"), [Cesc Fàbregas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesc_F%C3%A0bregas "Cesc Fàbregas"), [Ronaldinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronaldinho "Ronaldinho"), [Wayne Rooney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Rooney "Wayne Rooney"), and [Ruud van Nistelrooy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruud_van_Nistelrooy "Ruud van Nistelrooy").

Ritchie's next directorial effort was *[Sherlock Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes_(2009_film) "Sherlock Holmes (2009 film)")* (2009), starring [Robert Downey Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Downey_Jr. "Robert Downey Jr.") and [Jude Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jude_Law "Jude Law"). The film was released on 25 and 26 December 2009 in the United States and United Kingdom. It was a box-office hit, taking more than $520 million worldwide, and garnered mixed to favourable reviews from critics and general viewers. It received multiple accolades, including two [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards") nominations for [Best Original Score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Score "Academy Award for Best Original Score") and [Best Art Direction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Production_Design "Academy Award for Best Production Design"), and Downey won a [Golden Globe Award for Best Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actor_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Musical_or_Comedy "Golden Globe Award for Best Actor – Motion Picture Musical or Comedy"). In 2011, Ritchie directed the sequel *[Sherlock Holmes: A Game of Shadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes:_A_Game_of_Shadows "Sherlock Holmes: A Game of Shadows")*. It was released on 16 December and was an even greater commercial success, grossing more than $545 million worldwide. [A. O. Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._O._Scott "A. O. Scott") of *The New York Times* criticised Ritchie for "taking liberties" with the characters, and wrote that both films depict "a smoky, overcast Victorian world, infuses it with an air of jocular, hairy laddishness and stages a lot of fights in fussy and tiresome slow motion".

In 2012, Ritchie produced a trailer for the video game *[Call of Duty: Black Ops II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_of_Duty:_Black_Ops_II "Call of Duty: Black Ops II")*. In 2013, he directed a commercial for [H&M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%26M "H&M") featuring [David Beckham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Beckham "David Beckham"). A year later, he directed a commercial for whisky brand [Haig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haig_(whisky) "Haig (whisky)"), which also stars Beckham. He made a return to film in 2015 with *[The Man From U.N.C.L.E.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Man_from_U.N.C.L.E._(film) "The Man from U.N.C.L.E. (film)")*, a remake of the [1960s spy series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Man_from_U.N.C.L.E. "The Man from U.N.C.L.E."). Ritchie and [Lionel Wigram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Wigram_(film_producer) "Lionel Wigram (film producer)") wrote the screenplay about a [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Intelligence_Agency "Central Intelligence Agency") agent and a [KGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGB "KGB") agent who work together to stop a criminal organisation from constructing a nuclear weapon. [Henry Cavill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cavill "Henry Cavill") and [Armie Hammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armie_Hammer "Armie Hammer") played the lead roles. Principal photography commenced in 2013 in London and Italy, with rewrites to the script during filming. It was released on 14 August 2015 by [Warner Bros.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros. "Warner Bros.") to a mixed critical reception. [Glenn Kenny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Kenny "Glenn Kenny") on *[RogerEbert.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RogerEbert.com "RogerEbert.com")* writes that it "is only intermittently engaging and amusing, and those portions of the movie that succeed are also frustrating." However, he praised [Hugh Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Grant "Hugh Grant")'s performance which "saves the movie".

### 2016–present: *Aladdin* and return to crime films

In January 2014, Warner Bros. hired Ritchie to direct the first of six films in a franchise, fantasy adventure *[King Arthur: Legend of the Sword](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Arthur:_Legend_of_the_Sword "King Arthur: Legend of the Sword")* (2017) with [Charlie Hunnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Hunnam "Charlie Hunnam") portraying [King Arthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Arthur "King Arthur"). The feature was released in 2D and [3D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RealD_3D "RealD 3D") on 12 May 2017 in the United States, and a week later in the United Kingdom. Despite high expectations from the studio, it received mixed reviews and was a box-office failure, causing large financial losses for Warner Bros. and [Village Roadshow Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Roadshow_Pictures "Village Roadshow Pictures"), and leading to the cancellation of the subsequent sequels.

In August 2017, Ritchie received the [Raindance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raindance_Film_Festival "Raindance Film Festival") Auteur Award for his contributions to the [British film industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_the_United_Kingdom "Cinema of the United Kingdom"). Next, Ritchie directed [Disney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company "The Walt Disney Company")'s [live-action adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_remakes_and_adaptations_of_Disney_animated_films "List of remakes and adaptations of Disney animated films") of *[Aladdin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aladdin_(2019_film) "Aladdin (2019 film)")* (2019), based on the 1992 animated film of the [same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aladdin_(1992_Disney_film) "Aladdin (1992 Disney film)"). The plot follows [Aladdin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aladdin_(Disney_character) "Aladdin (Disney character)"), a street urchin, as he falls in love with [Princess Jasmine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Jasmine "Princess Jasmine"), befriends a [Genie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genie_(Disney) "Genie (Disney)"), and battles the wicked [Jafar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jafar_(Aladdin) "Jafar (Aladdin)"). Released on 24 May 2019 in the United States, the film was a commercial success despite mixed reviews; *Aladdin* earned more than $1 billion worldwide, becoming one of the [highest-grossing films in 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_in_film#Highest-grossing_films "2019 in film"), and the 34th [highest-grossing film of all-time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_films#Highest-grossing_films "List of highest-grossing films") during its theatrical run. Writing for the *[Chicago Sun-Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Sun-Times "Chicago Sun-Times")*, [Richard Roeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Roeper "Richard Roeper") gave the film 3 out of 4 stars, praising [Will Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Smith "Will Smith"), [Naomi Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Scott "Naomi Scott"), and [Mena Massoud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mena_Massoud "Mena Massoud")'s performances. On [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), *Aladdin* has a [weighted average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_arithmetic_mean "Weighted arithmetic mean") score of 53 out of 100 based on 50 critics, indicating "mixed or average reviews", and on Rotten Tomatoes, the film holds an approval rating of 57% based on 372 reviews with an average rating of 5.88/10.

In 2020, Ritchie returned to crime comedy with *[The Gentlemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gentlemen_(2019_film) "The Gentlemen (2019 film)")*. The story is about an American expat who tries to sell his marijuana business, which triggers a chain reaction response from various criminals. Released on 1 January in the United Kingdom, and a few weeks later in other countries, the film was generally well received. Anton Bitel of *[Sight & Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sight_%26_Sound "Sight & Sound")* wrote that it "returns to the sense and sensibilities of his earliest features". Film critic [James Berardinelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Berardinelli "James Berardinelli") gave the film 3+1⁄2 out of four stars, writing, "*The Gentlemen* takes Ritchie back to his roots" although "the screenplay is too clever by half, with some of the quirkiness being awkward and intrusive." On Rotten Tomatoes, the film gained an approval rating of 74% based on 260 reviews, and earned $115 million worldwide. In October 2020, it was announced that Ritchie was developing a [spin-off television series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gentlemen_(2024_TV_series) "The Gentlemen (2024 TV series)"), which was eventually acquired by [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix"); the series was released on Netflix on 7 March 2024.

Ritchie next directed the action thriller *[Wrath of Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrath_of_Man "Wrath of Man")*, a remake of the 2004 film *[Cash Truck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_Truck "Cash Truck")*, reuniting with Statham. It was released in the United States on 7 May 2021. It was initially set for theatrical release in the United Kingdom by [Lionsgate UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionsgate_UK "Lionsgate UK"), but was instead released straight to streaming on [Amazon Prime Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Prime_Video "Amazon Prime Video") on 10 December 2021.

Ritchie followed up *Wrath of Man* with the spy thriller *[Operation Fortune: Ruse de Guerre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Fortune:_Ruse_de_Guerre "Operation Fortune: Ruse de Guerre")*, again starring Statham. It was scheduled for release in cinemas on 18 March 2022, but was pulled from the schedule a month prior without an explanation; it was eventually released in the United States on 3 March 2023, by [Lionsgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionsgate_Films "Lionsgate Films"). Ritchie next filmed the war drama *[The Covenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Ritchie%27s_The_Covenant "Guy Ritchie's The Covenant")*, starring [Jake Gyllenhaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Gyllenhaal "Jake Gyllenhaal"), [Dar Salim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar_Salim "Dar Salim"), [Antony Starr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antony_Starr "Antony Starr") and [Emily Beecham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Beecham "Emily Beecham"), in February 2022 in [Alicante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alicante "Alicante"), [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain "Spain"). The film was released in the United States on 21 April 2023. In June 2022, it was announced that Ritchie had signed on to direct a live-action film adaptation of Disney's *[Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hercules_(1997_film) "Hercules (1997 film)")*.

In February 2021, Ritchie agreed to direct and write the [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") film *[The Ministry of Ungentlemanly Warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ministry_of_Ungentlemanly_Warfare "The Ministry of Ungentlemanly Warfare")*, based on the book by [Damien Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damien_Lewis_(filmmaker) "Damien Lewis (filmmaker)"), for producer [Jerry Bruckheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Bruckheimer "Jerry Bruckheimer") and [Paramount Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures "Paramount Pictures"). In October 2022, [Henry Cavill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cavill "Henry Cavill") and [Eiza González](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiza_Gonz%C3%A1lez "Eiza González") were cast in the lead roles, with Paramount no longer involved. The film was released in theatres in the United States on 19 April 2024 by [Lionsgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionsgate_Films "Lionsgate Films").

In May 2023, weeks after production wrapped on *The Ministry of Ungentlemanly Warfare*, it was announced Ritchie would next direct the film *[In the Grey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Grey "In the Grey")* (untitled at the time), starring Cavill, Gyllenhaal and González, set to shoot in Spain in the summer of 2023. It concluded production in October 2023, with [Lionsgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionsgate_Films "Lionsgate Films") acquiring domestic distribution rights and [Black Bear UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Bear_Pictures "Black Bear Pictures") releasing in the United Kingdom. The film was set to be released in theatres in the United States on 17 January 2025, but was pulled from the release schedule in November 2024 as post-production was not yet finished. Lionsgate would ultimately relinquish distribution rights, with Black Bear Pictures set to release the film themselves on April 10, 2026.

On 6 July 2023, it was announced Ritchie would executive produce the ESPN documentary series *Gracie*, to be directed by [Chris Fuller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Fuller_(director) "Chris Fuller (director)").

On 11 January 2024, it was announced Ritchie would direct the action adventure film *[Fountain of Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountain_of_Youth_(film) "Fountain of Youth (film)")*, for [Skydance Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydance_Media "Skydance Media") and [Apple Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Studios "Apple Studios"), with [Natalie Portman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalie_Portman "Natalie Portman"), [John Krasinski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Krasinski "John Krasinski") and [Domhnall Gleeson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domhnall_Gleeson "Domhnall Gleeson") set to star and production began in February 2024. The film was released on [Apple TV+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_TV%2B "Apple TV+") on 23 May 2025.

On 28 February 2024, it was announced that Ritchie would serve as director and executive producer alongside *[Top Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Boy "Top Boy")* creator [Ronan Bennett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronan_Bennett "Ronan Bennett") on the *[Ray Donovan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Donovan "Ray Donovan")* spin-off series *The Donovans* for the streaming service [Paramount+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount%2B "Paramount+"). By October 9, the project had been retitled *The Associates* and had been reworked into a standalone series, with [Tom Hardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Hardy "Tom Hardy"), [Helen Mirren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Mirren "Helen Mirren"), and [Pierce Brosnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierce_Brosnan "Pierce Brosnan") in final negotiations to star in the project, now called *[MobLand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MobLand "MobLand")*. On 13 May, it was announced Ritchie would write and direct the film *[Wife & Dog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wife_%26_Dog "Wife & Dog")*; in November, it was announced the project would star [Benedict Cumberbatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedict_Cumberbatch "Benedict Cumberbatch"), [Rosamund Pike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosamund_Pike "Rosamund Pike"), and [Anthony Hopkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Hopkins "Anthony Hopkins"), with production expected to begin in February 2025.

On 29 May 2024, it was announced that Ritchie would serve as executive producer and director on the [Amazon Prime Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Prime_Video "Amazon Prime Video") television series *[Young Sherlock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Sherlock_(British_TV_series) "Young Sherlock (British TV series)")*, based on the *[Young Sherlock Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Sherlock_Holmes_(books) "Young Sherlock Holmes (books)")* series of [young adult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_adult_literature "Young adult literature") novels by [Andy Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Lane "Andy Lane"). On 30 April 2025 it was announced that Ritchie would reunite with Gyllenhaal to direct the sequel to *[Road House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_House_(2024_film) "Road House (2024 film)")*, with [Will Beall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Beall "Will Beall") writing the screenplay; however, in July Ritchie exited the film. On 29 October, it was announced that Ritchie and Statham would reunite on the action thriller *[Viva La Madness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viva_La_Madness "Viva La Madness")*, based on the 2011 novel by [J. ## Filmmaking

### Influences and style

Ritchie has cited [Quentin Tarantino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quentin_Tarantino "Quentin Tarantino") and [Sergio Leone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Leone "Sergio Leone") as influences on his work. However, he has stated "just about every film — any good film — that's ever been made has had an influence on me. But then how much of it, I have no idea". He has complimented several films including *[The Long Good Friday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Long_Good_Friday "The Long Good Friday")* (1980), [*The Good, the Bad, and the Ugly*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Good,_the_Bad_and_the_Ugly "The Good, the Bad and the Ugly") (1966), *[Seven Samurai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Samurai "Seven Samurai")* (1954) and *[Once Upon a Time in the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Once_Upon_a_Time_in_the_West "Once Upon a Time in the West")* (1968). When asked about the influence of criminals [Ronald and Reginald Kray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kray_twins "Kray twins"), Ritchie states "It's inevitable ... everything, more or less, of the old-school villainy related back to the Krays at some point. And the Krays were a lot worse than everyone thinks they are. ... And I know what those boys were doing was a hundred times worse than what everyone thought was going on. So it's inevitable that anything that is genuine, and old, and British will somehow have something to do with the Krays".

Ritchie's films often incorporate memorable and "colourful" characters, for instance, Irish [boxer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxing "Boxing") Mickey O'Neil in *Snatch*, and crime boss "Hatchet" Harry in *Lock, Stock, and Two Smoking Barrels.* In his crime films, there is also fragmented dialogue, with many characters behaving menacingly and using [cockney slang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhyming_slang "Rhyming slang"). The portrayal of the [British class system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_class_in_the_United_Kingdom "Social class in the United Kingdom") has also been explored. Costume designer for *The Gentlemen*, [Michael Wilkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Wilkinson_(costume_designer) "Michael Wilkinson (costume designer)"), said "Each character has an iconic, memorable look — a little larger than life".

Fast-paced and energetic action scenes serve as [cinematic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinematic_techniques "Cinematic techniques") choices, as well as placing his characters in combat or violent situations. Ritchie has used fast-[cutting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_cutting "Fast cutting") and slow motion to build momentum in the story, and to create a high-impact viewing experience, respectively. He is also known to use interweaving stories and a [non-linear narrative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_narrative "Nonlinear narrative") such as a circular plot in his films; this is found in the case of *Lock, Stock, and Two Smoking Barrels,* *Sherlock Holmes* and *Snatch*.

Ritchie has said this on his creative process:

> My creative process has never been something I can put into words. It's very random, very scattered and can sometimes lead down dark alleyways and dead ends. What I will say is I think any director needs to immerse himself in both real life and in history to fully open up creative processes. And you must be prepared for the reality that any creative process worth its salt needs to be revised, reworked and, on occasion, thrown out the window entirely.

Ritchie has worked multiple times with [Vinnie Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinnie_Jones "Vinnie Jones"), [Jason Statham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Statham "Jason Statham"), [Jason Flemyng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Flemyng "Jason Flemyng"), [Alan Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Ford_(actor) "Alan Ford (actor)"), [Geoff Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoff_Bell_(actor) "Geoff Bell (actor)"), [Mark Strong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Strong "Mark Strong"), [Jude Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jude_Law "Jude Law"), [Eddie Marsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Marsan "Eddie Marsan"), [Jared Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jared_Harris "Jared Harris"), [Charlie Hunnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Hunnam "Charlie Hunnam"), [Josh Hartnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Hartnett "Josh Hartnett"), [Hugh Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Grant "Hugh Grant"), [Jake Gyllenhaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Gyllenhaal "Jake Gyllenhaal"), [Eiza González](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiza_Gonz%C3%A1lez "Eiza González"), [Henry Golding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Golding "Henry Golding"), [Cary Elwes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cary_Elwes "Cary Elwes"), [Bugzy Malone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugzy_Malone "Bugzy Malone") and [Henry Cavill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cavill "Henry Cavill").

## Personal life

Ritchie started training in [Shotokan karate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shotokan "Shotokan") at age seven at the [Budokwai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budokwai "Budokwai") in London, where he later achieved a black belt in both Shotokan and [Judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo "Judo"). He is also a third degree black belt in [Brazilian jiu-jitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_jiu-jitsu "Brazilian jiu-jitsu") under [Renzo Gracie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renzo_Gracie "Renzo Gracie").

Ritchie says that he can speak Hebrew.

Ritchie met American singer-songwriter [Madonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna "Madonna") at a dinner party hosted by mutual friends [Sting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sting_(musician) "Sting (musician)") and [Trudie Styler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trudie_Styler "Trudie Styler") in 1998, and they began a relationship. On 18 May 2000, Ritchie was arrested by police for allegedly assaulting a man outside the [Kensington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kensington "Kensington") home that he shared with Madonna. On 22 December 2000, he married Madonna at [Skibo Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skibo_Castle "Skibo Castle") in Scotland. They had a son, Rocco John Ritchie, born in 2000, and adopted a Malawian baby, David Banda, in 2006.

Madonna filed for divorce in October 2008, citing irreconcilable differences. On 15 December 2008, Madonna announced that she had agreed to a divorce settlement with Ritchie, the terms of which granted him £50–60 million, which includes the value of the couple's [London pub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Punch_Bowl,_Mayfair "The Punch Bowl, Mayfair") and [Wiltshire estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashcombe_House,_Wiltshire "Ashcombe House, Wiltshire"). The couple issued a joint statement calling settlement reports "misleading and inaccurate". At the Central Family Court in [Holborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holborn "Holborn"), district judge Caroline Reid pronounced the *[decree nisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decree_nisi "Decree nisi"),* which dissolved the marriage within six weeks. Madonna and Ritchie reached a custody agreement for their children.

In February 2011, his London home was briefly occupied by members of the [Really Free School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Really_Free_School "Really Free School"), a squatter organisation.

In 2010, Ritchie met English model Jacqui Ainsley. They married on 30 July 2015. The couple have three children.

In July 2020, Ritchie received a six-month driving ban after he was caught by [CyclingMikey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CyclingMikey "CyclingMikey") using a mobile device while operating a motor vehicle.

## Other business ventures

Ritchie owns a pub, *The Lore of the Land*, in London, and previously co-owned another, *The Walmer Castle* with [David Beckham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Beckham "David Beckham") until 2022 when it was taken over by Piers Adams, a French entrepreneur. Ritchie owns a small brewing company, Gritchie Brewing Company which brews beer on his [Ashcombe Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashcombe_House,_Wiltshire "Ashcombe House, Wiltshire") in Wiltshire. He also owns The Wild Kitchen, a firm producing outdoor cooking equipment and tents, which launched at [Chelsea Flower Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_Flower_Show "Chelsea Flower Show") in 2021.

In October 2022 it was announced that [Compton Abbas Airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compton_Abbas_Airfield "Compton Abbas Airfield") was being sold to Ritchie, who owns the neighbouring Ashcombe Estate. Ritchie took over running of the airfield in February 2023.

Ritchie became a comic writer from 2007 to 2008 with the release of the [Virgin Comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_Comics "Liquid Comics") series *[Guy Ritchie's Gamekeeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Ritchie%27s_Gamekeeper "Guy Ritchie's Gamekeeper")*.
